\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:sharp-ppml-forbidden-sign}]
Write \(W_{gt}=\widetilde W_{gt}(\delta)\), \(\mu^\star_{gt}=\mu^\star_{gt}(\delta)\), and
\[
\mathcal E=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}W_{gt}^2 .
\]
A collapsed parameter is written \(\theta=(a,\beta)\) with \(r_{gt}'\theta=\sum_jX_{gt,j}a_j+D_{gt}\beta\), and \(\rho^\star(\delta)\) is the weighted nuisance coefficient of \cref{obj:def:weighted-fwl-residual}, so that
\[
W_{gt}=D_{gt}-\sum_jX_{gt,j}\rho^\star_j(\delta).
\]

\textit{Step 1: \(\mathcal E>0\).} Each summand is nonnegative, since \(q_{gt}>0\), \(\mu^\star_{gt}=\exp\{r_{gt}'\theta^\star(\delta)\}>0\), and \(W_{gt}^2\ge0\). If \(\mathcal E=0\), every summand vanishes, and positivity of \(q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}\) forces \(W_{gt}=0\) at every supported cell. Consider the collapsed parameter
\[
a=(-\rho^\star(\delta),1),
\]
whose treatment coordinate is one, so \(a\neq0\). Its collapsed index is exactly the residual,
\[
r_{gt}'a=D_{gt}-\sum_jX_{gt,j}\rho^\star_j(\delta)=W_{gt}=0
\qquad\text{at every supported cell,}
\]
whence \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}(r_{gt}'a)^2=0\), contradicting \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank}. Thus \(\mathcal E>0\).
% lean: fwlEnergy_pos_of_collapsedDesignRank, weightedFWLResidual_eq_rhoStar

\textit{Step 2: the perturbed criterion.} For \(x\in\mathbb R\) let \(\delta^x\) be \(\delta\) with the coordinate \(\delta_{ks}\) replaced by \(x\), and write \(x_0=\delta_{ks}\). Because \(D_{ks}=1\) and \(m_{gt}(\delta)=B_{gt}\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})\), the perturbation moves exactly one collapsed mean:
\[
m_{ks}(\delta^x)=B_{ks}\exp(x),
\qquad
m_{gt}(\delta^x)=m_{gt}(\delta)\quad\text{for }(g,t)\neq(k,s),
\]
Setting
\[
p_{gt}=
\begin{cases}
B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks}), & (g,t)=(k,s),\\
0, & (g,t)\neq(k,s),
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{we have}\qquad
\frac{d}{dx}\,m_{gt}(\delta^x)\Big|_{x=x_0}=p_{gt}.
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.expCellUpdatedMean, betaStar_update_hasDerivAt

\textit{Step 3: the score system and its solution branch.} For a direction \(d\) put
\[
S_d(x,\theta)
=
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}(r_{gt}'d)\left\{m_{gt}(\delta^x)-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta)\right\}.
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:pseudo-true-ppml-projection} applied at the effect array \(\delta^x\), the criterion \(L(\cdot;\delta^x)\) has a unique global maximizer, and \(S_d(x,\theta)=0\) for every \(d\) both holds at that maximizer and, conversely, characterizes it: vanishing of every directional score is sufficient for global maximality of a Poisson criterion with nonnegative weights, by the tangent-line bound \(\exp(\eta')\ge\exp(\eta)\{1+(\eta'-\eta)\}\).
% lean: Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_score, Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_isMax_of_score
The system is smooth in \((x,\theta)\), it is solved at \((x_0,\theta^\star(\delta))\), and its derivative in \(\theta\) at that point is the bilinear form
\[
d,e\ \mapsto\ -\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\,\mu^\star_{gt}\,(r_{gt}'d)(r_{gt}'e),
\]
which is negative definite — \(e\neq0\) gives some supported cell with \(r_{gt}'e\neq0\) by the injectivity of \(\theta\mapsto(r_{gt}'\theta)_{(g,t)}\) established from \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank} in \cref{obj:lem:pseudo-true-ppml-projection} — and hence invertible. The implicit function theorem therefore supplies a continuously differentiable \(x\mapsto\theta(x)\) defined near \(x_0\), with \(\theta(x_0)=\theta^\star(\delta)\) and \(S_d(x,\theta(x))=0\) for every \(d\); by the characterization above and uniqueness of the maximizer, \(\theta(x)=\theta^\star(\delta^x)\) for \(x\) near \(x_0\). In particular \(x\mapsto\beta^\star(\delta^x)\) is differentiable at \(x_0\), with derivative the treatment coordinate \(v_\beta\) of \(v=\theta'(x_0)\).
% lean: Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_expCell_argmax_snd_hasDerivAt
Differentiating \(S_d(x,\theta(x))=0\) at \(x=x_0\) and using Step 2 gives the linearized score: for every direction \(d\),
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gt}(r_{gt}'d)
\left\{
p_{gt}-\mu^\star_{gt}\,(r_{gt}'v)
\right\}=0 .
\]

\textit{Step 4: solving the linearized score by weighted FWL.} Put
\[
K=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}>0,
\qquad
\psi_{gt}=\frac{q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}}{K},
\qquad
\langle f,h\rangle=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T\psi_{gt}f_{gt}h_{gt},
\]
and define the mean-normalized one-cell source and the nuisance part of \(v=(v_a,v_\beta)\) by
\[
u_{gt}=\frac{p_{gt}}{\mu^\star_{gt}},
\qquad
\alpha_{gt}=\sum_jX_{gt,j}v_{a,j},
\]
so that \(r_{gt}'v=\alpha_{gt}+v_\beta D_{gt}\) and \(\alpha\) lies in the span of the nuisance columns \(X_{\cdot,j}\). Because \(\mu^\star_{gt}>0\) and \(K\psi_{gt}=q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}\), each summand of the linearized score factors as
\[
q_{gt}(r_{gt}'d)\left\{p_{gt}-\mu^\star_{gt}\,(r_{gt}'v)\right\}
=
K\,\psi_{gt}\,(r_{gt}'d)\left\{u_{gt}-v_\beta D_{gt}-\alpha_{gt}\right\},
\]
so dividing the whole linearized score by \(K>0\) rewrites it as
\[
\langle u-v_\beta D-\alpha,\ (r_{gt}'d)_{g,t}\rangle=0
\qquad\text{for every direction }d .
\]
Taking \(d\) to be the pure treatment direction gives \(\langle u-v_\beta D-\alpha,\ D\rangle=0\); taking \(d=(\rho,0)\) for arbitrary \(\rho\) gives \(\langle u-v_\beta D-\alpha,\ h\rangle=0\) for every \(h\) in the nuisance span.
% lean: linearizedScore_snd_eq_weightedFWL
By \cref{obj:def:weighted-fwl-residual}, \(D-W\) is the \(\psi\)-weighted projection of \(D\) on the nuisance span, so \(W\) is \(\psi\)-orthogonal to that span and \(\langle W,D\rangle=\langle W,W\rangle\). Pairing \(W\) with \(u-v_\beta D-\alpha\) and using both normal equations (the second one at \(h=D-W\) and at \(h=\alpha\)),
\[
0=\langle W,\ u-v_\beta D-\alpha\rangle=\langle W,u\rangle-v_\beta\langle W,W\rangle .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Panel.Weighted.WeightedSupport.scalar_fwl_of_normalEqs
Now \(\langle W,W\rangle=\mathcal E/K>0\) by Step 1, and, since \(p\) is supported on the single cell \((k,s)\),
\[
\langle W,u\rangle
=
\frac{1}{K}\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}W_{gt}\frac{p_{gt}}{\mu^\star_{gt}}
=
\frac{q_{ks}B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks})W_{ks}}{K}.
\]
Dividing,
\[
v_\beta=\frac{\langle W,u\rangle}{\langle W,W\rangle}
=\frac{q_{ks}B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks})W_{ks}}{\mathcal E}.
\]

\textit{Step 5: the derivative formula.} Combining Steps 3 and 4,
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\,\beta^\star\!\bigl(\delta\text{ with }\delta_{ks}\text{ replaced by }x\bigr)\Big|_{x=\delta_{ks}}
=
v_\beta
=
\frac{q_{ks}B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks})\widetilde W_{ks}(\delta)}{\mathcal E(\delta)} ,
\]
which is the asserted differentiability at \(x=\delta_{ks}\) together with the displayed derivative.
% lean: betaStar_update_hasDerivAt, sharp_ppml_forbidden_sign

\textit{Step 6: the sign equivalences.} The derivative is \(cW_{ks}\) with
\[
c=\frac{q_{ks}B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks})}{\mathcal E}>0,
\]
because \(q_{ks}>0\), \(B_{ks}=\bar b_k\exp(\gamma_{s0})>0\), \(\exp(\delta_{ks})>0\), and \(\mathcal E>0\) by Step 1. Multiplication by a positive scalar preserves and reflects each of the three order relations, so
\[
cW_{ks}<0\iff W_{ks}<0,
\qquad
cW_{ks}=0\iff W_{ks}=0,
\qquad
0<cW_{ks}\iff 0<W_{ks},
\]
which, with \(W_{ks}=\widetilde W_{ks}(\delta)\), are exactly the three asserted equivalences.
% lean: sharp_ppml_forbidden_sign
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:homogeneous-effect-reduction}]
For a collapsed parameter \(\theta\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal C|+T}\) and a supported cell \((g,t)\), write the collapsed linear index as
\[
\eta_{gt}(\theta)=r_{gt}'\theta,
\]
so that, by \cref{obj:def:collapsed-population-projection},
\[
L(\theta;\delta)
=
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gt}\left\{m_{gt}(\delta)\,\eta_{gt}(\theta)-\exp\!\left(\eta_{gt}(\theta)\right)\right\},
\qquad
\mu^\star_{gt}(\delta)=\exp\!\left\{\eta_{gt}(\theta^\star(\delta))\right\}.
\]
The map \(\theta\mapsto\eta_{gt}(\theta)\) is linear, so \(\eta_{gt}(\theta)-\eta_{gt}(\theta')=\eta_{gt}(\theta-\theta')\) for all \(\theta,\theta'\).

\textit{Step 1: the candidate parameter.} Let \(\infty\) be the never-treated reference cohort and define \(\theta^0=(a^0,\delta_0)\) coordinatewise by
\[
a^0_{\text{int}}=\log\bar b_\infty,
\qquad
a^0_{\text{coh},c}=\log\bar b_c-\log\bar b_\infty\quad(c\in\mathcal C,\ c\neq\infty),
\qquad
a^0_{\text{time},u}=\gamma_{u0}\quad(u\neq1),
\]
with treatment coordinate \(\delta_0\).
% lean: homogeneous_effect_reduction
\Cref{obj:ass:unit-untreated-exponential-mean} carries the normalization \(\gamma_{10}=0\). Hence, for every period \(t\),
\[
\begin{cases}
0, & t=1,\\
a^0_{\text{time},t}, & t\neq1,
\end{cases}
\quad=\quad\gamma_{t0},
\]
since for \(t=1\) all period dummies are switched off and \(\gamma_{10}=0\), while for \(t\neq1\) exactly the dummy of period \(t\) contributes. Likewise, for every \(g\in\mathcal C\),
\[
\begin{cases}
0, & g=\infty,\\
a^0_{\text{coh},g}, & g\neq\infty,
\end{cases}
\quad=\quad
\begin{cases}
0, & g=\infty,\\
\log\bar b_g-\log\bar b_\infty, & g\neq\infty,
\end{cases}
\]
because exactly the dummy of cohort \(g\) contributes when \(g\neq\infty\), and none when \(g=\infty\).

\textit{Step 2: the index at \(\theta^0\).} Adding the intercept, cohort, period, and treatment contributions in the two cases \(g=\infty\) and \(g\neq\infty\) gives, for every \(g\in\mathcal C\) and every period \(t\),
\[
\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)=\log\bar b_g+\gamma_{t0}+D_{gt}\delta_0 .
\]
% lean: homogeneous_effect_reduction

\textit{Step 3: the homogeneous-effect hypothesis in product form.} For every \(g\in\mathcal C\) and every \(t\),
\[
D_{gt}\delta_{gt}=D_{gt}\delta_0 :
\]
if \(D_{gt}=1\) the common treated-cell hypothesis gives \(\delta_{gt}=\delta_0\), and if \(D_{gt}=0\) both sides vanish.

\textit{Step 4: the criterion is exactly specified at \(\theta^0\).} Using \(B_{gt}=\bar b_g\exp(\gamma_{t0})\) together with Steps 2 and 3, the observed collapsed mean satisfies
\[
m_{gt}(\delta)
=
B_{gt}\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})
=
\exp\{\log\bar b_g+\gamma_{t0}+D_{gt}\delta_0\}
=
\exp\{\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)\}.
\]
In particular \(m_{gt}(\delta)>0\) and
\[
\log m_{gt}(\delta)=\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)
\qquad\text{for every supported cell.}
\]

\textit{Step 5: the scalar Poisson cell inequality.} For every \(m>0\) and every real \(\eta\),
\[
m\eta-\exp(\eta)\le m\log m-\exp(\log m),
\]
with strict inequality when \(\eta\neq\log m\). Apply \(1+x\le\exp(x)\), strict for \(x\neq0\), at \(x=\eta-\log m\), multiply by \(m>0\), and rearrange using \(\exp(\eta)=m\exp(\eta-\log m)\).
% lean: homogeneous_effect_reduction

\textit{Step 6: the cell masses are strictly positive.} First \(T>0\): if \(T=0\), then for the nonzero collapsed parameter with zero nuisance block and treatment coordinate one the weighted sum \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}(r_{gt}'a)^2\) is an empty sum, hence zero, contradicting \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank}. Since \(\pi_g\in(0,1)\), it follows that
\[
q_{gt}=\frac{\pi_g}{T}>0
\qquad\text{for every supported cell.}
\]

\textit{Step 7: \(\theta^0\) is a global maximizer.} Fix any \(\theta\). Applying Step 5 cell by cell with \(m=m_{gt}(\delta)>0\) and \(\eta=\eta_{gt}(\theta)\), and using \(\log m_{gt}(\delta)=\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)\) from Step 4,
\[
m_{gt}(\delta)\eta_{gt}(\theta)-\exp\!\left(\eta_{gt}(\theta)\right)
\le
m_{gt}(\delta)\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)-\exp\!\left(\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)\right).
\]
Multiplying by \(q_{gt}>0\) and summing over supported cells,
\[
L(\theta;\delta)\le L(\theta^0;\delta).
\]

\textit{Step 8: the maximizer is unique.} Suppose \(L(\theta;\delta)=L(\theta^0;\delta)\) and \(\theta\neq\theta^0\). Then \(\theta-\theta^0\neq0\), so \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank} gives
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\left(\eta_{gt}(\theta-\theta^0)\right)^2>0 ,
\]
and by linearity \(\eta_{gt}(\theta-\theta^0)=\eta_{gt}(\theta)-\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)\), so some supported cell \((g_0,t_0)\) has
\[
\eta_{g_0t_0}(\theta)\neq\eta_{g_0t_0}(\theta^0)=\log m_{g_0t_0}(\delta).
\]
At that cell the strict half of Step 5 applies, and all other cells obey the weak inequality of Step 7. Since \(q_{g_0t_0}>0\), summing gives
\[
L(\theta;\delta)<L(\theta^0;\delta),
\]
contradicting the assumed equality. Hence \(\theta=\theta^0\).

\textit{Step 9: identification of the projection.} By Steps 7 and 8 the criterion \(L(\cdot;\delta)\) has \(\theta^0\) as its unique global maximizer, and \cref{obj:def:collapsed-population-projection} selects \(\theta^\star(\delta)\) among the global maximizers, so
\[
\theta^\star(\delta)=\theta^0 .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.maximizerOrZero, homogeneous_effect_reduction

\textit{Step 10: the two conclusions.} The treatment coordinate of \(\theta^0\) is \(\delta_0\), so
\[
\beta^\star(\delta)=\delta_0 ,
\]
and for every \(g\in\mathcal C\) and every period \(t\), by Step 2,
\[
\mu^\star_{gt}(\delta)
=
\exp\{\eta_{gt}(\theta^\star(\delta))\}
=
\exp\{\log\bar b_g+\gamma_{t0}+D_{gt}\delta_0\}
=
B_{gt}\exp(D_{gt}\delta_0).
\]
% lean: homogeneous_effect_reduction
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:primitive-global-frontier}]
Write \(q_{gt}=\pi_g/T\) and \(m_{gt}=m_{gt}(\delta)=B_{gt}\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})\) with \(B_{gt}=\bar b_g\exp(\gamma_{t0})\), so that the quantities of \cref{obj:def:frontier-elimination-handle} satisfy
\[
h_{gt}=\pi_g m_{gt}=T\,q_{gt}m_{gt},
\qquad
R_g=\sum_{t=1}^T h_{gt},
\qquad
C_t=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}h_{gt},
\qquad
M=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}R_g,
\qquad
A=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T D_{gt}h_{gt}.
\]
A collapsed parameter is written \(\theta=(a,\beta)\) with nuisance block \(a\) and treatment coordinate \(\beta\), and \(r_{gt}'\theta=\sum_jX_{gt,j}a_j+D_{gt}\beta\). Finally set
\[
B^{obs}_{gt}=\frac{m_{g1}m_{\infty t}}{m_{\infty 1}},
\qquad
H=\{(g,t):g\in\mathcal C,\ D_{gt}=1\},
\qquad
Z=\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt},
\]
\[
\tau_{gt}=\frac{m_{gt}}{B^{obs}_{gt}}-1,
\qquad
\omega_{gt}=\frac{q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}}{Z},
\qquad
PTT=\sum_{(g,t)\in H}\omega_{gt}\tau_{gt} .
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier, observedCounterfactualBaseline, granularTau, pttNormalizer, pttWeight, counterfactualSharePTT

\textit{Step 1: positivity of the primitive margins.} Since \(T\ge4\), in particular \(T>0\), and \(\infty\in\mathcal C\), so \(\mathcal C\) is nonempty. Every \(h_{gt}=\pi_g\bar b_g\exp(\gamma_{t0})\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})\) is a product of strictly positive factors, hence
\[
h_{gt}>0,\qquad R_g>0,\qquad C_t>0,\qquad M>0 .
\]
% lean: frontierPrimitiveH_pos, frontierPrimitiveRow_pos, frontierPrimitiveColumn_pos, frontierPrimitiveTotal_pos
Also, summing \(h_{gt}\) in the two possible orders,
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T C_t=M=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}R_g .
\]
% lean: frontierPrimitiveColumn_sum

\textit{Step 2: the zero-treatment row-column fit.} Define the collapsed parameter \(\theta^0=(a^0,0)\), whose treatment coordinate is zero and whose nuisance levels are
\[
a^0_{\text{int}}=\log\frac{R_\infty}{\pi_\infty}+\log C_1-\log M,
\qquad
a^0_{\text{coh},g}=\log\frac{R_g}{\pi_g}-\log\frac{R_\infty}{\pi_\infty}\quad(g\neq\infty),
\qquad
a^0_{\text{time},t}=\log C_t-\log C_1\quad(t\neq1).
\]
Adding the intercept, cohort, and period contributions — the cohort dummy being absent for \(g=\infty\) and the period dummy for \(t=1\) — gives, for every supported cell,
\[
r_{gt}'\theta^0=\log\frac{R_g}{\pi_g}+\log C_t-\log M,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)=\frac{R_gC_t}{\pi_g M},
\]
the exponentiation being legitimate because \(R_g/\pi_g\), \(C_t\), and \(M\) are strictly positive by Step 1.
% lean: frontierNuisanceParameter, frontierNuisanceParameter_index, frontierNuisanceParameter_exp

\textit{Step 3: the conditional residuals and their margins.} Define the mass-weighted residual of \(\theta^0\) in cell \((g,t)\),
\[
e_{gt}
=
q_{gt}\left\{m_{gt}-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\right\}
=
\frac{\pi_g}{T}\left\{m_{gt}-\frac{R_gC_t}{\pi_gM}\right\}
=
\frac{1}{T}\left\{h_{gt}-\frac{R_gC_t}{M}\right\}.
\]
% lean: frontierConditionalResidual, frontierConditionalResidual_eq
Its row and column sums vanish: using \(\sum_tC_t=M\) and \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}R_g=M\) from Step 1,
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T e_{gt}=\frac{1}{T}\left\{R_g-\frac{R_g}{M}\sum_{t=1}^TC_t\right\}=0
\qquad(g\in\mathcal C),
\]
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C} e_{gt}=\frac{1}{T}\left\{C_t-\frac{C_t}{M}\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}R_g\right\}=0
\qquad(t=1,\ldots,T).
\]
% lean: frontierConditionalResidual_row_sum, frontierConditionalResidual_column_sum

\textit{Step 4: the scores at \(\theta^0\).} Every collapsed nuisance regressor \(X_{\cdot,j}\) is either the constant \(1\), the indicator of one cohort \(g_0\neq\infty\), or the indicator of one period \(t_0\neq1\). The corresponding score at \(\theta^0\) is therefore \(\sum_{g,t}e_{gt}\), \(\sum_te_{g_0t}\), or \(\sum_ge_{gt_0}\), and all three vanish by Step 3:
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}X_{gt,j}\left\{m_{gt}-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\right\}=0
\qquad\text{for every nuisance coordinate }j .
\]
% lean: frontierConditionalNuisanceScore
The treatment score at \(\theta^0\) is, by Step 3 and the definitions of \(A\) and \(\Phi\),
\[
S
=
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}D_{gt}\left\{m_{gt}-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\right\}
=
\frac{1}{T}\left\{A-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T D_{gt}R_gC_t\right\}
=
\frac{\Phi}{TM},
\]
and \(TM>0\) by Step 1.
% lean: frontierConditionalScalarScore

\textit{Step 5: the pseudo-true coefficient has the sign of \(S\).} By \cref{obj:lem:pseudo-true-ppml-projection}, \(\theta^\star(\delta)\) is the unique global maximizer of \(L(\cdot;\delta)\) and every directional score vanishes there:
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}(r_{gt}'d)\left\{m_{gt}-\mu^\star_{gt}(\delta)\right\}=0
\qquad\text{for every direction }d .
\]
Write \(v=\theta^\star(\delta)-\theta^0\), whose treatment coordinate is \(v_\beta=\beta^\star(\delta)\) because \(\theta^0\) has treatment coordinate zero. Decomposing an arbitrary direction \(d=(d_a,d_\beta)\) into its nuisance and treatment parts and using Step 4, the score at \(\theta^0\) in direction \(d\) is
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}(r_{gt}'d)\left\{m_{gt}-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\right\}
=
d_\beta\,S .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_snd_sign_of_nuisance_score
Suppose first \(v\neq0\). Subtracting the vanishing score at \(\theta^\star(\delta)\) in direction \(v\) from the score at \(\theta^0\) in direction \(v\),
\[
\beta^\star(\delta)\,S
=
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}(r_{gt}'v)\left\{\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^\star(\delta))-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\right\}.
\]
Each summand is nonnegative: \(q_{gt}>0\), and since \(r_{gt}'\theta^\star(\delta)-r_{gt}'\theta^0=r_{gt}'v\) and the exponential is increasing, \(r_{gt}'v\) and \(\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^\star(\delta))-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\) have the same sign. Moreover \(v\neq0\) and the injectivity of \(\theta\mapsto(r_{gt}'\theta)_{(g,t)}\) established from \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank} in \cref{obj:lem:pseudo-true-ppml-projection} give a supported cell with \(r_{gt}'v\neq0\), where the summand is strictly positive by strict monotonicity. Hence
\[
\beta^\star(\delta)\,S>0
\qquad\text{whenever }\theta^\star(\delta)\neq\theta^0 .
\]
If instead \(v=0\), then \(\beta^\star(\delta)=0\), and applying the vanishing score at \(\theta^\star(\delta)=\theta^0\) to the pure treatment direction gives \(S=0\). Conversely, if \(S=0\) then by the previous display every score at \(\theta^0\) vanishes in every direction, so \(\theta^0\) is a global maximizer of \(L(\cdot;\delta)\) — for a Poisson criterion with nonnegative weights the tangent-line bound \(\exp(\eta')\ge\exp(\eta)\{1+(\eta'-\eta)\}\) makes vanishing scores sufficient for global maximality — and uniqueness forces \(\theta^\star(\delta)=\theta^0\), i.e. \(\beta^\star(\delta)=0\).
% lean: Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_isMax_of_score
Collecting the two cases: \(\beta^\star(\delta)=0\iff S=0\), and when both are nonzero their product is positive, so they have the same sign. Since \(S=\Phi/(TM)\) with \(TM>0\), the sign of \(S\) is the sign of \(\Phi\), and therefore
\[
\beta^\star(\delta)<0\iff \Phi<0,\qquad
\beta^\star(\delta)=0\iff \Phi=0,\qquad
0<\beta^\star(\delta)\iff 0<\Phi .
\]
% lean: betaStar_sign_frontierEliminationHandle

\textit{Step 6: the observed baseline proxy reproduces the untreated mean.} For every supported cohort \(g\) the first-period treatment indicator is zero: if \(g=\infty\) this is the never-treated path, and if \(g\) is finite then \(D_{g1}=1\) would force the adoption date to be the first period, which the support excludes. Also \(D_{\infty t}=0\) for every \(t\), and \cref{obj:ass:unit-untreated-exponential-mean} gives \(\gamma_{10}=0\). Hence, for every \(g\in\mathcal C\) and every \(t\),
\[
B^{obs}_{gt}
=
\frac{m_{g1}m_{\infty t}}{m_{\infty 1}}
=
\frac{\bar b_ge^{\gamma_{10}}\cdot\bar b_\infty e^{\gamma_{t0}}}{\bar b_\infty e^{\gamma_{10}}}
=
\bar b_ge^{\gamma_{t0}}
=
B_{gt},
\]
the cancellation being legitimate because \(m_{\infty1}=\bar b_\infty>0\).
% lean: primitive_global_frontier

\textit{Step 7: \(H\) is nonempty and \(Z>0\).} \Cref{obj:ass:multicohort-frontier-scope} supplies a supported finite cohort adopting in the second period; in that period its treatment indicator equals one, so \(H\) is nonempty. For every \((g,t)\in H\) we have \(q_{gt}>0\) and, by Step 6, \(B^{obs}_{gt}=B_{gt}>0\). Hence every summand of \(Z\) is positive and at least one exists:
\[
Z=\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}>0 .
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier

\textit{Step 8: membership in \(\mathcal R_T\) is equivalent to \(\Phi<0\).} If the induced primitive tuple lies in \(\mathcal R_T\), then the last clause of \cref{obj:def:global-sign-reversal-region} asserts negativity of its treatment coordinate; that coordinate is the treatment coordinate of the maximizer of the criterion written in the restricted primitive coordinates, which is the same function of \(\theta\) as \(L(\theta;\delta)\), so it equals \(\beta^\star(\delta)\), and Step 5 gives \(\Phi<0\).
% lean: primitiveLimitingCriterion_restrict, primitiveBetaStar_restrict
Conversely suppose \(\Phi<0\). The horizon, support, and frontier-scope clauses of \cref{obj:def:global-sign-reversal-region} are hypotheses of the present theorem. The simplex clause holds because the finite-array shares sum to one for every \(N\ge|\mathcal C|\),
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\frac{n_{gN}}{N}=1,
\]
every unit label lying in \(\mathcal C\), and passing to the limit with \cref{obj:ass:cohort-share-limit} gives \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\pi_g=1\).
% lean: cohortShareLimit_sum_eq_one
The untreated-mean clause is witnessed by \(g\mapsto\bar b_g\), since the primitive untreated coordinate in cell \((g,t)\) is \(B_{gt}=\bar b_ge^{\gamma_{t0}}\). The positive-effect clause is \cref{obj:ass:strict-positive-effects}, and the rank clause is \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank} after rewriting the sum over supported cells as the double sum over \(g\in\mathcal C\) and \(t\). Finally Step 5 gives \(\beta^\star(\delta)<0\), which is the tuple's treatment coordinate. Hence the tuple lies in \(\mathcal R_T\).

\textit{Step 9: the four-cohort polynomial.} Let \(x>1\), \(y>1\), and consider the four-period support \(\{2,3,4,\infty\}\) with \(T=4\), equal shares \(\pi_g=1/4\), unit limiting baselines, zero untreated time effects (so \(B_{gt}=1\)), multiplier \(x\) on every treated cell except \((2,4)\), and multiplier \(y\) at \((2,4)\); that is, \(\exp(\delta_{gt})=x\) on treated cells other than \((2,4)\) and \(\exp(\delta_{2,4})=y\). With treatment absorbing from the adoption date on, so that \(D_{gt}=1\) exactly when \(g\le t\) and \(D_{\infty t}=0\), the array \(h_{gt}=\pi_gB_{gt}\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})\) is
\[
4\,(h_{gt})=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&x&x&y\\
1&1&x&x\\
1&1&1&x\\
1&1&1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with rows \(g=2,3,4,\infty\) and columns \(t=1,2,3,4\). Summing rows and columns,
\[
4R_2=1+2x+y,\quad 4R_3=2+2x,\quad 4R_4=3+x,\quad 4R_\infty=4,
\]
\[
4C_1=4,\quad 4C_2=x+3,\quad 4C_3=2x+2,\quad 4C_4=2x+y+1,
\]
\[
4M=10+5x+y,
\qquad
4A=5x+y .
\]
The treated cells of row \(g=2\) are \(t=2,3,4\), of row \(g=3\) are \(t=3,4\), and of row \(g=4\) is \(t=4\), so
\[
16\sum_{g,t}D_{gt}R_gC_t
=
(1+2x+y)(5x+y+6)+(2+2x)(4x+y+3)+(3+x)(2x+y+1),
\]
which expands to \(20x^2+10xy+y^2+38x+12y+15\), while
\[
16\,MA=(10+5x+y)(5x+y)=25x^2+10xy+y^2+50x+10y .
\]
Subtracting,
\[
\Phi=MA-\sum_{g,t}D_{gt}R_gC_t=\frac{5x^2+12x-2y-15}{16}.
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier, fourMultiplierEffects, frontierEliminationHandle

\textit{Step 10: the numerical identity.} With \(5(101/100)^2=51005/10000\) and \(12(101/100)=1212/100\),
\[
\frac{5(101/100)^2+12(101/100)-15}{2}=\frac{4441}{4000}.
\]

\textit{Step 11: the value at the witness.} For \(W_4\) of \cref{obj:def:four-cohort-witness} the treated multipliers are \(\exp(\log(101/100))=101/100\) off cell \((2,4)\) and \(\exp(\log4)=4\) at \((2,4)\), so Step 9 applies with \(x=101/100\) and \(y=4\):
\[
\Phi=\frac{5(101/100)^2+12(101/100)-2\cdot4-15}{16}=-\frac{11559}{32000}.
\]
% lean: fourCohortDelta_eq_multiplier, fourFrontierEliminationHandle_exact

\textit{Step 12: the counterfactual-share objects on treated cells.} Let \((g,t)\in H\), so \(D_{gt}=1\). Step 6 gives \(B^{obs}_{gt}=B_{gt}\), and \(B_{gt}>0\), so
\[
\tau_{gt}=\frac{m_{gt}}{B^{obs}_{gt}}-1=\frac{B_{gt}\exp(\delta_{gt})}{B_{gt}}-1=\exp(\delta_{gt})-1 ,
\]
and, using \(q_{gt}>0\), \(B^{obs}_{gt}>0\), and \(Z>0\) from Step 7,
\[
\omega_{gt}=\frac{q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}}{Z}>0 .
\]

\textit{Step 13: the weights sum to one.} By the definition of \(Z\) and \(Z>0\),
\[
\sum_{(g,t)\in H}\omega_{gt}
=
\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}
=
\frac{Z}{Z}
=
1 .
\]

\textit{Step 14: the ratio form of \(PTT\).} Expanding the definition and using \(B^{obs}_{gt}>0\) to cancel it inside each term,
\[
PTT
=
\sum_{(g,t)\in H}\frac{q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}}{Z}\left(\frac{m_{gt}}{B^{obs}_{gt}}-1\right)
=
\frac{\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}m_{gt}}{Z}-\frac{\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}}{Z},
\]
and the second fraction equals \(1\) by Step 13, so
\[
PTT=\frac{\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}m_{gt}}{\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}}-1 .
\]

\textit{Step 15: the sign contrast.} Suppose \(\Phi<0\). Step 5 gives \(\beta^\star(\delta)<0\). For \(PTT\), each treated cell has \(\omega_{gt}>0\) by Step 12, and \cref{obj:ass:strict-positive-effects} gives \(\delta_{gt}>0\), hence \(\exp(\delta_{gt})>1\) and \(\tau_{gt}>0\). The sum defining \(PTT\) thus has no negative term, and by Step 7 it has at least one term, which is strictly positive. Therefore \(PTT>0\), and
\[
\Phi<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \beta^\star(\delta)<0<PTT .
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Four-Cohort Sign Reversal]
\begin{enumerate}
\item The defining conditions of the region in \cref{obj:def:global-sign-reversal-region} are checked directly for the displayed primitive.  The horizon condition is \(4\le 4\).  The support contains the never-treated cohort and the finite cohorts \(2,3,4\), all dated after period \(1\), and these four cohorts give the required four-cohort frontier scope.  The shares sum to
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\pi_g=4\cdot \frac14=1,
\]
and, since \(\bar b_g=1\) and \(\gamma_{t0}=0\), the untreated mean representation is \(1=1\cdot \exp(0)\) in every supported cell.

For rank, let \(a\) be a collapsed parameter whose weighted quadratic design norm is zero.  Each summand is nonnegative and each supported cell mass is nonzero, so every collapsed index \(r_{gt}'a\) is zero.  Evaluating these zero equations first at the never-treated cohort in period \(1\) gives the intercept equal to zero; evaluating finite cohorts in period \(1\) gives all cohort dummy coordinates equal to zero; evaluating the never-treated cohort across periods gives all time dummy coordinates equal to zero; and evaluating the cohort \(2\), period \(2\), treated cell then gives the treatment coordinate equal to zero.  Hence \(a=0\), proving the collapsed design rank condition.

The sign condition is obtained from the primitive sign index \(\Phi\) in \cref{obj:def:frontier-elimination-handle}.  In the multiplier subfamily with common treated multiplier \(x\) off cell \((2,4)\) and multiplier \(y\) at \((2,4)\),
\[
\Phi=\frac{5x^2+12x-2y-15}{16}.
\]
Substituting \(x=101/100\) and \(y=4\) gives
\[
\Phi=-\frac{11559}{32000}<0.
\]
The sign characterization of the frontier index gives \(\beta^\star(\delta)<0\), and the restricted primitive has the same primitive treatment coordinate.  Together with the strict positivity of the treated effects verified in the next step, all clauses of \cref{obj:def:global-sign-reversal-region} hold.

\item If \(D_{gt}=1\), then the specified effect is either \(\log 4\) or \(\log(101/100)\).  Both are strictly positive because \(4>1\) and \(101/100>1\).

\item For the designated cell \((2,4)\), treatment is on and the effect is \(\delta_{2,4}=\log 4\).  For every other treated supported cell, the effect is \(\log(101/100)\).  Since \(101/100<4\) and the logarithm is strictly increasing on positive numbers,
\[
\log(101/100)<\log 4=\delta_{2,4}.
\]


\item At the zero-effect vector, the fitted mean is \(1\) in every supported cell by \cref{obj:prop:homogeneous-effect-reduction}.  Hence the mean weights in \cref{obj:def:weighted-fwl-residual} are all \(1/16\).  The displayed candidate residual table has row sums and column sums all equal to zero.  Moreover, treatment minus this table equals
\[
-\frac38+c_g+s_t,
\]
with
\[
(c_2,c_3,c_4,c_\infty)=\Bigl(\frac34,\frac12,\frac14,0\Bigr),
\qquad
(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4)=\Bigl(0,\frac14,\frac12,\frac34\Bigr),
\]
so treatment minus the table lies in the nuisance fixed-effect space.  The zero row and column sums make the table orthogonal, under the constant weights \(1/16\), to every nuisance regressor and therefore to the whole nuisance space.  Thus the weighted projection of treatment onto the nuisance space is treatment minus the table, and the residual is the table.  This gives exactly
\[
\widetilde W_{gt}
=
\frac{1}{8}
\begin{pmatrix}
-3 & 3 & 1 & -1\\
-1 & -3 & 3 & 1\\
1 & -1 & -3 & 3\\
3 & 1 & -1 & -3
\end{pmatrix}_{gt}.
\]


\item Evaluating the residual table at the designated cell gives
\[
\widetilde W_{2,4}(0)=-\frac18.
\]


\item The residual energy at zero effects is
\[
\sum_{g,t} q_{gt}\,\mu^\star_{gt}(0)\,\widetilde W_{gt}(0)^2
=
\frac1{16}\sum_{g,t}\widetilde W_{gt}(0)^2
=
\frac1{16}\cdot\frac{80}{64}
=
\frac5{64}.
\]
\cref{obj:thm:sharp-ppml-forbidden-sign}, applied to the treated cell \((2,4)\), gives
\[
\left.\frac{d}{dx}\beta^\star(\delta_{2,4}=x,\delta_{gt}=0\text{ otherwise})\right|_{x=0}
=
\frac{q_{2,4}\,B_{2,4}\,\exp(0)\,\widetilde W_{2,4}(0)}
{5/64}.
\]
Here \(q_{2,4}=1/16\), \(B_{2,4}=1\), and \(\widetilde W_{2,4}(0)=-1/8\).  Therefore the derivative is
\[
\frac{(1/16)\cdot 1\cdot 1\cdot(-1/8)}{5/64}
=
-\frac{1}{10}.
\]


\item By continuity of the weighted FWL residual in the effect vector, \(\widetilde W_{2,4}(\delta)\) is continuous at the zero-effect vector.  Since \(\widetilde W_{2,4}(0)=-1/8<0\), the inverse image of \(( -\infty,0)\) is a neighborhood of zero.  Choose a finite coordinate neighborhood contained in that inverse image, choose a positive radius for each coordinate neighborhood, and let \(\varepsilon\) be the minimum of those radii, or \(1\) if the finite coordinate set is empty.

Now take any \(\delta'\) such that \(|\delta'_{gt}|<\varepsilon\) on every supported cell and \(\delta'_{gt}>0\) on every treated supported cell.  Define the supported truncation \(\delta^s\) by \(\delta^s_{gt}=\delta'_{gt}\) on \(g\in\mathcal C\) and \(\delta^s_{gt}=0\) outside the support.  The choice of \(\varepsilon\) puts \(\delta^s\) in the coordinate neighborhood, hence \(\widetilde W_{2,4}(\delta^s)<0\).  Changing effects outside supported cohorts leaves the limiting criterion, fitted means, and weighted FWL residuals unchanged, so \(\widetilde W_{2,4}(\delta')=\widetilde W_{2,4}(\delta^s)<0\).  Applying \cref{obj:thm:sharp-ppml-forbidden-sign} again at the treated cell \((2,4)\), the derivative of \(x\mapsto\beta^\star\) with the \((2,4)\) coordinate of \(\delta'\) replaced by \(x\), evaluated at \(x=\delta'_{2,4}\), has the same sign as \(\widetilde W_{2,4}(\delta')\), because the denominator and multiplicative prefactor are strictly positive.  Thus this derivative is strictly negative for every such strictly positive \(\delta'\).

\item The seven conclusions above are assembled in the order stated: region membership, positivity of treated effects, uniqueness of the largest treated effect at \((2,4)\), the full weighted FWL residual table, the late-cell residual \(-1/8\), the exact late-cell derivative at zero, and the local negative derivative statement under strict positivity of treated supported effects.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
