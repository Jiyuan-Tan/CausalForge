\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:unit-fe-collapse}]
Throughout, a collapsed parameter \(\theta\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal C|+T}\) is written through the levels it induces,
\[
c_g(\theta)=
\begin{cases}
\theta_{\text{int}}, & g=\infty,\\
\theta_{\text{int}}+\theta_{\text{coh},g}, & g\neq\infty,
\end{cases}
\qquad
w_t(\theta)=
\begin{cases}
0, & t=1,\\
\theta_{\text{time},t}, & t\neq1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\beta(\theta)=\theta_{\text{trt}},
\]
so that for every supported cell \((g,t)\), \(g\in\mathcal C\),
\[
r_{gt}'\theta=c_g(\theta)+w_t(\theta)+\beta(\theta)D_{gt}.
\]
A unit parameter \(\theta_N\in\mathbb R^{N+T}\) is written the same way,
\[
\alpha_i(\theta_N)=
\begin{cases}
\theta_{N,\text{int}}, & i=1,\\
\theta_{N,\text{int}}+\theta_{N,\text{unit},i}, & i\neq1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
w_t(\theta_N)=
\begin{cases}
0, & t=1,\\
\theta_{N,\text{time},t}, & t\neq1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\beta(\theta_N)=\theta_{N,\text{trt}},
\]
so that
\[
v_{itN}'\theta_N=\alpha_i(\theta_N)+w_t(\theta_N)+\beta(\theta_N)D_{it},
\qquad D_{it}=D_{G_it}.
\]
Both identities are the statement that the intercept, dummy, and treatment coordinates of the regressor pick out exactly these terms.
% lean: unitIndex_eq_levels, collapsedIndex_eq_levels

Write the finite-array collapsed primitives as
\[
q_{gtN}=\frac{n_{gN}}{NT},
\qquad
m_{gtN}(\delta)=\bar b_{gN}\exp(\gamma_{t0})\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt}),
\]
\[
L_{Nc}(\theta;\delta)
=
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gtN}\{m_{gtN}(\delta)\,r_{gt}'\theta-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta)\},
\]
and write the unit-time observed mean entering the criterion \(L_N(\theta_N;\delta)\) of \cref{obj:def:unit-fe-population-projection} as
\[
m_{itN}(\delta)=b_{iN}\exp(\gamma_{t0}+D_{it}\delta_{G_it}),
\]
which is the form generated by the untreated mean of \cref{obj:ass:unit-untreated-exponential-mean} together with the proportional effects of \cref{obj:ass:proportional-effects}.

\textit{Step 0: standing positivity.} The horizon assumption \(T\ge4\) gives \(T>0\), and \(\infty\in\mathcal C\) makes \(\mathcal C\) nonempty, so at least one supported cell exists. Fix \(N\) with \(|\mathcal C|\le N\). \Cref{obj:ass:cohort-share-limit} then supplies \(N>0\) and \(n_{gN}>0\) for every \(g\in\mathcal C\); every unit label lies in \(\mathcal C\) by the array-support hypothesis. Consequently, for every \(g\in\mathcal C\) and every \(t\),
\[
q_{gtN}=\frac{n_{gN}}{NT}>0,
\qquad
\bar b_{gN}=n_{gN}^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_{gN}}b_{iN}>0,
\qquad
m_{gtN}(\delta)>0,
\qquad
m_{itN}(\delta)>0 ,
\]
the last two because they are products of positive baselines and exponentials.
% lean: unit_fe_collapse, withinCohortBaseline_pos, finiteObservedCohortMean_pos

\textit{Step 1: the collapsed designs are injective, and \(L_{Nc}\) has a unique maximizer.} If \(r_{gt}'\theta_1=r_{gt}'\theta_2\) at every supported cell, then \(a=\theta_1-\theta_2\) has \(r_{gt}'a=0\) at every supported cell, so \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_t q_{gt}(r_{gt}'a)^2=0\), and \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank} forces \(a=0\). Thus \(\theta\mapsto(r_{gt}'\theta)_{(g,t)}\) is injective.
% lean: collapsedDesignMap_injective
With positive masses \(q_{gtN}\), positive means \(m_{gtN}(\delta)\), and this injective design, the finite collapsed criterion is a finite positive-mass, positive-mean Poisson criterion, so it has exactly one global maximizer \(\theta_N^c\): each cell contribution \(\eta\mapsto m\eta-\exp(\eta)\) is strictly concave and satisfies \(m\eta-\exp(\eta)\le (m+1)^2/2-\min(m,1)|\eta|\), which by injectivity makes \(L_{Nc}(\cdot;\delta)\) continuous, coercive, and strictly concave.
% lean: finiteCollapsedCriterion_exists_unique_max, Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_exists_unique_max
Because \(\theta_N^c\) maximizes and the criterion is differentiable, every collapsed directional score vanishes: for every \(a\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal C|+T}\),
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gtN}\,(r_{gt}'a)\{m_{gtN}(\delta)-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta_N^c)\}=0 .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_score

\textit{Step 2: lifting the collapsed maximizer to unit fixed effects.} Define the unit parameter \(\theta_N^{\text{lift}}\) by
\[
\alpha_i(\theta_N^{\text{lift}})
=
\log\frac{b_{iN}}{\bar b_{G_iN}}+c_{G_i}(\theta_N^c),
\qquad
w_t(\theta_N^{\text{lift}})=w_t(\theta_N^c),
\qquad
\beta(\theta_N^{\text{lift}})=\beta(\theta_N^c).
\]
(Any prescribed levels are realized by a unique choice of intercept, dummy, and treatment coordinates.) By the two level identities,
\[
v_{itN}'\theta_N^{\text{lift}}
=
\log\frac{b_{iN}}{\bar b_{G_iN}}+r_{G_it}'\theta_N^c .
\]
% lean: liftCollapsedParameter, unitIndex_liftCollapsedParameter
Exponentiating and using \(m_{itN}(\delta)=(b_{iN}/\bar b_{G_iN})\,m_{G_it,N}(\delta)\), which holds because \(m_{itN}(\delta)=b_{iN}\exp(\gamma_{t0})\exp(D_{G_it}\delta_{G_it})\) and \(m_{G_it,N}(\delta)=\bar b_{G_iN}\exp(\gamma_{t0})\exp(D_{G_it}\delta_{G_it})\), each unit residual is the corresponding cell residual scaled by the baseline ratio:
\[
m_{itN}(\delta)-\exp(v_{itN}'\theta_N^{\text{lift}})
=
\frac{b_{iN}}{\bar b_{G_iN}}
\left\{m_{G_it,N}(\delta)-\exp(r_{G_it}'\theta_N^c)\right\}.
\]
% lean: unitResidual_liftCollapsedParameter

\textit{Step 3: unit scores at the lift are collapsed scores.} For every \(g\in\mathcal C\), the definition of \(\bar b_{gN}\) gives
\[
\sum_{i\in I_{gN}}\frac{b_{iN}}{\bar b_{gN}}=n_{gN}.
\]
% lean: sum_baselineRatio_within_cohort
Given a unit direction \(d\in\mathbb R^{N+T}\), define the collapsed direction \(d^{c}\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal C|+T}\) by its levels
\[
c_g(d^{c})=n_{gN}^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_{gN}}\frac{b_{iN}}{\bar b_{gN}}\,\alpha_i(d),
\qquad
w_t(d^{c})=w_t(d),
\qquad
\beta(d^{c})=\beta(d).
\]
% lean: aggregateUnitDirection, collapsedIndex_aggregateUnitDirection
Then
\[
(NT)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{t=1}^T
(v_{itN}'d)\left\{m_{itN}(\delta)-\exp(v_{itN}'\theta_N^{\text{lift}})\right\}
=
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gtN}\,(r_{gt}'d^{c})\left\{m_{gtN}(\delta)-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta_N^c)\right\}.
\]
Indeed, substituting the residual identity of Step 2 and grouping units by cohort (legitimate because every \(G_i\) lies in \(\mathcal C\)), the inner sum over \(i\in I_{gN}\) at fixed \((g,t)\) is
\[
(NT)^{-1}\left\{m_{gtN}(\delta)-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta_N^c)\right\}
\sum_{i\in I_{gN}}\frac{b_{iN}}{\bar b_{gN}}
\left\{\alpha_i(d)+w_t(d)+\beta(d)D_{gt}\right\},
\]
and the displayed baseline-ratio identity turns that bracketed sum into \(n_{gN}\{c_g(d^{c})+w_t(d^{c})+\beta(d^{c})D_{gt}\}=n_{gN}\,r_{gt}'d^{c}\), while \(n_{gN}/(NT)=q_{gtN}\).
% lean: unitScore_eq_collapsedScore, sum_baselineRatio_fiberwise, sum_units_by_supported_cohort
Applying the vanishing collapsed score of Step 1 to \(a=d^{c}\), we conclude that every unit directional score at \(\theta_N^{\text{lift}}\) vanishes:
\[
(NT)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{t=1}^T
(v_{itN}'d)\left\{m_{itN}(\delta)-\exp(v_{itN}'\theta_N^{\text{lift}})\right\}=0
\qquad\text{for every }d .
\]

\textit{Step 4: the lift maximizes the unit criterion.} For a Poisson criterion with nonnegative weights, vanishing of all directional scores is sufficient for global maximality:
\[
L_N(\theta_N;\delta)\le L_N(\theta_N^{\text{lift}};\delta)
\qquad\text{for every }\theta_N\in\mathbb R^{N+T},
\]
because the tangent-line bound \(\exp(\eta')\ge\exp(\eta)\{1+(\eta'-\eta)\}\) makes each cell increment at most its linearization, and the sum of the linearizations is the score in the direction \(\theta_N-\theta_N^{\text{lift}}\), which is zero by Step 3.
% lean: Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_isMax_of_score

\textit{Step 5: the unit design is injective.} Suppose \(v_{itN}'d=0\) for every unit \(i\) and every period \(t\). For each \(g\in\mathcal C\) choose a representative unit \(j_g\in I_{gN}\); this is possible because \(n_{gN}>0\). Define a collapsed parameter \(a\) by \(c_g(a)=\alpha_{j_g}(d)\), \(w_t(a)=w_t(d)\), \(\beta(a)=\beta(d)\). Then for every supported cell,
\[
r_{gt}'a
=
\alpha_{j_g}(d)+w_t(d)+\beta(d)D_{gt}
=
v_{j_gtN}'d
=
0,
\]
using \(G_{j_g}=g\). Hence \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_t q_{gt}(r_{gt}'a)^2=0\), so \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank} gives \(a=0\); in particular \(\beta(d)=0\) and \(w_t(d)=0\) for every \(t\). Evaluating \(0=v_{i1N}'d=\alpha_i(d)+w_1(d)+\beta(d)D_{i1}\) in the first period and substituting \(w_1(d)=0\) and \(\beta(d)=0\) gives \(\alpha_i(d)=0\) for every unit \(i\). All levels of \(d\) vanish, so \(d=0\), and the unit design \(\theta_N\mapsto(v_{itN}'\theta_N)_{i,t}\) is injective.
% lean: unitDesignMap_injective

\textit{Step 6: unique unit maximizer and equality of treatment coordinates.} The unit criterion \(L_N(\cdot;\delta)\) is a finite Poisson criterion with the constant positive weights \((NT)^{-1}\), positive means \(m_{itN}(\delta)\), and, by Step 5, an injective design; exactly as in Step 1 it therefore has a unique global maximizer. By Step 4 that maximizer is \(\theta_N^{\text{lift}}\). Hence \(L_N(\cdot;\delta)\) and \(L_{Nc}(\cdot;\delta)\) each have a unique global maximizer, and
\[
\beta_N^\star(\delta)=\beta(\theta_N^{\text{lift}})=\beta(\theta_N^c),
\]
i.e. \(\beta_N^\star(\delta)\) is the treatment coordinate of the unique maximizer of \(L_{Nc}(\theta;\delta)\).
% lean: finite_unit_and_collapsed_unique_beta, Causalean.Stat.uniqueGlobalMax_maximizerOrZero

\textit{Step 7: convergence to the limiting collapsed projection.} Fix a supported cell \((g,t)\). By \cref{obj:ass:cohort-share-limit},
\[
q_{gtN}=\frac{n_{gN}/N}{T}\longrightarrow \frac{\pi_g}{T}=q_{gt},
\]
and by \cref{obj:ass:within-cohort-baseline-limit}, since \(\exp(\gamma_{t0})\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})\) does not depend on \(N\),
\[
m_{gtN}(\delta)=\bar b_{gN}\exp(\gamma_{t0})\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})
\longrightarrow
\bar b_g\exp(\gamma_{t0})\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})=m_{gt}(\delta).
\]
Write the finite set of supported cells as \(z=(g,t)\), put \(q_z=q_{gt}\), \(m_z=m_{gt}(\delta)\), and write \(\eta_z(\theta)=r_{gt}'\theta\) for the supported-cell linear predictor. The preceding convergence is cellwise convergence of \((q_{zN},m_{zN})\) to the strictly positive array \((q_z,m_z)\), and the map \(\theta\mapsto(\eta_z(\theta))_z\) is injective by Step 1. Define
\[
s_z=\frac{q_z}{2}\min\!\left\{\frac{m_z}{2},1\right\},
\qquad
(B\theta)_z=s_z\eta_z(\theta) .
\]
Since every \(s_z\) is positive, \(B\) is injective. Finite-dimensional norm equivalence therefore gives a constant \(\kappa>0\) such that
\[
\kappa\|\theta\|\le \|B\theta\|
\qquad\text{for every }\theta .
\]
For all sufficiently large \(N\), uniformly over supported cells,
\[
\frac{q_z}{2}<q_{zN}<2q_z,
\qquad
\frac{m_z}{2}<m_{zN}<2m_z .
\]
Set
\[
U_0=\sum_z 2q_z\frac{(2m_z+1)^2}{2},
\qquad
Q_0=\sum_z 2q_z,
\qquad
R_0=\frac{U_0+Q_0}{\kappa}.
\]
The one-cell bound \(m\eta-\exp(\eta)\le (m+1)^2/2-\min(m,1)|\eta|\), combined with the displayed eventual coefficient bounds, gives for every \(\theta\) and all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
L_{Nc}(\theta;\delta)\le U_0-\|B\theta\|.
\]
The same coefficient bounds give \(-Q_0\le L_{Nc}(0;\delta)\). Since \(\theta_N^c\) maximizes \(L_{Nc}(\cdot;\delta)\),
\[
-Q_0\le L_{Nc}(0;\delta)
\le L_{Nc}(\theta_N^c;\delta)
\le U_0-\|B\theta_N^c\|,
\]
and therefore \(\|\theta_N^c\|\le R_0\) eventually. Thus the finite maximizers are eventually contained in the compact ball \(K=\{\theta:\|\theta\|\le R_0\}\).

Because there are finitely many supported cells, the cellwise convergence of \((q_{zN},m_{zN})\) also gives uniform convergence \(L_{Nc}(\cdot;\delta)\to L(\cdot;\delta)\) on this compact set, and \(L\) is continuous there. By \cref{obj:lem:pseudo-true-ppml-projection}, the limiting criterion \(L(\cdot;\delta)\) has the unique global maximizer \(\theta^\star(\delta)\), hence the unique maximizer on \(K\cup\{\theta^\star(\delta)\}\). The compact-containment bound, compact-uniform convergence, eventual global maximality of \(\theta_N^c\), and this uniqueness yield
\[
\theta_N^c\longrightarrow\theta^\star(\delta).
\]
% lean: selectedFiniteCollapsed_tendsto, Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_eventually_common_compact, Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_argmax_tendsto
Taking the treatment coordinate, which is a continuous function of the parameter, gives \(\beta(\theta_N^c)\to\beta^\star(\delta)\); since \(\beta_N^\star(\delta)=\beta(\theta_N^c)\) for every \(N\ge|\mathcal C|\) by Step 6,
\[
\beta_N^\star(\delta)\longrightarrow\beta^\star(\delta).
\]

\textit{Step 8: invariance to within-cohort baseline rearrangement.} Fix \(N\ge|\mathcal C|\) and a positive baseline array \(b'_{iN}\) with \(\bar b'_{gN}=\bar b_{gN}\) for every \(g\in\mathcal C\). The finite collapsed observed mean depends on the baselines only through the within-cohort average,
\[
m_{gtN}(\delta;b')=\bar b'_{gN}\exp(\gamma_{t0})\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})
=\bar b_{gN}\exp(\gamma_{t0})\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})=m_{gtN}(\delta;b),
\]
and the masses \(q_{gtN}\) do not involve the baselines at all. Hence \(L_{Nc}(\cdot;\delta;b')=L_{Nc}(\cdot;\delta;b)\) as functions of \(\theta\), so their unique maximizers coincide. Applying Step 6 once with \(b'\) and once with \(b\),
\[
\beta_N^\star(\delta;b')=\beta(\theta_N^c)=\beta_N^\star(\delta;b).
\]
% lean: unit_fe_collapse
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:sharp-ppml-forbidden-sign}]
Write \(W_{gt}=\widetilde W_{gt}(\delta)\), \(\mu^\star_{gt}=\mu^\star_{gt}(\delta)\), and
\[
\mathcal E=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}W_{gt}^2 .
\]
A collapsed parameter is written \(\vartheta=(\eta,\beta)\) with \(r_{gt}'\vartheta=\sum_jX_{gt,j}\eta_j+D_{gt}\beta\), and \(\rho^\star(\delta)\) is the weighted nuisance coefficient of \cref{obj:def:weighted-fwl-residual}, so that
\[
W_{gt}=D_{gt}-\sum_jX_{gt,j}\rho^\star_j(\delta).
\]

\textit{Step 1: \(\mathcal E>0\).} Each summand is nonnegative, since \(q_{gt}>0\), \(\mu^\star_{gt}=\exp\{r_{gt}'\theta^\star(\delta)\}>0\), and \(W_{gt}^2\ge0\). If \(\mathcal E=0\), every summand vanishes, and positivity of \(q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}\) forces \(W_{gt}=0\) at every supported cell. Consider the collapsed parameter
\[
\bar\vartheta=(-\rho^\star(\delta),1),
\]
whose treatment coordinate is one, so \(\bar\vartheta\neq0\). Its collapsed index is exactly the residual,
\[
r_{gt}'\bar\vartheta=D_{gt}-\sum_jX_{gt,j}\rho^\star_j(\delta)=W_{gt}=0
\qquad\text{at every supported cell,}
\]
whence \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}(r_{gt}'\bar\vartheta)^2=0\), contradicting \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank}. Thus \(\mathcal E>0\).
% lean: fwlEnergy_pos_of_collapsedDesignRank, weightedFWLResidual_eq_rhoStar

\textit{Step 2: the perturbed criterion.} For \(x\in\mathbb R\) let \(\delta^x\) be \(\delta\) with the coordinate \(\delta_{ks}\) replaced by \(x\), and write \(x_0=\delta_{ks}\). Because \(D_{ks}=1\) and \(m_{gt}(\delta)=B_{gt}\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})\), the perturbation moves exactly one collapsed mean:
\[
m_{ks}(\delta^x)=B_{ks}\exp(x),
\qquad
m_{gt}(\delta^x)=m_{gt}(\delta)\quad\text{for }(g,t)\neq(k,s),
\]
Setting
\[
p_{gt}=
\begin{cases}
B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks}), & (g,t)=(k,s),\\
0, & (g,t)\neq(k,s),
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{we have}\qquad
\frac{d}{dx}\,m_{gt}(\delta^x)\Big|_{x=x_0}=p_{gt}.
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.expCellUpdatedMean, betaStar_update_hasDerivAt

\textit{Step 3: the score system and its solution branch.} For a direction \(d\) put
\[
S_d(x,\theta)
=
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}(r_{gt}'d)\left\{m_{gt}(\delta^x)-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta)\right\}.
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:pseudo-true-ppml-projection} applied at the effect array \(\delta^x\), the criterion \(L(\cdot;\delta^x)\) has a unique global maximizer, and the nuisance and treatment score equations imply by linearity that \(S_d(x,\theta)=0\) for every direction \(d\) at that maximizer. Conversely, vanishing of every directional score is sufficient for global maximality of a Poisson criterion with nonnegative weights, by the tangent-line bound \(\exp(\eta')\ge\exp(\eta)\{1+(\eta'-\eta)\}\).
% lean: Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_score, Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_isMax_of_score
The system is smooth in \((x,\theta)\), it is solved at \((x_0,\theta^\star(\delta))\), and its derivative in \(\theta\) at that point is the bilinear form
\[
d,e\ \mapsto\ -\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\,\mu^\star_{gt}\,(r_{gt}'d)(r_{gt}'e),
\]
which is negative definite: if \(e\neq0\), the injectivity of \(\theta\mapsto(r_{gt}'\theta)_{(g,t)}\), obtained from \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank} as in \cref{obj:lem:pseudo-true-ppml-projection}, gives a supported cell with \(r_{gt}'e\neq0\). Hence the derivative is invertible. The implicit function theorem therefore supplies a continuously differentiable \(x\mapsto\theta(x)\) defined near \(x_0\), with \(\theta(x_0)=\theta^\star(\delta)\) and \(S_d(x,\theta(x))=0\) for every \(d\); by the characterization above and uniqueness of the maximizer, \(\theta(x)=\theta^\star(\delta^x)\) for \(x\) near \(x_0\). In particular \(x\mapsto\beta^\star(\delta^x)\) is differentiable at \(x_0\), with derivative the treatment coordinate \(v_\beta\) of \(v=\theta'(x_0)\).
% lean: Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_expCell_argmax_snd_hasDerivAt
Differentiating \(S_d(x,\theta(x))=0\) at \(x=x_0\) and using the one-cell derivative display above gives the linearized score: for every direction \(d\),
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gt}(r_{gt}'d)
\left\{
p_{gt}-\mu^\star_{gt}\,(r_{gt}'v)
\right\}=0 .
\]

\textit{Step 4: solving the linearized score by weighted FWL.} Put
\[
K=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}>0,
\qquad
\psi_{gt}=\frac{q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}}{K},
\qquad
\langle f,h\rangle=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T\psi_{gt}f_{gt}h_{gt},
\]
and define the mean-normalized one-cell source and the nuisance part of \(v=(v_\eta,v_\beta)\) by
\[
u_{gt}=\frac{p_{gt}}{\mu^\star_{gt}},
\qquad
\alpha_{gt}=\sum_jX_{gt,j}v_{\eta,j},
\]
so that \(r_{gt}'v=\alpha_{gt}+v_\beta D_{gt}\) and \(\alpha\) lies in the span of the nuisance columns \(X_{\cdot,j}\). Because \(\mu^\star_{gt}>0\) and \(K\psi_{gt}=q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}\), each summand of the linearized score factors as
\[
q_{gt}(r_{gt}'d)\left\{p_{gt}-\mu^\star_{gt}\,(r_{gt}'v)\right\}
=
K\,\psi_{gt}\,(r_{gt}'d)\left\{u_{gt}-v_\beta D_{gt}-\alpha_{gt}\right\},
\]
so dividing the whole linearized score by \(K>0\) rewrites it as
\[
\langle u-v_\beta D-\alpha,\ (r_{gt}'d)_{g,t}\rangle=0
\qquad\text{for every direction }d .
\]
Taking \(d\) to be the pure treatment direction gives \(\langle u-v_\beta D-\alpha,\ D\rangle=0\); taking \(d=(\rho,0)\) for arbitrary \(\rho\) gives \(\langle u-v_\beta D-\alpha,\ h\rangle=0\) for every \(h\) in the nuisance span.
% lean: linearizedScore_snd_eq_weightedFWL
The coefficient \(\rho^\star(\delta)\) in \cref{obj:def:weighted-fwl-residual} minimizes a finite weighted least-squares projection of \(D\) on the nuisance span. Its normal equations give
\[
\langle W,h\rangle=0\quad\text{for every }h\text{ in the nuisance span},
\qquad
D-W\text{ lies in the nuisance span}.
\]
Consequently \(\langle W,D\rangle=\langle W,W\rangle\), and, since \(\alpha\) also lies in the nuisance span, \(\langle W,\alpha\rangle=0\). Combining the score normal equations with \(D-W\) in the nuisance span gives
\[
0=\langle u-v_\beta D-\alpha,\ D\rangle
 -\langle u-v_\beta D-\alpha,\ D-W\rangle
=\langle u-v_\beta D-\alpha,\ W\rangle.
\]
Expanding this last display and using the projection orthogonality identities yields
\[
0=\langle W,\ u-v_\beta D-\alpha\rangle
=\langle W,u\rangle-v_\beta\langle W,W\rangle .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Panel.Weighted.WeightedSupport.scalar_fwl_of_normalEqs
Now \(\langle W,W\rangle=\mathcal E/K>0\) by the positivity of \(\mathcal E\) shown above, and, since \(p\) is supported on the single cell \((k,s)\),
\[
\langle W,u\rangle
=
\frac{1}{K}\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\mu^\star_{gt}W_{gt}\frac{p_{gt}}{\mu^\star_{gt}}
=
\frac{q_{ks}B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks})W_{ks}}{K}.
\]
Dividing,
\[
v_\beta=\frac{\langle W,u\rangle}{\langle W,W\rangle}
=\frac{q_{ks}B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks})W_{ks}}{\mathcal E}.
\]

\textit{Step 5: the derivative formula.} Combining the differentiability branch with the FWL calculation,
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\,\beta^\star\!\bigl(\delta\text{ with }\delta_{ks}\text{ replaced by }x\bigr)\Big|_{x=\delta_{ks}}
=
v_\beta
=
\frac{q_{ks}B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks})\widetilde W_{ks}(\delta)}{\mathcal E(\delta)} ,
\]
which is the asserted differentiability at \(x=\delta_{ks}\) together with the displayed derivative.
% lean: betaStar_update_hasDerivAt, sharp_ppml_forbidden_sign

\textit{Step 6: the sign equivalences.} The derivative is \(cW_{ks}\) with
\[
c=\frac{q_{ks}B_{ks}\exp(\delta_{ks})}{\mathcal E}>0,
\]
because \(q_{ks}>0\), \(B_{ks}=\bar b_k\exp(\gamma_{s0})>0\), \(\exp(\delta_{ks})>0\), and \(\mathcal E>0\) as shown above. Multiplication by a positive scalar preserves and reflects each of the three order relations, so
\[
cW_{ks}<0\iff W_{ks}<0,
\qquad
cW_{ks}=0\iff W_{ks}=0,
\qquad
0<cW_{ks}\iff 0<W_{ks},
\]
which, with \(W_{ks}=\widetilde W_{ks}(\delta)\), are exactly the three asserted equivalences.
% lean: sharp_ppml_forbidden_sign
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:homogeneous-effect-reduction}]
For a collapsed parameter \(\theta\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal C|+T}\) and a supported cell \((g,t)\), write the collapsed linear index as
\[
\eta_{gt}(\theta)=r_{gt}'\theta,
\]
so that, by \cref{obj:def:collapsed-population-projection},
\[
L(\theta;\delta)
=
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gt}\left\{m_{gt}(\delta)\,\eta_{gt}(\theta)-\exp\!\left(\eta_{gt}(\theta)\right)\right\},
\qquad
\mu^\star_{gt}(\delta)=\exp\!\left\{\eta_{gt}(\theta^\star(\delta))\right\}.
\]
The map \(\theta\mapsto\eta_{gt}(\theta)\) is linear, so \(\eta_{gt}(\theta)-\eta_{gt}(\theta')=\eta_{gt}(\theta-\theta')\) for all \(\theta,\theta'\).

\textit{Step 1: the candidate parameter.} Let \(\infty\) be the never-treated reference cohort and define \(\theta^0=(a^0,\delta_0)\) coordinatewise by
\[
a^0_{\text{int}}=\log\bar b_\infty,
\qquad
a^0_{\text{coh},c}=\log\bar b_c-\log\bar b_\infty\quad(c\in\mathcal C,\ c\neq\infty),
\qquad
a^0_{\text{time},u}=\gamma_{u0}\quad(u\neq1),
\]
with treatment coordinate \(\delta_0\).
% lean: homogeneous_effect_reduction
\Cref{obj:ass:unit-untreated-exponential-mean} carries the normalization \(\gamma_{10}=0\). Hence, for every period \(t\),
\[
\begin{cases}
0, & t=1,\\
a^0_{\text{time},t}, & t\neq1,
\end{cases}
\quad=\quad\gamma_{t0},
\]
since for \(t=1\) all period dummies are switched off and \(\gamma_{10}=0\), while for \(t\neq1\) exactly the dummy of period \(t\) contributes. Likewise, for every \(g\in\mathcal C\),
\[
\begin{cases}
0, & g=\infty,\\
a^0_{\text{coh},g}, & g\neq\infty,
\end{cases}
\quad=\quad
\begin{cases}
0, & g=\infty,\\
\log\bar b_g-\log\bar b_\infty, & g\neq\infty,
\end{cases}
\]
because exactly the dummy of cohort \(g\) contributes when \(g\neq\infty\), and none when \(g=\infty\).

\textit{Step 2: the index at \(\theta^0\).} Adding the intercept, cohort, period, and treatment contributions in the two cases \(g=\infty\) and \(g\neq\infty\) gives, for every \(g\in\mathcal C\) and every period \(t\),
\[
\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)=\log\bar b_g+\gamma_{t0}+D_{gt}\delta_0 .
\]
% lean: homogeneous_effect_reduction

\textit{Step 3: the homogeneous-effect hypothesis in product form.} For every \(g\in\mathcal C\) and every \(t\),
\[
D_{gt}\delta_{gt}=D_{gt}\delta_0 :
\]
if \(D_{gt}=1\) the common treated-cell hypothesis gives \(\delta_{gt}=\delta_0\), and if \(D_{gt}=0\) both sides vanish.

\textit{Step 4: the criterion is exactly specified at \(\theta^0\).} Using \(B_{gt}=\bar b_g\exp(\gamma_{t0})\) together with Steps 2 and 3, the observed collapsed mean satisfies
\[
m_{gt}(\delta)
=
B_{gt}\exp(D_{gt}\delta_{gt})
=
\exp\{\log\bar b_g+\gamma_{t0}+D_{gt}\delta_0\}
=
\exp\{\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)\}.
\]
In particular \(m_{gt}(\delta)>0\) and
\[
\log m_{gt}(\delta)=\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)
\qquad\text{for every supported cell.}
\]

\textit{Step 5: the scalar Poisson cell inequality.} For every \(m>0\) and every real \(\eta\),
\[
m\eta-\exp(\eta)\le m\log m-\exp(\log m),
\]
with strict inequality when \(\eta\neq\log m\). Apply \(1+x\le\exp(x)\), strict for \(x\neq0\), at \(x=\eta-\log m\), multiply by \(m>0\), and rearrange using \(\exp(\eta)=m\exp(\eta-\log m)\).
% lean: homogeneous_effect_reduction

\textit{Step 6: the cell masses are strictly positive.} First \(T>0\): if \(T=0\), then for the nonzero collapsed parameter with zero nuisance block and treatment coordinate one the weighted sum \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}(r_{gt}'a)^2\) is an empty sum, hence zero, contradicting \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank}. Since \(\pi_g\in(0,1)\), it follows that
\[
q_{gt}=\frac{\pi_g}{T}>0
\qquad\text{for every supported cell.}
\]

\textit{Step 7: \(\theta^0\) is a global maximizer.} Fix any \(\theta\). Applying Step 5 cell by cell with \(m=m_{gt}(\delta)>0\) and \(\eta=\eta_{gt}(\theta)\), and using \(\log m_{gt}(\delta)=\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)\) from Step 4,
\[
m_{gt}(\delta)\eta_{gt}(\theta)-\exp\!\left(\eta_{gt}(\theta)\right)
\le
m_{gt}(\delta)\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)-\exp\!\left(\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)\right).
\]
Multiplying by \(q_{gt}>0\) and summing over supported cells,
\[
L(\theta;\delta)\le L(\theta^0;\delta).
\]

\textit{Step 8: the maximizer is unique.} Suppose \(L(\theta;\delta)=L(\theta^0;\delta)\) and \(\theta\neq\theta^0\). Then \(\theta-\theta^0\neq0\), so \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank} gives
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T q_{gt}\left(\eta_{gt}(\theta-\theta^0)\right)^2>0 ,
\]
and by linearity \(\eta_{gt}(\theta-\theta^0)=\eta_{gt}(\theta)-\eta_{gt}(\theta^0)\), so some supported cell \((g_0,t_0)\) has
\[
\eta_{g_0t_0}(\theta)\neq\eta_{g_0t_0}(\theta^0)=\log m_{g_0t_0}(\delta).
\]
At that cell the strict half of Step 5 applies, and all other cells obey the weak inequality of Step 7. Since \(q_{g_0t_0}>0\), summing gives
\[
L(\theta;\delta)<L(\theta^0;\delta),
\]
contradicting the assumed equality. Hence \(\theta=\theta^0\).

\textit{Step 9: identification of the projection.} By Steps 7 and 8 the criterion \(L(\cdot;\delta)\) has \(\theta^0\) as its unique global maximizer, and \cref{obj:def:collapsed-population-projection} selects \(\theta^\star(\delta)\) among the global maximizers, so
\[
\theta^\star(\delta)=\theta^0 .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.maximizerOrZero, homogeneous_effect_reduction

\textit{Step 10: the two conclusions.} The treatment coordinate of \(\theta^0\) is \(\delta_0\), so
\[
\beta^\star(\delta)=\delta_0 ,
\]
and for every \(g\in\mathcal C\) and every period \(t\), by Step 2,
\[
\mu^\star_{gt}(\delta)
=
\exp\{\eta_{gt}(\theta^\star(\delta))\}
=
\exp\{\log\bar b_g+\gamma_{t0}+D_{gt}\delta_0\}
=
B_{gt}\exp(D_{gt}\delta_0).
\]
% lean: homogeneous_effect_reduction
\end{proof}

Also, summing \(h_{gt}\) in the two possible orders gives
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T C_t=M=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}R_g .
\]
% lean: frontierPrimitiveColumn_sum

\textit{Step 2: the zero-treatment row-column fit.} Define the collapsed parameter \(\theta^0=(a^0,0)\), whose treatment coordinate is zero and whose nuisance levels are
\[
a^0_{\mathrm{int}}=\log\frac{R_\infty}{\pi_\infty}+\log C_1-\log M,
\qquad
a^0_{\mathrm{coh},g}=\log\frac{R_g}{\pi_g}-\log\frac{R_\infty}{\pi_\infty}\quad(g\neq\infty),
\]
\[
a^0_{\mathrm{time},t}=\log C_t-\log C_1\quad(t\neq1).
\]
Adding the intercept, cohort, and period contributions, with the cohort dummy absent for \(g=\infty\) and the period dummy absent for \(t=1\), gives, for every supported cell,
\[
r_{gt}'\theta^0=\log\frac{R_g}{\pi_g}+\log C_t-\log M,
\qquad
\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)=\frac{R_gC_t}{\pi_g M},
\]
where the logarithms and division use the positivity in Step 1.
% lean: frontierNuisanceParameter, frontierNuisanceParameter_index, frontierNuisanceParameter_exp

\textit{Step 3: the conditional residuals and their margins.} Define the mass-weighted residual of \(\theta^0\) in cell \((g,t)\) by
\[
e_{gt}
=q_{gt}\{m_{gt}-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\}
=\frac{\pi_g}{T}\left\{m_{gt}-\frac{R_gC_t}{\pi_gM}\right\}
=\frac{1}{T}\left\{h_{gt}-\frac{R_gC_t}{M}\right\}.
\]
% lean: frontierConditionalResidual, frontierConditionalResidual_eq
Using \(\sum_tC_t=M\) and \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}R_g=M\) from Step 1, its row and column sums vanish:
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T e_{gt}=\frac{1}{T}\left\{R_g-\frac{R_g}{M}\sum_{t=1}^TC_t\right\}=0
\qquad(g\in\mathcal C),
\]
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C} e_{gt}=\frac{1}{T}\left\{C_t-\frac{C_t}{M}\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}R_g\right\}=0
\qquad(t=1,\ldots,T).
\]
% lean: frontierConditionalResidual_row_sum, frontierConditionalResidual_column_sum

\textit{Step 4: the scores at \(\theta^0\).} Every collapsed nuisance regressor \(X_{\cdot,j}\) is either the constant \(1\), the indicator of one cohort \(g_0\neq\infty\), or the indicator of one period \(t_0\neq1\). The corresponding nuisance score at \(\theta^0\) is therefore \(\sum_{g,t}e_{gt}\), \(\sum_te_{g_0t}\), or \(\sum_ge_{gt_0}\), and these vanish by Step 3:
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gt}X_{gt,j}\{m_{gt}-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\}=0
\qquad\text{for every nuisance coordinate }j .
\]
Because the nuisance block is finite-dimensional and \(X_{gt}'u\) is the corresponding linear combination of the coordinate regressors, the same conclusion holds for every nuisance direction \(u\):
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gt}(X_{gt}'u)\{m_{gt}-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\}
=
\sum_j u_j
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gt}X_{gt,j}\{m_{gt}-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\}
=0 .
\]
% lean: frontierConditionalNuisanceScore
The treatment score at \(\theta^0\) is
\[
S
=
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T
q_{gt}D_{gt}\{m_{gt}-\exp(r_{gt}'\theta^0)\}
=
\frac{1}{T}\left\{A-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\sum_{t=1}^T D_{gt}R_gC_t\right\}
=
\frac{\Phi}{TM},
\]
and \(TM>0\) by Step 1.
% lean: frontierConditionalScalarScore

\textit{Step 5: the pseudo-true coefficient has the sign of \(S\).} The finite-dimensional Poisson sign comparison used here is the following. For a finite index set with strictly positive weights \(q_i\), strictly positive means \(m_i\), and an injective linear design map \(\mathscr A\), suppose that a zero-treatment point \((u^0,0)\) clears every nuisance-direction score,
\[
\sum_i q_i \mathscr A(u,0)_i\{m_i-\exp(\mathscr A(u^0,0)_i)\}=0
\qquad\text{for every nuisance direction }u.
\]
If
\[
s_0=\sum_i q_i \mathscr A(0,1)_i\{m_i-\exp(\mathscr A(u^0,0)_i)\}
\]
and \(\beta\) is the treatment coordinate of the unique maximizer of
\(\sum_i q_i\{m_i(\mathscr A\theta)_i-\exp((\mathscr A\theta)_i)\}\), then
\[
\beta<0\iff s_0<0,
\qquad
\beta=0\iff s_0=0,
\qquad
0<\beta\iff 0<s_0 .
\]
Indeed, the score at a maximizer vanishes in every direction. Writing \(\widehat\theta\) for the maximizer, \(\theta^0=(u^0,0)\), and \(v=\widehat\theta-\theta^0\), the score identities and the nuisance-direction condition give
\[
\widehat\beta s_0
=
\sum_i q_i(\mathscr A v)_i\{\exp((\mathscr A\widehat\theta)_i)-\exp((\mathscr A\theta^0)_i)\} .
\]
The summands on the right are nonnegative, and at least one is strictly positive when \(v\neq0\), by injectivity of \(\mathscr A\) and strict monotonicity of the exponential. If \(s_0=0\), all scores at \(\theta^0\) vanish, and the tangent-line inequality for the exponential makes \(\theta^0\) globally maximizing; uniqueness gives the zero treatment coordinate.

Apply this comparison with index set \(\mathcal C\times\{1,\ldots,T\}\), design \(\mathscr A\theta=(r_{gt}'\theta)_{g,t}\), and \((u^0,0)=\theta^0\). The weights and means are positive by Step 1, Step 4 supplies the nuisance-direction score condition, and \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank} gives injectivity: if two collapsed parameters have the same fitted-index array, their difference has weighted sum of squared indices equal to zero, so the rank condition forces the difference to be zero. Therefore the treatment coordinate \(\beta^\star(\delta)\) of \(\theta^\star(\delta)\) has the same three-way sign as \(S\). Since \(S=\Phi/(TM)\) with \(TM>0\),
\[
\beta^\star(\delta)<0\iff \Phi<0,
\qquad
\beta^\star(\delta)=0\iff \Phi=0,
\qquad
0<\beta^\star(\delta)\iff 0<\Phi .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_snd_sign_of_nuisance_score, Causalean.Stat.finitePoissonObjective_isMax_of_score, collapsedDesignMap_injective, betaStar_sign_frontierEliminationHandle

\textit{Step 6: the observed baseline proxy reproduces the untreated mean.} For every supported cohort \(g\), the first-period treatment indicator is zero: if \(g=\infty\) this is the never-treated path, and if \(g\) is finite then \(D_{g1}=1\) would force the adoption date to be the first period, which the support excludes. Also \(D_{\infty t}=0\) for every \(t\), and \cref{obj:ass:unit-untreated-exponential-mean} gives \(\gamma_{10}=0\). Hence, for every \(g\in\mathcal C\) and every \(t\),
\[
B^{obs}_{gt}
=
\frac{m_{g1}m_{\infty t}}{m_{\infty 1}}
=
\frac{\bar b_ge^{\gamma_{10}}\cdot\bar b_\infty e^{\gamma_{t0}}}{\bar b_\infty e^{\gamma_{10}}}
=
\bar b_ge^{\gamma_{t0}}
=
B_{gt},
\]
where \(m_{\infty1}=\bar b_\infty>0\) justifies the cancellation.
% lean: primitive_global_frontier

\textit{Step 7: \(H\) is nonempty and \(Z>0\).} \Cref{obj:ass:multicohort-frontier-scope} supplies a supported finite cohort adopting in the second period; in that period its treatment indicator equals one, so \(H\) is nonempty. For every \((g,t)\in H\), \(q_{gt}>0\), and Step 6 gives \(B^{obs}_{gt}=B_{gt}>0\). Hence every summand of \(Z\) is nonnegative and at least one summand is strictly positive:
\[
Z=\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}>0 .
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier

\textit{Step 8: membership in \(\mathcal R_T\) is equivalent to \(\Phi<0\).} If the induced primitive tuple lies in \(\mathcal R_T\), then the last clause of \cref{obj:def:global-sign-reversal-region} gives negativity of its treatment coordinate. In the restricted primitive coordinates the limiting criterion is the same collapsed criterion \(L(\theta;\delta)\), so this coordinate equals \(\beta^\star(\delta)\), and Step 5 gives \(\Phi<0\).
% lean: primitiveLimitingCriterion_restrict, primitiveBetaStar_restrict
Conversely suppose \(\Phi<0\). The horizon, support, and frontier-scope clauses of \cref{obj:def:global-sign-reversal-region} are hypotheses of the present theorem. The simplex clause holds because the finite-array shares sum to one for every \(N\ge|\mathcal C|\),
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\frac{n_{gN}}{N}=1,
\]
every unit label lies in \(\mathcal C\), and \cref{obj:ass:cohort-share-limit} gives
\(\sum_{g\in\mathcal C}\pi_g=1\) after passage to the limit.
% lean: cohortShareLimit_sum_eq_one
The untreated-mean clause is witnessed by \(g\mapsto\bar b_g\), since the primitive untreated coordinate is \(B_{gt}=\bar b_g e^{\gamma_{t0}}\). The positive-effect clause is \cref{obj:ass:strict-positive-effects}. The rank clause is \cref{obj:ass:collapsed-design-rank}, after rewriting the sum over supported cells as the double sum over \(g\in\mathcal C\) and \(t\). Finally Step 5 gives \(\beta^\star(\delta)<0\), which is the tuple's treatment coordinate. Hence the tuple lies in \(\mathcal R_T\).

\textit{Step 9: the four-cohort polynomial.} Let \(x>1\), \(y>1\), and consider the four-period support \(\{2,3,4,\infty\}\) with \(T=4\), equal shares \(\pi_g=1/4\), unit limiting baselines, zero untreated time effects, multiplier \(x\) on every treated cell except \((2,4)\), and multiplier \(y\) at \((2,4)\). With treatment absorbing from the adoption date on, \(D_{gt}=1\) exactly when \(g\le t\) and \(D_{\infty t}=0\), so
\[
4\,(h_{gt})=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&x&x&y\\
1&1&x&x\\
1&1&1&x\\
1&1&1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with rows \(g=2,3,4,\infty\) and columns \(t=1,2,3,4\). Summing rows and columns,
\[
4R_2=1+2x+y,
\quad
4R_3=2+2x,
\quad
4R_4=3+x,
\quad
4R_\infty=4,
\]
\[
4C_1=4,
\quad
4C_2=x+3,
\quad
4C_3=2x+2,
\quad
4C_4=2x+y+1,
\]
\[
4M=10+5x+y,
\qquad
4A=5x+y .
\]
The treated cells of row \(g=2\) are \(t=2,3,4\), those of row \(g=3\) are \(t=3,4\), and the treated cell of row \(g=4\) is \(t=4\), so
\[
16\sum_{g,t}D_{gt}R_gC_t
=
(1+2x+y)(5x+y+6)+(2+2x)(4x+y+3)+(3+x)(2x+y+1),
\]
which expands to \(20x^2+10xy+y^2+38x+12y+15\), while
\[
16\,MA=(10+5x+y)(5x+y)=25x^2+10xy+y^2+50x+10y .
\]
Subtracting gives
\[
\Phi=MA-\sum_{g,t}D_{gt}R_gC_t=\frac{5x^2+12x-2y-15}{16}.
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier, fourMultiplierEffects, frontierEliminationHandle

\textit{Step 10: the numerical identity.} With \(5(101/100)^2=51005/10000\) and \(12(101/100)=1212/100\),
\[
\frac{5(101/100)^2+12(101/100)-15}{2}=\frac{4441}{4000}.
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier

\textit{Step 11: the value at the witness.} For \(W_4\) of \cref{obj:def:four-cohort-witness}, the treated multipliers are \(\exp(\log(101/100))=101/100\) off cell \((2,4)\) and \(\exp(\log4)=4\) at \((2,4)\), so Step 9 applies with \(x=101/100\) and \(y=4\):
\[
\Phi=\frac{5(101/100)^2+12(101/100)-2\cdot4-15}{16}=-\frac{11559}{32000}.
\]
% lean: fourCohortDelta_eq_multiplier, fourFrontierEliminationHandle_exact

\textit{Step 12: the counterfactual-share objects on treated cells.} Let \((g,t)\in H\), so \(D_{gt}=1\). Step 6 gives \(B^{obs}_{gt}=B_{gt}\), and \(B_{gt}>0\), so
\[
\tau_{gt}=\frac{m_{gt}}{B^{obs}_{gt}}-1
=\frac{B_{gt}\exp(\delta_{gt})}{B_{gt}}-1
=\exp(\delta_{gt})-1 .
\]
Moreover \(q_{gt}>0\), \(B^{obs}_{gt}>0\), and \(Z>0\) by Step 7, hence
\[
\omega_{gt}=\frac{q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}}{Z}>0 .
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier, granularTau, pttWeight

\textit{Step 13: the weights sum to one.} By the definition of \(Z\) and the positivity \(Z>0\),
\[
\sum_{(g,t)\in H}\omega_{gt}
=
\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}
=
\frac{Z}{Z}
=
1 .
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier, pttWeight, pttNormalizer

\textit{Step 14: the ratio form of \(PTT\).} Expanding the definition and using \(B^{obs}_{gt}>0\) on every treated cell,
\[
PTT
=
\sum_{(g,t)\in H}\frac{q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}}{Z}\left(\frac{m_{gt}}{B^{obs}_{gt}}-1\right)
=
\frac{\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}m_{gt}}{Z}
-
\frac{\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}}{Z}.
\]
The second fraction equals \(1\), so
\[
PTT=\frac{\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}m_{gt}}{\sum_{(g,t)\in H}q_{gt}B^{obs}_{gt}}-1 .
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier, counterfactualSharePTT

\textit{Step 15: the sign contrast.} Suppose \(\Phi<0\). Step 5 gives \(\beta^\star(\delta)<0\). For \(PTT\), each treated cell has \(\omega_{gt}>0\) by Step 12, and \cref{obj:ass:strict-positive-effects} gives \(\delta_{gt}>0\), hence \(\exp(\delta_{gt})>1\) and \(\tau_{gt}>0\). The sum defining \(PTT\) has nonnegative terms and, by Step 7, at least one strictly positive term. Therefore \(PTT>0\), and
\[
\Phi<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \beta^\star(\delta)<0<PTT .
\]
% lean: primitive_global_frontier
\end{proof}
