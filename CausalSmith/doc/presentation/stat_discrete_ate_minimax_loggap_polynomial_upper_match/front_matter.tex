\begin{abstract}
This paper characterizes the fixed-interior minimax mean-squared-error risk for average treatment effect estimation in a finite-alphabet observational experiment with unrestricted category probabilities in the calibrated range stated below. For each fixed overlap parameter \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and for alphabet sizes \(d\) in the calibrated range \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), the sharp risk is
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\asymp_\epsilon
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}.
\]
The upper bound is attained by a computable split-sample hybrid estimator with universal numerical tuning. Pilot-heavy categories are estimated by empirical within-category treatment-control ratios, while pilot-light categories are estimated by a Chebyshev reciprocal polynomial and unbiased multinomial factorial moments. The matching lower bound follows by transferring the large-alphabet lower construction of \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024} to a control-zero subclass on which the average treatment effect equals a treated-response functional. The characterization implies a parametric-risk region \(d=O(\sqrt n\log n)\) and, along the stated calibrated range, consistency exactly when \(d=o(n\log n)\). The same construction gives a deterministic upper envelope near the randomized endpoint, while a centered linear estimator has risk at most \(n^{-1}+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2\) for every \(n,d\); at \(\epsilon=1/2\), the minimax risk is bracketed between \(1/(100n)\) and \(1/n\), uniformly over positive alphabet sizes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Average treatment effect estimation under unconfoundedness is usually presented as a problem of adjustment: estimate treatment propensities and outcome regressions well enough, then combine them through inverse-probability, regression, doubly robust, or targeted estimators. In regular low-dimensional models, the semiparametric theory gives the familiar \(1/n\) mean-squared-error scale and identifies efficient first-order estimators \citep{Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003,RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,BangRobins2005,VanDerLaanRubin2006}. When the adjustment variable has many levels, however, the leading difficulty can come from the empirical scarcity of treatment and control observations inside many covariate categories. This paper studies that difficulty in a discrete benchmark where the covariate \(X\), the adjustment variable, takes \(d\) unrestricted categories, treatment and outcome are binary, and overlap is fixed away from zero and one. Large alphabets arise routinely in practice: adjustment by geographic units, provider or product identifiers, or interacted demographic cells produces many sparsely populated categories, and \(K\) binary covariates encode as a single categorical variable with \(d=2^K\) levels, so even a moderate number of binary adjustments places \(d\) far beyond \(\sqrt n\).

The target is the average treatment effect functional identified by the finite observed law under the usual consistency and conditional-exchangeability conditions. In category \(k\), let \(p_k\) be the category mass, \(\pi_k\) the treatment probability, and \(\mu_{ak}\) the conditional mean outcome in arm \(a\). Under fixed overlap, \(\epsilon\le \pi_k\le 1-\epsilon\) for every positive-mass category. The resulting estimand is
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^d p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}),
\]
equivalently the four-cell functional in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}. The minimax risk \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\), the worst-case mean-squared error over the overlap-restricted iid experiment, is defined in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.

The contributions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each fixed interior overlap level \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), the sharp minimax mean-squared-error rate \(n^{-1}+d^2/\{n^2(\log n)^2\}\) for the average treatment effect over the unrestricted finite-alphabet class, throughout the calibrated range \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\) (\cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}). The result identifies the sharp large-alphabet term \(d^2/\{n^2(\log n)^2\}\) in this fixed-interior calibrated range.
\item A computable split-sample ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator with universal numerical tuning that attains the upper bound: empirical ratios on pilot-certified heavy categories, and a Chebyshev reciprocal polynomial lifted by unbiased factorial moments on light categories (\cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}).
\item The resulting regime characterization inside the calibrated range: the parametric scale \(1/n\) throughout \(d=O(\sqrt n\log n)\), and consistency exactly when \(d=o(n\log n)\).
\item An endpoint analysis connecting the construction to randomized assignment: a deterministic risk envelope near \(\epsilon=1/2\) and a bracketing of the minimax risk at the parametric order at the endpoint itself, uniformly in \(d\) (\cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid}).
\end{enumerate}

The main result, \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}, establishes the sharp fixed-interior minimax rate. For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there are constants \(a_\epsilon,\rho_\epsilon,C_\epsilon>0\) and \(N_\epsilon<\infty\) such that, whenever \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), \(d>0\), and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\),
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
The same theorem identifies two immediate consequences inside this calibrated range. The parametric scale \(1/n\) holds throughout \(d=O(\sqrt n\log n)\). Along admissible sequences, consistency is equivalent to \(d/(n\log n)\to0\).

The upper bound is constructive. The estimator in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} splits the sample deterministically. The pilot split classifies categories as heavy or light. Heavy categories use direct empirical treatment-control ratios, where the pilot mass floor gives denominator control. Light categories use a Chebyshev-derived polynomial approximation to the reciprocal ratio structure, and the polynomial is lifted to the sample by factorial moments of the split-one multinomial counts. This produces a finite arithmetic estimator with fixed numerical tuning \(A_0=6\), \(\lambda_0=256\), and \(b_0=4096\); the tuning is shared across fixed interior overlap classes, while the constants in the risk bound depend on \(\epsilon\).

The logarithmic improvement arises from treating sparse categories as a nonsmooth functional-estimation problem. The cell contribution is a homogeneous ratio functional: it multiplies category mass by the difference of two conditional outcome means. In low-mass cells, direct ratios have unstable denominators. Polynomial approximation replaces the reciprocal behavior by a degree of order \(\log n\), and factorial moments estimate the resulting polynomial without plug-in bias. This is the same approximation-versus-variance mechanism that appears in large-alphabet functional estimation \citep{Paninski2003,JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016,HanJiaoWeissman2020}, here applied to the causal four-cell contribution and aggregated over unrestricted confounding categories.

The closest comparison is \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. Their discrete strong-overlap analysis provides the large-alphabet lower-bound input used below, with scale \(d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)+1/n\) in the corresponding range. The present paper attains that lower scale in the fixed-interior regime by constructing the ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator. The lower side of \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} uses their published control-zero lower-bound conclusion and transfers it to the ATE risk because, on that subclass, the ATE functional equals the treated-response functional.

The endpoint analysis in \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} records how the construction connects to randomized assignment. A centered linear estimator satisfies the uniform bound
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\]
for every \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\) and every positive \(n,d\). At \(\epsilon=1/2\), where treatment is randomized within every positive-mass category, the minimax risk lies between \(1/(100n)\) and \(1/n\). For fixed interior \(\epsilon\), the selected estimator in \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} compares the hybrid envelope with the centered endpoint envelope and attains
\[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},
(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right\}
\]
up to the displayed fixed-\(\epsilon\) constant in the same calibrated range.

The paper also includes a two-category witness, \cref{obj:prop:two-category-confounding}, to separate the adjusted causal target from the marginal treated-minus-control contrast. The construction satisfies consistency, conditional exchangeability, and fixed overlap; its ATE equals \(1/2\), while its naive observed contrast equals \(\epsilon\). This example fixes the causal interpretation before the minimax analysis turns to the many-category problem.

The formal statements and proofs are supported by a Lean 4 development, with the precise verification scope recorded in the verification appendix. The main text presents the assumptions, estimators, and risk statements in the usual mathematical form.
