\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}]
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Throughout, \(n\log n\ge0\) for every sample size, so an inequality of the form \(d\le\rho\,n\log n\) is preserved when \(\rho\) is replaced by any larger constant.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The upper half.}  We first prove the following claim.  There are constants \(C_U,\rho_U>0\) and an integer \(N_U<\infty\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that for every \(n,d\), every single-observation law \(P\) with \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), every \(n\ge N_U\) and every
\[
d\le \rho_U n\log n,
\]
one has
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_Q\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(Q)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_U\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
% lean: hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\emph{Constants.}  Since \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\), \cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial} applies; write \(C_L,c_L>0\) for the two constants it supplies.  Since \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate} applies; write \(C_H,\rho_H>0\) and \(N_H<\infty\) for the constants and cutoff it supplies.  Set
\[
C_U=2(C_L+C_H),
\qquad
\rho_U=\min\{c_L,\rho_H\},
\qquad
N_U=N_H .
\]
Then \(C_U>0\) and \(\rho_U>0\), and the hypothesis \(d\le\rho_U n\log n\) yields both growth gates,
\[
d\le \rho_U n\log n\le c_L n\log n,
\qquad
d\le \rho_U n\log n\le \rho_H n\log n .
\]
% lean: hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\emph{The truncation cannot increase squared error.}  For every real \(x\) and every \(t\in[-1,1]\),
\[
\left(\max\{-1,\min(1,x)\}-t\right)^2\le (x-t)^2 .
\]
Indeed, if \(x\le-1\) the left-hand entry is \(-1\), which lies between \(x\) and \(t\); if \(x>1\) it is \(1\), which again lies between \(x\) and \(t\); otherwise it is \(x\) and the two sides agree.
% lean: clamp_sq_error_le
Moreover, by \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} the estimand is \(\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^dp_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})\) with \(\sum_{k=1}^dp_k=1\) and \(\mu_{0k},\mu_{1k}\in[0,1]\), whence
\[
\tau(P)\in[-1,1].
\]
% lean: ateFunctional_eq_weighted_regression, ateFunctional_mem_interval
Applying the previous display with \(x=\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\) and \(t=\tau(P)\), and using the definition of \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, gives the sample-by-sample bound
\[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\le
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\tau(P)\right)^2 .
\]
% lean: hybrid_mse_le_component_errors

\emph{Splitting the target.}  The pilot-light set is the complement of the pilot-heavy set, \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\widehat{\mathcal H}_n^c\), so the two index sets are disjoint with union \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\) and \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} gives, sample by sample,
\[
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
+
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
=
\sum_{k=1}^d\phi(q_k)
=
\tau(P).
\]
% lean: targetHeavy_add_targetLight, lightCells_eq_compl
Substituting this identity and using \((u+v)^2\le2u^2+2v^2\), which is \(0\le(u-v)^2\), we obtain sample by sample
\begin{align*}
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\tau(P)\right)^2
&=
\left\{
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)
+
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)
\right\}^2\\
&\le
2\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2
+
2\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2 .
\end{align*}
% lean: hybrid_mse_le_component_errors

\emph{Integration and the two component bounds.}  The observation alphabet is finite, so every function of the sample appearing above is bounded and integrable, and integrating the previous two displays against \(P^{\otimes n}\) preserves the inequality and splits the right-hand side by linearity:
\[
E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
2E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2\right]
+
2E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2\right].
\]
% lean: hybrid_mse_le_component_errors
Nothing in this derivation, nor in the two growth gates displayed above, refers to the particular law: the gates constrain only \(n\) and \(d\), and the constants \(C_L,c_L,C_H,\rho_H,N_H\) depend only on \(\epsilon\).  Hence for \emph{every} \(Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) the heavy bound of \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate} (available because \(n\ge N_U=N_H\) and \(d\le\rho_H n\log n\)) and the light bound of \cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial} (available because \(d\le c_L n\log n\)) may be inserted, giving
\begin{align*}
E_Q\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(Q)\right)^2\right]
&\le
2C_H\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
+
2C_L\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}\\
&=
C_U\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\end{align*}
The experiment class is nonempty, since \(P^{\otimes n}\) belongs to it, so taking the supremum over \(Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) gives the second inequality of the claim.
% lean: hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\emph{The minimax risk does not exceed the hybrid worst case.}  The sample space \((\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)^n\) is finite, so \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) is a measurable estimator and is therefore one of the competitors in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.  For any measurable \(\widehat\tau\) and any \(Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), the bound \(\tau(Q)\in[-1,1]\) obtained above gives \(|\widehat\tau(x)-\tau(Q)|\le\sum_{x'}|\widehat\tau(x')|+1\) at every sample point \(x\), where \(x'\) ranges over the finite sample space, and hence
\[
E_Q\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau-\tau(Q)\right)^2\right]
\le
\left(\sum_{x'}|\widehat\tau(x')|+1\right)^2 ,
\]
so each estimator's worst-case risk is a finite nonnegative number and the infimum in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} is taken over a nonempty set of reals bounded below by \(0\).  Evaluating that infimum at \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) gives the first inequality of the claim, and the claim is proved.
% lean: mse_le_estimator_abs_sum_bound, hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{The lower half and the constants of the theorem.}  \Cref{obj:lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer}, which transfers the fixed-sample one-arm lower bound of \citep[Theorem 2; Appendix C.5; Lemma 4; Appendix D.2]{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}, supplies constants \(a>0\) and \(\rho_L>0\) and an integer cutoff \(N_L<\infty\) such that its displayed lower bound for \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) holds whenever \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_L\) and \(d\le\rho_L n\log n\).  Now define
\[
a_\epsilon=a,\qquad
\rho_\epsilon=\min\{\rho_U,\rho_L\},\qquad
C_\epsilon=C_U,\qquad
N_\epsilon=\max\{N_U,N_L\}.
\]
These constants are positive, and \(N_\epsilon<\infty\).
% lean: ate_lower_bound_transfer, sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{Sharp sandwich.}  Assume \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\).  Then \(n\ge N_U\) and \(n\ge N_L\), while
\[
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\le \rho_U n\log n,
\qquad
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\le \rho_L n\log n,
\]
so both growth gates are in force.  Step 2 and Step 1 therefore give
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_Q\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(Q)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\},
\]
which is the sharp sandwich.
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{Parametric window.}  Fix \(K>0\) and put
\[
C_K=C_U(1+K^2)>0 .
\]
Assume \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), \(d\le K\sqrt n\,\log n\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\).

Because \(K\sqrt n\ge0\) and \(d>0\), the inequality \(d\le K\sqrt n\,\log n\) forces \(\log n>0\); in particular \(n\ge2\), so \(n\) and \(n^2(\log n)^2\) are strictly positive.  Both sides of \(d\le K\sqrt n\,\log n\) are nonnegative, so squaring is legitimate and \((\sqrt n)^2=n\) gives
\[
d^2\le K^2n(\log n)^2 .
\]
Dividing by \(n^2(\log n)^2>0\),
\[
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\le\frac{K^2}{n}.
\]
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

For the lower bound, \(d^2/\{n^2(\log n)^2\}\ge0\) and the retained gate \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\le\rho_L n\log n\) with \(d>0\) and \(n\ge N_L\) let us invoke Step 2:
\[
\frac{a_\epsilon}{n}
\le
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}.
\]
For the upper bound, the same gate gives \(d\le\rho_U n\log n\) and \(n\ge N_U\), so Step 1 applies; combining it with the displayed ratio bound,
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
C_U\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
C_U\left(\frac1n+\frac{K^2}{n}\right)
=
\frac{C_U(1+K^2)}{n}
=
\frac{C_K}{n}.
\]
This proves the parametric window.
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{Consistency threshold.}  Let \(n_j,d_j\) satisfy \(n_j\to\infty\), \(d_j>0\) eventually, and \(d_j\le\rho_\epsilon n_j\log n_j\) eventually.  Set
\[
q_j=\frac{d_j}{n_j\log n_j}.
\]
For every \(j\), squaring the definition of \(q_j\) gives the identity
\[
\frac1{n_j}+\frac{d_j^2}{n_j^2(\log n_j)^2}
=
\frac1{n_j}+q_j^2 .
\]
% lean: hrate_eq
Since \(n_j\to\infty\) we have \(1/n_j\to0\); and eventually \(n_j\ge2\), so eventually \(\log n_j>0\) and therefore \(q_j\ge0\).
% lean: hinv, hq_nonneg

Consider those indices \(j\) — all sufficiently large ones — for which simultaneously \(d_j>0\), \(d_j\le\rho_\epsilon n_j\log n_j\) and \(n_j\ge N_\epsilon\).  Step 1 requires a member of the experiment class to be exhibited, and \(d_j>0\) permits one: let \(P^\star_{d}\) be the law on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\) with
\[
P^\star_{d}(X=k,A=a,Y=y)=\frac1{4d}
\qquad\text{for all }k\in\{1,\ldots,d\},\ a,y\in\{0,1\},
\]
that is, \(p_k=1/d\), \(\pi_k=1/2\) and \(\mu_{0k}=\mu_{1k}=1/2\) in every category.  Because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) we have \(\epsilon\le1/2\le1-\epsilon\), so \(\pi_k\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\) for every category, \cref{obj:ass:overlap} holds with overlap constant \(\epsilon\), and \((P^\star_{d_j})^{\otimes n_j}\in\mathcal E_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\).
% lean: canonicalClassLaw, endpointParametricLaw_overlap
Applying Step 2 and then Step 1 with this law, and rewriting by the identity above, gives for those same indices
\[
a_\epsilon\left(\frac1{n_j}+q_j^2\right)
\le
\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}
\le
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1{n_j}+q_j^2\right).
\]
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

Suppose first that \(\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\to0\).  Since \(a_\epsilon>0\), the left inequality above reads
\[
0\le \frac1{n_j}+q_j^2\le \frac{1}{a_\epsilon}\,\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}
\]
eventually, and the right side tends to \(0\), so \(1/n_j+q_j^2\to0\).  Then \(0\le q_j^2\le n_j^{-1}+q_j^2\) gives \(q_j^2\to0\), and since \(\sqrt{q_j^2}=|q_j|=q_j\) eventually, continuity of the square-root map at \(0\) gives \(q_j\to0\).

Conversely, suppose \(q_j\to0\).  Then \(q_j^2\to0\), and together with \(1/n_j\to0\) this yields
\[
\frac1{n_j}+q_j^2\to0 .
\]
The minimax risk is nonnegative, because in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} it is an infimum of worst-case mean squared errors, each of which is nonnegative; combined with the right inequality of the two-sided bound above this squeezes \(\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\to0\).
% lean: minimaxRisk_mem_unitInterval, sharp_minimax_fixed_interior
This proves the consistency threshold and completes the proof.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid}]
Throughout, for \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) write
\[
r_{n,d}
=
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\]
for the target rate, and, for a single-observation law \(P\) and a sample
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), write
\[
\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}
=
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k),
\qquad
\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}
=
\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
\]
for the errors of the two branches of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}
against their pilot-selected targets, with \(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}\),
\(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\), \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\),
\(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\) as defined there and \(\phi\) as in
\cref{obj:def:cell-functional}.  Both \(\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}\) and
\(\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}\) are functions of the sample alone.
% lean: heavyContribution, targetHeavy, lightContribution, targetLight, minimaxRate
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{(Computability.)}  The first assertion of
\cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial} is precisely the computability clause: there
is a universal constant \(K>0\) such that, for every \(n\) and \(d\), a
real-arithmetic program reading the hybrid count vector returns
\(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) exactly on every sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) and
uses at most \(K\,d\,M(n)^4\) arithmetic and comparison operations.  It is proved
there with the explicit value \(K=450\), which is a positive integer, so the
clause holds with that \(K\).
% lean: hybridEstimatorComputable, hybridArithmeticProgram_eval, hybridArithmeticProgram_operationCount

\item \textbf{(Calibration of the constants.)}  Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
\Cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial}, applicable because \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\),
supplies constants \(C_L,c_L>0\) depending only on \(\epsilon\) such that
\(\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}[\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}^2]\le C_L\,r_{n,d}\) for every
\(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) with \(d\le c_L n\log n\).
\Cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate}, applicable because
\(0<\epsilon<1/2\), supplies constants \(C_H,c_H>0\) and an integer
\(N_H<\infty\), again depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that
\(\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}[\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}^2]\le C_H\,r_{n,d}\) for every
\(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) with \(n\ge N_H\) and
\(d\le c_H n\log n\).  Set
\[
C_\epsilon=2(C_L+C_H),\qquad
\rho_\epsilon=\min\{c_L,c_H\},\qquad
N_\epsilon=N_H .
\]
Then \(C_\epsilon>0\) and \(\rho_\epsilon>0\).  Fix \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with
\(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\).  Since \(n\ge1\) gives
\(n\log n\ge0\), and since \(\rho_\epsilon\le c_L\) and
\(\rho_\epsilon\le c_H\), the growth hypothesis transfers to both component
lemmas:
\[
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\le c_L n\log n,
\qquad
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\le c_H n\log n .
\]
% lean: light_cell_polynomial, universal_heavy_cell_rate, hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{(Hybrid envelope.)}  Let \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\)
be arbitrary, so that \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:overlap} with constant
\(\epsilon\) and the sample law is the product law of
\cref{obj:def:experiment-class}.  We first bound the squared error pointwise in
the sample.

Because \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\) is the complement of
\(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\) in \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\), the two pilot-selected targets
add up to the estimand: by \cref{obj:def:ate-functional},
\[
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
+
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}\phi(q_k)
=
\tau(P),
\]
for every realization of the pilot split.
% lean: targetHeavy_add_targetLight, lightCells_eq_compl

Next, the estimand lies in the range enforced by the truncation.  By
\cref{obj:def:ate-functional}, \(\tau(P)=\sum_kp_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})\); the
category masses satisfy \(p_k\ge0\) and \(\sum_kp_k=1\), and each conditional
mean lies in \([0,1]\), so \(|\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}|\le1\).  Hence
\[
|\tau(P)|\le\sum_{k=1}^dp_k|\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}|\le\sum_{k=1}^dp_k=1,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\tau(P)\in[-1,1].
\]
% lean: ateFunctional_mem_interval, ateFunctional_eq_weighted_regression

Consequently the truncation in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} cannot
increase the squared error: for every \(x\in\mathbb R\) and every \(t\in[-1,1]\),
\[
\left(\max\{-1,\min(1,x)\}-t\right)^2\le (x-t)^2 .
\]
Indeed, if \(-1\le x\le1\) the two sides are equal; if \(x>1\) the left-hand side
is \((1-t)^2\) and \(0\le 1-t\le x-t\); and if \(x<-1\) the left-hand side is
\((-1-t)^2\) with \(x-t\le -1-t\le0\).
% lean: clamp_sq_error_le

Applying this with \(x=\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\) and
\(t=\tau(P)\), so that
\(\max\{-1,\min(1,x)\}=\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\), then substituting the
target identity and using \((\alpha-\beta)^2\ge0\), we obtain pointwise in the
sample
\[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\le
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\tau(P)\right)^2
=
\left(\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}+\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}\right)^2
\le
2\,\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}^2+2\,\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}^2 .
\]
All the quantities involved are functions of a sample from a finite alphabet,
hence bounded and integrable, so integrating this inequality against
\(P^{\otimes n}\) and using linearity gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
2\,\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}^2\right]
+
2\,\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}^2\right].
\]
% lean: hybrid_mse_le_component_errors

The two component bounds of Step 2 are in force at this \(n\), \(d\), and law,
so the right-hand side is at most
\[
2C_H\,r_{n,d}+2C_L\,r_{n,d}
=
C_\epsilon\,r_{n,d}.
\]
The constants \(C_L,C_H\) do not depend on the member of the class, so the same
bound holds for every \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) and therefore
for the supremum:
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 \left[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
This is the first display of the fixed-overlap clause, and it holds for every
\(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and every \(d\) with \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\).
% lean: hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{(Selected estimator.)}  Keep \(n,d\) as in Step 2.  The selection
rule of \cref{obj:def:selected-estimator} compares the two real numbers
\(C_\epsilon\{1/n+d^2/(n^2\log^2n)\}\) and \(1/n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2\), neither of
which depends on the data; \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}\)
is therefore equal to \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) or to
\(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) as a fixed function of the sample, and in either
case it is a measurable estimator based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\).  Since the
infimum in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} ranges over all such estimators, it is no
larger than the worst-case risk of this one:
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 \left[(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}
        -\tau(P))^2\right].
\]
% lean: combined_upper_envelope

For the envelope itself, the hypotheses of
\cref{obj:lem:combined-upper-envelope} have all been verified: \(0<\epsilon<1/2\)
by assumption, \(C_\epsilon>0\) and \(\rho_\epsilon>0\) by Step 2, and the hybrid
worst-case bound at every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and
\(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\) by Step 3.  Its selected estimator is the one just
described, formed from the same \(C_\epsilon\) and \(\epsilon\), and it yields
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 \left[(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}
        -\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}
\left\{
\frac1n+
\min\left[
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right]
\right\},
\]
which is the remaining display of the fixed-overlap clause with
\(K_\epsilon=\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}\).
% lean: combined_upper_envelope, selectedEstimator

\item \textbf{(Centered estimator.)}  Now fix \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\) and
\(n,d\in\mathbb N\).  If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) has no members, its
supremum is \(0\) and the asserted bound holds because
\(1/n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2\ge0\).  Otherwise, let
\(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\).  The hypotheses of
\cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} hold, since \(n>0\) and
\(P^{\otimes n}\) is a member of the experiment class generated by \(P\), so
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 \left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2 ,
\]
with \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) the estimator of
\cref{obj:def:centered-estimator}.  The right-hand side does not depend on the
member, so taking the supremum over the class gives the centered-estimator
clause.
% lean: near_randomization_linear_upper, overlap_adaptive_universal_hybrid_statistical

\item \textbf{(Endpoint bracket.)}  Finally, for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with
\(n>0\) and \(d>0\), \cref{obj:lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax} gives the
endpoint clause directly:
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \frac1n .
\]
Together with Steps 1, 3, 4, and 5 this proves the theorem.
% lean: randomized_endpoint_minimax, overlap_adaptive_universal_hybrid
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
