\section{Related work}

The paper builds on the potential-outcomes formulation of causal effects and the semiparametric theory of average treatment effect estimation. The potential-outcomes framework introduced by \citet{Rubin1974} and the propensity-score analysis of \citet{RosenbaumRubin1983} give the observational model its target estimand and overlap structure. Semiparametric efficiency calculations and doubly robust constructions then identify the first-order behavior of average treatment effect estimators under regular low-dimensional conditions \citep{Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003,RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,BangRobins2005,VanDerLaanRubin2006}. Higher-order influence-function methods extend this line of work to settings where nuisance estimation and functional curvature affect attainable risk \citep{Robins2008,Robins2009,Robins2017,LiuMukherjeeNeweyRobins2017,Kennedy2024}.

A second strand studies causal estimation when covariates are rich enough that classical fixed-dimensional approximations no longer describe the leading difficulty. Results on high-dimensional and weak-overlap causal inference emphasize how overlap, nuisance complexity, and target curvature interact in observational problems \citep{DAmour2021,Balakrishnan2023,JinSyrgkanis2025}. The present analysis isolates one discrete benchmark within that broader program: finite-alphabet confounding with unrestricted category probabilities and fixed interior overlap. In that benchmark, the alphabet size \leanref{sym:d}{$d$} and sample size \leanref{sym:n}{$n$} enter the minimax rate explicitly, so the problem connects causal identification with large-alphabet functional estimation.

The minimax perspective follows the classical decision-theoretic and nonparametric tradition in which rates are derived from least favorable local alternatives and matching constructive estimators \citep{Bickel1993,VanDerVaart1998,Tsybakov2009}. Adaptation and nonsmooth functional estimation provide the relevant methodological template: logarithmic factors can be intrinsic when the target functional has singular or boundary-sensitive structure, and polynomial approximation can convert that structure into an estimable object \citep{Lepski1999,CaiLow2011}. For the average treatment effect under discrete confounding, the ratio form of the cell contribution creates the nonsmooth feature that drives the large-alphabet term.

The technical construction is also close to the literature on discrete-distribution functionals. Classical species and support-size problems introduced polynomial and moment ideas for unseen mass and related functionals \citep{Fisher1943,GoodToulmin1956,EfronThisted1976}. Modern large-alphabet theory sharpened this approach through approximation-theoretic estimators, factorial moments, and matching lower bounds \citep{Paninski2003,Valiant2011,Valiant2017,Orlitsky2016,WuYang2019,JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016,HanJiaoWeissman2015,HanJiaoWeissman2020}. The hybrid estimator in this paper uses that same approximation principle in a causal cell problem: heavy categories are handled by ratio estimation, while light categories are handled by a factorial-moment polynomial approximation.

The closest comparison is \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. Their strong-overlap discrete-covariate analysis gives an \(O(d^2/n^2+1/n)\) upper bound for the standard plug-in, inverse-probability-weighted, and doubly robust estimator class, together with a minimax lower bound of order \(d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)+1/n\). The result established here attains that lower scale in the fixed-interior overlap regime by constructing a computable balanced ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator, thereby identifying the sharp fixed-interior minimax mean-squared-error rate \(n^{-1}+d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)\) over the stated finite-alphabet experiment class.
