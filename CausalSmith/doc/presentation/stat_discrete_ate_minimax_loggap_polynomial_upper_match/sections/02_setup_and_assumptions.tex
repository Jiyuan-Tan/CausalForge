\section{Setup and assumptions}

Throughout the paper, \(C\) denotes a finite positive constant whose value may change from line to line, with dependence displayed when it matters. Asymptotic notation is along sequences in the sample size \(n\) and the alphabet size \(d\), with the overlap level \(\epsilon\) treated as fixed unless stated otherwise. Category indices range over \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), treatment arms and outcomes range over \(\{0,1\}\), and all risks are mean-squared risks under the product law generated by the observed-data distribution.

The observed-data model is finite and fully discrete. We first fix the variables and sampling law, then impose overlap, define the experiment class, and express the target functional as a sum of categorywise four-cell contributions.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_7}[Observed treatment, outcome, and potential outcomes]
We write \(A\in\{0,1\}\) for the observed binary treatment, with \(A=1\) denoting treated status and \(A=0\) untreated status, and \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) for the observed binary outcome. The potential outcomes \(Y(0)\) and \(Y(1)\) are \(\{0,1\}\)-valued random variables on the same probability space as the observed variables, where \(Y(a)\) is the outcome that would be recorded under treatment level \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iid-sampling}[IID observed-data model]
The observed data are \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\), with \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P\), where the covariate \(X\) takes values in \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\), the treatment \(A\) and outcome \(Y\) are binary. For each category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\) write \(p_k=P(X=k)\) for the category mass, \(\pi_k=P(A=1\mid X=k)\) for the treatment probability, and, for each treatment arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), \(\mu_{ak}=E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=k]\) for the conditional mean outcome.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} is the standard independent and identically distributed sampling condition for a finite-alphabet observational study \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. It allows the law \(P\) to assign arbitrary probabilities to the category--treatment--outcome cells, so all heterogeneity across categories is carried by the unrestricted masses, propensities, and conditional outcome means.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:overlap}[Fixed overlap]
For the overlap level \(\epsilon\), every category \(k\) with category mass \(p_k>0\) satisfies
\[
\epsilon \le \pi_k \le 1-\epsilon .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:overlap} is the standard strong overlap condition \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. It fixes an interior range for each positive-mass category, ensuring that both treatment arms are represented at the population level in every category contributing to the target.

\begin{definitionv}{def:experiment-class}[Experiment class \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\)]
For \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) and \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\), the overlap-restricted iid experiment class is
\[
\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}
:=
\left\{
P^{\otimes n}:
P\in\mathsf{Prob}(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)
\ \text{and } P \text{ satisfies } \cref{obj:ass:overlap}
\right\}.
\]
The law \(P^{\otimes n}\) is formed from the single-observation law in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:overlap-cone}[Overlap cone \(\mathcal C_\epsilon\)]
The overlap-restricted cone of nonnegative four-cell vectors is
\[
\mathcal C_\epsilon
:=
\left\{
u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\in\mathbb R_+^4:
\epsilon(u_{00}+u_{01}+u_{10}+u_{11})
\le
u_{10}+u_{11}
\le
(1-\epsilon)(u_{00}+u_{01}+u_{10}+u_{11})
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:cell-functional}[Cell functional \(\phi(u)\)]
For \(u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\in\mathcal C_\epsilon\) as in \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone}, define
\[
\phi(u)
:=
\begin{cases}
(u_{00}+u_{01}+u_{10}+u_{11})
\left[
\dfrac{u_{11}}{u_{10}+u_{11}}
-
\dfrac{u_{01}}{u_{00}+u_{01}}
\right],
& u\neq(0,0,0,0),\\[1.2em]
0,
& u=(0,0,0,0).
\end{cases}
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:ate-functional}[Average treatment effect \(\tau(P)\)]
For a probability law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), with arbitrary masses on this finite observation alphabet, define, for \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\) and \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), the joint cell mass
\[
q_{aky}=P(A=a,X=k,Y=y),
\]
and set
\[
q_k=(q_{0k0},q_{0k1},q_{1k0},q_{1k1}).
\]
The average treatment effect functional is
\[
\tau(P)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}\phi(q_k).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The experiment class in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} is the decision-theoretic object over which risks are evaluated. The cone in \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone} rewrites fixed overlap directly in terms of the four joint treatment--outcome masses within a category, and \cref{obj:def:cell-functional,obj:def:ate-functional} turn those cell masses into the categorywise contribution and the population ATE functional. This representation is useful because the analysis can treat the target as a sum of homogeneous finite-alphabet cell functionals.

\begin{definitionv}{def:minimax-risk}[Minimax risk \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\)]
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
:=
\inf_{\widehat\tau}
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right].
\]
The infimum ranges over all measurable estimators \(\widehat\tau\) based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\).
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} measures the worst-case mean-squared error over the fixed-overlap experiment class. The main rate theorem below evaluates this risk and compares it with the performance of a concrete estimator.

\paragraph{The extrema in the minimax risk.}
Throughout the paper the two extrema in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} are the ordinary ones: the inner supremum is the supremum of a nonempty family of nonnegative reals that is bounded above, and the outer infimum is taken over a family of nonnegative reals bounded below by \(0\). Three facts supply this. Whenever \(n>0\), \(d>0\), and \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\), the class \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} is nonempty, since the law with \(p_k=1/d\) and \(\pi_k=1/2\) for every category satisfies \cref{obj:ass:overlap}. The sample space \((\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\})^n\) is finite, so an estimator takes finitely many values and is a bounded function of the sample. And \(|\tau(P)|\le1\) for every law \(P\), since \cref{obj:def:cell-functional,obj:def:ate-functional} express \(\tau(P)=\sum_kp_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})\) as a convex combination of differences of conditional means in \([0,1]\). Together they bound the squared losses \((\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\) uniformly over the class, so \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is a well-defined number in \([0,1]\) and no extended-real value arises.

Readers comparing the text with the machine-checked development will find one bookkeeping difference. That development makes \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) a real number by fiat, assigning the value \(0\) to a supremum over an empty family or over a family with no real upper bound, and reading the quotients in the displayed rate as real division with \(x/0=0\). The appendix proofs therefore carry the corresponding boundary cases explicitly, even though the paragraph above shows that the supremum and infimum defining \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) are the ordinary ones throughout.

The estimator uses one deterministic split to classify categories by pilot abundance and the other split to estimate the resulting heavy and light contributions. In words, before any notation: cut the sample in half; use the first half only to sort categories into those with enough observations and those without; on the well-observed categories estimate the treatment-control contrast by the obvious empirical ratios, computed from the second half; on the sparse categories, where those ratios are unstable, replace the contrast by a polynomial of degree about \(\log n\) that approximates it, and estimate that polynomial without bias from falling-factorial moments of the second-half cell counts; add the two parts and clip the total to \([-1,1]\). The definitions below record the split counts, the polynomial notation, and the calibrated hybrid estimator built from these steps, and a step-by-step evaluation recipe follows the definition.

\begin{definitionv}{def:sample-splits}[Sample splits \(I_0,I_1\) and counts \(N^{(j)}_{aky}\)]
For a sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) with \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), define the deterministic half-sample splits, their sizes, and the logarithmic scale
\[
I_0=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\},\qquad I_1=\{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1,\ldots,n\},\qquad m_j=|I_j|,\qquad L=\log(en),
\]
and, for each split \(j\in\{0,1\}\), treatment arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), and outcome \(y\in\{0,1\}\), the split cell count
\[
N^{(j)}_{aky}=\#\{i\in I_j:(A_i,X_i,Y_i)=(a,k,y)\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_3}[Chebyshev polynomials, falling factorials, and four-cell monomials]
For \(M\in\mathbb N\), \(T_M\) denotes the degree-\(M\) Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind, characterized by \(T_M(\cos\theta)=\cos(M\theta)\) for every \(\theta\in\mathbb R\). For a real number \(z\) and an integer \(t\ge1\), the falling factorial is \((z)_t=z(z-1)\cdots(z-t+1)\), with \((z)_0=1\); it is applied to the split cell counts as \((N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}\) and to the estimation-split size as \((m_1)_{|r|}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{|r|-1}(m_1-\ell)\), the empty product being \(1\). For a four-cell vector \(u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\) and a multi-index \(r=(r_{ay})_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}\in\mathbb N_0^4\) of total degree \(|r|=\sum_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}r_{ay}\), the monomial \(u^r\) is \(\prod_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}u_{ay}^{r_{ay}}\). For a category \(k\) and a multi-index \(r\), the normalized factorial-moment monomial is \[ \mathfrak m_{k,r} = \frac{\prod_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}}{(m_1)_{|r|}}. \]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:hybrid-estimator-handle}[Hybrid estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\)]
Using the deterministic splits \(I_0,I_1\), split sizes \(m_j\), logarithmic scale \(L=\log(en)\), and split counts \(N^{(j)}_{aky}\) from \cref{obj:def:sample-splits}, set
\[
A_0=6,\qquad \lambda_0=256,\qquad b_0=4096,
\]
\[
D_{A_0}=8\log(9A_0/8)=8\log(27/4),
\]
and
\[
\alpha_0=\min\left\{1,\frac1{32\log A_0},\frac1{256D_{A_0}}\right\}.
\]
For \(n\ge2\), define
\[
M(n)=\max\{2,\lfloor\alpha_0L\rfloor\},\qquad B(n)=b_0L/m_1.
\]
Let \(N_0\) be the least numerical cutoff such that, for every \(n\ge N_0\),
\[
\alpha_0L\ge2,\qquad 4M(n)^2\le m_1,\qquad 4M(n)/m_1\le 3B(n)/4.
\]
For every \(n\), define the pilot-certified heavy and light category sets
\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}_n
=
\left\{k:\sum_{a,y}N^{(0)}_{aky}>\lambda_0L\right\},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\widehat{\mathcal H}_n^c\quad(n\ge N_0),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\varnothing\quad(n<N_0),
\]
so that for \(n<N_0\) every category is treated by the ratio branch below. For \(n\ge N_0\), abbreviate \(M=M(n)\) and \(B=B(n)\).
Put
\[
N_k^{(1)}=\sum_{a,y}N^{(1)}_{aky},
\qquad
N_{ak}^{(1)}=\sum_yN^{(1)}_{aky},
\]
and define the heavy-category ratio contribution
\[
\widehat h_k
=
\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}
\left\{
\frac{N^{(1)}_{1k1}}{N^{(1)}_{1k}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{1k}>0\}
-
\frac{N^{(1)}_{0k1}}{N^{(1)}_{0k}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{0k}>0\}
\right\}.
\]
Define
\[
H_M(x)=\frac{1-T_M(1-2x)}{2M^2},
\qquad
E_M(x)=\frac{H_M(x)}{x},
\qquad
G_M(x)=\frac{1-E_M(x)}{x},
\]
where \(E_M\) and \(G_M\) are interpreted by polynomial continuation at zero. For \(u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\), set
\[
s_a(u)=u_{a0}+u_{a1},\qquad s(u)=s_0(u)+s_1(u),
\]
\[
t_1(u)=u_{11},\qquad t_0(u)=u_{01},
\]
and define the light-cell polynomial
\[
P_{M,B}(u)
=
\frac{s(u)t_1(u)}B
G_M\!\left(\frac{s_1(u)}B\right)
-
\frac{s(u)t_0(u)}B
G_M\!\left(\frac{s_0(u)}B\right).
\]
Expand
\[
P_{M,B}(u)=\sum_{|r|\le M}c_ru^r.
\]
For each category \(k\), define
\[
\widehat P_k
=
\sum_{|r|\le M}
c_r
\frac{\prod_{a,y}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}}{(m_1)_{|r|}}.
\]
The heavy and light sums are
\[
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\widehat h_k,
\qquad
\widehat T_{\mathcal L}
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\widehat P_k.
\]
The truncated balanced ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator is
\[
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}
=
\max\{-1,\min(1,\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L})\}.
\]
The same calibrated estimator is used for every overlap level.
\end{definitionv}

The split in \cref{obj:def:sample-splits} separates category selection from contribution estimation. The polynomial notation in \cref{obj:synth_3} supports unbiased estimation of polynomial cell functionals through factorial moments. The estimator in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} then combines direct ratio estimation on pilot-certified heavy categories with a Chebyshev-based polynomial contribution on pilot-certified light categories, using fixed numerical tuning.

\paragraph{Computing the hybrid estimator.}
The definition is a finite recipe in the split counts, and it evaluates in the following steps. Throughout, \(L=\log(en)\), \(M=M(n)\), and \(B=B(n)\) are the scale, degree, and light-cell scale fixed in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}; the cutoff \(N_0\) there is the least integer meeting the three displayed inequalities, so it is pinned down by the numerical constants \(\alpha_0,\lambda_0,b_0\) alone and can be tabulated once, before any data are seen.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Pilot counts and classification.} From the pilot split \(I_0\), form the counts \(N^{(0)}_{aky}\) and the category totals \(N^{(0)}_k=\sum_{a,y}N^{(0)}_{aky}\). Place category \(k\) in \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\) when \(N^{(0)}_k>\lambda_0L\). For \(n\ge N_0\) the remaining categories form \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\); for \(n<N_0\) every category goes to the ratio branch.
\item \emph{Estimation counts.} From the estimation split \(I_1\), form \(N^{(1)}_{aky}\) together with the arm totals \(N^{(1)}_{ak}=\sum_yN^{(1)}_{aky}\) and the category totals \(N^{(1)}_k=\sum_{a,y}N^{(1)}_{aky}\).
\item \emph{Ratio branch.} For each \(k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\), evaluate \(\widehat h_k\) from the displayed ratio formula; an arm with no observation contributes zero through the accompanying indicator.
\item \emph{Polynomial coefficients.} Compute the reciprocal coefficients \(g_{M,j}\), \(0\le j\le M-2\), of the continuation \(G_M\), which are the coefficients characterized in \cref{obj:synth_19} and are read off from those of the Chebyshev polynomial \(T_M\). They depend on \(n\) alone, so one list serves every category.
\item \emph{Light branch.} For each \(k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\) and each arm \(a\), accumulate the normalized factorial moments \(\mathfrak m_{k,r}\) of \cref{obj:synth_3} against the weights \(B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\) prescribed by the sparse-arm expansion of \cref{obj:lem:sparse-arm-expansion}, and set \(\widehat P_k=\widehat A_{1,k}-\widehat A_{0,k}\). This evaluates \(\widehat P_k=\sum_{|r|\le M}c_r\mathfrak m_{k,r}\) directly from counts, so the multivariate polynomial \(P_{M,B}\) enters only through those weights.
\item \emph{Aggregate and truncate.} Add the heavy sum \(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}\) and the light sum \(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\), then clip the total to \([-1,1]\).
\end{enumerate}
Every step reads only the split counts, and the computability statement of \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} bounds the whole evaluation by \(K\,d\,M(n)^4\) operations for a universal integer \(K\), with \(M(n)\) of order \(\log n\).

We next connect the observed-data functional to the usual potential-outcome interpretation of the ATE. The following assumptions are stated separately from the experiment class because the minimax problem is formulated on observed laws, while causal interpretation uses a full-data overlay.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:consistency}[Observed-outcome consistency]
The observed outcome satisfies
\[
Y=Y(A)
\]
almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:consistency} is the standard consistency condition linking the recorded outcome to the potential outcome under the realized treatment \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. It gives the observed binary outcome its causal interpretation once treatment is assigned.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:conditional-exchangeability}[Conditional exchangeability]
The potential outcomes satisfy
\[
(Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid X .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability} is the standard conditional exchangeability condition \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. Together with \cref{obj:ass:consistency,obj:ass:overlap}, it identifies the category-adjusted contrast represented by \(\tau(P)\) in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}; this is the finite-alphabet version of the usual adjustment argument in observational causal inference \citep{Rubin1974,RosenbaumRubin1983}.

A two-category construction illustrates how the causal target can differ from the unadjusted observed contrast even in the smallest nontrivial discrete model. The example fixes the category probabilities, treatment propensities, and conditional means, then supplies a full-data extension satisfying the potential-outcome conditions.

\begin{definitionv}{def:two-category-witness}[Two-category witness law]
For \(d=2\) and \(0\le \epsilon\le 1\), set
\[
p_1=p_2=\frac12,\qquad
\pi_1=\epsilon,\qquad
\pi_2=1-\epsilon,\qquad
\mu_{01}=\mu_{02}=0,\qquad
\mu_{11}=1,\qquad
\mu_{12}=0.
\]
Define the corresponding full-data extension by drawing
\[
(Y(0),Y(1))=(0,\mathbf 1\{X=1\})
\]
setting \(Y=Y(A)\), and then drawing \(A\mid X=k\sim\mathsf{Bernoulli}(\pi_k)\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{propositionv}{prop:two-category-confounding}[Two-category confounding witness]
For any overlap level \(\epsilon\) satisfying \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\), let \(P\) be the full-data law constructed in \cref{obj:def:two-category-witness}. Then \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:consistency,obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability,obj:ass:overlap}. Its average treatment effect, with \(\tau(P)\) as in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}, and its naive observed contrast are
\[
\tau(P)=\frac12,
\qquad
E_P[Y\mid A=1]-E_P[Y\mid A=0]=\epsilon .
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:two-category-confounding} records the exact separation between the adjusted causal estimand and the naive treated-minus-control contrast in this witness law. The construction satisfies the same consistency, conditional exchangeability, and fixed-overlap conditions used for causal interpretation, so the discrepancy arises from category imbalance in the observed treatment groups while the estimand remains the adjusted ATE.
