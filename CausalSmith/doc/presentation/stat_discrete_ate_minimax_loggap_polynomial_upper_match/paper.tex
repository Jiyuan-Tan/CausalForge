\begin{abstract}
This paper studies average treatment effect estimation with binary treatment and outcome when the covariate is a discrete variable with \(d\) categories and otherwise unrestricted marginal distribution. Under iid sampling and uniform overlap, the estimand is the covariate-adjusted ATE, written as a sum of homogeneous four-cell functionals. For each fixed overlap constant \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there are constants \(\rho_\epsilon>0\) and \(N_\epsilon<\infty\) such that, for \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), \(d>0\), and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), we establish the minimax mean-squared-error rate
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\asymp_\epsilon
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}.
\]
Under the same fixed-interior calibration conditions, the upper bound is attained by a computable split-sample hybrid estimator: empirical ratios are used on pilot-certified heavy categories, while light categories are estimated by a Chebyshev-polynomial continuation implemented through factorial moments. The lower-bound scale is imported from the fixed-sample construction of \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}; the matching computable upper bound, the fixed-interior rate statement, and the resulting parametric-rate and consistency regimes are proved in this paper. The result implies a parametric-rate window \(d=O(\sqrt n\log n)\), within the fixed-interior range, and a consistency threshold \(d=o(n\log n)\). For each fixed interior \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), under the same hybrid calibration range \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), we also record an oracle-calibrated deterministic comparison envelope near exact randomization. This theoretical comparison uses the known constants \(\epsilon\) and \(C_\epsilon\). At the separate endpoint \(\epsilon=1/2\), the minimax risk is bracketed between constants times \(n^{-1}\), uniformly in \(d\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Covariate adjustment is central to observational causal inference. Under consistency, conditional exchangeability, and overlap, the average treatment effect can be identified by comparing treated and control outcomes within covariate strata and averaging over the covariate distribution \citep{Rubin1974,RosenbaumRubin1983,Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003}. Classical semiparametric theory describes this problem well when the adjustment dimension is fixed or when nuisance functions can be estimated with enough structure. This paper studies a finite-alphabet benchmark in which that structure is deliberately absent.

The model is simple. A single observation is \(O=(X,A,Y)\), where \(X\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), \(A\in\{0,1\}\), and \(Y\in\{0,1\}\). The observations are iid as in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}. The covariate marginal is unrestricted, and treatment assignment satisfies the categorywise overlap condition in \cref{obj:ass:overlap}. With the usual potential-outcome interpretation supplied by \cref{obj:ass:consistency} and \cref{obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability}, the ATE is the functional in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}. The statistical object is the minimax mean-squared-error risk over the finite-alphabet overlap class in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} and \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.

The main result is that, for each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), the sharp minimax rate is \(n^{-1}+d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)\) under the stated fixed-interior calibration range. \Cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} shows that there are constants depending on \(\epsilon\) such that, whenever \(n\) is large enough, \(d>0\), and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\),
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\asymp_\epsilon
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}.
\]
Within the same fixed-interior range, the theorem also gives a parametric-rate window when \(d\le K\sqrt n\log n\), and it gives the consistency threshold \(d=o(n\log n)\). These statements are uniform over unrestricted covariate marginals satisfying only the stated overlap restriction.

The upper bound is constructive. \Cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} gives a split-sample estimator that first uses pilot counts to classify covariate categories as heavy or light. Heavy categories are estimated by empirical ratios on an independent split. Light categories are estimated by a polynomial approximation to the homogeneous cell functional in \cref{obj:def:cell-functional}, with factorial moments used to estimate the polynomial terms without unstable empirical denominators. \Cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} records both the fixed-overlap risk envelope and the real-arithmetic computability bound for this estimator.

The estimator uses polynomial approximation on weakly sampled cells, a device related to large-alphabet nonsmooth functional estimation \citep{Paninski2003,JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016,HanJiaoWeissman2020}. Here the cell contribution is the four-cell ATE term
\[
p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}).
\]
\citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024} provide the fixed-sample lower-bound ingredient used here and analyze standard regression, weighting, and doubly robust estimators for this high-dimensional discrete ATE problem. This paper constructs the ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator and derives the fixed-interior consistency and parametric-rate regimes. The lower-bound transfer itself is stated in \cref{obj:lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer}.

The result provides a complementary finite-alphabet minimax benchmark for semiparametric and doubly robust theory. In settings with smoothness, sparsity, or accurate nuisance regression and propensity estimates, inverse-probability, doubly robust, targeted, double-machine-learning, and higher-order approaches address different regimes \citep{RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,BangRobins2005,VanDerLaanRubin2006,BelloniChernozhukovHansen2014,Chernozhukov2018,Robins2008,Robins2009,Robins2017,Kennedy2024}. In the growing unrestricted-alphabet regime studied here, overlap is the sole regularity condition and many small categories create the nonregular term \(d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)\).

The endpoint \(\epsilon=1/2\) is different. At exact within-category randomization, \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} brackets the minimax risk between \(1/(100n)\) and \(1/n\), uniformly in \(d\). For each fixed interior \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and only under the same calibration conditions \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), the same theorem also gives an oracle-calibrated deterministic comparison envelope near randomization,
\[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right\},
\]
up to constants. This comparison chooses between two already analyzed estimators using the known fixed-interior constants and supplies an oracle-calibrated upper envelope near the randomized endpoint.

The formal statements and derivations are supported by a Lean 4 development, with the precise verification boundary recorded in the verification appendix.

\section{Setup and assumptions}

We work with a finite-alphabet observational model. A single observation is
\leanref{sym:O_i}{\ensuremath{O_i}}\(=(\leanref{sym:X}{\ensuremath{X}},\leanref{sym:A}{\ensuremath{A}},\leanref{sym:Y}{\ensuremath{Y}})\), where \(X\in\{1,\ldots,\leanref{sym:d}{\ensuremath{d}}\}\) is a discrete covariate, \(A\in\{0,1\}\) is treatment, and \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) is the observed outcome. The \leanref{S-1}{finite-alphabet i.i.d. observational world} is deliberately nonparametric in the distribution of \(X\): the cell probabilities may be arbitrary subject only to normalization and the overlap restriction introduced below.

\begin{definitionv}{def:finite-alphabet-probability-laws}[Finite-alphabet probability laws \(\mathsf{Prob}\)]
We say \(P\in\mathsf{Prob}(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)\) when \(P\) is a probability measure on the finite observation alphabet \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,\ldots,1\}\), equivalently a nonnegative mass assignment to each cell \((k,a,y)\) whose total mass over all \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), \(a\in\{0,1\}\), and \(y\in\{0,1\}\) is one.
\end{definitionv}

For \(P\) as in \cref{obj:def:finite-alphabet-probability-laws}, write \leanref{sym:q_{aky}}{\ensuremath{q_{aky}}} for \(P(X=k,A=a,Y=y)\), \leanref{sym:p_k}{\ensuremath{p_k}} for \(P(X=k)\), \leanref{sym:pi_k}{\ensuremath{\pi_k}} for \(P(A=1\mid X=k)\) when \(p_k>0\), and \leanref{sym:mu_{ak}}{\ensuremath{\mu_{ak}}} for \(E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=k]\) when the conditioning event has positive probability, with the convention \(\pi_k=0\) and \(\mu_{ak}=0\) on categories and arm-cells of zero mass; both then lie in \([0,1]\) for every \(k\) and \(a\), and such categories contribute nothing to \(\tau(P)\). We also write \leanref{sym:q_k}{\ensuremath{q_k}} for the four-vector \((q_{0k0},q_{0k1},q_{1k0},q_{1k1})\), ordered by treatment and outcome. The sample size \leanref{sym:n}{\ensuremath{n}} is fixed throughout the experiment.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iid-sampling}[Iid sampling]
The observations \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent and identically distributed with single-observation law \(P\).
\end{assumptionv}

\cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} is the standard independent and identically distributed sampling condition; it isolates the difficulty of unrestricted discrete confounding from dependence or sampling-design complications \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}.

Treatment assignment is summarized category by category.

\begin{definitionv}{def:bernoulli-law}[Bernoulli law \(\mathsf{Bernoulli}(\pi_k)\)]
We say \(A\mid X=k\sim\mathsf{Bernoulli}(\pi_k)\) when \(A\in\{0,1\}\) and, conditional on \(X=k\), \(P(A=1\mid X=k)=\pi_k\) and \(P(A=0\mid X=k)=1-\pi_k\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:overlap}[Uniform overlap]
For every \(k\) with \(p_k>0\),
\[
\epsilon\le \pi_k\le 1-\epsilon .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\cref{obj:ass:overlap} is the standard strong overlap condition, requiring every observed covariate category to retain treatment and control observations with probability bounded away from zero \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. The constant \leanref{sym:epsilon}{\ensuremath{\epsilon}} may affect constants in the fixed-interior theory, while the estimator defined below uses the same formula across overlap classes.

For causal interpretation, we use the usual potential-outcome overlay only where needed.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:consistency}[Consistency]
The observed outcome satisfies
\[
Y=Y(A)
\]
almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

\cref{obj:ass:consistency} is the standard consistency condition, linking the realized observed outcome to the potential outcome under the assigned treatment \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. The potential outcomes \leanref{sym:Y(a)}{\ensuremath{Y(a)}} are binary in the present model.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:conditional-exchangeability}[Conditional exchangeability]
The binary potential outcomes satisfy
\[
(Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid X .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\cref{obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability} is the standard conditional exchangeability condition, under which comparisons within a covariate category identify the category-specific causal contrast \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. Together with \cref{obj:ass:consistency} and~\cref{obj:ass:overlap}, it gives the observational ATE functional its causal interpretation.

The statistical problem is indexed by all finite-alphabet laws obeying these restrictions.

\begin{definitionv}{def:experiment-class}[Experiment class \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\)]
Under \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} and \cref{obj:ass:overlap}, the experiment class is
\[
\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}
:=
\{P^{\otimes n}: P\in\mathsf{Prob}(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2),\ n,d\in\mathbb N,\ 0<\epsilon\le 1/2,\ P\text{ satisfies \cref{obj:ass:overlap}}\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The class \leanref{sym:\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}{\ensuremath{\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}} in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} allows the covariate marginal to vary freely over the \(d\) categories. Thus sparsity of categories is part of the estimation problem rather than a regularity condition imposed away. A member of \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) determines its generating law: \(P^{\otimes n}\) has \(P\) as its one-observation marginal, so writing \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) fixes \(P\) and the functional \(\tau(P)\) attached to that member is unambiguous.

It is convenient to express each covariate category through its four observed cell masses.

\begin{definitionv}{def:overlap-cone}[Overlap cone \(\mathcal C_\epsilon\)]
The overlap-restricted cone of nonnegative four-cell vectors is
\[
\mathcal C_\epsilon
:=
\{u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\in\mathbb R_+^4:
\epsilon(u_{00}+u_{01}+u_{10}+u_{11})
\le u_{10}+u_{11}
\le (1-\epsilon)(u_{00}+u_{01}+u_{10}+u_{11})\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\cref{obj:def:overlap-cone} records overlap as a constraint on a nonnegative four-cell vector \leanref{sym:\mathcal C_\epsilon}{\ensuremath{\mathcal C_\epsilon}}. The cone formulation keeps the category mass and the treatment split in the same object, which is useful when categories have small or vanishing probability.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cell-functional}[Cell functional \(\phi(u)\)]
For \(u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\in\mathcal C_\epsilon\), with \(\mathcal C_\epsilon\) as in \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone}, define
\[
\phi(u)
=
(u_{00}+u_{01}+u_{10}+u_{11})
\left\{
\frac{u_{11}}{u_{10}+u_{11}}
-
\frac{u_{01}}{u_{00}+u_{01}}
\right\}
\]
when \(u\neq 0\), and set \(\phi(0,0,0,0)=0\).
\end{definitionv}

The homogeneous contribution \leanref{sym:\phi}{\ensuremath{\phi}} in \cref{obj:def:cell-functional} is the category mass multiplied by the treated-minus-control conditional mean contrast. Its homogeneity is the source of the nonsmooth behavior at small cell masses.

\begin{definitionv}{def:ate-functional}[ATE functional \(\tau(P)\)]
The average treatment effect functional is
\[
\tau(P)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})
=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}\phi(q_k).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The estimand \leanref{sym:\tau(P)}{\ensuremath{\tau(P)}} in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} is therefore both the usual covariate-adjusted ATE and a sum of four-cell contributions. This second representation is the one used by the estimator and by the minimax analysis.

The estimator separates categories using a deterministic half-sample split.

\begin{definitionv}{def:balanced-sample-split}[Balanced sample split $I_j$]
For $j\in\{0,1\}$, the balanced deterministic half-sample index set $I_j\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ is $I_j=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}: i\le \lfloor n/2\rfloor\}$ when $j=0$, and $I_j=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}: \lfloor n/2\rfloor<i\le n\}$ when $j=1$.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:split-cell-counts}[Split cell counts $N^{(0)}_{aky}$ and $N^{(1)}_{aky}$]
For $j\in\{0,1\}$, $k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$, and $a,y\in\{0,1\}$, the split cell count $N^{(j)}_{aky}$ is the number of observations in the deterministic split $I_j$ with covariate value $k$, treatment value $a$, and outcome value $y$: \[N^{(j)}_{aky}:=\sum_{i\in I_j}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,\ A_i=a,\ Y_i=y\}.\]
\end{definitionv}

The first split is used only to classify categories as empirically heavy or light, while the second split estimates their contributions. The count notation \leanref{sym:N^{(j)}_{aky}}{\ensuremath{N^{(j)}_{aky}}} in \cref{obj:def:split-cell-counts} will also be used to form factorial moments; for a nonnegative integer \(z\) and order \(r\), the falling factorial \leanref{sym:z_r}{\ensuremath{(z)_r}} is defined next. In the two definitions that follow, the degree \(M\) and the order \(r\) are free nonnegative integers; the estimator of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} then fixes the degree at the calibrated value \(M=M(n)\) for every sample size \(n\ge N_0\), where \(N_0\) is the numerical cutoff recorded there. We also write \(N^{(j)}_{k}=\sum_{a,y}N^{(j)}_{aky}\) for the split-\(j\) count of category \(k\); in particular \(N^{(0)}_{k}\) is the pilot count of category \(k\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:chebyshev-polynomial}[Chebyshev Polynomial $T_M$]
We write $T_M$ for the degree-$M$ Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind on $\mathbb R$, characterized by $T_0(x)=1$, $T_1(x)=x$, and $T_{M+2}(x)=2xT_{M+1}(x)-T_M(x)$ for all $M\ge0$ and $x\in\mathbb R$.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:falling-factorial}[Falling factorial \((x)_r\)]
For \(x,r\in\mathbb N_0\), we write \((x)_r\) for the falling factorial of \(x\) of order \(r\), defined by \((x)_r=\prod_{\ell=0}^{r-1}(x-\ell)\), with the empty product convention \((x)_0=1\); hence \((x)_r=0\) when \(r>x\).
\end{definitionv}

The Chebyshev polynomial in \cref{obj:def:chebyshev-polynomial} supplies the light-cell approximation, and \cref{obj:def:falling-factorial} supplies the unbiased factorial-moment representation for its polynomial terms. For the multi-index notation below, \leanref{sym:r=(r_{ay})_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}}{\ensuremath{r=(r_{ay})_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}}} ranges over four nonnegative integer coordinates. Throughout, we write \(\widehat\tau\) for a generic measurable estimator of \(\tau(P)\) formed from the sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\); the particular estimator constructed next is \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\). For such a multi-index write \(|r|=\sum_{a,y}r_{ay}\) for its total order and \(u^r=\prod_{a,y}u_{ay}^{r_{ay}}\) for the corresponding monomial in a four-cell vector \(u\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:hybrid-estimator-handle}[Hybrid estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\)]
For a sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) with \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), set
\[
I_0=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\},\qquad
I_1=\{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1,\ldots,n\},\qquad
m_j=|I_j|,\qquad
L=\log(en).
\]
Let \(A=6\), and set
\[
\lambda_0=256,\qquad
b_0=4096,\qquad
D_A=8\log(9A/8)=8\log(27/4),
\]
and
\[
\alpha_0
=
\min\left\{
1,\frac1{32\log A},\frac1{256D_A}
\right\}.
\]
For \(n\ge2\), define
\[
M(n)=\max\{2,\lfloor\alpha_0L\rfloor\},
\qquad
B(n)=b_0L/m_1.
\]
Let \(N_0\) be the least numerical cutoff such that, for every \(n\ge N_0\),
\[
\alpha_0L\ge2,\qquad
4M(n)^2\le m_1,\qquad
4M(n)/m_1\le 3B(n)/4.
\]
For \(n\ge N_0\), write \(M=M(n)\) and \(B=B(n)\), and define the pilot-certified heavy and light category sets by
\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}_n
=
\left\{
k:\sum_{a,y}N^{(0)}_{aky}>\lambda_0L
\right\},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\widehat{\mathcal H}_n^c.
\]
For the second split, put
\[
N_k^{(1)}=\sum_{a,y}N^{(1)}_{aky},
\qquad
N_{ak}^{(1)}=\sum_yN^{(1)}_{aky},
\]
and define the heavy-category ratio contribution
\[
\widehat h_k
=
\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}
\left\{
\frac{N^{(1)}_{1k1}}{N^{(1)}_{1k}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{1k}>0\}
-
\frac{N^{(1)}_{0k1}}{N^{(1)}_{0k}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{0k}>0\}
\right\}.
\]
Let
\[
H_M(x)=\frac{1-T_M(1-2x)}{2M^2},
\qquad
E_M(x)=\frac{H_M(x)}{x},
\qquad
G_M(x)=\frac{1-E_M(x)}{x},
\]
where \(E_M\) and \(G_M\) are interpreted by polynomial continuation at zero. For \(u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\), set
\[
s_a(u)=u_{a0}+u_{a1},\qquad
s(u)=s_0(u)+s_1(u),\qquad
t_1(u)=u_{11},\qquad
t_0(u)=u_{01}.
\]
Define the light-cell polynomial approximation
\[
P_{M,B}(u)
=
\frac{s(u)t_1(u)}B
G_M\!\left(\frac{s_1(u)}B\right)
-
\frac{s(u)t_0(u)}B
G_M\!\left(\frac{s_0(u)}B\right).
\]
Expanding \(P_{M,B}(u)=\sum_{|r|\le M}c_ru^r\), set
\[
\widehat P_k
=
\sum_{|r|\le M}
c_r
\frac{\prod_{a,y}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}}{(m_1)_{|r|}},
\qquad
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\widehat h_k,
\qquad
\widehat T_{\mathcal L}
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\widehat P_k.
\]
The truncated balanced ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator is
\[
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}
=
\max\{-1,\min(1,\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L})\}.
\]
For \(n<N_0\), set \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\varnothing\) and use the ratio branch on every category. The definition uses no overlap parameter.
\end{definitionv}

\cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} combines a ratio estimator on pilot-certified heavy categories with a polynomial estimator on pilot-certified light categories. The sets \leanref{sym:\(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n,\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\)}{\ensuremath{\widehat{\mathcal H}_n,\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}} are determined from \(I_0\), while the light contribution \leanref{sym:\widehat T_{\mathcal L}}{\ensuremath{\widehat T_{\mathcal L}}} and the final estimator \leanref{sym:\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}}{\ensuremath{\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}}} use the second split for estimation. The truncation simply enforces the natural range of an ATE with binary outcomes.

Throughout the reader-facing statistical statements, \(\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}\), so both the sample size and alphabet size are positive. For the operation-count notation set \(M(1)=2\). When \(1\le n<N_0\), the ratio-only branch is evaluated directly; the light-cell quantities \(B(n)\), polynomial coefficients, and factorial-moment terms begin at the large-sample branch. The statistical experiment stated here therefore uses positive sample and alphabet sizes, alongside Lean's total conventions at zero.

For comparison near randomized treatment, we also record a centered linear estimator, which we write \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:centered-estimator}[Centered estimator \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\)]
For observations \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\), with binary treatment \(A_i\) and binary outcome \(Y_i\), the centered estimator is \[\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}=\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n(2A_i-1)\left(Y_i-\frac12\right).\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:selected-estimator}[Selected estimator \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}\)]
For fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and fixed-interior upper-bound constant \(C_\epsilon\), define \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}\) to be \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) when
\[
C_\epsilon\{1/n+d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)\}\le 1/n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2,
\]
and to be \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) otherwise. At \(n=1\), the quotient involving \(\log n\) is interpreted as zero; the asymptotic results use this selector only above their stated large-sample cutoff.
\end{definitionv}

This is an oracle-calibrated deterministic comparison rule. It combines the fixed-interior hybrid bound with the near-randomization linear bound using the fixed overlap constant and the fixed-interior upper-bound constant \(C_\epsilon\).

The risk criterion is worst-case mean squared error over the overlap experiment class.

\begin{definitionv}{def:minimax-risk}[Minimax risk \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\)]
The minimax mean-squared-error risk is
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
=
\inf_{\widehat\tau}
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right],
\]
where the infimum ranges over all measurable estimators \(\widehat\tau\) based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\).
\end{definitionv}

The quantity \leanref{sym:\(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\)}{\ensuremath{\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}}} in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} is the object characterized in the main results. The supremum is over product laws in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}, so the risk treats the covariate distribution and all conditional outcome probabilities as unknown nuisance features.

We close the setup with a two-category construction showing why covariate adjustment is essential even in the smallest nontrivial alphabet.

\begin{definitionv}{def:two-category-witness}[Two-category witness law]
For \(d=2\), set
\[
p_1=p_2=\frac12,\qquad
\pi_1=\epsilon,\qquad
\pi_2=1-\epsilon,\qquad
\mu_{01}=\mu_{02}=0,\qquad
\mu_{11}=1,\qquad
\mu_{12}=0.
\]
Define a full-data extension by drawing
\[
(Y(0),Y(1))=(0,\mathbf 1\{X=1\}),
\]
and then drawing \(A\mid X=k\sim\mathsf{Bernoulli}(\pi_k)\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{propositionv}{prop:two-category-confounding}[Two-Category Confounding Witness]
For \(\epsilon\), suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Real parameter.)} \(\epsilon\in\mathbb R\).
\item \textbf{(Positive contrast.)} \(0<\epsilon\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap range.)} \(\epsilon\le 1/2\).
\item \textbf{(Witness law.)} \(Q\) is the full-data law constructed in \cref{obj:def:two-category-witness}, and \(P\) is its observed marginal law.
\end{itemize}
Then \(Q\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:consistency} and \cref{obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability}, the observed law \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:overlap} with overlap constant \(\epsilon\), and the ATE functional of \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} obeys
\[
\tau(P)=\frac12,
\qquad
E_P[Y\mid A=1]-E_P[Y\mid A=0]=\epsilon .
\]
\end{propositionv}

\cref{obj:prop:two-category-confounding} separates the causal estimand from the unadjusted observed contrast. The construction satisfies the usual causal restrictions, but the treated and control groups place different weights on the two covariate categories. As a result, the naive contrast can be as small as the overlap constant even though the ATE equals one half.

\section{Main results}

The setup in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} reduces the statistical question to a worst-case mean-squared-error problem over finite-alphabet overlap classes. The main theorem identifies the rate in the fixed-interior case \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), for large enough \(n\) and within the calibrated growth range \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\). For each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) under the calibrated range, the nonparametric term is \(d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)\), reflecting the cost of binary outcomes and unrestricted covariate heterogeneity in this finite-alphabet benchmark. The lower-bound scale is imported from the fixed-sample construction of \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}; this paper proves the matching computable hybrid upper bound.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}[Fixed-Interior Minimax Rate]*
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\). Then there exist constants \(a_\epsilon,\rho_\epsilon,C_\epsilon>0\) and an integer \(N_\epsilon<\infty\) such that the following hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sharp sandwich.)} For every \(n,d\), every single-observation law \(P\), and every sample law \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) as in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}, if \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), and
\[
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n,
\]
then, with \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) as in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} and \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) as in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle},
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_Q\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(Q)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Parametric window.)} For every \(K>0\), there exists \(C_K>0\) such that, for every \(n,d\), every single-observation law \(P\), and every sample law \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), if \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\),
\[
d\le K\sqrt n\,\log n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n,
\]
then
\[
\frac{a_\epsilon}{n}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\frac{C_K}{n}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Consistency threshold.)} For any sequences \(n_j,d_j\) with \(n_j\to\infty\), eventually \(d_j>0\), and eventually
\[
d_j\le \rho_\epsilon n_j\log n_j,
\]
one has
\[
\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\to0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{d_j}{n_j\log n_j}\to0.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\verificationfootnotetext{\textbf{Formalization scope.} This result uses the published conclusion \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024} (Theorem 2; Appendix C.5; Lemma 4; Appendix D.2). The cited source proof is not formalized here: Lean verifies its use and all remaining steps. If that cited conclusion is false, the portions of this result that depend on it are not certified.}
% CAUSALSMITH-CITED-SCOPE-END thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior
\cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} gives three consequences under the displayed fixed-interior range. First, throughout \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), the minimax risk is of exact order
\[
n^{-1}+\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2 n}.
\]
Second, the parametric rate remains attainable when \(d=O(\sqrt n\log n)\). Third, within the same displayed range, consistent estimation is possible exactly when \(d=o(n\log n)\). Thus the logarithmic factor changes the consistency boundary as well as the constants. The constants in the theorem may depend on the fixed overlap margin, while the estimator in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} uses the same formula across \(\epsilon\).

The next theorem records the hybrid computability statement, the fixed-interior upper envelope, and the separate behavior near the randomized endpoint. The hybrid part uses the same estimator for all fixed-interior overlap classes. For each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), under \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), the selected rule in \cref{obj:def:selected-estimator} is only an oracle-calibrated deterministic comparison between that fixed-interior guarantee and the centered linear estimator from \cref{obj:def:centered-estimator}. Polynomial approximation and adaptation ideas of this form are standard in nonsmooth functional estimation \citep{CaiLow2011, Lepski1999, JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015}, but here they are applied to the four-cell ATE contribution in \cref{obj:def:cell-functional}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid}[Universal Hybrid Envelope]
The following conclusions hold for the hybrid estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} and the centered estimator \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) of \cref{obj:def:centered-estimator}, with minimax risk \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) as in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Computability.)} There is a constant \(K\in\mathbb N\), \(K>0\), such that for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) there is a real-arithmetic program which, on the hybrid count input, returns exactly \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) for every sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), and whose arithmetic/comparison operation count is at most \(K\,d\,M(n)^4\).

\item \textbf{(Fixed-overlap hybrid calibration and envelope.)} For every \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there exist constants \(C_\epsilon>0\), \(\rho_\epsilon>0\), and \(N_\epsilon\in\mathbb N\) such that, for all \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\), \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), and
\[
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n,
\]
the hybrid estimator satisfies
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
For the deterministic selected estimator obtained from the rule that chooses \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) when
\[
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\]
and otherwise chooses \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\), one also has
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\]
and
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}
\left\{
\frac1n+
\min\left[
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right]
\right\}.
\]

\item \textbf{(Centered estimator.)} For every \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\) and every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and \(d>0\),
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2 .
\]

\item \textbf{(Endpoint bracket.)} For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and \(d>0\),
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \frac1n .
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} separates three issues that are often conflated. The hybrid estimator is computable from the finite set of split counts, with operation count polynomial in \(M(n)\) and linear in the alphabet size. For each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), the same hybrid formula attains the fixed-overlap upper rate in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}. Near \(\epsilon=1/2\), however, the centered estimator has the sharper envelope \(n^{-1}+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2\), and at the endpoint \(\epsilon=1/2\) the minimax risk is bracketed at the parametric order \(n^{-1}\). The deterministic selected rule uses the displayed theoretical constants and is therefore a comparison device, not an adaptive estimator for unknown overlap or unknown calibration constants.

\section{Discussion and extensions}

The fixed-interior result extends the usual semiparametric picture. For fixed \(d\), or more generally throughout the parametric window in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}, the minimax risk is of order \(n^{-1}\), in line with the classical efficiency theory for the average treatment effect under overlap \citep{Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003}. Allowing the alphabet to grow freely adds the challenge of estimating many small, poorly sampled conditional contrasts, reflected by the additional term \(d^2/(n^2(\log n)^2)\).

This distinction explains the hybrid estimator's improvement over direct plug-in and weighting estimators in the unrestricted discrete model. A plug-in estimator based on empirical conditional means treats each category as if its arm-specific denominators were reliable. In light categories, those denominators are often zero or very small, and trimming or stabilization introduces bias whose worst-case size depends on the number of categories. Inverse-probability weighting faces the same local denominator problem from the other side: bounded population overlap in \cref{obj:ass:overlap} coexists with unstable empirical treatment-cell counts when category mass is small. The hybrid estimator in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} uses ratios on pilot-certified heavy categories and a polynomial continuation on the light categories. The sharp logarithmic factor in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} confirms that this distinction is essential to the rate.

Doubly robust and targeted estimators are designed to protect first-order inference against nuisance estimation error, and they are central in modern observational causal inference \citep{RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,BangRobins2005,VanDerLaanRubin2006}. Their usual guarantees are most naturally expressed through convergence conditions on nuisance regressions or propensities. The warning of \citet{RobinsRitov1997} that unrestricted high-dimensional adjustment can obstruct uniform model-free inference is therefore especially relevant here. The experiment class in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} imposes no smoothness, sparsity, margin, or ordering condition that would make such nuisance rates available uniformly. The minimax statement isolates that difficulty in a finite-alphabet benchmark: for each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) under the calibrated range, binary outcomes and unrestricted covariate heterogeneity yield the term \(d^2/(n^2(\log n)^2)\).

Higher-order influence-function methods are also closely related in motivation, since they seek to reduce bias from estimating high-dimensional nuisance components \citep{Robins2008,Robins2009,Robins2017,LiuMukherjeeNeweyRobins2017,Kennedy2024}. The present construction is complementary: it replaces unstable light-cell ratios directly by a polynomial approximation to the cell functional in \cref{obj:def:cell-functional}. The rate in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} is achieved by the hybrid estimator, whose light-cell branch uses a degree-\(M(n)\) polynomial approximation and factorial-moment plug-in terms.

This result is related to recent discussions of limited overlap and high-dimensional adjustment \citep{DAmour2021,Balakrishnan2023,JinSyrgkanis2025}. \Cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} fixes \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and shows that many covariate categories with small marginal mass remain difficult under bounded overlap. \Cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} separately characterizes the exact-randomization endpoint \(\epsilon=1/2\), where the minimax risk is bracketed between constants times \(n^{-1}\), uniformly in \(d\).

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

The selected-envelope part of \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} connects these two regimes without claiming a full transition theory or an adaptive implementation. For each fixed interior \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and under the theorem's hybrid calibration range \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), it compares the fixed-overlap hybrid estimator with the centered estimator using the known theoretical constants and gives an upper bound involving
\[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right\}.
\]
This is useful as a theoretical envelope when the assignment mechanism is close to randomized, because the centered estimator improves as \(\epsilon\) approaches \(1/2\). The selector depends on \(\epsilon\) and \(C_\epsilon\), so it should not be read as a data-driven procedure when those quantities are unknown. Moreover, the theorem does not assert that this combined upper envelope is minimax sharp for triangular arrays with \(\epsilon=\epsilon_n\). The fixed-interior lower bound applies for each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and the endpoint bracket applies at \(\epsilon=1/2\); together they leave open the possibility that the exact minimax transition depends on the joint rates at which \(d\), \(n\), and \(1/2-\epsilon_n\) vary.

\section{proofs and auxiliary lemmas}

This appendix records the auxiliary statements used to assemble the upper and lower risk bounds in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} and~\cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid}. The organizing distinction is the one built into \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}: pilot-certified heavy categories are handled by empirical ratios, while pilot-certified light categories are handled by polynomial continuation and factorial moments. The statements below are given as mathematical inputs to the main theorems, with external approximation and minimax ingredients cited where they enter.

The light-cell part is where the logarithmic factor enters the upper bound. Polynomial approximation of nonsmooth distribution functionals has long been used to improve over direct plug-in estimators in large-alphabet problems \citep{Paninski2003,Valiant2011,Valiant2017,Orlitsky2016,JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016,HanJiaoWeissman2015,HanJiaoWeissman2020,WuYang2019}. Here the same principle is applied to the homogeneous four-cell contribution in \cref{obj:def:cell-functional}. The factorial-moment representation in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} turns the polynomial \(P_{M,B}\) into an estimable light-category contribution without dividing by the small empirical treatment counts that make naive ratios unstable.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:light-cell-polynomial}[Light-Cell Polynomial Rate]
Fix an overlap constant \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\). The hybrid estimator is computable in the real-arithmetic model: there is a universal operation-count constant \(K\in\mathbb N\) with \(K>0\) such that, for every \(n\) and \(d\), a real-arithmetic program reading the hybrid count vector returns \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) exactly and uses at most \(K d M(n)^4\) arithmetic and comparison operations.

Moreover, there exist constants \(C_\epsilon,c_\epsilon>0\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that for every sample size \(n\), alphabet size \(d\), law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), and sample law \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) as in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}, if
\[
d\le c_\epsilon n\log n,
\]
then the light-cell contribution satisfies
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\left\{\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac{1}{n}+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial}]
\emph{Computability.}  The program reads the \(8d\) split counts \(N^{(j)}_{aky}\) of \cref{obj:def:split-cell-counts} and evaluates the expression tree obtained by transcribing \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} literally; a ratio with vanishing denominator returns \(0\), which is what the indicators \(\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{ak}>0\}\) in \(\widehat h_k\) prescribe.  We count the cost of the expression tree: one operation for each arithmetic node, and for each comparison node \(\mathrm{br}(c;x,y)\) (``return \(x\) if \(c\le0\), else \(y\)'') one operation plus the cost of \(c\), \(x\) and \(y\), since both alternatives occur in the tree.  A list of \(m\) terms is summed (or multiplied) by folding onto an initial \(0\) (resp.\ \(1\)), costing \(m\) operations.  Reading an input or a constant is free, and a truncated subtraction \(N-\ell\) is the comparison node \(\mathrm{br}(N-\ell;0,N-\ell)\), costing \(3\).  Recall \(M=M(n)\ge2\).
% lean: hybridEstimatorComputable, hybridArithmeticProgram, hybridArithmeticProgram_eval
Building up from the innermost term:
\begin{itemize}
\item a falling factorial \((N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}\) costs \(4r_{ay}\) operations, four per factor \(N^{(1)}_{aky}-\ell\); the four-cell product followed by the division by \((m_1)_{|r|}\) therefore makes one factorial monomial \(\prod_{a,y}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}/(m_1)_{|r|}\) cost \(4|r|+5\) operations;
% lean: operationCount_fallingExpression, operationCount_factorialMonomialExpression
\item the monomials that occur in the expansion of \(P_{M,B}\) are indexed by an arm \(a\), a cell \((a',y')\), and integers \(0\le t\le j\le M-2\), and the corresponding multi-index has \(|r|=j+2\), so one coefficient-times-monomial term costs \(4j+14\) operations;
% lean: multiDegree_hybridTermIndex, operationCount_factorialTerm
\item summing the four cells costs \(16j+60\le 50M\); summing over \(t\le j\) costs at most \(M(50M+1)\le51M^2\); summing over \(j\le M-2\) costs at most \((M-1)(51M^2+1)\le52M^3\); hence each arm costs at most \(52M^3\), and \(\widehat P_k=\)\,(arm \(1\))\,\(-\)\,(arm \(0\)) costs at most \(2\cdot52M^3+1\le105M^4\);

% lean: operationCount_heavyCellExpression, operationCount_selectedCellExpression_le

% lean: operationCount_untruncatedHybridExpression_le, operationCount_clampExpression, operationCount_hybridExpression_le
\end{itemize}
For \(d=0\) the program is the constant \(0\) and costs nothing.  In both cases the program returns \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) exactly, so the first assertion holds with the universal constant \(K=450\).
% lean: hybridEstimatorComputable

\emph{Risk bound.}  Write \(\mathsf r_{n,d}=1/n+d^2/\{n^2(\log n)^2\}\) for the target rate, put

% lean: light_cell_polynomial

All expectations below are under \(P^{\otimes n}\), equivalently under the law of an infinite iid sequence with marginal \(P\), because every integrand depends only on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\); all of them are finite sums over a finite sample space, so every expectation exists and integration is linear and monotone.

If \(n<N_0\), then \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\varnothing\) by \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, so \(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}=0\) and \(\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)=0\); the left-hand side vanishes and \(C_\epsilon\mathsf r_{n,d}\ge0\).

\le 8(e+1)8192^2\,\frac{d\,L^26^{2M}}{n^2}+16\cdot8192^2\,\frac{d^2L^46^{2M}}{n^3},
\]
where the second step used \(B\le8192L/n\), \(M\le L\) and \(1/m_1\le2/n\).
% lean: selectedFixedLightCentered_second_moment_le
The growth inequality \(6^{4M}L^6\le n\) together with \(0<\log n\le L\) converts these into rate terms,
\[
\frac{d\,L^26^{2M}}{n^2}\le\frac{\mathsf r_{n,d}}2,
\qquad
\frac{d^2L^46^{2M}}{n^3}\le\mathsf r_{n,d},
\]
% lean: diagonal_rate_algebra, cross_rate_algebra
so that
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[W_{\mathrm{var}}^2\right]\le K_c\,\mathsf r_{n,d}.
\]
% lean: selectedGenuineLightCentered_rate

\emph{Bound for \(W_{\mathrm{app}}\)} (approximation error).  Fix \(k\in\mathcal G_n\).  The four-cell vector \(q_k\) has total mass \(s(q_k)=p_k\le B/4\le B\), and it lies in the cone \(\mathcal C_\epsilon\) of \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone}: on categories with \(p_k>0\) this is \cref{obj:ass:overlap} written as \(\epsilon s(q_k)\le s_1(q_k)\le(1-\epsilon)s(q_k)\), and on categories with \(p_k=0\) all four coordinates vanish.
% lean: cellVector_mem_overlapCone
The Chebyshev polynomial of \cref{obj:def:chebyshev-polynomial} satisfies \(T_M(1-2x)=1-2M^2x+2M^2x^2G_M(x)\) for the continuation \(G_M\) of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, and \(|T_M|\le1\) on \([-1,1]\), whence the reciprocal-approximation certificate
\[
x\left|1-xG_M(x)\right|\le\frac1{M^2},\qquad 0\le x\le1 .
\]
% lean: chebyshev_gPolynomial_identity, gPolynomial_certificate
Applying it at \(x=s_a(u)/B\in[0,1]\) for each arm \(a\), and using \(t_a(u)\le s_a(u)\) and \(s_a(u)\ge\epsilon s(u)\) from \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone}, the two arms of \(P_{M,B}\) each approximate the corresponding arm of \(\phi\) to within \(B/(\epsilon M^2)\), so
\[
\left|P_{M,B}(u)-\phi(u)\right|\le\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}
\qquad\text{for all }u\in\mathcal C_\epsilon\text{ with }s(u)\le B .
\]
% lean: cellApproxPolynomial_error
Since the selection indicator is bounded by one and \(|\mathcal G_n|\le d\), the triangle inequality gives, pointwise in the sample,
\[
|W_{\mathrm{app}}|\le\sum_{k\in\mathcal G_n}\left|P_{M,B}(q_k)-\phi(q_k)\right|\le d\,\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}.
\]
% lean: selectedGenuineLightBias_abs
Now insert the calibration bounds \(2B\le16384L/n\) and \(M\ge\alpha_0L/2\), and then \(\log n\le L\):
\[
\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}
\le\frac{16384\,L/n}{\epsilon\left(\alpha_0L/2\right)^2}
=\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2\,nL}
\le\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2\,n\log n}.
\]
Squaring \(|W_{\mathrm{app}}|\le 65536\,d/(\epsilon\alpha_0^2n\log n)\) gives, pointwise in the sample,
\[
W_{\mathrm{app}}^2\le\left(\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2}\right)^2\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\le K_b\,\mathsf r_{n,d},
\]
and taking expectations of a pointwise bound by a constant preserves it:
\(E_{P^{\otimes n}}[W_{\mathrm{app}}^2]\le K_b\,\mathsf r_{n,d}\).
% lean: selectedGenuineLightBias_sq_rate

\emph{Bound for \(W_{\mathrm{sel}}\)} (misclassified cells).  Fix \(k\) with \(p_k>B/4\).  Such a category is selected as light only when its pilot count on \(I_0\) falls below \(\lambda_0L\), an event of probability at most \(\exp\{-\lfloor n/2\rfloor p_k/8\}\) by the Bernoulli lower-tail estimate.
% lean: falseLight_selection_probability
The selection indicator is a function of \(I_0\) and the cell error a function of \(I_1\), so they factor, and the unselected second moment is controlled by the coefficient envelope of \(P_{M,B}\) at a category of mass \(p_k\):
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}\left\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
=\Pr\left(k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\right)\,
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\left\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
\le e^{-\lfloor n/2\rfloor p_k/8}\cdot8(e+1)B^2\left\{6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}B\right)^M\right\}^2 .
\]
% lean: falseLight_selection_probability, falseLight_selected_error_sq
The pilot tail now absorbs the coefficient growth, uniformly over the whole range \(p_k>B/4\).  Write \(z=p_k/B\), so that \(z>1/4\) and hence \(1+z\le5z\) and \(6(1+z)\le30z\).  Since \(\log x\le x-1\) for \(x>0\),
\[
\log\left\{6(1+z)\right\}\le6(1+z)-1\le30z ,
\]
and this logarithm is nonnegative because \(6(1+z)\ge1\); combining with \(M\le L\) gives the growth exponent bound
\[
2M\log\left\{6\left(1+\frac{p_k}B\right)\right\}\le60Lz .
\]
For the tail exponent, \(p_k=Bz\) with \(B=4096L/m_1\), and \(m_1=n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor\le2\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), so
\[
\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor\,p_k}8
=512Lz\,\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{m_1}
\ge256Lz .
\]
Subtracting the two displays and using \(z\ge1/4\) and \(L>0\),
\[
-\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor\,p_k}8+2M\log\left\{6\left(1+\frac{p_k}B\right)\right\}
\le-196Lz\le-49L ,
\]
and exponentiating gives the calibration inequality
\[
e^{-\lfloor n/2\rfloor p_k/8}\left\{6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}B\right)^M\right\}^2\le e^{-49L}.
\]
% lean: falseLight_tail_times_growth
Multiplying it by \(8(e+1)B^2\ge0\) therefore yields
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}\left\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
\le 8(e+1)B^2e^{-49L}.
\]
% lean: falseLight_selected_error_sq
By Cauchy--Schwarz, \(W_{\mathrm{sel}}^2\le|\mathcal G_n^{c}|\sum_{k\in\mathcal G_n^{c}}\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\}^2\) pointwise, and \(|\mathcal G_n^{c}|\le d\), so
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[W_{\mathrm{sel}}^2\right]\le d^2\cdot8(e+1)B^2e^{-49L}.
\]
% lean: selectedFalseLightError_second_moment_le
Since \(L\ge1\) and \(e^{L}=en\), we have \(e^{-49L}\le e^{-L}\le1/n\); with \(B\le8192L/n\) this yields
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[W_{\mathrm{sel}}^2\right]\le K_f\,\frac{d^2L^2}{n^3}\le K_f\,\mathsf r_{n,d},
\]
the last step because \(L^2(\log n)^2\le L^4\le6^{4M}L^6\le n\).
% lean: selectedFalseLightError_rate

\emph{Assembly.}  From \((W_{\mathrm{var}}-W_{\mathrm{app}})^2+(W_{\mathrm{var}}-W_{\mathrm{sel}})^2+(W_{\mathrm{app}}-W_{\mathrm{sel}})^2\ge0\) one gets the pointwise inequality
\[
(W_{\mathrm{var}}+W_{\mathrm{app}}+W_{\mathrm{sel}})^2\le3\left(W_{\mathrm{var}}^2+W_{\mathrm{app}}^2+W_{\mathrm{sel}}^2\right),
\]
so, taking expectations and adding the three bounds,
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\left\{\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
\le3\left(K_c+K_b+K_f\right)\mathsf r_{n,d}
=C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\},
\]
which is the asserted bound.
% lean: light_cell_polynomial
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial} has two roles. Its computability clause gives an explicit finite algorithm whose operation count is polynomial in the approximation degree after the split counts are formed. Its risk clause controls the stochastic error and approximation bias of the light side at the target scale. The degree \(M(n)\asymp \log n\) is what produces the denominator \((\log n)^2\) in the squared nonparametric term.

The pilot split must classify categories well enough that the two estimators are used on their intended mass ranges. The next statement gives the required sandwich: pilot-heavy categories have population mass above a lower threshold, while pilot-light categories have population mass below an upper threshold, except on an event whose probability is polynomially small.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:pilot-sandwich}[Pilot Sandwich Bound]
Let \(L=\log(en)\). For a sample satisfying \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} with single-observation law \(P\), let \(\widehat{\mathcal H}\) be the pilot heavy set at threshold \(t\), let \(\widehat{\mathcal L}=\widehat{\mathcal H}^{c}\), and write \(m_0=|I_0|\), \(B_-=tL/(2m_0)\), and \(B_+=2tL/m_0\).

For every \(K>0\) and \(c>0\), there exist constants \(t_0>0\), \(C>0\), and an integer cutoff \(N\) such that the following holds. If
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Threshold.)} \(t\ge t_0\);
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\ge N\);
\item \textbf{(Dimension.)} \(d\) is a nonnegative integer;
\item \textbf{(Law.)} \(P\) is a law on the finite observation alphabet \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\);
\item \textbf{(Growth.)} \(d\le c\,n\,L\);
\item \textbf{(Sampling.)} the sample law satisfies \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} with single-observation law \(P\),
\end{itemize}
then
\[
\Pr\!\left(\widehat{\mathcal H}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge B_-\}\ \text{or}\ \widehat{\mathcal L}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\le B_+\}\right)
\le C n^{-K}.
\]

Moreover, for every \(c>0\), there exist \(C>0\) and an integer cutoff \(N\) such that the following holds with \(t=256\). If
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\ge N\);
\item \textbf{(Dimension.)} \(d\) is a nonnegative integer;
\item \textbf{(Law.)} \(P\) is a law on the finite observation alphabet \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\);
\item \textbf{(Growth.)} \(d\le c\,n\,L\);
\item \textbf{(Sampling.)} the sample law satisfies \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} with single-observation law \(P\),
\end{itemize}
then
\[
\Pr\!\left(\widehat{\mathcal H}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge 256L/(2m_0)\}\ \text{or}\ \widehat{\mathcal L}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\le 512L/m_0\}\right)
\le C n^{-4}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:pilot-sandwich}]
Throughout, \(L=\log(en)\), the first split \(I_0\) of \cref{obj:def:balanced-sample-split} has size \(m_0=|I_0|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), and for a category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\) we write, with \(N^{(0)}_{aky}\) as in \cref{obj:def:split-cell-counts},
\[
N_k^{(0)}=\sum_{a,y}N^{(0)}_{aky}=\sum_{i\in I_0}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k\},
\qquad
p_k=P(X=k)=\sum_{a,y}q_{aky},
\]
so that \(N_k^{(0)}\) is the pilot count of category \(k\) and \(p_k\) is its population mass. The pilot heavy set at threshold \(t\) and its complement are
\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}=\left\{k:\lfloor tL\rfloor<N_k^{(0)}\right\},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal L}=\widehat{\mathcal H}^{c}=\left\{k:N_k^{(0)}\le\lfloor tL\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Because \(N_k^{(0)}\) is an integer, \(\lfloor tL\rfloor<N_k^{(0)}\) holds exactly when \(tL<N_k^{(0)}\), and \(N_k^{(0)}\le\lfloor tL\rfloor\) exactly when \(N_k^{(0)}\le tL\); at \(t=\lambda_0=256\) this is the set \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\) of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}. % lean: pilotHeavyAt
Write \(\mathcal B_t\) for the failure event of the statement, that is, the event that
\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge B_-\}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\widehat{\mathcal L}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\le B_+\},
\qquad
B_-=\frac{tL}{2m_0},
\qquad
B_+=\frac{2tL}{m_0}.
\]
% lean: pilotBadEvent

On \(\mathcal B_t\) there is a category \(k\) for which either
\[
p_k<\frac{tL}{2m_0}
\quad\text{and}\quad
tL<N_k^{(0)},
\]
or
\[
\frac{2tL}{m_0}<p_k
\quad\text{and}\quad
N_k^{(0)}\le tL .
\]
Indeed, if the first inclusion fails there is a \(k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}\) with \(p_k<B_-\), and membership in \(\widehat{\mathcal H}\) means \(\lfloor tL\rfloor<N_k^{(0)}\), i.e. \(tL<N_k^{(0)}\); if the second inclusion fails there is a \(k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}\) with \(p_k>B_+\), and membership in \(\widehat{\mathcal L}\) means \(N_k^{(0)}\le\lfloor tL\rfloor\), i.e. \(N_k^{(0)}\le tL\). Consequently
\[
\Pr\left(\mathcal B_t\right)
\le
\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Pr\!\left(p_k<\frac{tL}{2m_0}\ \text{and}\ tL<N_k^{(0)}\right)
+
\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Pr\!\left(\frac{2tL}{m_0}<p_k\ \text{and}\ N_k^{(0)}\le tL\right).
\]
% lean: pilotBadEvent_subset_cellwise

Fix \(k\) and assume \(n\ge2\), so that \(m_0=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\ge1\). Under \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} the sample law is \(P^{\otimes n}\), and the coordinates indexed by \(I_0\) are \(m_0\) independent draws from \(P\); the pilot count is therefore a sum
\[
N_k^{(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_0}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k\}
\]
of \(m_0\) independent copies of the indicator \(\mathbf 1\{X=k\}\), which takes values in \(\{0,1\}\) and has population mean
\[
E_P\!\left[\mathbf 1\{X=k\}\right]=p_k .
\]
% lean: pilot_count_eq_bernoulliCount, categoryIndicator_mean, finProductLaw_eq_map

For such a count the exponential Markov inequality gives, for every level \(\ell\) and every tilt \(s\ge0\),
\[
\Pr\!\left(N_k^{(0)}>\ell\right)\le\exp\left\{-s\ell+m_0p_k(e^{s}-1)\right\},
\]
and, for every tilt \(s\le0\),
\[
\Pr\!\left(N_k^{(0)}\le \ell\right)\le\exp\left\{-s\ell+m_0p_k(e^{s}-1)\right\},
\]
because a single indicator with mean \(p_k\) has moment generating function \(1+p_k(e^{s}-1)\le\exp\{p_k(e^{s}-1)\}\) and the \(m_0\) summands are independent. Taking \(s=\log2\) in the first display and \(s=-\log2\) in the second, so that \(e^{s}-1\) equals \(1\) respectively \(-1/2\),
\[
\Pr\!\left(N_k^{(0)}>\ell\right)\le\exp\left\{-\ell\log2+m_0p_k\right\},
\qquad
\Pr\!\left(N_k^{(0)}\le \ell\right)\le\exp\left\{\ell\log2-\frac{m_0p_k}{2}\right\}.
\]
Hence, if \(m_0p_k<\ell/2\), then
\[
\Pr\!\left(N_k^{(0)}>\ell\right)\le\exp\left\{-\ell\left(\log2-\tfrac12\right)\right\},
\]
and if \(2\ell<m_0p_k\), then
\[
\Pr\!\left(N_k^{(0)}\le \ell\right)\le\exp\left\{-\frac{m_0p_k}{8}\right\},
\]
the second because \(2\ell<m_0p_k\), \(m_0p_k\ge0\) and \(\log2<3/4\) give \(\ell\log2\le\tfrac34\max\{\ell,0\}\le\tfrac38m_0p_k\). % lean: bernoulliCount_upper_tail, bernoulliCount_lower_tail

Apply these two bounds at the level \(\ell=tL\), with \(t\ge0\). If \(p_k<tL/(2m_0)\), then multiplying by \(m_0\ge1\) gives \(m_0p_k<tL/2\), so
\[
\Pr\!\left(p_k<\frac{tL}{2m_0}\ \text{and}\ tL<N_k^{(0)}\right)
\le
\Pr\!\left(N_k^{(0)}>tL\right)
\le
\exp\left\{-tL\left(\log2-\tfrac12\right)\right\}.
\]
% lean: pilotCategory_upper_tail
If instead \(p_k>2tL/m_0\), then \(m_0p_k>2tL\), so
\[
\Pr\!\left(\frac{2tL}{m_0}<p_k\ \text{and}\ N_k^{(0)}\le tL\right)
\le
\Pr\!\left(N_k^{(0)}\le tL\right)
\le
\exp\left\{-\frac{m_0p_k}{8}\right\}
\le
\exp\left\{-\frac{tL}{4}\right\},
\]
the last step by monotonicity of the exponential and \(m_0p_k>2tL\). % lean: pilotCategory_lower_tail
For a category \(k\) whose mass violates the stated condition, the corresponding event is empty and its probability is \(0\), so every one of the \(2d\) terms in the previous sum obeys the displayed bound, and for all \(n\ge2\) and \(t\ge0\),
\[
\Pr\left(\mathcal B_t\right)
\le
d\left[\exp\left\{-tL\left(\log2-\tfrac12\right)\right\}+\exp\left\{-\frac{tL}{4}\right\}\right].
\]
% lean: pilotBadEvent_probability

We convert this into a polynomial bound. Let \(\gamma>0\), and suppose \(n\ge1\), \(d\le cnL\), \(t\ge0\), and
\[
\gamma\le t\left(\log2-\tfrac12\right),
\qquad
\gamma\le\frac{t}{4}.
\]
Since \(n\ge1\), the scale \(L=\log(en)=1+\log n\) satisfies
\[
1\le L\le n,
\]
the upper bound because \(\log n\le n-1\). Multiplying the two threshold inequalities by \(L>0\) gives \(tL(\log2-\tfrac12)\ge \gamma L\) and \(tL/4\ge \gamma L\), so both exponential factors are at most \(e^{-\gamma L}\), and therefore
\[
d\left[\exp\left\{-tL\left(\log2-\tfrac12\right)\right\}+\exp\left\{-\frac{tL}{4}\right\}\right]
\le
cnL\cdot2e^{-\gamma L}
\le
2cn^{2}e^{-\gamma L},
\]
using \(d\le cnL\) and then \(L\le n\). Because \(L=\log(en)\),
\[
e^{-\gamma L}=\exp\{-\gamma\log(en)\}=e^{-\gamma}n^{-\gamma},
\]
so that, using \(e^{-\gamma}\le1\),
\[
2cn^{2}e^{-\gamma L}
=
2ce^{-\gamma}n^{2-\gamma}
\le
2cn^{2-\gamma}.
\]
% lean: exp_neg_mul_logScale, pilot_decay_bound

For the first assertion, let \(K>0\) and \(c>0\) be given. Since \(\log2>0.693\) we have \(\log2-\tfrac12>0\), so we may set
\[
t_0=\max\left\{\frac{K+4}{\log2-1/2},\,4(K+4)\right\},
\qquad
C=2c,
\qquad
N=2,
\]
and then \(t_0>0\) and \(C>0\), because \(K+4>0\) and \(c>0\). Let \(t\ge t_0\), let \(n\ge N=2\), let \(d\le cnL\), and let the sample law satisfy \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} with single-observation law \(P\), so that it equals \(P^{\otimes n}\). Then \(t\ge t_0>0\) and
\[
t\left(\log2-\tfrac12\right)
\ge
\frac{K+4}{\log2-1/2}\left(\log2-\tfrac12\right)
=
K+4,
\qquad
\frac{t}{4}\ge\frac{4(K+4)}{4}=K+4 .
\]
So the last two displays apply with \(\gamma=K+4\), and since \(2-\gamma=-K-2\le-K\) and \(n\ge1\),
\[
\Pr\left(\mathcal B_t\right)
\le
2cn^{-K-2}
\le
2cn^{-K}
=
Cn^{-K}.
\]
This is the first assertion. % lean: pilot_sandwich

For the numerical specialization, let \(c>0\) and take \(t=256\), so that the two threshold inequalities hold outright with \(\gamma=K+4=8\) at \(K=4\):
\[
8\le256\left(\log2-\tfrac12\right),
\qquad
8\le\frac{256}{4}=64,
\]
the first because \(\log2>0.693\) gives \(256(\log2-\tfrac12)>49\). Hence, with \(C=2c\) and \(N=2\), the same two paragraphs give, for every \(n\ge2\) with \(d\le cnL\) and every sample law satisfying \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} with single-observation law \(P\),
\[
\Pr\left(\mathcal B_{256}\right)
\le
2cn^{2-8}
=
2cn^{-6}
\le
2cn^{-4}
=
Cn^{-4},
\]
which is the stated bound at the thresholds \(B_-=256L/(2m_0)\) and \(B_+=512L/m_0\). % lean: pilot_sandwich
\end{proof}

The constants in \cref{obj:lem:pilot-sandwich} are deliberately one-sided. The estimator uses the stated separation to justify ratio bounds on the heavy side and polynomial bounds on the light side. The special threshold \(t=256\) is the calibration used in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.

Once the pilot split supplies that separation, the heavy categories can be treated by ordinary ratio estimation. Overlap controls the arm-specific denominators in population, and the pilot event keeps the selected category masses large enough for the second-split empirical ratios to have the required mean-squared error.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate}[Heavy Cell Rate]
For every \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there exist constants \(C_\epsilon,\rho_\epsilon>0\) and an integer \(N_\epsilon<\infty\) such that the following holds.  For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\), every single-observation law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), and every sample law \(\mu_n\), assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\ge N_\epsilon\).
\item \textbf{(Dimension growth.)} \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\).
\item \textbf{(Experiment class.)} \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\) belongs to \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}.
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
\int \left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2\,d\mu_n
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate}]
Throughout, \(L=\log(en)\), \(m_0=|I_0|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) and \(m_1=|I_1|=n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) are as in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, \(p_k\) is the mass of category \(k\), \(\mu_{ak}=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=k,A=a]\), \(q_k\) is the four-cell vector of category \(k\), and
\[
\pi_{ak}=\Pr\nolimits_P(A=a\mid X=k),
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\pi_{1k}=\pi_k,\quad \pi_{0k}=1-\pi_k .
\]
By \cref{obj:ass:overlap}, \(\pi_{ak}\ge\epsilon\) for both arms \(a\in\{0,1\}\) whenever \(p_k>0\).

\textbf{Step 0: the constants.}
Apply the numerical (\(t=256\), \(K=4\)) part of \cref{obj:lem:pilot-sandwich} with growth constant \(c=1\).  It supplies \(C>0\) and a cutoff \(N_{\mathrm{bad}}\) such that, writing
\[
\mathcal B
=
\left\{\widehat{\mathcal H}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge B_-\}\right\}
\cup
\left\{\widehat{\mathcal L}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\le B_+\}\right\},
\qquad
B_-=\frac{256L}{2m_0},\quad B_+=\frac{512L}{m_0},
\]
for the pilot heavy set at threshold \(256\), one has
\[
\Pr\nolimits_{P^{\otimes n}}(\mathcal B)\le Cn^{-4}
\qquad\text{whenever } n\ge N_{\mathrm{bad}}\text{ and } d\le nL .
\]
Set
\[
\kappa_\epsilon=\frac{16}{\epsilon}+12+\frac1{\epsilon^4},\qquad
C_{\mathrm{bad}}=4C,\qquad
C_\epsilon=4\kappa_\epsilon+C_{\mathrm{bad}},
\]
\[
\rho_\epsilon=1,\qquad
N_\epsilon=\max\{8,N_0,N_{\mathrm{bad}}\},
\]
with \(N_0\) the calibration cutoff of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.  Since \(\epsilon>0\) and \(C>0\), both \(C_\epsilon>0\) and \(\rho_\epsilon>0\).
% lean: pilot_sandwich_256, heavy_bad_pilot_rate, universal_heavy_cell_rate

Fix \(n,d,P\) satisfying the three hypotheses.  Membership of \(P^{\otimes n}\) in \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) gives overlap at \(\epsilon\), so all integrals below are against \(P^{\otimes n}\).  From \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) we get \(n\ge8\), \(n\ge N_0\) and \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{bad}}\); and \(\log n\le L\) turns the dimension hypothesis \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n=n\log n\) into \(d\le nL\), so the pilot bound above is in force.  Since \(n\ge 8\),
\[
m_1\ge \frac n2\ge 4,\qquad 2m_0\le n,\qquad 4(m_1-2)\ge n,\qquad L=1+\log n>0 .
\]
Abbreviate the heavy error by
\[
\mathcal D=\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k),
\]
and split the integral over \(\mathcal B\) and its complement,
\[
\int\mathcal D^2\,dP^{\otimes n}
=
\int_{\mathcal B}\mathcal D^2\,dP^{\otimes n}
+
\int_{\mathcal B^c}\mathcal D^2\,dP^{\otimes n}.
\]
% lean: integral_add_compl, splitSize_one_eq, splitSize_zero_eq, logScale

\textbf{Step 1: the pilot-bad event.}
Both \(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}\) and its target lie in \([-1,1]\).  For the estimator, each of the two empirical arm ratios in \(\widehat h_k\) lies in \([0,1]\), being a count divided by a larger count (or \(0\) when the arm count vanishes), so their difference has modulus at most one and
\[
|\widehat h_k|\le \frac{N^{(1)}_k}{m_1},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
|\widehat T_{\mathcal H}|
\le\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\frac{N^{(1)}_k}{m_1}
\le\sum_{k=1}^d\frac{N^{(1)}_k}{m_1}
=\frac{m_1}{m_1}=1,
\]
the last equality because every index of \(I_1\) is counted in exactly one category.
% lean: empirical_arm_ratio_mem_unitInterval, abs_empiricalRatioCell_le, sum_splitCategoryCount, abs_heavyContribution_le_one

For the target, evaluating \cref{obj:def:cell-functional} at the four cell masses of category \(k\) gives
\[
\phi(q_k)=p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}),
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
|\phi(q_k)|\le p_k ,
\]
because \(\mu_{1k},\mu_{0k}\in[0,1]\); summing and using \(\sum_{k=1}^dp_k=1\) gives \(\left|\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)\right|\le1\).
% lean: cellPhi_cellVector_eq_weighted, abs_cellPhi_cellVector_le_mass_unconditional, sum_cellMass_eq_one, abs_targetHeavy_le_one

Consequently \(|\mathcal D|\le2\), so \(\mathcal D^2\le4\) pointwise and
\[
\int_{\mathcal B}\mathcal D^2\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
4\Pr\nolimits_{P^{\otimes n}}(\mathcal B)
\le
4Cn^{-4}
=
C_{\mathrm{bad}}n^{-4}.
\]
% lean: heavy_component_sq_le_four, heavy_bad_pilot_setIntegral_le, heavy_bad_pilot_rate

\textbf{Step 2: the selected categories are heavy in population off \(\mathcal B\).}
Because \(n\ge N_0\), \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} selects \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n=\{k:N^{(0)}_k>256L\}\), and since the pilot counts \(N^{(0)}_k\) are integers this is the same set as \(\{k:N^{(0)}_k>\lfloor256L\rfloor\}\), i.e.\ exactly the pilot heavy set \(\widehat{\mathcal H}\) at threshold \(t=256\) appearing in \(\mathcal B\).  Hence, off \(\mathcal B\),
\[
p_k\ge B_-=\frac{256L}{2m_0}>0
\qquad\text{for every }k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n .
\]
% lean: heavyCells_eq_pilotHeavyAt_of_cutoff_le, heavy_cell_mass_lower_of_good_pilot

\textbf{Step 3: arm decomposition for a deterministic category set.}
For a deterministic \(H\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\) and an arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
\widehat\theta_a(H)=\sum_{k\in H}\frac{N^{(1)}_k}{m_1}\cdot\frac{N^{(1)}_{ak1}}{N^{(1)}_{ak}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{ak}>0\},
\qquad
\bar\theta_a(H)=\sum_{k\in H}\frac{N^{(1)}_k}{m_1}\mu_{ak},
\qquad
\theta_a(H)=\sum_{k\in H}p_k\mu_{ak}.
\]
By the definition of \(\widehat h_k\) in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} and by \(\phi(q_k)=p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})\) from Step 1,
\[
\sum_{k\in H}\widehat h_k=\widehat\theta_1(H)-\widehat\theta_0(H),
\qquad
\sum_{k\in H}\phi(q_k)=\theta_1(H)-\theta_0(H),
\]
so \((x_1-x_0)^2\le2x_1^2+2x_0^2\) applied to \(x_a=\widehat\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\) gives, pointwise in the sample,
\[
\left(\sum_{k\in H}\widehat h_k-\sum_{k\in H}\phi(q_k)\right)^2
\le
2\left(\widehat\theta_1(H)-\theta_1(H)\right)^2
+2\left(\widehat\theta_0(H)-\theta_0(H)\right)^2 .
\]
% lean: fixedHeavyArmContribution, fixedEmpiricalMassArm, fixedTargetArm, fixedHeavyContribution_eq_arm_sub, fixedTargetHeavy_eq_arm_sub, fixedHeavy_error_sq_le_two_arms

\textbf{Step 4: the fixed-set arm bound.}
We claim the following \emph{fixed-set arm bound}: for every deterministic \(H\) with \(p_k\ge B_-\) for all \(k\in H\), and every \(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\int\left(\widehat\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\right)^2dP^{\otimes n}
\le
\frac{8\sum_{k\in H}p_k}{m_1\epsilon}
+\frac{6}{m_1}
+4\left(\frac{|H|}{u_n^2B_-}\right)^2,
\qquad
u_n=\frac{m_1-2}{2}\,\epsilon .
\]
Splitting about \(\bar\theta_a(H)\) with \((x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\),
\[
\left(\widehat\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\right)^2
\le
2\left(\widehat\theta_a(H)-\bar\theta_a(H)\right)^2
+2\left(\bar\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\right)^2 .
\]
% lean: fixedHeavyArm_error_sq_le_noise_mass

\emph{(4a) The empirical-mass term.}  Put \(g_{a,H}(O)=\mu_{aX}\mathbf1\{X\in H\}\), a function of one observation with values in \([0,1]\).  Grouping the observations of \(I_1\) by category,
\[
\bar\theta_a(H)=\frac1{m_1}\sum_{i\in I_1}g_{a,H}(O_i),
\qquad
\theta_a(H)=\mathbb E_P\left[g_{a,H}(O)\right],
\]
so \(\bar\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\) is the centered sample mean of an iid bounded score over the \(m_1\) observations of \(I_1\), whence
\[
\int\left(\bar\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\right)^2dP^{\otimes n}
\le
\frac{\mathbb E_P\left[g_{a,H}(O)^2\right]}{m_1}
\le
\frac1{m_1},
\]
the second inequality because \(0\le g_{a,H}\le1\).
% lean: fixedMassScore, fixedMassScore_eq, fixedMassScore_mem_unitInterval, integral_fixedMassScore_eq, fixedEmpiricalMassArm_sub_target_eq_score, integral_fixedEmpiricalMassArm_sub_target_sq_le, iid_mean_sq_le_fintype

\emph{(4b) Exact decomposition of the ratio noise.}  Set
\[
\eta_{ak}(O)=\mathbf1\{X=k,\,A=a\}\left(Y-\mu_{ak}\right),
\qquad
w_{ak}=\frac{N^{(1)}_k}{N^{(1)}_{ak}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{ak}>0\},
\]
and
\[
R_a(H)=\sum_{k\in H}w_{ak}\sum_{i\in I_1}\eta_{ak}(O_i),
\qquad
\Delta_a(H)=\sum_{k\in H}\mu_{ak}N^{(1)}_k\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{ak}=0\}.
\]
Since \(\sum_{i\in I_1}\eta_{ak}(O_i)=N^{(1)}_{ak1}-\mu_{ak}N^{(1)}_{ak}\), a term-by-term comparison of the two cases \(N^{(1)}_{ak}>0\) and \(N^{(1)}_{ak}=0\) gives the pointwise identity
\[
\widehat\theta_a(H)-\bar\theta_a(H)
=
\frac{R_a(H)-\Delta_a(H)}{m_1},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\left(\widehat\theta_a(H)-\bar\theta_a(H)\right)^2
\le
\frac{2R_a(H)^2}{m_1^2}+\frac{2\Delta_a(H)^2}{m_1^2}.
\]
% lean: fixedRatioResidual, tupleRatioCoeff, armOutcomeResidual, fixedMissingOutcomeBias, missingIndexCount, fixedHeavyArm_noise_decomposition

\emph{(4c) The residual term.}  All cross terms in the expansion of \(R_a(H)^2\) integrate to zero: the weights \(w_{ak}\) depend on the sample only through the labels \((X_i,A_i)_{i\in I_1}\), and conditionally on those labels the residuals \(\eta_{ak}(O_i)\) are independent across \(i\) with mean zero, while for \(k\ne l\) the product \(\eta_{ak}(O_i)\eta_{al}(O_i)\) vanishes identically because one observation lies in one category.  Only the diagonal survives, and \(|Y-\mu_{ak}|\le1\) gives \(\eta_{ak}(O_i)^2\le\mathbf1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\}\), so that
\[
\sum_{i\in I_1}w_{ak}^2\,\eta_{ak}(O_i)^2
\le
w_{ak}^2N^{(1)}_{ak}
=
\frac{\left(N^{(1)}_k\right)^2}{N^{(1)}_{ak}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{ak}>0\}.
\]
The nested-count second-moment identity for a multinomial category and its nested arm, evaluated with the category probability \(p_k\) and arm probability \(\pi_{ak}\) and then bounded using \(\pi_{ak}\ge\epsilon\) from \cref{obj:ass:overlap}, yields
\[
\int\frac{\left(N^{(1)}_k\right)^2}{N^{(1)}_{ak}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{ak}>0\}\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
\frac{2m_1p_k}{\pi_{ak}}
\le
\frac{2m_1p_k}{\epsilon},
\]
which requires \(p_k>0\) and is available here since \(p_k\ge B_->0\) on \(H\).  Summing over \(k\in H\),
\[
\int R_a(H)^2\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
\frac{2m_1}{\epsilon}\sum_{k\in H}p_k .
\]
% lean: integral_fixedRatioResidual_sq_eq_diagonal, integral_ratioResidual_diagonal_le, ate_nested_count_ratio_integral_le, integral_fixedRatioResidual_sq_le

\emph{(4d) The missing-arm term.}  Since \(0\le\mu_{ak}\le1\),
\[
|\Delta_a(H)|\le\mathcal N_a(H):=\sum_{k\in H}N^{(1)}_k\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{ak}=0\},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\Delta_a(H)^2\le\mathcal N_a(H)^2 .
\]
The second moment of \(\mathcal N_a(H)\) is an exact multinomial expression in the masses \(p_k\) and arm probabilities \(\pi_{ak}\): its diagonal part is
\(m_1p_k(1-\pi_{ak})(1-p_k\pi_{ak})^{m_1-1}+m_1(m_1-1)\left\{p_k(1-\pi_{ak})\right\}^2(1-p_k\pi_{ak})^{m_1-2}\)
and its \(k\ne l\) part is
\(m_1(m_1-1)p_k(1-\pi_{ak})p_l(1-\pi_{al})(1-p_k\pi_{ak}-p_l\pi_{al})^{m_1-2}\).
Bounding \(1-\pi_{ak}\le1\), \(m_1(m_1-1)\le m_1^2\), and \((1-p_k\pi_{ak})^{m_1-2}\le e^{-(m_1-2)\epsilon p_k}\) by \(\pi_{ak}\ge\epsilon\), and writing \(f_k=p_ke^{-u_np_k}\) with \(u_n=(m_1-2)\epsilon/2\), the diagonal is at most \(m_1p_k+m_1^2f_k^2\) and each cross term at most \(m_1^2f_kf_l\), so that
\[
\int\mathcal N_a(H)^2\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
m_1\sum_{k\in H}p_k+m_1^2\left(\sum_{k\in H}f_k\right)^2
\le
m_1+m_1^2\left(\sum_{k\in H}f_k\right)^2 ,
\]
using \(\sum_{k\in H}p_k\le1\).  Finally \(s^2\le e^{s}\) for \(s\ge0\), applied with \(s=u_np_k\), gives \(f_k=p_ke^{-u_np_k}\le1/(u_n^2p_k)\le1/(u_n^2B_-)\) for each \(k\in H\), whence
\[
\sum_{k\in H}f_k\le\frac{|H|}{u_n^2B_-},
\qquad\text{and so}\qquad
\int\Delta_a(H)^2\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
m_1+m_1^2\left(\frac{|H|}{u_n^2B_-}\right)^2 .
\]
% lean: fixedMissingCount, abs_fixedMissingOutcomeBias_le, fixedMissingOutcomeBias_sq_le, integral_fixedMissingCount_sq_eq, missing_diag_envelope, missing_cross_envelope, sum_mass_mul_exp_neg_mul_le, integral_fixedMissingCount_sq_le

\emph{(4e) Assembling the fixed-set arm bound.}  Integrating the pointwise bound of (4b) and inserting (4c) and (4d),
\begin{align*}
\int\left(\widehat\theta_a(H)-\bar\theta_a(H)\right)^2dP^{\otimes n}
&\le
\frac{2}{m_1^2}\cdot\frac{2m_1}{\epsilon}\sum_{k\in H}p_k
+\frac{2}{m_1^2}\left\{m_1+m_1^2\left(\frac{|H|}{u_n^2B_-}\right)^2\right\}\\
&=
\frac{4\sum_{k\in H}p_k}{m_1\epsilon}+\frac2{m_1}+2\left(\frac{|H|}{u_n^2B_-}\right)^2 .
\end{align*}
Doubling this, adding twice the bound \(1/m_1\) of (4a), and collecting terms gives exactly the fixed-set arm bound.  (Note \(m_1\ge4>2\), so \(u_n>0\).)
% lean: integral_fixedHeavyArm_noise_sq_le, integral_fixedHeavyArm_error_sq_le

\textbf{Step 5: the fixed-set envelope is at the target rate.}
Write
\[
V=\kappa_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
We claim that the right-hand side of the fixed-set arm bound is at most \(V\) for every \(H\).  Indeed:
\[
\frac{8\sum_{k\in H}p_k}{m_1\epsilon}\le\frac{8}{m_1\epsilon}\le\frac{16}{\epsilon n},
\qquad
\frac{6}{m_1}\le\frac{12}{n},
\]
using \(\sum_{k\in H}p_k\le1\) and \(m_1\ge n/2\).  For the third term, \(2m_0\le n\) gives
\[
B_-=\frac{256L}{2m_0}\ge\frac{256L}{n},
\qquad
u_n=\frac{m_1-2}{2}\epsilon\ge\frac{n\epsilon}{8}
\quad\text{(from }4(m_1-2)\ge n\text{)},
\]
hence \(u_n^2B_-\ge(n\epsilon/8)^2\cdot256L/n=4n\epsilon^2L\).  With \(|H|\le d\) and \(0<\log n\le L\),
\[
4\left(\frac{|H|}{u_n^2B_-}\right)^2
\le
4\left(\frac{d}{4n\epsilon^2L}\right)^2
=
\frac{1}{4\epsilon^4}\cdot\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}
\le
\frac1{\epsilon^4}\cdot\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}.
\]
Adding the three bounds,
\[
\frac{8\sum_{k\in H}p_k}{m_1\epsilon}+\frac{6}{m_1}+4\left(\frac{|H|}{u_n^2B_-}\right)^2
\le
\left(\frac{16}\epsilon+12\right)\frac1n+\frac1{\epsilon^4}\cdot\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\le
\kappa_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}=V,
\]
the last step because both \(16/\epsilon+12\) and \(1/\epsilon^4\) are at most \(\kappa_\epsilon\) and both rate terms are nonnegative.  In particular \(V\ge0\).
% lean: heavy_fixed_envelope_rate, minimaxRate

\textbf{Step 6: transfer to the random heavy set.}
Fix an arm \(a\) and let \(\mathfrak E=\{H\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}:p_k\ge B_-\text{ for all }k\in H\}\).  By Step 2, off \(\mathcal B\) the realized set \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\) belongs to \(\mathfrak E\), so pointwise
\[
\mathbf1_{\mathcal B^c}\left(\widehat\theta_a(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)-\theta_a(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)\right)^2
\le
\sum_{H\in\mathfrak E}\mathbf1\{\widehat{\mathcal H}_n=H\}\left(\widehat\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\right)^2 .
\]
For each fixed \(H\), the event \(\{\widehat{\mathcal H}_n=H\}\) is a function of the pilot observations \((O_i)_{i\in I_0}\) alone, while \(\left(\widehat\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\right)^2\) is a function of the estimation observations \((O_i)_{i\in I_1}\) alone; under \(P^{\otimes n}\) these two disjoint index blocks are independent, so the fiber integral factorizes,
\[
\int\mathbf1\{\widehat{\mathcal H}_n=H\}\left(\widehat\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\right)^2dP^{\otimes n}
=
\Pr\nolimits_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n=H\right)
\int\left(\widehat\theta_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\right)^2dP^{\otimes n}.
\]
Every \(H\in\mathfrak E\) satisfies the eligibility hypothesis of Step 4, so by Steps 4 and 5 the second factor is at most \(V\).  Summing over \(H\in\mathfrak E\) and using \(\sum_{H\in\mathfrak E}\Pr(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n=H)\le1\) together with \(V\ge0\),
\[
\int_{\mathcal B^c}\left(\widehat\theta_a(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)-\theta_a(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)\right)^2dP^{\otimes n}\le V
\qquad (a\in\{0,1\}).
\]
% lean: infinite_fixedHeavyArm_fiber_factorization, oneShot_integral_estimate_restrict_pilot, heavy_good_arm_setIntegral_le

\textbf{Step 7: the pilot-good integral.}
Applying the pointwise bound of Step 3 with the random set \(H=\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\) — legitimate because that bound holds for every realized set — and integrating over \(\mathcal B^c\),
\[
\int_{\mathcal B^c}\mathcal D^2\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
2\int_{\mathcal B^c}\left(\widehat\theta_1(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)-\theta_1(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)\right)^2dP^{\otimes n}
+2\int_{\mathcal B^c}\left(\widehat\theta_0(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)-\theta_0(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)\right)^2dP^{\otimes n}
\le
4V .
\]
% lean: fixedHeavy_error_sq_le_two_arms, heavyContribution_eq_fixed, targetHeavy_eq_fixed

\textbf{Step 8: conclusion.}
Since \(n\ge1\),
\[
n^{-4}\le n^{-1}\le\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},
\]
so combining Step 1 with Step 7 in the decomposition of Step 0,
\[
\int\mathcal D^2\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
C_{\mathrm{bad}}n^{-4}+4V
\le
\left(C_{\mathrm{bad}}+4\kappa_\epsilon\right)\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
=
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\},
\]
which is the asserted bound.
% lean: universal_heavy_cell_rate
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate} matches the light-cell rate from \cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial}. Combining the two controls the untruncated sum \(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\); the final truncation in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} cannot increase squared error against an ATE lying in the binary-outcome range.

The lower bound is transferred from the one-sided subclass in which only treated conditional means vary. This subclass is contained in the observational ATE experiment class and identifies the same hard functional through \(\tau(P)\). The external minimax input is the fixed-sample large-alphabet lower bound of \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}; standard lower-bound comparisons then pass it to \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}, in the same spirit as classical minimax reductions \citep{Tsybakov2009,CaiLow2011}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer}[ATE Minimax Lower Bound]*
For any \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), let \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) denote the minimax mean-squared-error risk of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}. There exist constants \(a_\epsilon,b_\epsilon\in\mathbb R\) and a natural-number cutoff \(N_\epsilon\) such that \(a_\epsilon>0\), \(b_\epsilon>0\), and for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), and
\[
d\le b_\epsilon n\log n,
\]
one has
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\ge
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac{1}{n}+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

The cited source proof is not formalized here: Lean verifies its use and all remaining steps. If that cited conclusion is false, the portions of this result that depend on it are not certified.}
% CAUSALSMITH-CITED-SCOPE-END lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer
\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer}]
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and write \(\mathcal O_n:=(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\})^n\) for the finite sample space, so that a measurable estimator is any map \(\widehat\tau:\mathcal O_n\to\mathbb R\) and \(E_P\) denotes expectation under \(P^{\otimes n}\).  A supremum over an empty index set is read as \(0\), the convention already in force in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.

\textbf{Step 1: the control-zero subclass and its treated-arm risk.}  Define the subclass of laws whose control regression vanishes in every category,
\[
\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}
:=
\left\{P: P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon},\ \mu_{0k}=0\ \text{for every }k=1,\ldots,d\right\},
\]
% lean: ControlZeroClass, ControlZeroLaw
the treated-arm functional
\[
\psi_1(P):=\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k\mu_{1k},
\]
% lean: treatedFunctional
and the treated-arm minimax risk over that subclass,
\[
\mathsf R^{(1)}_{n,d,\epsilon}
:=
\inf_{\widehat\tau}\ \sup_{P\in\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}}E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\psi_1(P))^2\right],
\]
% lean: oneArmWorstCaseMSE, oneArmMinimaxRisk
the infimum again ranging over all measurable estimators based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), exactly as in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.  The cited fixed-sample control-zero lower bound \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024} is imported in the following form: there exist constants \(a_\epsilon>0\), \(b_\epsilon>0\) and a cutoff \(N_\epsilon\in\mathbb N\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R^{(1)}_{n,d,\epsilon}
\qquad\text{whenever } d>0,\ n\ge N_\epsilon,\ d\le b_\epsilon n\log n.
\]
% lean: ZengOneArmMinimaxLower, minimaxRate

\textbf{Step 2: a uniform risk envelope.}  For every law \(P\) satisfying \cref{obj:ass:overlap} and every estimator \(\widehat\tau\),
\[
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\left(S(\widehat\tau)+1\right)^2,
\qquad
S(\widehat\tau):=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_n}|\widehat\tau(o)|.
\]
% lean: mse_le_estimator_abs_sum_bound
Indeed, the weighted-regression form of \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} gives \(\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})\) with \(\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k=1\), \(p_k\ge0\) and \(|\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}|\le1\), whence
\[
|\tau(P)|\le 1;
\]
% lean: ateFunctional_eq_weighted_regression, ateFunctional_mem_interval
and \(|\widehat\tau(o)|\le S(\widehat\tau)\) for each \(o\in\mathcal O_n\), since \(S(\widehat\tau)\) is a finite sum of nonnegative terms one of which is \(|\widehat\tau(o)|\).  Therefore \(|\widehat\tau(o)-\tau(P)|\le S(\widehat\tau)+1\) pointwise on \(\mathcal O_n\), and integrating the squared bound against the probability law \(P^{\otimes n}\) gives the display.  Two consequences are used below: for each fixed \(\widehat\tau\), both the supremum over \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) and the supremum over \(\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}\) are taken over sets of reals bounded above by \((S(\widehat\tau)+1)^2\), so both are genuine finite suprema.

\textbf{Step 3: the target identity on the subclass.}  Let \(P\in\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}\).  Then \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:overlap}, so the weighted-regression form of \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} applies, and \(\mu_{0k}=0\) for every \(k\) gives
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})=\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k\mu_{1k}=\psi_1(P).
\]
% lean: ateFunctional_eq_treated_on_controlZero

\textbf{Step 4: the risk comparison.}  We claim
\[
\mathsf R^{(1)}_{n,d,\epsilon}\le\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}.
\]
% lean: oneArmMinimaxRisk_le_minimaxRisk
Fix a measurable estimator \(\widehat\tau\) and split on whether the subclass is inhabited.

If no law belongs to \(\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}\), then \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}}E_P[(\widehat\tau-\psi_1(P))^2]=0\) by the empty-supremum convention, and the right-hand worst-case risk is nonnegative: if \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is likewise empty it too equals \(0\), and otherwise it dominates the single nonnegative value \(E_{P}[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2]\ge0\) attained at any member \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), that domination being legitimate because Step 2 makes the supremum finite.

If instead \(\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}\) contains a law, take any \(P\in\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}\).  By definition of \(\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}\) we have \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), and by Step 3 the treated-arm loss of \(\widehat\tau\) at \(P\) is its ATE loss, so
\[
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\psi_1(P))^2\right]
=
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\sup_{Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}E_Q\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(Q))^2\right],
\]
the last step again using the finiteness supplied by Step 2.  Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal D_{n,d,\epsilon}}E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\psi_1(P))^2\right]
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right].
\]
Both cases give this inequality for every measurable \(\widehat\tau\).  Moreover the left-hand side is bounded below by \(0\) uniformly in \(\widehat\tau\): it is \(0\) when the subclass is empty, and otherwise it dominates the nonnegative squared-error risk at any one member of the subclass, whose supremum is finite by Step 2.  Hence the infima over measurable estimators may be compared termwise, which is the claimed inequality \(\mathsf R^{(1)}_{n,d,\epsilon}\le\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\).

\textbf{Step 5: conclusion.}  Let \(a_\epsilon,b_\epsilon,N_\epsilon\) be the constants of Step 1 and let \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) satisfy \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le b_\epsilon n\log n\).  Chaining the cited bound of Step 1 with the comparison of Step 4,
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R^{(1)}_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon},
\]
which is the asserted lower bound, with the same constants \(a_\epsilon>0\), \(b_\epsilon>0\) and cutoff \(N_\epsilon\).
% lean: ate_lower_bound_transfer
\end{proof}
\cref{obj:lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer} is the only auxiliary statement in this section whose substantive lower-bound scale is imported from the published large-alphabet ATE construction \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. Its conclusion is stated directly for the minimax risk in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}, so it can be paired with the hybrid upper bound without changing the experiment class.

The remaining statements describe the randomized endpoint and the envelope near it. At exact randomization, treatment assignment carries no category-specific imbalance, and the centered linear estimator in \cref{obj:def:centered-estimator} becomes the natural comparison estimator.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:near-randomization-linear-upper}[Centered Linear MSE Bound]
For any \(n,d\in\mathbb N\), any \(\epsilon\in\mathbb R\), any single-observation law \(P\), and any sample law \(\mu_n\), suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n>0\).
\item \textbf{(Experiment class.)} \(\mu_n\) belongs to the experiment class \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) generated by \(P\), as in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}.
\end{itemize}
Define
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
2(2A_i-1)\left(Y_i-\frac12\right).
\]
Then, with \(\tau(P)\) as in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional},
\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_n}\left[\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper}]
Membership of \(\mu_n\) in \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) supplies \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\), the range restriction \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\), and the overlap bounds of \cref{obj:ass:overlap} for \(P\); these are used throughout.
% lean: ExperimentClass

Write
\[
S(O)=2(2A-1)\left(Y-\frac12\right),
\qquad
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nS(O_i).
\]
% lean: centeredUnitScore, centeredEstimator_eq_scoreMean
Since \(A\) and \(Y\) are binary, \(S(O)=1\) when \(A=Y\) and \(S(O)=-1\) when \(A\neq Y\); in particular \(S(O)^2=1\) pointwise, so
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P(S)
=
E_P\!\left[S(O)^2\right]-\left\{E_PS(O)\right\}^2
=
1-\left\{E_PS(O)\right\}^2
\le
1 .
\]
% lean: centeredUnitScore_sq, centeredUnitScore_variance_le_one
Under \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\) the observations \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are iid, so the variance of their average is the one-observation variance divided by \(n\):
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_n}(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}})
=
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(S)}{n}
\le
\frac1n .
\]
% lean: centeredEstimator_variance_le

It remains to bound the bias.  Evaluating \(S\) on the four atoms of a category, its value is \(+1\) at \((a,y)\in\{(1,1),(0,0)\}\) and \(-1\) at \((a,y)\in\{(1,0),(0,1)\}\), so summing the atom masses against these values,
\[
E_PS(O)
=
\sum_{k=1}^d\left(q_{1k1}-q_{1k0}+q_{0k0}-q_{0k1}\right).
\]
% lean: centeredUnitScore_mean

Fix \(k\) with \(p_k>0\).  Overlap together with \(\epsilon>0\) gives \(0<\epsilon\le\pi_k\le1-\epsilon<1\), so both arm masses \(q_{1k0}+q_{1k1}=\pi_kp_k\) and \(q_{0k0}+q_{0k1}=(1-\pi_k)p_k\) are strictly positive and \(\mu_{1k},\mu_{0k}\) are the genuine conditional means of \(Y\) in the two arms.  Consequently
\[
q_{1k1}=\mu_{1k}\pi_kp_k,
\quad
q_{1k0}=(1-\mu_{1k})\pi_kp_k,
\quad
q_{0k1}=\mu_{0k}(1-\pi_k)p_k,
\quad
q_{0k0}=(1-\mu_{0k})(1-\pi_k)p_k,
\]
and substituting these four expressions and rearranging yields the exact conditional-bias identity
\[
q_{1k1}-q_{1k0}+q_{0k0}-q_{0k1}
=
p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})
+2\left(\pi_k-\tfrac12\right)p_k(\mu_{1k}+\mu_{0k}-1).
\]
% lean: centeredScore_category_identity
If instead \(p_k=0\), then the four nonnegative masses \(q_{0k0},q_{0k1},q_{1k0},q_{1k1}\) sum to \(0\) and therefore all vanish, so the left-hand side above is \(0\); the right-hand side is \(0\) as well, both of its terms carrying the factor \(p_k=0\).  The displayed identity therefore holds for every \(k\).
% lean: jointMass_eq_zero_of_cellMass_eq_zero

Summing the identity over \(k\) and subtracting \(\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^dp_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})\) from \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}, which is the form the ATE functional takes under \cref{obj:ass:overlap}, gives
\[
E_PS(O)-\tau(P)
=
\sum_{k=1}^d2\left(\pi_k-\tfrac12\right)p_k(\mu_{1k}+\mu_{0k}-1).
\]
% lean: centeredUnitScore_bias_identity, ateFunctional_eq_weighted_regression

For a category with \(p_k>0\), the overlap bounds \(\epsilon\le\pi_k\le1-\epsilon\) give \(|\pi_k-\tfrac12|\le\tfrac12-\epsilon\), while \(\mu_{1k},\mu_{0k}\in[0,1]\) gives \(|\mu_{1k}+\mu_{0k}-1|\le1\); since \(\tfrac12-\epsilon\ge0\) and \(p_k\ge0\), multiplying these bounds yields
\[
\left|2\left(\pi_k-\tfrac12\right)p_k(\mu_{1k}+\mu_{0k}-1)\right|
\le
2\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)p_k ,
\]
and for \(p_k=0\) both sides vanish, so the bound holds for every \(k\).  The triangle inequality applied to the previous display, followed by \(\sum_{k=1}^dp_k=1\) (the categories partition the sample space), gives
\[
\left|E_PS(O)-\tau(P)\right|
\le
\sum_{k=1}^d2\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)p_k
=
2\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right).
\]
% lean: centeredUnitScore_bias_bound, cellMass_sum

Finally, \(n>0\) and \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\) give
\[
E_{\mu_n}\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nE_PS(O_i)
=
E_PS(O),
\]
% lean: centeredEstimator_mean
so the bias of \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) is exactly the quantity just bounded, and since \(2(\tfrac12-\epsilon)\ge0\) the bound squares to
\[
\left\{E_{\mu_n}\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\right\}^2
\le
4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2 .
\]
The mean-squared error splits as
\[
E_{\mu_n}\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
=
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_n}(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}})
+\left\{E_{\mu_n}\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\right\}^2 ,
\]
% lean: mse_eq_variance_add_sq_bias
and adding the variance bound to the squared-bias bound gives
\[
E_{\mu_n}\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2 .
\]
% lean: near_randomization_linear_upper
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} quantifies the cost of using the centered estimator away from exact randomization. The variance term is parametric, while the bias term is controlled by the distance from \(\epsilon=1/2\). This bound complements the fixed-interior hybrid analysis in overlap classes close to randomized treatment.

A matching parametric lower bound at the endpoint follows already from a one-category binary-outcome subproblem.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower}[Bernoulli Randomization Lower Bound]
For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and \(d>0\), the minimax mean-squared-error risk \(\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\) of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} satisfies
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\ge \frac{1}{100n}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower}]
Fix \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and \(d>0\), and put
\[
\delta=\frac{2/5}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Since \(\sqrt n\ge1\),
\[
0\le\delta\le\frac25,
\qquad
\delta^2=\frac{4}{25n},
\qquad
\frac12+\delta\le\frac{9}{10}\le1 .
\]
% lean: one_category_bernoulli_lower (hdelta0, hdeltasq, hdeltaU)

\textbf{Step 1: the two competing laws.}
Define two laws \(P_0,P_1\in\mathsf{Prob}(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)\) as in \cref{obj:def:finite-alphabet-probability-laws} by prescribing, for every category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), the four-cell vector \(q_k=(q_{0k0},q_{0k1},q_{1k0},q_{1k1})\) of \cref{obj:def:finite-alphabet-probability-laws}, whose entries are the cell masses \(q_{aky}\) of \((X,A,Y)=(k,a,y)\) listed in the treatment--outcome order \((a,y)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\):
\[
q_k(P_0)=\left(\frac{1}{4d},\ \frac{1}{4d},\ \frac{1}{4d},\ \frac{1}{4d}\right),
\qquad
q_k(P_1)=\left(\frac{1}{4d},\ \frac{1}{4d},\ \frac{1}{2d}\left(\frac12-\delta\right),\ \frac{1}{2d}\left(\frac12+\delta\right)\right).
\]
Both prescriptions are legitimate laws: all coordinates are nonnegative because \(0\le\delta\le2/5<1/2\), and in each case the \(4d\) masses sum to
\(d\cdot\{1/(4d)+1/(4d)+1/(2d)\}=1\).
Equivalently, under both laws \(X\) is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\) and \(A\mid X=k\sim\mathsf{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:bernoulli-law}; the control arm has \(Y\mid A=0,X=k\sim\mathsf{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) under both laws, while the treated arm has \(Y\mid A=1,X=k\sim\mathsf{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) under \(P_0\) and \(Y\mid A=1,X=k\sim\mathsf{Bernoulli}(1/2+\delta)\) under \(P_1\).
% lean: endpointParametricLaw, endpointParametricPertLaw, endpointParametricLaw_jointMass, endpointParametricPertLaw_jointMass, Parametric.validDGP_null, Parametric.validDGP_pert

\textbf{Step 2: both product laws lie in the class.}
For \(j\in\{0,1\}\) and every \(k\), summing the four displayed coordinates and the two treated coordinates gives
\[
p_k=\sum_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}q_{aky}(P_j)=\frac1d>0,
\qquad
\pi_k=\frac{q_{1k0}(P_j)+q_{1k1}(P_j)}{p_k}=\frac{1/(2d)}{1/d}=\frac12 .
\]
Hence \(1/2\le\pi_k\le1-1/2\) for every category, so each \(P_j\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:overlap} with overlap constant \(\epsilon=1/2\), and therefore
\[
P_0^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2},
\qquad
P_1^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}
\]
in the sense of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}.
% lean: endpointParametricLaw_overlap, endpointParametricPertLaw_overlap, ExperimentClass

\textbf{Step 3: the two estimand values.}
The conditional outcome means of \cref{obj:def:finite-alphabet-probability-laws} are read off the same coordinates: for every \(k\),
\[
\mu_{0k}(P_j)=\frac{q_{0k1}(P_j)}{q_{0k0}(P_j)+q_{0k1}(P_j)}=\frac12\quad(j=0,1),
\qquad
\mu_{1k}(P_0)=\frac{1/(4d)}{1/(2d)}=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1k}(P_1)=\frac{(1/(2d))(1/2+\delta)}{1/(2d)}=\frac12+\delta .
\]
Substituting into the weighted-regression form of \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} and using \(p_k=1/d\),
\[
\tau(P_0)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k\{\mu_{1k}(P_0)-\mu_{0k}(P_0)\}=0,
\qquad
\tau(P_1)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac1d\,\delta=\delta .
\]
% lean: endpointParametricLaw_ate, endpointParametricPertLaw_ate, ateFunctional_eq_weighted_regression

\textbf{Step 4: the two sample laws are statistically indistinguishable.}
For probability laws \(\varrho,\varrho'\) on a common finite alphabet \(\mathcal Z\) with \(\varrho'\) charging every point, write
\[
\chi^2(\varrho\|\varrho')=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\frac{\{\varrho(z)-\varrho'(z)\}^2}{\varrho'(z)},
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(\varrho,\varrho')=\sup_{B\subseteq\mathcal Z}|\varrho(B)-\varrho'(B)| .
\]
The laws \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) differ only in the two treated cells of each category, where the masses differ by \(\pm\delta/(2d)\), and \(P_0\) puts mass \(1/(4d)\) on each of those cells; summing over the \(2d\) treated cells gives the single-observation value
\[
\chi^2(P_1\|P_0)
=2d\cdot\frac{\{\delta/(2d)\}^2}{1/(4d)}
=2\delta^2 .
\]
Because the \(n\) observations are independent and identically distributed under each product law, the quantity \(1+\chi^2\) factorizes over coordinates (\(1+\chi^2(\varrho^{\otimes n}\|{\varrho'}^{\otimes n})=\{1+\chi^2(\varrho\|\varrho')\}^n\), the standard tensorization of the \(\chi^2\)-divergence), and \(1+x\le e^{x}\) gives
\[
1+\chi^2\!\left(P_1^{\otimes n}\,\big\|\,P_0^{\otimes n}\right)
=\left\{1+\chi^2(P_1\|P_0)\right\}^{n}
=\left(1+2\delta^2\right)^{n}
\le
\exp\!\left(2n\delta^2\right).
\]
With \(\delta^2=4/(25n)\) the exponent is calibrated below \(\log 2\):
\[
2n\delta^2=\frac{8}{25}\le\log 2 ,
\]
since \(\log2>0.693>8/25\). Hence \(1+\chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n}\|P_0^{\otimes n})\le2\), that is,
\[
\chi^2\!\left(P_1^{\otimes n}\,\big\|\,P_0^{\otimes n}\right)\le1 ,
\]
and the Cauchy--Schwarz comparison \(\operatorname{TV}(\varrho,\varrho')\le\tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2(\varrho\|\varrho')}\) yields
\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}\right)\le\frac12\sqrt1=\frac12 .
\]
% lean: Parametric.one_add_chiSqDiv_obsPert_obsNull, Parametric.chiSqDiv_productLaw_le_one, Parametric.tvDist_productLaw_le_half

\textbf{Step 5: two-point testing bound.}
Let \(\widehat\tau\) be any measurable estimator based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), as in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}. By Step 3 the two estimand values are separated at radius \(\delta/2\):
\[
2\cdot\frac{\delta}{2}=\delta=\left|\tau(P_0)-\tau(P_1)\right| .
\]
No number is within \(\delta/2\) of both \(\tau(P_0)\) and \(\tau(P_1)\), so the two events \(\{|\widehat\tau-\tau(P_0)|<\delta/2\}\) and \(\{|\widehat\tau-\tau(P_1)|<\delta/2\}\) are disjoint.  Writing \(B_j\) for the \(j\)th of these two events, \(B_1\subseteq B_0^{c}\), so \(P_0^{\otimes n}(B_0^{c})+P_1^{\otimes n}(B_1^{c})\ge 1-P_0^{\otimes n}(B_0)+P_1^{\otimes n}(B_0)\ge 1-\mathrm{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n})\), and the larger of the two miss probabilities is at least half of this; with the bound of Step 4 this gives
\[
\max_{j=0,1}
P_j^{\otimes n}\!\left(\left|\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j)\right|\ge\frac{\delta}{2}\right)
\ge
\frac{1-\operatorname{TV}\!\left(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}\right)}{2}
\ge\frac{1-1/2}{2}
=\frac14 .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.two_point_lower_bound_of_tvDist_le, Causalean.Stat.real_two_point_lower_bound

\textbf{Step 6: from miss probability to mean squared error.}
For each \(j=0,1\), the miss event coincides with
\(\left\{(\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j))^2\ge(\delta/2)^2\right\}\),
and the squared loss is nonnegative, so integrating it over that event gives
\[
\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^2
P_j^{\otimes n}\!\left(\left|\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j)\right|\ge\frac{\delta}{2}\right)
\le
E_{P_j^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j))^2\right].
\]
Multiplying the bound of Step 5 by \((\delta/2)^2\ge0\) and using \(\delta^2=4/(25n)\),
\[
\max_{j=0,1}
E_{P_j^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j))^2\right]
\ge
\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^2\cdot\frac14
=
\frac{\delta^2}{16}
=
\frac{1}{100n}.
\]
% lean: one_category_bernoulli_lower (hmse0, hmse1, htwo)

\textbf{Step 7: passing to the minimax risk.}
By Step 2 both \(P_0^{\otimes n}\) and \(P_1^{\otimes n}\) are members of \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\), and for a fixed estimator the risks over the class are bounded above uniformly, because the sample space is finite and \(|\tau(P)|\le1\) for every law in the class. Hence the worst-case risk of \(\widehat\tau\) dominates each of the two risks in Step 6:
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}}
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right]
\ge
\max_{j=0,1}
E_{P_j^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j))^2\right]
\ge
\frac{1}{100n}.
\]
The estimator \(\widehat\tau\) was an arbitrary measurable estimator, so taking the infimum over all such estimators in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} gives
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\ge\frac{1}{100n},
\]
which is the claim.
% lean: mse_le_estimator_abs_sum_bound, worstCaseMSE, minimaxRisk, one_category_bernoulli_lower

\end{proof}

The one-category reduction in \cref{obj:lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower} removes the large-alphabet feature entirely, leaving the ordinary difficulty of estimating a Bernoulli mean contrast under randomized assignment. Combining it with the centered-estimator upper bound gives the endpoint bracket.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax}[Randomized Endpoint Minimax Risk]
For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and \(d>0\), the minimax mean-squared-error risk of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} at exact randomization satisfies
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \frac{1}{n}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax}]
Fix \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and \(d>0\). The two halves of the bracket are proved separately.

\emph{Lower bound.} The hypotheses \(n>0\) and \(d>0\) fixed here are exactly those of \cref{obj:lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower}, which therefore gives
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}.
\]
% lean: one_category_bernoulli_lower

\emph{Upper bound.} For a measurable estimator \(\widehat\tau\) based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), write
\[
\mathsf W(\widehat\tau)
:=
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}}
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right]
\]
for its worst-case risk over the experiment class of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} at \(\epsilon=1/2\), with \(\tau(P)\) the ATE functional of \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}; by \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}, \(\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}=\inf_{\widehat\tau}\mathsf W(\widehat\tau)\), the infimum ranging over all measurable estimators.

\emph{Step 1: the infimum may be compared with a single candidate.} For every measurable \(\widehat\tau\) and every \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\), the risk \(E_{P^{\otimes n}}[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2]=\int(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\,dP^{\otimes n}\) is the integral of a nonnegative function and hence nonnegative, so
\[
0\le \mathsf W(\widehat\tau)
\qquad\text{for every measurable }\widehat\tau,
\]
with the supremum read as \(0\) when \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is empty. The set of worst-case risks appearing in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} is therefore bounded below, so its infimum is no larger than the value at any one measurable candidate.
% lean: randomized_endpoint_minimax

\emph{Step 2: the candidate.} Take the centered estimator of \cref{obj:def:centered-estimator},
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
2(2A_i-1)\left(Y_i-\frac12\right),
\]
which is a fixed real-valued function of the sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) alone; since each \(O_i\) takes finitely many values, this function is measurable, so \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) is an admissible candidate. By Step 1,
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \mathsf W(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}).
\]
% lean: randomized_endpoint_minimax

\emph{Step 3: the worst-case risk of the candidate.} If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is empty, then \(\mathsf W(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}})=0\le 1/n\). Otherwise, let \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) be arbitrary, with single-observation law \(P\). Both hypotheses of \cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} hold for the sample law \(P^{\otimes n}\), namely \(n>0\) and \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\), so with overlap constant \(\epsilon=1/2\) it gives
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\frac12\right)^2
=
\frac1n .
\]
% lean: near_randomization_linear_upper
The right-hand side does not depend on \(P\), so taking the supremum over the class,
\[
\mathsf W(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}})\le\frac1n .
\]

Chaining Step 2 with Step 3 gives \(\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le 1/n\), and together with the lower bound,
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le\frac1n .
\]
% lean: randomized_endpoint_minimax
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax} shows that the endpoint \(\epsilon=1/2\) is qualitatively different from the fixed-interior large-alphabet problem: uniformly in \(d\), the risk remains of order \(n^{-1}\). The next lemma combines this observation with the hybrid upper bound through the oracle-calibrated selected estimator introduced in \cref{obj:def:selected-estimator}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:combined-upper-envelope}[Combined Upper Envelope]
Fix constants \(\epsilon,C_\epsilon,\rho_\epsilon\in\mathbb R\) and \(N_\epsilon\in\mathbb N\). Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Interior overlap.)} \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
\item \textbf{(Positive constants.)} \(C_\epsilon>0\) and \(\rho_\epsilon>0\).
\item \textbf{(Hybrid bound.)} For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\), if \(0<n\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\), and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), then the hybrid estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} satisfies
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
\end{itemize}
Then for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(0<n\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\), and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), let \(K_\epsilon=\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}\). Define the \(C_\epsilon,\epsilon\)-dependent selected estimator \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}\) to be \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) when
\[
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\]
and to be \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) otherwise. This selected estimator satisfies
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
K_\epsilon\left[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},(1/2-\epsilon)^2\right\}
\right].
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:combined-upper-envelope}]
Fix \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(0<n\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\), and abbreviate
\[
\gamma_1=\frac1n,\qquad
\gamma_2=\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},\qquad
\gamma_3=\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2,\qquad
K_\epsilon=\max\{C_\epsilon,4\},
\]
so that the hybrid rate is \(\gamma_1+\gamma_2\) and the asserted envelope is \(K_\epsilon\{\gamma_1+\min(\gamma_2,\gamma_3)\}\). Because \(n>0\), we have \(\gamma_1>0\); \(\gamma_2\ge0\) as a ratio of squares; and \(\gamma_3\ge0\) as a square. Also \(C_\epsilon\le K_\epsilon\) and \(4\le K_\epsilon\) by the definition of the maximum.

\textbf{Step 1: the centered estimator.} With \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) as in \cref{obj:def:centered-estimator},
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le \gamma_1+4\gamma_3 .
\]
If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is empty the supremum is \(0\), and \(0\le \gamma_1+4\gamma_3\) because \(\gamma_1>0\) and \(\gamma_3\ge0\). Otherwise, for each \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), \cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper}, applied with \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\) and the overlap constant \(\epsilon\), gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2
= \gamma_1+4\gamma_3 ,
\]
and taking the supremum over the class preserves the bound.
% lean: near_randomization_linear_upper, centeredEstimator, worstCaseMSE

\textbf{Step 2: the selected estimator.} We claim
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
\min\left\{C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2),\,\gamma_1+4\gamma_3\right\}.
\]
By \cref{obj:def:selected-estimator} the selector splits on the comparison \(C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2)\le \gamma_1+4\gamma_3\), so we treat the two cases separately.

If \(C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2)\le \gamma_1+4\gamma_3\), then \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}=\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\), and the same comparison identifies the minimum,
\[
\min\left\{C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2),\,\gamma_1+4\gamma_3\right\}=C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2).
\]
Since \(0<n\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\), the assumed hybrid bound applies and yields
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
=C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2),
\]
which is the claim in this case.

If instead \(C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2)>\gamma_1+4\gamma_3\), then \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}=\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\), and the reversed comparison \(\gamma_1+4\gamma_3\le C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2)\) identifies the minimum,
\[
\min\left\{C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2),\,\gamma_1+4\gamma_3\right\}=\gamma_1+4\gamma_3,
\]
so the claim is exactly the bound of Step 1.
% lean: selectedEstimator, hHybrid, min_eq_left, min_eq_right

\textbf{Step 3: comparison with the minimax risk.} The sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) takes values in the finite set \((\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)^n\), so every real-valued function of it, in particular \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}\), is measurable and is therefore one of the candidates over which the infimum in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} is taken. That infimum is over a set of reals bounded below by \(0\): for a fixed measurable \(\widehat\tau\), each \(\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2]\) is nonnegative as the integral of a square, and the supremum over an empty class is \(0\). Hence
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right].
\]
% lean: minimaxRisk, measurable_of_finite, ciInf_le

\textbf{Step 4: the minimum is below the envelope.} It remains to show
\[
\min\left\{C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2),\,\gamma_1+4\gamma_3\right\}
\le
K_\epsilon\{\gamma_1+\min(\gamma_2,\gamma_3)\}.
\]
Suppose first \(\gamma_2\le \gamma_3\), so \(\min(\gamma_2,\gamma_3)=\gamma_2\). Since \(\gamma_1+\gamma_2\ge0\) and \(C_\epsilon\le K_\epsilon\),
\[
\min\left\{C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2),\,\gamma_1+4\gamma_3\right\}
\le C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2)
\le K_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2)
= K_\epsilon\{\gamma_1+\min(\gamma_2,\gamma_3)\}.
\]
Suppose instead \(\gamma_3<\gamma_2\), so \(\min(\gamma_2,\gamma_3)=\gamma_3\). From \(4\le K_\epsilon\) we get both \(\gamma_1\le K_\epsilon \gamma_1\) (as \(1\le4\le K_\epsilon\) and \(\gamma_1\ge0\)) and \(4\gamma_3\le K_\epsilon \gamma_3\) (as \(\gamma_3\ge0\)); adding the two and factoring gives
\[
\gamma_1+4\gamma_3\le K_\epsilon \gamma_1+K_\epsilon \gamma_3=K_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_3),
\]
whence
\[
\min\left\{C_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_2),\,\gamma_1+4\gamma_3\right\}
\le \gamma_1+4\gamma_3
\le K_\epsilon(\gamma_1+\gamma_3)
= K_\epsilon\{\gamma_1+\min(\gamma_2,\gamma_3)\}.
\]
% lean: le_max_left, le_max_right, mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_right, min_le_left, min_le_right

Chaining Step 2 with Step 4 and substituting back the definitions of \(\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3\) gives
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
K_\epsilon\left[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2\right\}
\right],
\]
which is the asserted bound; combined with Step 3 it also bounds \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) by the same envelope.
% lean: combined_upper_envelope

\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:combined-upper-envelope} formalizes the oracle-calibrated deterministic upper-envelope comparison between the fixed-interior hybrid estimator and the centered near-randomization estimator. Consequently, \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} gives a theoretical risk guarantee near \(\epsilon=1/2\). Data-driven adaptation and the sharp triangular-array minimax transition are identified as future directions in the limitations section.

\section{Proofs of the main results}\label{sec:deferred-proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}]
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Throughout, \(n\log n\ge0\) for every sample size, so an inequality of the form \(d\le\rho\,n\log n\) is preserved when \(\rho\) is replaced by any larger constant.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The upper half.}  We first prove the following claim.  There are constants \(C_U,\rho_U>0\) and an integer \(N_U<\infty\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that for every \(n,d\), every single-observation law \(P\) with \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), every \(n\ge N_U\) and every
\[
d\le \rho_U n\log n,
\]
one has
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_Q\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(Q)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_U\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
% lean: hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\emph{Constants.}  Since \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\), \cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial} applies; write \(C_L,c_L>0\) for the two constants it supplies.  Since \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate} applies; write \(C_H,\rho_H>0\) and \(N_H<\infty\) for the constants and cutoff it supplies.  Set
\[
C_U=2(C_L+C_H),
\qquad
\rho_U=\min\{c_L,\rho_H\},
\qquad
N_U=N_H .
\]
Then \(C_U>0\) and \(\rho_U>0\), and the hypothesis \(d\le\rho_U n\log n\) yields both growth gates,
\[
d\le \rho_U n\log n\le c_L n\log n,
\qquad
d\le \rho_U n\log n\le \rho_H n\log n .
\]
% lean: hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\emph{The truncation cannot increase squared error.}  For every real \(x\) and every \(t\in[-1,1]\),
\[
\left(\max\{-1,\min(1,x)\}-t\right)^2\le (x-t)^2 .
\]
Indeed, if \(x\le-1\) the left-hand entry is \(-1\), which lies between \(x\) and \(t\); if \(x>1\) it is \(1\), which again lies between \(x\) and \(t\); otherwise it is \(x\) and the two sides agree.
% lean: clamp_sq_error_le
Moreover, by \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} the estimand is \(\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^dp_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})\) with \(\sum_{k=1}^dp_k=1\) and \(\mu_{0k},\mu_{1k}\in[0,1]\), whence
\[
\tau(P)\in[-1,1].
\]
% lean: ateFunctional_eq_weighted_regression, ateFunctional_mem_interval
Applying the previous display with \(x=\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\) and \(t=\tau(P)\), and using the definition of \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, gives the sample-by-sample bound
\[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\le
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\tau(P)\right)^2 .
\]
% lean: hybrid_mse_le_component_errors

\emph{Splitting the target.}  The pilot-light set is the complement of the pilot-heavy set, \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\widehat{\mathcal H}_n^c\), so the two index sets are disjoint with union \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\) and \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} gives, sample by sample,
\[
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
+
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
=
\sum_{k=1}^d\phi(q_k)
=
\tau(P).
\]
% lean: targetHeavy_add_targetLight, lightCells_eq_compl
Substituting this identity and using \((\alpha+\beta)^2\le2\alpha^2+2\beta^2\), which is \(0\le(\alpha-\beta)^2\), we obtain sample by sample
\begin{align*}
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\tau(P)\right)^2
&=
\left\{
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)
+
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)
\right\}^2\\
&\le
2\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2
+
2\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2 .
\end{align*}
% lean: hybrid_mse_le_component_errors

\emph{Integration and the two component bounds.}  The observation alphabet is finite, so every function of the sample appearing above is bounded and integrable, and integrating the previous two displays against \(P^{\otimes n}\) preserves the inequality and splits the right-hand side by linearity:
\[
E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
2E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2\right]
+
2E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2\right].
\]
% lean: hybrid_mse_le_component_errors
Nothing in this derivation, nor in the two growth gates displayed above, refers to the particular law: the gates constrain only \(n\) and \(d\), and the constants \(C_L,c_L,C_H,\rho_H,N_H\) depend only on \(\epsilon\).  Hence for \emph{every} \(Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) the heavy bound of \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate} (available because \(n\ge N_U=N_H\) and \(d\le\rho_H n\log n\)) and the light bound of \cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial} (available because \(d\le c_L n\log n\)) may be inserted, giving
\begin{align*}
E_Q\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(Q)\right)^2\right]
&\le
2C_H\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
+
2C_L\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}\\
&=
C_U\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\end{align*}
The experiment class is nonempty, since \(P^{\otimes n}\) belongs to it, so taking the supremum over \(Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) gives the second inequality of the claim.
% lean: hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\emph{The minimax risk does not exceed the hybrid worst case.}  The sample space \((\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)^n\) is finite, so \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) is a measurable estimator and is therefore one of the competitors in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.  For any measurable \(\widehat\tau\) and any \(Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), the bound \(\tau(Q)\in[-1,1]\) obtained above gives \(|\widehat\tau(x)-\tau(Q)|\le\sum_{x'}|\widehat\tau(x')|+1\) at every sample point \(x\), where \(x'\) ranges over the finite sample space, and hence
\[
E_Q\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau-\tau(Q)\right)^2\right]
\le
\left(\sum_{x'}|\widehat\tau(x')|+1\right)^2 ,
\]
so each estimator's worst-case risk is a finite nonnegative number and the infimum in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} is taken over a nonempty set of reals bounded below by \(0\).  Evaluating that infimum at \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) gives the first inequality of the claim, and the claim is proved.
% lean: mse_le_estimator_abs_sum_bound, hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{The lower half and the constants of the theorem.}  \Cref{obj:lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer}, which transfers the fixed-sample one-arm lower bound of \citep[Theorem 2; Appendix C.5; Lemma 4; Appendix D.2]{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}, supplies constants \(a>0\) and \(\rho_L>0\) and an integer cutoff \(N_L<\infty\) such that its displayed lower bound for \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) holds whenever \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_L\) and \(d\le\rho_L n\log n\).  Now define
\[
a_\epsilon=a,\qquad
\rho_\epsilon=\min\{\rho_U,\rho_L\},\qquad
C_\epsilon=C_U,\qquad
N_\epsilon=\max\{N_U,N_L\}.
\]
These constants are positive, and \(N_\epsilon<\infty\).
% lean: ate_lower_bound_transfer, sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{Sharp sandwich.}  Assume \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\).  Then \(n\ge N_U\) and \(n\ge N_L\), while
\[
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\le \rho_U n\log n,
\qquad
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\le \rho_L n\log n,
\]
so both growth gates are in force.  Step 2 and Step 1 therefore give
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_Q\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(Q)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\},
\]
which is the sharp sandwich.
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{Parametric window.}  Fix \(K>0\) and put
\[
C_K=C_U(1+K^2)>0 .
\]
Assume \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), \(d\le K\sqrt n\,\log n\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\).

Because \(K\sqrt n\ge0\) and \(d>0\), the inequality \(d\le K\sqrt n\,\log n\) forces \(\log n>0\); in particular \(n\ge2\), so \(n\) and \(n^2(\log n)^2\) are strictly positive.  Both sides of \(d\le K\sqrt n\,\log n\) are nonnegative, so squaring is legitimate and \((\sqrt n)^2=n\) gives
\[
d^2\le K^2n(\log n)^2 .
\]
Dividing by \(n^2(\log n)^2>0\),
\[
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\le\frac{K^2}{n}.
\]
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

For the lower bound, \(d^2/\{n^2(\log n)^2\}\ge0\) and the retained gate \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\le\rho_L n\log n\) with \(d>0\) and \(n\ge N_L\) let us invoke Step 2:
\[
\frac{a_\epsilon}{n}
\le
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}.
\]
For the upper bound, the same gate gives \(d\le\rho_U n\log n\) and \(n\ge N_U\), so Step 1 applies; combining it with the displayed ratio bound,
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
C_U\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
C_U\left(\frac1n+\frac{K^2}{n}\right)
=
\frac{C_U(1+K^2)}{n}
=
\frac{C_K}{n}.
\]
This proves the parametric window.
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{Consistency threshold.}  Let \(n_j,d_j\) satisfy \(n_j\to\infty\), \(d_j>0\) eventually, and \(d_j\le\rho_\epsilon n_j\log n_j\) eventually.  Set
\[
\zeta_j=\frac{d_j}{n_j\log n_j}.
\]
For every \(j\), squaring the definition of \(\zeta_j\) gives the identity
\[
\frac1{n_j}+\frac{d_j^2}{n_j^2(\log n_j)^2}
=
\frac1{n_j}+\zeta_j^2 .
\]
% lean: hrate_eq
Since \(n_j\to\infty\) we have \(1/n_j\to0\); and eventually \(n_j\ge2\), so eventually \(\log n_j>0\) and therefore \(\zeta_j\ge0\).
% lean: hinv, hq_nonneg

Consider those indices \(j\) — all sufficiently large ones — for which simultaneously \(d_j>0\), \(d_j\le\rho_\epsilon n_j\log n_j\) and \(n_j\ge N_\epsilon\).  Step 1 requires a member of the experiment class to be exhibited, and \(d_j>0\) permits one: let \(P^\star_{d}\) be the law on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\) with
\[
P^\star_{d}(X=k,A=a,Y=y)=\frac1{4d}
\qquad\text{for all }k\in\{1,\ldots,d\},\ a,y\in\{0,1\},
\]
that is, \(p_k=1/d\), \(\pi_k=1/2\) and \(\mu_{0k}=\mu_{1k}=1/2\) in every category.  Because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) we have \(\epsilon\le1/2\le1-\epsilon\), so \(\pi_k\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\) for every category, \cref{obj:ass:overlap} holds with overlap constant \(\epsilon\), and \((P^\star_{d_j})^{\otimes n_j}\in\mathcal E_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\).
% lean: canonicalClassLaw, endpointParametricLaw_overlap
Applying Step 2 and then Step 1 with this law, and rewriting by the identity above, gives for those same indices
\[
a_\epsilon\left(\frac1{n_j}+\zeta_j^2\right)
\le
\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}
\le
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1{n_j}+\zeta_j^2\right).
\]
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

Suppose first that \(\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\to0\).  Since \(a_\epsilon>0\), the left inequality above reads
\[
0\le \frac1{n_j}+\zeta_j^2\le \frac{1}{a_\epsilon}\,\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}
\]
eventually, and the right side tends to \(0\), so \(1/n_j+\zeta_j^2\to0\).  Then \(0\le \zeta_j^2\le n_j^{-1}+\zeta_j^2\) gives \(\zeta_j^2\to0\), and since \(\sqrt{\zeta_j^2}=|\zeta_j|=\zeta_j\) eventually, continuity of the square-root map at \(0\) gives \(\zeta_j\to0\).

Conversely, suppose \(\zeta_j\to0\).  Then \(\zeta_j^2\to0\), and together with \(1/n_j\to0\) this yields
\[
\frac1{n_j}+\zeta_j^2\to0 .
\]
The minimax risk is nonnegative, because in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} it is an infimum of worst-case mean squared errors, each of which is nonnegative; combined with the right inequality of the two-sided bound above this squeezes \(\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\to0\).
% lean: minimaxRisk_mem_unitInterval, sharp_minimax_fixed_interior
This proves the consistency threshold and completes the proof.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid}]
Throughout, for \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) write
\[
r_{n,d}
=
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\]
for the target rate, and, for a single-observation law \(P\) and a sample
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), write
\[
\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}
=
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k),
\qquad
\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}
=
\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
\]
for the errors of the two branches of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}
against their pilot-selected targets, with \(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}\),
\(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\), \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\),
\(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\) as defined there and \(\phi\) as in
\cref{obj:def:cell-functional}.  Both \(\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}\) and
\(\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}\) are functions of the sample alone.
% lean: heavyContribution, targetHeavy, lightContribution, targetLight, minimaxRate
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{(Computability.)}  The first assertion of
\cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial} is precisely the computability clause: there
is a universal constant \(K>0\) such that, for every \(n\) and \(d\), a
real-arithmetic program reading the hybrid count vector returns
\(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) exactly on every sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) and
uses at most \(K\,d\,M(n)^4\) arithmetic and comparison operations.  It is proved
there with the explicit value \(K=450\), which is a positive integer, so the
clause holds with that \(K\).
% lean: hybridEstimatorComputable, hybridArithmeticProgram_eval, hybridArithmeticProgram_operationCount

\item \textbf{(Calibration of the constants.)}  Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
\Cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial}, applicable because \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\),
supplies constants \(C_L,c_L>0\) depending only on \(\epsilon\) such that
\(\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}[\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}^2]\le C_L\,r_{n,d}\) for every
\(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) with \(d\le c_L n\log n\).
\Cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate}, applicable because
\(0<\epsilon<1/2\), supplies constants \(C_H,c_H>0\) and an integer
\(N_H<\infty\), again depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that
\(\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}[\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}^2]\le C_H\,r_{n,d}\) for every
\(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) with \(n\ge N_H\) and
\(d\le c_H n\log n\).  Set
\[
C_\epsilon=2(C_L+C_H),\qquad
\rho_\epsilon=\min\{c_L,c_H\},\qquad
N_\epsilon=N_H .
\]
Then \(C_\epsilon>0\) and \(\rho_\epsilon>0\).  Fix \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with
\(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\).  Since \(n\ge1\) gives
\(n\log n\ge0\), and since \(\rho_\epsilon\le c_L\) and
\(\rho_\epsilon\le c_H\), the growth hypothesis transfers to both component
lemmas:
\[
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\le c_L n\log n,
\qquad
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\le c_H n\log n .
\]
% lean: light_cell_polynomial, universal_heavy_cell_rate, hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{(Hybrid envelope.)}  If \(d=0\) there is no probability law on the empty alphabet, so \(\mathcal E_{n,0,\epsilon}\) is empty, its supremum is \(0\), and the bound holds since the right-hand side is nonnegative.  Assume therefore \(d>0\).  Let \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\)
be arbitrary, so that \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:overlap} with constant
\(\epsilon\) and the sample law is the product law of
\cref{obj:def:experiment-class}.  We first bound the squared error pointwise in
the sample.

Because \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\) is the complement of
\(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\) in \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\), the two pilot-selected targets
add up to the estimand: by \cref{obj:def:ate-functional},
\[
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
+
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}\phi(q_k)
=
\tau(P),
\]
for every realization of the pilot split.
% lean: targetHeavy_add_targetLight, lightCells_eq_compl

Next, the estimand lies in the range enforced by the truncation.  By
\cref{obj:def:ate-functional}, \(\tau(P)=\sum_kp_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})\); the
category masses satisfy \(p_k\ge0\) and \(\sum_kp_k=1\), and each conditional
mean lies in \([0,1]\), so \(|\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}|\le1\).  Hence
\[
|\tau(P)|\le\sum_{k=1}^dp_k|\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}|\le\sum_{k=1}^dp_k=1,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\tau(P)\in[-1,1].
\]
% lean: ateFunctional_mem_interval, ateFunctional_eq_weighted_regression

Consequently the truncation in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} cannot
increase the squared error: for every \(x\in\mathbb R\) and every \(t\in[-1,1]\),
\[
\left(\max\{-1,\min(1,x)\}-t\right)^2\le (x-t)^2 .
\]
Indeed, if \(-1\le x\le1\) the two sides are equal; if \(x>1\) the left-hand side
is \((1-t)^2\) and \(0\le 1-t\le x-t\); and if \(x<-1\) the left-hand side is
\((-1-t)^2\) with \(x-t\le -1-t\le0\).
% lean: clamp_sq_error_le

Applying this with \(x=\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\) and
\(t=\tau(P)\), so that
\(\max\{-1,\min(1,x)\}=\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\), then substituting the
target identity and using \((\alpha-\beta)^2\ge0\), we obtain pointwise in the
sample
\[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\le
\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\tau(P)\right)^2
=
\left(\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}+\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}\right)^2
\le
2\,\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}^2+2\,\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}^2 .
\]
All the quantities involved are functions of a sample from a finite alphabet,
hence bounded and integrable, so integrating this inequality against
\(P^{\otimes n}\) and using linearity gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
2\,\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\mathcal D_{\mathcal H}^2\right]
+
2\,\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}^2\right].
\]
% lean: hybrid_mse_le_component_errors

The two component bounds of Step 2 are in force at this \(n\), \(d\), and law,
so the right-hand side is at most
\[
2C_H\,r_{n,d}+2C_L\,r_{n,d}
=
C_\epsilon\,r_{n,d}.
\]
The constants \(C_L,C_H\) do not depend on the member of the class, so the same
bound holds for every \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) and therefore
for the supremum:
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 \left[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
This is the first display of the fixed-overlap clause, and it holds for every
\(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and every \(d\) with \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\).
% lean: hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{(Selected estimator.)}  Keep \(n,d\) as in Step 2.  The selection
rule of \cref{obj:def:selected-estimator} compares the two real numbers
\(C_\epsilon\{1/n+d^2/(n^2\log^2n)\}\) and \(1/n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2\), neither of
which depends on the data; \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}\)
is therefore equal to \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) or to
\(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) as a fixed function of the sample, and in either
case it is a measurable estimator based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\).  Since the
infimum in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} ranges over all such estimators, it is no
larger than the worst-case risk of this one:
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 \left[(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}
        -\tau(P))^2\right].
\]
% lean: combined_upper_envelope

For the envelope itself, the hypotheses of
\cref{obj:lem:combined-upper-envelope} have all been verified: \(0<\epsilon<1/2\)
by assumption, \(C_\epsilon>0\) and \(\rho_\epsilon>0\) by Step 2, and the hybrid
worst-case bound at every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and
\(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\) by Step 3.  Its selected estimator is the one just
described, formed from the same \(C_\epsilon\) and \(\epsilon\), and it yields
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 \left[(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}
        -\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}
\left\{
\frac1n+
\min\left[
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right]
\right\},
\]
which is the remaining display of the fixed-overlap clause with
\(K_\epsilon=\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}\).
% lean: combined_upper_envelope, selectedEstimator

\item \textbf{(Centered estimator.)}  Now fix \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\) and
\(n,d\in\mathbb N\).  If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) has no members, its
supremum is \(0\) and the asserted bound holds because
\(1/n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2\ge0\).  Otherwise, let
\(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\).  The hypotheses of
\cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} hold, since \(n>0\) and
\(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), so
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 \left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2 ,
\]
with \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) the estimator of
\cref{obj:def:centered-estimator}.  The right-hand side does not depend on the
member, so taking the supremum over the class gives the centered-estimator
clause.
% lean: near_randomization_linear_upper, overlap_adaptive_universal_hybrid_statistical

\item \textbf{(Endpoint bracket.)}  Finally, for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with
\(n>0\) and \(d>0\), \cref{obj:lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax} gives the
endpoint clause directly:
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \frac1n .
\]
Together with Steps 1, 3, 4, and 5 this proves the theorem.
% lean: randomized_endpoint_minimax, overlap_adaptive_universal_hybrid
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
