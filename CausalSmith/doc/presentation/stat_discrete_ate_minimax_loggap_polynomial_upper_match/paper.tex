\begin{abstract}
This paper studies minimax estimation of the average treatment effect in a finite-alphabet observational model with binary treatment and outcome, unrestricted categorywise nuisance functions, and fixed overlap. For sufficiently large sample size \(n\), positive covariate alphabet size \(d\), and fixed interior overlap level \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\), the minimax mean-squared risk over the overlap-restricted iid experiment class is
\[
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\]
up to constants depending on \(\epsilon\), in the range \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\). The upper bound is attained by a computable two-split hybrid estimator with universal numerical tuning: pilot-certified heavy categories are estimated by empirical treatment-control ratios, while pilot-certified light categories are estimated by a Chebyshev reciprocal polynomial whose monomials are lifted by factorial moments. The rate implies a parametric regime \(d=O(\sqrt n\log n)\) and a consistency frontier \(d=o(n\log n)\) within the calibrated fixed-interior range. The paper also gives an overlap-phase upper envelope: a centered estimator has risk at most \(n^{-1}+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2\) for every \(n,d\), and at the randomized endpoint \(\epsilon=1/2\) the minimax risk is bracketed between \(1/(100n)\) and \(1/n\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Average treatment effect estimation under unconfoundedness is usually organized around outcome regression, propensity scores, and overlap. In regular low-dimensional models, this program leads to semiparametric efficiency theory and first-order robust estimators \citep{Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003,RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,BangRobins2005,VanDerLaanRubin2006}. Modern high-dimensional causal inference extends the same target with structured nuisance estimation, orthogonal moments, and debiasing \citep{Belloni2017,Chernozhukov2018,Chernozhukov2022}. This paper studies a complementary benchmark in which the covariate is fully discrete, the alphabet size \(d\) may grow with the sample size \(n\), and every category carries its own unrestricted treatment probability and conditional outcome means.

The benchmark isolates the cost of discrete confounding under fixed overlap. The observed data are iid triples \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\), with \(X_i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), binary treatment \(A_i\), and binary outcome \(Y_i\). Under the overlap level \(\epsilon\), every positive-mass category has treatment probability in \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\). The target is the adjusted average treatment effect
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^d p_k\{\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}\},
\]
written in the body as a homogeneous four-cell functional so that categories with small empirical support can be analyzed directly. Under consistency and conditional exchangeability, this observed-law functional has the usual potential-outcome interpretation.

The main result gives the sharp fixed-interior minimax rate. For each fixed \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\), \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} establishes constants \(a_\epsilon,\rho_\epsilon,C_\epsilon>0\) and \(N_\epsilon<\infty\) such that, for \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\),
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
The same result records the parametric interior \(d=O(\sqrt n\log n)\), where the risk is of order \(1/n\), and the consistency frontier \(d=o(n\log n)\) along sequences inside the displayed calibrated range.

The estimator attaining the upper bound is explicit. It splits the sample into a pilot half and an estimation half. Categories with large pilot counts enter a ratio branch, using empirical treated and untreated outcome proportions. Categories with small pilot counts enter a polynomial branch. The polynomial is a Chebyshev-based approximation to the reciprocal terms in the categorywise treatment-control contrast, and its monomials are estimated through normalized falling-factorial moments of the four treatment-outcome cell counts. \Cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} records that this hybrid is computable with operation count linear in \(d\) and polynomial in the logarithmic degree \(M(n)\). The same numerical tuning is used across fixed interior overlap levels.

The rate closes the logarithmic upper-bound gap left by the closest discrete-covariate causal benchmark. \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024} show that standard plug-in, inverse-probability-weighted, and doubly robust estimators have worst-case upper scale \(d^2/n^2+1/n\) in a strong-overlap finite-alphabet model, while their lower bound has scale \(d^2/\{n^2(\log n)^2\}+1/n\). The hybrid construction developed here attains that lower scale in the fixed-interior regime. The lower side is transferred from their fixed-sample control-zero construction, and the upper side is supplied by the ratio-polynomial estimator.

The estimator reflects the same approximation principle that appears in large-alphabet nonsmooth functional estimation \citep{Paninski2003,JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016,HanJiaoWeissman2020}. Sparse categories have too little information for stable cellwise ratio estimation. A degree of order \(\log n\) balances approximation bias against factorial-moment variance, producing the logarithmic improvement in the large-alphabet term. Heavy categories are handled separately because their denominators are large enough for direct ratio estimation to achieve the required aggregate rate.

The paper also characterizes the randomized endpoint behavior. \Cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} gives a centered estimator with risk at most
\[
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\]
for every \(n,d\) and every \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2]\). At \(\epsilon=1/2\), treatment is randomized within each positive-mass category, and the minimax risk lies between \(1/(100n)\) and \(1/n\). Combining the centered estimator with the hybrid estimator yields a deterministic upper envelope involving
\[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right\}
\]
within the fixed-interior calibration range.

A two-category witness in \cref{obj:prop:two-category-confounding} illustrates the causal content of the adjusted target. The construction satisfies consistency, conditional exchangeability, and fixed overlap, while the adjusted ATE equals \(1/2\) and the naive observed treated-minus-control contrast equals \(\epsilon\). This example fixes the distinction between the causal estimand analyzed in the minimax problem and the unadjusted association in the observed treatment groups.

\section{Related work}

The paper sits at the intersection of causal inference for average treatment effects, high-dimensional observational methods, minimax theory for nonsmooth functionals, and large-alphabet discrete-distribution estimation. In the potential-outcomes tradition of \citet{Rubin1974} and \citet{RosenbaumRubin1983}, identification and estimation of average treatment effects are organized around consistency, conditional exchangeability, and overlap. Semiparametric analyses then characterize efficient influence functions and regular estimators under smooth low-dimensional structure \citep{Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003,RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994}. Doubly robust and targeted procedures extend this program by combining outcome regression and propensity-score estimation in ways that preserve first-order behavior under appropriate nuisance control \citep{BangRobins2005,VanDerLaanRubin2006}. The present analysis uses that causal estimand and overlap vocabulary, but its rate question is driven by unrestricted finite-alphabet confounding: the covariate alphabet may grow with the sample size, and the categorywise nuisance functions are unconstrained across cells.

High-dimensional causal inference has developed complementary tools for flexible nuisance estimation and valid inference under structural restrictions. Orthogonal and debiased methods use moment equations that reduce sensitivity to first-stage regularization error \citep{Belloni2017,Chernozhukov2018,Chernozhukov2022}; related analyses clarify how overlap, support, and design complexity shape attainable performance in observational problems \citep{DAmour2021,JinSyrgkanis2025}. Work on higher-order influence functions and nonparametric bias correction shows that estimating causal functionals can require corrections beyond first-order semiparametric theory when nuisance features are complex \citep{Robins2008,Robins2009,Robins2017,LiuMukherjeeNeweyRobins2017,Kennedy2024}. The fixed-overlap discrete model considered here gives a complementary benchmark in which the complexity is combinatorial rather than smooth: each category carries its own treatment probability and conditional outcome means, so the sharp rate depends on the number of categories rather than on smoothness or sparsity.

The minimax perspective follows the classical decision-theoretic treatment of statistical rates \citep{Bickel1993,VanDerVaart1998,Tsybakov2009}. For nonsmooth functionals, sharp procedures often combine approximation theory with bias correction, and logarithmic factors can mark the difference between plug-in behavior and the optimal risk \citep{Lepski1999,CaiLow2011}. Classical unseen-species work provides an early large-alphabet precedent \citep{Fisher1943,GoodToulmin1956,EfronThisted1976}. Modern large-alphabet functional estimation uses polynomial approximation and unbiased estimation of polynomial moments to attain sharp rates for entropy, support size, and related functionals \citep{Paninski2003,Valiant2011,Valiant2017,Orlitsky2016,WuYang2019,JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016,HanJiaoWeissman2015,HanJiaoWeissman2020}. The estimator constructed in this paper imports that approximation-and-factorial-moment logic into the causal four-cell contribution associated with each covariate category, while ratio estimation handles categories with enough observations.

The closest comparison is \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. In their strong-overlap discrete-covariate analysis, standard plug-in, inverse-probability-weighted, and doubly robust estimators achieve an upper bound of order \(d^2/n^2+1/n\), where \(d\), the covariate alphabet size, may grow with \(n\), the sample size. Their minimax lower bound scales as \(d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)+1/n\). The present paper constructs a computable balanced ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator that attains this lower scale in the fixed-interior overlap regime, thereby matching the logarithmically sharpened large-alphabet term together with the parametric \(1/n\) component.


\section{Setup and assumptions}

Throughout the paper, \(C\) denotes a finite positive constant whose value may change from line to line, with dependence displayed when it matters. Asymptotic notation is along sequences in the sample size \(n\) and the alphabet size \(d\), with the overlap level \(\epsilon\) treated as fixed unless stated otherwise. Category indices range over \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), treatment arms and outcomes range over \(\{0,1\}\), and all risks are mean-squared risks under the product law generated by the observed-data distribution.

The observed-data model is finite and fully discrete. We first fix the variables and sampling law, then impose overlap, define the experiment class, and express the target functional as a sum of categorywise four-cell contributions.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_7}[Observed treatment, outcome, and potential outcomes]
We write \(A\in\{0,1\}\) for the observed binary treatment, with \(A=1\) denoting treated status and \(A=0\) untreated status, and \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) for the observed binary outcome. The potential outcomes \(Y(0)\) and \(Y(1)\) are \(\{0,1\}\)-valued random variables on the same probability space as the observed variables, where \(Y(a)\) is the outcome that would be recorded under treatment level \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iid-sampling}[IID observed-data model]
The observed data are \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\), with \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P\), where the covariate \(X\) takes values in \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\), the treatment \(A\) and outcome \(Y\) are binary. For each category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\) write \(p_k=P(X=k)\) for the category mass, \(\pi_k=P(A=1\mid X=k)\) for the treatment probability, and, for each treatment arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), \(\mu_{ak}=E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=k]\) for the conditional mean outcome.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} is the standard independent and identically distributed sampling condition for a finite-alphabet observational study \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. It allows the law \(P\) to assign arbitrary probabilities to the category--treatment--outcome cells, so all heterogeneity across categories is carried by the unrestricted masses, propensities, and conditional outcome means.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:overlap}[Fixed overlap]
For the overlap level \(\epsilon\), every category \(k\) with category mass \(p_k>0\) satisfies
\[
\epsilon \le \pi_k \le 1-\epsilon .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:overlap} is the standard strong overlap condition \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. It fixes an interior range for each positive-mass category, ensuring that both treatment arms are represented at the population level in every category contributing to the target.

\begin{definitionv}{def:experiment-class}[Experiment class \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\)]
For \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) and \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\), the overlap-restricted iid experiment class is
\[
\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}
:=
\left\{
P^{\otimes n}:
P\in\mathsf{Prob}(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)
\ \text{and } P \text{ satisfies } \cref{obj:ass:overlap}
\right\}.
\]
The law \(P^{\otimes n}\) is formed from the single-observation law in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:overlap-cone}[Overlap cone \(\mathcal C_\epsilon\)]
The overlap-restricted cone of nonnegative four-cell vectors is
\[
\mathcal C_\epsilon
:=
\left\{
u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\in\mathbb R_+^4:
\epsilon(u_{00}+u_{01}+u_{10}+u_{11})
\le
u_{10}+u_{11}
\le
(1-\epsilon)(u_{00}+u_{01}+u_{10}+u_{11})
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:cell-functional}[Cell functional \(\phi(u)\)]
For \(u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\in\mathcal C_\epsilon\) as in \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone}, define
\[
\phi(u)
:=
\begin{cases}
(u_{00}+u_{01}+u_{10}+u_{11})
\left[
\dfrac{u_{11}}{u_{10}+u_{11}}
-
\dfrac{u_{01}}{u_{00}+u_{01}}
\right],
& u\neq(0,0,0,0),\\[1.2em]
0,
& u=(0,0,0,0).
\end{cases}
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:ate-functional}[Average treatment effect \(\tau(P)\)]
For a probability law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), with arbitrary masses on this finite observation alphabet, define, for \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\) and \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), the joint cell mass
\[
q_{aky}=P(A=a,X=k,Y=y),
\]
and set
\[
q_k=(q_{0k0},q_{0k1},q_{1k0},q_{1k1}).
\]
The average treatment effect functional is
\[
\tau(P)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}\phi(q_k).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The experiment class in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} is the decision-theoretic object over which risks are evaluated. The cone in \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone} rewrites fixed overlap directly in terms of the four joint treatment--outcome masses within a category, and \cref{obj:def:cell-functional,obj:def:ate-functional} turn those cell masses into the categorywise contribution and the population ATE functional. This representation is useful because the analysis can treat the target as a sum of homogeneous finite-alphabet cell functionals.

\begin{definitionv}{def:minimax-risk}[Minimax risk \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\)]
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
:=
\inf_{\widehat\tau}
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right].
\]
The infimum ranges over all measurable estimators \(\widehat\tau\) based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\).
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} measures the worst-case mean-squared error over the fixed-overlap experiment class. The main rate theorem below evaluates this risk and compares it with the performance of a concrete estimator.

The estimator uses one deterministic split to classify categories by pilot abundance and the other split to estimate the resulting heavy and light contributions. The following definitions record the split counts, the polynomial notation, and the calibrated hybrid estimator used in the main theorem.

\begin{definitionv}{def:sample-splits}[Sample splits \(I_0,I_1\) and counts \(N^{(j)}_{aky}\)]
For a sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) with \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), define the deterministic half-sample splits, their sizes, and the logarithmic scale
\[
I_0=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\},\qquad I_1=\{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1,\ldots,n\},\qquad m_j=|I_j|,\qquad L=\log(en),
\]
and, for each split \(j\in\{0,1\}\), treatment arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), and outcome \(y\in\{0,1\}\), the split cell count
\[
N^{(j)}_{aky}=\#\{i\in I_j:(A_i,X_i,Y_i)=(a,k,y)\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_3}[Chebyshev polynomials, falling factorials, and four-cell monomials]
For \(M\in\mathbb N\), \(T_M\) denotes the degree-\(M\) Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind, characterized by \(T_M(\cos\theta)=\cos(M\theta)\) for every \(\theta\in\mathbb R\). For a real number \(z\) and an integer \(t\ge1\), the falling factorial is \((z)_t=z(z-1)\cdots(z-t+1)\), with \((z)_0=1\); it is applied to the split cell counts as \((N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}\) and to the estimation-split size as \((m_1)_{|r|}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{|r|-1}(m_1-\ell)\), the empty product being \(1\). For a four-cell vector \(u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\) and a multi-index \(r=(r_{ay})_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}\in\mathbb N_0^4\) of total degree \(|r|=\sum_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}r_{ay}\), the monomial \(u^r\) is \(\prod_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}u_{ay}^{r_{ay}}\). For a category \(k\) and a multi-index \(r\), the normalized factorial-moment monomial is \[ \mathfrak m_{k,r} = \frac{\prod_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}}{(m_1)_{|r|}}. \]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:hybrid-estimator-handle}[Hybrid estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\)]
Using the deterministic splits \(I_0,I_1\), split sizes \(m_j\), logarithmic scale \(L=\log(en)\), and split counts \(N^{(j)}_{aky}\) from \cref{obj:def:sample-splits}, set
\[
A_0=6,\qquad \lambda_0=256,\qquad b_0=4096,
\]
\[
D_{A_0}=8\log(9A_0/8)=8\log(27/4),
\]
and
\[
\alpha_0=\min\left\{1,\frac1{32\log A_0},\frac1{256D_{A_0}}\right\}.
\]
For \(n\ge2\), define
\[
M(n)=\max\{2,\lfloor\alpha_0L\rfloor\},\qquad B(n)=b_0L/m_1.
\]
Let \(N_0\) be the least numerical cutoff such that, for every \(n\ge N_0\),
\[
\alpha_0L\ge2,\qquad 4M(n)^2\le m_1,\qquad 4M(n)/m_1\le 3B(n)/4.
\]
For every \(n\), define the pilot-certified heavy and light category sets
\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}_n
=
\left\{k:\sum_{a,y}N^{(0)}_{aky}>\lambda_0L\right\},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\widehat{\mathcal H}_n^c\quad(n\ge N_0),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\varnothing\quad(n<N_0),
\]
so that for \(n<N_0\) every category is treated by the ratio branch below. For \(n\ge N_0\), abbreviate \(M=M(n)\) and \(B=B(n)\).
Put
\[
N_k^{(1)}=\sum_{a,y}N^{(1)}_{aky},
\qquad
N_{ak}^{(1)}=\sum_yN^{(1)}_{aky},
\]
and define the heavy-category ratio contribution
\[
\widehat h_k
=
\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}
\left\{
\frac{N^{(1)}_{1k1}}{N^{(1)}_{1k}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{1k}>0\}
-
\frac{N^{(1)}_{0k1}}{N^{(1)}_{0k}}\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{0k}>0\}
\right\}.
\]
Define
\[
H_M(x)=\frac{1-T_M(1-2x)}{2M^2},
\qquad
E_M(x)=\frac{H_M(x)}{x},
\qquad
G_M(x)=\frac{1-E_M(x)}{x},
\]
where \(E_M\) and \(G_M\) are interpreted by polynomial continuation at zero. For \(u=(u_{00},u_{01},u_{10},u_{11})\), set
\[
s_a(u)=u_{a0}+u_{a1},\qquad s(u)=s_0(u)+s_1(u),
\]
\[
t_1(u)=u_{11},\qquad t_0(u)=u_{01},
\]
and define the light-cell polynomial
\[
P_{M,B}(u)
=
\frac{s(u)t_1(u)}B
G_M\!\left(\frac{s_1(u)}B\right)
-
\frac{s(u)t_0(u)}B
G_M\!\left(\frac{s_0(u)}B\right).
\]
Expand
\[
P_{M,B}(u)=\sum_{|r|\le M}c_ru^r.
\]
For each category \(k\), define
\[
\widehat P_k
=
\sum_{|r|\le M}
c_r
\frac{\prod_{a,y}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}}{(m_1)_{|r|}}.
\]
The heavy and light sums are
\[
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\widehat h_k,
\qquad
\widehat T_{\mathcal L}
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\widehat P_k.
\]
The truncated balanced ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator is
\[
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}
=
\max\{-1,\min(1,\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L})\}.
\]
The same calibrated estimator is used for every overlap level.
\end{definitionv}

The split in \cref{obj:def:sample-splits} separates category selection from contribution estimation. The polynomial notation in \cref{obj:synth_3} supports unbiased estimation of polynomial cell functionals through factorial moments. The estimator in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} then combines direct ratio estimation on pilot-certified heavy categories with a Chebyshev-based polynomial contribution on pilot-certified light categories, using fixed numerical tuning.

We next connect the observed-data functional to the usual potential-outcome interpretation of the ATE. The following assumptions are stated separately from the experiment class because the minimax problem is formulated on observed laws, while causal interpretation uses a full-data overlay.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:consistency}[Observed-outcome consistency]
The observed outcome satisfies
\[
Y=Y(A)
\]
almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:consistency} is the standard consistency condition linking the recorded outcome to the potential outcome under the realized treatment \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. It gives the observed binary outcome its causal interpretation once treatment is assigned.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:conditional-exchangeability}[Conditional exchangeability]
The potential outcomes satisfy
\[
(Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid X .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability} is the standard conditional exchangeability condition \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. Together with \cref{obj:ass:consistency,obj:ass:overlap}, it identifies the category-adjusted contrast represented by \(\tau(P)\) in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}; this is the finite-alphabet version of the usual adjustment argument in observational causal inference \citep{Rubin1974,RosenbaumRubin1983}.

A two-category construction illustrates how the causal target can differ from the unadjusted observed contrast even in the smallest nontrivial discrete model. The example fixes the category probabilities, treatment propensities, and conditional means, then supplies a full-data extension satisfying the potential-outcome conditions.

\begin{definitionv}{def:two-category-witness}[Two-category witness law]
For \(d=2\) and \(0\le \epsilon\le 1\), set
\[
p_1=p_2=\frac12,\qquad
\pi_1=\epsilon,\qquad
\pi_2=1-\epsilon,\qquad
\mu_{01}=\mu_{02}=0,\qquad
\mu_{11}=1,\qquad
\mu_{12}=0.
\]
Define the corresponding full-data extension by drawing
\[
(Y(0),Y(1))=(0,\mathbf 1\{X=1\})
\]
setting \(Y=Y(A)\), and then drawing \(A\mid X=k\sim\mathsf{Bernoulli}(\pi_k)\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{propositionv}{prop:two-category-confounding}[Two-category confounding witness]
For any overlap level \(\epsilon\) satisfying \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\), let \(P\) be the full-data law constructed in \cref{obj:def:two-category-witness}. Then \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:consistency,obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability,obj:ass:overlap}. Its average treatment effect, with \(\tau(P)\) as in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}, and its naive observed contrast are
\[
\tau(P)=\frac12,
\qquad
E_P[Y\mid A=1]-E_P[Y\mid A=0]=\epsilon .
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:two-category-confounding} records the exact separation between the adjusted causal estimand and the naive treated-minus-control contrast in this witness law. The construction satisfies the same consistency, conditional exchangeability, and fixed-overlap conditions used for causal interpretation, so the discrepancy is the result of category imbalance in the observed treatment groups rather than a change in the estimand.


\section{Main results}

The preceding section defined the overlap-restricted experiment class, the ATE functional, the minimax risk, and the balanced ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator. We now state the rate conclusions. Two auxiliary definitions fix the product-law notation used in the statements and the centered estimator used at the randomized endpoint.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_10}[Centered endpoint estimator $\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}$]
For \(n\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and observed units \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), define the centered endpoint estimator by \[ \widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n 2(2A_i-1)\left(Y_i-\frac12\right). \]
\end{definitionv}

The centered estimator in \cref{obj:synth_10} is a linear contrast tailored to the near-randomization regime. It supplies the benchmark used below when overlap approaches the randomized endpoint.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_13}[Product sampling laws]
For a finite index set \(J\) and a probability law \(P\) for one observed unit \(O=(X,A,Y)\), \(P^{\otimes J}\) denotes the product law on families \((O_j)_{j\in J}\) under which the coordinates are independent and each coordinate has law \(P\). For a sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) with single-observation law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), we write \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\) for the \(n\)-fold product law of the observed sample.
\end{definitionv}

The notation in \cref{obj:synth_13} keeps the single-observation law and its product sample law explicit. The minimax bounds below are stated over the same product-law experiment class as in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}[Sharp fixed-interior minimax rate]*
Fix an overlap level \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Then there are constants
\(a_\epsilon,\rho_\epsilon,C_\epsilon>0\) and an integer \(N_\epsilon<\infty\) such that, writing
\[
r_{n,d}:=\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},
\]
the following statements hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Matched minimax envelope.)} For every \(n,d\), every single-observation law \(P\), and every sample law \(\mu_n\) in the experiment class \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}, if
\[
d>0,\qquad n\ge N_\epsilon,\qquad d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n,
\]
then the minimax risk \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} and the truncated balanced ratio-polynomial hybrid estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} satisfy
\[
a_\epsilon r_{n,d}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon r_{n,d}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Parametric interior.)} For every \(K>0\) there is a constant \(C_K>0\) such that, for every \(n,d\), every single-observation law \(P\), and every sample law \(\mu_n\) in \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), if
\[
d>0,\qquad n\ge N_\epsilon,\qquad
d\le K\sqrt n\log n,\qquad
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n,
\]
then
\[
\frac{a_\epsilon}{n}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\frac{C_K}{n}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Consistency threshold.)} For every pair of integer sequences \(n_j,d_j\) with \(n_j\to\infty\), \(d_j>0\) eventually, and
\[
d_j\le \rho_\epsilon n_j\log n_j
\]
eventually, the minimax risk converges to zero exactly along the sequences satisfying the vanishing normalized dimension condition:
\[
\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\to 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{d_j}{n_j\log n_j}\to 0.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} identifies the fixed-interior minimax mean-squared-error rate as
\[
n^{-1}+d^2\{n^2(\log n)^2\}^{-1}
\]
through matching lower and upper bounds. The lower side uses the fixed-sample discrete-covariate lower-bound construction of \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}; the upper side is attained by the hybrid estimator already defined in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}. The theorem also records the parametric interior \(d=O(\sqrt n\log n)\), where the risk is of order \(1/n\), and the consistency criterion \(d=o(n\log n)\) along sequences satisfying the displayed fixed-interior dimension range.

The rate has the same two-component structure as other nonsmooth large-alphabet functional problems: an ordinary sampling term and an approximation-driven large-alphabet term \citep{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016,HanJiaoWeissman2020}. In this causal problem, the approximation term enters through the cell contribution \(\phi\) of \cref{obj:def:cell-functional}; heavy categories are handled by ratios, while light categories use a polynomial approximation whose coefficients can be estimated through factorial moments. The logarithmic denominator in the second term is the gain supplied by the polynomial branch relative to purely cellwise plug-in behavior.

The next result separates implementation, fixed-overlap calibration, near-randomization control, and the randomized endpoint. Its fixed-overlap statement uses the same numerical hybrid construction for every interior overlap class, while the deterministic selected estimator combines the hybrid and centered estimators according to their displayed envelopes.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid}[Overlap adaptive hybrid envelope]
With \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) as in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} and \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) as in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}, the following assertions hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Computability.} There is a universal integer \(K>0\) such that, for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\), a real-arithmetic program based on the hybrid count input has operation count at most
\[
K\,d\,M(n)^4
\]
and, for every sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), evaluates exactly to \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\).
\item \textbf{Fixed-overlap hybrid calibration.} For every overlap level \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there are constants \(C_\epsilon,\rho_\epsilon>0\) and an integer \(N_\epsilon\) such that, for all \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\), \(d>0\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), and
\[
d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n,
\]
the hybrid estimator satisfies
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
For the deterministic estimator
\[
\widehat\tau_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}
=
\begin{cases}
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}},
& \text{if } C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le \frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2,\\
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}},
& \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
one also has
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}
\left[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right\}
\right].
\]
\item \textbf{Centered randomization bound.} For every overlap level \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\) and every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and \(d>0\), the centered estimator
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
2(2A_i-1)\left(Y_i-\frac12\right)
\]
satisfies
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2.
\]
\item \textbf{Endpoint randomization bracket.} For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and \(d>0\),
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \frac1n.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} gives a computable form of the estimator and an overlap-phase envelope. The operation count is polynomial in the degree \(M(n)\) and linear in the alphabet size \(d\), so the construction is an explicit count-based procedure rather than an existential minimax rule. For every fixed interior overlap level, the same hybrid tuning from \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} attains the sharp fixed-interior upper rate in the calibrated range.

The centered estimator in \cref{obj:synth_10} explains the endpoint behavior. As \(\epsilon\) approaches \(1/2\), treatment assignment is close to randomized within each category, and the bound in \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} contracts to the \(1/n\) scale. The deterministic selected estimator therefore records, in one displayed envelope, the large-alphabet fixed-interior contribution and the near-randomization contribution. The endpoint bracket gives the exact order \(1/n\) at \(\epsilon=1/2\), completing the comparison between the interior fixed-overlap regime and the randomized endpoint.


\section{Discussion and extensions}

\Cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} identifies the fixed-interior minimax mean-squared-error rate for unrestricted finite-alphabet confounding under fixed overlap, for sufficiently large \(n\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\). The characterization separates the risk into the ordinary sampling term \(1/n\) and the large-alphabet term \(d^2/\{n^2(\log n)^2\}\). This scale gives a benchmark for observational problems in which the covariate distribution is unrestricted across many discrete categories, complementing classical semiparametric ATE theory for regular low-dimensional models \citep{Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003,RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,BangRobins2005,VanDerLaanRubin2006} and higher-order analyses of nonregular causal functionals \citep{Robins2008,Robins2009,Robins2017,LiuMukherjeeNeweyRobins2017,Kennedy2024}.

The estimator in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} explains how the rate is attained constructively. Heavy categories are estimated through empirical treatment-control ratios, where the relevant denominators are large enough for the ratio behavior to be controlled. Light categories are handled through a Chebyshev-derived polynomial approximation to the cell contribution in \cref{obj:def:cell-functional}, with factorial moments converting the polynomial into an unbiased split-sample estimate of its population analogue. The resulting hybrid treats the singular ratio structure of \(\phi\) as an approximation problem on sparse cells and as an ordinary ratio-estimation problem on sufficiently populated cells.

This decomposition also clarifies the logarithmic factor. In the finite-alphabet experiment class of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}, the alphabet size can create many categories whose population mass is too small for direct ratio estimation to behave parametrically. Polynomial approximation spreads the information in those sparse cells across a degree of order \(\log n\), and the factorial-moment construction makes that approximation estimable from counts. The main theorem therefore turns the large-alphabet causal difficulty into the same approximation-versus-variance balance that appears in nonsmooth functional estimation, while preserving the adjusted ATE target in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}.

The endpoint statements in \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} connect the fixed-interior result to the randomized boundary. When \(\epsilon=1/2\), treatment is randomized within every positive-mass category, and the minimax risk is bracketed at the parametric scale \(1/n\). For interior values close to the endpoint, the centered estimator has the displayed risk bound \(1/n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2\). The selected estimator combines this endpoint behavior with the hybrid envelope, yielding the upper scale
\[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},
(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right\}
\]
within the fixed-interior calibration range. Thus the same analysis gives both a sharp fixed-class rate and a deterministic upper envelope that reflects proximity to randomized assignment.

The results are also informative for high-dimensional causal inference. In many semiparametric and machine-learning approaches, structural conditions on nuisance functions, such as smoothness, sparsity, or predictive structure, shape attainable rates \citep{Balakrishnan2023,JinSyrgkanis2025}. Here the category probabilities and outcome regressions are unrestricted, so the rate isolates the cost of discrete confounding itself. The parametric interior regime \(d=O(\sqrt n\log n)\) and the consistency criterion \(d=o(n\log n)\), both under the stated calibration range of \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}, give concrete benchmarks against which additional structural restrictions can be compared.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
A remaining open direction is the triangular-array lower-envelope problem in which the overlap level varies with sample size, \(\epsilon=\epsilon_n\), especially near the randomized endpoint. The fixed-class theorem establishes the sharp rate for each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) in its calibrated large-sample range, and the endpoint bracket establishes the parametric scale at \(\epsilon=1/2\). The combined upper envelope in \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid} supplies a deterministic attainable bound across the fixed-interior calibration range. A matching lower envelope for sequences \(\epsilon_n\) would characterize the transition between the fixed-interior sparse-cell difficulty and the randomized endpoint behavior.

The first group fixes count notation for arbitrary subsamples and gives the pilot event that separates categories by population mass with polynomially small error probability.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_1}[Category and cell counts]
For observations \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\) indexed by a finite set \(I\), a category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), and an arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), the category count, the arm-specific category count, and the arm-specific outcome-one count are \[ N_k=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k\},\qquad N_{ak}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\},\qquad N_{ak1}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a,Y_i=1\}. \] For the deterministic pilot split \(I_0\) of \cref{obj:def:sample-splits} we write \(m_0=|I_0|\) for its size and \(N_k^{(0)}=\sum_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}N^{(0)}_{aky}\) for the pilot count of category \(k\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:pilot-sandwich}[Pilot sandwich tail bound]
Let \(L=\log(en)\) and let \(m_0=|I_0|\) be the pilot-split size from \cref{obj:def:sample-splits}.  For a threshold \(t\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}(t)=\{k:N_k^{(0)}>tL\},\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal L}(t)=\widehat{\mathcal H}(t)^c,
\]
and
\[
B_-(t)=\frac{tL}{2m_0},\qquad
B_+(t)=\frac{2tL}{m_0}.
\]
The pilot heavy/light sandwich has the following two polynomial tail guarantees.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Arbitrary polynomial exponent.)} For every \(K>0\) and \(c>0\), there exist constants \(t_0>0\), \(C>0\), and \(N\) such that, for every \(t\ge t_0\), every \(n\ge N\), every dimension \(d\) satisfying \(d\le c\,nL\), every probability law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2\), and every sample law \(\mu_n\) satisfying the iid sampling condition in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling},
\[
\mu_n\!\left(
\widehat{\mathcal H}(t)\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge B_-(t)\}
\;\text{or}\;
\widehat{\mathcal L}(t)\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\le B_+(t)\}
\right)
\le C n^{-K}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Fixed threshold specialization.)} For every \(c>0\), there exist constants \(C>0\) and \(N\) such that, for every \(n\ge N\), every dimension \(d\) satisfying \(d\le c\,nL\), every probability law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2\), and every sample law \(\mu_n\) satisfying the iid sampling condition in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling},
\[
\mu_n\!\left(
\widehat{\mathcal H}(256)\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge B_-(256)\}
\;\text{or}\;
\widehat{\mathcal L}(256)\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\le B_+(256)\}
\right)
\le C n^{-4}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:pilot-sandwich}]
Fix \(n,d,P,t\), and let \(\mu_n\) be a sample law satisfying \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}; thus \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\). Write \(L=\log(en)\), \(m_0=|I_0|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), and \(p_k=P(X=k)\). For a category \(k\), set
\[
Z_{ik}=\mathbf 1\{X_i=k\},\qquad i\in I_0,
\]
so that
\[
N_k^{(0)}=\sum_{i\in I_0} Z_{ik}.
\]
Under \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}, the one-observation mean of this indicator is \(p_k\), and \(N_k^{(0)}\) is the Bernoulli count from the deterministic pilot split of size \(m_0\).

The pilot heavy and light sets used in the bad event are
\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}
=
\left\{k:\lfloor tL\rfloor<N_k^{(0)}\right\},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal L}=\widehat{\mathcal H}^{c}.
\]
Equivalently, since \(N_k^{(0)}\) is integer-valued, \(k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}\) exactly when \(tL<N_k^{(0)}\). Thus
\[
\mathcal B_t
=
\left\{
\widehat{\mathcal H}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge tL/(2m_0)\}
\ \text{or}\ 
\widehat{\mathcal L}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\le 2tL/m_0\}
\right\}.
\]
If the heavy-set condition fails, then for some \(k\),
\(p_k<tL/(2m_0)\) and \(\lfloor tL\rfloor<N_k^{(0)}\), hence \(tL<N_k^{(0)}\). If the light-set condition fails, then for some \(k\),
\(2tL/m_0<p_k\) and \(k\notin\widehat{\mathcal H}\); equivalently
\(\lfloor tL\rfloor\ge N_k^{(0)}\), which gives \(N_k^{(0)}\le tL\). Hence
\[
\mathcal B_t
\subseteq
\bigcup_{k=1}^d
\left\{p_k<\frac{tL}{2m_0},\ tL<N_k^{(0)}\right\}
\cup
\bigcup_{k=1}^d
\left\{\frac{2tL}{m_0}<p_k,\ N_k^{(0)}\le tL\right\}.
\]

For any \(\ell\in\mathbb R\), the Bernoulli-count upper tail used here gives
\[
m_0p_k<\ell/2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_n\{\ell<N_k^{(0)}\}
\le
\exp\{-\ell(\log 2-1/2)\}.
\]
Applying this with \(\ell=tL\) bounds the upper-count branch whenever
\(p_k<tL/(2m_0)\).

Similarly, the lower-tail bound gives
\[
2\ell<m_0p_k
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_n\{N_k^{(0)}\le \ell\}
\le
\exp\{-m_0p_k/8\}
\le
\exp\{-\ell/4\}.
\]
Applying this with \(\ell=tL\) bounds the lower-count branch whenever
\(2tL/m_0<p_k\).

Now fix a category \(k\). For the first cellwise event in the displayed union, either
\(p_k<tL/(2m_0)\), in which case the event is contained in \(\{tL<N_k^{(0)}\}\) and the upper-tail bound applies, or the mass inequality fails, in which case the event is empty. For the second cellwise event, either \(2tL/m_0<p_k\), in which case the event is contained in \(\{N_k^{(0)}\le tL\}\) and the lower-tail bound applies, or the mass inequality fails, in which case the event is empty. Taking the union bound over the \(d\) categories therefore yields, for \(t\ge0\), \(n\ge2\), and every \(\mu_n\) satisfying \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling},
\[
\mu_n(\mathcal B_t)
\le
d\left[
\exp\{-(tL)(\log 2-1/2)\}
+
\exp\{-(tL)/4\}
\right].
\]

Now fix \(K>0\) and \(c>0\). Since \(\log 2-1/2>0\), set
\[
t_0
=
\max\left\{
\frac{K+4}{\log 2-1/2},\ 4(K+4)
\right\},
\qquad
C=2c,
\qquad
N=2 .
\]
For \(t\ge t_0\),
\[
K+4\le t(\log 2-1/2),
\qquad
K+4\le t/4 .
\]
Thus, whenever \(n\ge N\), \(d\le c\,nL\), and \(\mu_n\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling},
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_n(\mathcal B_t)
&\le d\left[
e^{-(tL)(\log 2-1/2)}+e^{-(tL)/4}
\right] \\
&\le
c\,nL\cdot 2e^{-(K+4)L} \\
&\le 2c\,n^2 e^{-(K+4)L} \\
&=2c\,e^{-(K+4)}n^{-K-2} \\
&\le 2c\,n^{-K}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(L\le n\) for \(n\ge1\), and
\[
e^{-qL}=e^{-q}n^{-q}
\]
has been used with \(q=K+4\), together with \(e^{-(K+4)}\le1\) and
\(n^{-K-2}\le n^{-K}\) for \(n\ge1\). This proves the arbitrary-polynomial assertion.

For the fixed calibration, take \(K=4\) and \(t=256\). The numerical inequalities
\[
8\le256(\log 2-1/2),
\qquad
8\le256/4
\]
follow from \(0.6931471803<\log 2\) and elementary arithmetic. The same decay calculation gives, for every \(c>0\), the choices \(C=2c\) and \(N=2\), and hence
\[
\mu_n(\mathcal B_{256})\le Cn^{-4}.
\]
This is the stated fixed-calibration bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer}]
With the cell-mass notation of \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}, write
\[
\mu_{ak}(P)=\frac{q_{ak1}}{q_{ak0}+q_{ak1}}
\]
for every arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and category \(k\), using the totalized real-ratio convention that the displayed value is \(0\) when \(q_{ak0}+q_{ak1}=0\).  On positive arm cells this is the usual conditional mean \(E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=k]\).
Define the control-zero subclass by
\[
\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)
:=
\left\{
P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}:
\mu_{0k}(P)=0\text{ for every }k=1,\ldots,d
\right\}.
\]
For \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)\), write the treated-arm functional as
\[
\psi_1(P)=\sum_{k=1}^d p_k\mu_{1k}.
\]
For this auxiliary subclass risk, the supremum over \(\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)\) is the order-theoretic supremum over the displayed class, equal to \(0\) when that class is empty.

Since \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), the fixed-sample control-zero lower-bound statement of \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024} gives constants \(a_\epsilon,b_\epsilon>0\) and \(N_\epsilon\in\mathbb N\) such that, for every \(n,d\) with \(0<d\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\), and \(d\le b_\epsilon n\log n\),
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\inf_{\widehat\psi}
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\psi-\psi_1(P))^2\right],
\]
where the infimum is over measurable estimators based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\).
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.ZengOneArmMinimaxLower

We next identify the ATE functional on this subclass from the displayed finite-cell definitions.  For a fixed category \(k\), set
\[
s_{ak}=q_{ak0}+q_{ak1},
\qquad
p_k=s_{0k}+s_{1k}.
\]
If \(q_k=(0,0,0,0)\), then \(\phi(q_k)=0\), \(p_k=0\), and hence
\[
\phi(q_k)=p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}).
\]
If \(q_k\ne(0,0,0,0)\), \cref{obj:def:cell-functional,obj:def:ate-functional} give
\[
\phi(q_k)
=
p_k\left\{\frac{q_{1k1}}{s_{1k}}-\frac{q_{0k1}}{s_{0k}}\right\}
=
p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}),
\]
where the second equality is the totalized definition of \(\mu_{ak}\).  Summing over categories yields
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^d p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}).
\]
For \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)\), the defining control-zero condition gives \(\mu_{0k}=0\) for every \(k\), so
\[
\tau(P)
=
\sum_{k=1}^d p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})
=
\sum_{k=1}^d p_k\mu_{1k}
=
\psi_1(P).
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.ateFunctional_eq_treated_on_controlZero

The class suprema used below are bounded above on inhabited classes.  For any overlap law \(P\), the same finite-cell identity gives
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^d p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}).
\]
The category masses satisfy \(p_k\ge0\) and \(\sum_{k=1}^d p_k=1\), and the totalized binary outcome means satisfy \(0\le\mu_{ak}\le1\).  Hence \(|\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}|\le1\) for each \(k\), so
\[
|\tau(P)|
\le
\sum_{k=1}^d p_k\,|\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}|
\le
\sum_{k=1}^d p_k
=
1.
\]
For any estimator \(\widehat\tau\), pointwise domination on the finite sample alphabet gives
\[
|\widehat\tau(o_1,\ldots,o_n)|
\le
\sum_{o'_1,\ldots,o'_n}|\widehat\tau(o'_1,\ldots,o'_n)|.
\]
Combining this inequality with \(|\tau(P)|\le1\) yields
\[
|\widehat\tau(o_1,\ldots,o_n)-\tau(P)|
\le
\sum_{o'_1,\ldots,o'_n}|\widehat\tau(o'_1,\ldots,o'_n)|+1.
\]
After squaring and integrating under \(P^{\otimes n}\),
\[
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\left\{\sum_{o_1,\ldots,o_n}|\widehat\tau(o_1,\ldots,o_n)|+1\right\}^2.
\]
For \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)\), the identity \(\tau(P)=\psi_1(P)\) gives the same bound with \(\psi_1(P)\) in place of \(\tau(P)\).  Each displayed mean squared error is also nonnegative because it is the integral of a square.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.mse_le_estimator_abs_sum_bound

Fix a measurable estimator \(\widehat\tau\).  If \(\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)\) is empty, its subclass supremum is \(0\).  The ambient supremum over \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is \(0\) when the ambient class is empty, and otherwise it is at least one nonnegative mean squared error.  Hence the estimatorwise comparison holds in the empty-subclass case.

If \(\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)\) is inhabited, take \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)\).  By the definition of the subclass, the same product law is in \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), and the target identity gives
\[
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\psi_1(P))^2\right]
=
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\sup_{Q^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_Q\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(Q))^2\right],
\]
where the upper boundedness needed for the final supremum is supplied by the preceding envelope.  Taking the supremum over \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)\) gives, for every measurable \(\widehat\tau\),
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\psi_1(P))^2\right]
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right].
\]
The same empty-class convention and nonnegativity show that every subclass worst-case risk is bounded below by \(0\).  Taking the infimum over the common measurable estimator class therefore yields
\[
\inf_{\widehat\psi}
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal D_0^{(n,d)}(\epsilon)}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\psi-\psi_1(P))^2\right]
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmMinimaxRisk_le_minimaxRisk

Combining the cited lower bound with this restriction comparison yields
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\]
for every \(n,d\) in the stated range.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.ate_lower_bound_transfer
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:near-randomization-linear-upper}[Near-randomization linear bound]
For \(n\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\), \(d\in\mathbb N\), a law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2\), and a sample law \(\mu_n\), suppose that \(\mu_n\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}. Thus \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\), \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\), and \(P\) satisfies overlap at level \(\epsilon\). Define
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
2(2A_i-1)\left(Y_i-\frac12\right).
\]
Then, for \(\tau(P)\) as in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional},
\[
\int
\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)-\tau(P)\right)^2
\,d\mu_n
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2 .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper}]
For one observation \(z=(x,a,y)\), define
\[
W(z)=2(2a-1)\left(y-\frac12\right),
\]
with \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\). Then
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n W(O_i),
\qquad
W(z)^2=1
\quad\text{for every }z .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.centeredEstimator_eq_scoreMean; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.centeredUnitScore_sq

Writing \(q_{aky}=P(A=a,X=k,Y=y)\), direct summation gives
\[
E_P[W(O)]
=
\sum_{k=1}^d
\{q_{1k1}-q_{1k0}+q_{0k0}-q_{0k1}\}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.centeredUnitScore_mean

For the sums below, set
\[
p_k=\sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{y=0}^1 q_{aky},
\qquad
\pi_k=\frac{q_{1k0}+q_{1k1}}{p_k},
\qquad
\mu_{ak}=\frac{q_{ak1}}{q_{ak0}+q_{ak1}},
\]
using ordinary real division, so a quotient with zero denominator is assigned the value zero. If \(p_k>0\), overlap and \(\epsilon>0\) make both arm masses positive, and the definitions give
\[
q_{1k1}=\mu_{1k}\pi_kp_k,
\qquad
q_{1k0}=(1-\mu_{1k})\pi_kp_k,
\]
and
\[
q_{0k1}=\mu_{0k}(1-\pi_k)p_k,
\qquad
q_{0k0}=(1-\mu_{0k})(1-\pi_k)p_k .
\]
Hence
\[
q_{1k1}-q_{1k0}+q_{0k0}-q_{0k1}
=
p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})
+
2\left(\pi_k-\frac12\right)p_k(\mu_{1k}+\mu_{0k}-1).
\]
If \(p_k=0\), all four joint masses in category \(k\) are zero; with the totalized definitions above, both weighted terms also vanish because they are multiplied by \(p_k\). Thus the same identity holds with zero contribution in that category. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.centeredScore_category_identity; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.jointMass_eq_zero_of_cellMass_eq_zero

By \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}, \(\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^d\phi(q_k)\), with \(q_k=(q_{0k0},q_{0k1},q_{1k0},q_{1k1})\). For a category with \(p_k=0\), the preceding zero-mass argument gives \(q_k=0\), so the zero-cell branch in \cref{obj:def:cell-functional} gives \(\phi(q_k)=0=p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})\). For a category with \(p_k>0\), overlap and \(\epsilon>0\) give positive treated and control arm masses, and the nonzero-cell branch of \cref{obj:def:cell-functional} gives
\[
\phi(q_k)
=
p_k\left\{
\frac{q_{1k1}}{q_{1k0}+q_{1k1}}
-
\frac{q_{0k1}}{q_{0k0}+q_{0k1}}
\right\}
=
p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}).
\]
Summing over categories yields
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^d p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}).
\]
Combining this identity with the preceding category identity gives the exact bias identity
\[
E_P[W(O)]-\tau(P)
=
\sum_{k=1}^d
2\left(\pi_k-\frac12\right)p_k(\mu_{1k}+\mu_{0k}-1).
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.centeredUnitScore_bias_identity

The class assumptions give \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\) and, on every category with \(p_k>0\),
\(\epsilon\le\pi_k\le1-\epsilon\). The totalized outcome means satisfy \(0\le\mu_{ak}\le1\) for every category and arm. Hence, for every positive-mass category,
\[
\left|\pi_k-\frac12\right|\le\frac12-\epsilon,
\qquad
|\mu_{1k}+\mu_{0k}-1|\le1,
\]
and therefore
\[
\left|2\left(\pi_k-\frac12\right)p_k(\mu_{1k}+\mu_{0k}-1)\right|
\le
2\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)p_k .
\]
For a zero-mass category, \(p_k=0\) makes both sides of this last bound equal to zero. Applying the triangle inequality to the bias identity and using \(\sum_{k=1}^d p_k=1\) gives
\[
\left|E_P[W(O)]-\tau(P)\right|
\le
\sum_{k=1}^d 2\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)p_k
=
2\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right).
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.centeredUnitScore_bias_bound; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.cellMass_sum

The sample law in the class is the product law, and \(n>0\), so
\[
E_{\mu_n}[\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}]=E_P[W(O)].
\]
Moreover, independence gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_n}(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}})
=
\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_P(W(O))
\le
\frac1n E_P[W(O)^2]
=
\frac1n .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.centeredEstimator_mean; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.centeredUnitScore_variance_le_one; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.centeredEstimator_variance_le

Finally, the bias--variance identity gives
\[
\int
\bigl(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\bigr)^2\,d\mu_n
=
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_n}(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}})
+
\left(E_{\mu_n}[\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}]-\tau(P)\right)^2 .
\]
Combining the preceding variance and bias bounds yields
\[
\int
\bigl(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\bigr)^2\,d\mu_n
\le
\frac1n+
\left\{2\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)\right\}^2
=
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2 .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.mse_eq_variance_add_sq_bias; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.near_randomization_linear_upper
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower}[One-category Bernoulli lower bound]
For any positive integers \(n\) and \(d\), the minimax mean-squared-error risk \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}, evaluated at randomization \(\epsilon=1/2\), satisfies
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower}]
Since \(d>0\), the category space is nonempty. Set
\[
\delta=\frac{2/5}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Since \(n>0\), \(\sqrt n\ge1\), and hence \(\delta\ge0\), \(\delta\le2/5\), and
\[
\delta^2=\frac{4}{25n}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.one_category_bernoulli_lower

Consider two laws \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) on the \(d\)-category observation space. Under both laws, \(X\) is uniform on the category space and \(A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\). Under \(P_0\), both treatment-arm outcome means equal \(1/2\). Under \(P_1\), the control outcome mean equals \(1/2\) and the treated outcome mean equals \(1/2+\delta\). The bounds above give \(0\le 1/2+\delta\le1\), so these Bernoulli probabilities are valid. For each \(P_j\), membership of the product sample law in \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is obtained from \(0<1/2\), \(1/2\le1/2\), the defining identity of the iid product law \(P_j^{\otimes n}\), and the overlap fact that every positive-mass category has treatment probability \(1/2\). Thus the two product sample laws belong to \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.one_category_bernoulli_lower; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.endpointParametricLaw_overlap; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.endpointParametricPertLaw_overlap

For these two laws,
\[
\tau(P_0)=0,
\qquad
\tau(P_1)=\delta,
\qquad
|\tau(P_1)-\tau(P_0)|=\delta .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.endpointParametricLaw_ate; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.endpointParametricPertLaw_ate

We next bound the total variation distance between the product laws. Write
\(p_{j,kay}=P_j(X=k,A=a,Y=y)\). The null law assigns positive mass to every singleton of the finite observation space, so \(P_1\ll P_0\), and hence \(P_1^{\otimes n}\ll P_0^{\otimes n}\). The finite chi-squared formula gives
\[
1+\chi^2(P_1\Vert P_0)
=\sum_{k=1}^d\sum_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}\frac{p_{1,kay}^2}{p_{0,kay}} .
\]
For a fixed category \(k\), the two control cells agree with the null and contribute \(1/(2d)\). The two treated cells contribute
\[
\frac{\left\{d^{-1}(1/2)(1/2-\delta)\right\}^2}{d^{-1}(1/2)(1/2)}
+
\frac{\left\{d^{-1}(1/2)(1/2+\delta)\right\}^2}{d^{-1}(1/2)(1/2)}
=
\frac{1}{2d}\left(1+\frac{\delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}\right).
\]
Summing over the \(d\) categories yields
\[
\chi^2(P_1\Vert P_0)
=
\frac{(1/2)\delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}
=2\delta^2 .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Estimation.MinimaxATE.Parametric.one_add_chiSqDiv_obsPert_obsNull
By tensorization of chi-squared divergence over the iid product law,
\[
1+\chi^2\!\left(P_1^{\otimes n}\Vert P_0^{\otimes n}\right)
=
\left(1+\frac{(1/2)\delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}\right)^n .
\]
The single-observation chi-squared value is nonnegative, so \((1+x)^n\le \exp(nx)\) gives
\[
1+\chi^2\!\left(P_1^{\otimes n}\Vert P_0^{\otimes n}\right)
\le
\exp\!\left(
n\,\frac{(1/2)\delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}
\right).
\]
Using \(\delta^2=4/(25n)\),
\[
n\,\frac{(1/2)\delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}
=
\frac{8}{25}
\le
\log 2,
\]
and therefore
\[
\chi^2\!\left(P_1^{\otimes n}\Vert P_0^{\otimes n}\right)\le1.
\]
The chi-squared-to-total-variation comparison on the finite product space, together with symmetry of total variation, yields
\[
\left\|P_0^{\otimes n}-P_1^{\otimes n}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
=
\left\|P_1^{\otimes n}-P_0^{\otimes n}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2\!\left(P_1^{\otimes n}\Vert P_0^{\otimes n}\right)}
\le
\frac12 .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Estimation.MinimaxATE.Parametric.tvDist_productLaw_le_half

Now fix any measurable estimator \(\widehat\tau\). With \(s=\delta/2\), the separation above and the total-variation bound imply
\[
\max_{j=0,1}
P_j^{\otimes n}\left(
|\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j)|\ge \frac{\delta}{2}
\right)
\ge \frac14 .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.two_point_lower_bound_of_tvDist_le

On each of the two laws,
\[
\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^2
P_j^{\otimes n}\left(
|\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j)|\ge \frac{\delta}{2}
\right)
\le
E_{P_j^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j))^2\right].
\]
Therefore
\[
\max_{j=0,1}
E_{P_j^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P_j))^2\right]
\ge
\frac{\delta^2}{16}
=
\frac{1}{100n}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.one_category_bernoulli_lower

It remains to compare these two risks with the supremum in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}. A point of that supremum is a single-observation law \(P\) together with the class membership assertion \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\). For any such \(P\), the overlap part of the membership assertion gives \(|\tau(P)|\le1\). Since the sample alphabet is finite, every sample point \(\omega\) satisfies
\[
|\widehat\tau(\omega)|
\le
\sum_{\omega'\in(\{1,
\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)^n}|\widehat\tau(\omega')| .
\]
Thus, under the product law generated by any such single-observation law \(P\),
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\left(
\sum_{\omega'\in(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)^n}|\widehat\tau(\omega')|+1
\right)^2 .
\]
This finite upper bound applies uniformly over the indexed family in the supremum of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}. Since \(P_0^{\otimes n}\) and \(P_1^{\otimes n}\) are members of \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\), that supremum is an upper bound for the two displayed risks. Hence the worst-case risk of the fixed estimator \(\widehat\tau\) is at least \(1/(100n)\). The class of measurable estimators is nonempty, for instance by the constant-zero estimator, so taking the infimum over estimators in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} gives
\[
\frac1{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.one_category_bernoulli_lower
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax}[Randomization endpoint risk]
Let \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) satisfy \(n>0\) and \(d>0\). For the minimax MSE risk \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) defined in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}, exact randomization satisfies
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \frac{1}{n}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax}]
\Cref{obj:lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower} gives the lower bound
\[
\frac1{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.one_category_bernoulli_lower; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.randomized_endpoint_minimax

For the upper bound, take the centered endpoint estimator of \cref{obj:synth_10},
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
2(2A_i-1)\left(Y_i-\frac12\right).
\]
It is measurable because the sample space is finite. To compare the infimum in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} with this estimator, first record that the worst-case values in that infimum have common lower bound \(0\) under the real-valued supremum convention used there. For a fixed measurable estimator, if \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is empty, the corresponding supremum is \(0\). If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is nonempty and the set of squared-loss integrals over \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is bounded above, choosing a class member gives an integral that is nonnegative and no larger than the supremum. If the same set is not bounded above, the real-valued supremum is again \(0\). Hence \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} gives the comparison
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}
\le
\sup_{\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}}
\int
\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)-\tau(P)\right)^2
\,d\mu_n .
\]

It remains to bound this worst-case risk. If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is empty, the displayed supremum is \(0\), and \(0\le 1/n\) because \(n>0\). Otherwise, for every member \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\), \cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} applied with \(\epsilon=1/2\) gives
\[
\int
\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)-\tau(P)\right)^2
\,d\mu_n
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\frac12\right)^2
=
\frac1n .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.near_randomization_linear_upper
Thus every term in the nonempty supremum is at most \(1/n\), so
\[
\sup_{\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}}
\int
\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)-\tau(P)\right)^2
\,d\mu_n
\le \frac1n .
\]
Therefore \(\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le 1/n\). Combining this upper bound with the lower bound proves
\[
\frac1{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \frac1n .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.randomized_endpoint_minimax
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer} is the lower half of the matched fixed-interior rate in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}. \Cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} controls the centered estimator by a sampling term and a squared distance from the randomized endpoint. Combining the one-category lower bound in \cref{obj:lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower} with that centered upper bound at \(\epsilon=1/2\) yields the endpoint bracket in \cref{obj:lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax}.

The following selector lemma turns the hybrid fixed-interior envelope and the centered near-randomization envelope into a deterministic method choice.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:combined-upper-envelope}[Combined envelope selector]
Fix constants \(\epsilon,C_\epsilon,\rho_\epsilon\in\mathbb R\) and \(N_\epsilon\in\mathbb N\). Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Interior overlap.)} \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
\item \textbf{(Positive constants.)} \(C_\epsilon>0\) and \(\rho_\epsilon>0\).
\item \textbf{(Hybrid bound.)} For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\), if \(0<n\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\), and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), then the hybrid estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} satisfies
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
\end{itemize}
Then for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(0<n\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\), and \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), set
\[
K_\epsilon=\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}.
\]
Define the \(C_\epsilon,\epsilon\)-dependent selected estimator \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}\) to be \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) when
\[
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\]
and to be \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) otherwise. This selected estimator satisfies
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\left(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
K_\epsilon\left[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},(1/2-\epsilon)^2\right\}
\right],
\]
where \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is the minimax risk of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:combined-upper-envelope}]
Fix \(n,d\) satisfying the displayed hypotheses, and set
\[
K_\epsilon=\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}.
\]
Throughout this proof, the quotient \(d^2/(n^2(\log n)^2)\) is interpreted with the paper's rate convention: it is the real-division term appearing in the definition of the rate, and equals \(0\) when the denominator is \(0\).

The centered estimator satisfies the uniform bound
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P))^2
\right]
\le \frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2.
\]
Indeed, when the experiment class is inhabited, \cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} applies to each product law in the class and the supremum preserves the same upper bound. When the class is empty, the empty worst-case supremum is the real number \(0\), and the same bound follows from nonnegativity of the displayed right-hand side. % lean: near_randomization_linear_upper

We compare the two branches in the deterministic definition of \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}\). If
\[
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right)
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2,
\]
then the selector returns \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\), and the assumed hybrid bound gives
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P))^2
\right]
\le
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right)
=
\min\!\left\{
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right),
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2
\right\}.
\]
If the displayed inequality fails, then
\[
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2
\le
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right),
\]
the selector returns \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\), and the centered bound gives the same conclusion with the second branch:
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P))^2
\right]
\le
\min\!\left\{
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right),
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2
\right\}.
\]
Thus, in all cases,
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P))^2
\right]
\le
\min\!\left\{
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right),
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2
\right\}. % lean: combined_upper_envelope
\]

The sample space is finite, so the selected estimator is measurable. For every measurable estimator, the worst-case mean squared error is bounded below by \(0\). To see this, if \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is empty, the empty worst-case supremum is \(0\). If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is inhabited and the real-valued family of mean squared errors over \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is bounded above, then a nonnegative squared-loss integral lies below the supremum. If that family is unbounded above, the totalized real supremum of the indexed family is, by convention, \(\sup\varnothing=0\), so the same lower bound holds. Hence the range over which the infimum defining \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is taken is bounded below, and evaluating that infimum at the selected estimator yields
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P))^2
\right]. % lean: measurable_of_finite
\]

It remains to simplify the deterministic envelope. Since \(K_\epsilon\ge C_\epsilon\), \(K_\epsilon\ge4\), and the scalar terms \(1/n\), \(d^2/(n^2(\log n)^2)\), and \((1/2-\epsilon)^2\) are nonnegative,
\[
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right)
\le
K_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right)
\]
and
\[
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2
\le
K_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2\right).
\]
If
\[
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\le \left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2,
\]
then the first of these deterministic bounds gives
\[
\min\!\left\{
C_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right),
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2
\right\}
\le
K_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\min\!\left(\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2\right)\right\}.
\]
In the remaining case,
\[
\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2\le \frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},
\]
the same conclusion follows from the second deterministic bound. Combining the selector bound with this deterministic comparison proves the stated upper envelope with \(K_\epsilon=\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}\). % lean: combined_upper_envelope
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:combined-upper-envelope} formalizes the comparison made in \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid}. Once the fixed-interior hybrid bound is available, the selector chooses the smaller displayed certificate between the hybrid estimator and the centered estimator, producing an upper envelope with the minimum of the large-alphabet term and the near-randomization term.

The remaining statements establish the hybrid upper bound by separating calibration, polynomial approximation, factorial-moment estimation, and heavy-cell ratio control. The first calibration result collects deterministic inequalities implied by the numerical constants in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:calibration-consequences}[Calibration consequences]
Let \(L=\log(en)\), let \(\ell=\log n\), and let \(m_1=|I_1|\) be the second split size from \cref{obj:def:sample-splits}. Let \(M=M(n)\) and \(B=B(n)\) be the polynomial degree and light-cell approximation scale from \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}. If \(n\ge N_0\) and \(L\ge240\), then
\[
0<\ell,\qquad
\ell\le L,\qquad
L\le2\ell,\qquad
0<m_1,\qquad
n\le2m_1,\qquad
M\le L,\qquad
B\le\frac{8192L}{n},\qquad
(6^{2M})^2L^6\le n .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:calibration-consequences}]
Because \(n\ge N_0\), the calibration cutoff gives
\[
\alpha_0L\ge2,\qquad
4M(n)^2\le m_1,\qquad
\frac{4M(n)}{m_1}\le \frac{3B(n)}4.
\]
In particular \(L>0\). The identity \(L=\log(en)=1+\log n=1+\ell\) is valid for this positive \(n\). Since \(L\ge240\), we have
\[
\ell=L-1>0,\qquad \ell\le L,\qquad L=1+\ell\le2\ell. % lean: large_calibration_bounds
\]

The definition \(M(n)=\max\{2,\lfloor\alpha_0L\rfloor\}\) gives \(M\ge2\). Hence \(4M^2\le m_1\) implies \(0<m_1\). For the deterministic second split,
\[
m_1=n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor,
\]
and therefore \(n\le2m_1\). % lean: splitSize_one_eq

Since \(\alpha_0L\ge2\), the maximum in the definition of \(M(n)\) is attained by the floor term:
\[
M=\lfloor\alpha_0L\rfloor.
\]
Using \(\alpha_0\le1\) and \(L>0\),
\[
M\le \alpha_0L\le L. % lean: large_calibration_bounds
\]

By the definition \(B(n)=4096L/m_1\) and the bound \(n\le2m_1\),
\[
B=\frac{4096L}{m_1}
\le
\frac{8192L}{n}. % lean: large_calibration_bounds
\]

Finally we verify the calibrated growth inequality. Since \(\alpha_0\le(32\log6)^{-1}\) and \(M\le\alpha_0L\),
\[
4M\log6\le \frac{L}{8},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
6^{4M}\le \exp(L/8).
\]
Also \(L\ge240\) gives
\[
\log L=\log 12+\log(L/12)
\le 11+(L/12-1)\le L/8,
\]
and hence
\[
L^6=\exp(6\log L)\le \exp(3L/4).
\]
Multiplying,
\[
6^{4M}L^6\le \exp(7L/8)\le \exp(L-1)=n.
\]
Since \((6^{2M})^2=6^{4M}\), this is exactly
\[
(6^{2M})^2L^6\le n. % lean: calibrated_polynomial_log_growth
\]
The displayed conclusions follow. % lean: large_calibration_bounds
\end{proof}

The Chebyshev branch is encoded through reciprocal coefficients and coefficient envelopes. These statements are the approximation-theoretic part of the construction, in the same spirit as polynomial methods for nonsmooth discrete-distribution functionals \citep{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016,HanJiaoWeissman2020}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_19}[Chebyshev reciprocal coefficients \(g_{M,j}\)]
For an integer \(M\ge2\), we say \(g_{M,j}\), \(0\le j\le M-2\), are the Chebyshev reciprocal coefficients when they are the unique real coefficients satisfying \[\frac{1-\frac{1-T_M(1-2x)}{2M^2x}}{x}=\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}g_{M,j}x^j,\] with the left-hand side interpreted by polynomial continuation at \(x=0\), where \(T_M\) is the degree-\(M\) Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:sparse-arm-expansion}[Sparse arm expansion]
For any sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), with each \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2\), and any category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), let \(M=M(n)\) and \(B=B(n)\) be the polynomial degree and light-cell approximation scale of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.  For each arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
\widehat A_{a,k}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
B^{-1} g_{M,j} B^{-j}\binom jt\,
\mathfrak m_{k,r^{a,c,j,t}},
\]
where \(g_{M,j}\) are the coefficients of \(G_M\), \(r^{a,c,j,t}\) is the four-cell exponent vector determined by
\(u^{r^{a,c,j,t}}=u_cu_{a1}u_{a0}^{\,t}u_{a1}^{\,j-t}\), and \(\mathfrak m_{k,r^{a,c,j,t}}\) is the corresponding normalized factorial-moment monomial computed from the sample.  Then the factorial-moment polynomial estimate for category \(k\) satisfies
\[
\widehat P_k=\widehat A_{1,k}-\widehat A_{0,k}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:sparse-arm-expansion}]
Fix the sample and the category \(k\), and abbreviate \(M=M(n)\) and \(B=B(n)\), using the split and calibration notation of \cref{obj:def:sample-splits,obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.  For a cell \(c\in\{0,1\}^2\), let \(e_c\) be the four-cell unit multi-index.  For each arm \(a\), degree index \(j\), and binomial index \(t\), the sparse index used in the factorial lift is
\[
r^{a,c,j,t}
=
e_c+e_{(a,1)}+t\,e_{(a,0)}+(j-t)\,e_{(a,1)}.
\]
Equivalently, its associated four-cell monomial is \(u_cu_{a1}u_{a0}^{\,t}u_{a1}^{\,j-t}\).  Let
\[
\gamma_{j,t}
=
B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt .
\]
The normalized factorial-moment monomial \(\mathfrak m_{k,r}\) is the one in \cref{obj:synth_3}.  Thus the sparse arm contribution for arm \(a\) is precisely
\[
\widehat A_{a,k}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
\gamma_{j,t}\,
\mathfrak m_{k,r^{a,c,j,t}} .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.factorialExpansionIndex
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.sparseArmContribution

The sparse factorial-polynomial contribution for category \(k\) is defined as the treated sparse arm minus the control sparse arm.  Substituting the preceding display for \(a=1\) and \(a=0\) gives
\[
\widehat P_k
=
\widehat A_{1,k}-\widehat A_{0,k}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.factorialPolynomialContribution_eq_sparseArms
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:factorial-moment-mean}[Factorial mean identity]
Let \(P\) be a probability law for one observation \(O=(X,A,Y)\) with \(X\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\) and \(A,Y\in\{0,1\}\), and let \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\). Let \(P^{\otimes\infty}\) denote the ambient infinite iid product law generated by \(P\). Suppose that \(n\ge N_0\), where \(N_0\) is the calibration cutoff in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}. For
\[
q_k=\bigl(P(X=k,A=a,Y=y)\bigr)_{a,y\in\{0,1\}},
\]
the factorial-moment polynomial estimate \(\widehat P_k\) from \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, evaluated on the first \(n\) coordinates under \(P^{\otimes\infty}\), satisfies
\[
\int \widehat P_k(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\,dP^{\otimes\infty}
=
P_{M(n),B(n)}(q_k).
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:factorial-moment-mean}]
Write
\[
\mu_\infty=P^{\otimes\infty},\qquad
M=M(n),\qquad B=B(n),\qquad m_1=|I_1|,
\]
where \(\mu_\infty\) is the ambient iid product law.  For a cell
\(c=(a,y)\in\{0,1\}^2\), write
\[
q_c=q_{aky}=P(X=k,A=a,Y=y),
\qquad
q_k=(q_c)_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}.
\]
Since \(n\ge N_0\), the calibration in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} gives
\[
4M^2\le m_1 .
\]
Because \(M\ge2\), this implies \(M\le m_1\).
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.calibrationCutoff

For any multi-index \(r=(r_c)_c\) with \(|r|\le m_1\), the normalized factorial monomial satisfies
\[
\int
\mathfrak m_{k,r}\,d\mu_\infty
=
\prod_{c\in\{0,1\}^2} q_c^{\,r_c}.
\]
Indeed, on the estimation split \(I_1\), define
\[
L_i=
\begin{cases}
(A_i,Y_i),& X_i=k,\\
\star,& X_i\ne k .
\end{cases}
\]
The variables \(L_i\), \(i\in I_1\), are iid, and \(P(L_i=c)=q_c\).  Expanding
\(\prod_c (N^{(1)}_{kc})_{r_c}\) counts ordered injective assignments of \(|r|\) requested labels to distinct indices in \(I_1\).  There are \((m_1)_{|r|}\) such assignments, each has probability \(\prod_c q_c^{r_c}\), and division by \((m_1)_{|r|}\) gives the displayed identity.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.integral_factorialMonomial_trunc

For the sparse arms in \cref{obj:lem:sparse-arm-expansion}, write
\[
\widehat A_{a,k}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\,
\mathfrak m_{k,r^{a,c,j,t}} .
\]
That lemma gives the pointwise identity
\[
\widehat P_k=\widehat A_{1,k}-\widehat A_{0,k}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.factorialPolynomialContribution_eq_sparseArms

For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), \(j\in\{0,\ldots,M-2\}\), \(t\in\{0,\ldots,j\}\), and \(c\in\{0,1\}^2\), the sparse exponent
\[
r^{a,c,j,t}
=
e_c+e_{a1}+t\,e_{a0}+(j-t)\,e_{a1}
\]
has total degree
\[
|r^{a,c,j,t}|=j+2\le M\le m_1 .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.multiDegree_factorialExpansionIndex
Therefore the factorial-monomial identity applies in every summand of each sparse arm.  Finite-sum linearity gives, for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\int \widehat A_{a,k}\,d\mu_\infty
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\,
q_k^{\,r^{a,c,j,t}} .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.integral_factorialPolynomialContribution_trunc

By the definition of \(r^{a,c,j,t}\),
\[
q_k^{\,r^{a,c,j,t}}
=
q_c\,q_{a1}\,q_{a0}^{\,t}\,q_{a1}^{\,j-t}.
\]
Thus, for each fixed \(a\) and \(j\),
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
\binom jt q_k^{\,r^{a,c,j,t}}
=
\left(\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}q_c\right)
q_{a1}
(q_{a0}+q_{a1})^j,
\]
by the binomial theorem.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.factorialExpansionIndex_binomial_sum
Consequently, for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\int \widehat A_{a,k}\,d\mu_\infty
=
\frac{s(q_k)t_a(q_k)}{B}
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}
g_{M,j}
\left(\frac{s_a(q_k)}{B}\right)^j
=
\frac{s(q_k)t_a(q_k)}{B}
G_M\!\left(\frac{s_a(q_k)}{B}\right),
\]
where \(t_1(q_k)=q_{11}\) and \(t_0(q_k)=q_{01}\).
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.integral_factorialPolynomialContribution_trunc

Subtracting the two arm identities and using \cref{obj:lem:sparse-arm-expansion},
\[
\int \widehat P_k\,d\mu_\infty
=
\frac{s(q_k)t_1(q_k)}{B}
G_M\!\left(\frac{s_1(q_k)}{B}\right)
-
\frac{s(q_k)t_0(q_k)}{B}
G_M\!\left(\frac{s_0(q_k)}{B}\right)
=
P_{M,B}(q_k),
\]
where the final equality is the definition of the light-cell polynomial in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.  Substituting \(M=M(n)\) and \(B=B(n)\) gives the claimed identity.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.integral_factorialPolynomialContribution_trunc
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:coefficient-envelope}[Absolute coefficient envelope]
Let \(P_{M,B}\) be the light-cell polynomial approximation in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, and write
\[
P_{M,B}(u)=\sum_{r} c_r u^r
\]
in multi-index form over the four treatment--outcome cells. Suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Degree.)} \(M\in\mathbb N\).
\item \textbf{(Scale.)} The approximation scale satisfies \(B>0\).
\item \textbf{(Evaluation vector.)} The four-cell vector \(v\) is nonnegative, so \(v_{ay}\ge 0\) for every cell \((a,y)\).
\end{itemize}
Then, with \(s(v)\) denoting the total four-cell mass from \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle},
\[
\sum_{r\in \operatorname{supp}(P_{M,B})}|c_r|\,v^r
\le
B\,6^M\max\left\{\left(\frac{s(v)}{B}\right)^2,\left(\frac{s(v)}{B}\right)^M\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:coefficient-envelope}]
For a polynomial \(Q(u)=\sum_r q_ru^r\), write its absolute coefficient envelope at the nonnegative vector \(v\) as
\[
\mathcal A_v(Q)=\sum_{r\in\operatorname{supp}(Q)}|q_r|\prod_i v_i^{r_i}.
\]
For \(Q=P_{M,B}\), this is the left-hand side of the asserted inequality. If \(M=0\) or \(M=1\), the defining range \(j\in\{0,\ldots,M-2\}\) is empty, so \(P_{M,B}=0\); the right-hand side is nonnegative because \(B>0\) and \(v\ge0\). % lean: cellApproxPolynomial_coeff_envelope

Assume now that \(M\ge2\). Write the polynomial continuation as
\[
G_M(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}g_{M,j}x^j,
\qquad
 g_{M,j}=(-1)^j\frac{2^{2j+3}}{M(M+j+2)}{M+j+2\choose 2j+4}.
\]
Set
\[
R=s(v),\qquad z=\frac RB,
\qquad
\Gamma_M(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|z^j.
\]
Then \(R\ge0\) and \(z\ge0\). The coefficient envelope satisfies, for nonnegative \(v\),
\[
\mathcal A_v(Q_1-Q_0)\le \mathcal A_v(Q_1)+\mathcal A_v(Q_0),
\qquad
\mathcal A_v(Q_1Q_2)\le \mathcal A_v(Q_1)\mathcal A_v(Q_2),
\]
and iterating the product bound gives the corresponding four-factor bound. % lean: coefficientEnvelope_sub_le coefficientEnvelope_mul_le env_mul4_le
For the linear mass polynomials,
\[
\mathcal A_v(s)\le R,
\qquad
\mathcal A_v(s_a)\le s_a(v)\le R,
\qquad
\mathcal A_v(u_{a1})=v_{a1}.
\] % lean: env_mass_le env_arm_le env_X
Thus, for each arm \(a\), the inner polynomial obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_v\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}g_{M,j}\{B^{-1}s_a(u)\}^j\right)
&\le \sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|
       \{B^{-1}\mathcal A_v(s_a)\}^j  \\
&\le \sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|(R/B)^j
=\Gamma_M(z).
\end{aligned}
\] % lean: env_evalG_le
Combining this bound with the four-factor envelope inequality gives
\[
\mathcal A_v\!\left(
B^{-1}s(u)u_{a1}
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}g_{M,j}\{B^{-1}s_a(u)\}^j
\right)
\le
B^{-1}R\,v_{a1}\,\Gamma_M(z),
\] % lean: env_mul4_le env_mass_le env_X gpos_nonneg
and applying the subtraction bound to the treated and control terms in \(P_{M,B}\) yields
\[
\mathcal A_v(P_{M,B})
\le
B^{-1}R\{v_{11}+v_{01}\}\Gamma_M(z)
\le
B^{-1}R^2\Gamma_M(z),
\]
because \(v_{11}+v_{01}\le s(v)=R\). % lean: cellApproxPolynomial_coeff_envelope

It remains to bound \(\Gamma_M\). Let \(T_M\) be the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. The shifted-Chebyshev expansion is
\[
T_M(1-2x)
=1+\sum_{j=1}^{M}(-1)^j\frac{M}{M+j}{M+j\choose 2j}4^j x^j.
\]
Separating the \(j=1\) term and reindexing the remaining terms by \(j=k+2\) gives
\[
T_M(1-2x)
=1-2M^2x+2M^2x^2\sum_{k=0}^{M-2}g_{M,k}x^k
=1-2M^2x+2M^2x^2G_M(x).
\]
Since \(M>0\), the explicit coefficient formula gives
\[
|g_{M,j}|=(-1)^jg_{M,j},
\qquad
\Gamma_M(t)=G_M(-t)\quad(t\ge0).
\]
Putting \(x=-1\) in the shifted identity therefore yields
\[
T_M(3)=1+2M^2+2M^2\Gamma_M(1).
\]
Because \(M\ge1\) and \(\Gamma_M(1)\ge0\), this implies
\[
\Gamma_M(1)\le T_M(3).
\]
The standard inequality \(T_m(x)\ge1\) for \(x\ge1\) gives \(T_m(3)\ge0\). Together with the recurrence \(T_{m+2}(3)=6T_{m+1}(3)-T_m(3)\) and the initial values \(T_0(3)=1\), \(T_1(3)=3\), induction gives
\[
T_M(3)\le6^M.
\]
Therefore \(\Gamma_M(1)\le6^M\). If \(0\le z\le1\), termwise comparison in the nonnegative-power sum gives \(\Gamma_M(z)\le\Gamma_M(1)\le6^M\). If \(z\ge1\), then \(z^j\le z^{M-2}\) for every \(0\le j\le M-2\), hence
\[
\Gamma_M(z)\le z^{M-2}\Gamma_M(1)\le6^Mz^{M-2}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\Gamma_M(z)\le6^M\max\{1,z^{M-2}\}\qquad(z\ge0). % lean: gpos_bound
\]

Combining the coefficient-envelope bound with this estimate,
\[
\mathcal A_v(P_{M,B})
\le
B^{-1}R^2\,6^M\max\{1,z^{M-2}\}.
\]
Since \(R=Bz\), \(B>0\), and \(M\ge2\),
\[
B^{-1}R^2\,6^M\max\{1,z^{M-2}\}
=
B\,6^M\max\{z^2,z^M\}.
\]
Substituting \(z=s(v)/B\) gives the claimed coefficient envelope. % lean: cellApproxPolynomial_coeff_envelope
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:chebyshev-reciprocal-certificate}[Polynomial approximation certificate]
Fix a degree \(M\in\mathbb N\), a real scale \(B\), the overlap level \(\epsilon\), and a four-cell vector \(u\). Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Degree.)} \(M\ge 1\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap level.)} \(\epsilon>0\).
\item \textbf{(Cone membership.)} \(u\in\mathcal C_\epsilon\), where \(\mathcal C_\epsilon\) is the overlap-restricted cone in \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone}.
\item \textbf{(Mass bound.)} \(s(u)\le B\).
\end{itemize}
For the light-cell polynomial \(P_{M,B}\) from \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} and the homogeneous cell contribution \(\phi\) from \cref{obj:def:cell-functional},
\[
\left|P_{M,B}(u)-\phi(u)\right|\le \frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:chebyshev-reciprocal-certificate}]
Let \(G_M(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}g_{M,j}x^j\). The shifted-Chebyshev expansion gives, for every real \(x\),
\[
T_M(1-2x)=1-2M^2x+2M^2x^2G_M(x).
\]
If \(0\le x\le1\), then \(|T_M(1-2x)|\le1\), and the displayed identity implies
\[
x\,|1-xG_M(x)|\le \frac1{M^2}.
\]
% lean: chebyshev_gPolynomial_identity
% lean: gPolynomial_certificate

First consider \(u=0\). Then \(s(u)=s_0(u)=s_1(u)=u_{01}=u_{11}=0\), so both \(P_{M,B}(u)\) and \(\phi(u)\) equal \(0\). Since \(s(u)\le B\), this case also gives \(B\ge0\), and hence
\[
|P_{M,B}(u)-\phi(u)|=0\le \frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}.
\]
% lean: cellApproxPolynomial_error

Now suppose \(u\ne0\). Nonnegativity of the four coordinates gives \(s(u)\ge0\), and \(u\ne0\) gives \(s(u)>0\). Since \(s(u)\le B\), we have \(B>0\). The overlap inequalities give
\[
s_1(u)\ge \epsilon s(u)>0,
\qquad
s_0(u)=s(u)-s_1(u)\ge \epsilon s(u)>0 .
\]
Also \(s_a(u)\le s(u)\le B\), so
\[
x_a:=\frac{s_a(u)}{B}\in[0,1],
\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
\]
% lean: cellApproxPolynomial_error

For each arm \(a\), the preceding Chebyshev certificate gives
\[
\left|1-\frac{s_a(u)}{B}G_M\!\left(\frac{s_a(u)}{B}\right)\right|
\le
\frac{B}{s_a(u)M^2}.
\]
Using \(0\le u_{a1}\le s_a(u)\), this yields
\[
\left|
B^{-1}s(u)u_{a1}G_M\!\left(\frac{s_a(u)}{B}\right)
-
s(u)\frac{u_{a1}}{s_a(u)}
\right|
\le
\frac{B}{\epsilon M^2}.
\]
Indeed, the left-hand side equals
\[
\frac{s(u)u_{a1}}{s_a(u)}
\left|1-\frac{s_a(u)}{B}G_M\!\left(\frac{s_a(u)}{B}\right)\right|,
\]
and the factor \(s(u)/s_a(u)\) is at most \(1/\epsilon\). % lean: cellApproxPolynomial_error

Finally,
\[
P_{M,B}(u)-\phi(u)
=
\left[
B^{-1}s(u)u_{11}G_M\!\left(\frac{s_1(u)}{B}\right)
-
s(u)\frac{u_{11}}{s_1(u)}
\right]
-
\left[
B^{-1}s(u)u_{01}G_M\!\left(\frac{s_0(u)}{B}\right)
-
s(u)\frac{u_{01}}{s_0(u)}
\right].
\]
The triangle inequality and the two arm bounds give
\[
|P_{M,B}(u)-\phi(u)|
\le
\frac{B}{\epsilon M^2}+\frac{B}{\epsilon M^2}
=
\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}.
\]
% lean: cellApproxPolynomial_error
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:sparse-arm-expansion} rewrites the light-cell statistic as treated and control sparse-arm pieces. \Cref{obj:lem:factorial-moment-mean} then identifies the mean of the factorial-moment statistic with the polynomial target \(P_{M(n),B(n)}(q_k)\). The envelope in \cref{obj:lem:coefficient-envelope} controls absolute polynomial coefficients at nonnegative evaluation vectors, and \cref{obj:lem:chebyshev-reciprocal-certificate} supplies the light-cell approximation error on the overlap cone when the cell mass lies below the approximation scale.

Heavy categories are controlled by ratio residuals and missing-arm probabilities. The next two bounds apply to deterministic category sets, which allows them to be conditioned on the pilot split before summing over the selected heavy categories.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:heavy-residual-bound}[Fixed-set residual variance]
Let \(P\) be a probability law on the finite observed-data alphabet
\(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2\), let \(I\) be a finite index set, and write
\(m=|I|\).  For observations \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\), \(i\in I\), drawn independently
from \(P\), let \(H\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\) be deterministic and fix
\(a\in\{0,1\}\).

Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Overlap.)} The overlap level \(\epsilon\) satisfies \(\epsilon>0\), and for every category \(k\) with \(p_k=P(X=k)>0\),
\[
\epsilon\le \pi_k\le 1-\epsilon .
\]
\item \textbf{(Positive mass on \(H\).)} For every \(k\in H\), \(p_k>0\).
\end{itemize}

With counts computed from the sample,
\[
\mathcal R_a(H)
=
\sum_{k\in H}
N_k\frac{\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}>0\}}{N_{ak}}
\left\{N_{ak1}-\mu_{ak}N_{ak}\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
E_P\!\left[\mathcal R_a(H)^2\right]
\le
\frac{2m}{\epsilon}\sum_{k\in H}p_k .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:heavy-residual-bound}]
For an observation array \(z=(O_i)_{i\in I}\), with \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\), write
\[
N_k(z)=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k\},
\qquad
N_{ak}(z)=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\},
\]
and
\[
N_{ak1}(z)=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a,Y_i=1\}.
\]
Let \(m=|I|\), and put
\[
C_{ak}(z)
=
N_k(z)\frac{\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}(z)>0\}}{N_{ak}(z)},
\qquad
\xi_{ak,i}(z)
=
\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\}\{Y_i-\mu_{ak}\}.
\]
With the convention that the displayed ratio is zero when \(N_{ak}(z)=0\), the residual can be written as
\[
\mathcal R_a(H)
=
\sum_{k\in H}C_{ak}(z)\sum_{i\in I}\xi_{ak,i}(z),
\]
because \(\sum_i\xi_{ak,i}=N_{ak1}(z)-\mu_{ak}N_{ak}(z)\).

Expand the square as a double sum over pairs \((k,i)\) and \((l,j)\). If \(i\ne j\), then the factor
\(C_{ak}(z)C_{al}(z)\xi_{al,j}(z)\) is unchanged when only the outcome coordinate of observation \(i\) is replaced; conditioning on all other coordinates and using the centered arm-category residual gives integral zero. If \(i=j\) and \(k\ne l\), then the two category indicators cannot both hold for the same observation, so the product of residuals is identically zero. Hence only the diagonal terms remain:
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[\mathcal R_a(H)^2\right]
=
\sum_{k\in H}\sum_{i\in I}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[C_{ak}(z)^2\xi_{ak,i}(z)^2\right].
\]

For each fixed \(k\) and \(i\), the multiplier \(C_{ak}(z)^2\) is nonnegative and depends only on the category and treatment coordinates of the array. The one-coordinate conditional Bernoulli variance bound gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[C_{ak}(z)^2\xi_{ak,i}(z)^2\right]
\le
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[
C_{ak}(z)^2\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\}
\right].
\]
Therefore, for each fixed \(k\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i\in I}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[C_{ak}(z)^2\xi_{ak,i}(z)^2\right]
&\le
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[
\sum_{i\in I}C_{ak}(z)^2\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\}
\right]\\
&=
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[
N_k(z)^2\frac{\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}(z)>0\}}{N_{ak}(z)}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(\rho_{ak}=P(A=a\mid X=k)\), so \(\rho_{1k}=\pi_k\) and \(\rho_{0k}=1-\pi_k\). For \(k\in H\), the positive-mass hypothesis gives \(p_k>0\). Overlap gives \(\rho_{ak}\ge\epsilon\), and \(\rho_{ak}\le1\) because it is an arm probability. Put
\[
E_k=\{X=k\},
\qquad
R_{ak}=\{X=k,A=a\}.
\]
Then \(R_{ak}\subset E_k\), and under the one-observation law
\[
P(E_k)=p_k,
\quad
P(R_{ak})=p_k\rho_{ak},
\quad
P(E_k\setminus R_{ak})=p_k(1-\rho_{ak}),
\quad
P(E_k^c)=1-p_k.
\]
Partition the product sample space according to
\[
U=\{i:O_i\in E_k\},
\qquad
V=\{i:O_i\in R_{ak}\}.
\]
The nested relation forces \(V\subset U\). For fixed \(U,V\) with \(V\subset U\), independence gives the product probability
\[
(p_k\rho_{ak})^{|V|}\{p_k(1-\rho_{ak})\}^{|U|-|V|}(1-p_k)^{m-|U|},
\]
and the integrand equals
\[
|U|^2\frac{\mathbf 1\{|V|>0\}}{|V|}.
\]
Summing over all such pairs and then collecting first by \(t=|U|\) and then by \(s=|V|\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[
N_k^2\frac{\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}>0\}}{N_{ak}}
\right] \\
&\quad=
\sum_{t=0}^{m}{m\choose t}p_k^t(1-p_k)^{m-t}t^2
\sum_{s=0}^{t}{t\choose s}\rho_{ak}^{\,s}(1-\rho_{ak})^{t-s}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{s>0\}}{s}.
\end{aligned}
\]

It remains to bound the inner reciprocal-count sum. For every integer \(s\ge0\), with the left side interpreted as zero at \(s=0\),
\[
\frac{\mathbf 1\{s>0\}}{s}\le \frac{2}{s+1}.
\]
Since \(0<\rho_{ak}\le1\), the binomial weights are nonnegative, and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{s=0}^{t}{t\choose s}\rho_{ak}^{\,s}(1-\rho_{ak})^{t-s}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{s>0\}}{s}\\
&\quad\le
2\sum_{s=0}^{t}{t\choose s}\rho_{ak}^{\,s}(1-\rho_{ak})^{t-s}\frac{1}{s+1}\\
&\quad=
\frac{2}{(t+1)\rho_{ak}}
\sum_{s=0}^{t}{t+1\choose s+1}\rho_{ak}^{\,s+1}(1-\rho_{ak})^{t-s}\\
&\quad=
\frac{2\{1-(1-\rho_{ak})^{t+1}\}}{(t+1)\rho_{ak}}
\le
\frac{2}{(t+1)\rho_{ak}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the middle equality uses
\({t\choose s}/(s+1)={t+1\choose s+1}/(t+1)\), and the final inequality uses \((1-\rho_{ak})^{t+1}\ge0\). Hence, using \(t^2/(t+1)\le t\) and the binomial first moment,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[
N_k^2\frac{\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}>0\}}{N_{ak}}
\right]
&\le
\sum_{t=0}^{m}{m\choose t}p_k^t(1-p_k)^{m-t}\frac{2t}{\rho_{ak}}\\
&=
\frac{2mp_k}{\rho_{ak}}
\le
\frac{2mp_k}{\epsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Summing over \(k\in H\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes I}}\!\left[\mathcal R_a(H)^2\right]
\le
\sum_{k\in H}\frac{2mp_k}{\epsilon}
=
\frac{2m}{\epsilon}\sum_{k\in H}p_k .
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:missing-arm-envelope}[Missing-arm second moment]
Let \(J\) be a finite sample-index set and write \(m=|J|\). For a probability law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2\), a deterministic category set \(H\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\), and an arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
Z_{ak}=N_k\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}=0\},
\]
where \(N_k\) is the number of observations in category \(k\) and \(N_{ak}\) is the number of observations in category \(k\) and arm \(a\) in the \(J\)-indexed iid sample from \(P\).

Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(m\ge 3\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap.)} The overlap level \(\epsilon\) satisfies \(\epsilon>0\), and for every category \(k\) with \(p_k=P(X=k)>0\),
\[
\epsilon\le P(A=1\mid X=k)\le 1-\epsilon .
\]
\item \textbf{(Mass floor.)} \(B>0\), and \(p_k\ge B\) for every \(k\in H\).
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes J}}\left[
\left(\sum_{k\in H} Z_{ak}\right)^2
\right]
\le
m+m^2
\left[
\frac{|H|}
{\left\{\left((m-2)/2\right)\epsilon\right\}^2 B}
\right]^2 .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:missing-arm-envelope}]
For a sample \(z=(O_1,\ldots,O_m)\), let \(Z_{ak}(z)\) be the missing-arm category count
\[
Z_{ak}(z)=
\begin{cases}
\#\{i:X_i=k\}, & \#\{i:X_i=k,\ A_i=a\}=0,\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus \(\sum_{k\in H}Z_{ak}(z)\) is the fixed missing-arm count over \(H\) for arm \(a\). Let
\[
\rho_{ak}=P(A=a\mid X=k),
\qquad
u=\frac{m-2}{2}\epsilon,
\qquad
f_k=p_k\exp(-up_k).
\]
The assumptions \(m\ge3\), \(\epsilon>0\), and \(B>0\) give \(u>0\), and the mass floor gives
\(p_k\ge B>0\) for every \(k\in H\). Hence \(\rho_{ak}\) is the ordinary conditional arm probability on
\(H\), with \(0\le \rho_{ak}\le1\), and overlap gives
\(\epsilon\le \rho_{ak}\), equivalently \(\epsilon p_k\le p_k\rho_{ak}\). % lean: integral_fixedMissingCount_sq_le

We first record the exact second moment. For a fixed category \(k\), expand \(Z_{ak}^2\) by separating
one selected index from two ordered distinct selected indices. The one-index event requires
\(O_i\) to lie in category \(k\) outside arm \(a\), while the whole sample avoids the arm-\(a\)
subcell in category \(k\). Its probability is
\[
p_k(1-\rho_{ak})(1-p_k\rho_{ak})^{m-1}.
\]
For an ordered pair \(i\ne j\), the corresponding two-index event has probability
\[
\{p_k(1-\rho_{ak})\}^2(1-p_k\rho_{ak})^{m-2}.
\]
Summing over the \(m\) one-index choices and the \((m)_2=m(m-1)\) ordered pairs gives the diagonal
contribution
\[
m\,p_k(1-\rho_{ak})(1-p_k\rho_{ak})^{m-1}
+
(m)_2\{p_k(1-\rho_{ak})\}^2(1-p_k\rho_{ak})^{m-2}.
\]
For distinct \(k,l\), the same-index part in \(Z_{ak}Z_{al}\) is zero because the category events are
disjoint. The remaining ordered-pair event places one selected observation in category \(k\) outside arm
\(a\), the other in category \(l\) outside arm \(a\), and the whole sample outside the two forbidden
arm-\(a\) subcells. Since those forbidden subcells are disjoint, its probability is
\[
p_k(1-\rho_{ak})p_l(1-\rho_{al})
(1-p_k\rho_{ak}-p_l\rho_{al})^{m-2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int \left(\sum_{k\in H}Z_{ak}(z)\right)^2\,dP^{\otimes m}(z)
&=
\sum_{k\in H}
\Bigl[
m\,p_k(1-\rho_{ak})(1-p_k\rho_{ak})^{m-1}
\\
&\qquad\qquad
+
(m)_2\{p_k(1-\rho_{ak})\}^2(1-p_k\rho_{ak})^{m-2}
\Bigr]
\\
&\quad+
\sum_{k\in H}\sum_{l\in H\setminus\{k\}}
(m)_2 p_k(1-\rho_{ak})p_l(1-\rho_{al})
(1-p_k\rho_{ak}-p_l\rho_{al})^{m-2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
% lean: integral_fixedMissingCount_sq_eq

For the diagonal terms, \(0\le p_k\le1\), \(0\le\rho_{ak}\le1\), \((m)_2\le m^2\), and
\(\epsilon p_k\le p_k\rho_{ak}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
&m\,p_k(1-\rho_{ak})(1-p_k\rho_{ak})^{m-1}
+
(m)_2\{p_k(1-\rho_{ak})\}^2(1-p_k\rho_{ak})^{m-2}
\\
&\qquad\le
m p_k+m^2 f_k^2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the first summand is at most \(m p_k\). For the second summand,
\[
(1-p_k\rho_{ak})^{m-2}
\le
\exp\{-(m-2)\epsilon p_k\}
=
\exp(-up_k)^2,
\]
and \(p_k(1-\rho_{ak})\le p_k\). % lean: missing_diag_envelope

For \(k\ne l\), disjointness of the two category events gives \(p_k+p_l\le1\), and hence
\(p_k\rho_{ak}+p_l\rho_{al}\le1\). Together with the two overlap inequalities,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-p_k\rho_{ak}-p_l\rho_{al})^{m-2}
&\le
\exp\{-(m-2)(p_k\rho_{ak}+p_l\rho_{al})\}
\\
&\le
\exp\{-(m-2)\epsilon(p_k+p_l)\}
\\
&\le
\exp(-up_k)\exp(-up_l).
\end{aligned}
\]
Using also \(p_k(1-\rho_{ak})\le p_k\), \(p_l(1-\rho_{al})\le p_l\), and \((m)_2\le m^2\), we obtain
\[
(m)_2 p_k(1-\rho_{ak})p_l(1-\rho_{al})
(1-p_k\rho_{ak}-p_l\rho_{al})^{m-2}
\le
m^2 f_kf_l .
\]
% lean: missing_cross_envelope

Substituting the diagonal and cross envelopes into the exact expansion and collecting the square of the
exponential sum gives
\[
\int \left(\sum_{k\in H}Z_{ak}(z)\right)^2\,dP^{\otimes m}(z)
\le
m\sum_{k\in H}p_k
+
m^2\left(\sum_{k\in H}f_k\right)^2 .
\]
The category masses over \(H\) sum to at most one, so the first term is at most \(m\). % lean: integral_fixedMissingCount_sq_le

It remains to bound the exponential sum. For \(t>0\),
\[
\exp(-t)\le t^{-2}.
\]
Applying this with \(t=up_k\), and using \(p_k\ge B\), gives
\[
p_k\exp(-up_k)
\le
\frac{1}{u^2p_k}
\le
\frac{1}{u^2B}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{k\in H}f_k
\le
\frac{|H|}{u^2B}.
\]
% lean: exp_neg_le_inv_sq
% lean: sum_mass_mul_exp_neg_mul_le

Since each \(f_k\ge0\), combining the last two bounds and substituting
\(u=((m-2)/2)\epsilon\) yields
\[
\int \left(\sum_{k\in H}Z_{ak}(z)\right)^2\,dP^{\otimes m}(z)
\le
m+m^2
\left[
\frac{|H|}
{\left(\left(\frac{m-2}{2}\epsilon\right)^2B\right)}
\right]^2 .
\]
% lean: integral_fixedMissingCount_sq_le
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:heavy-residual-bound} gives the variance scale for centered ratio noise over a fixed heavy set. \Cref{obj:lem:missing-arm-envelope} controls the contribution from categories whose empirical denominator in an arm is zero; the mass floor supplied by the pilot sandwich makes this event sufficiently rare in aggregate.

For light categories, the factorial-moment analysis uses product-moment and coefficient-sum controls. These statements quantify how multinomial dependence across cells and categories enters the variance calculation.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:factorial-product-moment}[Factorial product moment]
Fix \(n,d\in\mathbb N\), \(P\in\mathsf{Prob}(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)\), a category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), and four-cell multi-indices \(r,s\). Using the split counts and split size in \cref{obj:def:sample-splits}, let
\[
\mathfrak m_{k,r}
=
\frac{\prod_{(a,y)}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}}{(m_1)_{|r|}},
\]
where \((z)_t\) denotes the falling factorial and \(|r|=\sum_{(a,y)}r_{ay}\). Let \(q_{aky}\) denote the treatment--outcome cell mass in category \(k\), as in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}. Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Nonempty estimation split.)} \(m_1>0\).
\item \textbf{(Degree ordering.)} \(|r|\le |s|\).
\item \textbf{(Split-size condition.)} \(4|s|^2\le m_1\).
\end{itemize}
Then, under the iid product law generated by \(P\),
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\mathfrak m_{k,r}\mathfrak m_{k,s}\right]
\le \mathrm e\,\prod_{(a,y)}q_{aky}^{\,r_{ay}}
\prod_{(a,y)}
\left(q_{aky}+\frac{|s|}{m_1}\right)^{s_{ay}} .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:factorial-product-moment}]
Let \(\mathcal C=\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), let \(m=m_1\), and write
\[
q_{ay}=q_{aky},\qquad r_{ay}=r(a,y),\qquad s_{ay}=s(a,y),
\qquad |r|=\sum_{(a,y)\in\mathcal C}r_{ay},\quad |s|=\sum_{(a,y)\in\mathcal C}s_{ay}.
\]
Adjoin one extra label \(\varnothing\) for observations outside category \(k\).  For the \(j\)-th observation in the estimation split define
\[
Z_j=
\begin{cases}
(a,y),& (X_j,A_j,Y_j)=(k,a,y),\\
\varnothing,& X_j\ne k,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\alpha_{ay}=q_{ay},\quad
\alpha_{\varnothing}=1-\sum_{(a,y)\in\mathcal C}q_{ay}.
\]
The variables \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\) are iid on \(\mathcal C\cup\{\varnothing\}\) with masses \(\alpha\).  Extend \(r,s\) by setting \(r_{\varnothing}=s_{\varnothing}=0\).  If \(N_b=\#\{j:Z_j=b\}\), then
\[
\mathfrak m_{k,r}
=
\frac{\prod_{b\in\mathcal C\cup\{\varnothing\}}(N_b)_{r_b}}{(m)_{|r|}},
\qquad
\mathfrak m_{k,s}
=
\frac{\prod_{b\in\mathcal C\cup\{\varnothing\}}(N_b)_{s_b}}{(m)_{|s|}} .
\]
This is just the split-count notation of \cref{obj:def:sample-splits}, with the outside-category exponent equal to zero. % lean: factorialMonomial_trunc_eq_iidCount

For an overlap choice
\[
H=(H_b)_{b\in\mathcal C\cup\{\varnothing\}},
\qquad
0\le H_b\le \min\{r_b,s_b\},
\]
set
\[
|H|=\sum_b H_b,\qquad
t_b(H)=r_b+s_b-H_b,\qquad
A_H=\prod_b {r_b\choose H_b}{s_b\choose H_b}H_b! .
\]
The cellwise falling-factorial product identity gives
\[
\prod_b (N_b)_{r_b}\prod_b (N_b)_{s_b}
=
\sum_H
A_H\prod_b (N_b)_{t_b(H)} .
\]
% lean: multinomialFactorialCount_mul

Taking expectations and using the factorial moment formula for multinomial counts,
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\mathfrak m_{k,r}\mathfrak m_{k,s}
\right]
=
\sum_H
A_H
\frac{(m)_{|r|+|s|-|H|}}{(m)_{|r|}(m)_{|s|}}
\prod_b \alpha_b^{\,r_b+s_b-H_b}.
\]
% lean: integral_normalized_multinomialFactorialCount_mul_sample

The normalization ratio satisfies, for every such \(H\),
\[
\frac{(m)_{|r|+|s|-|H|}}{(m)_{|r|}(m)_{|s|}}
\le
\frac{\mathrm e}{m^{|H|}} .
\]
Indeed, if \(|s|=0\) then also \(|r|=0\), and the bound is immediate.  Otherwise put \(u=m-|s|\).  The assumptions \(4|s|^2\le m\) and \(|r|\le |s|\) imply \(u>0\) and \(2|s|^2\le u\).  Also
\[
(m)_{|r|+|s|-|H|}\le m^{|r|+|s|-|H|},\qquad
(m)_{|r|}\ge u^{|r|},\qquad
(m)_{|s|}\ge u^{|s|}.
\]
Hence the ratio is at most
\[
\frac{m^{|r|+|s|-|H|}}{u^{|r|+|s|}}
=
\frac{(m/u)^{|r|+|s|}}{m^{|H|}} .
\]
Since \(m/u=1+|s|/u\),
\[
(|r|+|s|)\log(m/u)
\le
(|r|+|s|)\frac{|s|}{u}
\le 1,
\]
and therefore \((m/u)^{|r|+|s|}\le \mathrm e\). % lean: factorial_ratio_bound

Because all masses \(\alpha_b\) are nonnegative,
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\mathfrak m_{k,r}\mathfrak m_{k,s}
\right]
\le
\mathrm e
\sum_H
A_H\,m^{-|H|}
\prod_b \alpha_b^{\,r_b+s_b-H_b}.
\]
% lean: normalized_multinomial_joint_moment_bound

The remaining overlap sum factors by coordinates:
\[
\sum_H
A_H\,m^{-|H|}
\prod_b \alpha_b^{\,r_b+s_b-H_b}
=
\prod_b
\sum_{h=0}^{\min(r_b,s_b)}
{r_b\choose h}{s_b\choose h}h!\,m^{-h}
\alpha_b^{\,r_b+s_b-h}.
\]
% lean: overlap_sum_eq_prod

For each \(b\) and each \(0\le h\le\min\{r_b,s_b\}\), one has \(h\le s_b\) and
\[
{r_b\choose h}h!=(r_b)_h\le r_b^h\le |r|^h\le |s|^h .
\]
The assumption \(m_1>0\) gives \(m>0\), and \(\alpha_b\ge0\).  After rewriting
\(\alpha_b^{r_b+s_b-h}=\alpha_b^{r_b}\alpha_b^{s_b-h}\), the preceding coefficient bound gives
\[
{r_b\choose h}{s_b\choose h}h!\,m^{-h}
\alpha_b^{\,r_b+s_b-h}
\le
\alpha_b^{r_b}{s_b\choose h}
\left(\frac{|s|}{m}\right)^h
\alpha_b^{s_b-h}.
\]
The right-hand summands are nonnegative for every \(0\le h\le s_b\), so enlarging the summation range and applying the binomial theorem yields
\[
\sum_{h=0}^{\min(r_b,s_b)}
{r_b\choose h}{s_b\choose h}h!\,m^{-h}
\alpha_b^{\,r_b+s_b-h}
\le
\alpha_b^{r_b}
\sum_{h=0}^{s_b}{s_b\choose h}
\left(\frac{|s|}{m}\right)^h
\alpha_b^{s_b-h}
=
\alpha_b^{r_b}
\left(\alpha_b+\frac{|s|}{m}\right)^{s_b}.
\]
% lean: cell_overlap_sum_le

Multiplying these coordinatewise bounds gives
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\mathfrak m_{k,r}\mathfrak m_{k,s}
\right]
\le
\mathrm e
\prod_{b}\alpha_b^{r_b}
\prod_b\left(\alpha_b+\frac{|s|}{m}\right)^{s_b}.
\]
The \(\varnothing\)-coordinate contributes \(1\), since \(r_{\varnothing}=s_{\varnothing}=0\).  Replacing \(b\) by \((a,y)\in\mathcal C\) and \(m\) by \(m_1\) yields
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\mathfrak m_{k,r}\mathfrak m_{k,s}\right]
\le
\mathrm e
\prod_{(a,y)}q_{aky}^{\,r_{ay}}
\prod_{(a,y)}
\left(q_{aky}+\frac{|s|}{m_1}\right)^{s_{ay}} .
\]
% lean: integral_factorialMonomial_mul_trunc_le
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:coefficient-sum-certificate}[Coefficient sum certificate]
Let \(M\) be a positive integer and let \(z\ge 0\). With \(g_{M,j}\) denoting the coefficients of the polynomial continuation \(G_M\) from \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, define
\[
g_M^+(z)
=
\sum_{j\in\{0,\ldots,M-2\}} |g_{M,j}| z^j,
\]
with the sum interpreted as empty when \(M=1\). Then
\[
g_M^+(z)
\le
6^M \max\{1,z^{M\dotminus 2}\},
\]
where \(M\dotminus 2=\max\{M-2,0\}\).
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:coefficient-sum-certificate}]
All powers below have nonnegative integer exponents; in particular, the exponent \(M-2\) in the statement is interpreted as natural-number subtraction, so it equals \(0\) when \(M=1\).  Let \(T_M\) be the Chebyshev polynomial and let \(G_M\) be the polynomial continuation used in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.  The continuation has coefficients \(g_{M,j}\) satisfying
\[
T_M(1-2x)
=
1-2M^2x+2M^2x^2G_M(x).
\]
% lean: chebyshev_gPolynomial_identity

For these coefficients, the closed form is
\[
g_{M,j}
=
(-1)^j
\frac{2^{2j+3}}{M(M+j+2)}
{M+j+2\choose 2j+4}
\qquad (j\ge0).
\]
Because \(M>0\), the factor after \((-1)^j\) is nonnegative.  Hence
\[
|g_{M,j}|
=
\frac{2^{2j+3}}{M(M+j+2)}
{M+j+2\choose 2j+4}
=
(-1)^j g_{M,j},
\]
and therefore, for every \(z\),
\[
g_M^+(z)
=
\sum_{0\le j<M-1}|g_{M,j}|z^j
=
\sum_{0\le j<M-1}g_{M,j}(-z)^j
=
G_M(-z).
\]
% lean: abs_gCoefficient
% lean: sign_gCoefficient
% lean: gpos_eq

Taking \(z=1\) in this identity and substituting \(x=-1\) in the Chebyshev identity gives
\[
T_M(3)
=
1+2M^2+2M^2g_M^+(1).
\]
The sum defining \(g_M^+(1)\) is nonnegative.  Since \(M\ge1\), we have \(M^2\ge1\), and the displayed identity implies
\[
g_M^+(1)\le T_M(3).
\]
% lean: gpos_nonneg
% lean: chebyshev_gPolynomial_identity

The Chebyshev recurrence and the standard positivity bound \(T_m(x)\ge1\) for \(x\ge1\) give, at \(x=3\),
\[
T_0(3)=1,\qquad T_1(3)=3,\qquad
T_{m+2}(3)=6T_{m+1}(3)-T_m(3),\qquad T_m(3)\ge0.
\]
Induction using these facts yields
\[
T_M(3)\le 6^M.
\]
Consequently,
\[
g_M^+(1)\le 6^M.
\]
% lean: chebyshev_three_le

Now split according to \(z\).  If \(0\le z\le1\), then \(z^j\le1\) for every index in the sum, so
\[
g_M^+(z)
=
\sum_{0\le j<M-1}|g_{M,j}|z^j
\le
\sum_{0\le j<M-1}|g_{M,j}|
=
g_M^+(1)
\le 6^M
\le 6^M\max\{1,z^{M-2}\}.
\]
% lean: gpos_bound

If \(1\le z\), then every index in the finite sum satisfies \(j\le M-2\) with the same natural-number subtraction convention.  Hence \(z^j\le z^{M-2}\), and
\[
g_M^+(z)
\le
z^{M-2}\sum_{0\le j<M-1}|g_{M,j}|
=
z^{M-2}g_M^+(1)
\le
6^M z^{M-2}
\le
6^M\max\{1,z^{M-2}\}.
\]
% lean: gpos_bound

The two cases prove the claimed bound.
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:factorial-product-moment} is the basic moment inequality for normalized falling-factorial monomials. \Cref{obj:lem:coefficient-sum-certificate} bounds the one-arm absolute coefficient sum generated by the Chebyshev reciprocal polynomial, which keeps the light-cell variance compatible with the logarithmic degree calibration in \cref{obj:lem:calibration-consequences}.

The aggregate heavy-cell result and the sparse factorial-polynomial definitions now prepare the two branches of the hybrid estimator for the final light-cell rate bound.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate}[Heavy-cell rate bound]
For every overlap level \(\epsilon\) satisfying \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there exist constants \(C_\epsilon,\rho_\epsilon>0\) and an integer \(N_\epsilon\) such that the following holds. For every \(n,d\), every single-observation law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2\), and every sample law \(\mu_n\), if
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\ge N_\epsilon\);
\item \textbf{(Dimension range.)} \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\);
\item \textbf{(Experiment membership.)} \(\mu_n\) and \(P\) belong to the overlap-restricted iid product-law experiment class \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class},
\end{itemize}
then the aggregate heavy-cell ratio estimator satisfies
\[
\int \left\{
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n(O_1,\ldots,O_n)} \phi(q_k)
\right\}^2\,d\mu_n
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate}]
Write
\[
L=\log(en),\qquad m_j=|I_j|\quad (j=0,1),
\]
and let \(N_0\) denote the calibration cutoff in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}. For
\(n\ge N_0\), the implemented heavy set \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\) is the pilot set at
threshold \(256\):
\[
\widehat{\mathcal H}
=
\{k:\lfloor 256L\rfloor<N_k^{(0)}\}.
\]
The corresponding pilot-bad event is
\[
\mathcal B
=
\left\{
\neg\left(\forall k\in\widehat{\mathcal H},\,\frac{256L}{2m_0}\le p_k\right)
\mathrel{\text{or}}
\neg\left(\forall k\notin\widehat{\mathcal H},\,p_k\le \frac{2\cdot256L}{m_0}\right)
\right\}.
\]

For every sample, the empirical arm ratios in \(\widehat h_k\) use totalized division, so the
ratio is \(0\) when the corresponding arm count is zero; with this convention each such ratio
lies in \([0,1]\). Hence the absolute empirical ratio contribution of category \(k\) is at most
\(N_k^{(1)}/m_1\), and summing over the selected categories gives
\[
|\widehat T_{\mathcal H}|
\le
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}
\le
\sum_k\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}
=
\frac{m_1}{m_1}
\le1.
\]
For an overlap law \(P\), the category vector \(q_k\) from \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}
lies in the overlap cone of \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone}. If \(p_k=0\), then \(q_k=0\) and
\cref{obj:def:cell-functional} sets \(\phi(q_k)=0\). If \(p_k>0\), the arm masses in
\(q_k\) are positive and the displayed formula in \cref{obj:def:cell-functional} gives
\[
\phi(q_k)=p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}),
\]
where the binary-outcome regressions \(\mu_{0k}\) and \(\mu_{1k}\) lie in \([0,1]\). Thus, in
both cases, \(|\phi(q_k)|\le p_k\). Since the category masses sum to one,
\[
\left|\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)\right|
\le
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}p_k
\le
\sum_k p_k
=1.
\]
Thus the squared heavy-cell error is bounded by \(4\) pointwise for the overlap laws considered
below. The fixed-threshold pilot sandwich from \cref{obj:lem:pilot-sandwich}, applied with
dimension constant \(1\), gives constants \(C_0>0\) and \(N_{\mathrm{bad}}\) such that, whenever
\(n\ge N_{\mathrm{bad}}\) and \(d\le nL\),
\[
P^{\otimes n}(\mathcal B)\le C_0 n^{-4}.
\]
With \(C_{\mathrm{bad}}=4C_0\), the range bound gives
\[
\int_{\mathcal B}
\left(
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
\right)^2
\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le C_{\mathrm{bad}}\,n^{-4}.
\] % lean: heavy_bad_pilot_rate

Set
\[
K^{\mathcal H}_\epsilon=\frac{16}{\epsilon}+12+\frac1{\epsilon^4},
\qquad
C_\epsilon=4K^{\mathcal H}_\epsilon+C_{\mathrm{bad}},
\qquad
\rho_\epsilon=1,
\]
and take
\[
N_\epsilon=\max\{8,N_0,N_{\mathrm{bad}}\}.
\] % lean: universal_heavy_cell_rate
These constants satisfy the required positivity conditions.

Assume \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), \(d\le \rho_\epsilon n\log n\), and
\(\mu_n\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\). The experiment-class assumption gives
\(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\) and \(\epsilon\)-overlap for \(P\). Also
\(n\ge8\), \(n\ge N_0\), \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{bad}}\), and, since \(\log n\le L\),
\[
d\le nL.
\]
Let
\[
B_- = \frac{256L}{2m_0}.
\]
On \(\mathcal B^c\), the first half of the pilot sandwich and the equality
\(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n=\widehat{\mathcal H}\) give
\[
k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B_-\le p_k .
\] % lean: heavy_cell_mass_lower_of_good_pilot

Fix a deterministic category set \(H\) satisfying \(p_k\ge B_-\) for every \(k\in H\), and fix
\(a\in\{0,1\}\). Define
\[
S_a(H)
=
\sum_{k\in H}
\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}
\frac{N_{ak1}^{(1)}}{N_{ak}^{(1)}}
\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}^{(1)}>0\},
\qquad
\theta_a(H)=\sum_{k\in H}p_k\mu_{ak},
\]
where the displayed ratio is taken to be \(0\) when \(N_{ak}^{(1)}=0\). The empirical
category-mass centering is
\[
\sum_{k\in H}\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}\mu_{ak}.
\]
The pointwise decomposition about this centering is
\[
S_a(H)-\sum_{k\in H}\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}\mu_{ak}
=
\frac1{m_1}
\left[
\sum_{k\in H}
N_k^{(1)}\frac{\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}^{(1)}>0\}}{N_{ak}^{(1)}}
\{N_{ak1}^{(1)}-\mu_{ak}N_{ak}^{(1)}\}
-
\sum_{k\in H}\mu_{ak}N_k^{(1)}\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}^{(1)}=0\}
\right].
\] % lean: fixedHeavyArm_noise_decomposition

For the first bracket, write
\(N_{ak1}^{(1)}-\mu_{ak}N_{ak}^{(1)}\) as the sum over the second split of the centered
outcome residual in category \(k\) and arm \(a\). Expanding the square, all off-diagonal
terms vanish: terms from distinct categories are pointwise disjoint when they use the same
observation, and terms using different observations integrate to zero by conditioning on one
outcome coordinate and using the centered residual. The remaining diagonal terms are bounded by
replacing the squared residual with the category-arm indicator. For each \(k\), the resulting
nested-count integral is
\[
\int
(N_k^{(1)})^2
\frac{\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}^{(1)}>0\}}{N_{ak}^{(1)}}
\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
\frac{2m_1p_k}{\epsilon},
\]
where overlap supplies the lower bound by \(\epsilon\) on the arm probability inside category
\(k\). Summing over \(k\in H\) yields
\[
\int
\left[
\sum_{k\in H}
N_k^{(1)}\frac{\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}^{(1)}>0\}}{N_{ak}^{(1)}}
\{N_{ak1}^{(1)}-\mu_{ak}N_{ak}^{(1)}\}
\right]^2
\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
\frac{2m_1}{\epsilon}\sum_{k\in H}p_k .
\] % lean: integral_fixedRatioResidual_sq_le

For the second bracket, \(0\le\mu_{ak}\le1\), so its square is bounded by the square of the
missing-arm count
\[
\sum_{k\in H}N_k^{(1)}\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}^{(1)}=0\}.
\]
Since \(n\ge8\), \(m_1\ge3\). The exact multinomial expansion of this count has diagonal terms
\[
\begin{aligned}
&m_1 p_k\{1-P(A=a\mid X=k)\}\{1-p_kP(A=a\mid X=k)\}^{m_1-1} \\
&\quad +(m_1)_2\bigl[p_k\{1-P(A=a\mid X=k)\}\bigr]^2
\{1-p_kP(A=a\mid X=k)\}^{m_1-2}
\end{aligned}
\]
and cross terms, for \(k\ne \ell\),
\[
(m_1)_2 p_k\{1-P(A=a\mid X=k)\}p_\ell\{1-P(A=a\mid X=\ell)\}
\{1-p_kP(A=a\mid X=k)-p_\ell P(A=a\mid X=\ell)\}^{m_1-2}.
\]
Overlap gives \(p_kP(A=a\mid X=k)\ge\epsilon p_k\). With
\(u=((m_1-2)/2)\epsilon\), the diagonal display is bounded by
\[
m_1p_k+m_1^2\{p_k e^{-u p_k}\}^2,
\]
and each cross term is bounded by
\[
m_1^2\{p_k e^{-u p_k}\}\{p_\ell e^{-u p_\ell}\}.
\]
The masses of categories in \(H\) are at least \(B_-\), so the elementary envelope
\[
\sum_{k\in H}p_k e^{-u p_k}
\le
\frac{|H|}{u^2B_-}
\]
applies
\[
\int
\left[
\sum_{k\in H}\mu_{ak}N_k^{(1)}\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}^{(1)}=0\}
\right]^2
\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
m_1+m_1^2
\left[
\frac{|H|}
{\{((m_1-2)/2)\epsilon\}^2 B_-}
\right]^2 .
\] % lean: fixedMissingOutcomeBias_sq_le integral_fixedMissingCount_sq_le
Together with \((x-y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\), the two bracket bounds give
\[
\int
\left(
S_a(H)-\sum_{k\in H}\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}\mu_{ak}
\right)^2
\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
\frac{4\sum_{k\in H}p_k}{m_1\epsilon}
+
\frac2{m_1}
+
2\left[
\frac{|H|}
{\{((m_1-2)/2)\epsilon\}^2 B_-}
\right]^2 .
\] % lean: integral_fixedHeavyArm_noise_sq_le

For the empirical category-mass fluctuation, use the one-observation score
\[
g_{a,H}(O)=\mu_{aX}\mathbf 1\{X\in H\}.
\]
Its second-split average is the empirical category-mass centering, its expectation is
\(\theta_a(H)\), and \(0\le g_{a,H}\le1\). The iid bounded-score variance bound therefore gives
\[
\int
\left(
\sum_{k\in H}\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}\mu_{ak}-\theta_a(H)
\right)^2
\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le \frac1{m_1}.
\] % lean: integral_fixedEmpiricalMassArm_sub_target_sq_le
Splitting \(S_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\) at the empirical category-mass centering yields
\[
\int \{S_a(H)-\theta_a(H)\}^2\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
\frac{8\sum_{k\in H}p_k}{m_1\epsilon}
+
\frac6{m_1}
+
4\left[
\frac{|H|}
{\{((m_1-2)/2)\epsilon\}^2 B_-}
\right]^2 .
\] % lean: integral_fixedHeavyArm_error_sq_le

The probability-mass identities give
\[
\sum_{k\in H}p_k\le\sum_k p_k=1,
\]
and the finite-set cardinality bound gives \(|H|\le d\). The split-size calibration for
\(n\ge8\) gives
\[
\frac n2\le m_1,
\qquad
\frac n8\le\frac{m_1-2}{2},
\qquad
\frac{256L}{n}\le B_-,
\]
and \(\log n\le L\). Hence
\[
\frac{8\sum_{k\in H}p_k}{m_1\epsilon}
+
\frac6{m_1}
+
4\left[
\frac{|H|}
{\{((m_1-2)/2)\epsilon\}^2 B_-}
\right]^2
\le
K^{\mathcal H}_\epsilon
\left\{
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\right\}.
\] % lean: heavy_fixed_envelope_rate

Let \(V=K^{\mathcal H}_\epsilon\{n^{-1}+d^2/[n^2(\log n)^2]\}\). The preceding deterministic bound applies
uniformly to every fixed \(H\) whose elements have mass at least \(B_-\). To pass to the random
pilot-heavy set on \(\mathcal B^c\), partition the pilot sample space into the fibers on which
\(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n=H\). On each contributing fiber, the good-pilot implication gives
\(p_k\ge B_-\) for every \(k\in H\). The fixed-\(H\) arm error is a function of the estimation
split, while the fiber is determined by the pilot split, so the product law factors the restricted
integral into the fiber probability times the fixed-\(H\) product-law integral. Summing over the
eligible fibers and using that their probabilities sum to at most one gives, for each
\(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\int_{\mathcal B^c}
\{S_a(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)-\theta_a(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)\}^2
\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le V.
\] % lean: heavy_good_arm_setIntegral_le

The heavy contribution and target decompose into their two arm components:
\[
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
=
\{S_1(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)-\theta_1(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)\}
-
\{S_0(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)-\theta_0(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n)\}.
\] % lean: fixedHeavyContribution_eq_arm_sub fixedTargetHeavy_eq_arm_sub
Thus \((x-y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\) gives
\[
\int_{\mathcal B^c}
\left(
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
\right)^2
\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
4K^{\mathcal H}_\epsilon
\left\{
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\right\}.
\] % lean: fixedHeavy_error_sq_le_two_arms

The bad-event contribution satisfies
\[
\int_{\mathcal B}
\left(
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
\right)^2
\,dP^{\otimes n}
\le
C_{\mathrm{bad}} n^{-4}
\le
C_{\mathrm{bad}}
\left\{
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\right\},
\] % lean: heavy_bad_pilot_rate
because \(n^{-4}\le n^{-1}\) for \(n\ge8\) and \(n^{-1}\le n^{-1}+d^2/[n^2(\log n)^2]\). Adding the
integrals over \(\mathcal B\) and \(\mathcal B^c\), and using \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\), yields
\[
\int
\left(
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
\right)^2
\,d\mu_n
\le
(4K^{\mathcal H}_\epsilon+C_{\mathrm{bad}})
\left\{
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\right\},
\]
which is the claimed bound with the chosen \(C_\epsilon\). % lean: universal_heavy_cell_rate
\end{proof}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_20}[Sparse factorial-polynomial means]
Given a law \(P\), a category \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), a degree \(M\), and a scale \(B\), write \(q_k=(q_{0k0},q_{0k1},q_{1k0},q_{1k1})\) for the four-cell mass vector of category \(k\) under \(P\). For an arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), the sparse-arm mean is \[ \bar A_{a,k}(P,M,B) = \frac{s(q_k)\,t_a(q_k)}{B}\,G_M\!\left(\frac{s_a(q_k)}{B}\right), \] with \(s\), \(s_a\), \(t_a\), and \(G_M\) as in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}. The sparse factorial-polynomial mean of category \(k\) is the difference of the two arm means, \[ \bar P_k = P_{M,B}(q_k) = \bar A_{1,k}(P,M,B) - \bar A_{0,k}(P,M,B). \]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:factorial-moment-variance}[Factorial moment variance bound]
Use the split notation and hybrid-estimator calibration in \cref{obj:def:sample-splits,obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.  For any sample size \(n\), alphabet size \(d\), probability law \(P\) on the finite observed-data alphabet, and category \(k\), let \(\bar P_k\) denote the sparse factorial-polynomial mean of the light-cell statistic \(\widehat P_k\).  Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Split size.)} \(m_1>0\).
\item \textbf{(Degree and scale.)} \(M(n)>0\) and \(B(n)>0\).
\item \textbf{(Sample-size calibration.)} \(4M(n)^2\le m_1\).
\item \textbf{(Mass envelope.)} The category mass \(p_k=P(X=k)\) satisfies
\[
p_k+\frac{4M(n)}{m_1}\le B(n)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
p_k\le B(n).
\]
\end{itemize}
Then, under the ambient infinite iid product law \(P^{\otimes\infty}\),
\[
\int
\left(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k\right)^2
\,dP^{\otimes\infty}
\le
8(\mathrm e+1)\left(B(n)6^{M(n)}\right)^2 .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:factorial-moment-variance}]
All expectations below are taken under the iid sequence law generated by \(P\), equivalently under the corresponding \(n\)-fold product law for functions of \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\). Write \(M=M(n)\), \(B=B(n)\), \(m_1=|I_1|\), and \(q=q_k\). The coefficients in the sparse expansion are the coefficients of the polynomial continuation \(G_M\):
\[
g_{M,j}=(-1)^j\frac{2^{2j+3}}{M(M+j+2)}\binom{M+j+2}{2j+4},
\qquad
G_M(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-2} g_{M,j}x^j .
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), \(c\in\{0,1\}^2\), and \(0\le t\le j\le M-2\), let \(r^{a,c,j,t}\) be the sparse multi-index determined by
\[
u^{r^{a,c,j,t}}=u_c\,u_{a1}\,u_{a0}^{\,t}\,u_{a1}^{\,j-t}.
\]
The sparse arm mean is
\[
\bar A_{a,k}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\,q^{r^{a,c,j,t}},
\]
and the sparse polynomial mean is
\[
\bar P_k=\bar A_{1,k}-\bar A_{0,k}.
\]
For a nonnegative four-cell vector \(v\), set
\[
\mathcal E_a(v)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
\left|B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\right|\,v^{r^{a,c,j,t}}.
\]
The triangle inequality gives \(|\bar A_{a,k}|\le \mathcal E_a(q)\). We first record the envelope bound used for both \(q\) and its shifted version. If \(v\ge0\), \(\sum_c v_c\le B\), \(M>0\), and \(B>0\), then summing first over \(c\) and then using the binomial theorem gives
\[
\mathcal E_a(v)
= B^{-1}\left(\sum_c v_c\right)v_{a1}
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|
\left(\frac{v_{a0}+v_{a1}}{B}\right)^j .
\]
The coefficient-sum certificate in \cref{obj:lem:coefficient-sum-certificate} gives, for every \(z\ge0\),
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|z^j
\le 6^M\max\{1,z^{M-2}\}.
\]
In the envelope application, \(z=(v_{a0}+v_{a1})/B\) lies in \([0,1]\). Since \(0\le v_{a1}\le \sum_c v_c\le B\),
\[
\mathcal E_a(v)
\le B^{-1}\left(\sum_c v_c\right)^2 6^M
\le B6^M .
\]
Applying this with \(v=q\), using \(q\ge0\) and \(\sum_c q_c=p_k\le B\), gives
\[
|\bar P_k|
\le |\bar A_{1,k}|+|\bar A_{0,k}|
\le 2B6^M,
\qquad
\bar P_k^2\le4(B6^M)^2.
\]

The sample arm contribution has the parallel factorial-moment expansion
\[
\widehat A_{a,k}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\,
\frac{\prod_{b,y}(N^{(1)}_{bky})_{r^{a,c,j,t}_{by}}}{(m_1)_{|r^{a,c,j,t}|}},
\qquad
\widehat P_k=\widehat A_{1,k}-\widehat A_{0,k}.
\]
For a multi-index \(r\), write
\[
F_r=\frac{\prod_{b,y}(N^{(1)}_{bky})_{r_{by}}}{(m_1)_{|r|}}.
\]
For two displayed terms with multi-indices \(r\) and \(s\), the range restrictions give \(|r|=j+2\le M\) and \(|s|=j'+2\le M\). Suppose first that \(|r|\le |s|\). Since \(|s|\le M\) and \(4M^2\le m_1\), the split-size condition in \cref{obj:lem:factorial-product-moment} applies and gives
\[
\mathbb E[F_rF_s]
\le
\mathrm e\,q^r
\left(q+\frac{|s|}{m_1}\mathbf 1\right)^s .
\]
Coordinatewise nonnegativity of \(q\), the inequality \(|s|\le M\), and monotonicity of nonnegative monomials yield
\[
\mathbb E[F_rF_s]
\le
\mathrm e\,
\left(q+\frac{M}{m_1}\mathbf 1\right)^r
\left(q+\frac{M}{m_1}\mathbf 1\right)^s .
\]
When \(|s|\le |r|\), the same argument with \(r\) and \(s\) interchanged gives the same bound. The unweighted factorial monomial product is nonnegative pointwise, and the product of the two displayed coefficients is bounded above by the product of their absolute values. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\!\left[
\left(B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\,
F_{r^{a,c,j,t}}\right)
\left(B^{-1}g_{M,j'}B^{-j'}\binom {j'}{t'}\,
F_{r^{a,c',j',t'}}\right)
\right]
\\
&\quad\le
\mathrm e\,
\left|B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\right|
\left(q+\frac{M}{m_1}\mathbf 1\right)^{r^{a,c,j,t}}
\left|B^{-1}g_{M,j'}B^{-j'}\binom {j'}{t'}\right|
\left(q+\frac{M}{m_1}\mathbf 1\right)^{r^{a,c',j',t'}} .
\end{aligned}
\]
Expanding \(\widehat A_{a,k}^2\) as the finite double sum, applying this estimate term by term, and reassembling the absolute-coefficient sums gives
\[
\mathbb E[\widehat A_{a,k}^2]
\le
\mathrm e\,\mathcal E_a\!\left(q+\frac{M}{m_1}\mathbf 1\right)^2.
\]
The shifted vector is nonnegative and has total mass
\[
\sum_{b,y}\left(q_{by}+\frac{M}{m_1}\right)
=p_k+\frac{4M}{m_1}
\le B,
\]
by the shifted light-cell mass condition. Applying the envelope bound to the shifted vector gives
\[
\mathbb E[\widehat A_{a,k}^2]
\le \mathrm e\,(B6^M)^2
\qquad
(a=0,1).
\]
Using \((u-v)^2\le2u^2+2v^2\) with \(u=\widehat A_{1,k}\) and \(v=\widehat A_{0,k}\),
\[
\mathbb E[\widehat P_k^2]
\le 2\mathbb E[\widehat A_{1,k}^2]+2\mathbb E[\widehat A_{0,k}^2]
\le4\mathrm e\,(B6^M)^2.
\]

Finally, pointwise,
\[
(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k)^2
\le 2\widehat P_k^2+2\bar P_k^2,
\]
because \((\widehat P_k+\bar P_k)^2\ge0\). Integrating and substituting the two bounds above gives
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k)^2\right]
\le
2\cdot4\mathrm e\,(B6^M)^2+2\cdot4(B6^M)^2
=
8(\mathrm e+1)(B6^M)^2,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:factorial-moment-covariance}[Off-diagonal factorial covariance]
Use the split notation and calibration \(m_1\), \(M(n)\), and \(B(n)\) from \cref{obj:def:sample-splits,obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.  For each category \(k\), let \(\widehat P_k\) be the factorial-moment polynomial estimate from \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, computed from the first \(n\) coordinates of the ambient iid sequence, and let
\[
\bar P_k
=
\bar A_{1,k}\bigl(P,M(n),B(n)\bigr)
-
\bar A_{0,k}\bigl(P,M(n),B(n)\bigr)
\]
denote the corresponding sparse factorial-polynomial mean, written as the treated sparse-arm mean minus the control sparse-arm mean.

For any single-observation law \(P\) on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2\) and any two categories \(k,l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Distinct categories.} \(k\ne l\).
\item \textbf{Positive calibration.} \(M(n)>0\) and \(B(n)>0\).
\item \textbf{Second-split size.} \(4M(n)^2\le m_1\).
\item \textbf{Light masses.} \(p_k\le B(n)\) and \(p_l\le B(n)\).
\end{itemize}
Then, under the ambient infinite iid product law \(P^{\otimes\infty}\),
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes\infty}}
\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k\bigr)
\bigl(\widehat P_l-\bar P_l\bigr)
\right]
\le
\frac{8M(n)^2}{m_1}
\left\{B(n)\,6^{M(n)}\right\}^2 .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:factorial-moment-covariance}]
Let \(\mu\) be the iid product law generated by \(P\) on the sample sequence, and abbreviate
\[
M=M(n),\qquad B=B(n),\qquad m=m_1,\qquad
R=B\,6^M,\qquad \eta=\frac{2M^2}{m}.
\]
Write the polynomial continuation as
\[
G_M(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}g_{M,j}x^j.
\]
For \(h\in\{k,l\}\), let \(q_h\) be the four-cell vector of category \(h\) under
\(P\), and write \(q_h^r=\prod_{b,y}(q_h)_{by}^{r_{by}}\).  For an arm
\(a\in\{0,1\}\), a cell \(c\in\{0,1\}^2\), and integers
\(0\le t\le j\le M-2\), let \(r^{a,c,j,t}\) be the multi-index determined by
\[
u^{r^{a,c,j,t}}
=u_c\,u_{a1}\,u_{a0}^{\,t}\,u_{a1}^{\,j-t}.
\]
The sparse arm contribution and its sparse mean are
\[
\widehat A_{a,h}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\,
\frac{\prod_{b,y}(N^{(1)}_{bhy})_{r^{a,c,j,t}_{by}}}{(m)_{|r^{a,c,j,t}|}},
\]
\[
\bar A_{a,h}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\,q_h^{\,r^{a,c,j,t}}.
\]
Thus
\[
\widehat P_h=\widehat A_{1,h}-\widehat A_{0,h},
\qquad
\bar P_h=\bar A_{1,h}-\bar A_{0,h}.
\]
The size condition \(4M^2\le m\), together with \(M>0\), gives \(M\le m\).  Since
each displayed sparse multi-index has total degree \(j+2\le M\), the factorial-moment
identity gives, for \(h=k,l\),
\[
\int \widehat A_{a,h}\,d\mu=\bar A_{a,h}\quad(a=0,1),
\qquad
\int \widehat P_h\,d\mu=\bar P_h .
\]
Consequently, by expanding the centered product and integrating term by term,
\[
\int(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k)(\widehat P_l-\bar P_l)\,d\mu
=
\int \widehat P_k\widehat P_l\,d\mu-\bar P_k\bar P_l .
\]
% lean: integral_factorialPolynomialContribution_eq_sparsePolynomialMean

For \(h\in\{k,l\}\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define the absolute arm envelope
\[
\mathsf{Env}_{a,h}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
\left|B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\right|\,q_h^{\,r^{a,c,j,t}}.
\]
Fix two sparse monomials with multi-indices \(r\) and \(s\) from categories \(k\) and
\(l\), and put
\[
\widehat F_{h,r}=\frac{\prod_{b,y}(N^{(1)}_{bhy})_{r_{by}}}{(m)_{|r|}}.
\]
The product of the two normalized factorial counts is an average over ordered injective
selections for the \(k\)-monomial and ordered injective selections for the \(l\)-monomial.
Because \(k\ne l\), an observation cannot satisfy both category patterns; hence exactly the
disjoint pairs of selections contribute.  There are \((m)_{|r|+|s|}\) such ordered pairs,
and independence gives the same cell-mass product \(q_k^r q_l^s\) for each pair.  Therefore
\[
\int \widehat F_{k,r}\widehat F_{l,s}\,d\mu
=
\Gamma_{r,s}\,q_k^rq_l^s,
\qquad
\Gamma_{r,s}
=
\frac{(m)_{|r|+|s|}}{(m)_{|r|}(m)_{|s|}}.
\]
For sparse terms, \(|r|\le M\) and \(|s|\le M\).  We next bound the normalization factor.  Suppose
first that \(|r|\le |s|\).  If \(|s|=0\), then \(|r|=0\) and \(\Gamma_{r,s}=1\).  If
\(|s|>0\), the condition \(4|s|^2\le m\) follows from \(|s|\le M\) and \(4M^2\le m\), and it
implies \(2|s|\le m\).  Using
\[
\Gamma_{r,s}
=\frac{(m-|r|)_{|s|}}{(m)_{|s|}},
\]
we have \(\Gamma_{r,s}\le1\).  Also
\[
\Gamma_{r,s}
\ge
\frac{(m-2|s|)^{|s|}}{m^{|s|}}
=\left(1-\frac{2|s|}{m}\right)^{|s|}
\ge
1-\frac{2|s|^2}{m},
\]
where the last step is Bernoulli's inequality, using \(-1\le -2|s|/m\).  Hence
\[
|\Gamma_{r,s}-1|\le \frac{2|s|^2}{m}\le\eta .
\]
The case \(|s|\le |r|\) is identical after exchanging \(r\) and \(s\), using the symmetry of
\(\Gamma_{r,s}\).  Since the four-cell masses are nonnegative,
\[
\left|
\int \widehat F_{k,r}\widehat F_{l,s}\,d\mu-q_k^rq_l^s
\right|
\le
\eta\,q_k^rq_l^s,
\qquad |r|,|s|\le M.
\]
Multiplying by the two sparse coefficients, summing over the two finite arm expansions, and
using the triangle inequality yields
\[
\left|
\int \widehat A_{a,k}\widehat A_{b,l}\,d\mu-
\bar A_{a,k}\bar A_{b,l}
\right|
\le
\eta\,\mathsf{Env}_{a,k}\mathsf{Env}_{b,l}.
\]
% lean: sparseArm_cross_covariance_le

For \(h=k,l\), the coordinates of \(q_h\) are nonnegative and sum to \(p_h\).  For fixed
\(j\), positivity of \(B\) and of the binomial coefficients gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
\left|B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\right|q_h^{\,r^{a,c,j,t}} \\
&\quad=
B^{-1}|g_{M,j}|B^{-j}
\sum_{t=0}^{j}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^2}
\binom jt\,(q_h)_c(q_h)_{a1}(q_h)_{a0}^{t}(q_h)_{a1}^{j-t} \\
&\quad=
B^{-1}|g_{M,j}|B^{-j}
\,s(q_h)(q_h)_{a1}\bigl((q_h)_{a0}+(q_h)_{a1}\bigr)^j.
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing in \(j\), and using \(s_a(q_h)=(q_h)_{a0}+(q_h)_{a1}\), yields
\[
\mathsf{Env}_{a,h}
=
B^{-1}s(q_h)(q_h)_{a1}
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|\left\{\frac{s_a(q_h)}{B}\right\}^{j}.
\]
The light-cell hypotheses give \(p_h=s(q_h)\le B\), while \((q_h)_{a1}\le s(q_h)\) and
\(B>0\).  Hence
\[
B^{-1}s(q_h)(q_h)_{a1}\le B^{-1}s(q_h)^2\le B,
\qquad
0\le \frac{s_a(q_h)}{B}\le1.
\]
It remains to record the coefficient envelope used in this display.  For every \(z\ge0\),
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|z^j
\le
6^M\max\{1,z^{M-2}\}.
\]
Indeed, the sign pattern of the explicit coefficients gives
\(\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|=G_M(-1)\).  The defining Chebyshev identity for the continuation,
evaluated at \(-1\), gives
\[
T_M(3)=1+2M^2+2M^2\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|,
\]
so nonnegativity of the coefficient sum implies
\(\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|\le T_M(3)\).  The Chebyshev recurrence gives
\(T_M(3)\le6^M\).  If \(0\le z\le1\), termwise monotonicity gives
\(\sum |g_{M,j}|z^j\le\sum |g_{M,j}|\le6^M\).  If \(z\ge1\), then
\(j\le M-2\) on the displayed range, so
\(\sum |g_{M,j}|z^j\le z^{M-2}\sum |g_{M,j}|\le6^M z^{M-2}\).  This proves the stated
envelope.  Taking \(z=s_a(q_h)/B\) makes the maximum equal to \(1\), and therefore
\[
\mathsf{Env}_{a,h}\le B\,6^M=R.
\]
Hence, for all \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\left|
\int \widehat A_{a,k}\widehat A_{b,l}\,d\mu-
\bar A_{a,k}\bar A_{b,l}
\right|
\le \eta R^2 .
\]
% lean: sparseArmEnvelope_le

Define
\[
C_{ab}
=
\int \widehat A_{a,k}\widehat A_{b,l}\,d\mu-
\bar A_{a,k}\bar A_{b,l}.
\]
Expanding the two arm contrasts gives
\[
\int \widehat P_k\widehat P_l\,d\mu-
\bar P_k\bar P_l
=
C_{11}-C_{10}-C_{01}+C_{00}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\left|
\int \widehat P_k\widehat P_l\,d\mu-
\bar P_k\bar P_l
\right|
\le
|C_{11}|+|C_{10}|+|C_{01}|+|C_{00}|
\le 4\eta R^2
=
\frac{8M^2}{m}\{B\,6^M\}^2 .
\]
% lean: factorialPolynomial_cross_covariance_le

Combining this bound with the centering identity and using \(x\le |x|\) yields
\[
\int(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k)(\widehat P_l-\bar P_l)\,d\mu
\le
\frac{8M(n)^2}{m_1}\{B(n)6^{M(n)}\}^2 .
\]
% lean: integral_factorialPolynomial_centered_cross_le
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:false-light-envelope}[False-light error envelope]
Let \(P\) be a probability law on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2\). Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Overlap.)} For every category \(k\) with \(p_k>0\), the treatment probability satisfies
\[
\epsilon \le \pi_k \le 1-\epsilon .
\]
\item \textbf{(Calibration cutoff.)} The sample size satisfies \(n\ge N_0\), where \(N_0\) is the least numerical cutoff satisfying the calibration inequalities used in the estimator.
\item \textbf{(Logarithmic scale.)} With \(L=\log(en)\), one has \(L\ge 240\).
\end{itemize}
For the pilot-light indicator \(S_k=\mathbf 1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}\), the selected false-light error over categories whose population mass exceeds the light-cell threshold satisfies
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[
\left\{
\sum_{\substack{k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\\ p_k>B(n)/4}}
S_k\bigl(\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\bigr)
\right\}^2
\right]
\le
8(\mathrm e+1)8192^2
\left\{
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:false-light-envelope}]
All expectations below are under the iid infinite product law generating the observation sequence, with each statistic evaluated on its first \(n\) coordinates.  Let
\[
L=\log(en),\qquad \ell=\log n,
\qquad m_j=|I_j|\ (j=0,1),
\qquad B=B(n),\qquad M=M(n),
\qquad G=6^{2M}.
\]
The deterministic split construction of \cref{obj:def:sample-splits} gives
\[
m_0=\lfloor n/2\rfloor,
\qquad
m_1=n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor .
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:calibration-consequences},
\[
0<\ell,
\qquad \ell\le L,
\qquad L\le 2\ell,
\qquad m_1>0,
\qquad n\le 2m_1,
\qquad M\le L,
\]
\[
B\le\frac{8192L}{n},
\qquad G^2L^6\le n .
\]
% lean: large_calibration_bounds
In particular \(n>0\).  Since \(B=4096L/m_1\), \(L>0\), and \(m_1>0\), also \(B>0\), and hence
\[
B^2\le \frac{8192^2L^2}{n^2}.
\]

Because \(n>0\), \(L=1+\ell\).  Since \(L\ge240\),
\[
\exp(-49L)\le \exp(-L)=\frac1{en}\le \frac1n .
\]
Moreover \(G\ge1\), so \(L^4\le L^6\le G^2L^6\le n\).  Together with \(\ell\le L\), this gives
\[
L^2\ell^2\le L^4\le n.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{d^2L^2}{n^3}
\le
\frac{d^2}{n^2\ell^2}
\le
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2\ell^2}.
\]
% lean: selectedFalseLightError_rate

Let
\[
F=\{k:p_k>B/4\},
\qquad
S_k=\mathbf 1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\},
\qquad
e_k=S_k(\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)).
\]
By the definition of the false-light error, the selected false-light error is \(\sum_{k\in F}e_k\).  For each sample, Cauchy's inequality gives
\[
\left(\sum_{k\in F}e_k\right)^2
\le
|F|\sum_{k\in F}e_k^2 .
\]

Fix \(k\in F\).  The cutoff defining \(N_0\) in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} and the assumption \(n\ge N_0\) give
\[
\alpha_0L\ge2,
\qquad
4M^2\le m_1,
\qquad
\frac{4M}{m_1}\le \frac{3B}{4}.
\]
Since \(M\ge2\), the bound \(4M^2\le m_1\) gives \(16\le m_1\).  With the split identities above, this implies \(m_0>0\) and \(m_1\le2m_0\).

Write
\[
G_M(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}g_{M,j}x^j,
\qquad
 g_{M,j}=(-1)^j
 \frac{2^{2j+3}}{M(M+j+2)}\binom{M+j+2}{2j+4}.
\]
Let \(\mathbf e_{by}\) denote the unit multi-index at the four-cell coordinate \((b,y)\in\{0,1\}^2\).  In the next display, \(ay\) is a single four-cell label, say \(ay=(b,y')\), and \(\mathbf e_{ay}\) is the corresponding unit multi-index.  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), \(ay\in\{0,1\}^2\), and \(0\le t\le j\le M-2\), set
\[
r(a,ay,j,t)
=
\mathbf e_{ay}+\mathbf e_{a1}+t\mathbf e_{a0}+(j-t)\mathbf e_{a1},
\qquad |r(a,ay,j,t)|=j+2.
\]
For a multi-index \(r\), the factorial-moment monomial is
\[
\mathfrak m_{k,r}
=
\frac{\prod_{(b,y)\in\{0,1\}^2}
\bigl(N^{(1)}_{bky}\bigr)_{r_{by}}}
{(m_1)_{|r|}},
\]
where \((z)_s\) denotes the falling factorial.  The \(a\)-arm sparse factorial-moment contribution is
\[
\widehat P_{k,a}^{\mathrm{sp}}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^j\sum_{ay\in\{0,1\}^2}
B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\,
\mathfrak m_{k,r(a,ay,j,t)}.
\]
By \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, \(\widehat P_k=\sum_{|r|\le M}c_r\,\mathfrak m_{k,r}\) in collected coefficient form, where each \(c_r\) is obtained by expanding the two arms of \(P_{M,B}\) through the coefficients \(g_{M,j}\) and the binomial expansion of the arm masses, and summing the resulting sparse coefficients \(c_u=B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\) over the sparse indices \(u=(j,t,ay)\) with \(r_u=r\).  Since \(r\mapsto\mathfrak m_{k,r}\) enters linearly in each coefficient, regrouping this finite double sum arm by arm yields
\[
\widehat P_k=\widehat P_{k,1}^{\mathrm{sp}}-
\widehat P_{k,0}^{\mathrm{sp}} .
\]

For a nonnegative four-cell vector \(v\), define the absolute sparse arm envelope
\[
\mathcal A_a(v)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}\sum_{t=0}^j\sum_{ay\in\{0,1\}^2}
\left|B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\right|
 v^{r(a,ay,j,t)},
\qquad
v^r=\prod_{(b,y)\in\{0,1\}^2}v_{by}^{r_{by}}.
\]
Set
\[
v^{(k)}_{ay}=q_{k,ay}+\frac{M}{m_1}.
\]
This vector is nonnegative.  Fix two sparse indices \(u=(j,t,ay)\) and \(u'=(j',t',ay')\) with \(0\le t\le j\le M-2\) and \(0\le t'\le j'\le M-2\).  Both multi-indices \(r_u,r_{u'}\) have degree at most \(M\), so \(4\max\{|r_u|,|r_{u'}|\}^2\le4M^2\le m_1\).  Apply \cref{obj:lem:factorial-product-moment} to the degree-ordered pair.  Its unshifted monomial factor is bounded coordinatewise by \((v^{(k)})^{r}\), since \(q_{k,cy}\le q_{k,cy}+M/m_1\).  Its shifted monomial factor is also bounded coordinatewise by the corresponding power of \(v^{(k)}\): if \(s\) is the larger-degree multi-index in the ordered pair, then \(|s|\le M\), and hence
\[
q_{k,cy}+\frac{|s|}{m_1}
\le
q_{k,cy}+\frac{M}{m_1}
=v^{(k)}_{cy}
\qquad\text{for every coordinate }cy.
\]
Using these two coordinatewise bounds, and swapping \(u,u'\) before applying \cref{obj:lem:factorial-product-moment} when the opposite degree order holds, gives
\[
E\!\left[
\bigl(c_u\mathfrak m_{k,r_u}\bigr)
\bigl(c_{u'}\mathfrak m_{k,r_{u'}}\bigr)
\right]
\le
\mathrm e\,
\left|c_u\right|(v^{(k)})^{r_u}
\left|c_{u'}\right|(v^{(k)})^{r_{u'}},
\]
where \(c_u=B^{-1}g_{M,j}B^{-j}\binom jt\) and \(r_u=r(a,ay,j,t)\).  Expanding the square of \(\widehat P_{k,a}^{\mathrm{sp}}\), summing this bound over all ordered pairs of sparse indices, and collecting the two sums into the envelope gives
\[
E\!\left[(\widehat P_{k,a}^{\mathrm{sp}})^2\right]
\le
\mathrm e\,\mathcal A_a(v^{(k)})^2,
\qquad a=0,1.
\]

The shifted vector has total mass
\[
\sum_{ay}v^{(k)}_{ay}
=p_k+\frac{4M}{m_1}
\le p_k+\frac{3B}{4}
\le B\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right).
\]
Let \(R=\sum_{ay}v_{ay}\), \(A_a(v)=v_{a0}+v_{a1}\), and
\[
g_M^+(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}|g_{M,j}|z^j.
\]
For every nonnegative \(v\) with \(R\le Bw\) and \(w\ge1\), the binomial summation in the sparse envelope gives
\[
\mathcal A_a(v)
=
B^{-1}R\,v_{a1}\,g_M^+\!\left(\frac{A_a(v)}B\right),
\]
and, since \(0\le A_a(v)/B\le R/B\le w\) with \(w\ge1\), \cref{obj:lem:coefficient-sum-certificate} gives
\[
g_M^+\!\left(\frac{A_a(v)}B\right)
\le 6^M\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{A_a(v)}B\right)^{M-2}\right\}
\le 6^M w^{M-2}.
\]
Since \(v_{a1}\le R\le Bw\), these displays imply
\[
\mathcal A_a(v)
\le B6^M w^M.
\]
Applying this with \(v=v^{(k)}\) and \(w=1+p_k/B\) yields
\[
\mathcal A_a(v^{(k)})
\le
B6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)^M,
\qquad a=0,1.
\]
Since \(\widehat P_k\) is the difference of the two sparse-arm contributions, \((x-y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\) yields
\[
E[\widehat P_k^2]
\le
4\mathrm e\,
\left\{
B6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)^M
\right\}^2 .
\]
Because \(k\in F\) and \(B>0\), \(p_k>0\).  The overlap condition and \cref{obj:def:cell-functional} give \(|\phi(q_k)|\le p_k\).  Since \(p_k/B\ge0\) and \(M\ge2\),
\[
\frac{p_k}{B}
\le
6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)^M,
\]
and therefore
\[
\phi(q_k)^2
\le
\left\{
B6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)^M
\right\}^2 .
\]
Using \((x-t)^2\le2x^2+2t^2\),
\[
E\!\left[(\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k))^2\right]
\le
8(\mathrm e+1)B^2
\left\{6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)^M\right\}^2 .
\]

The indicator \(S_k\) is determined by the pilot split, while \(\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\) is computed from the estimation split and a fixed target.  The split factorization gives
\[
E\!\left[S_k^2(\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k))^2\right]
=
E[S_k^2]E\!\left[(\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k))^2\right].
\]
Since \(S_k\in\{0,1\}\), \(E[S_k^2]=E[S_k]\).  The indicator integral is the probability of the pilot-light event:
\[
E[S_k]
= P^{\otimes n}\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}.
\]
For \(n\ge N_0\), the event \(\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}\) is contained in
\[
\left\{N_k^{(0)}\le 256L\right\}.
\]
The false-light condition \(p_k>B/4\), the identity \(B=4096L/m_1\), and \(m_1\le2m_0\) imply
\[
2(256L)<m_0p_k.
\]
The pilot lower-tail bound for this threshold gives
\[
E[S_k]
\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac{m_0p_k}{8}\right).
\]
Since \(p_k/B>1/4\),
\[
1+\frac{p_k}{B}\le 5\frac{p_k}{B},
\qquad
6\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)\le30\frac{p_k}{B}.
\]
Also \(6(1+p_k/B)\ge1\), so \(\log(6(1+p_k/B))\ge0\); and from \(\log x\le x-1\) for \(x>0\),
\[
\log\!\left(6\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)\right)
\le 30\frac{p_k}{B} .
\]
Together with \(M\le L\), \(B=4096L/m_1\), and \(m_1\le2m_0\), this yields
\[
2M\log\!\left(6\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)\right)
\le60L\frac{p_k}{B},
\qquad
256L\frac{p_k}{B}\le \frac{m_0p_k}{8}.
\]
Consequently
\[
-\frac{m_0p_k}{8}
+2M\log\!\left(6\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)\right)
\le -196L\frac{p_k}{B}\le -49L,
\]
and hence
\[
\exp\!\left(-\frac{m_0p_k}{8}\right)
\left\{6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}{B}\right)^M\right\}^2
\le
\exp(-49L).
\]
Combining the preceding displays gives the single-cell bound
\[
E[e_k^2]
\le
8(\mathrm e+1)B^2\exp(-49L).
\]
% lean: falseLight_selected_error_sq

Since \(|F|\le d\), integrating the pointwise Cauchy bound and applying the single-cell estimate gives
\[
E\!\left[
\left(\sum_{k\in F}e_k\right)^2
\right]
\le
|F|\sum_{k\in F}8(\mathrm e+1)B^2\exp(-49L)
\le
d^2\,8(\mathrm e+1)B^2\exp(-49L).
\]
% lean: selectedFalseLightError_second_moment_le

Using the bounds on \(B^2\), \(\exp(-49L)\), and the rate algebra above,
\[
d^2\,8(\mathrm e+1)B^2\exp(-49L)
\le
8(\mathrm e+1)8192^2\,\frac{d^2L^2}{n^3}
\le
8(\mathrm e+1)8192^2
\left\{
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\right\}.
\]
% lean: selectedFalseLightError_rate
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate} aggregates the ratio branch over pilot-certified heavy categories at the target rate. The sparse means in \cref{obj:synth_20} identify the population polynomial quantities used to center the light-cell statistics. The variance bound in \cref{obj:lem:factorial-moment-variance}, the off-diagonal covariance bound in \cref{obj:lem:factorial-moment-covariance}, and the false-light envelope in \cref{obj:lem:false-light-envelope} together control the stochastic and pilot-selection terms that arise in the polynomial branch.

The light-cell theorem combines computability, approximation bias, factorial-moment variance, covariance control, and pilot classification. It is the complementary upper-bound component to \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:light-cell-polynomial}[Light-cell polynomial rate]
Fix an overlap level \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\). The hybrid estimator is computable in the real-arithmetic count model: there is a universal constant \(K\in\mathbb N\), \(K>0\), such that for every \(n,d\) a real-arithmetic program using the split-count vector evaluates \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) exactly and uses at most \(K\,d\,M(n)^4\) arithmetic and comparison operations.

There are constants \(C_\epsilon,c_\epsilon>0\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that, for every \(n,d\), every \(P\in\mathsf{Prob}(\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}^2)\), and every sample law \(\mu_n\), if
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Experiment class.)} \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\) belongs to the overlap-restricted iid product-law experiment class \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class};
\item \textbf{(Dimension range.)} \(d\le c_\epsilon n\log n\);
\end{itemize}
then the light-cell contribution \(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\) satisfies
\[
\int\left(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)
-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right)^2\,d\mu_n
\le
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial}]
The computability assertion uses the real-arithmetic program model in which an instruction is an input read, a constant, one of the arithmetic operations \(+, -, \times, /\), or a nonpositive comparison branch.  Input and constant instructions have cost zero, and each arithmetic or branch instruction has cost one.  The arithmetic is total: division by zero is interpreted as the real value \(0\).  A branch node is compiled as an expression-tree node containing its test and both alternatives, so its operation count is the count of the test, plus the counts of both alternatives, plus one for the branch instruction.  A finite list sum \(x_1+\cdots+x_s\) is compiled recursively as \(x_1+(x_2+\cdots+(x_s+0)\cdots)\); hence its cost is the sum of the costs of the listed expressions plus one addition for each list entry.  The corresponding list product is compiled with a terminal factor \(1\) and contributes one multiplication for each list entry.

The falling-factorial factors use the guarded natural-subtraction expression
\[
x\mathbin{\dot{-}} i
=
\begin{cases}
0,&x-i\le0,\\
x-i,&x-i>0,
\end{cases}
\]
implemented by one nonpositive branch with test \(x-i\) and alternatives \(0\) and \(x-i\).  On natural-valued count inputs this evaluates to the truncated difference, and its operation count is \(2\operatorname{ops}(x)+3\).  Consequently the recursive expression
\((x)_0=1\), \((x)_{r+1}=(x\mathbin{\dot{-}} r)(x)_r\) has cost \(r\{2\operatorname{ops}(x)+4\}\).  A split-count read has cost zero, so a falling-factorial expression of a split count of order \(r\) costs \(4r\).  Thus a factorial monomial of multi-degree \(|r|\), including the final division by \((m_1)_{|r|}\), costs
\[
4|r|+5 .
\]
For a light-polynomial term indexed by \((j,t,a,y)\), the multi-degree is \(j+2\), and multiplication by its coefficient gives cost \(4j+14\).  Using the list-sum convention above, summing the four cell terms costs at most \(50M(n)\), summing over \(t=0,\ldots,j\) costs at most \(51M(n)^2\), and summing over \(j=0,\ldots,M(n)-2\) gives one treatment-arm factorial-polynomial expression with cost at most \(52M(n)^3\).  The two treatment arms and their subtraction therefore give the light-cell expression cost
\[
\operatorname{ops}\{\text{one light category}\}
\le 105M(n)^4 .
\]
The heavy ratio expression has cost \(11\).  The selected-category expression is defined by cases.  If \(n<N_0\), it is the heavy ratio expression; in this range \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n\) is the full category set and \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\varnothing\), so this expression is exact for the selected contribution.  Its cost is \(11\le110M(n)^4\).  If \(n\ge N_0\), the expression branches on the first-split category count minus the threshold constant.  The category-count expression is the list sum of the four split-count reads and costs four additions, the subtraction of the threshold contributes one more operation, the branch contains both the light expression and the heavy ratio expression, and the branch instruction itself contributes one operation.  Since \(M(n)\ge2\), this selected-category branch also satisfies
\[
\operatorname{ops}\{\text{one selected category}\}
\le 110M(n)^4 .
\]
Summing these selected-category expressions over \(d\) categories uses the same recursive list sum and adds \(d\) summation nodes.  The resulting expression computes \(\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\) exactly, hence
\[
\operatorname{ops}\{\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\}
\le 111\,d\,M(n)^4 .
\]
For \(d>0\), the final clamp to \([-1,1]\), as displayed in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, has cost
\[
4\operatorname{ops}\{\widehat T_{\mathcal H}+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}\}+6
\le 450dM(n)^4 .
\]
For \(d=0\), the compiled program is the zero-cost constant zero, which agrees with the empty-category hybrid estimator.  Thus, for every \(n,d\), there is a real-arithmetic program reading the hybrid split-count vector, returning \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) exactly on every sample, and satisfying
\[
\operatorname{ops}\{\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\}
\le 450\,d\,M(n)^4 .
\] % lean: hybridEstimatorComputable
The computability clause therefore holds with the universal constant \(K=450\).

For the light-cell risk bound, fix \(n,d,P\) and a sample law \(\mu_n\) satisfying the hypotheses of the lemma.  By the experiment-class hypothesis in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}, \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\) and \(P\) satisfies \(\epsilon\)-overlap.  In the endpoint case \(n=0\), the displayed rate is interpreted with the same total real convention as the arithmetic model: real division by zero is \(0\) and \(\log 0=0\).  In the calibrated range used below, \(n>0\) and \(\log n>0\), so the displayed rate has its usual analytic meaning.  Set
\[
R_{n,d}:=\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}.
\]
Choose
\[
K_c=4(\mathrm e+1)8192^2+16\,8192^2,
\qquad K_b=\left(\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2}\right)^2,
\qquad K_f=8(\mathrm e+1)8192^2,
\]
and let
\[
C_\epsilon=3(K_c+K_b+K_f),\qquad c_\epsilon=1 .
\] % lean: light_cell_polynomial
The inequalities \(\epsilon>0\) and \(\alpha_0>0\) make these constants positive.  The estimates below are uniform over all \(d\), so the dimension-range hypothesis is accommodated by this choice of \(c_\epsilon\).

First suppose \(n\ge N_0\).  Write
\[
L=\log(en),\qquad \ell=\log n,\qquad M=M(n),\qquad B=B(n),
\qquad m_0=|I_0|,
\qquad m_1=|I_1|,
\]
and define the genuinely light and false-light population sets, together with the pilot light indicator,
\[
G=\{k:p_k\le B/4\},\qquad F=\{k:p_k>B/4\},
\qquad S_k=\mathbf 1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}.
\]
Let \(\bar P_k=P_{M,B}(q_k)\) be the sparse factorial-polynomial mean of \(\widehat P_k\).  Every multi-index \(r\) appearing in the expansion has \(|r|\le M\), and the cutoff inequalities give \(|r|\le M\le m_1\).  Hence the ordered-selection factorial-moment identity applies with the required denominator order:
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes\infty}}
\left[
\frac{\prod_{a,y}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}}{(m_1)_{|r|}}
\right]
=q_k^r .
\]
Substituting this identity into the polynomial expansion of \(\widehat P_k\), equivalently applying \cref{obj:lem:factorial-moment-mean}, gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes\infty}}[\widehat P_k]
=\bar P_k
=P_{M,B}(q_k).
\]
Put
\[
W_{\mathrm{var}}=\sum_{k\in G}S_k(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k),\qquad
W_{\mathrm{app}}=\sum_{k\in G}S_k(\bar P_k-\phi(q_k)),\qquad
W_{\mathrm{sel}}=\sum_{k\in F}S_k(\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)).
\]
Partitioning the selected light categories into \(G\) and \(F\), and adding and subtracting \(\bar P_k\) on \(G\), gives the pointwise identity
\[
\widehat T_{\mathcal L}
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
=
W_{\mathrm{var}}+W_{\mathrm{app}}+W_{\mathrm{sel}} .
\] % lean: light_error_decompose

The cutoff property in \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} gives \(2\le\alpha_0L\), \(4M^2\le m_1\), and \(4M/m_1\le3B/4\).  The numerical definition of \(\alpha_0\) gives \(\alpha_0\le1/120\), since \(D_A=8\log(27/4)>8\) and \((256D_A)^{-1}\le1/120\).  Combining this with \(2\le\alpha_0L\) yields \(L\ge240\).  Therefore \cref{obj:lem:calibration-consequences} supplies
\[
0<\ell,
\qquad \ell\le L,
\qquad n\le2m_1,
\qquad M\le L,
\qquad B\le\frac{8192L}{n},
\qquad \Gamma^2L^6\le n,
\]
where \(\Gamma=6^{2M}\), and also \(\Gamma\ge1\).  For every \(k\in G\), the cutoff shift gives
\[
p_k+\frac{4M}{m_1}\le B .
\]
Thus \cref{obj:lem:factorial-moment-variance} gives, for \(k\in G\),
\[
\int(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k)^2\,dP^{\otimes\infty}
\le
8(\mathrm e+1)(B6^M)^2.
\]
For distinct \(k,l\in G\), \cref{obj:lem:factorial-moment-covariance} gives
\[
\int(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k)(\widehat P_l-\bar P_l)\,dP^{\otimes\infty}
\le
\frac{8M^2}{m_1}(B6^M)^2 .
\]
The pilot indicators depend on the pilot split, while the centered polynomial products depend on the estimation split; their pair expectation factors from the centered product integral and lies in \([0,1]\).  Expanding the square over any deterministic \(\mathcal S\subseteq G\) yields
\[
\int
\left\{\sum_{k\in \mathcal S}S_k(\widehat P_k-\bar P_k)\right\}^2
\,dP^{\otimes\infty}
\le
|\mathcal S|\,8(\mathrm e+1)(B6^M)^2
+
|\mathcal S|^2\frac{8M^2}{m_1}(B6^M)^2 .
\] % lean: selectedFixedLightCentered_second_moment_le
Taking \(\mathcal S=G\) and using \(|G|\le d\), the diagonal rate conversion follows from
\[
(L^2\Gamma\ell^2)^2=L^4\Gamma^2\ell^4
\le n\ell^2,
\]
where the inequality uses \(\ell\le L\) and \(\Gamma^2L^6\le n\).  Thus \(2uv\le u^2+v^2\), with \(u=d\) and \(v=L^2\Gamma\ell^2\), gives
\[
\frac{dL^2\Gamma}{n^2}
\le
\frac12\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2\ell^2}\right\}
=
\frac12R_{n,d}.
\]
The cross-rate conversion uses
\[
L^4\Gamma\ell^2\le L^4\Gamma^2L^2=\Gamma^2L^6\le n,
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{d^2L^4\Gamma}{n^3}
\le
\frac{d^2}{n^2\ell^2}
\le R_{n,d}.
\]
Combining these algebraic inequalities with the preceding raw second-moment bound yields
\[
\int W_{\mathrm{var}}^2\,dP^{\otimes\infty}
\le
K_c R_{n,d}.
\] % lean: selectedGenuineLightCentered_rate

For the deterministic approximation term, overlap gives \(q_k\in\mathcal C_\epsilon\), and \(k\in G\) gives \(s(q_k)=p_k\le B/4\le B\).  Applying \cref{obj:lem:chebyshev-reciprocal-certificate} to the four-cell vector \(q_k\) gives
\[
|\bar P_k-\phi(q_k)|
=|P_{M,B}(q_k)-\phi(q_k)|
\le
\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}
\qquad (k\in G).
\]
Consequently
\[
|W_{\mathrm{app}}|\le d\,\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}.
\]
The cutoff inequality \(2\le\alpha_0L\) gives \(M\ge\alpha_0L/2\), while \cref{obj:lem:calibration-consequences} gives \(B\le8192L/n\) and \(\ell\le L\).  Therefore
\[
\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}
\le
\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2 nL}
\le
\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2 n\ell},
\]
and hence, pointwise in the sample,
\[
W_{\mathrm{app}}^2
\le
\left(\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2}\right)^2
\frac{d^2}{n^2\ell^2}
\le K_bR_{n,d}.
\] % lean: selectedGenuineLightBias_sq_rate

For the false-light term, observe that
\(W_{\mathrm{sel}}=\sum_{k\in F}S_k(\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k))\)
is exactly the selected false-light error of \cref{obj:lem:false-light-envelope}: the summation there ranges over the categories with \(p_k>B/4\), which is the set \(F\).  Its hypotheses hold in the present case: the overlap condition \(\epsilon\le\pi_k\le1-\epsilon\) holds for every category of positive mass because \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), the calibration cutoff \(n\ge N_0\) is the standing assumption of this case, and \(L\ge240\) was derived above from the cutoff property and the numerical value of \(\alpha_0\).  Since \(W_{\mathrm{sel}}\) is a function of the first \(n\) observations, its second moment under \(P^{\otimes\infty}\) equals its second moment under \(P^{\otimes n}\), and \cref{obj:lem:false-light-envelope} gives directly
\[
\int W_{\mathrm{sel}}^2\,dP^{\otimes\infty}
=
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\bigl[W_{\mathrm{sel}}^2\bigr]
\le
8(\mathrm e+1)8192^2
\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2\ell^2}\right\}
=
K_fR_{n,d}.
\] % lean: selectedFalseLightError_rate

The bound for \(W_{\mathrm{var}}\) is obtained on the infinite-product representation of the iid sample and transported to \(P^{\otimes n}\) through equality of integrals for functions of the first \(n\) observations.  Using \((x+y+z)^2\le3(x^2+y^2+z^2)\), the pointwise bound on \(W_{\mathrm{app}}^2\), and the two second-moment bounds, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int
\left\{
\widehat T_{\mathcal L}
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
\right\}^2
\,dP^{\otimes n}
&\le
3\int(W_{\mathrm{var}}^2+W_{\mathrm{app}}^2+W_{\mathrm{sel}}^2)\,dP^{\otimes n} \\
&\le
3(K_c+K_b+K_f)R_{n,d}
=
C_\epsilon R_{n,d}.
\end{aligned}
\] % lean: light_cell_polynomial
Since \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\), this is the desired bound under \(\mu_n\).

If \(n<N_0\), then \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\varnothing\) by \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.  The light contribution and its selected target are both zero, so the left-hand side is zero.  Since \(R_{n,d}\ge0\), the same bound holds in this case. % lean: light_cell_polynomial
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial} establishes that the count-based hybrid estimator is computable with operation count linear in \(d\) and polynomial in \(M(n)\), and that the light-cell contribution attains the same rate as the heavy-cell branch under the displayed dimension range. Together with \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate}, it gives the upper envelope used in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} and \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid}.


\section{Verification note}

This appendix records the verification boundary for the finite-alphabet product-law formulation used throughout the paper. The formal development represents the observed-data experiment in two compatible ways: a finite single-observation law for \(O=(X,A,Y)\), as used in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling,obj:def:experiment-class}, and an ambient infinite iid sample sequence from which finite sample segments can be extracted. The following statement gives the bridge between those two views.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:ambient-sample-marginal}[Ambient sample marginal law]
Let \(P\) be a probability law on a measurable single-observation space, and let \(n\in\mathbb N\). Write \(P^{\otimes\infty}\) for the infinite product law on sequences \((O_i)_{i\ge 0}\), and write \(P^{\otimes n}\) for the finite product law on \(n\)-tuples. Then the coordinate restriction map
\[
(O_i)_{i\ge 0}\longmapsto (O_i)_{i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}}
\]
pushes \(P^{\otimes\infty}\) forward to \(P^{\otimes n}\):
\[
(P^{\otimes\infty})\circ\bigl((O_i)_{i\ge 0}\mapsto (O_i)_{i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}}\bigr)^{-1}
=
P^{\otimes n}.
\]
In particular, for the observed-data laws \(P\) used in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}, the initial sample segment has the \(n\)-fold product law \(P^{\otimes n}\) appearing in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:ambient-sample-marginal}]
Let
\[
\Omega=X^{\mathbb N},\qquad
\mu_\infty=P^{\otimes\infty},
\qquad
Z_i(\omega)=\omega_i .
\]
By the construction of the infinite product measure, the coordinate process
\((Z_i)_{i\ge0}\) is independent and each coordinate has marginal law \(P\):
\[
(Z_i)_\#\mu_\infty=P
\quad\text{for every }i\in\mathbb N .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.iidSample_infinitePi
For fixed \(n\), define the finite coordinate map
\[
C_n(\omega)=\bigl(Z_i(\omega)\bigr)_{i\in \operatorname{Fin}(n)}
          =\bigl(\omega_i\bigr)_{i=0}^{n-1}.
\]
The finite-dimensional law of an independent process is the product of its one-dimensional marginals, hence
\[
(C_n)_\#\mu_\infty
=
\bigotimes_{i\in\operatorname{Fin}(n)} (Z_i)_\#\mu_\infty
=
\bigotimes_{i\in\operatorname{Fin}(n)} P
=
P^{\otimes n}.
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.iidSample_finN_pushforward
This is exactly
\[
(P^{\otimes\infty})\circ C_n^{-1}=P^{\otimes n}.
\]
Taking \(X\) to be the observed-data alphabet and \(P\) to be the single-observation law in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} gives the \(n\)-fold product law used in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class}.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.finProductLaw_eq_map
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:ambient-sample-marginal} justifies using an infinite iid product space as an ambient construction while retaining the finite-sample experiment class in \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} as the law governing \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\). This connection is useful for statements that are naturally organized around coordinate projections, while the statistical risk in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} remains the finite-\(n\) mean-squared risk under \(P^{\otimes n}\).

The verification covers the finite-alphabet iid observational model, the potential-outcome overlay, the overlap-restricted four-cell cone, the cell functional, the ATE functional, the minimax risk, the deterministic split counts, and the hybrid estimator defined in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling,obj:ass:overlap,obj:def:overlap-cone,obj:def:cell-functional,obj:def:ate-functional,obj:def:minimax-risk,obj:def:sample-splits,obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}. The auxiliary proof layer verifies the estimator, approximation, calibration, residual, missing-arm, factorial-moment, and aggregation statements used by the main rate theorems. The theorem statements and proofs are machine-checked in Lean 4; theorem-local verification footnotes identify the published mathematical conclusions invoked as external dependencies.


\section{Proofs of the main results}\label{sec:deferred-proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:two-category-confounding}]
Fix \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\).  Write the two categories as \(x\in\{1,2\}\), and define
\[
\zeta_1=1,\qquad \zeta_2=0,
\]
so that the witness has \(Y(0)=0\) and \(Y(1)=\zeta_x\) in category \(x\).  For an arm value \(\mathfrak a\in\{0,1\}\), set
\[
\eta_{1,1}=\epsilon,\qquad
\eta_{1,0}=1-\epsilon,\qquad
\eta_{2,1}=1-\epsilon,\qquad
\eta_{2,0}=\epsilon .
\]
Then the full-data law \(Q\) in \cref{obj:def:two-category-witness} is
\[
Q\{X=x,A=\mathfrak a,Y=y,Y(0)=y_0,Y(1)=y_1\}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac12\,\eta_{x,\mathfrak a},
&
y_0=0,\ y_1=\zeta_x,\ y=\mathfrak a\zeta_x,\\[1ex]
0,& \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
The displayed masses are nonnegative, since \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\), and their total mass is
\[
\frac12\{\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)\}
+
\frac12\{(1-\epsilon)+\epsilon\}
=1.
\]
Marginalizing \(Q\) over \(Y(0),Y(1)\), the observed law \(P\) has nonzero cells
\[
P(X=1,A=1,Y=1)=\frac{\epsilon}{2},\qquad
P(X=1,A=0,Y=0)=\frac{1-\epsilon}{2},
\]
\[
P(X=2,A=1,Y=0)=\frac{1-\epsilon}{2},\qquad
P(X=2,A=0,Y=0)=\frac{\epsilon}{2}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.two_category_confounding

The category masses \(p_x=P(X=x)\) and propensities \(\pi_x=P(A=1\mid X=x)\) are therefore
\[
p_1=p_2=\frac12,\qquad
\pi_1=\frac{\epsilon/2}{1/2}=\epsilon,\qquad
\pi_2=\frac{(1-\epsilon)/2}{1/2}=1-\epsilon .
\]
Because \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\), each positive-mass category satisfies
\[
\epsilon\le \pi_x\le 1-\epsilon,\qquad x\in\{1,2\}.
\]
Thus \(P\) satisfies \(\epsilon\)-overlap in the sense of \cref{obj:ass:overlap}.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.two_category_confounding

With overlap established, evaluate the ATE functional through the weighted-regression identity
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_x p_x\{\mu_{1x}-\mu_{0x}\}.
\]
For completeness, this identity follows directly from \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}: for the four-cell vector \(q_x\), the totalized convention sets all quotients with zero denominator to \(0\); if \(q_x=0\), both \(\phi(q_x)\) and \(p_x\{\mu_{1x}-\mu_{0x}\}\) are zero, while otherwise expanding
\[
\phi(q_x)
=
p_x\left\{
\frac{q_{x,11}}{q_{x,10}+q_{x,11}}
-
\frac{q_{x,01}}{q_{x,00}+q_{x,01}}
\right\}
\]
gives \(p_x\{\mu_{1x}-\mu_{0x}\}\).  In the present law,
\[
\mu_{11}=1,\qquad \mu_{01}=0,\qquad
\mu_{12}=0,\qquad \mu_{02}=0,
\]
and hence
\[
\tau(P)
=
\frac12(1-0)+\frac12(0-0)
=
\frac12 .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.ateFunctional_eq_weighted_regression
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.two_category_confounding

The consistency assertion is immediate from the support display for \(Q\).  Every atom with positive \(Q\)-mass has \(Y(0)=0\), \(Y(1)=\zeta_x\), and
\[
Y=
\begin{cases}
Y(1),& A=1,\\
Y(0),& A=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Equivalently, every atom for which \(Y\) differs from the potential outcome selected by \(A\) receives mass \(0\), which is the condition in \cref{obj:ass:consistency}.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.two_category_confounding

For conditional exchangeability, define, for \(x\in\{1,2\}\), \(\mathfrak a\in\{0,1\}\), and \(\omega_0,\omega_1\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\Lambda_x(\mathfrak a,\omega_0,\omega_1)
=
\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}
Q\{X=x,A=\mathfrak a,Y=y,Y(0)=\omega_0,Y(1)=\omega_1\}.
\]
The finite conditional-independence identity in \cref{obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability} is
\[
\Lambda_x(\mathfrak a,\omega_0,\omega_1)
\sum_{\mathfrak a',\omega_0',\omega_1'}
\Lambda_x(\mathfrak a',\omega_0',\omega_1')
=
\left\{\sum_{\mathfrak a'}\Lambda_x(\mathfrak a',\omega_0,\omega_1)\right\}
\left\{\sum_{\omega_0',\omega_1'}\Lambda_x(\mathfrak a,\omega_0',\omega_1')\right\}.
\]
From the definition of \(Q\),
\[
\Lambda_x(\mathfrak a,\omega_0,\omega_1)
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac12\,\eta_{x,\mathfrak a},&(\omega_0,\omega_1)=(0,\zeta_x),\\[1ex]
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Also \(\sum_{\mathfrak a}\eta_{x,\mathfrak a}=1\), so the total mass in category \(x\) is \(1/2\).  If \((\omega_0,\omega_1)=(0,\zeta_x)\), the two sides of the displayed identity are both
\[
\left(\frac12\eta_{x,\mathfrak a}\right)\left(\frac12\right).
\]
For every other pair \((\omega_0,\omega_1)\), the factor involving that potential-outcome pair is zero on both sides.  Thus \(Q\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability}.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.two_category_confounding

Finally, the naive treated-minus-control contrast under \(P\) has
\[
\sum_x P(X=x,A=1,Y=1)=\frac{\epsilon}{2},\qquad
\sum_x P(X=x,A=0,Y=1)=0,
\]
and
\[
\sum_x P(X=x,A=1)=\frac12,\qquad
\sum_x P(X=x,A=0)=\frac12.
\]
Therefore
\[
E_P[Y\mid A=1]-E_P[Y\mid A=0]
=
\frac{\epsilon/2}{1/2}-\frac{0}{1/2}
=
\epsilon .
\]
Together with the preceding paragraphs, this proves all asserted conclusions.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.two_category_confounding
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}]
Fix an overlap level \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and use the theorem's cited fixed-sample control-zero one-arm minimax lower-bound input at this overlap level.  Write
\[
r_{n,d}=\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item First derive the fixed-interior upper envelope for the hybrid estimator.  From
\cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial} choose constants
\(\mathfrak C_{\mathcal L},\mathfrak c_{\mathcal L}>0\) such that, whenever
\(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) and
\(d\le \mathfrak c_{\mathcal L}n\log n\),
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
\left\{
\widehat T_{\mathcal L}
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
\right\}^2
\right]
\le
\mathfrak C_{\mathcal L} r_{n,d}.
\]
From \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate} choose
\(\mathfrak C_{\mathcal H},\mathfrak c_{\mathcal H}>0\) and
\(\mathfrak N_{\mathcal H}<\infty\) such that, whenever
\(n\ge\mathfrak N_{\mathcal H}\), \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), and
\(d\le \mathfrak c_{\mathcal H}n\log n\),
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
\left\{
\widehat T_{\mathcal H}
-
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n}\phi(q_k)
\right\}^2
\right]
\le
\mathfrak C_{\mathcal H} r_{n,d}.
\]
Set
\[
\mathfrak C_{\rm up}=2(\mathfrak C_{\mathcal L}+\mathfrak C_{\mathcal H}),
\qquad
\mathfrak\rho_{\rm up}=\min\{\mathfrak c_{\mathcal L},\mathfrak c_{\mathcal H}\},
\qquad
\mathfrak N_{\rm up}=\mathfrak N_{\mathcal H}.
\]
For a sample array \(o_{1:n}\), define the pilot targets
\[
\mathcal T_{\mathcal H}^{\circ}(P,o_{1:n})
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n(o_{1:n})}\phi(q_k),
\qquad
\mathcal T_{\mathcal L}^{\circ}(P,o_{1:n})
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n(o_{1:n})}\phi(q_k).
\]
The sets \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_n(o_{1:n})\) and \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n(o_{1:n})\) partition \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\), hence
\[
\mathcal T_{\mathcal H}^{\circ}(P,o_{1:n})
+
\mathcal T_{\mathcal L}^{\circ}(P,o_{1:n})
=
\sum_{k=1}^d\phi(q_k)
=
\tau(P),
\]
where the last equality is \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}.
The interval bound needed for truncation follows from the cell formula.  For each category, write
\[
p_k=\sum_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}q_{aky},
\qquad
s_{ak}=q_{ak0}+q_{ak1},
\qquad
\mu_{ak}=q_{ak1}/s_{ak},
\]
with the empty-arm value interpreted by the totalized convention \(\mu_{ak}=0\) when
\(s_{ak}=0\).  By \cref{obj:def:cell-functional}, the zero vector has
\(\phi(0)=0\), so a zero-mass category contributes
\(0=p_k\{\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}\}\).  On a positive-mass category satisfying overlap, the two arm
masses are positive, and the displayed formula in \cref{obj:def:cell-functional} gives
\[
\phi(q_k)
=
p_k\left\{\frac{q_{1k1}}{s_{1k}}-\frac{q_{0k1}}{s_{0k}}\right\}
=
p_k\{\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}\}.
\]
Summing this identity and using \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} gives
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^d p_k\{\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}\}.
\]
Here \(p_k\ge0\), \(\sum_{k=1}^d p_k=1\), and the totalized binary-outcome means satisfy
\(0\le\mu_{ak}\le1\).  Therefore \(|\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}|\le1\) for every \(k\), and
\[
|\tau(P)|
\le
\sum_{k=1}^d p_k |\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}|
\le
\sum_{k=1}^d p_k
=1.
\]
Thus \(\tau(P)\in[-1,1]\).  Let
\[
\Pi_{[-1,1]}(x)=\max\{-1,\min\{1,x\}\}.
\]
The elementary projection inequality
\[
\{\Pi_{[-1,1]}(x)-\tau(P)\}^2\le \{x-\tau(P)\}^2
\]
holds for every real \(x\).  Applying it to
\(x=\widehat T_{\mathcal H}(o_{1:n})+\widehat T_{\mathcal L}(o_{1:n})\), and then using
\((u+v)^2\le2u^2+2v^2\), gives the pointwise bound
\[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\le
2\left\{\widehat T_{\mathcal H}-\mathcal T_{\mathcal H}^{\circ}(P,\cdot)\right\}^2
+
2\left\{\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\mathcal T_{\mathcal L}^{\circ}(P,\cdot)\right\}^2 .
\]
Integrating with respect to \(P^{\otimes n}\) and using the two component bounds yields, for
\(n\ge\mathfrak N_{\rm up}\) and
\(d\le\mathfrak\rho_{\rm up}n\log n\),
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\right]
\le
\mathfrak C_{\rm up} r_{n,d}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) gives the same bound
for the worst-case risk of \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\).  Since this estimator is a
measurable estimator on the finite sample space and hence is one admissible estimator in the
infimum defining \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk},
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\right]
\le
\mathfrak C_{\rm up} r_{n,d}.
\]
% lean: clamp_sq_error_le; targetHeavy_add_targetLight; hybrid_mse_le_component_errors; hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\item The lower-transfer step is supplied by \cref{obj:lem:ate-lower-bound-transfer}, applied with the cited fixed-sample control-zero one-arm lower-bound input of \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}.  Thus there are
\(\mathfrak a_{\rm low},\mathfrak\rho_{\rm low}>0\) and
\(\mathfrak N_{\rm low}<\infty\) such that, for \(0<d\),
\(n\ge\mathfrak N_{\rm low}\), and
\(d\le \mathfrak\rho_{\rm low}n\log n\),
\[
\mathfrak a_{\rm low} r_{n,d}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}.
\]
Define
\[
a_\epsilon=\mathfrak a_{\rm low},\qquad
\rho_\epsilon=\min\{\mathfrak\rho_{\rm up},\mathfrak\rho_{\rm low}\},\qquad
C_\epsilon=\mathfrak C_{\rm up},\qquad
N_\epsilon=\max\{\mathfrak N_{\rm up},\mathfrak N_{\rm low}\}.
\]
These constants are positive, with \(N_\epsilon<\infty\).  If \(0<d\),
\(n\ge N_\epsilon\), and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\), then both the upper and lower
ranges apply, so
\[
a_\epsilon r_{n,d}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\right]
\le
C_\epsilon r_{n,d}.
\]
This is the sharp sandwich assertion.
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

\item Fix \(K>0\).  Set
\[
C_K=C_\epsilon(1+K^2).
\]
Then \(C_K>0\).  Suppose \(0<d\), \(n\ge N_\epsilon\),
\(d\le K\sqrt n\log n\), and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\).  The sample size is positive by the displayed range: if \(n=0\), then \(K\sqrt n\log n=0\), contradicting \(0<d\le K\sqrt n\log n\).  If \(\log n\le0\), then
\(K\sqrt n\ge0\) and therefore \(K\sqrt n\log n\le0\), again contradicting
\(0<d\le K\sqrt n\log n\).  Thus \(\log n>0\).
With both sides of \(d\le K\sqrt n\log n\) nonnegative, squaring gives
\[
d^2\le K^2 n(\log n)^2.
\]
Dividing by the positive denominator \(n^2(\log n)^2\) gives
\[
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\le \frac{K^2}{n}.
\]
Hence
\[
r_{n,d}
=
\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\le
\frac{1+K^2}{n}.
\]
The lower side of the sandwich gives
\[
\frac{a_\epsilon}{n}
\le
a_\epsilon r_{n,d}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon},
\]
and the upper side gives
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
C_\epsilon r_{n,d}
\le
\frac{C_\epsilon(1+K^2)}{n}
=
\frac{C_K}{n}.
\]
This proves the parametric interior assertion.
% lean: sharp_minimax_fixed_interior

\item Let \(n_j,d_j\) be sequences with \(n_j\to\infty\), with \(d_j>0\) eventually, and with
\[
d_j\le\rho_\epsilon n_j\log n_j
\]
eventually.  Finite initial indices leave convergence unchanged, so work on the tail where
\(n_j\ge N_\epsilon\), \(n_j\ge2\), \(d_j>0\), and the displayed range condition holds.  Define
\[
\eta_j=\frac{d_j}{n_j\log n_j},
\qquad
r_j=\frac1{n_j}+\eta_j^2.
\]
On this tail, \(r_j=r_{n_j,d_j}\) and \(\eta_j\ge0\).  The overlap class is nonempty for
such \(d_j\): since \(d_j>0\), the endpoint parametric law on \(\{1,\ldots,d_j\}\) with
uniform category masses, treatment probability \(1/2\), and both binary response means
\(1/2\) has propensity \(1/2\) in every positive-mass category, and therefore satisfies the
\(\epsilon\)-overlap condition because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Applying the sandwich on the tail
gives
\[
a_\epsilon r_j
\le
\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}
\le
C_\epsilon r_j .
\]
If \(\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\to0\), then
\[
0\le r_j\le a_\epsilon^{-1}\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}
\]
eventually, so \(r_j\to0\).  Since
\[
0\le \eta_j^2\le r_j,
\]
we have \(\eta_j^2\to0\), and the eventual nonnegativity of \(\eta_j\) gives
\(\eta_j\to0\).  Conversely, if \(\eta_j\to0\), then \(1/n_j\to0\) and
\(\eta_j^2\to0\), so \(r_j\to0\).  Since minimax risks are nonnegative, the eventual bound
\[
0\le
\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}
\le
C_\epsilon r_j
\]
then yields \(\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\to0\).  Therefore
\[
\mathsf R_{n_j,d_j,\epsilon}\to0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{d_j}{n_j\log n_j}\to0 .
\]
% lean: canonicalClassLaw; sharp_minimax_fixed_interior
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:overlap-adaptive-universal-hybrid}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Computability.}
Use the real-arithmetic program model in which a program is a finite register list with input reads, real constants, additions, subtractions, multiplications, divisions, and branch comparisons.  Input reads and constants have cost zero, while each arithmetic operation and comparison has cost one.  Division is the total real-field operation, so division by a zero denominator returns zero.  A branch comparison has a test expression and two branch expressions and returns the first branch when the test value is nonpositive and the second branch otherwise.

For \(n,d\in\mathbb N\), the input alphabet for the program is
\[
\{0,1\}\times\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}.
\]
At input coordinate \((j,k,a,y)\) the program reads the real number
\[
N^{(j)}_{aky}
=
\#\{i\in I_j:(X_i,A_i,Y_i)=(k,a,y)\}.
\]
Equivalently, the input vector is the collection of the \(8d\) split cell counts \(N^{(j)}_{aky}\).  Sums and products below are compiled as expression trees folded onto the initial constants \(0\) and \(1\): a list sum has cost equal to the sum of the costs of its entries plus one addition for each entry, and a list product has cost equal to the sum of the costs of its entries plus one multiplication for each entry.  Thus a four-term category count has cost \(4\), not \(3\).

If \(d=0\), the program is the constant-zero program.  Its operation count is zero, and its value agrees with \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\), since the heavy and light sums range over the empty alphabet and the truncation of zero is zero.

Now suppose \(d>0\), and write \(M=M(n)\).  A split-count input has cost zero.  For a natural-valued expression \(x\), the truncated subtraction \((x-i)_+\) is compiled as a branch on \(x-i\), returning \(0\) on the nonpositive branch and \(x-i\) on the positive branch.  This expression has cost \(2\operatorname{op}(x)+3\).  The falling factorial \(x(x-1)_+\cdots(x-r+1)_+\) is compiled by the corresponding folded product, so its cost is \(r\{2\operatorname{op}(x)+4\}\).  Therefore, for a split-count input \(x\), the falling-factorial expression of order \(r\) has cost \(4r\).

For a multi-index \(r\), the factorial monomial expression is
\[
\frac{\prod_{a,y}\bigl(N^{(1)}_{aky}\bigr)_{r_{ay}}}
     {(m_1)_{|r|}},
\]
with the same total division convention as the program model.  The folded product over the four cells contributes \(4|r|+4\) operations, and the final division by the constant denominator contributes one more, giving
\[
\operatorname{op}\{\text{factorial monomial }r\}=4|r|+5.
\]
For the multi-indices used in the arm expansion of \(P_{M,B}\), \(|r|=j+2\).  After multiplication by its coefficient, one fixed-cell summand at level \((j,t)\) has cost
\[
4j+14.
\]
Since \(M\ge2\), the nested sums in one treatment arm satisfy
\[
\operatorname{op}\{\text{sum over }(a,y)\text{ at fixed }j,t\}
\le 50M,
\]
\[
\operatorname{op}\{\text{sum over }t\text{ at fixed }j\}
\le 51M^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{op}\{\text{one arm sum}\}
\le 52M^3.
\]
Subtracting the two arm sums gives the light-cell expression, and hence
\[
\operatorname{op}\{\text{light-cell expression for category }k\}
\le 105M^4.
\]

The heavy expression for category \(k\) is
\[
\frac{N_k^{(1)}}{m_1}
\left\{
\frac{N^{(1)}_{1k1}}{N^{(1)}_{1k0}+N^{(1)}_{1k1}}
-
\frac{N^{(1)}_{0k1}}{N^{(1)}_{0k0}+N^{(1)}_{0k1}}
\right\},
\]
where \(N_k^{(1)}=\sum_{a,y}N^{(1)}_{aky}\), again using total division at zero denominators.  This expression has cost \(11\) and agrees exactly with \(\widehat h_k\).  The pilot count expression \(\sum_{a,y}N^{(0)}_{aky}\) has cost \(4\), so the comparison expression \(\sum_{a,y}N^{(0)}_{aky}-\lambda_0L\) has cost \(5\).  For \(n\ge N_0\), the selected category expression branches to the light expression when the pilot count is at most \(\lambda_0L\) and to the heavy expression when it is larger.  Its cost is at most
\[
5+105M^4+11+1\le 110M^4,
\]
where the final \(1\) is the branch comparison itself.  For \(n<N_0\), the selected category expression is the heavy expression, whose cost \(11\) is also at most \(110M^4\).  Thus each selected category expression has operation count at most \(110M(n)^4\).

Summing the \(d\) selected category expressions uses one addition node per category in the folded sum, so the untruncated expression has cost at most
\[
111\,d\,M(n)^4.
\]
The truncation
\[
x\longmapsto \max\{-1,\min\{1,x\}\}
\]
is compiled by two branch comparisons, with the relevant expression duplicated in the branch subtrees.  Consequently
\[
\operatorname{op}\{\operatorname{clamp}(x)\}
=4\operatorname{op}(x)+6.
\]
For \(d>0\), this gives
\[
\operatorname{op}(\mathscr A_{n,d})
\le 4\cdot 111\,d\,M(n)^4+6
\le 450\,d\,M(n)^4,
\]
because \(1\le dM(n)^4\).  Together with the zero-alphabet branch, the compiled program \(\mathscr A_{n,d}\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{op}(\mathscr A_{n,d})
\le 450\,d\,M(n)^4
\]
and, for every sample,
\[
\mathscr A_{n,d}\bigl((N^{(j)}_{aky})_{j,k,a,y}\bigr)
=
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}(O_{1:n}).
\]
Taking \(K=450\) proves the computability clause.
% lean: hybridEstimatorComputable

\item \textbf{Fixed-interior hybrid bound.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Let \(C_{\mathrm L},c_{\mathrm L}>0\) be the constants supplied by \cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial}, and let \(C_{\mathrm H},\rho_{\mathrm H}>0\) and \(N_{\mathrm H}\in\mathbb N\) be the constants supplied by \cref{obj:lem:universal-heavy-cell-rate}.  Set
\[
C_\epsilon=2(C_{\mathrm L}+C_{\mathrm H}),
\qquad
\rho_\epsilon=\min\{c_{\mathrm L},\rho_{\mathrm H}\},
\qquad
N_\epsilon=N_{\mathrm H}.
\]
These constants are positive in the required real coordinates.

For a sample \(z=O_{1:n}\), define
\[
T_{\mathcal H}^{\circ}(P,z)
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_n(z)}\phi(q_k),
\qquad
T_{\mathcal L}^{\circ}(P,z)
=
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n(z)}\phi(q_k).
\]
Since \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n(z)=\widehat{\mathcal H}_n(z)^c\),
\[
T_{\mathcal H}^{\circ}(P,z)+T_{\mathcal L}^{\circ}(P,z)=\tau(P).
\]
Also \(\tau(P)\in[-1,1]\) for every law in the overlap class.  Hence the truncation map is contractive around \(\tau(P)\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}(z)-\tau(P)\right)^2
&\le
\left[
\{\widehat T_{\mathcal H}(z)-T_{\mathcal H}^{\circ}(P,z)\}
+
\{\widehat T_{\mathcal L}(z)-T_{\mathcal L}^{\circ}(P,z)\}
\right]^2  \\
&\le
2\{\widehat T_{\mathcal H}(z)-T_{\mathcal H}^{\circ}(P,z)\}^2
+
2\{\widehat T_{\mathcal L}(z)-T_{\mathcal L}^{\circ}(P,z)\}^2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Integrating under any \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\right]
&\le
2C_{\mathrm H}\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
+
2C_{\mathrm L}\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}  \\
&=
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\},
\end{aligned}
\]
whenever \(0<n\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\), and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\).  The light and heavy bounds apply because this dimension condition implies both
\[
d\le c_{\mathrm L}n\log n
\quad\text{and}\quad
d\le \rho_{\mathrm H}n\log n .
\]
Taking the supremum over \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\) proves the fixed-interior calibration bound for \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\).  In the auxiliary zero-alphabet case used by the selector lemma, the experiment class is empty and the displayed upper bound follows from the empty-supremum convention and nonnegativity of the rate.
% lean: hybrid_mse_le_component_errors; hybrid_upper_fixed_interior

\item \textbf{Selector envelope.}
Apply \cref{obj:lem:combined-upper-envelope} with the constants just constructed and with the hybrid bound from the previous step.  For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(0<n\), \(0<d\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\), and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\), the selected estimator that chooses \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) when
\[
C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\]
and chooses \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) otherwise satisfies
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\le
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\right]
\]
and
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sel}}_{C_\epsilon,\epsilon}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\right]
\le
\max\{C_\epsilon,4\}
\left[
\frac1n+
\min\left\{
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2},
(1/2-\epsilon)^2
\right\}
\right].
\]
% lean: combined_upper_envelope

\item \textbf{Centered randomization bound.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\), \(0<n\), and \(0<d\).  For every \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\), \cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} gives
\[
E_P\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\right]
\le
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2 .
\]
The right-hand side is independent of \(P\), so taking the supremum over the experiment class gives
\[
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}}
E_P\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\right]
\le
\frac1n+4(1/2-\epsilon)^2 .
\]
The same conclusion covers the empty-class branch through the same empty-supremum convention.
% lean: near_randomization_linear_upper

\item \textbf{Endpoint bracket.}
For \(0<n\) and \(0<d\), \cref{obj:lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax} gives directly
\[
\frac{1}{100n}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}
\le
\frac1n .
\]
This is the endpoint bracket.
% lean: randomized_endpoint_minimax
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
