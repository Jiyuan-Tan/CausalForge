\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:light-cell-polynomial}]
\emph{Computability.}  The program reads the \(8d\) split counts \(N^{(j)}_{aky}\) of \cref{obj:def:split-cell-counts} and evaluates the expression tree obtained by transcribing \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} literally; a ratio with vanishing denominator returns \(0\), which is what the indicators \(\mathbf1\{N^{(1)}_{ak}>0\}\) in \(\widehat h_k\) prescribe.  We count one operation for each arithmetic operation and each comparison, and none for reading an input or a constant.  Recall \(M=M(n)\ge2\).
% lean: hybridEstimatorComputable, hybridArithmeticProgram, hybridArithmeticProgram_eval
Building up from the innermost term:
\begin{itemize}
\item a falling factorial \((N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}\) costs \(4r_{ay}\) operations, four per factor \(N^{(1)}_{aky}-\ell\); the four-cell product followed by the division by \((m_1)_{|r|}\) therefore makes one factorial monomial \(\prod_{a,y}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}/(m_1)_{|r|}\) cost \(4|r|+5\) operations;
% lean: operationCount_fallingExpression, operationCount_factorialMonomialExpression
\item the monomials that occur in the expansion of \(P_{M,B}\) are indexed by an arm \(a\), a cell \((a',y')\), and integers \(0\le t\le j\le M-2\), and the corresponding multi-index has \(|r|=j+2\), so one coefficient-times-monomial term costs \(4j+14\) operations;
% lean: multiDegree_hybridTermIndex, operationCount_factorialTerm
\item summing the four cells costs \(16j+60\le 50M\); summing over \(t\le j\) costs at most \(M(50M+1)\le51M^2\); summing over \(j\le M-2\) costs at most \((M-1)(51M^2+1)\le52M^3\); hence each arm costs at most \(52M^3\), and \(\widehat P_k=\)\,(arm \(1\))\,\(-\)\,(arm \(0\)) costs at most \(2\cdot52M^3+1\le105M^4\);
% lean: operationCount_armFactorialExpression_le, operationCount_lightCellExpression_le
\item for one category \(k\), forming the pilot count \(\sum_{a,y}N^{(0)}_{aky}\) costs \(4\), comparing it with \(\lambda_0L\) costs \(2\), and the ratio contribution \(\widehat h_k\) costs \(11\); so a category costs at most \(105M^4+17\le110M^4\) when \(n\ge N_0\), and exactly \(11\le110M^4\) when \(n<N_0\), where every category takes the ratio branch;
% lean: operationCount_heavyCellExpression, operationCount_selectedCellExpression_le
\item adding the \(d\) category contributions costs at most \(d\cdot110M^4+d\le111\,dM^4\), and the truncation \(\max\{-1,\min(1,\cdot)\}\) multiplies the count by four and adds six, giving at most \(4\cdot111\,dM^4+6\le450\,dM^4\) because \(dM^4\ge1\).
% lean: operationCount_untruncatedHybridExpression_le, operationCount_clampExpression, operationCount_hybridExpression_le
\end{itemize}
For \(d=0\) the program is the constant \(0\) and costs nothing.  In both cases the program returns \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{hyb}}\) exactly, so the first assertion holds with the universal constant \(K=450\).
% lean: hybridEstimatorComputable

\emph{Risk bound.}  Write \(\mathsf r_{n,d}=1/n+d^2/\{n^2(\log n)^2\}\) for the target rate, put
\[
K_c=4(e+1)8192^2+16\cdot8192^2,\qquad
K_b=\left(\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2}\right)^2,\qquad
K_f=8(e+1)8192^2,
\]
and take \(C_\epsilon=3(K_c+K_b+K_f)\) and \(c_\epsilon=1\).  These are positive and depend only on \(\epsilon\), since \(\alpha_0\) is the numerical constant of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}.
% lean: light_cell_polynomial
Fix \(n,d,P\) and a sample law in \(\mathcal E_{n,d,\epsilon}\); by \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} that law is \(P^{\otimes n}\) and \(P\) obeys \cref{obj:ass:overlap} with constant \(\epsilon\).  All expectations below are under \(P^{\otimes n}\), equivalently under the law of an infinite iid sequence with marginal \(P\), because every integrand depends only on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\); all of them are finite sums over a finite sample space, so every expectation exists and integration is linear and monotone.
% lean: light_integral_productLaw_eq_infinite

If \(n<N_0\), then \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n=\varnothing\) by \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, so \(\widehat T_{\mathcal L}=0\) and \(\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)=0\); the left-hand side vanishes and \(C_\epsilon\mathsf r_{n,d}\ge0\).
% lean: lightCells_eq_empty_of_lt_cutoff

Assume from now on \(n\ge N_0\), so that the three calibration inequalities of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle} are available:
\[
\alpha_0L\ge2,\qquad 4M^2\le m_1,\qquad \frac{4M}{m_1}\le\frac{3B}4 .
\]
% lean: cutoffProperty, calibrationCutoff
Four consequences are used repeatedly.  First, \(D_A=8\log(27/4)>8\) because \(\log(27/4)>1\), hence \(\alpha_0\le1/(256D_A)\le1/120\) and therefore
\[
L\ \ge\ \frac2{\alpha_0}\ \ge\ 240 .
\]
% lean: cutoff_logScale_ge_240
Second, \(m_1=n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor\ge n/2\), so
\[
B=\frac{4096\,L}{m_1}\le\frac{8192\,L}{n},
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac1{m_1}\le\frac2n .
\]
% lean: large_calibration_bounds
Third, \(\log n\le L\le2\log n\) and \(M\le L\), and the calibration also gives the growth inequality
\[
6^{4M}L^6\le n .
\]
% lean: large_calibration_bounds, calibrated_polynomial_log_growth
Fourth, \(\alpha_0L\ge2\) forces \(\lfloor\alpha_0L\rfloor\ge2\), so \(M=\lfloor\alpha_0L\rfloor\ge\alpha_0L-1\ge\alpha_0L/2\), i.e.
\[
M\ \ge\ \frac{\alpha_0L}2 .
\]
% lean: polynomialDegree_lower_half

\emph{The three error terms.}  Split the categories by their population mass into the deterministic sets
\[
\mathcal G_n=\left\{k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:p_k\le B/4\right\},
\qquad
\mathcal G_n^{c}=\left\{k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:p_k>B/4\right\},
\]
the genuinely light categories and the rest; \(\mathcal G_n\) depends on \(P\) only, whereas the selected set \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\) is random.
% lean: genuineLightSet, falseLightSet
Every factorial monomial appearing in \(\widehat P_k\) has multi-index of total order at most \(M\le4M^2\le m_1\), and for \(|r|\le m_1\) the ratio \(\prod_{a,y}(N^{(1)}_{aky})_{r_{ay}}/(m_1)_{|r|}\) is an unbiased estimator of the monomial \(\prod_{a,y}q_{aky}^{r_{ay}}\); summing the expansion of \(P_{M,B}\) term by term therefore gives, for every category \(k\),
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\widehat P_k\right]=P_{M,B}(q_k).
\]
% lean: integral_factorialPolynomialContribution_trunc, sparsePolynomialMean_eq_cellApprox
Define
\begin{align*}
X&=\sum_{k\in\mathcal G_n}\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}
\left\{\widehat P_k-P_{M,B}(q_k)\right\},\\
Y&=\sum_{k\in\mathcal G_n}\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}
\left\{P_{M,B}(q_k)-\phi(q_k)\right\},\\
Z&=\sum_{k\in\mathcal G_n^{c}}\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}
\left\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\right\}.
\end{align*}
Since the selection indicator annihilates the categories outside \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\), splitting the sum over all categories at \(\mathcal G_n\) and inserting \(\pm P_{M,B}(q_k)\) on \(\mathcal G_n\) gives the pointwise identity
\[
\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)
=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}\left\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\right\}
=X+Y+Z .
\]
% lean: light_error_decompose

\emph{Bound for \(X\).}  Every \(k\in\mathcal G_n\) satisfies \(p_k+4M/m_1\le B/4+3B/4=B\) by the third calibration inequality, and \(4M^2\le m_1\); for such categories the factorial-moment estimator obeys the variance and covariance bounds
\begin{align*}
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\left\{\widehat P_k-P_{M,B}(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
&\le8(e+1)\left(B6^M\right)^2,\\
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\left\{\widehat P_k-P_{M,B}(q_k)\right\}
\left\{\widehat P_l-P_{M,B}(q_l)\right\}\right]
&\le\frac{8M^2}{m_1}\left(B6^M\right)^2
\end{align*}
for \(k\ne l\), the factor \(6^M\) being the coefficient envelope of \(P_{M,B}\) on categories of mass at most \(B\).
% lean: integral_factorialPolynomial_centered_sq_le, integral_factorialPolynomial_centered_cross_le
The selection indicators are measurable functions of the pilot split \(I_0\) and the centered errors are functions of the estimation split \(I_1\), so the two factor in each pairwise expectation, and the indicator factor lies in \([0,1]\); expanding \(X^2\) into its \(|\mathcal G_n|^2\) pairs and using \(|\mathcal G_n|\le d\) gives
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[X^2\right]
\le |\mathcal G_n|\cdot8(e+1)\left(B6^M\right)^2+|\mathcal G_n|^2\cdot\frac{8M^2}{m_1}\left(B6^M\right)^2
\le 8(e+1)8192^2\,\frac{d\,L^26^{2M}}{n^2}+16\cdot8192^2\,\frac{d^2L^46^{2M}}{n^3},
\]
where the second step used \(B\le8192L/n\), \(M\le L\) and \(1/m_1\le2/n\).
% lean: selectedFixedLightCentered_second_moment_le
The growth inequality \(6^{4M}L^6\le n\) together with \(0<\log n\le L\) converts these into rate terms,
\[
\frac{d\,L^26^{2M}}{n^2}\le\frac{\mathsf r_{n,d}}2,
\qquad
\frac{d^2L^46^{2M}}{n^3}\le\mathsf r_{n,d},
\]
% lean: diagonal_rate_algebra, cross_rate_algebra
so that
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[X^2\right]\le K_c\,\mathsf r_{n,d}.
\]
% lean: selectedGenuineLightCentered_rate

\emph{Bound for \(Y\).}  Fix \(k\in\mathcal G_n\).  The four-cell vector \(q_k\) has total mass \(s(q_k)=p_k\le B/4\le B\), and it lies in the cone \(\mathcal C_\epsilon\) of \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone}: on categories with \(p_k>0\) this is \cref{obj:ass:overlap} written as \(\epsilon s(q_k)\le s_1(q_k)\le(1-\epsilon)s(q_k)\), and on categories with \(p_k=0\) all four coordinates vanish.
% lean: cellVector_mem_overlapCone
The Chebyshev polynomial of \cref{obj:def:chebyshev-polynomial} satisfies \(T_M(1-2x)=1-2M^2x+2M^2x^2G_M(x)\) for the continuation \(G_M\) of \cref{obj:def:hybrid-estimator-handle}, and \(|T_M|\le1\) on \([-1,1]\), whence the reciprocal-approximation certificate
\[
x\left|1-xG_M(x)\right|\le\frac1{M^2},\qquad 0\le x\le1 .
\]
% lean: chebyshev_gPolynomial_identity, gPolynomial_certificate
Applying it at \(x=s_a(u)/B\in[0,1]\) for each arm \(a\), and using \(t_a(u)\le s_a(u)\) and \(s_a(u)\ge\epsilon s(u)\) from \cref{obj:def:overlap-cone}, the two arms of \(P_{M,B}\) each approximate the corresponding arm of \(\phi\) to within \(B/(\epsilon M^2)\), so
\[
\left|P_{M,B}(u)-\phi(u)\right|\le\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}
\qquad\text{for all }u\in\mathcal C_\epsilon\text{ with }s(u)\le B .
\]
% lean: cellApproxPolynomial_error
Since the selection indicator is bounded by one and \(|\mathcal G_n|\le d\), the triangle inequality gives, pointwise in the sample,
\[
|Y|\le\sum_{k\in\mathcal G_n}\left|P_{M,B}(q_k)-\phi(q_k)\right|\le d\,\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}.
\]
% lean: selectedGenuineLightBias_abs
Now insert the calibration bounds \(2B\le16384L/n\) and \(M\ge\alpha_0L/2\), and then \(\log n\le L\):
\[
\frac{2B}{\epsilon M^2}
\le\frac{16384\,L/n}{\epsilon\left(\alpha_0L/2\right)^2}
=\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2\,nL}
\le\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2\,n\log n}.
\]
Squaring \(|Y|\le 65536\,d/(\epsilon\alpha_0^2n\log n)\) gives, pointwise in the sample,
\[
Y^2\le\left(\frac{65536}{\epsilon\alpha_0^2}\right)^2\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\le K_b\,\mathsf r_{n,d},
\]
and taking expectations of a pointwise bound by a constant preserves it:
\(E_{P^{\otimes n}}[Y^2]\le K_b\,\mathsf r_{n,d}\).
% lean: selectedGenuineLightBias_sq_rate

\emph{Bound for \(Z\).}  Fix \(k\) with \(p_k>B/4\).  Such a category is selected as light only when its pilot count on \(I_0\) falls below \(\lambda_0L\), an event of probability at most \(\exp\{-\lfloor n/2\rfloor p_k/8\}\) by the Bernoulli lower-tail estimate.
% lean: falseLight_selection_probability
The selection indicator is a function of \(I_0\) and the cell error a function of \(I_1\), so they factor, and the unselected second moment is controlled by the coefficient envelope of \(P_{M,B}\) at a category of mass \(p_k\):
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}\left\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
=\Pr\left(k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\right)\,
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\left\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
\le e^{-\lfloor n/2\rfloor p_k/8}\cdot8(e+1)B^2\left\{6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}B\right)^M\right\}^2 .
\]
% lean: falseLight_selection_probability, falseLight_selected_error_sq
The pilot tail now absorbs the coefficient growth, uniformly over the whole range \(p_k>B/4\).  Write \(z=p_k/B\), so that \(z>1/4\) and hence \(1+z\le5z\) and \(6(1+z)\le30z\).  Since \(\log x\le x-1\) for \(x>0\),
\[
\log\left\{6(1+z)\right\}\le6(1+z)-1\le30z ,
\]
and this logarithm is nonnegative because \(6(1+z)\ge1\); combining with \(M\le L\) gives the growth exponent bound
\[
2M\log\left\{6\left(1+\frac{p_k}B\right)\right\}\le60Lz .
\]
For the tail exponent, \(p_k=Bz\) with \(B=4096L/m_1\), and \(m_1=n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor\le2\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), so
\[
\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor\,p_k}8
=512Lz\,\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{m_1}
\ge256Lz .
\]
Subtracting the two displays and using \(z\ge1/4\) and \(L>0\),
\[
-\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor\,p_k}8+2M\log\left\{6\left(1+\frac{p_k}B\right)\right\}
\le-196Lz\le-49L ,
\]
and exponentiating gives the calibration inequality
\[
e^{-\lfloor n/2\rfloor p_k/8}\left\{6^M\left(1+\frac{p_k}B\right)^M\right\}^2\le e^{-49L}.
\]
% lean: falseLight_tail_times_growth
Multiplying it by \(8(e+1)B^2\ge0\) therefore yields
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}\left\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
\le 8(e+1)B^2e^{-49L}.
\]
% lean: falseLight_selected_error_sq
By Cauchy--Schwarz, \(Z^2\le|\mathcal G_n^{c}|\sum_{k\in\mathcal G_n^{c}}\mathbf1\{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n\}\{\widehat P_k-\phi(q_k)\}^2\) pointwise, and \(|\mathcal G_n^{c}|\le d\), so
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[Z^2\right]\le d^2\cdot8(e+1)B^2e^{-49L}.
\]
% lean: selectedFalseLightError_second_moment_le
Since \(L\ge1\) and \(e^{L}=en\), we have \(e^{-49L}\le e^{-L}\le1/n\); with \(B\le8192L/n\) this yields
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[Z^2\right]\le K_f\,\frac{d^2L^2}{n^3}\le K_f\,\mathsf r_{n,d},
\]
the last step because \(L^2(\log n)^2\le L^4\le6^{4M}L^6\le n\).
% lean: selectedFalseLightError_rate

\emph{Assembly.}  From \((X-Y)^2+(X-Z)^2+(Y-Z)^2\ge0\) one gets the pointwise inequality
\[
(X+Y+Z)^2\le3\left(X^2+Y^2+Z^2\right),
\]
so, taking expectations and adding the three bounds,
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[\left\{\widehat T_{\mathcal L}-\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_n}\phi(q_k)\right\}^2\right]
\le3\left(K_c+K_b+K_f\right)\mathsf r_{n,d}
=C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\},
\]
which is the asserted bound.
% lean: light_cell_polynomial
\end{proof}
