\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax}]
\Cref{obj:lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower} gives the lower bound
\[
\frac1{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.one_category_bernoulli_lower; CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.randomized_endpoint_minimax

For the upper bound, take the centered endpoint estimator of \cref{obj:synth_10},
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
2(2A_i-1)\left(Y_i-\frac12\right).
\]
It is measurable because the sample space is finite. To compare the infimum in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} with this estimator, interpret the displayed extrema there as real-valued order extrema and first check the bounded-below condition for the family of worst-case risks. Fix any measurable estimator. If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is empty, its worst-case risk is the supremum over an empty family and equals \(0\). If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is nonempty and the squared-loss integrals over the class are bounded above, any class member gives a nonnegative integral below the supremum, so the supremum is at least \(0\). If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is nonempty and those integrals are unbounded above, the same real-valued order convention evaluates the supremum at \(0\). Thus every worst-case risk in the infimum is bounded below by \(0\), and the infimum is no larger than the worst-case risk of \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\):
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}
\le
\sup_{\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}}
\int
\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)-\tau(P)\right)^2
\,d\mu_n .
\]

It remains to bound this worst-case risk. If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is empty, the displayed supremum is \(0\), and \(0\le 1/n\) because \(n>0\). Otherwise, for every member \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\), \cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} applied with \(\epsilon=1/2\) gives
\[
\int
\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)-\tau(P)\right)^2
\,d\mu_n
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\frac12\right)^2
=
\frac1n .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.near_randomization_linear_upper
Thus every term in the nonempty supremum is at most \(1/n\), so
\[
\sup_{\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}}
\int
\left(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)-\tau(P)\right)^2
\,d\mu_n
\le \frac1n .
\]
Therefore \(\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le 1/n\). Combining this upper bound with the lower bound proves
\[
\frac1{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \frac1n .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.randomized_endpoint_minimax
\end{proof}
