\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:randomized-endpoint-minimax}]
Fix \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(n>0\) and \(d>0\). The two halves of the bracket are proved separately.

\emph{Lower bound.} The hypotheses \(n>0\) and \(d>0\) fixed here are exactly those of \cref{obj:lem:one-category-bernoulli-lower}, which therefore gives
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}.
\]
% lean: one_category_bernoulli_lower

\emph{Upper bound.} For a measurable estimator \(\widehat\tau\) based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), write
\[
\mathsf W(\widehat\tau)
:=
\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}}
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\right]
\]
for its worst-case risk over the experiment class of \cref{obj:def:experiment-class} at \(\epsilon=1/2\), with \(\tau(P)\) the ATE functional of \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}; by \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}, \(\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}=\inf_{\widehat\tau}\mathsf W(\widehat\tau)\), the infimum ranging over all measurable estimators.

\emph{Step 1: the infimum may be compared with a single candidate.} For every measurable \(\widehat\tau\) and every \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\), the risk \(E_{P^{\otimes n}}[(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2]=\int(\widehat\tau-\tau(P))^2\,dP^{\otimes n}\) is the integral of a nonnegative function and hence nonnegative, so
\[
0\le \mathsf W(\widehat\tau)
\qquad\text{for every measurable }\widehat\tau,
\]
with the supremum read as \(0\) when \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is empty. The set of worst-case risks appearing in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} is therefore bounded below, so its infimum is no larger than the value at any one measurable candidate.
% lean: randomized_endpoint_minimax

\emph{Step 2: the candidate.} Take the centered estimator of \cref{obj:def:centered-estimator},
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
2(2A_i-1)\left(Y_i-\frac12\right),
\]
which is a fixed real-valued function of the sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) alone; since each \(O_i\) takes finitely many values, this function is measurable, so \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}\) is an admissible candidate. By Step 1,
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le \mathsf W(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}).
\]
% lean: randomized_endpoint_minimax

\emph{Step 3: the worst-case risk of the candidate.} If \(\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) is empty, then \(\mathsf W(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}})=0\le 1/n\). Otherwise, let \(P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal E_{n,d,1/2}\) be arbitrary, generated by the single-observation law \(P\). Both hypotheses of \cref{obj:lem:near-randomization-linear-upper} hold for the sample law \(\mu_n=P^{\otimes n}\), namely \(n>0\) and membership of \(\mu_n\) in the class generated by \(P\), so with overlap constant \(\epsilon=1/2\) it gives
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
\frac1n+4\left(\frac12-\frac12\right)^2
=
\frac1n .
\]
% lean: near_randomization_linear_upper
The right-hand side does not depend on \(P\), so taking the supremum over the class,
\[
\mathsf W(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{ctr}})\le\frac1n .
\]

Chaining Step 2 with Step 3 gives \(\mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le 1/n\), and together with the lower bound,
\[
\frac{1}{100n}\le \mathsf R_{n,d,1/2}\le\frac1n .
\]
% lean: randomized_endpoint_minimax
\end{proof}
