Regression discontinuity designs originate in Thistlethwaite and Campbell (1960) and were formalized for treatment-effect identification and estimation by Hahn, Todd, and van der Klaauw (2001). The methodological baseline for modern econometric practice is the continuity-based RD literature summarized by Imbens and Lemieux (2008), Lee and Lemieux (2010), Cattaneo and Titiunik (2022), and Cattaneo, Idrobo, and Titiunik (2020, 2023). Within that literature, local polynomial smoothing, bandwidth selection, robust bias correction, honest inference, and minimax linear methods are developed by Fan and Gijbels (1996), Imbens and Kalyanaraman (2012), Calonico, Cattaneo, and Titiunik (2014), Calonico, Cattaneo, and Farrell (2020), Armstrong and Kolesar (2020), Kolesar and Rothe (2018), and Imbens and Wager (2019). The present paper uses this nonparametric and minimax vocabulary to study uniform expected risk for boundary-indexed distance-based rules.

Boundary discontinuity designs extend the scalar-cutoff RD setup to bivariate scores and treatment assignment boundaries. Geographic RD work by Keele and Titiunik (2015), Keele, Titiunik, and Zubizarreta (2015), and Keele and Titiunik (2016) established the empirical and design-based interpretation of boundaries as quasi-experimental assignment rules. Multi-score and multivariate RD methods developed by Papay, Willett, and Murnane (2011), Reardon and Robinson (2012), Wong, Steiner, and Cook (2013), Choi and Lee (2018), Diaz and Zubizarreta (2023), Sawada et al. (2024), and Merlano (2025) broaden the treatment-boundary setting through alternative estimands, localization strategies, matching/weighting formulations, minimum-distance estimation, and multivariate local-polynomial approaches. Recent work by Kendall, Beck, and Antonelli (2026) adds robust inference tools for geographic RD settings, including point-process outcomes.

The direct comparator is Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu (2026), which studies distance-based local polynomial methods for boundary discontinuity designs and supplies identification, bias, stochastic-process, inference, and minimax lower-bound ingredients for the boundary average treatment-effect curve. The first result in the present paper sharpens the support-boundary lower-bound side on the exact full \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) law class: for integer \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\), arbitrary law-independent point-indexed Borel sections already incur the \((\log n/n)^{1/4}\) outer-expected uniform-risk lower bound, and the literal CTY common-map architecture inherits the converse by inclusion.

The second result sits on the distinct exact displayed \(L\)-uniformized Euclidean-distance, uniform-kernel CTY Assumptions 1--2 plus Theorem-2-envelope class \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\). Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu (2026) identify the causal boundary curve and develop sequence-scoped bias and probability-order stochastic control for distance-based local polynomials; the present paper assembles a common law class, risk, and decision triple in which an explicit winsorized, Gram-stabilized signed-distance local-polynomial estimator attains expected risk of order \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) for \(L\geq4\), while the fixed-known-interior-boundary lower experiment gives the matched outer-expected minimax frontier for \(L\geq L_0(p)\).

The proof technology also draws on canonical nonparametric and empirical-process sources. Stone (1982), Tsybakov (2009), Fan and Gijbels (1996), and Ruppert and Wand (1994) provide the surrounding minimax and local-polynomial context; Mammen and Tsybakov (1995) and Cuevas and Rodriguez-Casal (2004) represent the related support and boundary-estimation strand; Vapnik and Chervonenkis (1971), Pollard (1984), and van der Vaart and Wellner (1996) supply the VC and outer-expectation empirical-process language underlying the uniform maximal and measurability arguments.
