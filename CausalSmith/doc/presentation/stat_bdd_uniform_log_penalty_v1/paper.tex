\begin{abstract}
This paper establishes two new unconditional logarithmic minimax lower bounds for distance-compressed boundary regression. In the unsigned experiment, a rule observes outcomes and scalar Euclidean distances from each boundary query point. Over the compact nonparametric law class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class}, with \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\), both the CTY common-map risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk} and the larger point-indexed outer risk in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} have minimax expected boundary sup-loss bounded below on the scale
\[
a_n=\left({\log n\over n}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
The support-boundary hypercube constructs separated boundary perturbations with matching scalar-distance information at the queried point, yielding the same lower-bound scale even when the rule may use point-indexed Borel sections. Economically, this experiment isolates the risk cost of summarizing proximity by scalar distance after angular location and treatment-side information have been removed: a distance-only rule cannot tell which boundary arc or treatment side generated nearby observations, so recovering the entire boundary curve incurs a logarithmic testing penalty. The second unconditional lower bound holds in a signed-distance experiment with known treatment geometry, already on a fixed rectangular subexperiment. Conditional on the three CTY-style analytic inputs collected in \cref{obj:synth_139}, the stabilized local-polynomial estimator and that lower bound characterize the signed-distance expected outer-risk rate on the same scale for every fixed polynomial order \(p\), moment exponent \(\nu\ge2\), and envelope \(L\ge L_0(p)\). Thus the unsigned contribution is lower-bound sharpness, while the signed two-sided frontier is explicitly conditional on \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Regression discontinuity designs use discontinuities in treatment assignment to identify local causal effects near assignment thresholds. The classical scalar-cutoff design begins with the threshold assignment framework of \citet{Thistlethwaite1960} and the continuity-based identification analysis of \citet{Hahn2001}; modern econometric treatments develop local comparisons, bandwidth selection, bias correction, and inference around that threshold \citep{Imbens2008,Lee2010,Imbens2012,Calonico2014,Calonico2020,Armstrong2020,Kolesar2018,Imbens2019}. Boundary and geographic RD designs extend the same logic to bivariate or multivariate scores, where treatment changes across a curve or interface rather than at a scalar cutoff \citep{Keele2015a,Keele2015b,Keele2016,Papay2011,Reardon2012,Wong2013,Choi2018,Diaz2023,Sawada2024,Merlano2025,Kendall2026}.

Distance-to-boundary reductions are central in this setting. They turn a two-dimensional local problem into a one-dimensional running-coordinate problem and are used both as empirical summaries of proximity to the boundary and as coordinates for local-polynomial fitting. \citet{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance} develop distance-based identification and estimation tools for boundary discontinuity designs, including local-polynomial methods for boundary treatment-effect curves. This paper studies the minimax expected-risk consequences of two precise distance experiments: an unsigned experiment in which angular and side information is compressed away, and a known-geometry signed experiment in which the rule retains the treatment side induced by the boundary.

The first experiment is an unsigned distance-compressed boundary regression problem. The law class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class} consists of compact bivariate random-design regression laws with bounded continuous design density, Hölder regression smoothness of order \(q\), and uniformly nondegenerate conditional variance. At a boundary query point \(x\), the data available to the rule are
\[
V_{n,P}(x)=\bigl((Y_i,\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2):1\leq i\leq n\bigr),
\]
as defined in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data}. The target is the boundary regression function \(x\mapsto\mu_P(x)\), and the loss is expected supremum error over \(\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\).

For this unsigned experiment, \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse} establishes that, for every integer \(q\ge1\) and every \(L\ge4\), the common-map risk \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk} is bounded below on the scale \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\). \Cref{obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse} gives the same lower scale for the larger point-indexed class in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class}, evaluated with the outer-expectation risk in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}. Thus the logarithmic lower bound is tied to the unsigned distance information set itself: even law-independent Borel sections chosen separately at each boundary point face the same order of uniform expected loss over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\).

The proof of the unsigned converse combines a direct-product testing inequality with a support-boundary packing. \Cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product} converts many small coordinatewise overlaps into a lower bound on the probability of at least one decoding error. \Cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing} builds many separated boundary points on a square support and assigns an independent binary regression perturbation to each local cell. The perturbations are smooth enough to remain in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), their boundary values differ by order \(a_n\), and the unsigned distance laws at each coordinate have Kullback--Leibler divergence of logarithmic size. The support-boundary packing yields separated boundary signals of order \(a_n\) and logarithmic compressed-distance KL control.

The second experiment is a known-geometry signed-distance boundary RD problem. The law class \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} is the displayed Euclidean-distance, uniform-kernel specialization of the CTY Assumptions 1--2 boundary design, with rectangular support, bounded continuous density, smooth potential-outcome regressions, continuous variances, a \(2+\nu\) moment envelope, a rectifiable interior interface, two-sided local mass, and a population Gram lower bound. The rule observes signed-distance data \(U^{\pm}_{n,P}(x)\) from \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data} and may depend on the known geometry \(G_P\) from \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry}. The target is the interface treatment-effect curve \(\tau_P(x)=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)\) in \cref{obj:synth_46}.

For the signed-distance experiment, \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders} proves a lower bound on the signed-distance minimax risk \(R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. For every fixed order \(p\), there is an envelope threshold \(L_0(p)\ge48\) such that, for every \(\nu\ge2\) and \(L\ge L_0(p)\), the risk is bounded below on the \(a_n\) scale. The same lower bound is already present in the fixed rectangular subexperiment in \cref{obj:synth_126}. The hard family in \cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders} places independent local perturbations along the bottom edge of a known treated rectangle and keeps all vertices inside the same known-geometry design.

The signed-distance upper bound uses an explicit local-polynomial rule. \Cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} defines a degree-\(p\) estimator that winsorizes outcomes at \(B(h)=h^{-1/3}\), inverts the empirical Gram matrix only on a uniform conditioning event, and clips the resulting treatment-effect estimate to \([-2L,2L]\). Under the distance-identification, first-order bias, and expected maximal inputs collected in \cref{obj:synth_139}, \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper} shows that this estimator with bandwidth \(h_n=a_n\) has expected outer risk bounded by a constant multiple of \(a_n\) uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\).

Under \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\), the signed-distance upper bound combines with the lower bound to give the conditional frontier in \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier}. For every fixed \(p\), \(\nu\ge2\), and \(L\ge L_0(p)\), the signed-distance minimax risk satisfies
\[
0<c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)\over a_n}
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)\over a_n}
\le C<\infty.
\]
The same theorem records that the winsorized, Gram-stabilized signed-distance local-polynomial estimator attains the upper side of this bound under the same three analytic inputs. A short reproducibility note records the checked scope for the anchored mathematical statements in \cref{sec:verification-note}.

The comparison between the two experiments is organized by law class, information set, and risk. In the unsigned problem, scalar Euclidean distances from each query point create a boundary-indexed compression in which many angularly separated alternatives remain hard to distinguish, producing unconditional lower bounds. In the signed problem, known boundary geometry and treatment side supply the one-dimensional coordinate used by local-polynomial RD methods; its fixed-geometry lower bound is unconditional, while its two-sided rate characterization uses the displayed analytic inputs. The common normalization \(a_n\) therefore records a shared logarithmic lower scale, with a conditional upper side for the signed experiment.

The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows. \Cref{sec:unsigned-setup} defines the compact nonparametric unsigned distance experiment, its decision classes, and its minimax risks. \Cref{sec:signed-setup} defines the known-geometry signed-distance RD experiment, the A1/A2 law class, the treatment-effect target, and the stabilized local-polynomial estimator. \Cref{sec:main-results} states the unsigned lower bounds, the signed-distance lower bound, the conditional upper bound, and the matched signed-distance frontier. \Cref{sec:discussion} discusses the interpretation for boundary RD designs. \Cref{sec:app-direct-product,sec:app-signed-upper,sec:app-signed-hypercube,sec:verification-note,sec:deferred-proofs} contain the direct-product and packing arguments, the signed-distance empirical-process ingredients, the fixed-geometry signed hypercube, the reproducibility note, and deferred proofs.

\section{Related Literature}
\label{sec:related-literature}

Regression discontinuity designs originate in the threshold assignment framework of \citet{Thistlethwaite1960} and the identification analysis of \citet{Hahn2001}, with modern econometric treatments emphasizing local comparisons, bandwidth choice, and inferential refinements \citep{Imbens2008,Lee2010,Imbens2012}. Geographic and boundary designs extend this logic to assignment regions in space, where the score is vector-valued and the estimand is indexed by an interface rather than a scalar cutoff \citep{Keele2015a,Keele2015b,Keele2016,Papay2011,Reardon2012,Wong2013,Choi2018,Diaz2023,Sawada2024,Merlano2025}. Within that literature, \citet{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance} develop distance-based identification and estimation tools for boundary regression designs. Their analysis establishes identification from distance-to-boundary reductions, sequence-scoped bias expansions, and probability-order stochastic control for local-polynomial procedures under their maintained design conditions.

Theorem~6 of \citet{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance} studies the unsigned minimax risk
\[
\inf_{T_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
\left|T_n(U_n(x))-\mu_P(x)\right|\right],
\qquad
U_n(x)=((Y_i,\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2):i\le n),
\]
where one law-independent measurable map \(T_n\) is used at every boundary point. Their theorem proves a positive lower bound after multiplication by \(n^{1/4}\). The printed conjecture immediately following that theorem replaces \(n^{1/4}\) by \((n/\log n)^{1/4}\), matching the scale of their uniform upper bound. The unsigned common-map result here proves that conjectured strengthening without changing the law class, transformed data, target, decision class, or expected supremum loss. The point-indexed result changes only the decision rule: a law-independent Borel section may now be selected separately for every query point, while the transformed data and regression target remain the same; outer expectation supplies the measurable envelope for the boundary supremum. It proves the same logarithmic lower scale for this larger class.

The signed result concerns a different econometric target and information set. Motivated by CTY's signed-distance identification and local-polynomial analysis, it studies the boundary treatment-effect curve when the assignment geometry and treatment side are available to the rule. The paper proves a new minimax lower bound already on one fixed known rectangular geometry. Its expected-risk upper bound is stated under the displayed identification, bias, and expected maximal hypotheses. \Cref{tab:cty-comparison} records these three deliverables in parallel.

This paper studies the uniform expected-risk consequences of those distance reductions. In the unsigned experiment, the relevant object is the compact bivariate nonparametric regression class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class}, where a rule observes outcomes and unsigned Euclidean distances as in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data}. The decision problem uses the common-map class \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class} and its completed-expectation risk \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk}. The resulting converse is a same-class statement: over the law class and information set just described, the minimax expected boundary sup-loss has the logarithmic scale \(a_n\) introduced in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates}. The point-indexed extension uses the sectionwise-Borel outer-risk setup in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class,obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}, thereby characterizing the same compression barrier for rules that may choose a law-independent Borel section separately at each boundary point.

This focus differs from the usual role of distance in geographic RD applications. Empirical distance reductions often serve as low-dimensional summaries of proximity to a boundary or as practical coordinates for local fitting \citep{Keele2015a,Keele2015b,Keele2016,Kendall2026}. Here the distance reduction is part of the statistical experiment: the rule receives the outcome and the scalar unsigned distance from the query point, and the risk is the expected supremum over boundary points. The lower bound therefore speaks to information loss under the compressed observation scheme, rather than to specification choice in a geographic RD regression. Related diagnostic and design tools, including manipulation tests and geographic balance analyses \citep{McCrary2008,Cattaneo2022,Cattaneo2020,Cattaneo2023,Cattaneo2025}, address complementary features of RD credibility; the present comparison is organized around the target, the available data transformation, and the uniform loss criterion.

The signed-distance part connects to local-polynomial boundary estimation. Local-polynomial methods provide the standard approximation architecture for nonparametric regression and RD estimation \citep{Fan1996,Ruppert1994,Calonico2014,Calonico2020,Imbens2019}. Robust bias correction and optimal inference results refine confidence interval construction and coverage for scalar or boundary-local targets \citep{Calonico2014,Calonico2020,Armstrong2020,Kolesar2018}. The known-geometry signed-distance experiment fixes the CTY Assumptions 1--2 potential-outcome boundary design through \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, observes signed-distance data in \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data}, and evaluates treatment-effect rules in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-decision-class,obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. Assuming the CTY distance-identification, uniform first-order bias, and expected maximal inputs collected as \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) in \cref{obj:synth_139}, the paper combines the signed-distance lower bound with the winsorized, Gram-stabilized local-polynomial upper bound to obtain the two-sided expected-risk rate.

The minimax structure draws on classical nonparametric lower-bound and empirical-process ideas. Packing and modulus arguments underlie the rate calculations in nonparametric estimation \citep{Stone1982,Tsybakov2009}, while boundary and set-indexed empirical-process tools support the stochastic controls used in local smoothing problems \citep{Mammen1995,Cuevas2004,Vapnik1971,Pollard1984,VanderVaart1996}. The lower-bound construction in this paper adapts those ideas to boundary-indexed distance compression: many separated boundary locations carry independent binary perturbations, while scalar distance observations constrain the coordinatewise information available to any admissible rule. The empirical-process component enters the signed-distance upper result through the stated analytic inputs and the bounded-envelope maximal inequality used for the winsorized score. Together, these ingredients place the paper between geographic RD methodology and minimax nonparametric theory: the estimand is a boundary treatment-effect or boundary regression curve, the information set is a distance-compressed experiment, and the criterion is uniform expected loss over the displayed law classes.





\section{Setup: Distance-Compressed Boundary Regression}
\label{sec:unsigned-setup}

This section fixes the statistical experiment used for the unsigned lower bounds. The object of interest is a boundary-indexed regression function under a compact bivariate random-design law, and the information available at a boundary point is the outcome together with the scalar Euclidean distance from that point. The normalization used throughout the converse arguments is introduced first.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_25}[Law operator $\mathcal L$]
For a random element \(Z\) taking values in a measurable space \((E,\mathcal E)\) under a probability law \(P\), write \(\mathcal L_P(Z)=P\circ Z^{-1}\) for the distribution of \(Z\); when \(P\) is clear, write \(\mathcal L(Z)\). For an event \(A\) with \(P(A)>0\), write \(\mathcal L_P(Z\mid A)\) for the conditional distribution satisfying \(\mathcal L_P(Z\mid A)(B)=P(Z\in B,A)/P(A)\) for every \(B\in\mathcal E\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_28}[Smallest quadratic-form eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$]
For a real symmetric matrix $\Psi$, we write $\lambda_{\min}(\Psi)=\inf_{\|v\|_2=1} v^\top\Psi v$, equivalently $\lambda_{\min}(\Psi)\ge c$ means $c\|v\|_2^2\le v^\top\Psi v$ for every vector $v$.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_43}[Closed Euclidean ball $B_2(x,r)$]
For $x\in\mathbb R^2$ and $r\geq0$, we write $B_2(x,r)$ for the closed Euclidean ball $\{z\in\mathbb R^2:\lVert z-x\rVert_2\leq r\}$.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_36}[Euclidean \(q\)-Hölder ball \(\mathcal H^q(\mathcal X_P,L)\)]
We say \(g\in\mathcal H^q(\mathcal X_P,L)\) when \(g:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) is \(C^{q-1}\) on \(\mathcal X_P\), satisfies \(\lVert D^j g(x)\rVert\leq L\) for every \(0\leq j\leq q-1\) and every \(x\in\mathcal X_P\), and its \((q-1)\)st Fréchet derivative is \(L\)-Lipschitz on \(\mathcal X_P\): \(\lVert D^{q-1}g(x)-D^{q-1}g(y)\rVert\leq L\lVert x-y\rVert_2\) for all \(x,y\in\mathcal X_P\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_72}[Faithful random-design law space \(\mathcal P\)]
We say \(P\in\mathcal P\) when \(P\) is a probability law of \((Y,X)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) with square-integrable outcome, whose carried objects \(\mathcal X_P\), \(f_P\), \(\mu_P\), and \(\sigma_P^2\) are tied to the law as follows: \(\mathcal X_P\) is the topological support of the \(X\)-marginal, the \(X\)-marginal has Lebesgue density \(f_P\mathbf 1_{\mathcal X_P}\), \(\mu_P\) is an \(X\)-marginal-almost-everywhere version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=x]\), and \(\sigma_P^2\) is an \(X\)-marginal-almost-everywhere version of \(\operatorname{Var}_P(Y\mid X=x)\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_138}[Bivariate design support \(\mathcal X\)]
For a law \(P\) of \((Y,X)\) on \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\), we say \(\mathcal X\) is the bivariate design support when \(\mathcal X=\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal L_P(X))\), the topological support of the \(X\)-marginal under \(P\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_29}[Total variation distance $\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}$]
For the two probability laws $Q_{j,0}$ and $Q_{j,1}$ on the common measurable sample space of $S_j$, define their total variation distance by \[\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup_{A}\left|Q_{j,0}(A)-Q_{j,1}(A)\right|,\] where the supremum ranges over all measurable sets $A$ in that sample space.
\end{definitionv}

These preliminary conventions give the basic probabilistic and geometric vocabulary. In particular, the law-space convention ties the support, density, regression, and conditional variance to the same underlying law, while the Hölder and ball notation provide the local smoothness and Euclidean localization language used in the packing arguments. Total variation enters later through the direct-product comparison of coordinatewise compressed experiments.

\begin{definitionv}{def:frontier-rates}[Frontier rate $a_n$]
The frontier normalization is
\[
a_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The scale in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates} is the common normalization for the unsigned boundary lower bounds. Its logarithmic factor reflects the uniform boundary supremum loss rather than a pointwise loss criterion.

The nonparametric class now fixes the bivariate random-design model. It combines compact support, a bounded continuous design density, Hölder regression smoothness, and uniformly nondegenerate conditional variance.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-nonparametric-class}[Nonparametric law class $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)$]
For \(q\in\mathbb N\) and \(L\in\mathbb R\), the class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) consists of all random-design regression laws \(P\) satisfying the following conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Parameter regime.)} \(1\leq q\) and \(4\leq L\).
\item \textbf{(Sampling.)} \(P\) is a probability law for one observation \((Y,X)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\), \(Y\) is square-integrable under \(P\), and for every \(n\), \((Y_i,X_i)_{i=1}^n\) are sampled independently and identically from \(P\).
\item \textbf{(Design.)} The \(X\)-marginal has Lebesgue density \(f_P\mathbf 1_{\mathcal X_P}\), where \(\mathcal X_P\) is the exact topological support of the \(X\)-marginal. Write \(f_P\) for this density. It is continuous on \(\mathcal X_P\), \(\mathcal X_P\) is compact and satisfies \(\mathcal X_P\subseteq[-L,L]^2\), and
\[
L^{-1}\leq f_P(x)\leq L
\]
for every \(x\in\mathcal X_P\). The boundary \(\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\) admits a Lipschitz parametrization by \([0,1]\).
\item \textbf{(Regression.)} The function \(\mu_P\) is an \(X\)-marginal-almost-everywhere version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=x]\) and belongs to \(\mathcal H^q(\mathcal X_P,L)\).
\item \textbf{(Variance.)} The function \(\sigma_P^2\) is an \(X\)-marginal-almost-everywhere version of \(\operatorname{Var}_P(Y\mid X=x)\), is continuous on \(\mathcal X_P\), and satisfies
\[
L^{-1}\leq \sigma_P^2(x)\leq L
\]
for every \(x\in\mathcal X_P\).
\end{itemize}
Equivalently,
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)
=
\{P:\text{\(P\) satisfies the conditions above}\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The class in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class} is a compact-support version of a random-design nonparametric regression model. The density bounds keep local sample sizes comparable across admissible laws, the Lipschitz boundary condition gives a one-dimensional interface over which a supremum can be evaluated, and the Hölder condition calibrates how quickly regression values may change near separated boundary locations. These conditions are standard in nonparametric minimax analysis, with compactness and smoothness playing the same organizational role as in classical regression lower bounds \citep{Stone1982,Tsybakov2009}.

At a boundary query point, the unsigned experiment compresses each covariate to its distance from that point. The rule retains the outcome and the scalar distance, so all angular information around the query point is absorbed into this one-dimensional summary.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-distance-data}[Distance data $V_{n,P}(x)$]
For \(x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\), the distance data are
\[
V_{n,P}(x)
=
\bigl((Y_i,\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2):1\leq i\leq n\bigr).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The observation in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data} is indexed by the boundary point because the same sample is re-expressed relative to each query location. This formulation matches the distance-to-boundary reductions studied by \citet{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance}, while the present risk criterion evaluates the resulting boundary-indexed regression rule uniformly over \(\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\).

The first decision class uses one law-independent map for all query points. This common-map restriction captures procedures that process the distance data by a single measurable rule once the sample size is fixed.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-distance-decision-class}[Common-map class $\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}$]
For a represented rule, write \(T_n\) for its selected law-independent measurable map. Define the sample-size-indexed class by
\[
\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
=
\left\{
\widehat\mu_n:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{there exists a measurable map }T_n:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\to\mathbb R
\text{ selected independently of }P,\\
\widehat\mu_n(x)=T_n(V_{n,P}(x))
\text{ for every }x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-distance-risk}[Common-map risk $R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}$]
The common-map minimax risk is
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
=
\inf_{\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)}
\mathbb E_P
\left[
\sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
|\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_P(x)|
\right].
\]
\end{definitionv}

Together, \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class,obj:def:cty-distance-risk} define the completed-expectation minimax problem for common-map rules. The expectation is the ordinary expectation under the product law generated by \(P\), and the loss is the supremum error along the support boundary. The infimum therefore ranges over estimators that share the same measurable distance-data transformation across all admissible laws and all boundary points.

For comparison, the point-indexed decision class lets the measurable section depend on the query point while remaining law independent. This keeps the unsigned distance information set fixed and enlarges the admissible class by allowing a separate Borel rule at each \(x\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class}[Point-indexed class $\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}$]
The point-indexed decision class is
\[
\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}
=
\left\{
\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{PI}}:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{there exists a family }\{T_{n,x}:x\in\mathbb R^2\}
\text{ selected independently of }P,\\
T_{n,x}:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\to\mathbb R
\text{ is Borel measurable for every fixed }x,\\
\widehat\mu_{n,P}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)=T_{n,x}(V_{n,P}(x))
\text{ for every }P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\\
\text{and every }x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
This class consists exactly of such law-independent fixed-\(x\) Borel sections.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:point-indexed-distance-risk}[Point-indexed risk \(R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\)]
For an extended nonnegative function \(Z\) on the sample space, define the outer expectation by
\[
\mathbb E_P^*[Z]
=
\inf\left\{
\mathbb E_P[W]:
W\text{ is extended nonnegative and measurable, and }W\geq Z\text{ pointwise}
\right\}.
\]
The point-indexed distance risk is
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}
=
\inf_{\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
\left|\widehat\mu_{n,P}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)-\mu_P(x)\right|
\right].
\]
\end{definitionv}

The outer expectation in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} follows the standard empirical-process convention for suprema that may require measurable majorants \citep{VanderVaart1996}. With this convention, \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class,obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} define a sectionwise Borel problem that preserves the same distance-compressed observations as \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data}. The main lower bounds compare these two unsigned risks after the common notation and signed-distance setup have been fixed.




\section{Setup: Known-Geometry Signed-Distance Designs}
\label{sec:signed-setup}

The second experiment keeps the boundary geometry available to the rule and records which side of the interface each nearby observation occupies. This is the setting in which local-polynomial methods use distance as a one-dimensional coordinate while preserving the sign induced by treatment assignment, as in boundary regression designs developed by \citet{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance}. We first fix the assignment, interface, basis, and coordinate conventions that underlie the signed-distance reduction.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_4}[Assignment regions $\mathcal A_{t,P}$]
We say $\mathcal A_{t,P}$ is the arm-$t$ assignment region of $P$, for $t\in\{0,1\}$, when $\mathcal A_{0,P}$ and $\mathcal A_{1,P}$ are Borel subsets of $\mathcal X_P$ satisfying $\mathcal A_{0,P}\cup\mathcal A_{1,P}=\mathcal X_P$ and $\mathcal A_{0,P}\cap\mathcal A_{1,P}=\varnothing$, with deterministic treatment assignment $T=\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_{1,P}\}$.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_35}[Known treatment interface \(\mathcal B\)]
We say \(\mathcal B\) is the known treatment interface for the fixed hypercube geometry when it is the boundary of the treated rectangle,\[\mathcal B=\partial([-1,1]\times[0,2]).\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_71}[Degree-$p$ Polynomial Basis $r_p$]
For an integer $p\geq0$ and $u\in\mathbb R$, define $r_p(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^p)^\top\in\mathbb R^{p+1}$.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_24}[Score-coordinate projection $\operatorname{score}$]
We say $\operatorname{score}$ is the score-coordinate projection when, for every potential-outcome observation $w=(y_0,y_1,x)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2$, $\operatorname{score}(w)=x$.
\end{definitionv}

The partition in \cref{obj:synth_4} makes treatment deterministic given location, so the side of the boundary is a feature of the design rather than an additional random assignment variable. The fixed interface in \cref{obj:synth_35} is the rectangular geometry used in the lower-bound construction, while \cref{obj:synth_71} supplies the signed-distance polynomial basis used by the local fits. The projection in \cref{obj:synth_24} records that the score coordinate is the Euclidean covariate.

The known-geometry object packages the support, assignment regions, interface, Euclidean metric, and smoothing weight. The canonical smoothing weight is the one-dimensional uniform kernel \(K_\square\), which selects observations whose signed distance lies within the bandwidth window.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-uniform-kernel}[Uniform kernel \(K_\square\)]
The fixed uniform kernel is
\[
K_\square(u)=\mathbf 1\{|u|\leq1\},
\qquad u\in\mathbb R.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-known-geometry}[Known geometry \(G_P\)]
Define \(\mathcal A\) to be the class of admissible known geometries \(G=(\mathcal X,\mathcal A_0,\mathcal A_1,\mathcal B,d,K_\square)\), where \(\mathcal A_0,\mathcal A_1\) are a Borel partition of \(\mathcal X\),
\[
\mathcal B=\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal A_0)\cap\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal A_1)\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal X),
\qquad d(x,z)=\lVert x-z\rVert_2.
\]
For a member law \(P\), write \(\mathcal A_{0,P},\mathcal A_{1,P}\) for its assignment regions and write \(\mathcal B_P=\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal A_{0,P})\cap\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal A_{1,P})\). Define its known design object by
\[
G_P=(\mathcal X_P,\mathcal A_{0,P},\mathcal A_{1,P},\mathcal B_P,d_P,K_\square)\in\mathcal A.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data}[Signed-distance data \(U^{\pm}_{n,P}(x)\)]
For \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), define the signed distance
\[
D^{\pm}_{P,x}
=
\{\mathbf 1(X\in\mathcal A_{1,P})-\mathbf 1(X\in\mathcal A_{0,P})\}
\lVert X-x\rVert_2
\]
and the signed-distance sample
\[
U^{\pm}_{n,P}(x)=((Y_i,D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}):1\leq i\leq n).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The known design object \(G_P\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry} gives the rule the relevant boundary and side labels. For an interface point \(x\), the signed distance \(D^{\pm}_{P,x}\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data} retains the Euclidean distance magnitude and assigns its sign according to the treatment region. Thus the signed-distance sample \(U^{\pm}_{n,P}(x)\) provides the one-dimensional running coordinate used by local-polynomial RD estimators \citep{Fan1996,Ruppert1994,Calonico2014,Calonico2020,Imbens2019}.

The law class below is the displayed Euclidean, uniform-kernel specialization of the CTY Assumptions 1--2 boundary design. Its clauses have four statistical roles. The support, assignment regions, interface, metric, and signed coordinate define the experiment and information available to a rule. The density, regression smoothness, conditional variance, and moment bounds provide uniform design and outcome envelopes. The population-Gram, arm-mass, distance-slice, and VC conditions keep local-polynomial fitting uniformly conditioned along the interface. The separate inputs \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\), introduced in \cref{obj:synth_139}, supply identification, first-order approximation, and expected uniform stochastic control for the conditional upper bound.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-a1-a2-class}[A1/A2 law class \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\)]
For integers \(p\geq0\), real numbers \(\nu\geq2\), and \(L\geq4\), let \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) be the set of laws \(P\) satisfying the following conditions.

Write \(Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\) for the sampled potential outcomes of observation \(i\), and write \(\mathcal H^1(\mathcal B_P)\) for the one-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the common interface.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sampling and consistency.)} For every \(n\), the vectors \((Y_i(0),Y_i(1),X_i)\), \(1\leq i\leq n\), are independently and identically distributed,
\[
T=\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_{1,P}\},
\qquad
Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0).
\]

\item \textbf{(Rectangular support and density.)} The support has the form
\[
\mathcal X_P=[\underline x_P,\overline x_P]^2\subseteq[-L,L]^2.
\]
The variable \(X\) has a Lebesgue density \(f_P\), continuous on \(\mathcal X_P\), such that
\[
L^{-1}\leq f_P(x)\leq L,
\qquad x\in\mathcal X_P.
\]

\item \textbf{(Smooth potential-outcome regressions.)} For each \(t\in\{0,1\}\), the regression
\[
\mu_{t,P}(x)=\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mid X=x]
\]
has a \(C^{p+1}\) extension to an open neighborhood of \(\mathcal X_P\), and
\[
\max_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq p}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X_P}|\partial^\alpha\mu_{t,P}(x)|
+
\max_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq p}\sup_{x\neq z\in\mathcal X_P}
{|\partial^\alpha\mu_{t,P}(x)-\partial^\alpha\mu_{t,P}(z)|
\over
\lVert x-z\rVert_2}
\leq L.
\]

\item \textbf{(Conditional variances.)} For each \(t\in\{0,1\}\), the conditional variance
\[
\sigma^2_{t,P}(x)=\operatorname{Var}_P(Y(t)\mid X=x)
\]
is continuous and satisfies
\[
L^{-1}\leq\sigma^2_{t,P}(x)\leq L,
\qquad x\in\mathcal X_P.
\]

\item \textbf{(Moment envelope.)} For each \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and every \(x\in\mathcal X_P\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[|Y(t)|^{2+\nu}\mid X=x]\leq L.
\]

\item \textbf{(Assignment geometry.)} The sets \(\mathcal A_{0,P}\) and \(\mathcal A_{1,P}\) form a Borel partition of \(\mathcal X_P\), and their known common interface \(\mathcal B_P\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal X_P)\) is a compact rectifiable curve satisfying
\[
L^{-1}\leq\mathcal H^1(\mathcal B_P)\leq L.
\]

\item \textbf{(Metric, kernel, and VC bound.)} The known metric and kernel are
\[
d_P(x,z)=\lVert x-z\rVert_2,
\qquad
K_\square.
\]
The collection
\[
\{\{z\in\mathcal X_P:\lVert z-x\rVert_2\leq r\}:x\in\mathcal X_P,\ r\geq0\}
\]
is a VC class with VC index at most four.

\item \textbf{(Population Gram lower bound.)} Put
\[
r_p(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^p)^\top,
\qquad
I_1=[0,\infty),
\qquad
I_0=(-\infty,0),
\]
and
\[
\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)
=
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
{1\over h^2}\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}
K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)
r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)^\top
\right].
\]
For every \(0<h\leq L^{-1}\), every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and both \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\lambda_{\min}(\Psi_{t,P,x}(h))\geq L^{-1}.
\]

\item \textbf{(Small-bandwidth arm mass.)} For every \(0<h\leq L^{-1}\), every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and both \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
{1\over h^2}\int_{\mathcal A_{t,P}}
K_\square(\{(2t-1)\lVert z-x\rVert_2\}/h)\,dz
\geq L^{-1}.
\]

\item \textbf{(Distance-slice density.)} For every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), both \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and every \(0<s\leq L^{-1}\), the one-dimensional Hausdorff integral of \(f_P\) over
\[
\{z\in\mathcal A_{t,P}:\lVert z-x\rVert_2=s\}
\]
is finite and strictly positive.
\end{itemize}

This class is the displayed \(L\)-uniformized Euclidean-distance, uniform-kernel specialization of the two baseline smoothness and design conditions, augmented by the displayed envelope restrictions and quantitative small-bandwidth thresholds.
\end{definitionv}

The assumptions in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} fall into three groups. First, the support, assignment partition, rectifiable interface, signed coordinate, and distance-slice conditions define the design and the information observed by the signed-distance procedure. Second, the density, moments, smoothness, variance, arm-mass, population-Gram, and VC conditions provide uniform outcome, conditioning, and complexity envelopes for local fitting. Third, \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) in \cref{obj:synth_139} collects the identification, bias, and expected maximal inputs invoked specifically by the conditional upper bound. The unconditional signed lower bound uses the fixed-geometry subexperiment inside the first two groups; the stabilized estimator and conditional frontier use all three groups.

The potential outcomes \(Y(t)\) and deterministic treatment \(T\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} give the boundary target its causal RD interpretation. The arm-specific regressions \(\mu_{t,P}\) support degree-\(p\) signed-distance approximation, while the arm-specific variances \(\sigma^2_{t,P}\) and \(2+\nu\) moment envelope keep the outcome scale uniform. Geometrically, the class includes compact rectifiable interfaces of controlled length inside rectangular supports and requires two-sided local mass and stable signed-distance Gram matrices at every interface point. These quantitative conditions also cover the corners of the fixed treated rectangle: \cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders} verifies the Gram and arm-mass thresholds there rather than relying on a smooth-boundary shortcut.

The treatment-effect target is the jump between the two smooth potential-outcome regression surfaces at the interface. The population local-polynomial coefficient records the signed-distance normal equations that the estimator approximates.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_46}[Interface treatment-effect curve $\tau_P(x)$]
For a law $P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)$ and an interface point $x\in\mathcal B_P$, define the treatment-effect curve by $\tau_P(x)=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)$, where $\mu_{t,P}(x)=\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mid X=x]$ is the continuous conditional-mean version for arm $t\in\{0,1\}$.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_128}[Population local-polynomial coefficient $\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)$]
We say $\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)$ is the unwinsorized population degree-$p$ signed-distance local-polynomial coefficient when $\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\in\mathbb R^{p+1}$ satisfies the normal equations \[\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\,\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)=\mathbb E_P\!\left[{1\over h^2}\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)Y\right].\]
\end{definitionv}

The curve \(\tau_P\) in \cref{obj:synth_46} is evaluated uniformly over \(\mathcal B_P\). The coefficient in \cref{obj:synth_128} is a population projection onto the signed-distance polynomial basis; its intercept difference is the population analogue of the treatment-effect estimator, and its approximation error is the first-order bias component isolated later.

Rules in the signed-distance experiment may depend on the known geometry and the query point through law-independent Borel sections. The risk then uses the outer expectation already introduced for point-indexed suprema in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-decision-class}[Known-geometry rules \(\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n\)]
Let \(\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n\) be the class of rules for which there is one collection
\[
\{T_{n,G,x}:G\text{ is an admissible known geometry},\ x\in\mathbb R^2\}
\]
selected independently of the unknown member law, such that every fixed \((G,x)\) section is a Borel map from \((\mathbb R\times\mathbb R)^n\) to \(\mathbb R\) and, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\), every \(p\geq0\), and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\),
\[
\widehat\tau_{n,P}(x)
=
T_{n,G_P,x}(U^{\pm}_{n,P}(x)).
\]
The imposed regularity is fixed-\((G,x)\) Borel measurability of each section.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}[Signed-distance minimax risk \(R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\)]
Using the outer expectation \(\mathbb E_P^*\) defined in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}, for \(p\geq0\) the signed-distance minimax risk is
\[
 R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)
 =
 \inf_{\widehat\tau_n\in\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
 \mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}|\widehat\tau_{n,P}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right].
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_126}[Fixed-geometry signed-distance outer risk \(R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)\)]
We define \(R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)\) as the signed-distance outer minimax risk with the law supremum restricted to the fixed hard geometry:\[ R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)= \inf_{\widehat\tau_n\in\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n}\ \sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L):\ \mathcal X_P=[-3,3]^2,\ \mathcal A_{1,P}=[-1,1]\times[0,2],\ \mathcal A_{0,P}=\mathcal X_P\setminus\mathcal A_{1,P},\ \mathcal B_P=\partial\mathcal A_{1,P},\ d_P(x,z)=\lVert x-z\rVert_2,\ \text{and the known kernel is }K_\square}} \mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}|\widehat\tau_{n,P}(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]. \]
\end{definitionv}

The decision class \(\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-decision-class} gives procedures access to the known geometry while keeping the section family fixed before the member law is chosen. The signed-distance risk \(R_n^{12,\pm}\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk} measures the expected worst interface error for treatment-effect estimation. The fixed-geometry risk in \cref{obj:synth_126} is the same criterion restricted to the rectangular hard family, which is the family used for the signed-distance converse in \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders}.

It remains to name the estimator used for the upper bound. The construction follows the standard local-polynomial template, with three stabilizing features: outcomes are winsorized at a bandwidth-dependent level, the empirical Gram matrix is inverted only when it has a uniform quadratic-form lower bound, and the final treatment-effect estimate is clipped to the natural envelope scale.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}[Stabilized local-polynomial estimator \(\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}\)]
For \(B\in\mathbb R\), define the winsorization map by
\[
\psi_B(y)=\operatorname{sign}(y)\min\{|y|,B\}.
\]
For each \(t\in\{0,1\}\), query point \(x\), and bandwidth \(h\), define
\[
\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)
 =
 {1\over nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}\in I_t\}K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}/h)
 r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}/h)r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}/h)^\top .
\]
With \(B(h)=h^{-1/3}\), define
\[
\widehat m^B_{t,x}(h)
 =
 {1\over nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}\in I_t\}K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}/h)
 r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}/h)\psi_{B(h)}(Y_i).
\]
The stabilized coefficient is
\[
\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\widehat m^B_{t,x}(h),
 & \text{if }\forall v\in\mathbb R^{p+1},\ (2L)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^p v_j^2\leq v^\top\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)v,\\
 0,
 & \text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
With \(e_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)^\top\), the clipped signed-distance local-polynomial treatment-effect estimator is
\[
\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)
 =
 \max\!\left\{-2L,\min\!\left\{2L,
 e_1^\top(\widehat\beta^B_{1,x}(h)-\widehat\beta^B_{0,x}(h))
 \right\}\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The winsorization map \(\psi_B\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} converts the moment envelope into a bounded-score component at bandwidth \(h\). The empirical Gram matrix estimates the population Gram \(\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\), and the fallback rule keeps the coefficient map stable on samples where the empirical design is ill conditioned. The resulting estimator \(\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}\) is the concrete rule used in the signed-distance upper result.

The following three deviation objects separate approximation, design, and score variation. They are stated here because the main theorem collects analytic inputs in terms of these quantities before applying the estimator in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_39}[Uniform first-order bias ratio $\mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu',L}(h_n)$]
For $h_n>0$, define the uniform first-order bias ratio by \[ \mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu',L}(h_n)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu',L)}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}{\left|e_1^\top\{\beta^*_{1,P,x}(h_n)-\beta^*_{0,P,x}(h_n)\}-\tau_P(x)\right|\over h_n}. \]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_62}[Uniform empirical Gram deviation \(\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)\)]
For a law \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) and bandwidth \(h_n>0\), we write \(\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)\) for the extended nonnegative sample-space random variable\[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)=\sup_{t\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\max_{0\leq j,k\leq p}\left|\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h_n)_{jk}-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h_n)_{jk}\right|.\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_63}[Uniform raw-score deviation $\mathrm{ScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)$]
Define $\mathrm{ScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)$ as the sample-dependent uniform centered local-polynomial score deviation
\[
\mathrm{ScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)
=
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left\|
{1\over n h_n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}\in I_t\}K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}/h_n)r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}/h_n)Y_i
-
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
{1\over h_n^2}\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h_n)r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h_n)Y
\right]
\right\|,
\]
with the norm taken in $\mathbb R^{p+1}$.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_131}[Winsorized score \(\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h,j}\)]
We say \(\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h,j}\) is the winsorized local-polynomial residual score when, for \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\), sample size \(n\), arm \(t\in\{0,1\}\), interface point \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), bandwidth \(h>0\), winsorization level \(B>0\), and coordinate \(j\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\), it is the \(P^n\)-random variable\[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h,j}=\frac{1}{n h^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}\in I_t\}K_\square\!\left(\frac{D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}}{h}\right)r_p\!\left(\frac{D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}}{h}\right)_j\left[\psi_B(Y_i)-r_p\!\left(\frac{D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}}{h}\right)^\top\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right],\]with \(\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\) the unwinsorized population local-polynomial coefficient at \((t,x,h)\).
\end{definitionv}

The bias ratio in \cref{obj:synth_39} measures the uniform first-order approximation error of the population signed-distance fit. The Gram deviation in \cref{obj:synth_62} tracks the uniform empirical conditioning of the local design, and the raw-score deviation in \cref{obj:synth_63} records the centered stochastic part before winsorization. The winsorized score in \cref{obj:synth_131} is the bounded-envelope score component used by the maximal inequality in the upper-bound appendix. These objects place the later risk result in the usual bias, design-stability, and empirical-process decomposition for local-polynomial RD estimators \citep{Fan1996,Ruppert1994,Calonico2014,Calonico2020,VanderVaart1996}.





\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:main-results}

The results now put the two distance-compressed experiments on a common risk scale. The unsigned problem uses the nonparametric regression class and distance data introduced in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class,obj:def:cty-distance-data}; the signed problem uses the known-geometry law class, signed-distance data, and treatment-effect risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class,obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data,obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. Throughout the section, the normalizing sequence is the rate \(a_n\) in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates}.

The first result is the same-class converse for the unsigned common-map experiment. It evaluates exactly the decision class in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class} under the completed-expectation risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:cty-same-class-log-converse}[Same-Class Log Converse]
For every integer \(q\geq 1\) and every \(L\geq 4\), there exists a constant \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}=c_{\mathrm{CTY}}(q,L)>0\) such that, for the common-map minimax risk \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk} and \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4} R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}
\geq c_{\mathrm{CTY}} .
\]
\end{theoremv}

The theorem establishes that a single law-independent distance rule incurs expected boundary sup-loss at least of logarithmic order \(a_n\) over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\). The packing calculation behind the rate is easiest to read from the separated boundary construction used in the appendix. If the signal amplitude is \(\Delta\), then the construction places \(M\asymp\Delta^{-1/q}\) active locations along the boundary and uses cells with area scale \(w^2\asymp\Delta^{2/q}\). Unsigned distance compression leaves each coordinate with information of order \(n\Delta^4\), so the many-coordinate testing barrier is calibrated by
\[
n\Delta^4\asymp \log M,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\Delta\asymp \left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
The smoothness order \(q\) affects the packing constants and the geometric calibration of \(w\), while the exponent is governed by the fourth-order distance-compression information calculation. In the signed-distance local-polynomial results below, the polynomial order \(p\) likewise affects constants and regularity thresholds while the displayed rate normalization remains \(a_n\).

The next result carries the same logarithmic converse to the point-indexed unsigned class. The point-indexed class in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class} permits a separate Borel section at each query point, and the risk is evaluated with outer expectation as in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse}[Logarithmic Distance Converse]
For every \(q\in\mathbb N\) and \(L\in\mathbb R\) satisfying \(q\geq 1\) and \(L\geq 4\), there exists a constant \(c_{\mathrm{PI}}=c_{\mathrm{PI}}(q,L)>0\) such that, with \(R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) the point-indexed outer-expectation minimax risk in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} and \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4} R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}
\geq c_{\mathrm{PI}}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

Thus the lower-bound scale is a property of the unsigned distance information itself, even when the rule may choose its measurable section point by point. Outer expectation in this statement is the same measurable-majorant criterion introduced in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}, which is the standard way to state uniform risks over potentially nonseparable boundary index sets \citep{VanderVaart1996}.

The signed-distance lower bound uses the known-geometry experiment in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-decision-class,obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. It is stated for all local-polynomial orders after an envelope threshold that depends on the order.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders}[Point-Indexed Log Converse]
For every nonnegative integer \(p\), there exists a real number \(L_0=L_0(p)\) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Envelope threshold.)} \(L_0\ge 48\).
\item \textbf{(Moment exponent.)} \(\nu\ge 2\).
\item \textbf{(Uniform envelope.)} \(L\ge L_0\).
\item \textbf{(Risk definition.)} \(R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\) is the signed-distance minimax risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}.
\item \textbf{(Fixed-geometry risk.)} \(R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)\) is the infimum over the same known-geometry point-indexed decision rules of the same worst-case outer-expected interface sup-loss, with the law supremum further restricted to laws in \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) satisfying the fixed hard-geometry condition.
\end{itemize}
For every \(\nu\) and \(L\) satisfying these conditions, there exists a constant \(c=c(p,\nu,L)>0\) such that, writing \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty} a_n^{-1}R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\over a_n},
\]
and
\[
c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\over a_n},
\qquad
c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}{R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)\over a_n}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders} establishes the same logarithmic lower scale for the known-geometry signed-distance minimax risk. The second inequality records that the lower bound is already present inside the fixed rectangular geometry from \cref{obj:synth_126}. Consequently, the converse is calibrated by a concrete Euclidean boundary experiment rather than by variation over arbitrary interfaces.

The upper result for the signed-distance experiment combines the estimator in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} with three analytic inputs. These inputs are distance identification, uniform first-order bias control, and expected maximal inequalities; every signed-distance upper or matched-rate statement below is conditioned on this collection.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_139}[Analytic inputs for the signed-distance upper bound]
Define \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) as the following three signed-distance analytic inputs used by the expected-risk upper bound.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(CTY distance identification.)} For every A1/A2 law \(P\) and metric \(d\), the support is rectangular, the density is continuous and positive on the support, both potential outcomes are integrable, both arm regressions have \(C^{p+1}\) extensions, the boundary is a positive-length rectifiable curve, \(d\) is a metric uniformly equivalent to Euclidean distance on the support, and every sufficiently small positive armwise distance slice at every boundary point has finite positive density mass. Under these conditions there is one source-coherent selected conditional law of the observed outcome given signed distance, with armwise mean versions \(\theta_1,\theta_0\) whose one-sided limits at zero satisfy
\[
\tau_P(x)=\lim_{u\downarrow0}\theta_1(u)-\lim_{u\uparrow0}\theta_0(u).
\]
\item \textbf{(Uniform first-order bias.)} There is \(C_b>0\), depending only on \(p,L\), such that for every \(\nu'\ge2\) and every positive antitone deterministic sequence \(h_n\to0\) with \(nh_n^2\to\infty\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu',L}(h_n)\le C_b.
\]
\item \textbf{(Expected maximal bounds.)} There is \(C_m>0\) such that, along every positive deterministic \(h_n\to0\) satisfying
\[
{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2\over\log(h_n^{-1})}\to\infty,
\]
all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) satisfy
\[
\mathbb E_P^*[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)]\le C_m\sqrt{{\log(h_n^{-1})\over nh_n^2}}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_P^*[\mathrm{ScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)]\le C_m\left\{\sqrt{{\log(h_n^{-1})\over nh_n^2}}+{\log(h_n^{-1})\over n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}\right\}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

The three components of \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) are statistical assumptions with distinct roles. \emph{Identification} connects the one-sided signed-distance conditional-mean limits to the boundary treatment-effect curve \citep{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance}. \emph{Approximation} bounds the population local-polynomial intercept error uniformly at first order in the bandwidth \citep{Fan1996,Ruppert1994}. \emph{Stochastic control} bounds the expected uniform Gram and score deviations over arms, boundary points, and laws \citep{Vapnik1971,Pollard1984,VanderVaart1996}. The next proposition is conditional on all three displayed inputs and derives the expected-risk rate for the stabilized estimator from them.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}[Expected Outer Upper Bound]
Let \(p\geq 0\) be an integer, let \(\nu\geq 2\), and let \(L\geq 4\).  Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Distance identification.)}  For every A1/A2 law \(P\), every distance map \(d\), and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\) satisfying the CTY identification assumptions at order \(p\), there are signed-distance conditional-mean versions \(\theta_1,\theta_0:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) and one-sided limits \(\theta_1(0+)\) and \(\theta_0(0-)\) such that
\[
\tau_P(x)=\theta_1(0+)-\theta_0(0-).
\]
\item \textbf{(Uniform first-order bias.)}  There is a constant \(C_b>0\), depending on \(p\) and \(L\), such that for every \(\nu'\geq 2\) and every positive antitone deterministic bandwidth sequence \(h_n\) with \(h_n\to0\) and \(n h_n^2\to\infty\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu',L}(h_n)\leq C_b .
\]
\item \textbf{(Expected maximal bounds.)}  There is a constant \(C_m>0\) such that, for every positive deterministic bandwidth sequence \(h_n\) with \(h_n\to0\) and
\[
\frac{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}{\log(h_n^{-1})}\to\infty,
\]
the following bounds hold for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\):
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)\right]
\leq
C_m
\sqrt{\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n h_n^2}},
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{ScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)\right]
\leq
C_m\left\{
\sqrt{\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n h_n^2}}
+
\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}
\right\}.
\]
\end{itemize}
With \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) as in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates}, take the bandwidth \(h_n=a_n\) and the winsorization level \(B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\) in the winsorized, Gram-stabilized signed-distance local-polynomial estimator clipped to \([-2L,2L]\).  Then there are constants \(C>0\) and \(N\) such that, for every \(n\geq N\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]
\leq C a_n .
\]
\end{propositionv}

The proposition gives a concrete signed-distance procedure with expected outer risk bounded by a constant multiple of \(a_n\). The bandwidth choice balances the first-order signed-distance bias, of order \(h_n\), with the uniform stochastic scale \(\{\log(h_n^{-1})/(n h_n^2)\}^{1/2}\). At \(h_n=a_n\), both terms have order \(a_n\), and the additional heavy-tail term in the assumed maximal bound is controlled under the displayed moment condition. The winsorized-score lemma used in the appendix supplies the bounded-envelope score component needed for this decomposition. The full proposition additionally uses the assumed uniform Gram control and the assumed raw-score, law-uniform, heavy-tail maximal bound with exponent \(n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}\), so the displayed expected-risk conclusion is tied to the full collection \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) in \cref{obj:synth_139}.

\paragraph{Conditional signed-distance frontier.}
Under the analytic inputs in \cref{obj:synth_139}, the signed-distance lower and upper statements combine to yield the matched outer-expected rate over \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\).

\begin{theoremv}{thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier}[Matched Frontier Rate]
For every integer \(p\geq 0\), there exists a constant \(L_0\geq 48\) such that, for every \(\nu\geq 2\) and every \(L\geq L_0\), the following conditions imply the frontier conclusion below:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Distance identification.)} For every A1/A2 law \(P\), every map \(d\) satisfying the CTY identification assumptions for \(p\) and \(P\), and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), there are selected source-coherent signed-distance conditional-mean versions \(\theta_1,\theta_0:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) whose right and left limits at zero exist and identify
\[
\tau_P(x)=\lim_{u\downarrow 0}\theta_1(u)-\lim_{u\uparrow 0}\theta_0(u).
\]
\item \textbf{(Uniform first-order bias.)} There is a constant depending only on \(p\) and \(L\) such that, uniformly over every \(\nu'\geq 2\) and every positive antitone deterministic bandwidth sequence \(h_n\to0\) with \(n h_n^2\to\infty\), the finite limsup of the normalized uniform first-order bias ratio is bounded by that constant.
\item \textbf{(Expected maximal bounds.)} For this \(p,\nu,L\), there is a constant \(C_{\mathrm{max}}>0\) such that, along every positive bandwidth sequence \(h_n\to0\) with
\[
\frac{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}{\log(h_n^{-1})}\to\infty,
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\), the expected outer Gram-deviation supremum satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\sup_{j,k}
\left|\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h_n)_{jk}-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h_n)_{jk}\right|
\right]
\leq
C_{\mathrm{max}}
\sqrt{\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n h_n^2}},
\]
and the expected outer centered raw-score supremum satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left\|\widehat m_{t,x}(h_n)-m_{t,P,x}(h_n)\right\|
\right]
\leq
C_{\mathrm{max}}\left(
\sqrt{\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n h_n^2}}
+
\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}
\right).
\]
\end{itemize}
Then there exist constants \(c\) and \(C\) with \(0<c\leq C\) such that, for the signed-distance minimax risk \(R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk} and \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\),
\[
c
\leq
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}
\leq
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}
\leq C .
\]
Moreover, the explicit winsorized, Gram-stabilized degree-\(p\) signed-distance local-polynomial estimator with bandwidth \(h_n=a_n\) and winsorization level \(B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\), clipped to \([-2L,2L]\), has uniform outer risk over \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) satisfying
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]
}{a_n}
\leq C .
\]
\end{theoremv}

Under the analytic inputs in \cref{obj:synth_139}, \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier} characterizes the signed-distance known-geometry minimax risk up to constants under the same envelope threshold as the lower bound. The lower inequality is supplied by \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders}, while the upper inequality is attained by the explicit winsorized, Gram-stabilized local-polynomial estimator from \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}. The result places the signed-distance experiment on the logarithmic expected outer-risk scale \(a_n\) for every fixed polynomial order \(p\), moment exponent \(\nu\ge2\), and envelope \(L\) above the stated order-dependent threshold, with the upper side governed by the three analytic inputs.





\section{Discussion and Extensions}
\label{sec:discussion}

The preceding results separate two roles of distance in boundary regression designs. For the unsigned experiment, \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse,obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse} establish logarithmic lower bounds for boundary sup-loss when the rule observes only outcomes and scalar Euclidean distances from the query point. The common-map statement applies to the decision class and completed-expectation risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class,obj:def:cty-distance-risk}, while the point-indexed statement applies to the sectionwise Borel class and outer-expectation risk in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class,obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}. These conclusions identify an information barrier created by unsigned distance compression over the compact bivariate law class in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class}.

The signed-distance experiment has a different information set. There the rule observes signed distances generated by the known geometry in \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry,obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data} and is evaluated over the A1/A2 law class and risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class,obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. Assuming the analytic inputs collected in \cref{obj:synth_139}, \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper,obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier} combine the signed-distance lower bound with the stabilized local-polynomial upper argument to obtain a two-sided expected outer-risk rate. The role of \citet{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance} is therefore most direct in the signed design: their distance-identification framework supplies the population interpretation of the one-dimensional signed-distance coordinate, while the conditional result here packages the displayed inputs with the new lower bound and stabilized estimator.

The common normalization \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates} indexes different delivered objects across the two experiments. The unsigned experiment has unconditional common-map and point-indexed lower bounds: many separated boundary perturbations remain difficult because scalar distance hides their angular locations and treatment sides. The signed experiment has an unconditional fixed-geometry lower bound and, under the three inputs in \cref{obj:synth_139}, a conditional upper bound attained by a procedure that uses the treatment side, known boundary geometry, and local-polynomial structure. Accordingly, the shared normalization compares lower scales; a two-sided rate conclusion is available here for the signed experiment conditional on \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\).

The signed-distance result is stated for the known Euclidean geometry in \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry} and the one-dimensional uniform kernel in \cref{obj:def:cty-uniform-kernel}. The law class in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} imposes rectangular support, bounded continuous density, smooth potential-outcome regressions, continuous variances, a \(2+\nu\) moment envelope, rectifiable interior interface, two-sided small-bandwidth mass, and a population Gram lower bound. These conditions describe the boundary designs for which the signed-distance local-polynomial estimator has uniformly controlled bias, stable local design matrices, and expected stochastic fluctuations. They also align with the geographic RD setting in which boundary location and treatment side are part of the design information \citep{Keele2015a,Keele2015b,Keele2016,Kendall2026}.

The Euclidean and envelope restrictions also clarify the intended empirical interpretation. Geographic RD applications often use distance to a boundary as a practical running coordinate while retaining design knowledge about assignment regions and local geography \citep{Papay2011,Reardon2012,Wong2013,Choi2018,Diaz2023,Sawada2024,Merlano2025}. The signed-distance analysis formalizes that information set through the known-geometry class, whereas the unsigned analysis formalizes a more compressed experiment in which angular and side information are absent from the observation available at a query point. The results therefore organize comparisons by the data transformation and target risk, rather than by a universal ranking of distance-based empirical strategies.

\paragraph{Open questions.}
The unsigned results in \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse,obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse} are lower bounds only and establish no matching unsigned upper bound here. The signed-distance matched rate in \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier} is conditional on \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\), so a fully self-contained derivation of the signed-distance upper ingredients within the same displayed law class remains an open direction. Further extensions include non-Euclidean metrics, kernels beyond \(K_\square\), alternative interface regularity classes, and risk criteria adapted to inference rather than uniform expected loss.

\section{A: Direct-Product and Support-Boundary Lower Bound}
\label{sec:app-direct-product}

This appendix records the finite-experiment ingredients used to derive the unsigned logarithmic lower bounds. The argument has two components. The first is an overlap product inequality for many binary coordinates after each coordinate has been compressed. The second is a support-boundary hypercube inside the nonparametric class of \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class}, constructed so that the boundary regression values differ at many separated points while the corresponding unsigned distance experiments remain close coordinate by coordinate. These ingredients yield the common-map converse in \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse}; after the strict inclusion statement below, the same construction yields the point-indexed converse in \cref{obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse}.

The direct-product step is stated abstractly because the same form is also useful for the signed-distance lower experiment. It converts many small coordinatewise overlaps into a lower bound on the probability of at least one decoding error.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}[Coordinatewise overlap product]
Fix an integer \(M\geq 1\). Assume the following conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Spaces.)} The spaces carrying \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M,S_1,\ldots,S_M\) are standard Borel.
\item \textbf{(Binary coordinates.)} The variables \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) are independent and uniformly distributed on \(\{0,1\}\).
\item \textbf{(Conditional product structure.)} Conditionally on \((\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M)\), the variables \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) are independent, the law of \(Z_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\) for \(1\leq j\leq M\), and the law of \(Z_0\) is independent of \((\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M)\).
\item \textbf{(Compressed coordinate decoding.)} For each \(j\), \(S_j=c_j(Z_j)\) for a measurable map \(c_j\). The decoder \(\widehat\Omega_j\) is a measurable \(\{0,1\}\)-valued function of \((S_j,(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M),Z_0)\) and is unchanged when its raw coordinate vector is altered only in coordinate \(j\) while \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\) is fixed. Define
\[
 Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=b),
 \qquad
 \rho_j=1-\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
\end{itemize}
Then every such decentralized decoder satisfies
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat\Omega_j=\Omega_j\text{ for every }j\}
 \leq
 \frac12\left\{1+\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)\right\}.
\]
Moreover, for every real \(\kappa<1\), if
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\leq \max\{\kappa\log M,0\}
 \qquad\text{for all }j,
\]
then
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one decoding error}\}
 \geq
 \frac12\left[1-\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\}\right].
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}]
Write \(\mathcal S_j\) for the measurable space carrying \(S_j\), and let \(g_j\) be the measurable map representing the \(j\)th decoder, so that
\[
\widehat\Omega_j=g_j\bigl(S_j,(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M),Z_0\bigr),
\]
and so that the invariance hypothesis reads: for every \(s\in\mathcal S_j\), every \(z_0\), and all raw vectors \(z,z'\) with \(z_k=z'_k\) for every \(k\neq j\),
\[
g_j(s,z,z_0)=g_j(s,z',z_0).
\]
The two laws compared at coordinate \(j\) are the compressed conditional laws \(Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)\) of \cref{obj:synth_25}, and \(\rho_j=1-\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\) with the total variation distance of \cref{obj:synth_29}. Since \(0\leq\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\), we have \(0\leq\rho_j\leq1\) for every \(j\).
% lean: coordinateOverlap

\emph{Step 1: a coordinatewise coupling whose compressions agree with probability \(\rho_j\).}
The raw bit-conditional law at coordinate \(j\) is \(\mathcal L(Z_j\mid\Omega_j=b)\), and \(Q_{j,b}\) is its image under \(c_j\). Let \(\mathcal K_j\) be the common part of \(Q_{j,0}\) and \(Q_{j,1}\), that is, the measure on \(\mathcal S_j\) with density
\[
{d\mathcal K_j\over d(Q_{j,0}+Q_{j,1})}
=
\min\left\{
{dQ_{j,0}\over d(Q_{j,0}+Q_{j,1})},
{dQ_{j,1}\over d(Q_{j,0}+Q_{j,1})}
\right\}
\]
with respect to \(Q_{j,0}+Q_{j,1}\). Its total mass is
\[
\mathcal K_j(\mathcal S_j)=1-\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=\rho_j,
\]
because \(\min\{a,b\}=\{a+b-|a-b|\}/2\), both densities integrate to one, and half the \(L^1\) distance between them is the total variation distance.
% lean: rnCommonPart
% lean: rnCommonPart_mass_eq_one_sub_tvDist

Each residual \(Q_{j,0}-\mathcal K_j\) and \(Q_{j,1}-\mathcal K_j\) then has total mass \(1-\rho_j\). When \(\rho_j<1\), the maximal coupling of \(Q_{j,0}\) and \(Q_{j,1}\) is the law on \(\mathcal S_j\times\mathcal S_j\) given by
\[
\bigl(\text{image of }\mathcal K_j\text{ under }s\mapsto(s,s)\bigr)
+
(1-\rho_j)^{-1}\bigl\{(Q_{j,0}-\mathcal K_j)\times(Q_{j,1}-\mathcal K_j)\bigr\},
\]
and when \(\rho_j=1\) it is the image of \(Q_{j,0}\) under \(s\mapsto(s,s)\). In both cases its two marginals are \(Q_{j,0}\) and \(Q_{j,1}\), and its diagonal carries mass at least \(\mathcal K_j(\mathcal S_j)=\rho_j\).
% lean: maximalCoupling
% lean: residual_mass
% lean: maximalCoupling_map_fst
% lean: maximalCoupling_map_snd
% lean: maximalCoupling_eq_mass_ge

Because all the spaces are standard Borel, the regular conditional distributions of \(Z_j\) given \(c_j(Z_j)\) exist under both raw bit-conditional laws; feeding the two coordinates of the maximal coupling into them produces a law \(\mathcal C_j\) on pairs of raw coordinate blocks, whose coordinates we write \((Z_j^0,Z_j^1)\), such that
\[
\mathcal L(Z_j^0)=\mathcal L(Z_j\mid\Omega_j=0),
\qquad
\mathcal L(Z_j^1)=\mathcal L(Z_j\mid\Omega_j=1),
\qquad
\mathcal C_j\bigl\{c_j(Z_j^0)=c_j(Z_j^1)\bigr\}\geq\rho_j .
\]
The first two equalities hold because the lift preserves each marginal, and the third because the image of \(\mathcal C_j\) under \((z,z')\mapsto(c_j(z),c_j(z'))\) is exactly the maximal coupling above, whose diagonal has mass at least \(\rho_j\).
% lean: compressionCoupling
% lean: compressionCoupling_map_fst
% lean: compressionCoupling_map_snd
% lean: compressionCoupling_map_compress_pair
% lean: compressionCoupling_equal_compression_mass_ge

\emph{Step 2: the coupled experiment reproduces the original one at every vertex.}
Let \(\mathcal C\) be the product of the coordinatewise couplings, that is, the law of the array \((Z_j^0,Z_j^1)_{1\leq j\leq M}\) determined by
\[
\mathcal C\bigl\{(Z_1^0,Z_1^1)\in A_1,\ldots,(Z_M^0,Z_M^1)\in A_M\bigr\}
=\prod_{j=1}^M\mathcal C_j(A_j),
\]
and let \(\mathcal R=\mathcal L(Z_0)\), which by the conditional product structure does not depend on \((\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M)\). Write \(\mathcal R\times\mathcal C\) for the law under which \(Z_0\) has law \(\mathcal R\) and is independent of the array. For a vertex \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\) define the selected raw vector by
\[
Z_j(\omega)=Z_j^{\omega_j},
\qquad 1\leq j\leq M .
\]
Since the two marginals of \(\mathcal C_j\) are the two raw bit-conditional laws at coordinate \(j\), the selection map pushes \(\mathcal C\) forward to the product over \(j\) of \(\mathcal L(Z_j\mid\Omega_j=\omega_j)\), so that
\[
\mathcal L\bigl(Z_0,(Z_1(\omega),\ldots,Z_M(\omega))\bigr)
=
\mathcal L\bigl(Z_0,(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M)\mid\Omega=\omega\bigr),
\]
which is precisely the conditional product law assumed in the statement.
% lean: selectCoupledRaw
% lean: measurePreserving_pi

Consequently, writing
\[
G(\omega)
=
\left\{
g_j\bigl(c_j(Z_j(\omega)),(Z_1(\omega),\ldots,Z_M(\omega)),Z_0\bigr)=\omega_j
\text{ for every }1\leq j\leq M
\right\}
\]
for the simultaneous-correctness event at the vertex \(\omega\), the uniform hypercube prior gives
\[
\Pr\{\widehat\Omega_j=\Omega_j\text{ for every }j\}
=
2^{-M}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^M}(\mathcal R\times\mathcal C)\bigl(G(\omega)\bigr).
\]
% lean: coupledDecoderGood
% lean: coordinatewiseSuccessProbability

\emph{Step 3: the total-disagreement event.}
Let
\[
N=\bigl\{c_j(Z_j^0)\neq c_j(Z_j^1)\text{ for every }1\leq j\leq M\bigr\}
\]
be the event that every coupled coordinate has unequal compressed values. It is a product event across coordinates, so Step 1 gives
\[
\mathcal C(N)
=\prod_{j=1}^M\mathcal C_j\bigl\{c_j(Z_j^0)\neq c_j(Z_j^1)\bigr\}
\leq\prod_{j=1}^M\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
=\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j),
\]
each factor being the complement of an event of probability at least \(\rho_j\).
% lean: hgammaN
% lean: hGammaN

\emph{Step 4: a pointwise bound on the number of correctly decoded vertices.}
Fix one realization of \(Z_0\) and of the coupled array \((Z_j^0,Z_j^1)_{j\leq M}\). We claim
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^M}\mathbf 1\{G(\omega)\}
\leq
{2^M\over2}\bigl(1+\mathbf 1_N\bigr).
\]
On \(N\) this is immediate: each of the \(2^M\) indicators is at most one, and the right-hand side equals \(2^M\).

Off \(N\) there is a coordinate \(j\) with \(c_j(Z_j^0)=c_j(Z_j^1)\). Let \(\omega^{(j)}\) be \(\omega\) with coordinate \(j\) flipped. Selection at a coordinate \(k\neq j\) reads the same bit under \(\omega\) and \(\omega^{(j)}\), while at coordinate \(j\) the two selected blocks have equal compressions by the choice of \(j\); hence
\[
Z_k(\omega)=Z_k(\omega^{(j)})\quad(k\neq j),
\qquad
c_j\bigl(Z_j(\omega)\bigr)=c_j\bigl(Z_j(\omega^{(j)})\bigr).
\]
The invariance hypothesis applied to these two raw vectors therefore gives
\[
g_j\bigl(c_j(Z_j(\omega)),(Z_k(\omega))_{k\leq M},Z_0\bigr)
=
g_j\bigl(c_j(Z_j(\omega^{(j)})),(Z_k(\omega^{(j)}))_{k\leq M},Z_0\bigr),
\]
so this single value cannot equal both \(\omega_j\) and \(\omega^{(j)}_j=1-\omega_j\), and consequently
\[
\mathbf 1\{G(\omega)\}+\mathbf 1\{G(\omega^{(j)})\}\leq1
\qquad\text{for every }\omega\in\{0,1\}^M .
\]
% lean: coupledDecoderGood_flip_exclusive
Summing this over all \(\omega\) and using that \(\omega\mapsto\omega^{(j)}\) is an involution of \(\{0,1\}^M\), hence a bijection under which the sum is unchanged,
\[
2\sum_{\omega}\mathbf 1\{G(\omega)\}
=
\sum_{\omega}\bigl(\mathbf 1\{G(\omega)\}+\mathbf 1\{G(\omega^{(j)})\}\bigr)
\leq
\sum_{\omega}1
=2^M,
\]
that is, \(\sum_\omega\mathbf 1\{G(\omega)\}\leq2^M/2\), which is the claim off \(N\).
% lean: coupledDecoderGood_count_le_half

\emph{Step 5: the simultaneous success bound.}
Integrating the pointwise bound of Step 4 against \(\mathcal R\times\mathcal C\) and using Step 3,
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^M}(\mathcal R\times\mathcal C)\bigl(G(\omega)\bigr)
=
\int\sum_{\omega}\mathbf 1\{G(\omega)\}\,d(\mathcal R\times\mathcal C)
\leq
{2^M\over2}\bigl\{1+\mathcal C(N)\bigr\}
\leq
{2^M\over2}\left\{1+\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)\right\}.
\]
Dividing by \(2^M\) and inserting the identity of Step 2 gives the first assertion,
\[
\Pr\{\widehat\Omega_j=\Omega_j\text{ for every }j\}
\leq
\frac12\left\{1+\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)\right\}.
\]
% lean: coordinatewise_overlap_direct_product

\emph{Step 6: calibrating the product by the Kullback--Leibler budget.}
Fix a real \(\kappa<1\) and assume
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\leq\max\{\kappa\log M,0\}
\qquad\text{for every }j .
\]
Since \(M\geq1\) we have \(\log M\geq0\), and we split on the sign of \(\kappa\).

If \(\kappa\geq0\), then \(\kappa\log M\geq0\), so \(\max\{\kappa\log M,0\}=\kappa\log M\) and the budget reads \(\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\leq\kappa\log M\). The Bretagnolle--Huber overlap bound applied to the pair \((Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\) states
\[
\frac12\exp\{-\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\}
\leq
1-\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
=\rho_j,
\]
so monotonicity of \(t\mapsto\exp(-t)\) together with the budget gives
\[
\rho_j\geq\frac12\exp\{-\kappa\log M\}
\qquad\text{for every }1\leq j\leq M .
\]
% lean: overlap_ge_exp_neg_klBudget
Every factor \(1-\rho_j\) is nonnegative, so the elementary inequality \(1-t\leq\exp(-t)\) applied coordinatewise and then the common floor just obtained yield
\[
\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)
\leq
\exp\left\{-\sum_{j=1}^M\rho_j\right\}
\leq
\exp\left\{-M\cdot\frac12\exp(-\kappa\log M)\right\}
=
\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\},
\]
the final equality because \(M\exp(-\kappa\log M)=M^{1-\kappa}\).
% lean: prod_one_sub_le_exp_neg_sum
% lean: prod_one_sub_le_exp_neg_card_mul
% lean: card_mul_exp_neg_log_eq_rpow
% lean: coordinateOverlap_product_le_of_nonnegative_kl

If \(\kappa<0\), then \(\kappa\log M\leq0\), so \(\max\{\kappa\log M,0\}=0\) and the budget forces \(\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})=0\), hence \(Q_{j,0}=Q_{j,1}\) and \(\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=0\), that is, \(\rho_j=1\) for every \(j\). Since \(M\geq1\), the product below has at least one factor, so
\[
\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)=0\leq\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\}.
\]
% lean: tvDist_eq_zero_of_klBudget_nonpos

\emph{Step 7: passing to the error probability.}
Write \(p=\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)\) and \(e=\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\}\). Then \(0\leq p\), because every factor is a total variation distance; \(e\leq1\), because \(M^{1-\kappa}\geq0\); and \(p\leq e\) by Step 6. Hence
\[
\frac12(1-e)+\frac12(1+p)=1+\frac{p-e}{2}\leq1,
\]
so subtracting the success bound of Step 5 from one gives
\[
\Pr\{\text{at least one decoding error}\}
=
1-\Pr\{\widehat\Omega_j=\Omega_j\text{ for every }j\}
\geq
\frac12\left[1-\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\}\right].
\]
% lean: ennreal_error_lower_bound_of_success_upper_bound

Finally, along any sequence \(M_n\to\infty\) with \(\kappa<1\) fixed, \(M_n^{1-\kappa}\to\infty\) and hence \(\exp\{-M_n^{1-\kappa}/2\}\to0\), so the displayed lower bound is \(1/2-o(1)\).
% lean: coordinatewise_overlap_direct_product_asymptotic
\end{proof}

The invariance requirement in \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product} matches the distance-compressed experiment: when a rule estimates the value attached to coordinate \(j\), the information about the \(j\)th perturbation that remains after compression is summarized by \(S_j\), while the remaining raw coordinates and the background block may be held fixed. The total-variation overlap coefficient measures how often the compressed coordinate laws can be coupled to agree. The Kullback--Leibler consequence gives the logarithmic calibration used in the boundary packing, in the same testing spirit as standard minimax lower-bound arguments \citep{Stone1982,Tsybakov2009}.

The next statement supplies the geometric and probabilistic certificate for the unsigned law class. It places many separated locations on the boundary of a square support and assigns one binary regression perturbation to each local half-disc.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing}[Angular Packing Certificate]
For every integer \(q\geq 1\) and every envelope \(L\geq 4\), there exist constants
\[
c_0,c_1,c_w^-,c_w^+,c_{\mathrm{rad}},\alpha>0,
\qquad \alpha<\frac18,
\]
such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), writing \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), there are an integer \(M_n\), a radius \(w_n\), a mass \(m_n\), boundary points \(x_1,\ldots,x_{M_n}\), laws \(P_\omega\) indexed by \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^{M_n}\), and values \(u_{j,0},u_{j,1}\) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Packing size and scale.)}
\[
c_0 a_n^{-1/q}\leq M_n,
\qquad
c_w^- a_n^{1/q}\leq w_n\leq c_w^+ a_n^{1/q}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Boundary packing.)} With \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\), each \(x_j\) lies on \(\operatorname{bd}(S)\), and for \(i\neq j\),
\[
\|x_i-x_j\|_2\geq c_w^- a_n^{1/q}.
\]
The cells \(C_j=B_2(x_j,w_n)\cap S\) are pairwise disjoint.
\item \textbf{(Model class and support.)} For every \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^{M_n}\), the law \(P_\omega\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) and has covariate support \(S\).
\item \textbf{(Cell masses.)} For every \(\omega\) and every \(j\),
\[
P_\omega(X\in C_j)=m_n.
\]
\item \textbf{(Local dependence on one bit.)} If \(\omega_j=\omega'_j\), then the restricted laws of \((Y,X)\) on the cell \(C_j\) agree under \(P_\omega\) and \(P_{\omega'}\). The restricted laws of \((Y,X)\) on \(S\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_n}C_j\) are the same for all \(\omega\).
\item \textbf{(Boundary signal.)} For every \(\omega\) and \(j\),
\[
\mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)=u_{j,\omega_j},
\qquad
|u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|\geq c_1 a_n.
\]
\item \textbf{(Radial invariance.)} Let \(\omega^{(j)}\) be \(\omega\) with coordinate \(j\) flipped. For every \(\omega\) and \(j\), the laws of \(\|X-x_j\|_2\) under \(P_\omega\) and \(P_{\omega^{(j)}}\) agree. Moreover, the corresponding one-point distance laws agree after restriction to radii \(z_2\geq c_{\mathrm{rad}}a_n\).
\item \textbf{(Compressed-distance KL bound.)} For every \(\omega\) and \(j\),
\[
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
\mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
\mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
\right)
\leq \alpha\log M_n.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing}]

\emph{Step 1: the fixed profiles.}  Let \(\varphi:\mathbb R^2\to[0,1]\) be the normalized radial bump, that is, the smooth radially symmetric function with
\[
\varphi(0)=1,
\qquad
0\le\varphi\le1,
\qquad
\varphi(z)=0\ \text{ whenever }\ \lVert z\rVert_2\ge1,
\]
and let \(\Psi:\mathbb R\to[0,1]\) be the smooth transition function, which vanishes on \((-\infty,0]\) and equals one on \([1,\infty)\).  For each integer \(j\ge0\) put
\[
b_j=\sup_{z\in\mathbb R^2}\lVert D^j\varphi(z)\rVert<\infty ,
\]
the uniform bound on the \(j\)th Fréchet derivative of \(\varphi\).
% lean: packingBump, packingBump_zero, packingBump_mem_Icc, packingBump_eq_zero_of_one_le_norm, packingBumpDerivativeBound, packingBump_iteratedFDeriv_le_derivativeBound

\emph{Step 2: the constants.}  Define the smoothness-dependent derivative scale and the one-observation divergence constant by
\[
D_q=1+q+1310720+\sum_{j=0}^{q}b_j ,
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{KL}}=1310720 ,
\]
and set
\[
c_0=\frac1{24},\qquad
c_1=\frac1{1024D_q},\qquad
c_w^-=c_w^+=1,\qquad
c_{\mathrm{rad}}=\frac18,\qquad
\alpha=\frac1{16}.
\]
Since every \(b_j\ge0\), the scale \(D_q\) is finite and satisfies
\[
D_q\ge1,\qquad D_q\ge q,\qquad D_q\ge C_{\mathrm{KL}} .
\]
Hence all six constants are positive and \(\alpha=1/16<1/8\).
% lean: packingBumpDerivativeScale, packingBumpDerivativeScale_pos, angularPackingOnePointKLConstant, packingBumpDerivativeBound_le_scale

The three displayed inequalities give the divergence budget used below:
\[

\le

\le

\]
the first step because \(q\le D_q\) and \(C_{\mathrm{KL}}\le D_q\), the second because \(D_q\ge1\).
% lean: cty_support_boundary_angular_packing

\emph{Step 3: the eventual regime.}  With \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) as in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates}, set

in the sense of \cref{obj:synth_36}.  Consequently \(P_\omega\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) as in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class}, with \(\mathcal X_{P_\omega}=S\).

For the cell masses, the only \(\omega\)-dependent part of \(f_\omega\) on \(C_j\) is \(\omega_jt_n(\lVert x-x_j\rVert_2)\kappa_j(x)\), and reflecting \(C_j\) in the vertical line through \(x_j\) changes the sign of \(\kappa_j\) while fixing \(\lVert x-x_j\rVert_2\); hence that term integrates to zero over \(C_j\) and
\[
P_\omega(X\in C_j)=\operatorname{Leb}(C_j)=m_n ,
\]
where \(m_n=\operatorname{Leb}(C_1)\) is common to all \(j\) because the cells are translates of each other along the lower edge.
% lean: angularPackingCtyLaw_cell_mass_eq_volume, angularGrid_packingCell_volume_eq

\emph{Step 7: locality in one bit.}  The functions \(\varphi((\cdot-x_i)/w_n)\) and \(t_n(\lVert\cdot-x_i\rVert_2)\kappa_i(\cdot)\) vanish outside \(B_2(x_i,w_n)\), and the cells are pairwise disjoint.  Therefore, on \(C_j\) both \(f_\omega\) and \(\mu_\omega\) depend on \(\omega\) only through \(\omega_j\), so
\[
\omega_j=\omega'_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P_\omega\big|_{\{X\in C_j\}}=P_{\omega'}\big|_{\{X\in C_j\}},
\]
and on \(S\setminus\bigcup_jC_j\) we have \(f_\omega\equiv1\) and \(\mu_\omega(x)\equiv\tfrac12+\tfrac{x^{(1)}}{8}\), so
\[
P_\omega\big|_{\{X\in S\setminus\bigcup_jC_j\}}
=
P_{\omega'}\big|_{\{X\in S\setminus\bigcup_jC_j\}}
\qquad\text{for all }\omega,\omega' .
\]
% lean: angularPackingCtyLaw_locality_certificate

\emph{Step 8: boundary signal.}  Put
\[
u_{j,0}=\frac12+\frac{x_j^{(1)}}{8},
\qquad
u_{j,1}=u_{j,0}+\delta_n .
\]
At \(x_j\) every bump attached to another center vanishes, because \(\lVert x_j-x_i\rVert_2\ge3w_n>w_n\) for \(i\ne j\), while \(\varphi(0)=1\); hence
\[
\mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)=u_{j,\omega_j},
\qquad
|u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|=\delta_n=c_1a_n .
\]
% lean: packingRegression_center, packingCenterValue, packingRegression_eq_packingCenterValue, angularPackingCtyLaw_mu_center, packingCenterValue_scaledDelta_separation

\emph{Step 9: radial invariance.}  Let \(\omega^{(j)}\) be \(\omega\) with coordinate \(j\) flipped.  For every measurable \(A\subseteq[0,\infty)\), the reflection of Step 6 applied to the annular slice \(\{x:\lVert x-x_j\rVert_2\in A\}\) again cancels the \(\omega_j\)-dependent density term, so
\[
\mathcal L_{P_\omega}\bigl(\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\bigr)
=
\mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\bigl(\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\bigr).
\]
% lean: angularDesignMeasure_map_distance_eq, angularPackingCtyLaw_flip_map_distance_eq

For the tail statement, fix \(r\ge c_{\mathrm{rad}}a_n\).  The last display of Step 3 gives \(16\delta_n\le r/8\), so \(r/(64\delta_n)-1\ge1\) and \(r/8\ge8\delta_n\); therefore the cutoff is fully active,
\[
\chi_n(r)=1,
\qquad
t_n(r)=-\,\frac{16\,\delta_n\,\varphi\!\left(\tfrac{r}{w_n}e_\circ\right)}{r} .
\]
% lean: angularPackingScaledDelta_cutoff_fully_active, angularTilt_eq_formula
Let \(A\subseteq[c_{\mathrm{rad}}a_n,\infty)\) be measurable and let
\[
D_{j,A}=\left\{x\in B_2(x_j,w_n):x^{(2)}>-\tfrac12,\ \lVert x-x_j\rVert_2\in A\right\}
\]
be the part of the cell \(C_j\) on which the two vertices differ.  Assume \(\omega_j=1\), so that \(\omega^{(j)}_j=0\); the reverse case is symmetric.  On \(D_{j,A}\) the difference of the two outcome-weighted densities is
\[
\mu_\omega f_\omega-\mu_{\omega^{(j)}}f_{\omega^{(j)}}
=
\delta_n\varphi\!\left(\tfrac{r}{w_n}e_\circ\right)
+
\left\{\frac12+\frac{x^{(1)}}{8}+\delta_n\varphi\!\left(\tfrac{r}{w_n}e_\circ\right)\right\}

\qquad r=\lVert x-x_j\rVert_2 ,
\]
because on the cell all other bumps and angular corrections vanish.
% lean: packingRegression_mul_density_flip_cell_identity, packingRegression_mul_density_flip_cell_radial_identity
Splitting the brace into the radius-only part \(\tfrac12+\tfrac{x_j^{(1)}}{8}+\delta_n\varphi(\tfrac{r}{w_n}e_\circ)\) and the remainder \(\tfrac18(x^{(1)}-x_j^{(1)})\), the first part contributes \(\int_{D_{j,A}}g(r)\kappa_j=0\) by the same horizontal reflection, and the second contributes
\[
\int_{D_{j,A}}\frac{x^{(1)}-x_j^{(1)}}{8}\,t_n(r)\,\kappa_j(x)\,dx
=
\int_{D_{j,A}}\frac{r}{8}\,t_n(r)\,\kappa_j(x)^2\,dx
=
-\int_{D_{j,A}}2\delta_n\varphi\!\left(\tfrac{r}{w_n}e_\circ\right)\kappa_j(x)^2\,dx .
\]
% lean: angularGridCenter_openRadialSet_weighted_direction_cancellation
Since the average of \(\kappa_j^2\) over each half-circle \(\{\lVert x-x_j\rVert_2=r,\ x^{(2)}>-1/2\}\) equals \(1/2\), the two remaining terms cancel:
\[
\int_{D_{j,A}}\bigl(\mu_\omega f_\omega-\mu_{\omega^{(j)}}f_{\omega^{(j)}}\bigr)\,dx=0 .
\]
% lean: angularGridCenter_radialSet_angular_outcome_cancellation, packingRegression_density_flip_integral_eq_zero_of_active
Together with the equality of radial slice masses this says that, for every measurable \(A\subseteq[c_{\mathrm{rad}}a_n,\infty)\), the two experiments assign the same mass and the same conditional-mean mass to the slice \(\{\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\in A\}\); since the outcome mixture is determined by its conditional mean, the restricted one-point distance laws agree:
\[
\mathcal L_{P_\omega}\bigl(Y,\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\bigr)\Big|_{\{z_2\ge c_{\mathrm{rad}}a_n\}}
=
\mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\bigl(Y,\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\bigr)\Big|_{\{z_2\ge c_{\mathrm{rad}}a_n\}} .
\]
% lean: angularPacking_flip_setIntegral_eq_of_active, angularPackingCtyLaw_flip_restrict_Ici_eq_of_active

\emph{Step 10: the compressed-distance divergence.}  Below the fully active radius the same computation is no longer exact, but it is quantitatively small.  For every measurable \(A\subseteq[0,\infty)\),
\[
\left|
\int_{\{\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\in A\}}\mu_\omega\,dP_\omega^X
-
\int_{\{\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\in A\}}\mu_{\omega^{(j)}}\,dP_{\omega^{(j)}}^X
\right|
\le
\delta_n\,P_\omega\bigl(\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\in A\cap[0,128\delta_n)\bigr),
\]
where \(P^X\) denotes the covariate marginal: outside the radii \(r<2\cdot(8\delta_n)/(1/8)=128\delta_n\) the cutoff is fully active and Step 9 gives exact cancellation, while on those radii the uncancelled increment is at most the bump amplitude \(\delta_n\) per unit radial mass.
% lean: angularRadialSuccessIncrement_identity, angularRadialSuccessIncrement_abs_le, angularPacking_flip_success_setIntegral_abs_le
Moreover, since \(f_\omega\le5/4\) and a disc of radius \(R\) has area \(\pi R^2\le4R^2\),
\[
P_\omega\bigl(\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2<R\bigr)\le 5R^{2}
\qquad (R\ge0).
\]
% lean: angularDesignMeasure_radial_Iio_le

The two vertices induce one-observation distance experiments with a common radial marginal, with conditional success parameters taking values in \([1/4,3/4]\), and, by the previous display, with those parameters differing only at radii below \(128\delta_n\) and by at most \(2\delta_n\) there.  For two Bernoulli-plus-Gaussian mixtures the divergence at a fixed radius is at most four times the squared difference of the success parameters, so averaging over the common radial marginal gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
\mathcal L_{P_\omega}\bigl(Y,\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\bigr),
\mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\bigl(Y,\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\bigr)\right)
\le
4(2\delta_n)^{2}\,P_\omega\bigl(\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2<128\delta_n\bigr)
\le
4(2\delta_n)^{2}\cdot5(128\delta_n)^{2}
=
C_{\mathrm{KL}}\,\delta_n^{4},
\]
using \(4\cdot4\cdot5\cdot128^{2}=1310720=C_{\mathrm{KL}}\).
% lean: radialOutcomeLaw_klDiv_le_of_localized_success_bound, angularPacking_radialOutcome_klDiv_le

The distance data \(V_{n,P}(x_j)\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data} are \(n\) independent copies of the one-observation pair \((Y,\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2)\), so the divergence tensorizes; combining with the eventual budget of Step 3,
\[
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
\mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
\mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
\right)
\le
n\,C_{\mathrm{KL}}\,\delta_n^{4}
\le
\alpha\log M_n .
\]
% lean: compressedSampleLaw_klDiv_le_of_onePoint_finite_bound

Steps 4--10 supply every displayed clause with the constants of Step 2, for all sufficiently large \(n\).
% lean: angularPackingAt_of_certificates, angularPackingAt_scaledDelta_of_radial_certificates, cty_support_boundary_angular_packing
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing} is the concrete bridge between smooth boundary regression and the abstract product experiment. The Hölder restriction determines the relation between the signal height \(a_n\) and the cell radius \(w_n\), while the boundary packing supplies \(M_n\) separated query points. Local dependence on one bit makes each cell a coordinate of the hypercube, and the radial invariance clause expresses the angular information loss created by unsigned distance observations. The compressed-distance Kullback--Leibler bound then puts those coordinates in the range of \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}. This is the finite-sample testing structure that underlies the rate calculation summarized in the main text.

For common-map rules, the decoder associated with a proposed estimator applies the same measurable map to each query point. If the estimator were uniformly accurate on all \(x_j\)'s, the signs of the boundary signals in \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing} would be decoded correctly across all coordinates. Combining the signal separation with the overlap lower bound gives the positive lower scale in \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse}. The argument uses the law class and completed-expectation criterion exactly as defined in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class,obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class,obj:def:cty-distance-risk}.

The point-indexed class enlarges the set of admissible rules by allowing the Borel section to vary with the query point. The following inclusion statement records that relationship before applying the same hypercube construction to the outer-expectation risk.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:common-map-strict-in-point-indexed}[Strict Common-Map Inclusion]
For parameters satisfying
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\geq 1\).
\item \textbf{(Smoothness index.)} \(q\geq 1\).
\item \textbf{(Envelope.)} \(L\geq 4\).
\end{itemize}
the common-map decision class and the point-indexed decision class of \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class,obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class} satisfy
\[
\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\subsetneq \mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:common-map-strict-in-point-indexed}]
Throughout, a point of \(\mathbb R^2\) is written \(x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})\), and a sample point is written \(w=((y_i,z_i):1\leq i\leq n)\in(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2)^n\), so that the distance data of \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data} evaluated at \(w\) and at a query point \(x\) are
\[
V_{n,P}(x)\big|_{w}
=
\bigl((y_i,\lVert z_i-x\rVert_2):1\leq i\leq n\bigr).
\]

\emph{Inclusion.}  Let \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) and let \(T_n:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\to\mathbb R\) be a measurable map, selected independently of \(P\), representing it as in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class}.  Define a point-indexed family by
\[

\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R^2 .
\]
Each section is the single Borel map \(T_n\), hence Borel measurable for every fixed \(x\), and the family is selected independently of \(P\) because \(T_n\) is.  Moreover, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), every sample point, and every \(x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\),
\[
\widehat\mu_n(x)=T_n(V_{n,P}(x))=T_{n,x}(V_{n,P}(x)),
\]
so \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class}.  Therefore
\[
\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\subseteq \mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}.
\]
% lean: ctyClass_subset_piClass

\emph{A point-indexed rule outside the common-map class.}  Let the rule return the first coordinate of the query point, ignoring the data:
\[
\widehat\mu_{n}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)=x^{(1)},
\qquad\text{generated by the sections }\;
T_{n,x}(v)=x^{(1)}
\quad\text{for every }v\in(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n .
\]
For each fixed \(x\) the section \(T_{n,x}\) is constant in \(v\), hence Borel, and the family \(\{T_{n,x}\}\) does not depend on the member law.  Thus \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\).

Apply \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing} at the given \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\), at any sample size beyond the threshold it provides, and take the vertex \(\omega=(0,\ldots,0)\); its model-class and support clause supplies a law
\[

\qquad

Each of \(x_-\) and \(x_+\) has second coordinate \(-1/2\) and first coordinate of modulus \(1/4<1/2\), so it is a non-corner point of the lower edge of \(S\) and therefore

% lean: lowerEdgePoint_mem_frontier

\]
% lean: dist_scorePoint_same_second

% lean: distanceData

Thus the coordinatewise testing reduction induced by \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing} continues to apply after replacing a single common map by fixed-\(x\) Borel sections. \section{B: Signed-Distance Geometry, Maximal Inequalities, and Upper Bound}
\label{sec:app-signed-upper}

This appendix collects the empirical-process ingredients used by the signed-distance upper bound in \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}. The objects are those of the known-geometry design in \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry,obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data,obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class,obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}: local neighborhoods are Euclidean balls generated by the uniform weight \(K_\square\), the outcome moment envelope is handled through winsorization, and the stochastic term is measured by an outer-expected supremum over arms and interface points. The three statements below organize these roles before the estimator bound is assembled in \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}.

The first ingredient is geometric. Because the local windows are closed Euclidean balls, the relevant index class has finite VC complexity, giving the covering control used by the maximal inequality \citep{Vapnik1971,Pollard1984,VanderVaart1996}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:euclidean-balls-vc}[Planar Balls VC Bound]
For every finite set \(S\) of score points in \(\mathbb R^2\), if \(|S|\ge 4\), then \(S\) is not shattered by the class of closed Euclidean balls
\[
\{B_2(x,r): x\in\mathbb R^2,\ r\ge 0\}.
\]
Equivalently, no such \(S\) has the property that every subset of \(S\) can be written as \(S\cap B_2(x,r)\) for some center \(x\in\mathbb R^2\) and radius \(r\ge 0\).
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:euclidean-balls-vc}]
Suppose, for contradiction, that a finite set \(S\subset\mathbb R^2\) with \(|S|\ge4\) is
shattered by closed Euclidean balls, that is, for every subset \(T\subseteq S\) there are a
center \(c\in\mathbb R^2\) and a radius \(r\ge0\) with
\[
T=S\cap B_2(c,r)=\{z\in S:\|z-c\|_2\leq r\} .
\]
Since \(|S|\ge4\) we may fix four pairwise distinct points \(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4\in S\).
% lean: Finset.exists_subset_card_eq
Writing \(z_i=(z_{i,1},z_{i,2})\), put
\[
\Phi(z_i)=\bigl(1,z_{i,1},z_{i,2},\|z_i\|_2^2\bigr)\in\mathbb R^4 ,
\qquad i\in\{1,2,3,4\} .
\]

\emph{An algebraic identity.}  Let \(\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3,\ell_4)\in\mathbb R^4\) be
arbitrary coefficients and let \(w\in\mathbb R^4\) be the corresponding combination,
\[
w=\sum_{i=1}^4\ell_i\Phi(z_i) .
\]
For a center \(c=(c_1,c_2)\in\mathbb R^2\) and a radius \(r\ge0\), expanding the squared
distance gives, for each \(i\),
\[
\|z_i-c\|_2^2-r^2
=
\|z_i\|_2^2-2c_1z_{i,1}-2c_2z_{i,2}+(\|c\|_2^2-r^2)\cdot 1 ,
\]
which is one and the same linear combination of the four entries of \(\Phi(z_i)\), with
coefficients not depending on \(i\).  Multiplying by \(\ell_i\) and summing therefore
replaces those entries by the entries of \(w\):
\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\ell_i\{\|z_i-c\|_2^2-r^2\}
=
w_4-2c_1w_2-2c_2w_3+(\|c\|_2^2-r^2)w_1 .
\]
% lean: hweighted

\emph{A sign contradiction.}  We next record the contradiction used in both cases below.
Suppose the coefficients \(\ell\) are not all zero, that their combination \(w\) satisfies
\(w_1=0\), and that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\ell_i\{\|z_i-c\|_2^2-r^2\}\ge 0
\qquad\text{for every }c\in\mathbb R^2\text{ and every }r\ge0 .
\]
Reading off the first entry of \(w\) and using \(\Phi(z_i)_1=1\) for every \(i\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\ell_i=w_1=0 ,
\]
% lean: hlsum
so, some \(\ell_i\) being nonzero, at least one coefficient is strictly negative; fix an
index \(i_-\) with \(\ell_{i_-}<0\).
% lean: hineg
Apply the shattering hypothesis to the subset
\[
T=\{z_i:1\le i\le 4,\ \ell_i>0\}\subseteq S ,
\]
obtaining a center \(c\) and a radius \(r\ge0\) with \(T=S\cap B_2(c,r)\).  Because
\(z_1,\ldots,z_4\) are distinct, \(z_i\in T\) holds exactly when \(\ell_i>0\).  Hence every
term of the weighted sum is nonpositive,
\[
\ell_i\{\|z_i-c\|_2^2-r^2\}\le0 ,\qquad i\in\{1,2,3,4\} :
\]
if \(\ell_i>0\) then \(z_i\in T\subseteq B_2(c,r)\), so \(\|z_i-c\|_2\le r\), the bracket
is nonpositive, and the product is nonpositive; if \(\ell_i\le0\) then \(z_i\notin T\), so
\(\|z_i-c\|_2>r\), the bracket is strictly positive, and again the product is nonpositive.
% lean: hterm
At \(i_-\) the second alternative occurs with a strictly negative coefficient, so
\[
\ell_{i_-}\{\|z_{i_-}-c\|_2^2-r^2\}<0 ,
\]
% lean: hstrict
and summing the four terms gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\ell_i\{\|z_i-c\|_2^2-r^2\}<0 ,
\]
which contradicts the assumed nonnegativity at this \((c,r)\).
% lean: htotal
% lean: hsign_contra

\emph{Case 1: \(\Phi(z_1),\ldots,\Phi(z_4)\) are linearly independent.}  Four linearly
independent vectors in \(\mathbb R^4\) form a basis, so the fourth standard basis vector has
an expansion
\[
e_4=(0,0,0,1)=\sum_{i=1}^4\ell_i\Phi(z_i)
\]
with coefficients \(\ell\) that are not all zero, since \(e_4\neq0\).  Here \(w=e_4\), so
\(w_1=w_2=w_3=0\) and \(w_4=1\), and the algebraic identity gives, for every
\(c\in\mathbb R^2\) and every \(r\ge0\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\ell_i\{\|z_i-c\|_2^2-r^2\}=1\ge0 .
\]
The sign contradiction applies.
% lean: basisOfLinearIndependentOfCardEqFinrank

\emph{Case 2: \(\Phi(z_1),\ldots,\Phi(z_4)\) are linearly dependent.}  Choose coefficients
\(\ell\), not all zero, with
\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\ell_i\Phi(z_i)=0 .
\]
Here \(w=0\), so in particular \(w_1=0\), and the algebraic identity gives, for every
\(c\in\mathbb R^2\) and every \(r\ge0\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\ell_i\{\|z_i-c\|_2^2-r^2\}=0\ge0 .
\]
The sign contradiction applies again.
% lean: Fintype.not_linearIndependent_iff

Both cases are impossible, so no finite \(S\) with \(|S|\ge4\) is shattered by closed
Euclidean balls.
% lean: euclidean_balls_vc
\end{proof}

The bound in \cref{obj:lem:euclidean-balls-vc} is used with the ball notation introduced in \cref{obj:synth_43}. In the signed-distance design, the event selected by the uniform kernel is a side-restricted Euclidean neighborhood of the query point. Finite VC complexity therefore converts the continuum of possible interface centers into an empirical-process class with logarithmic entropy, matching the \(\log(h^{-1})\) factors in the risk calculation.

The second ingredient controls the deterministic effect of replacing the outcome by its winsorized version. The \(2+\nu\) moment envelope in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} implies a uniform tail contribution bound at every arm and covariate value.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:cty-winsorization-bias}[Winsorization Bias Bound]
Let \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) as in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, and let \(B\geq 1\).  Define
\[
\psi_B(y)=\operatorname{sign}(y)\min\{|y|,B\}.
\]
Then, for each \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and each \(x\in\mathcal X_P\), under the selected conditional law of \(Y(t)\) given \(X=x\),
\[
\left|
\int y\,dP_{Y(t)\mid X=x}(y)
-
\int \psi_B(y)\,dP_{Y(t)\mid X=x}(y)
\right|
\leq L B^{-3}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorization-bias}]
Fix \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(x\in\mathcal X_P\), and work under the selected
conditional law \(P_{Y(t)\mid X=x}\) of \(Y(t)\) given \(X=x\).  The class
conditions in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} give \(\nu\ge2\), \(L\ge4\), and the
conditional moment envelope
\[
\int |y|^{2+\nu}\,dP_{Y(t)\mid X=x}(y)\le L .
\]
% lean: A1A2Class
In particular \(|y|^{2+\nu}\) is integrable, hence so is \(y\) because
\(1\le 2+\nu\) and \(P_{Y(t)\mid X=x}\) is a probability law; and \(\psi_B\) is
measurable with \(|\psi_B(y)|\le B\), so \(\psi_B(y)\) is integrable as well.
% lean: hpow_int
% lean: hy
% lean: hwin

\emph{Two pointwise facts about the winsorization remainder.}  First, for every
real \(y\),
\[
|y-\psi_B(y)|\le |y| ,
\]
because \(\psi_B(y)=\operatorname{sign}(y)\min\{|y|,B\}\) has the same sign as
\(y\) and modulus \(\min\{|y|,B\}\le|y|\), so the remainder \(y-\psi_B(y)\) has
modulus \(|y|-\min\{|y|,B\}\le|y|\).
% lean: abs_sub_winsorize_le_abs
Second,
\[
\psi_B(y)=y
\qquad\text{whenever } |y|\le B ,
\]
since then \(\min\{|y|,B\}=|y|\), and \(\operatorname{sign}(y)|y|=y\) in each of
the three defining cases \(y<0\), \(y=0\), and \(0<y\).
% lean: winsorize

\emph{The pointwise majorant.}  For every real \(y\),
\[
|y-\psi_B(y)|\le |y|^{2+\nu}B^{-3}.
\]
If \(|y|\le B\), the left-hand side vanishes by the preceding identity and the
right-hand side is nonnegative.  If \(B<|y|\), then \(B\ge1\) gives \(|y|\ge1\),
so \(B^3\le|y|^3\) and \(4\le 2+\nu\) yield
\[
|y|\le |y|^{4}B^{-3}\le |y|^{2+\nu}B^{-3},
\]
the first inequality because \(|y|B^3\le|y|\cdot|y|^3=|y|^4\) and the second
because \(|y|\ge1\) makes the exponent monotone; combining with
\(|y-\psi_B(y)|\le|y|\) gives the displayed majorant.
% lean: winsorize_tail_le_moment

\emph{Integration.}  Using integrability to subtract inside the integral and
applying the pointwise majorant,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int y\,dP_{Y(t)\mid X=x}-\int \psi_B(y)\,dP_{Y(t)\mid X=x}\right|
&=\left|\int \{y-\psi_B(y)\}\,dP_{Y(t)\mid X=x}\right| \\
&\le \int |y|^{2+\nu}B^{-3}\,dP_{Y(t)\mid X=x} \\
&= B^{-3}\int |y|^{2+\nu}\,dP_{Y(t)\mid X=x} \\
&\le L B^{-3},
\end{aligned}
\]
the last step by the conditional moment envelope together with \(B^{-3}\ge0\).
% lean: norm_integral_le_of_norm_le
This is the claimed bias bound.
% lean: cty_winsorization_bias
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorization-bias} links the envelope condition to the estimator in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}. With \(B(h)=h^{-1/3}\), the displayed \(B^{-3}\) term is of order \(h\), so winsorization contributes on the same scale as the first-order local-polynomial bias assumed in \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}. The calculation preserves uniformity over the law class because the conditional moment bound is part of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}.

The final auxiliary statement supplies the expected maximal bound for the centered winsorized local-polynomial score. It combines the VC geometry of \cref{obj:lem:euclidean-balls-vc} with the bounded envelope created by \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorization-bias}, using the outer-expectation convention already built into \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}[Winsorized Score Maximal Bound]
Fix a nonnegative integer \(p\) and a real number \(L\).  There is a constant
\(C=C(p,L)>0\) such that the following holds for every real \(\nu\).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Law class.)} The law \(P\) belongs to the A1/A2 class
\(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}.
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} The sample size satisfies \(n\geq 1\).
\item \textbf{(Bandwidth and clipping.)} The bandwidth and winsorization level satisfy
\(0<h\leq L^{-1}\) and \(B\geq 1\).
\end{itemize}

Let \(P^n\) denote the \(n\)-fold i.i.d. law of the potential-outcome observations.
For each \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), let the winsorized centered local-polynomial
score be the vector whose \(j\)th coordinate is
\[
\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h,j}
  - \mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h,j}\right],
\]
where the empirical score uses the uniform kernel, the polynomial basis \(r_p\), bandwidth \(h\),
and the clipped outcome transform \(\psi_B\), centered around the corresponding population score.
Then
\[
\mathbb E_{P^n}^{*}\!\left[
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\},\,x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left\|
\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}
-\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]
\right\|
\right]
\leq
C\left\{
\sqrt{\frac{\log(B/h)}{n h^2}}
+
\frac{B\log(B/h)}{n h^2}
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}]
Throughout, \(Z=(Y(0),Y(1),X)\) denotes one potential-outcome observation, \(Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\) its observed outcome, and for \(u\in\mathbb R^{p+1}\) we write \(\lVert u\rVert=\max_{0\leq j\leq p}|u_j|\) for the coordinate maximum, which is the norm appearing in the displayed conclusion.  All constants below are fixed before the moment exponent \(\nu\), the law \(P\), the sample size \(n\), the bandwidth \(h\), and the winsorization level \(B\).

\textbf{Step 1: a coefficient radius depending only on \(p\) and \(L\).}  Set
\[
R=1+\left|L(p+1)\cdot 16L(2+L)\right|,
\]
which is positive.  We claim that for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\), every \(t\in\{0,1\}\), every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), every \(0<h\leq L^{-1}\) and every \(0\leq j\leq p\),
\[
\left|\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)_j\right|\leq R .
\]
Write
\[
m_{t,P,x}(h)
=
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
{1\over h^2}\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}
K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)\,r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)\,Y
\right]
\]
for the right-hand side of the population normal equations in \cref{obj:synth_128}.  Its coordinates obey
\[
\max_{0\leq j\leq p}\left|m_{t,P,x}(h)_j\right|\leq 16L(2+L).
\]
Indeed, the uniform kernel forces \(|D^{\pm}_{P,x}|\leq h\), hence \(X\in B_2(x,h)\) and \(|r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)_j|\leq1\), so the integrand is dominated pointwise by \(h^{-2}\mathbf 1\{X\in B_2(x,h)\}\{1+2Y(0)^2+2Y(1)^2\}\).  The density envelope \(f_P\leq L\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} and the area \(\pi h^2\leq4h^2\) of a planar disk give
\[
P\{X\in B_2(x,h)\}\leq 4Lh^2 .
\]
The conditional second moment comes from the moment envelope of the same definition, not from its mean and variance envelopes.  Since \(\nu\geq2\) there, the exponent obeys \(2+\nu\geq2\), so every real \(y\) satisfies
\[
y^2\leq 1+|y|^{2+\nu}
\]
(for \(|y|\leq1\) the left side is at most \(1\); for \(|y|>1\) it is at most \(|y|^{2+\nu}\)), and integrating this against the selected conditional law of \(Y(t)\) given \(X=x\) turns \(\mathbb E_P[|Y(t)|^{2+\nu}\mid X=x]\leq L\) into
\[
\mathbb E_P\bigl[Y(t)^2\mid X=x\bigr]\leq 1+L,
\qquad x\in\mathcal X_P .
\]
Multiplying this conditional bound by the localization gives \(\mathbb E_P[\mathbf 1\{X\in B_2(x,h)\}Y(t)^2]\leq (1+L)\,4Lh^2\) for each arm, so the whole integral is at most
\[
h^{-2}\bigl\{4Lh^2+4(1+L)4Lh^2\bigr\}
=
20L+16L^2
\leq
32L+16L^2
=
16L(2+L).
\]
% lean: populationScore_apply_uniform_bound
% lean: marginal_closedBall_le
% lean: localized_arm_sq_lintegral_le
The population Gram lower bound of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} and the normal equations of \cref{obj:synth_128}, written with \(\beta^*=\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\), then give
\[
L^{-1}\lVert\beta^*\rVert^2
\leq
L^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^p(\beta^*_k)^2
\leq
(\beta^*)^\top\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\beta^*
=
(\beta^*)^\top m_{t,P,x}(h)
\leq
(p+1)\lVert\beta^*\rVert\cdot 16L(2+L),
\]
the first step because \(\lVert\beta^*\rVert^2\) is one of the summands.  Hence \(\lVert\beta^*\rVert\leq L(p+1)\cdot16L(2+L)\leq R\), which is the claim.
% lean: populationCoefficient_uniform_bound_explicit

\textbf{Step 2: the bounded score class.}  For \(b\in\mathbb R^{p+1}\) let \(\Pi_R b\) be its componentwise clipping,
\[
(\Pi_R b)_k=\max\{-R,\min\{R,b_k\}\},\qquad 0\leq k\leq p,
\]
and let
\[
\mathcal I_{P,h}
=
\left\{
\iota=(q,t,x,b,j):
q\in[h,2h),\ t\in\{0,1\},\ x\in\mathcal B_P,\ b\in\mathbb R^{p+1},\ 0\leq j\leq p
\right\}.
\]
For \(\iota=(q,t,x,b,j)\in\mathcal I_{P,h}\) define the scalar function of one observation
\[
g_\iota(Z)
=
\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}
K_\square\!\left({D^{\pm}_{P,x}\over q}\right)
r_p\!\left({D^{\pm}_{P,x}\over h}\right)_j
\left\{
\psi_B(Y)-r_p\!\left({D^{\pm}_{P,x}\over h}\right)^\top\Pi_R b
\right\}.
\]
Only the kernel window uses the enlarged bandwidth \(q\); the polynomial arguments keep \(h\), so the target score is the member with \(q=h\).  The one-sided enlargement \(q\in[h,2h)\) is what makes the class countably reducible in Step 6: a bandwidth decreasing to \(h\) resolves observations sitting exactly on the closed kernel boundary.  Clipping the coefficient makes the class have a constant envelope.
% lean: separableWinsorizedScoreFunction

\textbf{Step 3: the score is a member of the class.}  Fix \(t\in\{0,1\}\), \(x\in\mathcal B_P\) and \(0\leq j\leq p\), and put \(\iota=(h,t,x,\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h),j)\in\mathcal I_{P,h}\).  By Step 1 the clipping is inactive at this index, \(\Pi_R\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)=\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\), so comparing with the definition of the winsorized score in \cref{obj:synth_131},
\[
\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h,j}
=
{1\over h^{2}}\cdot{1\over n}\sum_{i=1}^n g_\iota(Z_i).
\]
% lean: winsorizedCenteredScore_apply_eq_separableAverage
The envelope bound of Step 5 makes \(g_\iota\) integrable, so averaging the previous display over the independent and identically distributed observations gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h,j}\right]
=
{1\over h^{2}}\int g_\iota\,dP .
\]
% lean: winsorizedCenteredScore_integral_eq

\textbf{Step 4: reduction to one scalar empirical process.}  For each fixed sample the coordinate maximum \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) is attained at some \(j\), and every pair \((t,x)\) with \(x\in\mathcal B_P\) together with that \(j\) produces an index of \(\mathcal I_{P,h}\) by Step 3.  Hence, pointwise on the sample space,
\[
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\},\,x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left\lVert
\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}
-\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]
\right\rVert
\leq
{1\over h^{2}}
\sup_{\iota\in\mathcal I_{P,h}}
\left|
{1\over n}\sum_{i=1}^n g_\iota(Z_i)-\int g_\iota\,dP
\right| .
\]
% lean: winsorizedScoreDeviation_le_empiricalProcessSup
Outer expectation is monotone and pulls out the positive constant \(h^{-2}\), so
\[
\mathbb E_{P^n}^{*}\!\left[
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\},\,x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left\lVert
\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}
-\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]
\right\rVert
\right]
\leq
{1\over h^{2}}\,
\mathbb E_{P^n}^{*}\!\left[
\sup_{\iota\in\mathcal I_{P,h}}
\left|
{1\over n}\sum_{i=1}^n g_\iota(Z_i)-\int g_\iota\,dP
\right|
\right].
\]
% lean: outerLIntegral_mono
% lean: outerLIntegral_const_mul_le

\textbf{Step 5: envelope, radius and covering witnesses.}  Put
\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{env}}=(2\cdot4^p+2)\{1+(p+1)R\},
\qquad
\Lambda_{\mathrm{rad}}=1+\left|3\cdot4^p\left\{2(1+L)+\{(p+1)2^pR\}^2\right\}16L\right|,
\]
\[
C_0=1+\max\{\Lambda_{\mathrm{rad}},\Lambda_{\mathrm{env}}\}>0 .
\]
There are further constants \(A\geq\exp(1)\) and \(v\geq1\), depending only on \(p\) and \(L\), such that for every real \(\nu\), every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) and all \(0<h<B\) with \(B\geq1\) the class \(\{g_\iota:\iota\in\mathcal I_{P,h}\}\) satisfies the following three properties.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Envelope.)} For every \(\iota\in\mathcal I_{P,h}\) and every observation \(z\),
\[
|g_\iota(z)|\leq C_0B .
\]
On the kernel window \(|D^{\pm}_{P,x}|\leq q<2h\), so \(|r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)_k|\leq2^p\) for every \(k\); with \(|\psi_B(Y)|\leq B\) and \(|(\Pi_Rb)_k|\leq R\) this gives \(|g_\iota(z)|\leq(2\cdot4^p+2)\{B+(p+1)R\}\), which is at most \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{env}}B\leq C_0B\) because \(B\geq1\).
\item \textbf{(Population radius.)} For every \(\iota\in\mathcal I_{P,h}\),
\[
\left(\int g_\iota^2\,dP\right)^{1/2}\leq C_0h .
\]
The kernel window forces \(X\in B_2(x,2h)\), whose probability is at most \(4L(2h)^2=16Lh^2\) by the density envelope of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}; multiplying that localization by the conditional second-moment bound \(\mathbb E_P[Y(t)^2\mid X=x]\leq1+L\) derived in Step 1 from the moment envelope, and by the squared polynomial factors, bounds \(\int g_\iota^2\,dP\) by \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{rad}}^2h^2\leq C_0^2h^2\); the two arms are what produce the \(2(1+L)\) inside \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{rad}}\).  The winsorization level does not enter, because \(|\psi_B(Y)|\leq|Y|\).
\item \textbf{(Polynomial covering.)} For every finite list \(z_1,\ldots,z_m\) of observations and every \(0<\varepsilon\leq1\) there is a finite set \(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal I_{P,h}\) with
\[
\min_{\kappa\in\mathcal C}
\left({1\over m}\sum_{i=1}^m\{g_\iota(z_i)-g_\kappa(z_i)\}^2\right)^{1/2}
<\varepsilon\,C_0B
\quad\text{for every }\iota\in\mathcal I_{P,h},
\]
and with at most \((A/\varepsilon)^{v}\) members.
Each \(g_\iota\) is a fixed algebraic combination of an arm indicator, the indicator of a closed Euclidean ball around \(x\), finitely many monomials in \(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h\), and an affine functional of the clipped coefficient.  Planar closed balls carry finite VC complexity by \cref{obj:lem:euclidean-balls-vc}, and the packing bound for VC-subgraph families, propagated through products and finite sums, produces the displayed polynomial covering numbers with base \(A\) and exponent \(v\).
\end{itemize}
The three witnesses \(A\), \(v\), \(C_0\) are chosen before \(\nu\), \(P\), \(h\) and \(B\), which is what makes the final constant uniform in the moment exponent.
% lean: winsorizedScore_hasVCUniformEntropy_all_nu

The parameter-regime clause of \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) gives \(L\geq4\), so \(h\leq L^{-1}<1\leq B\) and \(h<B\); the certificate therefore applies at the bandwidth and clipping level of the statement.

\textbf{Step 6: countable reduction.}  Excluding the \(P\)-null set of observations whose covariate lies on \(\mathcal B_P\), which is null because the interface has finite one-dimensional Hausdorff measure by \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} and hence vanishing planar Lebesgue measure, every \(g_\iota\) is a pointwise limit of members indexed by a fixed countable dense sequence in \(\mathcal I_{P,h}\), with bandwidths decreasing to \(h\).  Consequently, for every \(n\), almost surely under \(P^n\),
\[
\sup_{\iota\in\mathcal I_{P,h}}
\left|
{1\over n}\sum_{i=1}^n g_\iota(Z_i)-\int g_\iota\,dP
\right|
=
\sup_{\kappa}
\left|
{1\over n}\sum_{i=1}^n g_{\kappa}(Z_i)-\int g_{\kappa}\,dP
\right| ,
\]
the right supremum running over that countable sequence.
% lean: winsorizedScore_hasCountableEmpiricalSupReduction_at

\textbf{Step 7: the variance-adaptive maximal inequality.}  Write
\[
\ell=\log\left(\max\left\{\exp(1),\ {A\,C_0B\over C_0h}\right\}\right).
\]
For a countably reducible class with envelope \(C_0B\), population \(L^2(P)\) radius \(C_0h\), and polynomial covering witnesses \((A,v)\) as in Step 5, symmetrization, chaining over the polynomial cover, and the localized envelope bound give, for every \(n\geq1\),
\[
\mathbb E_{P^n}^{*}\!\left[
\sup_{\iota\in\mathcal I_{P,h}}
\left|
{1\over n}\sum_{i=1}^n g_\iota(Z_i)-\int g_\iota\,dP
\right|
\right]
\leq
c_{\mathrm{VC}}
\left\{
C_0h\sqrt{{v\,\ell\over n}}
+
{v\,C_0B\,\ell\over n}
\right\},
\qquad
c_{\mathrm{VC}}=16384 .
\]
% lean: vcExpectedMaximalInequality_explicit

\textbf{Step 8: calibrating the logarithm.}  Since \(L\geq4\), \(h\leq L^{-1}\) and \(B\geq1\),
\[
4h\leq Lh\leq1\leq B,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
{B\over h}\geq4
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\log(B/h)\geq1 .
\]
Also \(A\geq\exp(1)\) gives \(\log A\geq1\), so \(A(B/h)\geq\exp(1)\) and the maximum in \(\ell\) is attained at the second entry.  Putting \(k=1+\log A\),
\[
\ell=\log A+\log(B/h)\leq k\log(B/h),
\]
because \(\{\log A-1\}\{\log(B/h)-1\}\geq0\).
% lean: cty_winsorized_score_maximal_bound

\textbf{Step 9: conclusion.}  Abbreviate
\[
\Gamma_1=\sqrt{{\log(B/h)\over nh^{2}}},
\qquad
\Gamma_2={B\log(B/h)\over nh^{2}},
\]
both nonnegative.  Multiplying the two terms of Step 7 by \(h^{-2}\) and using Step 8,
\[
{1\over h^{2}}\,C_0h\sqrt{{v\,\ell\over n}}
\leq
C_0\sqrt{vk}\;{1\over h}\sqrt{{\log(B/h)\over n}}
=
C_0\sqrt{vk}\,\Gamma_1,
\qquad
{1\over h^{2}}\cdot{v\,C_0B\,\ell\over n}
\leq
C_0vk\,\Gamma_2 .
\]
Therefore, chaining Steps 4, 6, 7 and the two displays above and setting
\[
C=1+\left|c_{\mathrm{VC}}C_0\left\{\sqrt{vk}+vk\right\}\right|,
\]
we obtain
\[
\mathbb E_{P^n}^{*}\!\left[
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\},\,x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left\lVert
\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}
-\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]
\right\rVert
\right]
\leq
c_{\mathrm{VC}}C_0\left\{\sqrt{vk}+vk\right\}(\Gamma_1+\Gamma_2)
\leq
C\left\{
\sqrt{\frac{\log(B/h)}{n h^2}}
+
\frac{B\log(B/h)}{n h^2}
\right\}.
\]
The constant \(C\) is positive and, since \(R\), \(C_0\), \(A\) and \(v\) were fixed from \(p\) and \(L\) alone, depends only on \(p\) and \(L\).
% lean: cty_winsorized_score_maximal_bound
\end{proof}

The two terms in \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound} have the usual variance and envelope forms for bounded empirical processes indexed by VC-type sets \citep{Pollard1984,VanderVaart1996}. The scaling \(h^{-2}\) reflects the two-dimensional local window around an interface point, while the supremum over \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(x\in\mathcal B_P\) matches the signed-distance risk criterion in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. Together with the Gram lower bound in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, the statement controls the stochastic perturbation of the intercepts in the stabilized estimator.

These ingredients enter the upper-bound calculation as follows. The population part is separated into the first-order local-polynomial approximation term and the winsorization term from \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorization-bias}. The sample part is decomposed into empirical Gram fluctuation and centered score fluctuation, with the score controlled by \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound} and the Gram term handled by the expected maximal condition stated in \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}. The stabilization rule in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} converts these uniform matrix and score bounds into a uniform intercept bound for each arm, and clipping keeps the treatment-effect estimate on the envelope scale.

At the bandwidth \(h_n=a_n\) from \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates} and winsorization level \(B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\), the first-order bias, winsorization bias, and stochastic terms in \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper} are all bounded by a constant multiple of \(a_n\) for sufficiently large \(n\). Thus the auxiliary results in this appendix support the expected outer-risk upper bound for the explicit signed-distance local-polynomial estimator, completing the upper side of the matched-rate comparison stated in \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier}.







\section{C: Signed-Distance Hypercube and Matched Rate}
\label{sec:app-signed-hypercube}

This appendix records the hard signed-distance subexperiment used for the lower side of the matched-rate statement. The construction fixes the known rectangular geometry from \cref{obj:synth_126} and places many separated local perturbations along the bottom edge of the treatment boundary. Each perturbation changes the treatment-effect target at one boundary point by a calibrated amount, while the corresponding one-observation signed-distance laws remain close enough for the overlap direct-product argument in \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product} to apply.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}[Rectangle Angular Hypercube]
For every integer \(p\geq 0\), there is a constant \(L_0=L_0(p)\geq 48\) such that, for every \(\nu\geq 2\) and every \(L\geq L_0\), there are constants \(c,A,C,C_0>0\), with \(2C<A\) when \(p=0\), and a constant \(\delta_0>0\) such that the following construction is available for every \(0<\Delta\leq\delta_0\).  Writing \(q=p+1\), there exist an integer \(M\), constants \(w,\rho>0\), points \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\), laws \(P_\omega\) indexed by \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\), and probability laws \(Q_{j,b}\) for \(j=1,\ldots,M\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\), satisfying:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Size and calibration.)}
\[

\item \textbf{(Common geometry.)} The support is \(\mathcal X=[-3,3]^2\), the treated region is \(\mathcal A_1=[-1,1]\times[0,2]\), the control region is \(\mathcal A_0=\mathcal X\setminus\mathcal A_1\), the boundary is \(\mathcal B=\partial\mathcal A_1\), the metric is Euclidean distance, and the kernel is \(K_\square\). \[

\]
The restriction of \(P_\omega\) to \(S_j\) depends on \(\omega\) only through \(\omega_j\), and the restriction of \(P_\omega\) outside \(\bigcup_{j=1}^M S_j\) is common across all \(\omega\).
\item \textbf{(Signed-observation laws.)} Conditional on \(\operatorname{score}(Z)\in S_j\), the one-observation signed-distance law at \(x_j\) under \(P_\omega\) is \(Q_{j,\omega_j}\).

\[

\]

% lean: causalHardHypercubeAt_of_constructorData A1A2HypercubeAt cty_a1_a2_rectangle_angular_hypercube
\end{proof}

The constants in \cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders} separate geometry from signal size. The radius \(w=A\Delta^{1/q}\) gives room for a \(C^{p+1}\) perturbation of height \(\Delta\), and the packing size \(M\gtrsim \Delta^{-1/q}\) supplies enough boundary coordinates to generate the logarithmic uniform-risk scale. The common rectangular geometry places every \(P_\omega\) inside the same fixed-known-geometry subexperiment, while the bit structure makes each disk a coordinate of the hypercube. The local comparison clause supplies the testing input: after \(n\) observations, the effective information in a coordinate is calibrated by the cell probability \(\rho\) and the Kullback--Leibler bounds.

To obtain \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders}, set the hypercube signal to the risk scale \(\Delta\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/4}\). Since \(q=p+1\), the calibration in \cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders} gives \(M\gtrsim \Delta^{-1/q}\), \(w=A\Delta^{1/q}\), and \(\rho=\pi w^2/36\). The comparison laws then satisfy
\[
n\rho\,\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
\lesssim
n w^2 \frac{\Delta^4}{w^2}
=
n\Delta^4
\asymp
\log n,
\]
with the same order for the reverse divergence. This calibration keeps the coordinatewise overlap controlled while the maximum over the packed boundary points contributes the logarithmic factor. Applying \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product} to the signed-distance observations \(U^{\pm}_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\) converts the overlap into a lower bound for recovering the hypercube coordinates, and the signal-separation clause converts that testing loss into the interface sup-loss in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. Because all vertices lie in the fixed rectangular subexperiment, the conclusion applies both to \(R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)\) from \cref{obj:synth_126} and to the full risk \(R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\).

The matched statement follows by combining the lower and upper components already stated in the main text. \Cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders} gives the positive lower constant for the signed-distance risk after the envelope threshold \(L_0(p)\) is met. \Cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper} gives a uniform outer-expected upper bound of order \(a_n\) for the explicit winsorized, Gram-stabilized local-polynomial estimator with bandwidth \(h_n=a_n\) and winsorization level \(B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\). Therefore \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier} characterizes the displayed known-geometry signed-distance minimax risk on the normalization \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), with the estimator in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} attaining the upper side of the rate over \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\).

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders}]
\emph{Step 1 (constants).}
Fix \(p\geq 0\) and put \(q=p+1\).  Let \(L_0=L_0(p)\) be the envelope threshold
supplied by \cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}, so
\(L_0\geq 48\).  Fix \(\nu\geq 2\) and \(L\geq L_0\), and let
\(c_\square,A,C,C_0,\delta_0\) be the positive constants that lemma attaches to
\((p,\nu,L)\), with \(C=128\) and \(C_0=262144\).  Set
\[
z=128\,q\,C_0,\qquad
\gamma=\min\{1,z^{-1}\},\qquad
d=\frac12\bigl\{1-\exp(-1/2)\bigr\},\qquad
c=\frac{\gamma d}{8}.
\]
Then \(\gamma>0\), \(d>0\) and \(c>0\).  Since \(0<\gamma\leq 1\) we have
\(\gamma^4\leq\gamma\), and \(\gamma\leq z^{-1}\) therefore gives the budget
inequality
\[
128\,q\,C_0\,\gamma^4\leq 128\,q\,C_0\,\gamma= z\gamma\leq 1 .
\]
% lean: a1a2_finite_max_eventually_lower

\emph{Step 2 (the logarithmic packing budget).}
We first record, as an eventual statement in \(n\), the calibration that will
put the hypercube of \cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}
inside the range of \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}.  For
\(n\geq2\) the rate \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) of \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates}
satisfies \(n\,a_n^4=\log n\), so the budget inequality of Step 1 gives
\[
C_0\,n\,(\gamma a_n)^4
=C_0\gamma^4\,n\,a_n^4
=C_0\gamma^4\log n
\leq\frac{\log n}{128\,q}.
\]
Also \(\log a_n=\frac14(\log\log n-\log n)\), so every integer \(M\) with
\(M\geq c_\square(\gamma a_n)^{-1/q}\) obeys
\[
\log M
\geq
\log c_\square-\frac1q\log\gamma
-\frac1{4q}\bigl(\log\log n-\log n\bigr).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\) we have \(\log\log n\leq\frac12\log n\), whence
\(-\frac1{4q}(\log\log n-\log n)\geq\frac1{8q}\log n\); and, since
\(\log n\to\infty\), also
\(\log c_\square-\frac1q\log\gamma\geq-\frac1{32q}\log n\).  For such \(n\) the
last display yields
\[
\log M\geq-\frac1{32q}\log n+\frac1{8q}\log n\geq\frac1{32q}\log n .
\]
Combining the two estimates, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every integer
\(M\geq c_\square(\gamma a_n)^{-1/q}\),
\[
C_0\,n\,(\gamma a_n)^4
\leq\frac{\log n}{128\,q}
=\frac14\cdot\frac{\log n}{32\,q}
\leq\frac14\log M .
\]
% lean: causalScaledFrontier_eventually_klBudget

The de-Poissonization remainder \(\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}\) decays exponentially in
\(n\), while \(a_n\) decays only polynomially, so
\(\exp\{-n(1-\log 2)\}=o(a_n)\).
% lean: poisson_remainder_isLittleO_frontier
Since \(a_n\to0\), we may therefore fix \(N\geq2\) such that, for every \(n\geq N\),
\[
\gamma a_n\leq\delta_0,
\qquad
2L\exp\{-n(1-\log 2)\}\leq c\,a_n,
\]
and the displayed logarithmic packing budget holds.

\emph{Step 3 (the hard family at scale \(\Delta\)).}
Fix \(n\geq N\) and a known-geometry point-indexed rule
\(\widehat\tau_n\in\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n\), and choose a family of Borel
sections \(\{T_{n,G,x}\}\) representing it as in
\cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-decision-class}.  Put
\(\Delta=\gamma a_n\), so \(0<\Delta\leq\delta_0\).  Applying
\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders} at separation
\(\Delta\) produces an integer \(M\), a radius \(w=A\Delta^{1/q}\), the disk
mass \(\rho=\pi w^2/36>0\), centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) on the middle of the
bottom edge with pairwise disjoint disks \(S_j=B_2(x_j,w)\subseteq[-3,3]^2\),
laws \(P_\omega\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) indexed by \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\)
all carrying the same fixed rectangular geometry
\[
\mathcal X=[-3,3]^2,\quad
\mathcal A_1=[-1,1]\times[0,2],\quad
\mathcal A_0=\mathcal X\setminus\mathcal A_1,\quad
\mathcal B=\partial\mathcal A_1,
\]
and conditional signed-distance laws \(Q_{j,b}\) with
\(M\geq c_\square\Delta^{-1/q}\).  In particular \(M\geq 1\), because
\(c_\square\Delta^{-1/q}>0\).
% lean: cty_a1_a2_rectangle_angular_hypercube

\emph{Step 4 (per-coordinate divergence budget).}
The Kullback--Leibler clause of
\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}, the identity
\(\rho=\pi w^2/36\), and \(\pi\leq4\) give, for every \(j\),
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\cdot 2n\rho
\leq
\frac{C_0\Delta^4}{w^2}\cdot 2n\,\frac{\pi w^2}{36}
=
C_0\,n\,\Delta^4\cdot\frac{\pi}{18}
\leq
C_0\,n\,\Delta^4
\leq
\frac14\log M ,
\]
the last inequality by Step 2 applied with this \(M\), which satisfies
\(M\geq c_\square(\gamma a_n)^{-1/q}\).
% lean: cty_a1_a2_rectangle_angular_hypercube, a1a2_finite_max_eventually_lower

\emph{Step 5 (target values at the centers).}
For \(j\leq M\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\) let \(\epsilon(j,b)\in\{0,1\}^M\) be the
vertex with \(j\)th coordinate \(b\) and all other coordinates \(0\), and put
\[
v_{j,b}=\tau_{P_{\epsilon(j,b)}}(x_j).
\]
Because the treatment-effect target at \(x_j\) depends on the vertex only
through its \(j\)th coordinate, and because flipping that coordinate moves the
target at \(x_j\) by exactly \(\Delta\)
(\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}),
\[
\tau_{P_\omega}(x_j)=v_{j,\omega_j}
\qquad\text{for every }\omega,
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|v_{j,1}-v_{j,0}|=\Delta .
\]
% lean: A1A2HypercubeAt, cty_a1_a2_rectangle_angular_hypercube

\emph{Step 6 (the Poissonized experiment).}
For a probability law \(Q\) on a standard Borel space and \(\lambda\geq0\), write
\(\Pi_\lambda(Q)\) for the law of the finite marked configuration obtained by
drawing \(N\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)\) points independently from \(Q\),
attaching to each point an independent mark uniform on \((0,1]\), and listing
the points in increasing order of their marks; the marks are independent of the
points and almost surely distinct, so they only fix a canonical ordering.  For
a configuration \(s\) write \(|s|\) for its number of points and, when
\(|s|\geq n\), write \((Y_1,X_1),\ldots,(Y_n,X_n)\) for its first \(n\) points.
Let \(G_0\) be the common known geometry of the hypercube, which is the same
tuple for every vertex because all \(P_\omega\) share
\(\mathcal X,\mathcal A_0,\mathcal A_1,\mathcal B\), the Euclidean metric and
\(K_\square\).  Define the Poissonized value of the rule at a point \(x\) by
\[
\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x;s)
=
\begin{cases}
T_{n,G_0,x}\bigl((Y_i,D^{\pm}_{x,i}):1\leq i\leq n\bigr),&|s|\geq n,\\
0,&|s|<n,
\end{cases}
\qquad
D^{\pm}_{x,i}=\bigl\{\mathbf 1(X_i\in\mathcal A_1)-\mathbf 1(X_i\in\mathcal A_0)\bigr\}
\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2 ,
\]
and the Poissonized maximum loss at a vertex \(\omega\) by
\[
\Lambda_\omega(s)=\max_{1\leq j\leq M}
\bigl|\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;s)-v_{j,\omega_j}\bigr| .
\]
Both are measurable, each section \(T_{n,G_0,x_j}\) being Borel.
% lean: globalCausalPackingPoissonValue, globalCausalPackingPoissonLoss

\emph{Step 7 (independent blocks and their compressions).}
Split a configuration drawn from \(\Pi_{2n}(P_\omega)\) according to the
partition of \([-3,3]^2\) into the pairwise disjoint disks \(S_1,\ldots,S_M\)
and their complement.  Because thinning a marked Poisson configuration by a
measurable partition produces independent marked Poisson configurations, the
blocks
\[
Z_j=\{\text{points with }\operatorname{score}\in S_j\}\ (1\leq j\leq M),
\qquad
Z_0=\Bigl\{\text{points with }\operatorname{score}\notin\bigcup_{k=1}^M S_k\Bigr\}
\]
are independent, \(Z_j\sim\Pi_{2n\rho}\bigl(P_\omega(\,\cdot\mid \operatorname{score}\in S_j)\bigr)\),
and \(Z_0\) is the corresponding marked Poisson configuration built from the
normalized law of \(P_\omega\) off \(\bigcup_{k}S_k\) at the complementary
intensity.  By the bit-structure clause of
\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders} the law of
\(Z_j\) depends on \(\omega\) only through \(\omega_j\), each disk having the
same mass \(\rho\), while the law of \(Z_0\) does not depend on \(\omega\) at
all.
% lean: causalPackingExperiment_synthesis_law

Compress the \(j\)th block by replacing each of its points by its signed
observation at \(x_j\) under the common geometry, keeping its mark; the
compressed blocks \(\widetilde S_1,\ldots,\widetilde S_M\) are the compressed
coordinates required by \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}:
\[
\widetilde S_j=c_j(Z_j),
\qquad
c_j:(Y,X,\text{mark})\longmapsto
\bigl(Y,\ \{\mathbf 1(X\in\mathcal A_1)-\mathbf 1(X\in\mathcal A_0)\}
\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2,\ \text{mark}\bigr).
\]
By the signed-observation clause of
\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}, the point law
of the compressed \(j\)th block at bit \(b\) is exactly \(Q_{j,b}\), so
\[
\mathcal L(\widetilde S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)=\Pi_{2n\rho}(Q_{j,b}),
\qquad b\in\{0,1\}.
\]
% lean: compressedCausalPackingCellExperiment_eq
Poisson tensorization gives
\(\operatorname{KL}\) equal to \(2n\rho\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\) for
the two unordered marked configurations, and mark-ordering is a measurable map,
which cannot increase divergence; hence, with Step 4,
\[
\operatorname{KL}\bigl(\mathcal L(\widetilde S_j\mid\Omega_j=0),\
\mathcal L(\widetilde S_j\mid\Omega_j=1)\bigr)
\leq
2n\rho\,\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
\leq
\frac14\log M .
\]
% lean: klDiv_finiteMeasureMarkedPoissonLaw, compressedCoordinateLaw_klDiv_le

\emph{Step 8 (direct-product testing bound).}
Let \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) be independent uniform bits, so that the
vertex \(\Omega=(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M)\) is uniform on \(\{0,1\}^M\) and the
observed configuration is drawn from \(\Pi_{2n}(P_\Omega)\).  Attach to
coordinate \(j\) the midpoint decoder
\[
\widehat\Omega_j
=\mathbf 1\bigl\{|\,\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;\cdot)-v_{j,1}|
\leq|\,\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;\cdot)-v_{j,0}|\bigr\}.
\]
The configuration feeding \(\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;\cdot)\) is obtained by
superposing the compressed blocks \(c_j(Z_k)\), \(k\neq j\), the compressed
ancillary block \(c_j(Z_0)\), and the already compressed block
\(\widetilde S_j\); the raw \(j\)th block therefore enters only through
\(\widetilde S_j\).  Hence \(\widehat\Omega_j\) is a measurable function of
\((\widetilde S_j,(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M),Z_0)\) that is unchanged when the raw
coordinate vector is altered only in coordinate \(j\) while
\((\widetilde S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\) is held fixed.
% lean: causalPackingPoissonDecoder_local
All hypotheses of \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product} are thus
met, and its Kullback--Leibler consequence with \(\kappa=1/4\), fed by Step 7,
gives
\[
\Pr\{\text{at least one decoding error}\}
\geq
\frac12\bigl\{1-\exp(-M^{3/4}/2)\bigr\}
\geq
\frac12\bigl\{1-\exp(-1/2)\bigr\}
=d,
\]
the middle inequality because \(M\geq1\) forces \(M^{3/4}\geq1\).
% lean: causalPackingCoordinatewiseError_lower_bound

\emph{Step 9 (from decoding error to loss, and averaging).}
If the midpoint decoder is wrong at coordinate \(j\) then the estimate is at
least as close to the wrong value as to the true one, so the separation
\(|v_{j,1}-v_{j,0}|=\Delta\) of Step 5 forces
\[
\widehat\Omega_j\neq\omega_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bigl|\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;s)-v_{j,\omega_j}\bigr|\geq\frac{\Delta}{2}.
\]
% lean: midpointDecoder_wrong_bit_error
Consequently, for every vertex \(\omega\),
\[
\frac{\Delta}{2}\,
\Pr_{P_\omega}\bigl\{\exists\,j:\widehat\Omega_j\neq\omega_j\bigr\}
\leq
\mathbb E_{\Pi_{2n}(P_\omega)}\bigl[\Lambda_\omega\bigr].
\]
Averaging this inequality over the \(2^M\) vertices and bounding the average of
the right-hand side by its maximum selects a vertex \(\omega\) with
\[
\frac{\Delta}{2}\cdot
2^{-M}\sum_{\omega'\in\{0,1\}^M}
\Pr_{P_{\omega'}}\bigl\{\exists\,j:\widehat\Omega_j\neq\omega'_j\bigr\}
\leq
\mathbb E_{\Pi_{2n}(P_{\omega})}\bigl[\Lambda_{\omega}\bigr].
\]
% lean: exists_vertex_causalPoissonLoss_ge_coordinatewiseError
The left factor is the average decoding-error probability bounded below in
Step 8, and \((\Delta/2)d=(\gamma a_n/2)d=4c\,a_n\) by the definition of \(c\).
Therefore
\[
4c\,a_n\leq\mathbb E_{\Pi_{2n}(P_{\omega})}\bigl[\Lambda_{\omega}\bigr].
\]

\emph{Step 10 (de-Poissonization).}
Write \(P=P_{\omega}\).  By Step 3, \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) and carries
the fixed rectangular geometry, so \(G_P=G_0\) and each center lies on
\(\mathcal B_P=\partial([-1,1]\times[0,2])\).  Define the finite packing loss of
the rule on a sample \(W\) of size \(n\) by
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}(W)
=\max_{1\leq j\leq M}
\bigl|\widehat\tau_{n,P}(x_j)-\tau_P(x_j)\bigr| ,
\]
which is measurable in \(W\) because each section \(T_{n,G_P,x_j}\) is Borel and
\(W\mapsto U^{\pm}_{n,P}(x_j)\) is measurable.
% lean: causalFinitePackingLoss, causalFinitePackingLoss_measurable
On the event \(\{|s|\geq n\}\) the Poissonized value at \(x_j\) is the value of
the rule on the retained prefix, because \(G_0=G_P\) makes the two signed
observations at \(x_j\) coincide, and \(\tau_P(x_j)=v_{j,\omega_j}\) by Step 5;
hence
\[
\Lambda_{\omega}(s)=\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\bigl((Y_i,X_i)_{i\leq n}\bigr)
\qquad\text{whenever }|s|\geq n .
\]
% lean: globalCausalPackingPoissonLoss_eq_finitePackingLoss
On the complementary event the Poissonized value is \(0\), so
\(\Lambda_{\omega}(s)\leq\max_j|v_{j,\omega_j}|\); and since the two
potential-outcome regressions of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} satisfy
\(\sup_{x\in\mathcal X_P}|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\), the target
\(\tau_P=\mu_{1,P}-\mu_{0,P}\) of \cref{obj:synth_46} obeys
\[
|v_{j,\omega_j}|=|\tau_P(x_j)|\leq 2L .
\]
% lean: causalEuclideanCExtEnvelope_abs_le
Conditionally on \(\{|s|\geq n\}\) the first \(n\) points of the configuration
are i.i.d.\ from \(P\), and \(\Pr\{\mathrm{Pois}(2n)<n\}\leq\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}\),
so splitting the Poissonized expectation over the two events gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\Pi_{2n}(P)}\bigl[\Lambda_{\omega}\bigr]
\leq
\mathbb E_{P^n}\bigl[\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\bigr]
+2L\exp\{-n(1-\log 2)\}.
\]
% lean: globalCausalPackingPoissonRisk_le_fixedRisk_add_tail, poisson_two_n_lower_tail
By the choice of \(N\) in Step 2 the second term is at most \(c\,a_n\).
Combining with the lower bound \(4c\,a_n\) of Step 9 and weakening \(4c\,a_n\)
to \(2c\,a_n\),
\[
2c\,a_n\leq \mathbb E_{P^n}\bigl[\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\bigr]+c\,a_n,
\]
and cancelling the finite common term \(c\,a_n\) yields
\[
c\,a_n\leq \mathbb E_{P^n}\bigl[\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\bigr].
\]

\emph{Step 11 (passage to the two risks).}
Each \(x_j\) lies on \(\mathcal B_P\), so pointwise in the sample
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}
\leq
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\bigl|\widehat\tau_{n,P}(x)-\tau_P(x)\bigr| ,
\]
and the ordinary expectation of the measurable left-hand side is at most the
outer expectation of the right-hand side.  Hence
\[
c\,a_n
\leq
\mathbb E_P^*\Bigl[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\bigl|\widehat\tau_{n,P}(x)-\tau_P(x)\bigr|\Bigr].
\]
% lean: causalFinitePackingLoss_le_boundaryLoss, lintegral_le_outerLIntegral_of_measurable, causalOuterLIntegral_mono
Since \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\), taking the supremum over the law class
and then the infimum over \(\widehat\tau_n\in\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n\) gives, by
\cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk},
\[
c\,a_n\leq R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)
\qquad\text{for every }n\geq N .
\]
Since \(P\) also satisfies the fixed hard-geometry condition, the same two
steps taken inside the restricted supremum of \cref{obj:synth_126} give
\[
c\,a_n\leq R_n^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}(p,\nu,L)
\qquad\text{for every }n\geq N .
\]
% lean: a1a2OuterRisk_eventually_lower

\emph{Step 12 (normalization and liminf).}
For \(n\geq2\) we have \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}>0\), hence
\[
a_n^{-1}R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)
=
\frac{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}.
\]
The two sequences are eventually equal, so their lower limits agree, which is
the first asserted identity.
% lean: scaledRisk_eventually_eq_normalizedRisk
Dividing the two eventual inequalities of Step 11 by \(a_n>0\) gives, for every
\(n\geq\max\{N,2\}\),
\[
c\leq\frac{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n},
\qquad
c\leq\frac{R_n^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n},
\]
and the order property of \(\liminf\) under an eventual lower bound yields
\[
c\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n},
\qquad
c\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}.
\]
This proves the theorem. % lean: cty_a1_a2_point_indexed_log_converse
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse}]
Write \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item By \cref{obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse}, applied with the same \(q\) and \(L\), choose \(c_{\mathrm{PI}}>0\) such that
\[
c_{\mathrm{PI}}
\leq
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}.
\]
Set \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}=c_{\mathrm{PI}}\).
% lean: point_indexed_distance_log_converse

\item The two normalizations of the common-map risk agree eventually. Indeed, for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(a_n>0\) and
\[
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4}R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
=
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n},
\]
because \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) is the reciprocal of \((n/\log n)^{1/4}\).  Taking liminfs of these eventually equal sequences gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4}R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}.
\]
% lean: scaledRisk_eventually_eq_normalizedRisk

\item \emph{For each \(n\), the common-map risk dominates the point-indexed risk:}
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}.
\]
Two facts combine to give this.

First, a common map in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class} is a point-indexed rule in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class}: take the same Borel section \(T_{n,x}=T_n\) at every boundary point, as recorded by \cref{obj:lem:common-map-strict-in-point-indexed}.  Hence the infimum defining \(R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) runs over a superset of the rules defining \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).
% lean: ctyClass_subset_piClass

Second, for a common-map rule the two expectation conventions coincide term by term.  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) and a common map \(T_n\), and write the boundary loss at the sample point \(w\) as
\[
\Lambda_P(w)=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
\bigl|T_n\bigl(V_{n,P}(x)\big|_{w}\bigr)-\mu_P(x)\bigr| .
\]
The map
\[
(x,w)\longmapsto
\bigl|T_n\bigl(V_{n,P}(x)\big|_{w}\bigr)-\mu_P(x)\bigr|,
\qquad
(x,w)\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\times(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2)^n,
\]
is jointly Borel: \(T_n\) is measurable, \((x,w)\mapsto V_{n,P}(x)|_{w}\) is continuous in \(x\) and Borel in \(w\), and \(\mu_P\) is continuous on the compact set \(\mathcal X_P\supseteq\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\) because it lies in the Hölder ball of \cref{obj:synth_36}.
% lean: commonMapBoundarySup_jointlyBorel
Consequently, for every level \(a\),
\[
\{w:\Lambda_P(w)>a\}
=
\Bigl\{w:\exists\,x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P),\ \bigl|T_n\bigl(V_{n,P}(x)\big|_{w}\bigr)-\mu_P(x)\bigr|>a\Bigr\}
\]
is the projection onto the sample coordinate of a Borel subset of the product of the Polish space \(\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\) with the sample space, hence an analytic set.
% lean: commonMapBoundarySup_upperSemianalytic
Analytic sets are universally measurable, so every superlevel set of \(\Lambda_P\) is measurable for the completion of the product law generated by \(P\); therefore \(\Lambda_P\) is measurable on that completed space and its completed integral coincides with its outer expectation,
\[
\int \Lambda_P\,d\overline{P^{\,n}}
=
\mathbb E_P^*\left[\sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}\bigl|\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_P(x)\bigr|\right].
\]
% lean: commonMapBoundarySup_completionMeasurable, ctyBoundarySup_completed_eq_outer

Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) in the last display and then the infimum over the larger point-indexed class in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} bounds \(R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) above by the common-map risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk}, which is the displayed inequality.  Dividing by the same nonnegative normalizing factor preserves this order:
\[
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}
\leq
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}.
\]
% lean: ctyRisk_ge_pointIndexedRisk

\item Applying monotonicity of liminf to the preceding pointwise normalized-risk inequality gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}
\leq
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}.
\]
Combining this with the choice of \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}\) in the first step yields
\[
c_{\mathrm{CTY}}
\leq
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n},
\]
and the equality of normalizations from the second step gives the displayed conclusion.
% lean: cty_same_class_log_converse
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse}]
Fix \(q\geq 1\) and \(L\geq 4\), and write \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item
\emph{A finite packing lower bound, uniform over point-indexed rules.}
Let \(c_0,c_1,c_w^-,c_w^+,c_{\mathrm{rad}},\alpha>0\) with \(\alpha<1/8\) be the constants of \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing}, and put
\[
d=\frac12\left(1-e^{-1/2}\right)>0,
\qquad
c=\frac{c_1d}{4}>0 .
\]
Take \(n\) beyond the threshold of that lemma, and let \(M_n\), \(w_n\), \(m_n\), the boundary points \(x_1,\ldots,x_{M_n}\), the laws \(P_\omega\), and the values \(u_{j,0},u_{j,1}\) be the objects it supplies, with \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\) the common covariate support.  Two of its clauses are used at once: \(M_n\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}>0\), so \(M_n\geq1\); and \(m_n>0\), because \(C_j\) contains a relatively open neighbourhood of the support point \(x_j\) inside \(S\), which therefore has positive covariate mass, and that mass is \(m_n\).
% lean: cty_support_boundary_angular_packing, class_pos_on_relopen

Fix a point-indexed rule \(\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) and law-independent Borel sections \(\{T_{n,x}\}\) representing it as in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class}.

\emph{The Poissonized product experiment.}  For a vertex \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^{M_n}\), let \(N\) be Poisson with mean \(2n\), independent of an i.i.d.\ stream of observations from \(P_\omega\), and attach to each observation an independent mark uniform on \((0,1]\); list the resulting \(N\) marked observations in increasing mark order.  Split this configuration along the partition of \(S\) into the cells \(C_1,\ldots,C_{M_n}\) and their complement, and write
\[
Z_j=\text{the sub-configuration with covariate in }C_j\ (1\le j\le M_n),
\qquad
Z_0=\text{the sub-configuration with covariate in }S\setminus\bigcup_{k}C_k .
\]
Splitting a Poisson configuration along a measurable partition makes the blocks independent, so \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_{M_n}\) are independent; \(Z_j\) is a marked Poisson configuration with mean \(2nm_n\) drawn from the law of \((Y,X)\) under \(P_\omega\) conditioned on \(X\in C_j\), and \(Z_0\) is a marked Poisson configuration with mean \(2n(1-M_nm_n)\) drawn from the conditional law off the cells.  By the equal-cell-mass, one-bit-locality and common-outside clauses of \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing}, the law of \(Z_j\) depends on \(\omega\) only through \(\omega_j\), and the law of \(Z_0\) does not depend on \(\omega\).
% lean: packingFinitePartition, packingCellExperiment, packingCommonExperiment, packingExperiment_synthesis_law, synthesizePackingConfiguration

\emph{The compressions and the decoders.}  The compressed blocks required by \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product} are written \(\widetilde S_1,\ldots,\widetilde S_{M_n}\) here, the plain letter \(S\) being reserved for the covariate support; let
\[
\widetilde S_j=c_j(Z_j),
\qquad
c_j:\ \bigl((y,z),\text{mark}\bigr)\longmapsto\bigl((y,\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2),\text{mark}\bigr),
\]
so that \(\widetilde S_j\) records, for each atom of the \(j\)th cell block, its outcome, its distance from \(x_j\), and its mark.
% lean: packingMarkedDistance, compressPackingCell
For coordinate \(j\), superpose in mark order the compressed own block \(\widetilde S_j\), the images under \(c_j\) of the other cell blocks \(\{Z_k\}_{k\neq j}\), and the image under \(c_j\) of the complement block \(Z_0\); call the result \(s_j\), and set
\[
\widehat u_j
=
\begin{cases}
T_{n,x_j}\bigl(\text{first \(n\) atoms of }s_j\text{ in mark order}\bigr), & \text{if }s_j\text{ has at least \(n\) atoms},\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\qquad
\widehat\Omega_j=\mathbf 1\bigl\{|\widehat u_j-u_{j,1}|\le|\widehat u_j-u_{j,0}|\bigr\}.
\]
% lean: assemblePackingDistanceBlocks, packingPoissonValue, packingPoissonDecoder
Each \(\widehat\Omega_j\) is a measurable \(\{0,1\}\)-valued function of \((\widetilde S_j,(Z_1,\ldots,Z_{M_n}),Z_0)\), and it is unchanged when the raw block \(Z_j\) is altered while \((\widetilde S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\) is held fixed, because \(Z_j\) enters \(s_j\) only through \(\widetilde S_j\).
% lean: packingPoissonDecoder_measurable, packingPoissonDecoder_local

\emph{The coordinatewise divergence budget.}  Write \(Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(\widetilde S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)\) as in \cref{obj:synth_25}.  Then
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\le 2\alpha\log M_n
\qquad\text{for every }j .
\]
Indeed, \(\widetilde S_j\) is exactly the block of radii at most \(w_n\) of the marked Poisson experiment generated by the one-observation outcome-distance law at \(x_j\); restricting to that block is a measurable map, so it cannot increase divergence, and a marked Poisson experiment of mean \(2n\) has divergence at most twice that of the corresponding \(n\)-fold product experiment, which is the compressed-distance divergence bounded by \(\alpha\log M_n\) in \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing}.
% lean: compressedPackingCellExperiment_eq_shortRadiusBlock, compressedCoordinateLaw_klDiv_le, markedPoissonKL_le_two_mul_of_piKL, compressedPackingCellExperiment_klDiv_le

\emph{The direct-product step.}  The blocks \((Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_{M_n})\), the compressions \(c_j\), and the decoders \(\widehat\Omega_j\) satisfy all the hypotheses of \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}, which applies with \(\kappa=2\alpha<1/4\) by the previous display and gives
\[
\Pr\{\text{at least one decoding error}\}
\ge
\frac12\left[1-\exp\{-M_n^{1-2\alpha}/2\}\right]
\ge d,
\]
the last inequality because \(M_n\ge1\) and \(1-2\alpha\ge0\) give \(M_n^{1-2\alpha}\ge1\).
% lean: coordinatewise_overlap_direct_product, packingCoordinatewiseError_lower_bound

\emph{From decoding errors to loss, and choice of a vertex.}  If \(\widehat\Omega_j\ne\omega_j\), then the nearest-value decoder has picked the wrong endpoint, so the triangle inequality and the signal separation \(|u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|\ge c_1a_n\) give
\[
\bigl|\widehat u_j-u_{j,\omega_j}\bigr|\ \ge\ \frac{c_1a_n}{2}.
\]
% lean: midpointDecoder_wrong_bit_error, finitePackingLoss_ge_of_midpointDecoder_wrong
Multiplying the previous two displays and averaging over the uniform prior on \(\{0,1\}^{M_n}\) selects a vertex \(\omega\) such that, writing \(\mathbb E^{\omega}_{2n}\) for expectation in the mean-\(2n\) marked Poisson experiment under \(P_\omega\),
\[
\mathbb E^{\omega}_{2n}\!\left[\max_{1\le j\le M_n}\bigl|\widehat u_j-u_{j,\omega_j}\bigr|\right]
\ \ge\
\frac{c_1a_n}{2}\cdot d
=
2c\,a_n .
\]
% lean: exists_vertex_poissonLoss_ge_coordinatewiseError

\emph{De-Poissonization.}  Put \(P=P_\omega\), so \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), \(\mathcal X_P=S\), and every \(x_j\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\).  Define the finite packing loss of the rule at a sample point \(w\) of size \(n\) by
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}(w)
=
\max_{1\le j\le M_n}\bigl|\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}(x_j)-\mu_P(x_j)\bigr|
\quad\text{evaluated at }w ,
\]
a measurable function of \(w\), being a finite maximum of compositions of the Borel sections \(T_{n,x_j}\) with the measurable distance compressions of \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data}.
% lean: finitePackingLoss, finitePackingLoss_measurable_of_pointIndexed
On the event \(\{N\ge n\}\) the first \(n\) atoms in mark order are an exact i.i.d.\ sample from \(P\), and by construction \(\widehat u_j\) is then the value of the rule at \(x_j\) on that retained sample and \(u_{j,\omega_j}=\mu_P(x_j)\); hence the Poissonized maximum equals \(\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\) of the retained sample.
% lean: globalPackingPoissonLoss_eq_finitePackingLoss, canonicalPrefix_finiteSampleMap_packingMarkedDistance
On the complementary event \(\{N<n\}\) every \(\widehat u_j\) equals the fallback value \(0\), so the maximum is at most \(\max_j|u_{j,\omega_j}|=\max_j|\mu_P(x_j)|\le L\), the envelope coming from the zeroth-order derivative bound in \(\mu_P\in\mathcal H^q(\mathcal X_P,L)\) of \cref{obj:synth_36}.
% lean: globalPackingPoissonLoss_le_on_count_lt
Since \(N\) is Poisson with mean \(2n\), a Chernoff bound for its lower tail gives \(\Pr\{N<n\}\le\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}\), and therefore
\[
\mathbb E^{\omega}_{2n}\!\left[\max_{1\le j\le M_n}\bigl|\widehat u_j-u_{j,\omega_j}\bigr|\right]
\le
\mathbb E_{P}\!\left[\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\right]
+
L\exp\{-n(1-\log 2)\} .
\]
% lean: poisson_two_n_lower_tail, globalPackingPoissonRisk_le_fixedRisk_add_tail
Because \(\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}=o(a_n)\), we may enlarge the threshold on \(n\) so that \(L\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}\le c\,a_n\).
% lean: poisson_remainder_isLittleO_frontier
Combining the last three displays and cancelling one copy of the finite quantity \(c\,a_n\) yields: for every point-indexed rule \(\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) there are \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), an integer \(M_n\), and boundary points \(x_1,\ldots,x_{M_n}\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\) with
\[
\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[
\max_{1\leq j\leq M_n}
\bigl|\widehat\mu_{n,P}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x_j)-\mu_P(x_j)\bigr|
\right]
\geq c\,a_n .
\]
% lean: packing_finite_max_lower_bound

\item
For such \(P\) and \(x_1,\ldots,x_{M_n}\), the finite maximum is bounded pointwise by the full boundary loss because each \(x_j\) lies on \(\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\):
\[
\max_{1\le j\le M_n}\bigl|\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}(x_j)-\mu_P(x_j)\bigr|
\le
\sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}\bigl|\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}(x)-\mu_P(x)\bigr| .
\]
The left-hand side is measurable, so its ordinary expectation is bounded above by the outer expectation of the right-hand side, that is, by the quantity appearing in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}.  Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), and then the infimum over all rules in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\), gives a sample-size threshold \(n_0\), distinct from the Poisson count \(N\) of Step 1, such that
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\geq c\,a_n
\qquad\text{for every } n\geq n_0 .
\]
% lean: lintegral_le_outerLIntegral_of_measurable_le, pointIndexedDistanceRisk_eventually_lower

\item
For every \(n\geq2\), \(a_n>0\) and
\[
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4}R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}
=
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n},
\]
because \((n/\log n)^{1/4}\) is the reciprocal of \(a_n\).  Thus the two normalized sequences agree eventually, so monotonicity of \(\liminf\) in both directions gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4}R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}.
\]
% lean: scaledRisk_eventually_eq_normalizedRisk

\item
For every \(n\geq \max\{n_0,2\}\), the positivity of \(a_n\) allows division in the preceding eventual lower bound:
\[
c\leq \frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}.
\]
Taking \(\liminf\) preserves this eventual lower bound.  With \(c_{\mathrm{PI}}=c>0\), the asserted equality of normalizations and the lower bound
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}\geq c_{\mathrm{PI}}
\]
follow.
% lean: point_indexed_distance_log_converse
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier}]
1. Apply \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders} for the chosen order \(p\), and let \(L_{\mathrm{conv}}\ge 48\) be the envelope threshold it provides.  Set
\[
L_0=\max\{L_{\mathrm{conv}},4\}.
\]
Then \(L_0\ge 48\).  Fix \(\nu\ge 2\), \(L\ge L_0\), and the three displayed hypotheses of the theorem.  The choice of \(L_0\) gives both \(L\ge L_{\mathrm{conv}}\) and \(L\ge 4\).
% lean: cty_a1_a2_point_indexed_log_converse

2. The converse theorem at \(L_{\mathrm{conv}}\) supplies a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}.
\]
That theorem also records the parallel lower bound for the fixed-geometry risk \(R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)\) of \cref{obj:synth_126}; only the full-risk statement just displayed is used below.
% lean: cty_a1_a2_point_indexed_log_converse

3. By \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}, applied with \(L\ge 4\) and the distance-identification, first-order-bias, and expected-maximal-bound hypotheses, there are \(C_0>0\) and \(N\) such that, for every \(n\ge N\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]
\le C_0 a_n .
\]
% lean: cty_a1_a2_winsorized_expected_outer_upper
Since \(a_n>0\) eventually, division by \(a_n\) and passage to the limsup give
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]}
{a_n}
\le C_0 .
\]
% lean: stabilizedLocalPolynomial_normalizedRisk_limsup_le

4. \emph{The explicit rule is admissible.}  For every \(n\ge 2\) the frontier bandwidth \(a_n\) is positive, so the winsorization level \(B(a_n)=a_n^{-1/3}\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} is well defined, and the estimator is generated by the law-independent family of sections
\[
T_{n,G,x}(u)
=
\max\!\left\{-2L,\min\!\left\{2L,\
e_1^\top\bigl(\widehat\beta^{B}_{1}(a_n;u)-\widehat\beta^{B}_{0}(a_n;u)\bigr)
\right\}\right\},
\qquad u\in(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R)^n,
\]
where \(\widehat\beta^{B}_{t}(a_n;u)\) is the stabilized coefficient of \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} computed from the signed-distance sample \(u\).  This family does not depend on the unknown member law, and each fixed-\((G,x)\) section is Borel in \(u\), because the empirical Gram \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(a_n)\), the uniform conditioning event \(\{(2L)^{-1}\sum_{j}v_j^2\le v^\top\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(a_n)v\ \text{for all }v\}\), the matrix inverse on that event, the winsorized empirical moment \(\widehat m^{B}_{t,x}(a_n)\), and the clipping map are all Borel functions of \(u\).  Moreover, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), the signed-distance sample built from the known geometry \(G_P\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry} is exactly \(U^{\pm}_{n,P}(x)\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data}, since the treatment indicator is the deterministic function \(\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_{1,P}\}\) of the covariate recorded in \(G_P\).  Hence
\[
\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}\in\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n .
\]
% lean: stabilizedLocalPolynomial, stabilizedLocalPolynomial_mem
Therefore the minimax risk of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk} is bounded above by the risk of this explicit rule:
\[
R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right].
\]
% lean: a1a2OuterRisk_le_stabilizedLocalPolynomialOuterRisk
After normalizing by \(a_n\), this inequality holds eventually in \(n\), and monotonicity of limsup yields
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]}
{a_n}
\le C_0 .
\]
% lean: cty_a1_a2_winsorized_matched_frontier

5. Finally set
\[
C=\max\{C_0,c\}.
\]
Then \(0<c\le C\).  The lower bound from Step 2, the general inequality
\(\liminf\le\limsup\), and the two upper bounds from Steps 3 and 4 give
\[
c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}
\le
C,
\]
and
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]}
{a_n}
\le
C.
\]
These are exactly the asserted frontier bounds.
% lean: cty_a1_a2_winsorized_matched_frontier
\end{proof}
