\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}]
Throughout, \(Z=(Y(0),Y(1),X)\) denotes one potential-outcome observation and \(Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\) its observed outcome, as in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}; \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\) is the sample and \(P^n\) its law.  For \(u\in\mathbb R^{p+1}\) we write
\[
\lVert u\rVert=\max_{0\leq j\leq p}|u_j|,
\qquad
\lVert u\rVert_2=\left(\sum_{j=0}^p u_j^2\right)^{1/2},
\]
so that \(\lVert u\rVert_2\leq(p+1)\lVert u\rVert\), the norm \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) being the one appearing in \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound} and in \cref{obj:synth_62}.  Fix \(p\geq0\), \(\nu\geq2\) and \(L\geq4\), and abbreviate the bandwidth and clipping schedules of the statement by
\[
h_n=a_n=\left({\log n\over n}\right)^{1/4},
\qquad
B_n=a_n^{-1/3},
\]
as in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates} and \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}.  We write \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)\), \(\widehat m^B_{t,x}(h)\), \(\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)\) and \(\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)\) for the objects of \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}, \(\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\) for the population Gram of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, \(\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\) for the population coefficient of \cref{obj:synth_128}, and \(\tau_P\) for the target of \cref{obj:synth_46}.

The first input of \cref{obj:synth_139} fixes what is being estimated: it is what makes \(\tau_P(x)\) the boundary contrast approached by the two one-sided signed-distance conditional-mean limits, and the bias input of the same collection is stated relative to that same target through \cref{obj:synth_39}.  Everything below is quantitative and uses the bias and expected-maximal inputs, \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}, and the envelopes of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}.
% lean: CtyDistanceIdentification

\textbf{Step 0: the constants.}  Let \(C_b>0\) be the constant supplied by the uniform first-order bias input of the statement and put
\[
\bar C_b=C_b+1>0 .
\]
Let \(C_m>0\) be the constant supplied by the expected maximal bounds of the statement, and let \(C_{\mathrm{sc}}=C_{\mathrm{sc}}(p,L)>0\) be the constant of \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}.  Define
\[
C
=
\bar C_b+32L^3(p+1)+16L(p+1)C_{\mathrm{sc}}+8L^2(p+1)C_m
>0 .
\]
All four summands are positive because \(L\geq4>0\).  This is the constant of the conclusion; the threshold \(N\) is fixed at the end of Step 2.
% lean: cty_a1_a2_winsorized_expected_outer_upper

\textbf{Step 1: the four bandwidth calibrations.}  Let \(n\geq3\) and write \(h=h_n\), \(B=B_n\).  We claim
\[
B^{-3}\leq h,
\qquad
\sqrt{\log(B/h)\over nh^2}\leq2h,
\qquad
{B\log(B/h)\over nh^2}\leq2h,
\qquad
\sqrt{\log(h^{-1})\over nh^2}\leq h .
\]
First, \(\log n<n\) for every \(n\geq1\), so \(\log n/n<1\) and hence \(0<h<1\) and \(B=h^{-1/3}\geq1\).  Raising the definition of \(a_n\) to the fourth power gives the identity that drives every calibration,
\[
h^4={\log n\over n},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
{\log n\over nh^2}=h^2 .
\]
% lean: t4_frontierRate_fourth_power
The first claim is the exponent computation \(B^{-3}=(h^{-1/3})^{-3}=h\).
% lean: t4_winsorization_power
For the remaining three, take logarithms in the definition of \(h\):
\[
\log(h^{-1})={\log n-\log\log n\over4},
\qquad
\log(B/h)=\log\bigl(h^{-4/3}\bigr)={4\over3}\log(h^{-1}),
\]
and, since \(n\geq3\) forces \(\log\log n>0\), both are nonnegative and satisfy
\[
\log(h^{-1})\leq\log n,
\qquad
\log(B/h)\leq{\log n\over3}\leq2\log n .
\]
Dividing by \(nh^2\) and using the displayed identity,
\[
{\log(B/h)\over nh^2}\leq{2\log n\over nh^2}=2h^2,
\qquad
{\log(h^{-1})\over nh^2}\leq{\log n\over nh^2}=h^2 .
\]
Taking square roots gives the second claim, because \(2h^2\leq(2h)^2\), and the fourth claim directly.  For the third, factor out \(B\) and use the previous display together with
\[
Bh=h^{-1/3}h=h^{2/3}\leq1 ,
\]
which holds because \(0<h<1\); thus \(B\log(B/h)/(nh^2)\leq B\cdot2h^2=2h(Bh)\leq2h\).
% lean: t4_frontier_rate_terms_le

\textbf{Step 2: activating the two assumed inputs along the frontier bandwidth.}  The bias input of the statement is asserted along \emph{antitone} bandwidth sequences, and \(n\mapsto a_n\) is antitone only from \(n=3\) onward.  Set therefore
\[
h^\sharp_n=a_{\max\{n,3\}},
\qquad n\in\mathbb N .
\]
Then \(h^\sharp_n>0\) for every \(n\), the sequence \(h^\sharp\) is antitone, \(h^\sharp_n\to0\), and
\[
n(h^\sharp_n)^2=\sqrt{n\log n}\to\infty
\qquad(n\geq3),
\]
so the bias input applies to \(h^\sharp\) with \(\nu'=\nu\) and yields
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu,L}(h^\sharp_n)\leq C_b<\bar C_b .
\]
Since \(h^\sharp_n=a_n\) for every \(n\geq3\), there is \(N_1\) such that
\[
\mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu,L}(a_n)\leq\bar C_b,
\qquad n\geq N_1 .
\]
% lean: t4Bandwidth_antitone
% lean: t4Bandwidth_nh2_tendsto_top
% lean: t4Bandwidth_eventually_eq_frontierRate
% lean: CtyUniformFirstOrderBias
The expected maximal bounds of the statement are asserted along positive deterministic sequences tending to zero whose heavy-tail regime diverges.  At \(h_n=a_n\), using \(\log(a_n^{-1})=(\log n-\log\log n)/4\) and \(a_n^2=\sqrt{\log n/n}\),
\[
{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}a_n^2\over\log(a_n^{-1})}
=
{4\,n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)-1/2}(\log n)^{1/2}\over\log n-\log\log n}
\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
because \((1+\nu)/(2+\nu)-1/2=\nu/\{2(2+\nu)\}>0\) for \(\nu\geq2\), so the numerator's polynomial factor dominates the logarithm.  Hence there is \(N_2\) such that, for every \(n\geq N_2\) and every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\),
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(a_n)\right]
\leq
C_m\sqrt{\log(a_n^{-1})\over na_n^2},
\]
with \(\mathrm{GramDev}\) as in \cref{obj:synth_62}.  Only this first half of the expected maximal bounds is used; the stochastic control of the score is supplied instead by \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}, whose bounded envelope is created by the winsorization built into \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}.
% lean: t4Bandwidth_maximal_regime_tendsto_top
% lean: CtyExpectedLocalPolynomialMaximalBounds
Finally \(a_n\to0\), so there is \(N_3\) with \(a_n\leq L^{-1}\) for \(n\geq N_3\), which is the small-bandwidth regime in which the population Gram floor, the arm-mass threshold and \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound} are all available.  Put
\[
N=\max\{3,N_1,N_2,N_3\}
\]
and fix \(n\geq N\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) for the rest of the proof.  Write \(h=a_n\) and \(B=B_n=h^{-1/3}\); by Step 1, \(1\leq B\) and \(0<h\leq L^{-1}\).

\textbf{Step 3: a small Gram deviation activates the stabilization guard.}  Put
\[
\delta={1\over2L(p+1)} >0 .
\]
We claim that on the event \(\{\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)\leq\delta\}\) the guard of \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} holds simultaneously at every arm \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and every interface point \(x\in\mathcal B_P\); that is,
\[
(2L)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^p v_j^2
\leq
v^\top\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)v,
\qquad v\in\mathbb R^{p+1} .
\]
Indeed, \(\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)\leq\delta\) means exactly that every entry satisfies \(|\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)_{jk}-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)_{jk}|\leq\delta\), and for any symmetric perturbation with entries bounded by \(\delta\),
\[
\left|v^\top\{\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\}v\right|
\leq
\sum_{j,k}|v_j|\,\delta\,|v_k|
\leq
\delta\sum_{j,k}{v_j^2+v_k^2\over2}
=
(p+1)\,\delta\sum_{j=0}^pv_j^2
=
(2L)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^pv_j^2 ,
\]
the middle step by \(2|v_j||v_k|\leq v_j^2+v_k^2\).
% lean: matrixQuadratic_sub_abs_le_entrywise
% lean: causalGramDeviation_entry_le
The population Gram lower bound of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, valid because \(0<h\leq L^{-1}\) and \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), gives \(v^\top\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)v\geq L^{-1}\sum_jv_j^2=2(2L)^{-1}\sum_jv_j^2\).  Subtracting the previous display leaves \(v^\top\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)v\geq(2L)^{-1}\sum_jv_j^2\), which is the guard.  This is where the factor \(2\) in the guard threshold \((2L)^{-1}\) is spent: it is exactly the room left by the population floor \(L^{-1}\) after the empirical perturbation is absorbed.
% lean: empiricalGramGuard_of_deviation_le

\textbf{Step 4: the guarded coefficient error is the inverted score.}  Suppose the guard holds at \((t,x)\).  Then \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)\) is invertible: if \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)v=0\) then the guard forces \(\sum_jv_j^2\leq0\), so \(v=0\).
% lean: empiricalGramGuard_isUnit
Moreover, for every \(\zeta\in\mathbb R^{p+1}\),
\[
\left\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\rVert_2
\leq
2L\lVert\zeta\rVert_2 .
\]
Indeed, applying the guard to the vector \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\) and then the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\[
(2L)^{-1}\left\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\rVert_2^2
\leq
\left\{\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\}^\top
\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)
\left\{\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\}
=
\left\{\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\}^\top\zeta
\leq
\left\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\rVert_2\lVert\zeta\rVert_2 ,
\]
and dividing by \(\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\rVert_2\) when it is nonzero gives the claim; when it is zero the claim is trivial.
% lean: empiricalGramGuard_inv_mulVec_euclidean_le
On the guarded branch \(\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)=\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\widehat m^B_{t,x}(h)\), so subtracting \(\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\) and using the definition of the winsorized score in \cref{obj:synth_131},
\[
\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)
=
\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}
\left\{\widehat m^B_{t,x}(h)-\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}
=
\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h} .
\]
Selecting the intercept, bounding a coordinate by the Euclidean norm, and converting norms through \(\lVert u\rVert_2\leq(p+1)\lVert u\rVert\),
\[
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}\right|
\leq
\left\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right\rVert_2
\leq
2L\left\lVert\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right\rVert_2
\leq
2L(p+1)\left\lVert\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right\rVert .
\]
% lean: euclideanNorm_le_card_mul_piNorm
% lean: guardedCoefficient_sub_populationCoefficient_le

\textbf{Step 5: the mean of the winsorized score is of order \(B^{-3}\).}  Write
\[
\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)
=
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left\lVert
\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}
-\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]
\right\rVert
\]
for the centered supremum appearing in \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}, so that for every \(t\) and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\),
\[
\left\lVert\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right\rVert
\leq
\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)
+
\left\lVert\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]\right\rVert .
\]
We claim the deterministic bound
\[
\left\lVert\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]\right\rVert
\leq
8L^2B^{-3},
\qquad t\in\{0,1\},\ x\in\mathcal B_P .
\]
Fix \(t\), \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and a coordinate \(0\leq j\leq p\), and write the single-observation weight
\[
\xi_j(Z)
=
\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}
K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)\,
r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)_j ,
\]
with \(D^{\pm}_{P,x}\) the signed distance of \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data}, \(K_\square\) the uniform kernel of \cref{obj:def:cty-uniform-kernel}, and \(r_p\) the basis of \cref{obj:synth_71}.  Since \(K_\square\) vanishes off \(\{|D^{\pm}_{P,x}|\leq h\}\) and \(|r_p(u)_j|\leq1\) for \(|u|\leq1\),
\[
|\xi_j(Z)|\leq\mathbf 1\{X\in B_2(x,h)\} ,
\]
with \(B_2(x,h)\) the ball of \cref{obj:synth_43}; the equivalence \(|D^{\pm}_{P,x}|\leq h\iff X\in B_2(x,h)\) is immediate from the definition of the signed distance, whose magnitude is \(\lVert X-x\rVert_2\).  By \cref{obj:synth_131} the \(j\)th coordinate of the winsorized score is the sample average \(h^{-2}n^{-1}\sum_{i\leq n}\xi_j(Z_i)\{\psi_B(Y_i)-r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}/h)^\top\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\}\), so the observations being independent and identically distributed and the summand integrable,
\[
\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h,j}\right]
=
{1\over h^2}\int
\xi_j(Z)\left\{\psi_B(Y)-r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)^\top\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}dP .
\]
% lean: winsorizedCenteredScore_integral_eq
Split the integrand by inserting and removing the unwinsorized outcome,
\[
\xi_j(Z)\left\{\psi_B(Y)-r_p^\top\beta^*\right\}
=
\underbrace{\xi_j(Z)\left\{Y-r_p^\top\beta^*\right\}}_{\text{population residual}}
+
\underbrace{\xi_j(Z)\left\{\psi_B(Y)-Y\right\}}_{\text{winsorization remainder}} .
\]
The first term integrates to zero: by the normal equations of \cref{obj:synth_128},
\[
{1\over h^2}\int\xi_j(Z)\left\{Y-r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)^\top\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}dP
=
\mathbb E_P\!\left[{1\over h^2}\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)Y\right]_j
-
\left\{\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}_j
=
0 .
\]
% lean: rawPopulationResidual_integral_eq_zero
For the second term, the moment envelope of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} is converted into a deterministic \(B^{-3}\) envelope pointwise in the outcome.  For \(B\geq1\) and \(\nu\geq2\), every real \(y\) satisfies
\[
|y-\psi_B(y)|\leq|y|^{2+\nu}B^{-3} .
\]
If \(|y|\leq B\) the left side vanishes; otherwise \(|y|>B\geq1\), so \(|y-\psi_B(y)|=|y|-B\leq|y|\leq|y|^4B^{-3}\leq|y|^{2+\nu}B^{-3}\), using \(|y|^3\geq B^3\) and then \(|y|\geq1\) with \(2+\nu\geq4\).
% lean: winsorize_tail_le_moment
Integrating this against the selected conditional law of \(Y(t')\) given \(X=z\) and applying the moment envelope \(\mathbb E_P[|Y(t')|^{2+\nu}\mid X=z]\leq L\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} gives, for each arm \(t'\in\{0,1\}\) and each \(z\in\mathcal X_P\),
\[
\mathbb E_P\bigl[\,|Y(t')-\psi_B(Y(t'))|\,\bigm|\,X=z\,\bigr]\leq LB^{-3} .
\]
Note that it is this absolute conditional remainder, and not merely the difference of the two conditional means, that the argument requires, because the remainder is multiplied by the random weight \(\xi_j\) before it is integrated.
% lean: condKer_winsorization_tail_lintegral_le
Combining the weight bound, the consistency relation \(Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, which makes \(|\psi_B(Y)-Y|\) at most the sum of the two armwise remainders, and the density envelope \(f_P\leq L\) together with the planar area bound \(\pi h^2\leq4h^2\), which give
\[
P\{X\in B_2(x,h)\}\leq4Lh^2 ,
\]
% lean: marginal_closedBall_le
we obtain
\[
\int\left|\xi_j(Z)\left\{\psi_B(Y)-Y\right\}\right|dP
\leq
\sum_{t'\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbf 1\{X\in B_2(x,h)\}\,\bigl|Y(t')-\psi_B(Y(t'))\bigr|\right]
\leq
2\cdot LB^{-3}\cdot4Lh^2
=
8L^2B^{-3}h^2 .
\]
% lean: winsorizedResidualTail_enorm_le_localized_arms
% lean: localized_arm_winsorization_tail_lintegral_le
% lean: winsorizedResidualTail_lintegral_le
Multiplying by \(h^{-2}\) and taking the maximum over \(j\) proves the claim, since the bound is free of \(j\), \(t\) and \(x\).
% lean: winsorizedCenteredScore_mean_norm_le

\textbf{Step 6: the pointwise interface loss.}  We claim that for every sample,
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\leq
\left\{\bar C_bh+4L(p+1)\cdot8L^2B^{-3}\right\}
+
4L(p+1)\,\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)
+
8L^2(p+1)\,\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h) .
\]
Two preliminary facts hold at every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\).  First, \(\mathcal B_P\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal X_P)\), and the derivative envelope of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} bounds each arm regression by \(|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\), so
\[
|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L,
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L] .
\]
% lean: euclideanCExtEnvelope_abs_le
Consequently, since projecting onto an interval containing \(\tau_P(x)\) cannot increase the distance to \(\tau_P(x)\), the clipping in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} is harmless:
\[
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\leq
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{1,x}(h)-\widehat\beta^B_{0,x}(h)\right\}-\tau_P(x)\right| .
\]
Second, \(n\geq N_1\) and the definition of the bias ratio in \cref{obj:synth_39} give the deterministic first-order bias bound
\[
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\beta^*_{1,P,x}(h)-\beta^*_{0,P,x}(h)\right\}-\tau_P(x)\right|
\leq
\bar C_bh,
\qquad x\in\mathcal B_P .
\]
Now split on the design.

\emph{Case 1: \(\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)\leq\delta\).}  By Step 3 the guard holds at both arms and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), so Step 4 applies at both arms.  Inserting the two population coefficients and using the triangle inequality,
\[
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{1,x}(h)-\widehat\beta^B_{0,x}(h)\right\}-\tau_P(x)\right|
\leq
\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}}
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}\right|
+
\bar C_bh .
\]
Bounding each summand by Step 4 and then each score by Step 5,
\[
\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}}
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}\right|
\leq
2\cdot2L(p+1)\left\{\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)+8L^2B^{-3}\right\} ,
\]
which is
\[
4L(p+1)\cdot8L^2B^{-3}
+
4L(p+1)\,\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B) .
\]
The right-hand side no longer depends on \(x\), so taking the supremum over \(x\in\mathcal B_P\) and adding the nonnegative Gram term gives the claim in this case.

\emph{Case 2: \(\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)>\delta\).}  Here the clipping alone controls the loss: \(|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)|\leq2L\) by construction and \(|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L\), so
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|\leq4L .
\]
On the other hand the case hypothesis and the value of \(\delta\) give
\[
8L^2(p+1)\,\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)
\geq
8L^2(p+1)\cdot{1\over2L(p+1)}
=
4L ,
\]
so the Gram term alone already dominates the loss, and the claim holds because the two remaining terms are nonnegative.  This is the calibration that fixes the coefficient \(8L^2(p+1)\): it is the smallest multiple of \(\delta^{-1}\) that pays for the trivial envelope \(4L\) on the ill-conditioned event.
% lean: stabilizedLocalPolynomial_boundaryLoss_le

\textbf{Step 7: integration.}  Take outer expectations under \(P^n\) in Step 6.  The outer expectation of \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} is monotone, is subadditive on sums, returns a constant on a constant because \(P^n\) is a probability measure, and satisfies \(\mathbb E_P^*[cW]\leq c\,\mathbb E_P^*[W]\) for every finite constant \(c>0\).  Applying these four properties in turn to the three summands of Step 6,
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|\right]
\leq
\bar C_bh+32L^3(p+1)B^{-3}
+
4L(p+1)\,\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)\right]
+
8L^2(p+1)\,\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)\right] ,
\]
where \(4L(p+1)\cdot8L^2=32L^3(p+1)\).  The measurability, integrability and extended-arithmetic bookkeeping needed to pass from the pointwise inequality to this display is routine and is suppressed.
% lean: outerLIntegral_mono
% lean: outerLIntegral_add_le
% lean: outerLIntegral_const_probability
% lean: outerLIntegral_const_mul_le

\textbf{Step 8: substituting the two maximal bounds.}  The three terms are now evaluated at \(h=a_n\) and \(B=B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\) using Step 1.

The deterministic term.  By the first calibration \(B_n^{-3}=a_n\), so
\[
\bar C_ba_n+32L^3(p+1)B_n^{-3}
=
\left\{\bar C_b+32L^3(p+1)\right\}a_n .
\]

The score term.  Since \(n\geq1\), \(0<a_n\leq L^{-1}\) and \(B_n\geq1\), \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound} applies to \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) at this bandwidth and clipping level and supplies
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(a_n,B_n)\right]
\leq
C_{\mathrm{sc}}\left\{
\sqrt{\log(B_n/a_n)\over na_n^2}
+
{B_n\log(B_n/a_n)\over na_n^2}
\right\}
\leq
C_{\mathrm{sc}}\{2a_n+2a_n\}
=
4C_{\mathrm{sc}}a_n ,
\]
the last inequality by the second and third calibrations of Step 1.  Hence
\[
4L(p+1)\,\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(a_n,B_n)\right]
\leq
16L(p+1)C_{\mathrm{sc}}\,a_n .
\]
This is the step at which the winsorization level is paid for twice over, and in opposite directions: raising \(B_n\) shrinks the deterministic term \(32L^3(p+1)B_n^{-3}\) but inflates the envelope term \(B_n\log(B_n/a_n)/(na_n^2)\).  The choice \(B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\) is exactly the one that places both on the scale \(a_n\).

The Gram term.  By Step 2 and the fourth calibration of Step 1,
\[
8L^2(p+1)\,\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(a_n)\right]
\leq
8L^2(p+1)C_m\sqrt{\log(a_n^{-1})\over na_n^2}
\leq
8L^2(p+1)C_m\,a_n .
\]

Adding the three displays and recalling the definition of \(C\) in Step 0,
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|\right]
\leq
\left\{\bar C_b+32L^3(p+1)+16L(p+1)C_{\mathrm{sc}}+8L^2(p+1)C_m\right\}a_n
=
Ca_n .
\]
Every quantity in \(C\) was fixed in Step 0, before \(P\) and \(n\), so the bound is uniform over the law class, and taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]
\leq
Ca_n,
\qquad n\geq N ,
\]
which is the assertion, with \(C>0\) and \(N\) as constructed.

\end{proof}
