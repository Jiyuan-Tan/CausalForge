\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:common-map-strict-in-point-indexed}]
Throughout, a point of \(\mathbb R^2\) is written \(x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})\), and a sample point is written \(w=((y_i,z_i):1\leq i\leq n)\in(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2)^n\), so that the distance data of \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data} evaluated at \(w\) and at a query point \(x\) are
\[
V_{n,P}(x)\big|_{w}
=
\bigl((y_i,\lVert z_i-x\rVert_2):1\leq i\leq n\bigr).
\]

\emph{Inclusion.}  Let \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) and let \(T_n:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\to\mathbb R\) be a measurable map, selected independently of \(P\), representing it as in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class}.  Define a point-indexed family by
\[
T_{n,x}=T_n
\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R^2 .
\]
Each section is the single Borel map \(T_n\), hence Borel measurable for every fixed \(x\), and the family is selected independently of \(P\) because \(T_n\) is.  Moreover, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), every sample point, and every \(x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\),
\[
\widehat\mu_n(x)=T_n(V_{n,P}(x))=T_{n,x}(V_{n,P}(x)),
\]
so \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class}.  Therefore
\[
\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\subseteq \mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}.
\]
% lean: ctyClass_subset_piClass

\emph{A point-indexed rule outside the common-map class.}  Let the rule return the first coordinate of the query point, ignoring the data:
\[
\widehat\mu_{n}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)=x^{(1)},
\qquad\text{generated by the sections }\;
T_{n,x}(v)=x^{(1)}
\quad\text{for every }v\in(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n .
\]
For each fixed \(x\) the section \(T_{n,x}\) is constant in \(v\), hence Borel, and the family \(\{T_{n,x}\}\) does not depend on the member law.  Thus \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\).
% lean: coordinateWitnessRule

Suppose, for contradiction, that this same rule also belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\), with common measurable map \(T_n\).  Apply \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing} at the given \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\), at any sample size beyond the threshold it provides, and take the vertex \(\omega=(0,\ldots,0)\); its model-class and support clause supplies a law
\[
P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q),
\qquad
\mathcal X_P=S=[-1/2,1/2]^2 .
\]
% lean: cty_support_boundary_angular_packing

Set
\[
x_-=(-1/4,-1/2),\qquad x_+=(1/4,-1/2),\qquad z=(0,-1/2).
\]
Each of \(x_-\) and \(x_+\) has second coordinate \(-1/2\) and first coordinate of modulus \(1/4<1/2\), so it is a non-corner point of the lower edge of \(S\) and therefore
\[
x_-\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P),
\qquad
x_+\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P).
\]
% lean: lowerEdgePoint_mem_frontier

Take the deterministic sample point \(w\) whose every observation equals \((0,z)\), that is \(y_i=0\) and \(z_i=z\) for all \(i\leq n\).  Since \(z\), \(x_-\) and \(x_+\) share their second coordinate, the Euclidean distances reduce to horizontal ones:
\[
\lVert z-x_-\rVert_2=\left|0-(-1/4)\right|=\frac14=\left|0-1/4\right|=\lVert z-x_+\rVert_2 .
\]
% lean: dist_scorePoint_same_second
Consequently the compressed samples at the two query points coincide:
\[
V_{n,P}(x_-)\big|_{w}
=
\bigl((0,1/4):1\leq i\leq n\bigr)
=
V_{n,P}(x_+)\big|_{w}.
\]
% lean: distanceData

Both \(x_-\) and \(x_+\) lie on \(\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\), so the assumed common-map representation applies at each of them and, with the displayed equality of distance data, gives
\[
x_-^{(1)}
=
T_n\bigl(V_{n,P}(x_-)\big|_{w}\bigr)
=
T_n\bigl(V_{n,P}(x_+)\big|_{w}\bigr)
=
x_+^{(1)} .
\]
But \(x_-^{(1)}=-1/4\) and \(x_+^{(1)}=1/4\), a contradiction.
% lean: scorePoint_apply_zero
% lean: piClass_not_subset_ctyClass

Hence \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\setminus\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\), so the inclusion proved first is proper:
\[
\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\subsetneq\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}} .
\]
% lean: common_map_strict_in_point_indexed
\end{proof}
