# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.8/10 — The formal results appear substantial and carefully scoped, but the manuscript still needs clearer econometric positioning, cleaner assumption narration, and several prose repairs before it reads as a publishable econometrics paper.

The paper establishes logarithmic minimax lower bounds for unsigned distance-compressed boundary regression, extends the converse to point-indexed distance rules, and gives a conditional matched expected-risk frontier for known-geometry signed-distance treatment-effect estimation. The verified statements support the core mathematical claims, and the contribution is potentially meaningful for boundary RD theory. As written, however, the paper is still too dominated by formalization scaffolding and does not yet give readers enough economic and literature-facing guidance about what is new relative to CTY and what is assumed for the signed upper bound.

## Strengths
- The main formal claims are carefully qualified and align well with the verification contract.
- The distinction among unsigned common-map risk, unsigned point-indexed risk, and signed known-geometry risk is conceptually valuable.
- The signed estimator is explicit, stabilized, and tied to a clear expected outer-risk criterion.
- The Open Questions section properly houses the main non-coverage statements.

## Findings
- **[major·citation] Related Literature** — The novelty boundary relative to Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu (2026) is still too compressed for a journal reader. The manuscript says "Their theorem proves a positive lower bound after multiplication by n^{1/4}. The printed conjecture immediately following that theorem replaces n^{1/4} by (n/\log n)^{1/4}, matching the scale of their uniform upper bound," but it does not give enough detail about CTY's exact risk, theorem numbering, conjecture wording, or whether the present signed result is changing target, information, risk, or law class in a way that should be judged as a separate experiment rather than a direct strengthening.
  - *Fix:* Add a short paragraph, or expand the comparison table, that states CTY's exact unsigned minimax object, the conjectured logarithmic scale, and the precise change made by the point-indexed extension. For the signed result, explicitly state that it is a known-geometry treatment-effect experiment with signed distances and conditional expected-risk upper inputs, not a same-object sharpening of CTY's unsigned theorem.
- **[major·prose] Main Results** — The analytic-input bundle still reads partly like a formal artifact rather than a statistical assumption set. The definition of \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) and the following theorem repeat long hypotheses, but the manuscript still leaves readers to infer which inputs are imported from CTY-style identification, which are strengthened to expected outer-risk form, and which are imposed directly for this paper's estimator bound.
  - *Fix:* Before the formal definition of \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\), add a compact reader-facing map: input 1 supplies population signed-distance identification; input 2 supplies uniform first-order approximation; input 3 supplies expected outer-risk maximal control. Then state explicitly that the matched signed frontier is conditional on all three inputs and that the lower bound itself is unconditional within the displayed class.
- **[major·prose] Setup: Known-Geometry Signed-Distance Designs** — The A1/A2 class remains difficult to parse as an econometric model. The manuscript lists support, density, smoothness, variance, moment, assignment, VC, Gram, arm-mass, and slice conditions, but the subsequent explanation still does not give readers a sharp map from each group of assumptions to the risk argument.
  - *Fix:* Reorganize the explanatory paragraph immediately before or after the definition into four named roles: experiment and information set; smoothness and outcome envelope; local-polynomial conditioning; conditional upper-bound inputs. Keep the formal definition unchanged, but make the statistical role of each block explicit in prose.
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The contribution count is ambiguous. The abstract opens with "This paper establishes two new unconditional logarithmic minimax lower bounds" but then discusses unsigned common-map and point-indexed lower bounds and also says "The second unconditional lower bound holds in a signed-distance experiment." This makes it unclear whether the paper claims two lower bounds, three lower bounds, or two experiments with multiple risks.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite the opening to enumerate the deliverables directly: unsigned common-map lower bound, unsigned point-indexed lower bound, signed known-geometry lower bound, and conditional signed upper/frontier. This can be done without strengthening any claim.
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The unsigned interpretation is accurate but still too abstract for boundary RD readers: "Economically, this experiment isolates the risk cost of summarizing proximity by scalar distance after angular location and treatment-side information have been removed."
  - *Fix:* Add one concrete sentence explaining the consequence in RD language: at a boundary query point, two observations at the same scalar distance can come from different arcs or sides, so a distance-only rule cannot allocate local evidence to the correct boundary segment uniformly over the curve.
- **[minor·statement] global** — The theorem titles remain hard to distinguish. "Same-Class Log Converse," "Logarithmic Distance Converse," and "Point-Indexed Log Converse" do not reliably signal unsigned versus signed, common-map versus point-indexed, or known-geometry status.
  - *Fix:* Rename the displayed titles to include the experiment and decision class, for example "Unsigned Common-Map Log Converse," "Unsigned Point-Indexed Log Converse," and "Signed Known-Geometry Point-Indexed Log Converse."
- **[minor·prose] Related Literature** — Some external references use manual theorem-style typography despite the manuscript's strict cross-reference convention, for example "Theorem~6 of \citet{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance}" and table entries beginning "Theorem~6:". Even for external references, this creates an inconsistent reference style under the stated policy.
  - *Fix:* Either define a consistent external-reference style outside the cleveref rule or rewrite these as bibliographic prose, such as "the sixth theorem in \citet{...}" or "the unsigned minimax theorem in \citet{...}". Apply the choice consistently in text and tables.
- **[minor·prose] Discussion and Extensions** — The section is mostly compliant with affirmative framing, but the sentence "The unsigned results ... are lower bounds only and establish no matching unsigned upper bound here" is correctly placed under Open Questions while the earlier Discussion repeatedly contrasts the experiments through absent information, such as "angular and side information are absent from the observation." This is not a theorem overclaim, but the contribution framing would be stronger if it stated the delivered information sets affirmatively.
  - *Fix:* Where outside Open Questions, phrase the contrast in terms of available information: the unsigned experiment supplies outcomes and scalar distances; the signed experiment supplies known geometry, side, and signed distance. Keep genuine non-coverage in Open Questions.

## Questions for authors
- Can you provide the exact CTY theorem/conjecture text or page reference that corresponds to the unsigned logarithmic strengthening?
- Are the expected maximal bounds in \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) intended as assumptions for this paper, as consequences of cited CTY results plus additional arguments, or as formally verified reductions conditional on CTY inputs?
- Do you want readers to view the signed-distance result as a minimax theorem for a new experiment, or as a rate statement for CTY's signed local-polynomial procedure under strengthened expected-risk inputs?
