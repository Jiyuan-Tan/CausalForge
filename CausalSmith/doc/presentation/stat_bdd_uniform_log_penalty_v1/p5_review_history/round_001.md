# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.3/10 — The verified lower-bound and conditional signed-rate results are technically coherent, but the manuscript still needs sharper novelty positioning, cleaner claim framing, and better exposition of the conditional upper-bound scope before it reads like a publishable econometrics paper.

The paper studies uniform expected boundary sup-risk for unsigned and signed distance-based boundary regression experiments. Its main delivered contribution is a set of verified minimax lower bounds for distance-compressed information sets and a conditional matched signed-distance frontier using explicit analytic inputs. The results are potentially useful for researchers working on geographic and boundary RD, but the current manuscript undersells its economic intuition in some places while overburdening the reader with formalization scaffolding and incompletely adjudicating the relationship to CTY (2026).

## Strengths
- The statistical experiments, decision classes, and risks are stated with unusual precision, which helps distinguish unsigned compression from known-geometry signed-distance designs.
- The verified lower bounds are meaningful: the point-indexed unsigned lower bound directly targets the information content of scalar distance observations rather than a particular estimator architecture.
- The signed-distance estimator is concrete and stabilized, with winsorization, Gram-event inversion, and clipping tied to an explicit outer-risk bound.
- The manuscript is generally careful about theorem conditions and repeatedly signals the analytic inputs used for the signed upper result.

## Findings
- **[major·citation] Related Literature** — The novelty boundary relative to Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu (2026) remains insufficiently adjudicated. The manuscript says CTY provide "distance-based identification and estimation tools" and the brief says CTY already supply identification, bias, stochastic-process, inference, and minimax lower-bound ingredients, but the paper does not crisply separate which lower bounds, decision-class enlargements, fixed-geometry hypercubes, and estimator-stabilization steps are new mathematical content versus formal repackaging or conditional application.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact contribution paragraph, preferably at the end of the introduction or beginning of Related Literature, with three affirmative categories: new lower-bound content, new decision/risk formulation, and conditional signed upper assembly. State precisely what CTY results enter as inputs and cite them at first use.
- **[major·prose] Main Results** — The signed matched frontier is still easy to read as an unconditional characterization before the reader has internalized the strength of \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\). For example, the abstract says the paper "combines the signed-distance lower bound with the stabilized local-polynomial upper argument to obtain a two-sided rate bound"; only the preceding clause mentions the assumptions, and the title "Matched Frontier Rate" can read as the main theorem rather than a conditional frontier.
  - *Fix:* Rename or subtitle the result as a conditional signed-distance frontier, and in the abstract and theorem-introduction sentence put the condition first: "Under \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\), the signed-distance risk has..." Also add one sentence explaining that the lower bound is self-contained in the displayed class while the upper side is conditional on the three analytic inputs.
- **[major·structure] global** — The manuscript still reads in part like a formal-verification report rather than an econometrics article. Phrases such as "These are exactly the three explicit antecedents represented in Lean..." and "The theorem statements and proofs for the formal layer are checked in Lean 4" foreground implementation artifacts in the main narrative and appendix.
  - *Fix:* Move Lean-specific implementation language into a short reproducibility note or footnote, and rewrite the main text in econometric terms: assumptions, reductions, estimator, and risk bounds. Keep formal verification as supporting evidence rather than a framing device for the contribution.
- **[major·prose] Setup: Known-Geometry Signed-Distance Designs** — The restrictive A1/A2 class is described clause by clause, but readers are not given enough guidance about which assumptions encode the signed-distance experiment, which provide uniform conditioning, and which are imported analytic regularity for the upper bound. The sentence "The signed upper result additionally assumes the three analytic inputs...; those inputs are not implied merely by membership in the displayed class" is clear but appears as a limitation-style noncoverage statement outside Open Questions.
  - *Fix:* Replace the noncoverage sentence with an affirmative roadmap: "Membership in \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) fixes the geometric and moment envelope; the upper theorem then adds \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\), which supplies identification, first-order bias, and maximal controls." Add a small table or paragraph grouping assumptions by role.
- **[minor·prose] Main Results** — The prose contract is violated by explicit noncoverage language outside Open Questions: "none is proved in this paper" in \(\cref{obj:synth_139}\), and "those inputs are not implied merely by membership in the displayed class" in the signed setup.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite affirmatively: "\(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) collects the three analytic hypotheses supplied to the upper-bound theorem" and "The displayed class supplies the design envelope; \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) supplies the analytic controls used by the estimator bound."
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The unsigned result is technically accurate but its statistical interpretation remains too compressed. The sentence "The support-boundary hypercube shows that unsigned distance observations cannot uniformly recover independently varying boundary values at this scale..." is useful, but the abstract does not clearly say why an econometric reader should care about this compression result.
  - *Fix:* Add one affirmative sentence explaining the implication: scalar unsigned distance rules lose the angular/side information needed for uniform boundary-curve recovery over the displayed nonparametric class, even when the rule is allowed point-indexed Borel sections.
- **[minor·statement] global** — Formal result titles remain hard to distinguish. There are multiple "Log Converse" style labels, including "Same-Class Log Converse," "Logarithmic Distance Converse," and "Point-Indexed Log Converse," and one of the signed results has the same generic title form as the unsigned point-indexed result.
  - *Fix:* Rename titles to encode experiment and risk, e.g. "Unsigned Common-Map Lower Bound," "Unsigned Point-Indexed Lower Bound," and "Signed Known-Geometry Lower Bound."
- **[minor·prose] Discussion and Extensions** — The discussion says "The common normalization ... should be read as an agreement of established lower scales across the two experiments." This is mostly accurate, but for the signed experiment the manuscript also has a conditional upper result; for the unsigned experiment it has only lower bounds. The sentence may blur lower-scale agreement and matched-rate characterization.
  - *Fix:* Revise to separate the two claims: the unsigned and signed lower bounds share the \(a_n\) scale, and under \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) the signed experiment also has an upper bound at that scale.
- **[nit·structure] Reproducibility Note** — The reproducibility note is useful but too broad for reader-facing prose; it lists many anchored objects and risks repeating the formal table rather than telling readers what was checked and what assumptions enter the checked implications.
  - *Fix:* Compress the note to one paragraph on checked formal statements and one paragraph on theorem-local analytic inputs. Move exhaustive object lists to supplementary material if needed.

## Questions for authors
- Which exact minimax lower-bound ingredients in CTY (2026) are reused, and which are newly proved here for the full \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) or fixed known-geometry class?
- Can the signed-distance analytic inputs \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) be stated as imported CTY propositions with precise theorem numbers or assumptions, rather than as a bundled assumption?
- Is there a natural unsigned upper benchmark under a different information set or smoother subclass that would help readers interpret the lower bound’s practical sharpness?
