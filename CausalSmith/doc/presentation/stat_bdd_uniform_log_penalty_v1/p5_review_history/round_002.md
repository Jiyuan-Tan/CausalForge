# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.3/10 — The verified mathematical core appears sound and potentially useful, but the paper still needs sharper novelty positioning, clearer conditional framing of the signed upper frontier, and substantial cleanup of formalization-facing prose before it reads as a publishable econometrics article.

The paper studies uniform expected risk for boundary regression under unsigned distance compression and known-geometry signed-distance information. Its strongest contribution is a verified pair of logarithmic lower-bound constructions, together with a conditional signed-distance upper result attained by a stabilized local-polynomial estimator. I view the contribution as promising, but the manuscript as written does not yet give readers a sufficiently crisp account of what is new relative to Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu (2026), and several passages still overemphasize proof-engine artifacts or frame the signed frontier more unconditionally than its hypotheses support.

## Strengths
- The formal results are stated with unusually precise law classes, decision classes, risk criteria, and information sets.
- The unsigned point-indexed lower bound is a clean way to isolate the statistical cost of scalar unsigned distance compression.
- The signed lower bound is strengthened by a fixed-known-geometry hard subexperiment, which makes the source of the rate transparent.
- The conditional upper result uses an explicit estimator with winsorization, Gram stabilization, and clipping rather than an abstract existence argument.
- The manuscript is attentive to measurability and outer-expectation issues that are often suppressed in uniform-risk problems.

## Findings
- **[major·citation] Related Literature** — The novelty boundary relative to Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu (2026) remains under-adjudicated. The manuscript says CTY develop distance-based identification and estimation tools, while the related-work context indicates CTY already supply identification, bias, stochastic-process, inference, and minimax lower-bound ingredients. The paper still does not crisply separate the new lower-bound architecture, decision-class enlargement, fixed-geometry hypercube, expected-risk formulation, and estimator stabilization from conditional repackaging or application of CTY inputs.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact contribution-comparison paragraph or table that lists, for CTY and for this paper, the law class, information set, target, risk criterion, lower-bound construction, upper-bound inputs, and whether the result is unconditional or conditional. State explicitly which components are new results in this paper and which are imported analytic inputs or reformulations of CTY-style ingredients.
- **[major·structure] abstract** — The signed matched frontier is still easy to read as a main unconditional result. The abstract says, "Under the CTY distance-identification, uniform first-order bias, and expected maximal inputs collected in \cref{obj:synth_139}, the signed-distance minimax risk ... has a two-sided rate bound," but the conditional nature is not reinforced in the title-level description of the contribution and can be missed by a reader scanning the abstract and main theorem title.
  - *Fix:* Revise the abstract and the theorem lead-in so the signed contribution is consistently named as a conditional signed-distance frontier. For example, use wording like "Conditional on the three CTY-style analytic inputs, the stabilized estimator and lower bound characterize the signed-distance expected outer-risk rate." Also rename the theorem heading from "Matched Frontier Rate" to "Conditional Signed-Distance Matched Frontier" or similar.
- **[major·prose] global** — The manuscript still reads in places like a formal-verification report rather than an econometrics submission. Examples include "These are exactly the three explicit antecedents represented in Lean..." in \cref{obj:synth_139}, the broad verification inventory in \cref{sec:verification-note}, and Lean-facing notation such as "\operatorname{ofReal}(c)" appearing in the proof of \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier}.
  - *Fix:* Move implementation-specific Lean terminology out of the main mathematical narrative and remove Lean-only notation from displayed arguments. The reproducibility note should briefly say which theorem implications were checked and which analytic inputs enter as hypotheses, while the proofs should be written entirely in standard mathematical notation.
- **[major·prose] Main Results** — The prose contract is violated by explicit noncoverage language outside a permitted section: \cref{obj:synth_139} says "none is proved in this paper." This is contribution-by-negation in a definition in the main results section.
  - *Fix:* Replace the sentence with affirmative scope language, such as "Define \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) as the collection of three analytic inputs used by the signed-distance upper bound." The Open Questions section may then separately discuss self-contained derivation of these inputs.
- **[minor·prose] Setup: Known-Geometry Signed-Distance Designs** — The restrictive A1/A2 law class is described clause by clause, but the conceptual map remains diffuse. Readers are told many assumptions at once, yet the manuscript could more clearly distinguish the conditions defining the signed-distance experiment from the conditioning and empirical-process requirements used for the upper bound.
  - *Fix:* Add a short organizing paragraph after \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} with three affirmative categories: design/information conditions, uniform conditioning conditions, and analytic inputs invoked later. Avoid saying the displayed class fails to imply later inputs; instead state exactly which results use each category.
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The unsigned result is technically accurate but its econometric interpretation remains too compressed. The abstract describes matching scalar-distance information and lower-bound scale, but it does not clearly tell the boundary RD reader why the unsigned experiment matters for distance-to-boundary reductions.
  - *Fix:* Add one affirmative interpretive sentence explaining that the unsigned result formalizes the risk cost of using distance from a query point after angular and side information have been removed, so it isolates the information content of a commonly used scalar proximity summary.
- **[minor·structure] global** — The result titles are still difficult to distinguish. The paper uses several variants of "Log Converse" and "Point-Indexed Log Converse" across different experiments, which makes it harder for readers to remember which result is unsigned, signed, common-map, or fixed-geometry.
  - *Fix:* Rename theorem display titles to encode experiment and decision class, for example "Unsigned Common-Map Distance Converse," "Unsigned Point-Indexed Distance Converse," "Signed Fixed-Geometry Lower Bound," and "Conditional Signed-Distance Matched Frontier."
- **[minor·prose] Discussion and Extensions** — The discussion sentence "The common normalization ... should be read as an agreement of established lower scales across the two experiments" risks blurring the fact that the unsigned side establishes lower bounds, while the signed side has a conditional two-sided rate.
  - *Fix:* Revise the comparison sentence to distinguish the delivered objects: the unsigned experiment has common-map and point-indexed lower scales, and the signed experiment has the same lower scale plus a conditional upper bound under \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\).
- **[minor·prose] Reproducibility Note** — The reproducibility note remains too broad for reader-facing prose. It lists many checked objects and risks duplicating the verification table rather than explaining the scope of checked implications in a way useful to econometric readers.
  - *Fix:* Condense the note to one paragraph: state that the displayed formal definitions and theorem implications were checked, that the signed upper and frontier use the three named analytic inputs as hypotheses, and that the surrounding econometric interpretation is supported by the cited literature.

## Questions for authors
- Which specific minimax lower-bound statement in Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu (2026) is closest to the unsigned point-indexed lower bound, and how do the law class and information set differ?
- Are the three analytic inputs in \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) intended to be imported from CTY as published results, or are they assumptions tailored to the displayed \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) class?
- Can the paper give a short example of an empirical boundary RD workflow corresponding to the unsigned information set, distinct from the signed known-geometry workflow?
