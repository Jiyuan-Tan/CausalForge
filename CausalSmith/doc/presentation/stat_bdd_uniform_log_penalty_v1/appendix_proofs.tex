\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}]
Throughout, \(Z=(Y(0),Y(1),X)\) denotes one potential-outcome observation and \(Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\) its observed outcome, as in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}; \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\) is the sample and \(P^n\) its law.  For \(u\in\mathbb R^{p+1}\) we write
\[
\lVert u\rVert=\max_{0\leq j\leq p}|u_j|,
\qquad
\lVert u\rVert_2=\left(\sum_{j=0}^p u_j^2\right)^{1/2},
\]
so that \(\lVert u\rVert_2\leq(p+1)\lVert u\rVert\), the norm \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) being the one appearing in \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound} and in \cref{obj:synth_62}.  Fix \(p\geq0\), \(\nu\geq2\) and \(L\geq4\), and abbreviate the bandwidth and clipping schedules of the statement by
\[
h_n=a_n=\left({\log n\over n}\right)^{1/4},
\qquad
B_n=a_n^{-1/3},
\]
as in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates} and \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}.  We write \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)\), \(\widehat m^B_{t,x}(h)\), \(\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)\) and \(\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)\) for the objects of \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}, \(\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\) for the population Gram of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, \(\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\) for the population coefficient of \cref{obj:synth_128}, and \(\tau_P\) for the target of \cref{obj:synth_46}.

The first input of \cref{obj:synth_139} fixes what is being estimated: it is what makes \(\tau_P(x)\) the boundary contrast approached by the two one-sided signed-distance conditional-mean limits, and the bias input of the same collection is stated relative to that same target through \cref{obj:synth_39}.  Everything below is quantitative and uses the bias and expected-maximal inputs, \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}, and the envelopes of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}.
% lean: CtyDistanceIdentification

\textbf{Step 0: the constants.}  Let \(C_b>0\) be the constant supplied by the uniform first-order bias input of the statement and put
\[
\bar C_b=C_b+1>0 .
\]
Let \(C_m>0\) be the constant supplied by the expected maximal bounds of the statement, and let \(C_{\mathrm{sc}}=C_{\mathrm{sc}}(p,L)>0\) be the constant of \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}.  Define
\[
C
=
\bar C_b+32L^3(p+1)+16L(p+1)C_{\mathrm{sc}}+8L^2(p+1)C_m
>0 .
\]
All four summands are positive because \(L\geq4>0\).  This is the constant of the conclusion; the threshold \(N\) is fixed at the end of Step 2.
% lean: cty_a1_a2_winsorized_expected_outer_upper

\textbf{Step 1: the four bandwidth calibrations.}  Let \(n\geq3\) and write \(h=h_n\), \(B=B_n\).  We claim
\[
B^{-3}\leq h,
\qquad
\sqrt{\log(B/h)\over nh^2}\leq2h,
\qquad
{B\log(B/h)\over nh^2}\leq2h,
\qquad
\sqrt{\log(h^{-1})\over nh^2}\leq h .
\]
First, \(\log n<n\) for every \(n\geq1\), so \(\log n/n<1\) and hence \(0<h<1\) and \(B=h^{-1/3}\geq1\).  Raising the definition of \(a_n\) to the fourth power gives the identity that drives every calibration,
\[
h^4={\log n\over n},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
{\log n\over nh^2}=h^2 .
\]
% lean: t4_frontierRate_fourth_power
The first claim is the exponent computation \(B^{-3}=(h^{-1/3})^{-3}=h\).
% lean: t4_winsorization_power
For the remaining three, take logarithms in the definition of \(h\):
\[
\log(h^{-1})={\log n-\log\log n\over4},
\qquad
\log(B/h)=\log\bigl(h^{-4/3}\bigr)={4\over3}\log(h^{-1}),
\]
and, since \(n\geq3\) forces \(\log\log n>0\), both are nonnegative and satisfy
\[
\log(h^{-1})\leq\log n,
\qquad
\log(B/h)\leq{\log n\over3}\leq2\log n .
\]
Dividing by \(nh^2\) and using the displayed identity,
\[
{\log(B/h)\over nh^2}\leq{2\log n\over nh^2}=2h^2,
\qquad
{\log(h^{-1})\over nh^2}\leq{\log n\over nh^2}=h^2 .
\]
Taking square roots gives the second claim, because \(2h^2\leq(2h)^2\), and the fourth claim directly.  For the third, factor out \(B\) and use the previous display together with
\[
Bh=h^{-1/3}h=h^{2/3}\leq1 ,
\]
which holds because \(0<h<1\); thus \(B\log(B/h)/(nh^2)\leq B\cdot2h^2=2h(Bh)\leq2h\).
% lean: t4_frontier_rate_terms_le

\textbf{Step 2: activating the two assumed inputs along the frontier bandwidth.}  The bias input of the statement is asserted along \emph{antitone} bandwidth sequences, and \(n\mapsto a_n\) is antitone only from \(n=3\) onward.  Set therefore
\[
h^\sharp_n=a_{\max\{n,3\}},
\qquad n\in\mathbb N .
\]
Then \(h^\sharp_n>0\) for every \(n\), the sequence \(h^\sharp\) is antitone, \(h^\sharp_n\to0\), and
\[
n(h^\sharp_n)^2=\sqrt{n\log n}\to\infty
\qquad(n\geq3),
\]
so the bias input applies to \(h^\sharp\) with \(\nu'=\nu\) and yields
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu,L}(h^\sharp_n)\leq C_b<\bar C_b .
\]
Since \(h^\sharp_n=a_n\) for every \(n\geq3\), there is \(N_1\) such that
\[
\mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu,L}(a_n)\leq\bar C_b,
\qquad n\geq N_1 .
\]
% lean: t4Bandwidth_antitone
% lean: t4Bandwidth_nh2_tendsto_top
% lean: t4Bandwidth_eventually_eq_frontierRate
% lean: CtyUniformFirstOrderBias
The expected maximal bounds of the statement are asserted along positive deterministic sequences tending to zero whose heavy-tail regime diverges.  At \(h_n=a_n\), using \(\log(a_n^{-1})=(\log n-\log\log n)/4\) and \(a_n^2=\sqrt{\log n/n}\),
\[
{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}a_n^2\over\log(a_n^{-1})}
=
{4\,n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)-1/2}(\log n)^{1/2}\over\log n-\log\log n}
\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
because \((1+\nu)/(2+\nu)-1/2=\nu/\{2(2+\nu)\}>0\) for \(\nu\geq2\), so the numerator's polynomial factor dominates the logarithm.  Hence there is \(N_2\) such that, for every \(n\geq N_2\) and every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\),
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(a_n)\right]
\leq
C_m\sqrt{\log(a_n^{-1})\over na_n^2},
\]
with \(\mathrm{GramDev}\) as in \cref{obj:synth_62}.  Only this first half of the expected maximal bounds is used; the stochastic control of the score is supplied instead by \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}, whose bounded envelope is created by the winsorization built into \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}.
% lean: t4Bandwidth_maximal_regime_tendsto_top
% lean: CtyExpectedLocalPolynomialMaximalBounds
Finally \(a_n\to0\), so there is \(N_3\) with \(a_n\leq L^{-1}\) for \(n\geq N_3\), which is the small-bandwidth regime in which the population Gram floor, the arm-mass threshold and \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound} are all available.  Put
\[
N=\max\{3,N_1,N_2,N_3\}
\]
and fix \(n\geq N\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) for the rest of the proof.  Write \(h=a_n\) and \(B=B_n=h^{-1/3}\); by Step 1, \(1\leq B\) and \(0<h\leq L^{-1}\).

\textbf{Step 3: a small Gram deviation activates the stabilization guard.}  Put
\[
\delta={1\over2L(p+1)} >0 .
\]
We claim that on the event \(\{\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)\leq\delta\}\) the guard of \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} holds simultaneously at every arm \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and every interface point \(x\in\mathcal B_P\); that is,
\[
(2L)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^p v_j^2
\leq
v^\top\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)v,
\qquad v\in\mathbb R^{p+1} .
\]
Indeed, \(\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)\leq\delta\) means exactly that every entry satisfies \(|\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)_{jk}-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)_{jk}|\leq\delta\), and for any symmetric perturbation with entries bounded by \(\delta\),
\[
\left|v^\top\{\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\}v\right|
\leq
\sum_{j,k}|v_j|\,\delta\,|v_k|
\leq
\delta\sum_{j,k}{v_j^2+v_k^2\over2}
=
(p+1)\,\delta\sum_{j=0}^pv_j^2
=
(2L)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^pv_j^2 ,
\]
the middle step by \(2|v_j||v_k|\leq v_j^2+v_k^2\).
% lean: matrixQuadratic_sub_abs_le_entrywise
% lean: causalGramDeviation_entry_le
The population Gram lower bound of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, valid because \(0<h\leq L^{-1}\) and \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), gives \(v^\top\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)v\geq L^{-1}\sum_jv_j^2=2(2L)^{-1}\sum_jv_j^2\).  Subtracting the previous display leaves \(v^\top\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)v\geq(2L)^{-1}\sum_jv_j^2\), which is the guard.  This is where the factor \(2\) in the guard threshold \((2L)^{-1}\) is spent: it is exactly the room left by the population floor \(L^{-1}\) after the empirical perturbation is absorbed.
% lean: empiricalGramGuard_of_deviation_le

\textbf{Step 4: the guarded coefficient error is the inverted score.}  Suppose the guard holds at \((t,x)\).  Then \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)\) is invertible: if \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)v=0\) then the guard forces \(\sum_jv_j^2\leq0\), so \(v=0\).
% lean: empiricalGramGuard_isUnit
Moreover, for every \(\zeta\in\mathbb R^{p+1}\),
\[
\left\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\rVert_2
\leq
2L\lVert\zeta\rVert_2 .
\]
Indeed, applying the guard to the vector \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\) and then the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\[
(2L)^{-1}\left\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\rVert_2^2
\leq
\left\{\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\}^\top
\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)
\left\{\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\}
=
\left\{\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\}^\top\zeta
\leq
\left\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\right\rVert_2\lVert\zeta\rVert_2 ,
\]
and dividing by \(\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\zeta\rVert_2\) when it is nonzero gives the claim; when it is zero the claim is trivial.
% lean: empiricalGramGuard_inv_mulVec_euclidean_le
On the guarded branch \(\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)=\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\widehat m^B_{t,x}(h)\), so subtracting \(\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\) and using the definition of the winsorized score in \cref{obj:synth_131},
\[
\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)
=
\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}
\left\{\widehat m^B_{t,x}(h)-\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}
=
\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h} .
\]
Selecting the intercept, bounding a coordinate by the Euclidean norm, and converting norms through \(\lVert u\rVert_2\leq(p+1)\lVert u\rVert\),
\[
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}\right|
\leq
\left\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right\rVert_2
\leq
2L\left\lVert\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right\rVert_2
\leq
2L(p+1)\left\lVert\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right\rVert .
\]
% lean: euclideanNorm_le_card_mul_piNorm
% lean: guardedCoefficient_sub_populationCoefficient_le

\textbf{Step 5: the mean of the winsorized score is of order \(B^{-3}\).}  Write
\[
\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)
=
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left\lVert
\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}
-\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]
\right\rVert
\]
for the centered supremum appearing in \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound}, so that for every \(t\) and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\),
\[
\left\lVert\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right\rVert
\leq
\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)
+
\left\lVert\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]\right\rVert .
\]
We claim the deterministic bound
\[
\left\lVert\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h}\right]\right\rVert
\leq
8L^2B^{-3},
\qquad t\in\{0,1\},\ x\in\mathcal B_P .
\]
Fix \(t\), \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and a coordinate \(0\leq j\leq p\), and write the single-observation weight
\[
\xi_j(Z)
=
\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}
K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)\,
r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)_j ,
\]
with \(D^{\pm}_{P,x}\) the signed distance of \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data}, \(K_\square\) the uniform kernel of \cref{obj:def:cty-uniform-kernel}, and \(r_p\) the basis of \cref{obj:synth_71}.  Since \(K_\square\) vanishes off \(\{|D^{\pm}_{P,x}|\leq h\}\) and \(|r_p(u)_j|\leq1\) for \(|u|\leq1\),
\[
|\xi_j(Z)|\leq\mathbf 1\{X\in B_2(x,h)\} ,
\]
with \(B_2(x,h)\) the ball of \cref{obj:synth_43}; the equivalence \(|D^{\pm}_{P,x}|\leq h\iff X\in B_2(x,h)\) is immediate from the definition of the signed distance, whose magnitude is \(\lVert X-x\rVert_2\).  By \cref{obj:synth_131} the \(j\)th coordinate of the winsorized score is the sample average \(h^{-2}n^{-1}\sum_{i\leq n}\xi_j(Z_i)\{\psi_B(Y_i)-r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}/h)^\top\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\}\), so the observations being independent and identically distributed and the summand integrable,
\[
\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\operatorname{score}^{B}_{t,x,h,j}\right]
=
{1\over h^2}\int
\xi_j(Z)\left\{\psi_B(Y)-r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)^\top\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}dP .
\]
% lean: winsorizedCenteredScore_integral_eq
Split the integrand by inserting and removing the unwinsorized outcome,
\[
\xi_j(Z)\left\{\psi_B(Y)-r_p^\top\beta^*\right\}
=
\underbrace{\xi_j(Z)\left\{Y-r_p^\top\beta^*\right\}}_{\text{population residual}}
+
\underbrace{\xi_j(Z)\left\{\psi_B(Y)-Y\right\}}_{\text{winsorization remainder}} .
\]
The first term integrates to zero: by the normal equations of \cref{obj:synth_128},
\[
{1\over h^2}\int\xi_j(Z)\left\{Y-r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)^\top\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}dP
=
\mathbb E_P\!\left[{1\over h^2}\mathbf 1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)Y\right]_j
-
\left\{\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}_j
=
0 .
\]
% lean: rawPopulationResidual_integral_eq_zero
For the second term, the moment envelope of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} is converted into a deterministic \(B^{-3}\) envelope pointwise in the outcome.  For \(B\geq1\) and \(\nu\geq2\), every real \(y\) satisfies
\[
|y-\psi_B(y)|\leq|y|^{2+\nu}B^{-3} .
\]
If \(|y|\leq B\) the left side vanishes; otherwise \(|y|>B\geq1\), so \(|y-\psi_B(y)|=|y|-B\leq|y|\leq|y|^4B^{-3}\leq|y|^{2+\nu}B^{-3}\), using \(|y|^3\geq B^3\) and then \(|y|\geq1\) with \(2+\nu\geq4\).
% lean: winsorize_tail_le_moment
Integrating this against the selected conditional law of \(Y(t')\) given \(X=z\) and applying the moment envelope \(\mathbb E_P[|Y(t')|^{2+\nu}\mid X=z]\leq L\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} gives, for each arm \(t'\in\{0,1\}\) and each \(z\in\mathcal X_P\),
\[
\mathbb E_P\bigl[\,|Y(t')-\psi_B(Y(t'))|\,\bigm|\,X=z\,\bigr]\leq LB^{-3} .
\]
Note that it is this absolute conditional remainder, and not merely the difference of the two conditional means, that the argument requires, because the remainder is multiplied by the random weight \(\xi_j\) before it is integrated.
% lean: condKer_winsorization_tail_lintegral_le
Combining the weight bound, the consistency relation \(Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, which makes \(|\psi_B(Y)-Y|\) at most the sum of the two armwise remainders, and the density envelope \(f_P\leq L\) together with the planar area bound \(\pi h^2\leq4h^2\), which give
\[
P\{X\in B_2(x,h)\}\leq4Lh^2 ,
\]
% lean: marginal_closedBall_le
we obtain
\[
\int\left|\xi_j(Z)\left\{\psi_B(Y)-Y\right\}\right|dP
\leq
\sum_{t'\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbf 1\{X\in B_2(x,h)\}\,\bigl|Y(t')-\psi_B(Y(t'))\bigr|\right]
\leq
2\cdot LB^{-3}\cdot4Lh^2
=
8L^2B^{-3}h^2 .
\]
% lean: winsorizedResidualTail_enorm_le_localized_arms
% lean: localized_arm_winsorization_tail_lintegral_le
% lean: winsorizedResidualTail_lintegral_le
Multiplying by \(h^{-2}\) and taking the maximum over \(j\) proves the claim, since the bound is free of \(j\), \(t\) and \(x\).
% lean: winsorizedCenteredScore_mean_norm_le

\textbf{Step 6: the pointwise interface loss.}  We claim that for every sample,
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\leq
\left\{\bar C_bh+4L(p+1)\cdot8L^2B^{-3}\right\}
+
4L(p+1)\,\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)
+
8L^2(p+1)\,\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h) .
\]
Two preliminary facts hold at every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\).  First, \(\mathcal B_P\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal X_P)\), and the derivative envelope of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} bounds each arm regression by \(|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\), so
\[
|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L,
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L] .
\]
% lean: euclideanCExtEnvelope_abs_le
Consequently, since projecting onto an interval containing \(\tau_P(x)\) cannot increase the distance to \(\tau_P(x)\), the clipping in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} is harmless:
\[
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\leq
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{1,x}(h)-\widehat\beta^B_{0,x}(h)\right\}-\tau_P(x)\right| .
\]
Second, \(n\geq N_1\) and the definition of the bias ratio in \cref{obj:synth_39} give the deterministic first-order bias bound
\[
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\beta^*_{1,P,x}(h)-\beta^*_{0,P,x}(h)\right\}-\tau_P(x)\right|
\leq
\bar C_bh,
\qquad x\in\mathcal B_P .
\]
Now split on the design.

\emph{Case 1: \(\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)\leq\delta\).}  By Step 3 the guard holds at both arms and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), so Step 4 applies at both arms.  Inserting the two population coefficients and using the triangle inequality,
\[
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{1,x}(h)-\widehat\beta^B_{0,x}(h)\right\}-\tau_P(x)\right|
\leq
\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}}
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}\right|
+
\bar C_bh .
\]
Bounding each summand by Step 4 and then each score by Step 5,
\[
\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}}
\left|e_1^\top\left\{\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\right\}\right|
\leq
2\cdot2L(p+1)\left\{\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)+8L^2B^{-3}\right\} ,
\]
which is
\[
4L(p+1)\cdot8L^2B^{-3}
+
4L(p+1)\,\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B) .
\]
The right-hand side no longer depends on \(x\), so taking the supremum over \(x\in\mathcal B_P\) and adding the nonnegative Gram term gives the claim in this case.

\emph{Case 2: \(\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)>\delta\).}  Here the clipping alone controls the loss: \(|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)|\leq2L\) by construction and \(|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L\), so
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|\leq4L .
\]
On the other hand the case hypothesis and the value of \(\delta\) give
\[
8L^2(p+1)\,\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)
\geq
8L^2(p+1)\cdot{1\over2L(p+1)}
=
4L ,
\]
so the Gram term alone already dominates the loss, and the claim holds because the two remaining terms are nonnegative.  This is the calibration that fixes the coefficient \(8L^2(p+1)\): it is the smallest multiple of \(\delta^{-1}\) that pays for the trivial envelope \(4L\) on the ill-conditioned event.
% lean: stabilizedLocalPolynomial_boundaryLoss_le

\textbf{Step 7: integration.}  Take outer expectations under \(P^n\) in Step 6.  The outer expectation of \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} is monotone, is subadditive on sums, returns a constant on a constant because \(P^n\) is a probability measure, and satisfies \(\mathbb E_P^*[cW]\leq c\,\mathbb E_P^*[W]\) for every finite constant \(c>0\).  Applying these four properties in turn to the three summands of Step 6,
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|\right]
\leq
\bar C_bh+32L^3(p+1)B^{-3}
+
4L(p+1)\,\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h,B)\right]
+
8L^2(p+1)\,\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h)\right] ,
\]
where \(4L(p+1)\cdot8L^2=32L^3(p+1)\).  The measurability, integrability and extended-arithmetic bookkeeping needed to pass from the pointwise inequality to this display is routine and is suppressed.
% lean: outerLIntegral_mono
% lean: outerLIntegral_add_le
% lean: outerLIntegral_const_probability
% lean: outerLIntegral_const_mul_le

\textbf{Step 8: substituting the two maximal bounds.}  The three terms are now evaluated at \(h=a_n\) and \(B=B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\) using Step 1.

The deterministic term.  By the first calibration \(B_n^{-3}=a_n\), so
\[
\bar C_ba_n+32L^3(p+1)B_n^{-3}
=
\left\{\bar C_b+32L^3(p+1)\right\}a_n .
\]

The score term.  Since \(n\geq1\), \(0<a_n\leq L^{-1}\) and \(B_n\geq1\), \cref{obj:lem:cty-winsorized-score-maximal-bound} applies to \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) at this bandwidth and clipping level and supplies
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(a_n,B_n)\right]
\leq
C_{\mathrm{sc}}\left\{
\sqrt{\log(B_n/a_n)\over na_n^2}
+
{B_n\log(B_n/a_n)\over na_n^2}
\right\}
\leq
C_{\mathrm{sc}}\{2a_n+2a_n\}
=
4C_{\mathrm{sc}}a_n ,
\]
the last inequality by the second and third calibrations of Step 1.  Hence
\[
4L(p+1)\,\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{WScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(a_n,B_n)\right]
\leq
16L(p+1)C_{\mathrm{sc}}\,a_n .
\]
This is the step at which the winsorization level is paid for twice over, and in opposite directions: raising \(B_n\) shrinks the deterministic term \(32L^3(p+1)B_n^{-3}\) but inflates the envelope term \(B_n\log(B_n/a_n)/(na_n^2)\).  The choice \(B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\) is exactly the one that places both on the scale \(a_n\).

The Gram term.  By Step 2 and the fourth calibration of Step 1,
\[
8L^2(p+1)\,\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(a_n)\right]
\leq
8L^2(p+1)C_m\sqrt{\log(a_n^{-1})\over na_n^2}
\leq
8L^2(p+1)C_m\,a_n .
\]

Adding the three displays and recalling the definition of \(C\) in Step 0,
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|\right]
\leq
\left\{\bar C_b+32L^3(p+1)+16L(p+1)C_{\mathrm{sc}}+8L^2(p+1)C_m\right\}a_n
=
Ca_n .
\]
Every quantity in \(C\) was fixed in Step 0, before \(P\) and \(n\), so the bound is uniform over the law class, and taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]
\leq
Ca_n,
\qquad n\geq N ,
\]
which is the assertion, with \(C>0\) and \(N\) as constructed.
% lean: stabilizedLocalPolynomialOuterRisk
% lean: cty_a1_a2_winsorized_expected_outer_upper
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders}]
\emph{Step 1 (constants).}
Fix \(p\geq 0\) and put \(q=p+1\).  Let \(L_0=L_0(p)\) be the envelope threshold
supplied by \cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}, so
\(L_0\geq 48\).  Fix \(\nu\geq 2\) and \(L\geq L_0\), and let
\(c_\square,A,C,C_0,\delta_0\) be the positive constants that lemma attaches to
\((p,\nu,L)\).  Set
\[
z=128\,q\,C_0,\qquad
\gamma=\min\{1,z^{-1}\},\qquad
d=\frac12\bigl\{1-\exp(-1/2)\bigr\},\qquad
c=\frac{\gamma d}{8}.
\]
Then \(\gamma>0\), \(d>0\) and \(c>0\).  Since \(0<\gamma\leq 1\) we have
\(\gamma^4\leq\gamma\), and \(\gamma\leq z^{-1}\) therefore gives the budget
inequality
\[
128\,q\,C_0\,\gamma^4\leq 128\,q\,C_0\,\gamma= z\gamma\leq 1 .
\]
% lean: a1a2_finite_max_eventually_lower

\emph{Step 2 (the logarithmic packing budget).}
We first record, as an eventual statement in \(n\), the calibration that will
put the hypercube of \cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}
inside the range of \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}.  For
\(n\geq2\) the rate \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) of \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates}
satisfies \(n\,a_n^4=\log n\), so the budget inequality above gives
\[
C_0\,n\,(\gamma a_n)^4
=C_0\gamma^4\,n\,a_n^4
=C_0\gamma^4\log n
\leq\frac{\log n}{128\,q}.
\]
Also \(\log a_n=\frac14(\log\log n-\log n)\), so every integer \(M\) with
\(M\geq c_\square(\gamma a_n)^{-1/q}\) obeys
\[
\log M
\geq
\log c_\square-\frac1q\log\gamma
-\frac1{4q}\bigl(\log\log n-\log n\bigr).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\) we have \(\log\log n\leq\frac12\log n\), whence
\(-\frac1{4q}(\log\log n-\log n)\geq\frac1{8q}\log n\); and, since
\(\log n\to\infty\), also
\(\log c_\square-\frac1q\log\gamma\geq-\frac1{32q}\log n\).  For such \(n\) the
last display yields
\[
\log M\geq-\frac1{32q}\log n+\frac1{8q}\log n\geq\frac1{32q}\log n .
\]
Combining the two estimates, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every integer
\(M\geq c_\square(\gamma a_n)^{-1/q}\),
\[
C_0\,n\,(\gamma a_n)^4
\leq\frac{\log n}{128\,q}
=\frac14\cdot\frac{\log n}{32\,q}
\leq\frac14\log M .
\]
% lean: causalScaledFrontier_eventually_klBudget

The de-Poissonization remainder \(\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}\) decays exponentially in
\(n\), while \(a_n\) decays only polynomially, so
\(\exp\{-n(1-\log 2)\}=o(a_n)\).
% lean: poisson_remainder_isLittleO_frontier
Since \(a_n\to0\), we may therefore fix \(N\geq2\) such that, for every \(n\geq N\),
\[
\gamma a_n\leq\delta_0,
\qquad
2L\exp\{-n(1-\log 2)\}\leq c\,a_n,
\]
and the displayed logarithmic packing budget holds.

\emph{Step 3 (the hard family at scale \(\Delta\)).}
Fix \(n\geq N\) and a known-geometry point-indexed rule
\(\widehat\tau_n\in\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n\), and choose a family of Borel
sections \(\{T_{n,G,x}\}\) representing it as in
\cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-decision-class}.  Put
\(\Delta=\gamma a_n\), so \(0<\Delta\leq\delta_0\).  Applying
\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders} at separation
\(\Delta\) produces an integer \(M\), a radius \(w=A\Delta^{1/q}\), the disk
mass \(\rho=\pi w^2/36>0\), centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) on the middle of the
bottom edge with pairwise disjoint disks \(S_j=B_2(x_j,w)\subseteq[-3,3]^2\),
laws \(P_\omega\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) indexed by \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\)
all carrying the same fixed rectangular geometry
\[
\mathcal X=[-3,3]^2,\quad
\mathcal A_1=[-1,1]\times[0,2],\quad
\mathcal A_0=\mathcal X\setminus\mathcal A_1,\quad
\mathcal B=\partial\mathcal A_1,
\]
and conditional signed-distance laws \(Q_{j,b}\) with
\(M\geq c_\square\Delta^{-1/q}\).  In particular \(M\geq 1\), because
\(c_\square\Delta^{-1/q}>0\).
% lean: cty_a1_a2_rectangle_angular_hypercube

\emph{Step 4 (per-coordinate divergence budget).}
The Kullback--Leibler clause of
\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}, the identity
\(\rho=\pi w^2/36\), and \(\pi\leq4\) give, for every \(j\),
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\cdot 2n\rho
\leq
\frac{C_0\Delta^4}{w^2}\cdot 2n\,\frac{\pi w^2}{36}
=
C_0\,n\,\Delta^4\cdot\frac{\pi}{18}
\leq
C_0\,n\,\Delta^4
\leq
\frac14\log M ,
\]
where the last inequality uses the logarithmic packing budget above and
\(M\geq c_\square(\gamma a_n)^{-1/q}\).
% lean: cty_a1_a2_rectangle_angular_hypercube, a1a2_finite_max_eventually_lower

\emph{Step 5 (target values at the centers).}
For \(j\leq M\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\) let \(\epsilon(j,b)\in\{0,1\}^M\) be the
vertex with \(j\)th coordinate \(b\) and all other coordinates \(0\), and put
\[
v_{j,b}=\tau_{P_{\epsilon(j,b)}}(x_j).
\]
Because the treatment-effect target at \(x_j\) depends on the vertex only
through its \(j\)th coordinate, and because flipping that coordinate moves the
target at \(x_j\) by exactly \(\Delta\)
(\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}),
\[
\tau_{P_\omega}(x_j)=v_{j,\omega_j}
\qquad\text{for every }\omega,
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|v_{j,1}-v_{j,0}|=\Delta .
\]
% lean: A1A2HypercubeAt, cty_a1_a2_rectangle_angular_hypercube

\emph{Step 6 (the Poissonized experiment).}
For a probability law \(Q\) on a standard Borel space and \(\lambda\geq0\), write
\(\Pi_\lambda(Q)\) for the law of the finite marked configuration obtained by
drawing \(N\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)\) points independently from \(Q\),
attaching to each point an independent mark uniform on \((0,1]\), and listing
the points in increasing order of their marks; the marks are independent of the
points and almost surely distinct, so they only fix a canonical ordering.  For
a configuration \(s\) write \(|s|\) for its number of points and, when
\(|s|\geq n\), write \((Y_1,X_1),\ldots,(Y_n,X_n)\) for its first \(n\) points.
Let \(G_0\) be the common known geometry of the hypercube, which is the same
tuple for every vertex because all \(P_\omega\) share
\(\mathcal X,\mathcal A_0,\mathcal A_1,\mathcal B\), the Euclidean metric and
\(K_\square\).  Define the Poissonized value of the rule at a point \(x\) by
\[
\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x;s)
=
\begin{cases}
T_{n,G_0,x}\bigl((Y_i,D^{\pm}_{x,i}):1\leq i\leq n\bigr),&|s|\geq n,\\
0,&|s|<n,
\end{cases}
\qquad
D^{\pm}_{x,i}=\bigl\{\mathbf 1(X_i\in\mathcal A_1)-\mathbf 1(X_i\in\mathcal A_0)\bigr\}
\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2 ,
\]
and the Poissonized maximum loss at a vertex \(\omega\) by
\[
\Lambda_\omega(s)=\max_{1\leq j\leq M}
\bigl|\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;s)-v_{j,\omega_j}\bigr| .
\]
Both are measurable, each section \(T_{n,G_0,x_j}\) being Borel.
% lean: globalCausalPackingPoissonValue, globalCausalPackingPoissonLoss

\emph{Step 7 (independent blocks and their compressions).}
Split a configuration drawn from \(\Pi_{2n}(P_\omega)\) according to the
partition of \([-3,3]^2\) into the pairwise disjoint disks \(S_1,\ldots,S_M\)
and their complement.  Because thinning a marked Poisson configuration by a
measurable partition produces independent marked Poisson configurations, the
blocks
\[
Z_j=\{\text{points with }\operatorname{score}\in S_j\}\ (1\leq j\leq M),
\qquad
Z_0=\Bigl\{\text{points with }\operatorname{score}\notin\bigcup_{k=1}^M S_k\Bigr\}
\]
are independent, \(Z_j\sim\Pi_{2n\rho}\bigl(P_\omega(\,\cdot\mid \operatorname{score}\in S_j)\bigr)\),
and \(Z_0\) is the corresponding marked Poisson configuration built from the
normalized law of \(P_\omega\) off \(\bigcup_{k}S_k\) at the complementary
intensity.  By the bit-structure clause of
\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders} the law of
\(Z_j\) depends on \(\omega\) only through \(\omega_j\), each disk having the
same mass \(\rho\), while the law of \(Z_0\) is common across all vertices.
% lean: causalPackingExperiment_synthesis_law

Compress the \(j\)th block by replacing each of its points by its signed
observation at \(x_j\) under the common geometry, keeping its mark; the
compressed blocks \(\widetilde S_1,\ldots,\widetilde S_M\) are the compressed
coordinates required by \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}:
\[
\widetilde S_j=c_j(Z_j),
\qquad
c_j:(Y,X,\text{mark})\longmapsto
\bigl(Y,\ \{\mathbf 1(X\in\mathcal A_1)-\mathbf 1(X\in\mathcal A_0)\}
\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2,\ \text{mark}\bigr).
\]
By the signed-observation clause of
\cref{obj:lem:cty-a1-a2-rectangle-angular-hypercube-all-orders}, the point law
of the compressed \(j\)th block at bit \(b\) is exactly \(Q_{j,b}\), so
\[
\mathcal L(\widetilde S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)=\Pi_{2n\rho}(Q_{j,b}),
\qquad b\in\{0,1\}.
\]
% lean: compressedCausalPackingCellExperiment_eq
Poisson tensorization gives
\(\operatorname{KL}\) equal to \(2n\rho\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\) for
the two unordered marked configurations, and mark-ordering is a measurable map,
which cannot increase divergence; hence, with the per-coordinate divergence
bound above,
\[
\operatorname{KL}\bigl(\mathcal L(\widetilde S_j\mid\Omega_j=0),
\mathcal L(\widetilde S_j\mid\Omega_j=1)\bigr)
\leq
2n\rho\,\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
\leq
\frac14\log M .
\]
% lean: klDiv_finiteMeasureMarkedPoissonLaw, compressedCoordinateLaw_klDiv_le

\emph{Step 8 (direct-product testing bound).}
Let \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) be independent uniform bits, so that the
vertex \(\Omega=(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M)\) is uniform on \(\{0,1\}^M\).  The
block product experiment first draws the common block and the cell blocks
conditionally independently according to the bit-conditional laws above, and
then superposes those blocks; at vertex \(\omega\), the superposed configuration
has the canonical marked-Poisson law \(\Pi_{2n}(P_\omega)\).  Attach to
coordinate \(j\) the midpoint decoder
\[
\widehat\Omega_j
=\mathbf 1\bigl\{|\,\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;\cdot)-v_{j,1}|
\leq|\,\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;\cdot)-v_{j,0}|\bigr\}.
\]
The configuration feeding \(\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;\cdot)\) is obtained by
superposing the compressed blocks \(c_j(Z_k)\), \(k\neq j\), the compressed
ancillary block \(c_j(Z_0)\), and the already compressed block
\(\widetilde S_j\); the raw \(j\)th block therefore enters only through
\(\widetilde S_j\).  Hence \(\widehat\Omega_j\) is a measurable function of
\((\widetilde S_j,(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M),Z_0)\) that is unchanged when the raw
coordinate vector is altered only in coordinate \(j\) while
\((\widetilde S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\) is held fixed.
% lean: causalPackingPoissonDecoder_local
All hypotheses of \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product} are thus
met, and its Kullback--Leibler consequence with \(\kappa=1/4\), fed by the
compressed-coordinate divergence bound, gives for the block experiment
\[
\Pr\{\text{at least one decoding error}\}
\geq
\frac12\bigl\{1-\exp(-M^{3/4}/2)\bigr\}
\geq
\frac12\bigl\{1-\exp(-1/2)\bigr\}
=d,
\]
the middle inequality because \(M\geq1\) forces \(M^{3/4}\geq1\).
% lean: causalPackingCoordinatewiseError_lower_bound

\emph{Step 9 (from decoding error to loss, and averaging).}
If the midpoint decoder is wrong at coordinate \(j\) under the block product
experiment, then the estimate is at least as close to the wrong value as to the
true one, so the separation \(|v_{j,1}-v_{j,0}|=\Delta\) forces
\[
\widehat\Omega_j\neq\omega_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bigl|\widehat\tau^{\Pi}_n(x_j;s)-v_{j,\omega_j}\bigr|\geq\frac{\Delta}{2}.
\]
% lean: midpointDecoder_wrong_bit_error
After superposition, this is a statement under the canonical marked-Poisson law
\(\Pi_{2n}(P_\omega)\).  Consequently, for every vertex \(\omega\),
\[
\frac{\Delta}{2}\,
\Pr_{\Pi_{2n}(P_\omega)}\bigl\{\exists\,j:\widehat\Omega_j\neq\omega_j\bigr\}
\leq
\mathbb E_{\Pi_{2n}(P_\omega)}\bigl[\Lambda_\omega\bigr].
\]
Averaging this inequality over the \(2^M\) vertices and bounding the average of
the right-hand side by its maximum selects a vertex \(\omega\) with
\[
\frac{\Delta}{2}\cdot
2^{-M}\sum_{\omega'\in\{0,1\}^M}
\Pr_{\Pi_{2n}(P_{\omega'})}\bigl\{\exists\,j:\widehat\Omega_j\neq\omega'_j\bigr\}
\leq
\mathbb E_{\Pi_{2n}(P_\omega)}\bigl[\Lambda_\omega\bigr].
\]
The left factor is the average block-product decoding-error probability bounded
below in the direct-product display, transported through the superposition map;
and \((\Delta/2)d=(\gamma a_n/2)d=4c\,a_n\) by the definition of \(c\).  Therefore
\[
4c\,a_n\leq\mathbb E_{\Pi_{2n}(P_\omega)}\bigl[\Lambda_\omega\bigr].
\]
% lean: exists_vertex_causalPoissonLoss_ge_coordinatewiseError

\emph{Step 10 (de-Poissonization).}
Write \(P=P_\omega\).  The hypercube construction gives
\(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) and the fixed rectangular geometry, so
\(G_P=G_0\) and each center lies on
\(\mathcal B_P=\partial([-1,1]\times[0,2])\).  Define the finite packing loss of
the rule on a sample \(W\) of size \(n\) by
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}(W)
=\max_{1\leq j\leq M}
\bigl|\widehat\tau_{n,P}(x_j)-\tau_P(x_j)\bigr|,
\]
which is measurable in \(W\) because each section \(T_{n,G_P,x_j}\) is Borel and
\(W\mapsto U^{\pm}_{n,P}(x_j)\) is measurable.
% lean: causalFinitePackingLoss, causalFinitePackingLoss_measurable
On the event \(\{|s|\geq n\}\) the Poissonized value at \(x_j\) is the value of
the rule on the retained prefix, because \(G_0=G_P\) makes the two signed
observations at \(x_j\) coincide, and \(\tau_P(x_j)=v_{j,\omega_j}\) by the
target identity above; hence
\[
\Lambda_\omega(s)=\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\bigl((Y_i,X_i)_{i\leq n}\bigr)
\qquad\text{whenever }|s|\geq n .
\]
% lean: globalCausalPackingPoissonLoss_eq_finitePackingLoss
On the complementary event the Poissonized value is \(0\), so
\(\Lambda_\omega(s)\leq\max_j|v_{j,\omega_j}|\); and since the two
potential-outcome regressions of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} satisfy
\(\sup_{x\in\mathcal X_P}|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\), the target
\(\tau_P=\mu_{1,P}-\mu_{0,P}\) of \cref{obj:synth_46} obeys
\[
|v_{j,\omega_j}|=|\tau_P(x_j)|\leq 2L .
\]
% lean: causalEuclideanCExtEnvelope_abs_le
Conditionally on \(\{|s|\geq n\}\) the first \(n\) points of the configuration
are i.i.d.\ from \(P\), and \(\Pr\{\mathrm{Pois}(2n)<n\}\leq\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}\),
so splitting the Poissonized expectation over the two events gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\Pi_{2n}(P)}\bigl[\Lambda_\omega\bigr]
\leq
\int \ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\,dP^n
+2L\exp\{-n(1-\log 2)\}.
\]
% lean: globalCausalPackingPoissonRisk_le_fixedRisk_add_tail, poisson_two_n_lower_tail
By the choice of \(N\) the second term is at most \(c\,a_n\).  Combining this
upper bound with the preceding Poissonized lower bound and weakening
\(4c\,a_n\) to \(2c\,a_n\),
\[
2c\,a_n\leq \int \ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\,dP^n+c\,a_n.
\]
Cancelling the finite common term \(c\,a_n\) yields
\[
c\,a_n\leq \int \ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\,dP^n .
\]

\emph{Step 11 (passage to the two risks).}
Each \(x_j\) lies on \(\mathcal B_P\), so pointwise in the sample
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}
\leq
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\bigl|\widehat\tau_{n,P}(x)-\tau_P(x)\bigr| .
\]
The finite packing loss is measurable, so its integral is at most the outer
expectation of the boundary supremum.  Hence
\[
c\,a_n
\leq
\mathbb E_P^*\Bigl[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\bigl|\widehat\tau_{n,P}(x)-\tau_P(x)\bigr|\Bigr].
\]
% lean: causalFinitePackingLoss_le_boundaryLoss, lintegral_le_outerLIntegral_of_measurable, causalOuterLIntegral_mono
Since \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\), taking the supremum over the law class
and then the infimum over \(\widehat\tau_n\in\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n\) gives, by
\cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk},
\[
c\,a_n\leq R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)
\qquad\text{for every }n\geq N .
\]
Since \(P\) also satisfies the fixed hard-geometry condition, the same two
steps taken inside the restricted supremum of \cref{obj:synth_126} give
\[
c\,a_n\leq R_n^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}(p,\nu,L)
\qquad\text{for every }n\geq N .
\]
% lean: a1a2OuterRisk_eventually_lower

\emph{Step 12 (normalization and liminf).}
For \(n\geq2\) we have \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}>0\), hence
\[
a_n^{-1}R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)
=
\frac{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}.
\]
The two sequences are eventually equal, so their lower limits agree, which is
the first asserted identity.
% lean: scaledRisk_eventually_eq_normalizedRisk
Dividing the two eventual risk inequalities by \(a_n>0\) gives, for every
\(n\geq\max\{N,2\}\),
\[
c\leq\frac{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n},
\qquad
c\leq\frac{R_n^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n},
\]
and the order property of \(\liminf\) under an eventual lower bound yields
\[
c\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n},
\qquad
c\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}.
\]
This proves the theorem. % lean: cty_a1_a2_point_indexed_log_converse
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse}]
Write \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item By \cref{obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse}, applied with the same \(q\) and \(L\), choose \(c_{\mathrm{PI}}>0\) such that
\[
c_{\mathrm{PI}}
\leq
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}.
\]
Set \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}=c_{\mathrm{PI}}\).
% lean: point_indexed_distance_log_converse

\item The two normalizations of the common-map risk agree eventually. Indeed, for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(a_n>0\) and
\[
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4}R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
=
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n},
\]
because \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) is the reciprocal of \((n/\log n)^{1/4}\).  Taking liminfs of these eventually equal sequences gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4}R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}.
\]
% lean: scaledRisk_eventually_eq_normalizedRisk

\item \emph{For each \(n\), the common-map risk dominates the point-indexed risk:}
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}.
\]
Two facts combine to give this.

First, a common map in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class} is a point-indexed rule in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class}: take the same Borel section \(T_{n,x}=T_n\) at every boundary point, as recorded by \cref{obj:lem:common-map-strict-in-point-indexed}.  Hence the infimum defining \(R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) runs over a superset of the rules defining \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).
% lean: ctyClass_subset_piClass

Second, for a common-map rule the two expectation conventions coincide term by term.  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) and a common map \(T_n\), and write the boundary loss at the sample point \(w\) as
\[
\Lambda_P(w)=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
\bigl|T_n\bigl(V_{n,P}(x)\big|_{w}\bigr)-\mu_P(x)\bigr| .
\]
The map
\[
(x,w)\longmapsto
\bigl|T_n\bigl(V_{n,P}(x)\big|_{w}\bigr)-\mu_P(x)\bigr|,
\qquad
(x,w)\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\times(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2)^n,
\]
is jointly Borel: \(T_n\) is measurable, \((x,w)\mapsto V_{n,P}(x)|_{w}\) is continuous in \(x\) and Borel in \(w\), and \(\mu_P\) is continuous on the compact set \(\mathcal X_P\supseteq\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\) because it lies in the Hölder ball of \cref{obj:synth_36}.
% lean: commonMapBoundarySup_jointlyBorel
Consequently, for every level \(a\),
\[
\{w:\Lambda_P(w)>a\}
=
\Bigl\{w:\exists\,x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P),\ \bigl|T_n\bigl(V_{n,P}(x)\big|_{w}\bigr)-\mu_P(x)\bigr|>a\Bigr\}
\]
is the projection onto the sample coordinate of a Borel subset of the product of the Polish space \(\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\) with the sample space, hence an analytic set.
% lean: commonMapBoundarySup_upperSemianalytic
Analytic sets are universally measurable, so every superlevel set of \(\Lambda_P\) is measurable for the completion of the product law generated by \(P\); therefore \(\Lambda_P\) is measurable on that completed space and its completed integral coincides with its outer expectation,
\[
\int \Lambda_P\,d\overline{P^{\,n}}
=
\mathbb E_P^*\left[\sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}\bigl|\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_P(x)\bigr|\right].
\]
% lean: commonMapBoundarySup_completionMeasurable, ctyBoundarySup_completed_eq_outer

Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) in the last display and then the infimum over the larger point-indexed class in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} bounds \(R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) above by the common-map risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk}, which is the displayed inequality.  Dividing by the same nonnegative normalizing factor preserves this order:
\[
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}
\leq
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}.
\]
% lean: ctyRisk_ge_pointIndexedRisk

\item Applying monotonicity of liminf to the preceding pointwise normalized-risk inequality gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}
\leq
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}.
\]
Combining this with the choice of \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}\) in the first step yields
\[
c_{\mathrm{CTY}}
\leq
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n},
\]
and the equality of normalizations from the second step gives the displayed conclusion.
% lean: cty_same_class_log_converse
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse}]
Fix \(q\geq 1\) and \(L\geq 4\), and write \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item
\emph{A finite packing lower bound, uniform over point-indexed rules.}
Let \(c_0,c_1,c_w^-,c_w^+,c_{\mathrm{rad}},\alpha>0\) with \(\alpha<1/8\) be the constants of \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing}, and put
\[
d=\frac12\left(1-e^{-1/2}\right)>0,
\qquad
c=\frac{c_1d}{4}>0 .
\]
Take \(n\) beyond the threshold of that lemma, and let \(M_n\), \(w_n\), \(m_n\), the boundary points \(x_1,\ldots,x_{M_n}\), the laws \(P_\omega\), and the values \(u_{j,0},u_{j,1}\) be the objects it supplies, with \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\) the common covariate support.  Two of its clauses are used at once: \(M_n\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}>0\), so \(M_n\geq1\); and \(m_n>0\), because \(C_j\) contains a relatively open neighbourhood of the support point \(x_j\) inside \(S\), which therefore has positive covariate mass, and that mass is \(m_n\).
% lean: cty_support_boundary_angular_packing, class_pos_on_relopen

Fix a point-indexed rule \(\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) and law-independent Borel sections \(\{T_{n,x}\}\) representing it as in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class}.

\emph{The Poissonized product experiment.}  Put independent uniform binary coordinates \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_{M_n}\) on \(\{0,1\}\).  Conditionally on \(\Omega=\omega\), let \(N\) be Poisson with mean \(2n\), independent of an i.i.d.\ stream of observations from \(P_\omega\), and attach to each observation an independent mark uniform on \((0,1]\); list the resulting \(N\) marked observations in increasing mark order.  Split this configuration along the partition of \(S\) into the cells \(C_1,\ldots,C_{M_n}\) and their complement, and write
\[
Z_j=\text{the sub-configuration with covariate in }C_j\ (1\le j\le M_n),
\qquad
Z_0=\text{the sub-configuration with covariate in }S\setminus\bigcup_{k}C_k .
\]
Splitting a Poisson configuration along a measurable partition makes the blocks independent conditionally on \(\Omega\).  For \(j\ge1\), \(Z_j\) is the marked Poisson configuration with intensity \(2n\) times the restriction of \(P_\omega\) to \(C_j\), equivalently with mean \(2nm_n\) and the conditional observation law on \(C_j\).  The complement block \(Z_0\) is the marked Poisson configuration with intensity \(2n\) times the restriction of \(P_\omega\) to \(S\setminus\bigcup_k C_k\); when this complement has positive mass this is the corresponding conditional law with that mean, and when the complement has zero mass it is the zero-intensity Poisson configuration.  By the equal-cell-mass, one-bit-locality and common-outside clauses of \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing}, the conditional law of \(Z_j\) depends on \(\Omega\) only through \(\Omega_j\), and the conditional law of \(Z_0\) is common for all values of \(\Omega\), including the zero-intensity case.
% lean: packingFinitePartition, packingCellExperiment, packingCommonExperiment, packingCanonicalComplementLaw_eq, packingExperiment_synthesis_law, synthesizePackingConfiguration

\emph{The compressions and the decoders.}  The compressed blocks required by \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product} are written \(\widetilde S_1,\ldots,\widetilde S_{M_n}\) here, the plain letter \(S\) being reserved for the covariate support; let
\[
\widetilde S_j=c_j(Z_j),
\qquad
c_j:\ \bigl((y,z),\text{mark}\bigr)\longmapsto\bigl((y,\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2),\text{mark}\bigr),
\]
so that \(\widetilde S_j\) records, for each atom of the \(j\)th cell block, its outcome, its distance from \(x_j\), and its mark.
% lean: packingMarkedDistance, compressPackingCell
For coordinate \(j\), superpose in mark order the compressed own block \(\widetilde S_j\), the images under \(c_j\) of the other cell blocks \(\{Z_k\}_{k\neq j}\), and the image under \(c_j\) of the complement block \(Z_0\); call the result \(s_j\), and set
\[
\widehat u_j
=
\begin{cases}
T_{n,x_j}\bigl(\text{first \(n\) atoms of }s_j\text{ in mark order}\bigr), & \text{if }s_j\text{ has at least \(n\) atoms},\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\qquad
\widehat\Omega_j=\mathbf 1\bigl\{|\widehat u_j-u_{j,1}|\le|\widehat u_j-u_{j,0}|\bigr\}.
\]
% lean: assemblePackingDistanceBlocks, packingPoissonValue, packingPoissonDecoder
Each \(\widehat\Omega_j\) is a measurable \(\{0,1\}\)-valued function of \((\widetilde S_j,(Z_1,\ldots,Z_{M_n}),Z_0)\), and it is unchanged when the raw block \(Z_j\) is altered while \((\widetilde S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\) is held fixed, because \(Z_j\) enters \(s_j\) only through \(\widetilde S_j\).
% lean: packingPoissonDecoder_measurable, packingPoissonDecoder_local

\emph{The coordinatewise divergence budget.}  Because \(\Pr\{\Omega_j=b\}=1/2\) for \(b\in\{0,1\}\), the conditional law in \cref{obj:synth_25} is defined; write
\[
Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(\widetilde S_j\mid\Omega_j=b).
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\le 2\alpha\log M_n
\qquad\text{for every }j .
\]
Indeed, under \(\Omega_j=b\), \(\widetilde S_j\) is exactly the block of radii at most \(w_n\) of the marked Poisson experiment generated by the one-observation outcome-distance law at \(x_j\) for the single-bit vertex with \(j\)th bit \(b\).  Restricting to that block is a measurable map, so it cannot increase divergence, and a marked Poisson experiment of mean \(2n\) has divergence at most twice that of the corresponding \(n\)-fold product experiment, which is the compressed-distance divergence bounded by \(\alpha\log M_n\) in \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing}.
% lean: compressedPackingCellExperiment_eq_shortRadiusBlock, compressedCoordinateLaw_klDiv_le, markedPoissonKL_le_two_mul_of_piKL, compressedPackingCellExperiment_klDiv_le

\emph{The direct-product step.}  The uniform bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_{M_n}\), the conditionally independent blocks \((Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_{M_n})\), the compressions \(c_j\), and the decoders \(\widehat\Omega_j\) satisfy all the hypotheses of \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}.  Applying that lemma with \(\kappa=2\alpha<1/4\) and using the previous display gives
\[
\Pr\{\text{at least one decoding error}\}
\ge
\frac12\left[1-\exp\{-M_n^{1-2\alpha}/2\}\right]
\ge d,
\]
the last inequality because \(M_n\ge1\) and \(1-2\alpha\ge0\) give \(M_n^{1-2\alpha}\ge1\).
% lean: coordinatewise_overlap_direct_product, packingCoordinatewiseError_lower_bound

\emph{From decoding errors to loss, and choice of a vertex.}  If \(\widehat\Omega_j\ne\Omega_j\), then the nearest-value decoder has picked the wrong endpoint, so the triangle inequality and the signal separation \(|u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|\ge c_1a_n\) give
\[
\bigl|\widehat u_j-u_{j,\Omega_j}\bigr|\ \ge\ \frac{c_1a_n}{2}.
\]
% lean: midpointDecoder_wrong_bit_error, finitePackingLoss_ge_of_midpointDecoder_wrong
Multiplying the previous two displays and averaging over the uniform prior on \(\{0,1\}^{M_n}\) selects a vertex \(\omega\) such that, writing \(\mathbb E^{\omega}_{2n}\) for expectation in the mean-\(2n\) marked Poisson experiment under \(P_\omega\),
\[
\mathbb E^{\omega}_{2n}\!\left[\max_{1\le j\le M_n}\bigl|\widehat u_j-u_{j,\omega_j}\bigr|\right]
\ \ge\
\frac{c_1a_n}{2}\cdot d
=
2c\,a_n .
\]
% lean: exists_vertex_poissonLoss_ge_coordinatewiseError

\emph{De-poissonization.}  Put \(P=P_\omega\), so \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), \(\mathcal X_P=S\), and every \(x_j\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\).  Define the finite packing loss of the rule at a sample point \(w\) of size \(n\) by
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}(w)
=
\max_{1\le j\le M_n}\bigl|\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}(x_j)-\mu_P(x_j)\bigr|
\quad\text{evaluated at }w ,
\]
a measurable function of \(w\), being a finite maximum of compositions of the Borel sections \(T_{n,x_j}\) with the measurable distance compressions of \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data}.
% lean: finitePackingLoss, finitePackingLoss_measurable_of_pointIndexed
On the event \(\{N\ge n\}\) the first \(n\) atoms in mark order are an exact i.i.d.\ sample from \(P\), and by construction \(\widehat u_j\) is then the value of the rule at \(x_j\) on that retained sample and \(u_{j,\omega_j}=\mu_P(x_j)\); hence the Poissonized maximum equals \(\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\) of the retained sample.
% lean: globalPackingPoissonLoss_eq_finitePackingLoss, canonicalPrefix_finiteSampleMap_packingMarkedDistance
On the complementary event \(\{N<n\}\) every \(\widehat u_j\) equals the fallback value \(0\), so the maximum is at most \(\max_j|u_{j,\omega_j}|=\max_j|\mu_P(x_j)|\le L\), the envelope coming from the zeroth-order derivative bound in \(\mu_P\in\mathcal H^q(\mathcal X_P,L)\) of \cref{obj:synth_36}.
% lean: globalPackingPoissonLoss_le_on_count_lt
Since \(N\) is Poisson with mean \(2n\), a Chernoff bound for its lower tail gives \(\Pr\{N<n\}\le\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}\), and therefore
\[
\mathbb E^{\omega}_{2n}\!\left[\max_{1\le j\le M_n}\bigl|\widehat u_j-u_{j,\omega_j}\bigr|\right]
\le
\mathbb E_{P}\!\left[\ell^{\mathrm{pack}}_{n,P}\right]
+
L\exp\{-n(1-\log 2)\} .
\]
% lean: poisson_two_n_lower_tail, globalPackingPoissonRisk_le_fixedRisk_add_tail
Because \(\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}=o(a_n)\), we may enlarge the threshold on \(n\) so that \(L\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}\le c\,a_n\).
% lean: poisson_remainder_isLittleO_frontier
Combining the last three displays and cancelling one copy of the finite quantity \(c\,a_n\) yields: for every point-indexed rule \(\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) there are \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), an integer \(M_n\), and boundary points \(x_1,\ldots,x_{M_n}\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\) with
\[
\mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[
\max_{1\leq j\leq M_n}
\bigl|\widehat\mu_{n,P}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x_j)-\mu_P(x_j)\bigr|
\right]
\geq c\,a_n .
\]
% lean: packing_finite_max_lower_bound

\item
For such \(P\) and \(x_1,\ldots,x_{M_n}\), the finite maximum is bounded pointwise by the full boundary loss because each \(x_j\) lies on \(\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\):
\[
\max_{1\le j\le M_n}\bigl|\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}(x_j)-\mu_P(x_j)\bigr|
\le
\sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}\bigl|\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{PI}}_{n,P}(x)-\mu_P(x)\bigr| .
\]
The left-hand side is measurable, so its ordinary expectation is bounded above by the outer expectation of the right-hand side, that is, by the quantity appearing in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}.  Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), and then the infimum over all rules in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\), gives a sample-size threshold \(n_0\), distinct from the Poisson count \(N\) introduced in the Poissonized experiment, such that
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\geq c\,a_n
\qquad\text{for every } n\geq n_0 .
\]
% lean: lintegral_le_outerLIntegral_of_measurable_le, pointIndexedDistanceRisk_eventually_lower

\item
For every \(n\geq2\), \(a_n>0\) and
\[
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4}R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}
=
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n},
\]
because \((n/\log n)^{1/4}\) is the reciprocal of \(a_n\).  Thus the two normalized sequences agree eventually, so monotonicity of \(\liminf\) in both directions gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4}R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}.
\]
% lean: scaledRisk_eventually_eq_normalizedRisk

\item
For every \(n\geq \max\{n_0,2\}\), the positivity of \(a_n\) allows division in the preceding eventual lower bound:
\[
c\leq \frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}.
\]
Taking \(\liminf\) preserves this eventual lower bound.  With \(c_{\mathrm{PI}}=c>0\), the asserted equality of normalizations and the lower bound
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}\geq c_{\mathrm{PI}}
\]
follow.
% lean: point_indexed_distance_log_converse
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier}]
1. Apply \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders} for the chosen order \(p\), and let \(L_{\mathrm{conv}}\ge 48\) be the envelope threshold it provides.  Set
\[
L_0=\max\{L_{\mathrm{conv}},4\}.
\]
Then \(L_0\ge 48\).  Fix \(\nu\ge 2\), \(L\ge L_0\), and the three displayed hypotheses of the theorem.  The choice of \(L_0\) gives both \(L\ge L_{\mathrm{conv}}\) and \(L\ge 4\).
% lean: cty_a1_a2_point_indexed_log_converse

2. The converse theorem at \(L_{\mathrm{conv}}\) supplies a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}.
\]
That theorem also records the parallel lower bound for the fixed-geometry risk \(R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)\) of \cref{obj:synth_126}; only the full-risk statement just displayed is used below.
% lean: cty_a1_a2_point_indexed_log_converse

3. By \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}, applied with \(L\ge 4\) and the distance-identification, first-order-bias, and expected-maximal-bound hypotheses, there are \(C_0>0\) and \(N\) such that, for every \(n\ge N\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]
\le C_0 a_n .
\]
% lean: cty_a1_a2_winsorized_expected_outer_upper
Since \(a_n>0\) eventually, division by \(a_n\) and passage to the limsup give
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]}
{a_n}
\le C_0 .
\]
% lean: stabilizedLocalPolynomial_normalizedRisk_limsup_le

4. \emph{The explicit rule is admissible.}  For every \(n\ge 2\) the frontier bandwidth \(a_n\) is positive, so the winsorization level \(B(a_n)=a_n^{-1/3}\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} is well defined, and the estimator is generated by the law-independent family of sections
\[
T_{n,G,x}(u)
=
\max\!\left\{-2L,\min\!\left\{2L,\
e_1^\top\bigl(\widehat\beta^{B}_{1}(a_n;u)-\widehat\beta^{B}_{0}(a_n;u)\bigr)
\right\}\right\},
\qquad u\in(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R)^n,
\]
where \(\widehat\beta^{B}_{t}(a_n;u)\) is the stabilized coefficient of \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} computed from the signed-distance sample \(u\).  This family does not depend on the unknown member law, and each fixed-\((G,x)\) section is Borel in \(u\), because the empirical Gram \(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(a_n)\), the uniform conditioning event \(\{(2L)^{-1}\sum_{j}v_j^2\le v^\top\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(a_n)v\ \text{for all }v\}\), the matrix inverse on that event, the winsorized empirical moment \(\widehat m^{B}_{t,x}(a_n)\), and the clipping map are all Borel functions of \(u\).  Moreover, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), the signed-distance sample built from the known geometry \(G_P\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry} is exactly \(U^{\pm}_{n,P}(x)\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data}, since the treatment indicator is the deterministic function \(\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_{1,P}\}\) of the covariate recorded in \(G_P\).  Hence
\[
\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}\in\mathcal T^{12,\pm}_n .
\]
% lean: stabilizedLocalPolynomial, stabilizedLocalPolynomial_mem
Therefore the minimax risk of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk} is bounded above by the risk of this explicit rule:
\[
R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right].
\]
% lean: a1a2OuterRisk_le_stabilizedLocalPolynomialOuterRisk
After normalizing by \(a_n\), this inequality holds eventually in \(n\), and monotonicity of limsup yields
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]}
{a_n}
\le C_0 .
\]
% lean: cty_a1_a2_winsorized_matched_frontier

5. Finally set
\[
C=\max\{C_0,c\}.
\]
Then \(0<c\le C\).  The lower bound from Step 2, the general inequality
\(\liminf\le\limsup\), and the two upper bounds from Steps 3 and 4 give
\[
c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}

and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]}
{a_n}
\le
C.
\]
These are exactly the asserted frontier bounds.
% lean: cty_a1_a2_winsorized_matched_frontier
\end{proof}
