\section{Related Literature}

Regression discontinuity designs originate in the threshold assignment framework of \citet{Thistlethwaite1960} and the identification analysis of \citet{Hahn2001}, with modern econometric treatments emphasizing local comparisons, bandwidth choice, and inferential refinements \citep{Imbens2008,Lee2010,Imbens2012}. Geographic and boundary designs extend this logic to assignment regions in space, where the score is vector-valued and the estimand is indexed by an interface rather than a scalar cutoff \citep{Keele2015a,Keele2015b,Keele2016,Papay2011,Reardon2012,Wong2013,Choi2018,Diaz2023,Sawada2024,Merlano2025}. Within that literature, \citet{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance} develop distance-based identification and estimation tools for boundary regression designs. Their analysis establishes identification from distance-to-boundary reductions, sequence-scoped bias expansions, and probability-order stochastic control for local-polynomial procedures under their maintained design conditions.

This paper studies the uniform expected-risk consequences of those distance reductions. In the unsigned experiment, the relevant object is the \leanref{S-1}{compact bivariate nonparametric regression world} \leanref{sym:\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{NP}}}{$\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}$}\((L,\leanref{sym:q}{$q$})\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class}, where a rule observes outcomes and unsigned Euclidean distances as in the \leanref{S-2}{coordinatewise distance-compressed observation world} \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data}. The decision problem uses the common-map class \leanref{sym:\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{NP}}_n}{$\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}$} in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class} and its completed-expectation risk \leanref{sym:R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{$R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}$} in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk}. The resulting converse is a same-class statement: over the law class and information set just described, the minimax expected boundary sup-loss has the logarithmic scale \leanref{sym:a_n}{$a_n$} introduced in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates}. The point-indexed extension uses the \leanref{S-4}{sectionwise-Borel decision world with outer expectation} in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class,obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}, thereby characterizing the same compression barrier for rules that may choose a law-independent Borel section separately at each boundary point.

This focus differs from the usual role of distance in geographic RD applications. Empirical distance reductions often serve as low-dimensional summaries of proximity to a boundary or as practical coordinates for local fitting \citep{Keele2015a,Keele2015b,Keele2016,Kendall2026}. Here the distance reduction is part of the statistical experiment: the rule receives the outcome and the scalar unsigned distance from the query point, and the risk is the expected supremum over boundary points. The lower bound therefore speaks to information loss under the compressed observation scheme, rather than to specification choice in a geographic RD regression. Related diagnostic and design tools, including manipulation tests and geographic balance analyses \citep{McCrary2008,Cattaneo2022,Cattaneo2020,Cattaneo2023,Cattaneo2025}, address complementary features of RD credibility; the present comparison is organized around the target, the available data transformation, and the uniform loss criterion.

The signed-distance part connects to local-polynomial boundary estimation. Local-polynomial methods provide the standard approximation architecture for nonparametric regression and RD estimation \citep{Fan1996,Ruppert1994,Calonico2014,Calonico2020,Imbens2019}. Robust bias correction and optimal inference results refine confidence interval construction and coverage for scalar or boundary-local targets \citep{Calonico2014,Calonico2020,Armstrong2020,Kolesar2018}. In contrast, the known-geometry signed-distance experiment fixes the \leanref{S-5}{CTY Assumptions 1--2 potential-outcome boundary design} through \leanref{sym:\mathcal{P}_{12}}{$\mathcal P_{12}$}\((\leanref{sym:p}{$p$},\leanref{sym:\nu}{$\nu$},\leanref{sym:L}{$L$})\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class}, observes signed-distance data in the \leanref{S-6}{local-polynomial signed-distance world} of \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data}, and evaluates treatment-effect rules in the \leanref{S-7}{known-geometry point-indexed outer-risk world} of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-decision-class,obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. Conditional on the three analytic inputs collected as \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) in \cref{obj:synth_139}, the paper assembles an outer-expected minimax equality for this signed-distance problem by matching the signed-distance lower bound with the winsorized, Gram-stabilized local-polynomial upper bound.

The minimax structure draws on classical nonparametric lower-bound and empirical-process ideas. Packing and modulus arguments underlie the rate calculations in nonparametric estimation \citep{Stone1982,Tsybakov2009}, while boundary and set-indexed empirical-process tools support the stochastic controls used in local smoothing problems \citep{Mammen1995,Cuevas2004,Vapnik1971,Pollard1984,VanderVaart1996}. The lower-bound construction in this paper adapts those ideas to boundary-indexed distance compression: many separated boundary locations carry independent binary perturbations, while scalar distance observations constrain the coordinatewise information available to any admissible rule. The empirical-process component enters the signed-distance upper result through the stated analytic inputs and the bounded-envelope maximal inequality used for the winsorized score. Together, these ingredients place the paper between geographic RD methodology and minimax nonparametric theory: the estimand is a boundary treatment-effect or boundary regression curve, the information set is a distance-compressed experiment, and the criterion is uniform expected loss over the displayed law classes.
