\section{Discussion and Extensions}

The preceding results separate two roles of distance in boundary regression designs. For the unsigned experiment, \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse,obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse} establish logarithmic lower bounds for boundary sup-loss when the rule observes only outcomes and scalar Euclidean distances from the query point. The common-map statement applies to the decision class and completed-expectation risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class,obj:def:cty-distance-risk}, while the point-indexed statement applies to the sectionwise Borel class and outer-expectation risk in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class,obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}. These conclusions identify an information barrier created by unsigned distance compression over the compact bivariate law class in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class}.

The signed-distance experiment has a different information set. There the rule observes signed distances generated by the known geometry in \cref{obj:def:cty-known-geometry,obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data} and is evaluated over the A1/A2 law class and risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class,obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. Under the analytic inputs collected in \cref{obj:synth_139}, \cref{obj:prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper,obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier} characterize the expected outer-risk scale attained by the winsorized, Gram-stabilized local-polynomial rule in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}. The role of \citet{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance} is therefore most direct in the signed design: their distance-identification framework supplies the population interpretation of the one-dimensional signed-distance coordinate, while the present result uses the displayed analytic inputs to assemble a uniform expected-risk statement.

The common normalization \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates} should be read as an agreement of established lower scales across the two experiments. In the unsigned setting, the rate is produced by many separated boundary perturbations whose angular locations are hidden by scalar distance observations. In the signed setting, the matched statement is conditional on the three inputs in \cref{obj:synth_139} and is attained by a procedure that uses the treatment side, known boundary geometry, and local-polynomial structure. Thus the comparison concerns the particular law classes, information sets, and risks displayed in the formal statements; it does not collapse the statistical distinction between unsigned and signed distance data.

The scope of the signed-distance result is intentionally exact. The known geometry uses the Euclidean metric and the one-dimensional uniform kernel from \cref{obj:def:cty-uniform-kernel,obj:def:cty-known-geometry}. The law class in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class} imposes rectangular support, bounded continuous density, smooth potential-outcome regressions, continuous variances, a \(2+\nu\) moment envelope, rectifiable interior interface, two-sided small-bandwidth mass, and a population Gram lower bound. These conditions describe the boundary designs for which the signed-distance local-polynomial estimator has uniformly controlled bias, stable local design matrices, and expected stochastic fluctuations. They also align with the geographic RD setting in which boundary location and treatment side are part of the design information \citep{Keele2015a,Keele2015b,Keele2016,Kendall2026}.

The Euclidean and envelope restrictions also clarify the intended empirical interpretation. Geographic RD applications often use distance to a boundary as a practical running coordinate while retaining design knowledge about assignment regions and local geography \citep{Papay2011,Reardon2012,Wong2013,Choi2018,Diaz2023,Sawada2024,Merlano2025}. The signed-distance analysis formalizes that information set through the known-geometry class, whereas the unsigned analysis formalizes a more compressed experiment in which angular and side information are absent from the observation available at a query point. The results therefore organize comparisons by the data transformation and target risk, rather than by a universal ranking of distance-based empirical strategies.

\paragraph{Open questions.}
The unsigned results in \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse,obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse} are lower bounds only and establish no matching unsigned upper bound here. The signed-distance matched rate in \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier} is conditional on \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\), so a fully self-contained derivation of the signed-distance upper ingredients within the same displayed law class remains an open direction. Further extensions include non-Euclidean metrics, kernels beyond \(K_\square\), alternative interface regularity classes, and risk criteria adapted to inference rather than uniform expected loss.
