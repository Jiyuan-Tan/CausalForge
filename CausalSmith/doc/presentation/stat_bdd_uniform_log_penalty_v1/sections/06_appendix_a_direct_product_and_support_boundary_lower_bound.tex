\section{Appendix A: Direct-Product and Support-Boundary Lower Bound}

This appendix records the finite-experiment ingredients used to derive the unsigned logarithmic lower bounds. The argument has two components. The first is an overlap product inequality for many binary coordinates after each coordinate has been compressed. The second is a support-boundary hypercube inside the nonparametric class of \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class}, constructed so that the boundary regression values differ at many separated points while the corresponding unsigned distance experiments remain close coordinate by coordinate. These ingredients yield the common-map converse in \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse}; after the strict inclusion statement below, the same construction yields the point-indexed converse in \cref{obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse}.

The direct-product step is stated abstractly because the same form is also useful for the signed-distance lower experiment. It converts many small coordinatewise overlaps into a lower bound on the probability of at least one decoding error.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}[Coordinatewise overlap product]
Fix an integer \(M\geq 1\). Assume the following conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Spaces.)} The spaces carrying \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M,S_1,\ldots,S_M\) are standard Borel.
\item \textbf{(Binary coordinates.)} The variables \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) are independent and uniformly distributed on \(\{0,1\}\).
\item \textbf{(Conditional product structure.)} Conditionally on \((\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M)\), the variables \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) are independent, the law of \(Z_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\) for \(1\leq j\leq M\), and the law of \(Z_0\) is independent of \((\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M)\).
\item \textbf{(Compressed coordinate decoding.)} For each \(j\), \(S_j=c_j(Z_j)\) for a measurable map \(c_j\). The decoder \(\widehat\Omega_j\) is a measurable \(\{0,1\}\)-valued function of \((S_j,(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M),Z_0)\) and is unchanged when its raw coordinate vector is altered only in coordinate \(j\) while \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\) is fixed. Define
\[
 Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=b),
 \qquad
 \rho_j=1-\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
\end{itemize}
Then every such decentralized decoder satisfies
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat\Omega_j=\Omega_j\text{ for every }j\}
 \leq
 \frac12\left\{1+\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)\right\}.
\]
Moreover, for every real \(\kappa<1\), if
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\leq \max\{\kappa\log M,0\}
 \qquad\text{for all }j,
\]
then
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one decoding error}\}
 \geq
 \frac12\left[1-\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\}\right].
\]
\end{lemmav}

The invariance requirement in \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product} matches the distance-compressed experiment: when a rule estimates the value attached to coordinate \(j\), the information about the \(j\)th perturbation that remains after compression is summarized by \(S_j\), while the remaining raw coordinates and the background block may be held fixed. The total-variation overlap coefficient measures how often the compressed coordinate laws can be coupled to agree. The Kullback--Leibler consequence gives the logarithmic calibration used in the boundary packing, in the same testing spirit as standard minimax lower-bound arguments \citep{Stone1982,Tsybakov2009}.

The next statement supplies the geometric and probabilistic certificate for the unsigned law class. It places many separated locations on the boundary of a square support and assigns one binary regression perturbation to each local half-disc.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing}[Angular Packing Certificate]
For every integer \(q\geq 1\) and every envelope \(L\geq 4\), there exist constants
\[
c_0,c_1,c_w^-,c_w^+,c_{\mathrm{rad}},\alpha>0,
\qquad \alpha<\frac18,
\]
such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), writing \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), there are an integer \(M_n\), a radius \(w_n\), a mass \(m_n\), boundary points \(x_1,\ldots,x_{M_n}\), laws \(P_\omega\) indexed by \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^{M_n}\), and values \(u_{j,0},u_{j,1}\) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Packing size and scale.)}
\[
c_0 a_n^{-1/q}\leq M_n,
\qquad
c_w^- a_n^{1/q}\leq w_n\leq c_w^+ a_n^{1/q}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Boundary packing.)} With \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\), each \(x_j\) lies on \(\operatorname{bd}(S)\), and for \(i\neq j\),
\[
\|x_i-x_j\|_2\geq c_w^- a_n^{1/q}.
\]
The cells \(C_j=B_2(x_j,w_n)\cap S\) are pairwise disjoint.
\item \textbf{(Model class and support.)} For every \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^{M_n}\), the law \(P_\omega\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) and has covariate support \(S\).
\item \textbf{(Cell masses.)} For every \(\omega\) and every \(j\),
\[
P_\omega(X\in C_j)=m_n.
\]
\item \textbf{(Local dependence on one bit.)} If \(\omega_j=\omega'_j\), then the restricted laws of \((Y,X)\) on the cell \(C_j\) agree under \(P_\omega\) and \(P_{\omega'}\). The restricted laws of \((Y,X)\) on \(S\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_n}C_j\) are the same for all \(\omega\).
\item \textbf{(Boundary signal.)} For every \(\omega\) and \(j\),
\[
\mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)=u_{j,\omega_j},
\qquad
|u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|\geq c_1 a_n.
\]
\item \textbf{(Radial invariance.)} Let \(\omega^{(j)}\) be \(\omega\) with coordinate \(j\) flipped. For every \(\omega\) and \(j\), the laws of \(\|X-x_j\|_2\) under \(P_\omega\) and \(P_{\omega^{(j)}}\) agree. Moreover, the corresponding one-point distance laws agree after restriction to radii \(z_2\geq c_{\mathrm{rad}}a_n\).
\item \textbf{(Compressed-distance KL bound.)} For every \(\omega\) and \(j\),
\[
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
\mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
\mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
\right)
\leq \alpha\log M_n.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing} is the concrete bridge between smooth boundary regression and the abstract product experiment. The Hölder restriction determines the relation between the signal height \(a_n\) and the cell radius \(w_n\), while the boundary packing supplies \(M_n\) separated query points. Local dependence on one bit makes each cell a coordinate of the hypercube, and the radial invariance clause expresses the angular information loss created by unsigned distance observations. The compressed-distance Kullback--Leibler bound then puts those coordinates in the range of \cref{obj:lem:coordinatewise-overlap-direct-product}. This is the finite-sample testing structure that underlies the rate calculation summarized in the main text.

For common-map rules, the decoder associated with a proposed estimator applies the same measurable map to each query point. If the estimator were uniformly accurate on all \(x_j\)'s, the signs of the boundary signals in \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing} would be decoded correctly across all coordinates. Combining the signal separation with the overlap lower bound gives the positive lower scale in \cref{obj:thm:cty-same-class-log-converse}. The argument uses the law class and completed-expectation criterion exactly as defined in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class,obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class,obj:def:cty-distance-risk}.

The point-indexed class enlarges the set of admissible rules by allowing the Borel section to vary with the query point. The following inclusion statement records that relationship before applying the same hypercube construction to the outer-expectation risk.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:common-map-strict-in-point-indexed}[Strict Common-Map Inclusion]
For parameters satisfying
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\geq 1\).
\item \textbf{(Smoothness index.)} \(q\geq 1\).
\item \textbf{(Envelope.)} \(L\geq 4\).
\end{itemize}
the common-map decision class and the point-indexed decision class of \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class,obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class} satisfy
\[
\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\subsetneq \mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

The strict inclusion in \cref{obj:lem:common-map-strict-in-point-indexed} places the two unsigned risks in the expected order of admissible decision classes. For the converse, the relevant fact is that each point-indexed section remains law independent and receives the same unsigned distance data \(V_{n,P}(x)\) from \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-data}. Thus the coordinatewise testing reduction induced by \cref{obj:lem:cty-support-boundary-angular-packing} continues to apply after replacing a single common map by fixed-\(x\) Borel sections. The outer expectation in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} supplies the measurable-majorant convention for the boundary supremum, and the resulting lower bound is the point-indexed logarithmic converse in \cref{obj:thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse}.
