\section{Main Results}

The results now put the two distance-compressed experiments on a common risk scale. The unsigned problem uses the nonparametric regression class and distance data introduced in \cref{obj:def:cty-nonparametric-class,obj:def:cty-distance-data}; the signed problem uses the known-geometry law class, signed-distance data, and treatment-effect risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-class,obj:def:cty-known-geometry-signed-distance-data,obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. Throughout the section, the normalizing sequence is the rate \(a_n\) in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates}.

The first result is the same-class converse for the unsigned common-map experiment. It evaluates exactly the decision class in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-decision-class} under the completed-expectation risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:cty-same-class-log-converse}[Same-Class Log Converse]
For every integer \(q\geq 1\) and every \(L\geq 4\), there exists a constant \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}=c_{\mathrm{CTY}}(q,L)>0\) such that, for the common-map minimax risk \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) in \cref{obj:def:cty-distance-risk} and \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4} R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}
\geq c_{\mathrm{CTY}} .
\]
\end{theoremv}

The theorem establishes that a single law-independent distance rule incurs expected boundary sup-loss at least of logarithmic order \(a_n\) over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\). The packing calculation behind the rate is easiest to read from the separated boundary construction used in the appendix. If the signal amplitude is \(\Delta\), then the construction places \(M\asymp\Delta^{-1/q}\) active locations along the boundary and uses cells with area scale \(w^2\asymp\Delta^{2/q}\). Unsigned distance compression leaves each coordinate with information of order \(n\Delta^4\), so the many-coordinate testing barrier is calibrated by
\[
n\Delta^4\asymp \log M,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\Delta\asymp \left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
The smoothness order \(q\) affects the packing constants and the geometric calibration of \(w\), while the exponent is governed by the fourth-order distance-compression information calculation. In the signed-distance local-polynomial results below, the polynomial order \(p\) likewise affects constants and regularity thresholds while the displayed rate normalization remains \(a_n\).

The next result carries the same logarithmic converse to the point-indexed unsigned class. The point-indexed class in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-decision-class} permits a separate Borel section at each query point, and the risk is evaluated with outer expectation as in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:point-indexed-distance-log-converse}[Logarithmic Distance Converse]
For every \(q\in\mathbb N\) and \(L\in\mathbb R\) satisfying \(q\geq 1\) and \(L\geq 4\), there exists a constant \(c_{\mathrm{PI}}=c_{\mathrm{PI}}(q,L)>0\) such that, with \(R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) the point-indexed outer-expectation minimax risk in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk} and \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4} R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}
\geq c_{\mathrm{PI}}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

Thus the lower-bound scale is a property of the unsigned distance information itself, even when the rule may choose its measurable section point by point. Outer expectation in this statement is the same measurable-majorant criterion introduced in \cref{obj:def:point-indexed-distance-risk}, which is the standard way to state uniform risks over potentially nonseparable boundary index sets \citep{VanderVaart1996}.

The signed-distance lower bound uses the known-geometry experiment in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-decision-class,obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}. It is stated for all local-polynomial orders after an envelope threshold that depends on the order.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders}[Point-Indexed Log Converse]
For every nonnegative integer \(p\), there exists a real number \(L_0=L_0(p)\) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Envelope threshold.)} \(L_0\ge 48\).
\item \textbf{(Moment exponent.)} \(\nu\ge 2\).
\item \textbf{(Uniform envelope.)} \(L\ge L_0\).
\item \textbf{(Risk definition.)} \(R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\) is the signed-distance minimax risk in \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk}.
\item \textbf{(Fixed-geometry risk.)} \(R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)\) is the infimum over the same known-geometry point-indexed decision rules of the same worst-case outer-expected interface sup-loss, with the law supremum further restricted to laws in \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) satisfying the fixed hard-geometry condition.
\end{itemize}
For every \(\nu\) and \(L\) satisfying these conditions, there exists a constant \(c=c(p,\nu,L)>0\) such that, writing \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty} a_n^{-1}R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)
=
\liminf_{n\to\infty}{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\over a_n},
\]
and
\[
c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\over a_n},
\qquad
c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}{R^{12,\pm,\mathrm{fix}}_n(p,\nu,L)\over a_n}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders} establishes the same logarithmic lower scale for the known-geometry signed-distance minimax risk. The second inequality records that the lower bound is already present inside the fixed rectangular geometry from \cref{obj:synth_126}. Consequently, the converse is calibrated by a concrete Euclidean boundary experiment rather than by variation over arbitrary interfaces.

The upper result for the signed-distance experiment combines the estimator in \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator} with three analytic inputs. We collect them in a single object because the expected-risk bound uses distance identification, a uniform first-order bias control, and expected maximal inequalities as assumptions.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_139}[Analytic inputs for the signed-distance upper bound]
Define \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) to mean that the following three signed-distance analytic inputs hold; none is proved in this paper.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(CTY distance identification.)} For every A1/A2 law \(P\) and metric \(d\), the support is rectangular, the density is continuous and positive on the support, both potential outcomes are integrable, both arm regressions have \(C^{p+1}\) extensions, the boundary is a positive-length rectifiable curve, \(d\) is a metric uniformly equivalent to Euclidean distance on the support, and every sufficiently small positive armwise distance slice at every boundary point has finite positive density mass. Under these conditions there is one source-coherent selected conditional law of the observed outcome given signed distance, with armwise mean versions \(\theta_1,\theta_0\) whose one-sided limits at zero satisfy
\[
\tau_P(x)=\lim_{u\downarrow0}\theta_1(u)-\lim_{u\uparrow0}\theta_0(u).
\]
\item \textbf{(Uniform first-order bias.)} There is \(C_b>0\), depending only on \(p,L\), such that for every \(\nu'\ge2\) and every positive antitone deterministic sequence \(h_n\to0\) with \(nh_n^2\to\infty\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu',L}(h_n)\le C_b.
\]
\item \textbf{(Expected maximal bounds.)} There is \(C_m>0\) such that, along every positive deterministic \(h_n\to0\) satisfying
\[
{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2\over\log(h_n^{-1})}\to\infty,
\]
all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) satisfy
\[
\mathbb E_P^*[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)]\le C_m\sqrt{{\log(h_n^{-1})\over nh_n^2}}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_P^*[\mathrm{ScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)]\le C_m\left\{\sqrt{{\log(h_n^{-1})\over nh_n^2}}+{\log(h_n^{-1})\over n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}\right\}.
\]
\end{itemize}
These are exactly the three explicit antecedents represented in Lean by \texttt{CtyDistanceIdentification}, \texttt{CtyUniformFirstOrderBias}, and \texttt{CtyExpectedLocalPolynomialMaximalBounds} and consumed by the Lean-backed upper and matched-frontier theorems.
\end{definitionv}

The first component of \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) is the CTY signed-distance identification condition, which connects the one-sided signed-distance regression limits to the boundary treatment-effect curve \citep{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance}. The second component supplies the uniform first-order local-polynomial bias control familiar from boundary smoothing arguments \citep{Fan1996,Ruppert1994}. The third component gives expected outer bounds for the uniform empirical Gram and score deviations; these are empirical-process inputs of the same type used to control set-indexed local averages \citep{Vapnik1971,Pollard1984,VanderVaart1996}. All three enter the next proposition as assumptions for the stated law class and bandwidth sequence.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-expected-outer-upper}[Expected Outer Upper Bound]
Let \(p\geq 0\) be an integer, let \(\nu\geq 2\), and let \(L\geq 4\).  Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Distance identification.)}  For every A1/A2 law \(P\), every distance map \(d\), and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\) satisfying the CTY identification assumptions at order \(p\), there are signed-distance conditional-mean versions \(\theta_1,\theta_0:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) and one-sided limits \(\theta_1(0+)\) and \(\theta_0(0-)\) such that
\[
\tau_P(x)=\theta_1(0+)-\theta_0(0-).
\]
\item \textbf{(Uniform first-order bias.)}  There is a constant \(C_b>0\), depending on \(p\) and \(L\), such that for every \(\nu'\geq 2\) and every positive antitone deterministic bandwidth sequence \(h_n\) with \(h_n\to0\) and \(n h_n^2\to\infty\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\mathrm{BiasRatio}_{p,\nu',L}(h_n)\leq C_b .
\]
\item \textbf{(Expected maximal bounds.)}  There is a constant \(C_m>0\) such that, for every positive deterministic bandwidth sequence \(h_n\) with \(h_n\to0\) and
\[
\frac{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}{\log(h_n^{-1})}\to\infty,
\]
the following bounds hold for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\):
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{GramDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)\right]
\leq
C_m
\sqrt{\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n h_n^2}},
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\mathrm{ScoreDev}_{n,p,P}(h_n)\right]
\leq
C_m\left\{
\sqrt{\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n h_n^2}}
+
\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}
\right\}.
\]
\end{itemize}
With \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) as in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rates}, take the bandwidth \(h_n=a_n\) and the winsorization level \(B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\) in the winsorized, Gram-stabilized signed-distance local-polynomial estimator clipped to \([-2L,2L]\).  Then there are constants \(C>0\) and \(N\) such that, for every \(n\geq N\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]
\leq C a_n .
\]
\end{propositionv}

The proposition gives a concrete signed-distance procedure with expected outer risk bounded by a constant multiple of \(a_n\). The bandwidth choice balances the first-order signed-distance bias, of order \(h_n\), with the uniform stochastic scale \(\{\log(h_n^{-1})/(n h_n^2)\}^{1/2}\). At \(h_n=a_n\), both terms have order \(a_n\), and the additional heavy-tail term in the assumed maximal bound is controlled under the displayed moment condition. The winsorized-score lemma used in the appendix supplies the bounded-envelope score component needed for this decomposition. The full proposition additionally uses the assumed uniform Gram control and the assumed raw-score, law-uniform, heavy-tail maximal bound with exponent \(n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}\), so the displayed expected-risk conclusion is tied to the full collection \(\mathsf{AI}_{p,\nu,L}\) in \cref{obj:synth_139}.

Combining the signed-distance lower and upper statements yields the matched outer-expected rate over \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\), conditional on the analytic inputs just displayed.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:cty-a1-a2-winsorized-matched-frontier}[Matched Frontier Rate]
For every integer \(p\geq 0\), there exists a constant \(L_0\geq 48\) such that, for every \(\nu\geq 2\) and every \(L\geq L_0\), the following conditions imply the frontier conclusion below:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Distance identification.)} For every A1/A2 law \(P\), every map \(d\) satisfying the CTY identification assumptions for \(p\) and \(P\), and every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), there are selected source-coherent signed-distance conditional-mean versions \(\theta_1,\theta_0:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) whose right and left limits at zero exist and identify
\[
\tau_P(x)=\lim_{u\downarrow 0}\theta_1(u)-\lim_{u\uparrow 0}\theta_0(u).
\]
\item \textbf{(Uniform first-order bias.)} There is a constant depending only on \(p\) and \(L\) such that, uniformly over every \(\nu'\geq 2\) and every positive antitone deterministic bandwidth sequence \(h_n\to0\) with \(n h_n^2\to\infty\), the finite limsup of the normalized uniform first-order bias ratio is bounded by that constant.
\item \textbf{(Expected maximal bounds.)} For this \(p,\nu,L\), there is a constant \(C_{\mathrm{max}}>0\) such that, along every positive bandwidth sequence \(h_n\to0\) with
\[
\frac{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}{\log(h_n^{-1})}\to\infty,
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\), the expected outer Gram-deviation supremum satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\sup_{j,k}
\left|\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h_n)_{jk}-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h_n)_{jk}\right|
\right]
\leq
C_{\mathrm{max}}
\sqrt{\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n h_n^2}},
\]
and the expected outer centered raw-score supremum satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{t\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left\|\widehat m_{t,x}(h_n)-m_{t,P,x}(h_n)\right\|
\right]
\leq
C_{\mathrm{max}}\left(
\sqrt{\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n h_n^2}}
+
\frac{\log(h_n^{-1})}{n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2}
\right).
\]
\end{itemize}
Then there exist constants \(c\) and \(C\) with \(0<c\leq C\) such that, for the signed-distance minimax risk \(R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)\) of \cref{obj:def:cty-a1-a2-outer-risk} and \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\),
\[
c
\leq
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}
\leq
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{R^{12,\pm}_n(p,\nu,L)}{a_n}
\leq C .
\]
Moreover, the explicit winsorized, Gram-stabilized degree-\(p\) signed-distance local-polynomial estimator with bandwidth \(h_n=a_n\) and winsorization level \(B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\), clipped to \([-2L,2L]\), has uniform outer risk over \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) satisfying
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
\left|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
\right]
}{a_n}
\leq C .
\]
\end{theoremv}

The matched statement characterizes the signed-distance known-geometry minimax risk up to constants under the same envelope threshold as the lower bound and under the analytic inputs in \cref{obj:synth_139}. The lower inequality is supplied by \cref{obj:thm:cty-a1-a2-point-indexed-log-converse-all-orders}, while the upper inequality is attained by the explicit winsorized, Gram-stabilized local-polynomial estimator from \cref{obj:def:cty-stabilized-local-polynomial-estimator}. The result places the signed-distance experiment on the logarithmic expected outer-risk scale \(a_n\) for every fixed polynomial order \(p\), moment exponent \(\nu\ge2\), and envelope \(L\) above the stated order-dependent threshold.
