The paper sits in the potential-outcomes and semiparametric program-evaluation tradition. Rubin (1974, 1979) formalized treatment effects and regression adjustment for randomized and observational studies; Rosenbaum and Rubin (1983) established the propensity score as the central balancing score under ignorability; Hahn (1998) and Hirano, Imbens, and Ridder (2003) developed efficiency theory for average treatment effects under unknown propensities. Robins, Rotnitzky, and Zhao (1994), van der Laan and Robins (2003), Tsiatis (2006), and the reviews and texts of Imbens and Wooldridge (2009), Imbens and Rubin (2015), Hernán and Robins (2020), and Kennedy (2022) provide the semiparametric missing-data and causal-functional framework in which consistency, conditional exchangeability, overlap, and outcome-regression functionals are standard primitives.

A second strand studies modern ATE and CATE estimation when nuisance functions are high-dimensional. Belloni, Chernozhukov, Fernández-Val, and Hansen (2017) give high-dimensional program-evaluation methods under approximate sparsity; Chernozhukov et al. (2018) formalize double/debiased machine learning with Neyman-orthogonal scores and cross-fitting for treatment and structural parameters. Yadlowsky (2022), Jiang, Mukherjee, Sen, and Sur (2025), and Celentano and Wainwright (2023) analyze high-dimensional asymptotic regimes where nuisance estimation creates variance inflation, cross-fit dependence, and new debiasing phenomena. For heterogeneous effects, Wager and Athey (2018), Nie and Wager (2021), Semenova and Chernozhukov (2021), Kennedy (2023), and Kennedy, Balakrishnan, Robins, and Wasserman (2024) develop inference, quasi-oracle, debiased-learning, and minimax CATE theory under continuous or structured covariate models. The present result targets the scalar ATE in a finite discrete arbitrary-cell-mass model with a known maximal cell-effect deviation radius, so its rate comparison is with discrete-adjustment ATE theory rather than smooth-model CATE recovery.

The nearest competitor is Zeng, Balakrishnan, Han, and Kennedy (2024; revised 2026). In their high-dimensional discrete observational model for binary outcomes, the usual regression, weighting, and doubly robust estimators have MSE of order \(d^2/n^2+1/n\), while their minimax lower benchmark for unrestricted ATE estimation is \(d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)+1/n\). They also show that exact effect homogeneity yields the faster rate \(d/n^2+1/n\), and their occupancy-weighted collision estimator under approximate homogeneity has an algebraic remainder of order \(\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}^2+d/n^2\), with an additional exponentially small adverse-event term. The present paper keeps the same discrete-confounder causal target and overlap logic, extends the upper analysis to real outcomes under a conditional-second-central-moment envelope, constructs a signed one-mark heavy--light Chebyshev/factorial estimator and an occupancy estimator, combines them by a known-radius selector, and proves a same-class two-sided bracket with upper remainder \(\min\{1,d^2/(n^2\log^2(en)),\sigma^2+d/n^2\}\) and lower radius term \(\sigma^2\min\{1,d^2/(n^2\log^2(en))\}\) plus the exact-homogeneity baseline.

The estimator and converse technology draws on the large-alphabet functional-estimation literature. Paninski (2003), Valiant and Valiant (2011a, 2011b, 2017), Jiao, Venkat, Han, and Weissman (2015), Wu and Yang (2016), and Wu and Yang (2019) established the polynomial-approximation, profile/unseen, and moment-matching toolkit behind rates such as \(S/\log S\) effective sample-size enlargement and minimax entropy risk \((k/(n\log k))^2+\log^2(k)/n\) in large discrete alphabets. Cai and Low (2011), Donoho, Liu, and MacGibbon (1990), Le Cam (1986), and Tsybakov (2009) provide the broader minimax and composite-testing machinery; Hoeffding (1948), Serfling (1980), van der Vaart (1998), Robins et al. (2008), and Robins et al. (2017) supply the U-statistic and higher-order functional-estimation lineage. Lugosi and Mendelson (2019) represents the weak-moment mean-estimation context relevant to handling real outcomes under second moments. The 401(k) application lineage, including Poterba, Venti, and Wise (1994, 1995), supplies the empirical program-evaluation setting used for the Zeng--Balakrishnan--Han--Kennedy comparison.
