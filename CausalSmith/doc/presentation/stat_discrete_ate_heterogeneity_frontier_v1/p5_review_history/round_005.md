# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.8/10 — The paper appears to deliver a technically meaningful verified minimax bracket, but publication would require substantial presentation repairs, tighter claim scoping, and clearer separation between proved results, cited inputs, and open-regime interpretation.

The submission establishes finite-sample upper and lower minimax benchmarks for scalar ATE estimation with finite discrete adjustment, real outcomes, fixed overlap, known scale, and known heterogeneity radius. The main mathematical statements are carefully scoped in the verification contract and the manuscript mostly respects the bracket nature of the result. The contribution is potentially valuable for econometricians studying sparse discrete adjustment, but the paper is difficult to read, contains notation inconsistencies and stale or ambiguous trust-boundary prose, and sometimes elevates auxiliary or open material in ways that obscure the main result.

## Strengths
- The same-class all-alphabet upper and lower bracket is a clear and nontrivial contribution beyond binary-outcome discrete-adjustment results.
- The manuscript states the key conditions explicitly: fixed overlap, arbitrary cell masses, known M and sigma, bounded conditional means, and conditional second moments.
- The estimator comparison is conceptually useful: polynomial rare-cell estimation, occupancy collision estimation, and a known-radius selector.
- The paper honestly identifies the residual shrinking-radius wedge rather than claiming a full uniform minimax rate.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] abstract** — The abstract is technically dense but underspecifies a key operational condition: the selector uses the heterogeneity radius as known input. The sentence "The results give a radius-indexed minimax bracket for discrete adjustment with real outcomes under weak conditional second-moment control" may be read as covering radius adaptation, while the verified estimator is known-radius.
  - *Fix:* Revise the abstract to state affirmatively that the constructive upper bound is attained by a selector that takes the known radius sigma as input, and keep the bracket/matched-regime language unchanged.
- **[major·prose] appendix / Verification note** — The verification note says, "where a result from the literature is a formal dependency, it enters as a published input rather than a Lean-checked step," while the contract records several Zeng-source lemmas as checked declarations with faithful proof audits and no external_dependencies. This creates a stale trust-boundary impression for the displayed binary source lemmas.
  - *Fix:* Replace the generic trust-boundary sentence with a current-scope statement: the displayed binary source lemmas are represented by checked declarations in this artifact, while citations provide origin, attribution, and statistical context; keep the binary collision comparison explicitly conditional on its displayed published guarantee.
- **[major·structure] Related work** — The positioning is adequate in breadth but not yet sharp enough for a leading econometrics journal. The comparison with Zeng et al. gives rates, but it does not crisply distinguish which parts are inherited by embedding, which parts are new real-outcome analysis, and which parts remain a bracket rather than an exact minimax solution for shrinking radii.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact comparison paragraph or table in Related work with columns for outcome type, cell masses, heterogeneity condition, estimator, lower bound, and matched regimes; explicitly identify the new pieces as the real-outcome moment-envelope upper analysis, known-radius selector, affine/radius-channel transfers, and same-class all-alphabet bracket.
- **[major·prose] Main bracket and matched regimes** — The term "frontier" is used repeatedly for the selector benchmark and residual region, but the verified results establish a bracket with a residual shrinking-radius gap. Outside the explicitly open Future work subsection, "frontier" risks suggesting a sharp boundary or exact minimax frontier in all regimes.
  - *Fix:* Rename or qualify uses of "frontier" in reader-facing prose before the Future work subsection, for example "selector benchmark" or "displayed bracket frontier," and reserve "shrinking-radius frontier" for the open-question discussion.
- **[major·structure] Estimators and upper bounds** — The algorithmic presentation of the polynomial estimator gives an operation-count statement but no practically reproducible pseudocode for computing the falling-factorial aggregates. The text says a direct implementation stores "powers or falling-factorial count products" but does not define the exact aggregated formula a reader would implement.
  - *Fix:* Add a short implementation-level display giving the aggregate expression for U_{k,a,j} in terms of N_{ak}^{(1)}, N_k^{(1)}, and S_{ak}^{(1)} or the required falling-factorial products; alternatively state that the algorithm is a mathematical estimator definition and move the operation count to a technical appendix.
- **[minor·prose] global** — There are multiple notation inconsistencies between u_{n,d} and nu_{n,d} in statement renderings and the verification contract excerpts, and the manuscript itself risks confusion by defining u, q, ell, r in several places.
  - *Fix:* Use a single macro/name for the polynomial component throughout, preferably u_{n,d}; audit every theorem statement, table, figure caption, and appendix proof for accidental \nu_{n,d}.
- **[minor·prose] Setup and assumptions** — The model class states fixed overlap and known scale/radius, but the assumptions are not sufficiently motivated economically or statistically. A reader outside minimax theory may not see why conditional means are bounded while outcomes are controlled only by conditional second central moments.
  - *Fix:* Add a short paragraph after the assumptions explaining the modeling role of bounded conditional means, second moments, and fixed overlap, and how these differ from bounded binary outcomes.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The brief supplied to the authors mentions Hahn and Hirano-Imbens-Ridder, Hernán and Robins, and the 401(k) application lineage, but the manuscript omits or only partially uses these references. The omission is not fatal, but it weakens econometric positioning.
  - *Fix:* Add the missing canonical semiparametric and applied-program-evaluation references where relevant, especially Hirano, Imbens, and Ridder; Hernán and Robins; and the 401(k) lineage if the paper invokes the Zeng et al. empirical motivation.
- **[minor·prose] Discussion and limitations** — The limitations section is appropriately titled, but the sentence "confidence intervals, adaptive radius selection, and data-driven choice of M are directions beyond the estimation bracket established here" is the only place where these non-coverage items appear. The main text would benefit from an affirmative scope statement earlier so readers do not infer adaptive claims.
  - *Fix:* In the introduction or setup, add a positive scope sentence such as "The minimax experiment treats epsilon, M, and sigma as fixed model indices, with the estimator using sigma in its deterministic branch rule."
- **[minor·structure] Clarity and presentation** — The manuscript includes many long proof sketches and theorem-local explanations in the main text, which makes the contribution hard to parse. The main estimator and bracket are buried among formal machinery.
  - *Fix:* Shorten main-text proof narration, move detailed proof-roadmap paragraphs to appendices, and add a concise theorem map near the end of the introduction listing the upper bound, lower bound, matched regimes, and open shrinking-radius wedge.
- **[nit·prose] global** — Several titles are sentence case, but title-like labels inside theorem item bullets vary in style and punctuation. This is minor but noticeable in a highly formal manuscript.
  - *Fix:* Apply a final typography pass to all section, subsection, theorem, table, and item labels for consistent sentence case and punctuation.

## Questions for authors
- Can the authors provide an explicit aggregate formula or reference implementation for the polynomial estimator, beyond the ordered-tuple mathematical definition?
- Do the authors intend the paper to claim any adaptive procedure for unknown sigma or M, or is known-radius known-scale estimation the complete constructive scope?
- Which Zeng et al. results are being used only for attribution/context, and which are formal conditional inputs in the current artifact?
