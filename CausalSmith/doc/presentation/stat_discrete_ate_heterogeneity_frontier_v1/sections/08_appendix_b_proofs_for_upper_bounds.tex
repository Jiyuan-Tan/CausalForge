\section{Appendix B. Proofs for upper bounds}

This appendix records the auxiliary inequalities that support the constructive upper bounds in \cref{obj:thm:robust-upper-construction-resolution-all-d,obj:thm:frontier-upper-all-d}. The proof has two mechanisms. The polynomial branch first controls the shifted-Chebyshev coefficients, then bounds same-cell and distinct-cell factorial covariances, then combines those bounds with a deterministic heavy-set plug-in argument. The collision branch controls the occupancy-weighted treated-control contrast on empirically crossed cells. These mechanisms correspond to the two estimator branches introduced in \cref{obj:def:polynomial-handle,obj:def:collision-handle}.

The first ingredient is a coefficient envelope for the shifted-Chebyshev polynomial used in the light-cell component of \cref{obj:def:polynomial-handle}. It converts the polynomial coefficients into a scale bound on cell masses below the light-cell threshold, matching the large-alphabet approximation strategy of \citet{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016Entropy}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:shifted-chebyshev-coefficient-envelope}[Shifted coefficient envelope]
Let \(K\) be a positive integer, let \(B,p\in\mathbb R\), and define, for \(0\le j\le K-2\),
\[
g_{Kj}
:=
(-1)^j
\frac{2^{2j+3}}{K(K+j+2)}
\binom{K+j+2}{2j+4}.
\]
Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Positive scale.)} \(B>0\).
\item \textbf{(Nonnegative mass.)} \(p\ge 0\).
\item \textbf{(Budget bound.)} \(p\le B\).
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}
\left|\frac{g_{Kj}}{B^{j+1}}\right|p^{j+2}
\le
B\,6^K.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:shifted-chebyshev-coefficient-envelope} gives the deterministic side of the light-cell calculation. Once a cell mass is below the approximation scale \(B\), the entire signed coefficient expansion is controlled by \(B6^K\), so the later variance bounds can aggregate the polynomial terms without tracking each coefficient separately.

The next two moment bounds handle the factorial statistics that estimate the light-cell polynomial terms. They are stated for an independent block of size \leanref{sym:m}{$m$} with product law \leanref{sym:Q_P^{(m)}}{$Q_P^{(m)}$}, the block analogue of the observed product experiment in \cref{obj:def:sample-experiment}. The same-cell bound includes the extra covariance contribution created when two statistics use the same adjustment cell, and the distinct-cell bound records the simpler interaction across different cells.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:same-cell-factorial-cross-moment}[Same-cell cross moment]
Let \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) be as in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, let \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), and write \(p_k=P(X=k)\) for the cell mass.  For a block of \(m\) independent observations with joint law \(Q_P^{(m)}\), write \(V_i=Y_i/M\).  For an arm \(c\in\{0,1\}\) and an order \(q\in\mathbb N\), define
\[
U_{k,c,q}
:=
\frac{1}{(m)_{q+2}}
\sum_{i_0,\ldots,i_{q+1}\ {\rm distinct}}
V_{i_0}\mathbf 1\{X_{i_0}=k,A_{i_0}=c\}
\mathbf 1\{X_{i_1}=k\}
\prod_{t=2}^{q+1}\mathbf 1\{X_{i_t}=k,A_{i_t}=c\}.
\]
Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(First order.)} \(j+2\le K\).
\item \textbf{(Second order.)} \(r+2\le K\).
\item \textbf{(Block size.)} \(4(K+2)^2\le m\).
\end{itemize}
Then, for all arms \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\left|\operatorname{Cov}_{Q_P^{(m)}}\!\left(U_{k,a,j},U_{k,b,r}\right)\right|
\le
\frac{2(K+2)^2}{m}\,p_k^{j+2}p_k^{r+2}
+
\exp(1)\left(\frac54p_k\right)^{r+2}
\left(\frac54p_k+\frac{r+2}{m}\right)^{j+2}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:distinct-cell-factorial-cross-moment}[Distinct-cell cross moment]
Let \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) be a model in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, and let \(O_1,\ldots,O_m\) be an independent block with joint law \(Q_P^{(m)}\).  For \(k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), \(a\in\{0,1\}\), and \(j\ge 0\), let \(U_{k,a,j}\) be the ordered distinct-index one-mark factorial statistic from \cref{obj:lem:same-cell-factorial-cross-moment}, equivalently
\[
U_{k,a,j}
=
\frac{1}{(m)_{j+2}}
\sum_{\substack{i_0,\ldots,i_{j+1}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\\ \text{all distinct}}}
\frac{Y_{i_0}}{M}
\mathbf 1\{X_{i_0}=k,A_{i_0}=a\}
\mathbf 1\{X_{i_1}=k\}
\prod_{q=2}^{j+1}\mathbf 1\{X_{i_q}=k,A_{i_q}=a\}.
\]
Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Distinct cells.)} \(k\ne \ell\).
\item \textbf{(Admissible orders.)} \(j+2\le K\) and \(r+2\le K\).
\item \textbf{(Block size.)} \(4(K+2)^2\le m\).
\end{itemize}
Then, for all arms \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\left|
\operatorname{Cov}_{Q_P^{(m)}}\!\left(U_{k,a,j},U_{\ell,b,r}\right)
\right|
\le
\frac{2(K+2)^2}{m}\,
p_k^{j+2}p_{\ell}^{r+2}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

Together, \cref{obj:lem:same-cell-factorial-cross-moment,obj:lem:distinct-cell-factorial-cross-moment} supply the weak-moment covariance control needed for the continuous-outcome polynomial lift. The same-cell inequality carries the additional term that remains after the two factorial arrays overlap in a cell; across distinct cells, the block-size term is the relevant covariance contribution. These estimates use the ordered distinct-index structure familiar from symmetric-statistic calculations \citep{Hoeffding1948,Serfling1980}.

For the heavy-cell part of a fixed polynomial branch, the argument uses empirical arm-cell means with a total value when an arm-cell count is empty. The convention is local to the block calculation and agrees with the totalized plug-in convention used in \cref{obj:def:polynomial-handle}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_6}[Empirical arm-cell mean \(\widehat\mu_{ak}\)]
For a block of observations \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\), define the arm-cell count and outcome total by \[N_{ak}:=\sum_i \mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\},\qquad S_{ak}:=\sum_i \mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\}Y_i .\] The empirical arm-cell mean is \[\widehat\mu_{ak}:=\begin{cases}S_{ak}/N_{ak},&N_{ak}>0,\\ 0,&N_{ak}=0.\end{cases}\]
\end{definitionv}

The next aggregate covariance bound combines the coefficient envelope with the same-cell and distinct-cell moment bounds. It is the main stochastic input for summing the light-cell polynomial contributions over a deterministic set.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:linear-mark-factorial-covariance}[Factorial covariance bound]
For every overlap parameter \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there is a constant \(C_{\epsilon}>0\) such that the following statement holds.  Let \(d,m\), and \(K\) be nonnegative integers, let \(M,\sigma,B\in\mathbb R\), let \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, and let \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\) be deterministic.  For a block of \(m\) independent observations with joint law \(Q_P^{(m)}\), write \(V_i=Y_i/M\).  For the polynomial degree \(K\), define the shifted-Chebyshev coefficients
\[
g_{Kj}
:=
(-1)^j\frac{2^{2j+3}}{K(K+j+2)}
\binom{K+j+2}{2j+4},
\qquad 0\le j\le K-2.
\]
For \(0\le j\le K-2\), define
\[
U_{k,a,j}
:=
\frac{1}{(m)_{j+2}}
\sum_{i_0,\ldots,i_{j+1}\ {\rm distinct}}
V_{i_0}\mathbf 1\{X_{i_0}=k,A_{i_0}=a\}
\mathbf 1\{X_{i_1}=k\}
\prod_{t=2}^{j+1}\mathbf 1\{X_{i_t}=k,A_{i_t}=a\}.
\]
Set
\[
\widehat P_k
:=
\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}
\frac{g_{Kj}}{B^{j+1}}
\{U_{k,1,j}-U_{k,0,j}\}.
\]
Assume that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Degree.)} \(K\ge 2\).
\item \textbf{(Scale.)} \(B>0\).
\item \textbf{(Light cells.)} \(p_k\le B/4\) for every \(k\in S\).
\item \textbf{(Block size.)} \(4(K+2)^2\le m\).
\item \textbf{(Shift calibration.)} \(4(K+2)/m\le 3B/4\).
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_P^{(m)}}\!\left(\sum_{k\in S}\widehat P_k\right)
\le
\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{m}
+
C_{\epsilon}6^{2K}
\left\{
dB^2+\frac{d^2K^2B^2}{m}
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:linear-mark-factorial-covariance} isolates the continuous-outcome step in the polynomial estimator. The conditional second central moment envelope in \cref{obj:def:model-class} is enough to control the one-mark factorial array after normalization by \(M\), and the final display separates the block sampling term from the two approximation-scale terms that depend on \(d\), \(B\), and \(K\).

The fixed-branch risk bound adds the heavy-cell plug-in component to the light-cell polynomial component. The statement is deterministic in the heavy set \(H\); in the estimator of \cref{obj:def:polynomial-handle}, the sample split allows this result to be applied conditionally on the pilot block.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:polynomial-fixed-branch-risk}[Fixed branch risk bound]
For every overlap parameter \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there is a constant \(C_{\epsilon}>0\) such that the following holds.  Let \(d,m,K\in\mathbb N\), let \(M,\sigma,B,\beta\in\mathbb R\), let \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, and let \(H\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\) be deterministic.  For one block of observations \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\), \(i=1,\ldots,m\), with joint law \(Q_P^{(m)}\), define
\[
\widehat R_H
:=
\sum_{k\in H}
\widehat p_k
\left\{
\widehat\mu_{1k}-\widehat\mu_{0k}
\right\},
\qquad
R_H(P)
:=
\sum_{k\in H}p_k\tau_k,
\]
where \(\widehat p_k\) is the empirical mass of cell \(k\) on the block and \(\widehat\mu_{ak}\) is the arm-cell sample mean on positive arm-cell counts, with the corresponding empty-cell contribution set to zero.  Let \(\widehat P_k\) be the light factorial-polynomial contribution at scale \(B\) and degree \(K\) from \cref{obj:def:polynomial-handle}, and define
\[
\Delta_H
:=
\frac{\widehat R_H-R_H(P)}{M}
+
\left\{
\sum_{k\in H^{\mathrm c}}\widehat P_k
-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{k\in H^{\mathrm c}}p_k\tau_k
\right\}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal E_{m,\epsilon,\beta}(H)\) denote the boundary-safe lower-mass missing envelope,
\[
\mathcal E_{m,\epsilon,\beta}(H)
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{|H|}{\{(m-2)\epsilon/2\}^2\beta},
& \{(m-2)\epsilon/2\}^2\beta>0,\\[1.2ex]
\sum_{k\in H}p_k,
& \{(m-2)\epsilon/2\}^2\beta\le 0.
\end{cases}
\]
Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Degree.)} \(2\le K\).
\item \textbf{(Scales.)} \(B>0\) and \(\beta>0\).
\item \textbf{(Heavy band.)} \(\beta\le p_k\) for every \(k\in H\).
\item \textbf{(Light scale.)} \(p_k\le B/4\) for every \(k\in H^{\mathrm c}\).
\item \textbf{(Block size.)} \(4(K+2)^2\le m\).
\item \textbf{(Shift calibration.)} \(4(K+2)/m\le 3B/4\).
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(m)}}\!\left[\Delta_H^2\right]
\le
8\left\{
\frac{8\sum_{k\in H}p_k}{(m\vee 1)\epsilon}
+\frac{6}{m\vee 1}
+4\,\mathcal E_{m,\epsilon,\beta}(H)^2
\right\}
+
2\left\{
\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{m}
+C_{\epsilon}6^{2K}
\left(dB^2+\frac{d^2K^2B^2}{m}\right)
+\left(\frac{|H^{\mathrm c}|B}{\epsilon K^2}\right)^2
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:polynomial-fixed-branch-risk} decomposes the normalized branch error into a heavy-cell plug-in part, a light-cell polynomial variance part, and an approximation remainder. The boundary-safe envelope records the cost of missing an arm within a heavy cell under the lower-mass band, while the final term \((|H^{\mathrm c}|B/(\epsilon K^2))^2\) is the polynomial approximation contribution.

We next record the full-sample occupancy bound for the collision estimator. The event defining crossed cells in \cref{obj:def:collision-handle} ensures that the treated and control sample means entering the statistic are evaluated only when both arm-cell counts are positive.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:continuous-occupancy-collision-upper-all-d}[Continuous occupancy collision bound]
For every overlap parameter \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there is a constant \(C_{\epsilon}>0\) such that the following holds.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size and alphabet.)} The integers \(n\) and \(d\) satisfy \(n\ge 1\) and \(d\ge 1\).
\item \textbf{(Model.)} The distribution \(P\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) from \cref{obj:def:model-class}, with its corresponding bounds on \(M\) and \(\sigma\).
\item \textbf{(Estimator.)} The estimator is the clipped occupancy-weighted treated-control estimator \(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\) defined in \cref{obj:def:collision-handle} with clipping level \(M\).
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
  E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[\left(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
  \le
  C_{\epsilon}M^2
  \left(
    \frac{1}{n}
    +\sigma^2
    +\frac{d}{n^2}
  \right).
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:continuous-occupancy-collision-upper-all-d} supplies the collision branch uniformly over all alphabet sizes. The three terms have the same interpretation as in the main text: ordinary sampling variation, the radius-controlled cost of replacing uncrossed cell effects by crossed-cell information, and the sparse-cell occupancy contribution.

The polynomial branch is calibrated by choosing the degree and the active range so that the fixed-branch inequality can be summed over the pilot-induced heavy-light split. The next result packages that calibration for the total estimator in \cref{obj:def:polynomial-handle}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:continuous-ratio-polynomial-upper-all-d}[All-alphabet polynomial upper bound]
For every overlap parameter \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there exist a constant \(C_{\epsilon}>0\) and a polynomial handle, consisting of a cutoff \(N_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb N\) and an active-range multiplier \(\rho_{\epsilon}>0\) such that
\[
N_{\epsilon}\le n,\qquad d\le \rho_{\epsilon}n\log(en)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
2\le K_n
\]
for all integers \(n,d\ge1\), with the following properties.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Complexity.)} The handle admits a constant \(C_{\mathrm{op}}\in\mathbb N\), independent of \(n,d\), and \(M\), such that for every \(n,d\) and \(M\) the post-aggregation polynomial program for \(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}\) has at most
\[
C_{\mathrm{op}}(d+1)(K_n+1)^2
\]
arithmetic operations.
\item \textbf{(Total bounded estimator.)} For every pair of integers \(n,d\ge1\) and every \(M\ge1\), the estimator \(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}\) determined by this handle is a measurable function of the \(n\)-sample observed data and satisfies
\[
T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}(s)\in[-M,M]
\]
for every sample \(s\).
\item \textbf{(Risk bound.)} For every pair of integers \(n,d\ge1\), every \(M\ge1\), every \(0\le\sigma\le2\), and every \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-class},
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[\{T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}-\tau(P)\}^2\right]
\le
C_{\epsilon}M^2
\left\{
\frac{1}{n}
+
\min\!\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right)
\right\}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:continuous-ratio-polynomial-upper-all-d} turns the light-cell analysis into a usable estimator bound. The zero fallback in the inactive range keeps the estimator total for every \(n,d\), and the displayed risk bound gives the polynomial scale used by the selector in \cref{obj:def:total-estimator}. The operation count records that, after aggregation by cell and order, the implementation grows polynomially in the alphabet size and degree.

The finite-range construction theorem combines the polynomial and collision branches before the all-alphabet packaging in \cref{obj:thm:robust-upper-construction-resolution-all-d}. It keeps the calibrated active range explicit, which is useful for downstream statements that invoke the original range condition.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:robust-upper-construction-resolution}[Robust upper construction]
For every overlap parameter \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there exist constants \(C_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon}>0\) and a calibrated polynomial handle consisting of a cutoff \(N\) and an active-range multiplier \(\rho>0\) such that, for every \(n,d\ge 1\),
\[
N\le n,\qquad d\le \rho n\log(en)
\]
implies that the associated polynomial degree is at least \(2\). For every \(n,d\ge 1\), \(M\ge 1\), and \(0\le \sigma\le 2\), the handle-indexed polynomial estimator \(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}\) and the occupancy-weighted estimator \(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\) of \cref{obj:def:collision-handle} are measurable functions of the sample and take values in \([-M,M]\).

If, in addition,
\[
d\le c_{\epsilon}\frac{n^2}{\log(en)},
\]
then for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) as defined in \cref{obj:def:model-class},
\[
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[\left(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_{\epsilon}M^2
\left\{
\frac1n+\min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right)
\right\},
\]
and
\[
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[\left(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}-\tau(P)\right)^2\right]
\le
C_{\epsilon}M^2
\left(
\frac1n+\sigma^2+\frac{d}{n^2}
\right).
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:robust-upper-construction-resolution} states the two estimators on the same calibrated range. The polynomial branch contributes the large-alphabet approximation rate, and the occupancy branch contributes the radius-sensitive collision rate. Since both estimators are clipped into \([-M,M]\), the selector can compare their deterministic benchmark terms within the admissible class of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.

The final result in this appendix is the restricted selector bound. It is the finite-range version of the selector statement used in the main text and follows by applying the branch with the smaller displayed nonparametric benchmark.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:frontier-upper}[Restricted frontier upper bound]
For every overlap parameter \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there exist constants \(C_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon}>0\) and a polynomial calibration handle \((N,\rho)\) with \(\rho>0\) such that, for all positive integers \(n,d\),
\[
N\le n
\quad\text{and}\quad
d\le \rho n\log(en)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
2\le K_n ,
\]
where \(K_n\) is the active polynomial degree in the polynomial estimator.  For every \(n,d,M,\sigma\), assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample and alphabet.)} \(n\ge1\) and \(d\ge1\).
\item \textbf{(Envelope and radius.)} \(M\ge1\) and \(0\le\sigma\le2\).
\item \textbf{(Restricted dimension.)}
\[
d\le c_{\epsilon}\frac{n^2}{\log(en)} .
\]
\end{itemize}
Let \(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\) be the polynomial estimator determined by this handle, let \(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\) be the collision estimator, and let \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}\) be the known-radius selector of \cref{obj:def:total-estimator} applied to \(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}\), \(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\), and \(\sigma\).  Put
\[
r_{n,d,\sigma}
=
\frac1n+
\min\left\{
1,\,
\min\left\{
\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)},\,
\sigma^2+\frac{d}{n^2}
\right\}
\right\}.
\]
Then \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\), and for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) in \cref{obj:def:model-class},
\[
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}-\tau(P)\right)^2
\right]
\le
C_{\epsilon}M^2 r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:frontier-upper} records the finite-range selector guarantee behind the all-alphabet statement in \cref{obj:thm:frontier-upper-all-d}. The benchmark \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\) combines the parametric term with the smallest of the clipped-zero, polynomial, and collision remainders. This is the constructive upper-bound input used when the main bracket is assembled in \cref{obj:thm:two-sided-minimax-bracket-all-d}.
