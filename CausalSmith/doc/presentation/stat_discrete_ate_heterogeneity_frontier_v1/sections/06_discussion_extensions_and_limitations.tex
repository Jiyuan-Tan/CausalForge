\section{Discussion and limitations}

The bracket in \cref{obj:thm:two-sided-minimax-bracket-all-d} gives a finite-sample interpretation of the known-radius problem on a single real-outcome observational class. The estimator side is entirely constructive through \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}\) in \cref{obj:def:total-estimator}, and the converse side is stated for the same class \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) from \cref{obj:def:model-class}. The radius \(\sigma M\) is an intrinsic coordinate of that class: it is the maximal supported-cell effect deviation inside the same fixed-overlap, arbitrary-cell-mass model used to define the minimax risk in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.

The bracket benchmarks also give a concrete reading of the two estimation mechanisms. When the radius is small enough for crossed cells to be informative, the collision branch uses observed treated-control meetings within cells. When the alphabet is sparse enough for rare-cell approximation to dominate, the polynomial branch uses the heavy-light construction in \cref{obj:def:polynomial-handle}. The selector benchmark in \cref{obj:thm:frontier-upper-all-d} records the better of these two mechanisms, together with the clipped-zero branch, while \cref{obj:thm:fixed-interior-tightness-and-shrinking-radius-gap-all-d} identifies the endpoint, fixed-positive-radius, saturation, and parametric-dominance regimes where the bracket is order matched.

\subsection{Future work}

The matched-regime summary in \cref{obj:thm:fixed-interior-tightness-and-shrinking-radius-gap-all-d} leaves a sharply described residual shrinking-radius wedge for further development. The following remark records the target problem in that region using the same notation as the regime theorem.

\begin{remarkv}{oeq:shrinking-radius-frontier}[Shrinking-radius frontier]
Let
\[
L:=\log(en),\qquad
b:=n^{-1}+\frac{d}{n^2},\qquad
u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}.
\]
In the residual shrinking-radius wedge
\[
b\ll u\ll1,
\qquad
b\ll\sigma^2\ll1,
\]
equivalently up to boundary constants
\[
\sqrt n\,L\ll d\ll nL,
\qquad
\sigma^2\gg b,
\]
a natural next question is whether one can either construct a realization-wise radius-constrained paired-cell moment-matching fuzzy experiment in \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) with ATE separation of order
\[
M\min\left\{\sigma,\frac{d}{nL}\right\}
\]
and sample-mixture total variation bounded away from one, or construct an explicit total estimator whose risk is strictly smaller in order than the selector benchmark
\[
M^2\{b+\min(u,\sigma^2)\},
\]
with the product benchmark
\[
M^2(b+\sigma^2u)
\]
as the target rate. The hypothesis-independent channel yields the product separation
\[
M\sigma\min\left\{1,\frac{d}{nL}\right\}.
\]
\end{remarkv}

\Cref{obj:oeq:shrinking-radius-frontier} records two possible mathematical routes in the residual shrinking-radius wedge. One route asks for a radius-constrained least-favorable construction with ATE separation at the selector scale; the other asks for an estimator whose risk improves on the current selector benchmark toward the product scale suggested by the channel lower bound. The divergence example in \cref{obj:thm:fixed-interior-tightness-and-shrinking-radius-gap-all-d} places \(d_n=n\) and \(\sigma_n=\log(en)^{-1/2}\) inside this region and gives logarithmic separation between the displayed selector and converse benchmarks.

\subsection{Limitations}

The results are scoped to fixed overlap, known outcome scale \(M\), known heterogeneity radius \(\sigma\), and scalar average treatment effect estimation. Within that scope, \cref{obj:thm:two-sided-minimax-bracket-all-d} gives risk bounds for clipped estimators in \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\) from \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}, and \cref{obj:thm:frontier-upper-all-d} constructs a selector that uses the radius as an input. The inference target is mean-squared error for point estimation over \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), so confidence intervals, adaptive radius selection, and data-driven choice of \(M\) are directions beyond the estimation bracket established here.

The moment and overlap assumptions also define the statistical environment in which the bracket should be read. Fixed overlap in \cref{obj:ass:overlap} keeps treatment and control assignment probabilities uniformly away from zero and one in every supported cell. Mean normalization and the second central moment envelope in \cref{obj:ass:mean-normalization,obj:ass:second-central-moment} set the real-outcome scale under weak conditional moment control. Approximate homogeneity in \cref{obj:ass:approximate-homogeneity} supplies the radius that determines which estimator branch is selected. Under exactly these conditions, the paper characterizes the delivered finite-sample minimax bracket and its matched regimes for arbitrary cell masses.
