\section{Appendix A. Binary source lemmas}

The lower-bound transfers in \cref{obj:prop:zeng-class-inclusion-and-lower-transfer,obj:lem:scaled-binary-exact-lower-transfer-all-d,obj:thm:radius-channel-converse-all-d} use binary discrete-adjustment experiments, originating in \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}, as source problems. This appendix records the binary source lemmas in the notation used by the main text, with attribution and statistical context supplied by the citation. The first definition fixes the fixed-sample exactly homogeneous binary experiment and its binary average response contrast.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_3}[Fixed-sample binary homogeneous experiment]
For integers \(n,d\ge1\) and \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), let \(\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\mathrm{bin}}(\epsilon)\) be the fixed-sample binary-outcome exactly homogeneous source experiment consisting of the product laws \(P^{\otimes n}\) generated by laws \(P\in\mathcal H_d^{\mathrm{bin}}(\epsilon)\). Its binary average response contrast is
\[
\psi(P)=\frac1d\sum_{k=1}^d\{\mu_{1k}^{\mathrm{bin}}(P)-\mu_{0k}^{\mathrm{bin}}(P)\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:synth_3} packages the source experiment used by the exact-homogeneity collision transfer. It aligns the fixed-sample product laws with the binary source classes introduced in \cref{obj:synth_2} and gives the target \(\psi(P)\) that is later scaled into the real-outcome average treatment effect through the affine embeddings.

The first finite-sample collision source lemma concerns the exactly homogeneous binary source experiment. It supplies the \(d/n^2\) component used in the exact-radius part of the real-outcome lower benchmark.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:binary-exact-collision-lower}[Binary collision lower bound]
Let \(u_{\epsilon}:=\min\{1/2,\,8(1/2-\epsilon)^2\}\).  Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample and alphabet.)} \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) satisfy \(n\ge 1\) and \(d\ge 1\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap.)} The overlap parameter \(\epsilon\in\mathbb R\) satisfies \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
\item \textbf{(Collision regime.)} The dimensions satisfy \(d\le n^2\).
\end{itemize}
For the fixed-sample binary exactly homogeneous source experiment \(\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\mathrm{bin}}(\epsilon)\), generated by binary laws with uniform cell masses, propensities in \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), and a common cell effect across all cells, the minimax mean-squared risk for estimating the binary average response contrast \(\psi(P)\) satisfies
\[
\frac{u_{\epsilon}^2}{64}\frac{d}{n^2}
\le
\inf_{\widehat\psi}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\mathrm{bin}}(\epsilon)}
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\{\widehat\psi-\psi(P)\}^2\right],
\]
where the infimum ranges over all measurable real-valued estimators \(\widehat\psi\) of the \(n\)-sample.
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:binary-exact-collision-lower} records the collision-scale binary statement associated with \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. The overlap-dependent constant \(u_{\epsilon}\) is fixed once \(\epsilon\) is fixed, and the displayed inequality gives a lower bound for estimating \(\psi(P)\) over uniform-mass exactly homogeneous binary laws when \(d\le n^2\).

A second exact-homogeneity source lemma records the same binary phenomenon with the parametric term included. This statement is used when the transfer argument carries the homogeneous source risk into the real-outcome class.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:zeng-binary-exact-homogeneity-lower}[Exact binary lower bound]
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  For integers \(n,d\) with \(d>0\), let \(\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\mathrm{bin}}(\epsilon)\) be the fixed-sample binary-outcome experiment with cell masses \(p_k=1/d\), overlap \(\epsilon\le \pi_k\le 1-\epsilon\), conditional response means in \([0,1]\), and a common cell treatment effect, and write
\[
\psi(P)=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^d(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}).
\]
There exist constants \(a_\epsilon,b_\epsilon>0\) and an integer \(N_\epsilon\) such that, for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(d>0\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\), and \(d\le b_\epsilon n^2\),
\[
a_\epsilon\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{d}{n^2}\right)
\le
\inf_{\widehat\psi}\sup_{P^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\mathrm{bin}}(\epsilon)}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\psi-\psi(P))^2\right].
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:zeng-binary-exact-homogeneity-lower} records the binary exact-homogeneity lower scale associated with \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. The constants depend only on the fixed overlap level, and the active range \(d\le b_\epsilon n^2\) is the range transported and then saturated in the all-alphabet real-outcome statement.

The radius-channel converse uses a different binary source: a one-arm problem with arbitrary cell masses and zero control-arm response mean. Its minimax lower bound carries the large-alphabet polynomial scale that appears after the channel attenuation.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:zeng-binary-one-arm-lower}[Binary one-arm lower bound]
For every real overlap constant \(\epsilon\), if \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), then there exist constants \(a_{\epsilon},b_{\epsilon}>0\) and an integer \(N_{\epsilon}\) such that the following holds.  For every sample size \(n\) and alphabet size \(d\),
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Positive alphabet.)} \(d\ge 1\).
\item \textbf{(Large sample.)} \(n\ge N_{\epsilon}\).
\item \textbf{(Source range.)} \(d\le b_{\epsilon}n\log n\).
\end{itemize}
In the fixed-sample binary observed-data experiment with observations \((X,A,Y)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), arbitrary cell masses \(p_k=P(X=k)\), product sampling law \(P^{\otimes n}\), overlap
\[
\epsilon\le P(A=1\mid X=k)\le 1-\epsilon
\]
on every positive-mass category, and identically zero control-arm response mean \(E_P[Y\mid A=0,X=k]=0\), the treated-arm minimax risk satisfies
\[
\inf_{\widehat\psi}
\sup_P
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[
\left\{\widehat\psi-\sum_{k=1}^d p_k E_P[Y\mid A=1,X=k]\right\}^2
\right]
\ge
a_{\epsilon}
\left\{
\frac{1}{n}
+
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\right\},
\]
where the infimum is over measurable estimators of the fixed sample.
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:zeng-binary-one-arm-lower} formalizes the arbitrary-mass binary rare-cell lower scale associated with \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. In the main lower-bound construction, the hypothesis-independent channel in \cref{obj:def:least-favorable-handle} attenuates this binary separation by the radius factor before the affine scaling by \(M\).

The final source lemma is an occupancy reciprocal bound. It controls the event that no empirically crossed cell appears and the reciprocal of the crossed-cell count used by the collision estimator.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:zeng-usable-occupancy-reciprocal}[Usable occupancy reciprocal]
For every overlap parameter \(\epsilon\in\mathbb R\), there are constants
\(b_{\epsilon},B_{\epsilon}>0\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that the following holds.
For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\), every \(M,\sigma\in\mathbb R\), and every
\(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), let \(Q_P^{(n)}\) denote the \(n\)-fold observed-data law and define
\[
N_{ak}:=\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\},\qquad
N_k:=N_{0k}+N_{1k},\qquad
I_k:=\mathbf 1\{N_{0k}>0,\ N_{1k}>0\},
\]
with
\[
R_n:=\sum_{k=1}^d N_k I_k .
\]
Then
\[
Q_P^{(n)}(R_n=0)
\le
2\exp\left\{-b_{\epsilon}\frac{n^2}{n\vee d}\right\},
\]
and
\[
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\left[
\mathbf 1\{R_n>0\}\frac{1}{R_n}
\right]
\le
B_{\epsilon}\left\{
\frac{n\vee d}{n^2}
+
\exp\left(-b_{\epsilon}\frac{n^2}{n\vee d}\right)
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:zeng-usable-occupancy-reciprocal} records the occupancy statement associated with the collision calculations of \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. The two displayed inequalities bound, respectively, the zero-crossing probability and the expected reciprocal occupancy on the crossed-cell event. These controls are the probabilistic ingredients behind the collision estimator's dependence on \(d/n^2\) and fixed overlap.
