\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:zeng-usable-occupancy-reciprocal}]
On the active range \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), let \(c_\epsilon>0\) be the constant in the independent-Poisson usable-occupancy Laplace bound, and set
\[
b_\epsilon:=c_\epsilon/8,\qquad B_\epsilon:=\max\{64/c_\epsilon,2\}.
\]
Set \(b_\epsilon=B_\epsilon=1\) on the complementary range.  For any \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), \cref{obj:def:model-class} gives \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), so the active values of the constants apply. % lean: zengUsableOccupancyReciprocal

If \(d=0\), the cell-mass identity gives \(0=1\), so the assertion is immediate.  Suppose \(n=0\) and hence, after the preceding case, \(d>0\).  The zero-event mass is at most one and therefore at most \(2\exp\{0\}\).  The displayed rate is interpreted at this endpoint with the real-division convention \(x/0=0\), so \((n\vee d)/n^2=0\) and \(\exp\{-b_\epsilon n^2/(n\vee d)\}=1\).  The reciprocal integrand \(\mathbf 1\{R_0>0\}/R_0\) is identically zero, while the right-hand side is \(B_\epsilon>0\). % lean: zengUsableOccupancyReciprocal

Assume now that \(n,d>0\).  Put
\[
\kappa_{n,d}:=c_\epsilon\frac{(n/2)^2}{\max\{n/2,d\}},\qquad
s_{n,d}:=\frac{n^2}{n\vee d}.
\]
Since \(\max\{n/2,d\}\le \max\{n,d\}\),
\[
\frac{c_\epsilon}{4}s_{n,d}\le \kappa_{n,d}.
\]
% lean: zengUsableOccupancyReciprocal

Let \(Q\) be the law of the observed cell-arm mark \((X,A)\).  In the half-intensity marked-Poisson experiment with mark law \(Q\) and total intensity \(n/2\), let \(\widetilde R\) be the usable total obtained after regrouping the independent arm-cell counts.  If \(p_k=P(X=k)\) and \(x_k=(n/2)p_k\), then the two arm-cell intensities in cell \(k\) are \((n/2)Q\{(k,0)\}\) and \((n/2)Q\{(k,1)\}\).  They sum to \(x_k\), the total cell intensities satisfy \(\sum_k x_k=n/2\), and fixed overlap gives
\[
\epsilon x_k\le (n/2)Q\{(k,0)\},\qquad
\epsilon x_k\le (n/2)Q\{(k,1)\}
\]
for every positive-mass cell, with zero-mass cells contributing trivially.  The independent-Poisson Laplace bound for these intensities gives
\[
E\exp\{-\widetilde R\}
\le
\exp\left\{-c_\epsilon\frac{(n/2)^2}{\max\{n/2,d\}}\right\}
=\exp\{-\kappa_{n,d}\}.
\]
For each integer \(r\ge0\),
\[
\mathbf 1\{r=0\}\le e^{-r},\qquad
\begin{cases}
1,& r=0,\\
1/r,& r>0
\end{cases}
\le \frac{2}{\kappa_{n,d}}+e^{\kappa_{n,d}/2}e^{-r},
\]
where the second inequality splits the positive case according to whether \(r\ge \kappa_{n,d}/2\).  Integrating these deterministic bounds and using the Laplace display yields
\[
\Pr(\widetilde R=0)\le e^{-\kappa_{n,d}},\qquad
E\left[\begin{cases}
1,& \widetilde R=0,\\
1/\widetilde R,& \widetilde R>0
\end{cases}\right]
\le \frac{2}{\kappa_{n,d}}+e^{-\kappa_{n,d}/2}.
\]
% lean: integral_exp_neg_markedUsable_le, zeroIndicator_le_exp_neg, reciprocalIndicator_le_cutoff, markedPoisson_zero_penalty_bounds

Let \(R_m^{\mathrm{str}}\) denote the usable total in the first \(m\) marks of an i.i.d. stream with one-step law \(Q\).  Adding observations can only increase the usable total, so both \(\mathbf 1\{R_m^{\mathrm{str}}=0\}\) and the zero-plus-reciprocal penalty
\[
\begin{cases}
1,& R_m^{\mathrm{str}}=0,\\
1/R_m^{\mathrm{str}},& R_m^{\mathrm{str}}>0
\end{cases}
\]
are nonincreasing in \(m\).  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(n/2)\), then Markov's inequality gives
\[
\Pr(N\ge n+1)\le \frac{n/2}{n+1}\le \frac12,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\Pr(N\le n)\ge \frac12.
\]
For either of the two nonnegative monotone prefix functionals \(g_m\) above,
\[
\Pr(N\le n)E g_n\le E g_N,
\]
because \(g_n\le g_m\) on the event \(m\le n\).  Therefore \(E g_n\le 2E g_N\).  The random-prefix expectation \(E g_N\) is exactly the corresponding marked-Poisson expectation with total intensity \(n/2\), and the deterministic auxiliary outcome mark does not change the prefix statistic.  The fixed-prefix bounds are thus
\[
E\mathbf 1\{R_n^{\mathrm{str}}=0\}
\le 2e^{-\kappa_{n,d}},\qquad
E\left[\begin{cases}
1,& R_n^{\mathrm{str}}=0,\\
1/R_n^{\mathrm{str}},& R_n^{\mathrm{str}}>0
\end{cases}\right]
\le 2\left\{\frac{2}{\kappa_{n,d}}+e^{-\kappa_{n,d}/2}\right\}.
\]
% lean: augmented_half_depoissonization

The \(n\)-prefix law of the cell-arm stream is the observed mark marginal of \(Q_P^{(n)}\), and forgetting outcomes identifies the fixed-sample usable total with the corresponding mark-prefix usable total.  Hence
\[
Q_P^{(n)}(R_n=0)\le 2e^{-\kappa_{n,d}}
\]
and, because \(\mathbf 1\{R_n>0\}/R_n\) is bounded by the zero-plus-reciprocal penalty,
\[
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\left[\mathbf 1\{R_n>0\}\frac1{R_n}\right]
\le
2\left\{\frac{2}{\kappa_{n,d}}+e^{-\kappa_{n,d}/2}\right\}.
\]
% lean: zengUsableOccupancyReciprocal

Using \(\kappa_{n,d}\ge(c_\epsilon/4)s_{n,d}\) and \(b_\epsilon=c_\epsilon/8\),
\[
Q_P^{(n)}(R_n=0)\le 2\exp\{-b_\epsilon s_{n,d}\}.
\]
For the reciprocal term,
\[
2\left\{\frac{2}{\kappa_{n,d}}+e^{-\kappa_{n,d}/2}\right\}
\le
\frac{64}{c_\epsilon}\frac{n\vee d}{n^2}
+2\exp\{-b_\epsilon s_{n,d}\}
\le
B_\epsilon\left\{\frac{n\vee d}{n^2}+\exp(-b_\epsilon s_{n,d})\right\},
\]
by the choice \(B_\epsilon\ge\max\{64/c_\epsilon,2\}\).  This is the asserted reciprocal inequality. % lean: zengUsableOccupancyReciprocal
\end{proof}
