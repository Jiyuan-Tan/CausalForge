\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:parametric-lower}]
Take
\[
c_\epsilon:=\frac1{100}.
\]
% lean: parametric_lower_core
Fix \(n,d,M,\sigma\) satisfying the displayed hypotheses, and choose a cell \(k\in[d]\).  Put
\[
\delta:=\frac{2/5}{\sqrt n},
\qquad
u_v:=M(1/2+v\delta-1/2)\quad (v\in\{0,1\}).
\]
Thus \(u_0=0\) and \(u_1=M\delta\).  Moreover,
\[
\delta^2=\frac{4}{25n},
\qquad
0\le\delta\le\frac25,
\qquad
|u_0|\le M/2,
\qquad
|u_1|\le M/2 .
\]
% lean: parametric_lower_core
For \(v\in\{0,1\}\), let \(P_v\) be the one-cell test law with \(X=k\), \(P(A=1\mid X=k)=1/2\), control potential outcome \(Y(0)=0\), and treated potential outcome \(Y(1)\) distributed on \(\{-M/2,M/2\}\) with mean \(u_v\).  Equivalently, draw \(A\) with probability \(1/2\) independently of this treated endpoint and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  Thus, for every cell \(\ell\), arm \(a\), and measurable \(S_0,S_1\subseteq\mathbb R\),
\[
P_v\{X=\ell,A=a,Y(0)\in S_0,Y(1)\in S_1\}\,P_v\{X=\ell\}
=
P_v\{X=\ell,A=a\}\,P_v\{X=\ell,Y(0)\in S_0,Y(1)\in S_1\},
\]
so the conditional exchangeability requirement in \(\cref{obj:def:model-class}\) holds, and consistency holds from \(Y=Y(A)\).  The same construction has cell mass one at \(k\), overlap \(1/2\), conditional means \(0\) and \(u_v\), second central moments bounded by \((M/2)^2\le M^2\), and cell deviation \(0\).  Together with \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), \(M\ge1\), and \(0\le\sigma\le2\), this gives
\[
P_v\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
\qquad (v=0,1).
\]
% lean: testModelClass
The targets are
\[
\tau(P_0)=0,
\qquad
\tau(P_1)=M\delta .
\]
% lean: testRealLaw_rawAte

Introduce the binary one-cell endpoint pair used for the comparison, and write \(\widetilde Q_v^{(m)}\) for its \(m\)-fold binary observed law under \(v\in\{0,1\}\).  Both source laws have the unique cell, propensity \(m_0=1/2\), and control endpoint probability \(g_0=1/2\).  The null source law has treated endpoint probability \(g_1=1/2\), while the perturbed source law has treated endpoint probability \(g_1+\delta=1/2+\delta\); this is admissible because \(0\le\delta\) and \(1/2+\delta\le1\).  The one-observation chi-square calculation gives
\[
\chi^2\!\left(\widetilde Q_1^{(1)},\widetilde Q_0^{(1)}\right)
=
\frac{(1/2)\delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}.
\]
The null one-observation atoms are positive, and tensorization gives
\[
1+
\chi^2\!\left(\widetilde Q_1^{(n)},\widetilde Q_0^{(n)}\right)
=
\left(1+\frac{(1/2)\delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}\right)^n
\le
\exp\!\left(
 n\frac{(1/2)\delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}
\right).
\]
Since
\[
n\,
\frac{(1/2)\delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}
=
\frac{8}{25}
\le \log 2,
\]
we have \(\chi^2(\widetilde Q_1^{(n)},\widetilde Q_0^{(n)})\le1\).  The chi-square-to-total-variation inequality and symmetry of total variation therefore yield
\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(\widetilde Q_0^{(n)},\widetilde Q_1^{(n)}\right)
\le
\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2\!\left(\widetilde Q_1^{(n)},\widetilde Q_0^{(n)}\right)}
\le\frac12 .
\]
The two real observed \(n\)-sample laws are the corresponding images under the coordinatewise affine map
\[
(x,a,b)\longmapsto (k,a,\;{\bf 1}\{a=1\}M(b-1/2)).
\]
% lean: test_product_map_null
% lean: test_product_map_pert
Therefore, by data processing for total variation,
\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(Q_{P_0}^{(n)},Q_{P_1}^{(n)}\right)\le\frac12 .
\]
% lean: parametric_lower_core

For any measurable estimator \(T\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\), the two-point testing inequality gives
\[
\frac{(M\delta)^2}{16}
\le
\max_{v\in\{0,1\}}
E_{Q_{P_v}^{(n)}}\!\left[(T-\tau(P_v))^2\right].
\]
% lean: test_minimax_two_point
The supremum over \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) is finite because \(T\in[-M,M]\) and \(|\tau(P)|\le M\) by \(\cref{obj:lem:scale-sanity}\), so the preceding bound passes through the estimator infimum in \(\cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}\). % lean: test_model_mse_le
Using \(\delta^2=4/(25n)\),
\[
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
\ge
\frac{M^2\delta^2}{16}
=
\frac{M^2}{100n}
=
\frac{c_\epsilon M^2}{n}.
\]
% lean: parametric_lower_core
\end{proof}
