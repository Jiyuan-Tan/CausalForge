\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:zeng-binary-one-arm-lower}]
Fix \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\).  The proof uses the fixed-sample binary one-arm interface in which \(X\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), \(A,Y\in\{0,1\}\), the control-arm response mean is zero, supported propensities lie in \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), and the target is
\[
\psi(P)=\sum_{k=1}^d p_k E_P[Y\mid A=1,X=k].
\]
The corresponding minimax risk is the one-arm risk used in the verified source layer. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmMinimaxRisk

Let
\[
\kappa_\epsilon:=\min\left\{1,\frac{1-2\epsilon}{2\epsilon}\right\}.
\]
The checked overlap algebra gives \(0<\kappa_\epsilon\le1\) and keeps the shifted propensities inside the upper overlap endpoint:
\[
\epsilon(1+\kappa_\epsilon)\le1-\epsilon .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmOverlapShiftRatio_pos
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmOverlapShiftRatio_le_one
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.epsilon_mul_one_add_overlapShiftRatio_le

The published large-alphabet construction, represented in the checked source layer, supplies constants and a sample cutoff depending only on \(\epsilon\).  On its high-dimensional range it yields the one-arm lower benchmark \(n^{-1}+d^2/\{n^2(\log n)^2\}\) up to an overlap-dependent constant. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.zengOneArmMinimaxLower

The verification artifact realizes this input through the shifted finite-grid prior and its calibrated signal calculation.  The proof-audit details of the grid, inverse-size tilt, good-event conditioning, product mixture comparison, and total-variation bound are kept in the Lean declarations named below; the paper uses their consequence as the binary source lower bound. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmHighDimensional_shiftedPrior
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmCalibratedSignal_lower
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmHighDimensional_rate_of_calibrated_signal

For remaining finite-sample or small-alphabet cases, the checked one-arm parametric lower bound supplies the \(1/n\) contribution.  Combining this fallback with the high-dimensional range and reducing constants gives constants \(a_\epsilon,b_\epsilon>0\) and \(N_\epsilon\) such that, whenever \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), \(d\ge1\), and \(d\le b_\epsilon n\log n\), the displayed one-arm minimax lower bound holds. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArm_parametric_lower

% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteHeterogeneityFrontier.zengOneArmMinimaxLower
\end{proof}
