\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:zeng-binary-one-arm-lower}]
Fix an overlap constant \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\).  For sample size \(n\) and alphabet size \(d\), write \(\mathcal R_{n,d}(\epsilon)\) for the one-arm minimax risk above, namely
\[
\mathcal R_{n,d}(\epsilon)
=
\inf_{\widehat\psi}
\sup_P
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[
\left\{\widehat\psi-
\sum_{k=1}^d p_k E_P[Y\mid A=1,X=k]\right\}^2
\right],
\]
where the infimum is over measurable fixed-sample estimators and the supremum ranges over the binary control-zero laws described above, with propensities in \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\).  Also write
\[
r_{n,d}:=\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmMinimaxRisk
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.minimaxRate

Set
\[
\kappa:=\min\left\{1,\frac{1-2\epsilon}{2\epsilon}\right\}.
\]
The overlap algebra gives
\[
0<\kappa\le1,
\qquad
\epsilon(1+\kappa)\le 1-\epsilon .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmOverlapShiftRatio_pos
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmOverlapShiftRatio_le_one
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.epsilon_mul_one_add_overlapShiftRatio_le

Let
\[
A_\epsilon:=\frac{\kappa^{14}}{4\cdot25600\,(153600000000000)^2},
\qquad
K_\epsilon:=\frac{10^{50}}{\kappa^{14}}.
\]
Both constants are positive.  Choose \(N_{0,\epsilon}\) so large that, for every \(n\ge N_{0,\epsilon}\),
\[
n\ge 400,
\qquad
(\log n)^4\le \frac{\kappa^{14}}{10^{50}}\,n .
\]
This is the elementary eventual domination of \((\log n)^4\) by \(n\).

We first prove the high-dimensional branch.  Suppose that \(q\ge1\), \(n\ge N_{0,\epsilon}\), and
\[
K_\epsilon(\log n)^4\le q\le n\log n .
\]
Because \(K_\epsilon\ge1\) and \(n\ge400\), this implies \(q\ge2\).  Put \(m=q-1\), and define
\[
D_n:=\left\lceil \frac{8\log n}{\log 2}\right\rceil,
\qquad
s_n:=\frac{D_n}{128n},
\qquad
\rho_{\kappa,n}:=\frac{\kappa^5}{10^{10}D_n^2},
\qquad
\gamma_\kappa:=\frac{1}{10}\frac{2\kappa^2}{(1+\kappa)(2+\kappa)(3+\kappa)}.
\]
Thus \(D_n\ge1\), \(D_n\le20\log n\), and
\[
D_n\ge \frac{8\log n}{\log 2},
\qquad
\rho_{\kappa,n}>0,
\qquad
\frac{\kappa^2}{120}\le\gamma_\kappa\le1.
\]
Set
\[
\Delta:=m\,s_n\rho_{\kappa,n}\gamma_\kappa .
\]
The scalar hypotheses needed for the shifted construction are as follows:
\[
m s_n\rho_{\kappa,n}\le 1,
\qquad
2m/n^4\le 1/8,
\qquad
2\frac{m s_n^2}{(\Delta/16)^2}\le 1/32,
\qquad
(m s_n)^2 n^{-2}\le \Delta^2/32 .
\]
The first bound follows from \(m\le q\le n\log n\), \(\kappa^5\le1\), and \(D_n\ge8\log n/\log2\).  The second follows from \(m\le n\log n\le n^2\) and \(n>0\), with \(n\ge400\) supplying the remaining numerical slack.  For the third, \(m\ge q/2\ge K_\epsilon(\log n)^4/2\),
\[
\rho_{\kappa,n}\gamma_\kappa
\ge
\frac{\kappa^7}{1200000000000\,D_n^2},
\qquad
D_n^4\le 20^4(\log n)^4,
\]
give
\[
m\rho_{\kappa,n}^2\gamma_\kappa^2
\ge 2\cdot 16^2\cdot 32,
\]
and therefore, using \(\Delta=m s_n\rho_{\kappa,n}\gamma_\kappa\),
\[
2\frac{m s_n^2}{(\Delta/16)^2}
=
\frac{2\cdot16^2}{m\rho_{\kappa,n}^2\gamma_\kappa^2}
\le \frac1{32}.
\]
For the fourth, the same lower bound on \(\rho_{\kappa,n}\gamma_\kappa\), the upper bound on \(D_n\), and \((\log n)^4\le \kappa^{14}n/10^{50}\) give
\[
32\le n^2\rho_{\kappa,n}^2\gamma_\kappa^2,
\]
which is equivalent to \((m s_n)^2 n^{-2}\le\Delta^2/32\).  These real inequalities are exactly the nonnegative extended-real budget used for the two discarded good-event complements.

We now construct the finite mixtures used in this branch.  Let \(a_{\kappa,n}:=\kappa^4/(10^{10}D_n^2)\), so \(\rho_{\kappa,n}=\kappa a_{\kappa,n}\).  The shifted grid consists of the three points \(a_{\kappa,n},2a_{\kappa,n},3a_{\kappa,n}\) and the \(2D_n+1\) affine Chebyshev mesh points on \([a_{\kappa,n},1]\).  Write these positive grid nodes as \(x_u\).  For a grid symbol \(u\), augmented by a null symbol \(\varnothing\), define
\[
p(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u=\varnothing,\\
s_n x_u,&u\ne\varnothing,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\pi(u)=
\begin{cases}
\epsilon,&u=\varnothing,\\
\epsilon\left(1+\rho_{\kappa,n}/x_u\right),&u\ne\varnothing,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
\mu(u)=
\begin{cases}
1,&u=\varnothing,\\
x_u/(x_u+\rho_{\kappa,n}),&u\ne\varnothing,
\end{cases}
\qquad
f(u):=p(u)\mu(u).
\]
The preceding overlap choice gives \(\pi(u)\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), and the grid construction gives \(0\le\mu(u)\le1\).

Choose signed weights \(w\) on the positive grid with total mass zero and positive Jordan mass.  Let \(\omega_0\) and \(\omega_1\) be the normalized positive and negative Jordan parts of \(w\).  They have the same moments against the shifted reciprocal-monomial basis, and their shifted target expectations satisfy
\[
\gamma_\kappa
\le
\sum_u \omega_0(u)\frac{x_u}{x_u+\rho_{\kappa,n}}
-
\sum_u \omega_1(u)\frac{x_u}{x_u+\rho_{\kappa,n}} .
\]
Now inverse-tilt these two laws by the factor \(\rho_{\kappa,n}/x_u\) and put the remaining mass at \(\varnothing\):
\[
\nu_t(\{u\})=\omega_t(u)\frac{\rho_{\kappa,n}}{x_u},
\qquad
\nu_t(\{\varnothing\})=1-\sum_u\omega_t(u)\frac{\rho_{\kappa,n}}{x_u},
\qquad  t=0,1 .
\]
Because \(\rho_{\kappa,n}\le x_u\) on the shifted grid, these are probability laws.  For every \(i+j+k\le D_n\), the test
\[
x\longmapsto x^{i+k}(x+\rho_{\kappa,n})^j/x
\]
is represented by that basis with degree at most \(D_n\).  The Jordan moment identities therefore transport through the inverse tilt to
\[
\int p(u)^i\{p(u)\pi(u)\}^j\{p(u)\pi(u)\mu(u)\}^k\,d\nu_0(u)
=
\int p(u)^i\{p(u)\pi(u)\}^j\{p(u)\pi(u)\mu(u)\}^k\,d\nu_1(u).
\]
The same inverse-tilt identities give common active mass and separated treated-functional atoms,
\[
\int p\,d\nu_0=\int p\,d\nu_1=s_n\rho_{\kappa,n},
\qquad
s_n\rho_{\kappa,n}\gamma_\kappa
\le
\int f\,d\nu_0-
\int f\,d\nu_1 .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.exists_oneArmShiftedInverseTiltPriors

Let \(z=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\) have product law \(\nu_t^{\otimes m}\) under side \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and set
\[
\theta_t:=m\int f\,d\nu_t,
\qquad
\alpha:=1-m s_n\rho_{\kappa,n},
\qquad
\delta:=\Delta/16 .
\]
By the first scalar budget, \(\alpha\ge0\).  Also \(\Delta>0\) by positivity of its factors, and the active-mass cap \(m s_n\rho_{\kappa,n}\le1\), together with \(\gamma_\kappa\le1\), gives \(\Delta\le1\).  Hence
\[
0<\delta<1,
\qquad
\delta\le \frac12 .
\]
Define the relaxed good event
\[
G_t:=\left\{z:
0<\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^m p(z_i),\quad
\left|\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^m p(z_i)-1\right|\le\delta,
\quad
\left|\sum_{i=1}^m f(z_i)-\theta_t\right|\le\delta
\right\}.
\]
The two-statistic Chebyshev envelope, applied with the displayed \(0<\delta<1\), and the displayed budget give
\[
\nu_t^{\otimes m}(G_t^c)
\le
2\frac{m s_n^2}{\delta^2}
\le \frac1{32},
\qquad t=0,1,
\]
so each \(G_t\) has positive probability.

For \(z\in G_t\), form a binary control-zero law \(P_{t,z}\) on \(q=m+1\) cells by assigning unnormalized cell masses \(\alpha,p(z_1),\ldots,p(z_m)\), anchor propensity \(\epsilon\), active propensities \(\pi(z_i)\), anchor treated mean zero, active treated means \(\mu(z_i)\), and then normalizing by \(S_z:=\alpha+\sum_i p(z_i)\).  The event \(G_t\) gives \(S_z>0\), the preceding bounds give the required overlap and binary means, and all control-arm means are zero.  Moreover
\[
\left|\psi(P_{t,z})-\theta_t\right|
\le
\left|S_z-1\right|+
\left|\sum_{i=1}^m f(z_i)-\theta_t\right|
\le 2\delta
=\Delta/8 .
\]
The separation display for \(\nu_0,\nu_1\) gives
\[
\Delta\le |\theta_0-\theta_1|.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmRelaxedLawOfGoodAtoms_target

For each realization \(z\), let \(K_z\) be the product law of independent triple-counts over the anchor and active cells with Poisson rates
\[
(0,4n\alpha\epsilon,4n\alpha(1-\epsilon))
\]
on the anchor cell and
\[
(4n p(z_i)\pi(z_i)\mu(z_i),\ 4n p(z_i)\pi(z_i)(1-\mu(z_i)),\ 4n p(z_i)(1-\pi(z_i)))
\]
on active cell \(i\).  The logarithmic degree supplies, for every grid atom,
\[
4\{4n p(u)\}+2\log(n^2)
\le (D_n+1)\log 2 .
\]
Together with the raw moment identities through degree \(D_n\), this Taylor budget bounds the one-coordinate mixed triple-Poisson total variation by \(2/n^4\).  Tensorization over the \(m\) active coordinates, with the common anchor law factored out, gives the unconditioned predictive bound
\[
\operatorname{TV}\left(
\int K_z\,d\nu_0^{\otimes m}(z),
\int K_z\,d\nu_1^{\otimes m}(z)
\right)
\le 2m/n^4\le 1/8 .
\]
After restricting \(\nu_t^{\otimes m}\) to \(G_t\) and renormalizing, the loss in total variation is bounded by the two discarded probabilities.  Hence the conditioned predictive mixtures satisfy
\[
\operatorname{TV}\left(
\int_{G_0} K_z\,d\nu_0^{\otimes m}(z\mid G_0),
\int_{G_1} K_z\,d\nu_1^{\otimes m}(z\mid G_1)
\right)
\le \frac14 .
\]
For every \(z\in G_t\), the Poissonized histogram generated from \(P_{t,z}\) at total intensity \(4nS_z\), after recording the three arm-outcome labels in each cell, maps to exactly the kernel \(K_z\).  Also \(S_z\ge1/2\), because \(\delta\le1/2\).  Therefore the total Poisson count \(N_z\sim\operatorname{Pois}(4nS_z)\) satisfies \(4nS_z\ge2n\), and the Poisson lower-tail bound gives \(\Pr\{N_z<n\}\le n^{-2}\).  Since \(|\theta_t|\le m s_n\), the last scalar budget yields
\[
\theta_t^2\Pr\{N_z<n\}
\le
(m s_n)^2 n^{-2}
\le
\Delta^2/32,
\qquad (z\in G_t,
\ t\in\{0,1\}).
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmHighDimensional_shiftedPrior

We use the following finite-mixture testing reduction.  If two finite mixtures have side centers \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), all component targets lie within radius \(r\) of their side center, \(2s\le |\theta_0-\theta_1|\), their predictive total variation is at most \(c\), and the Poisson truncation penalty on each side is bounded by \(\eta\), then
\[
\frac{s^2\{(1-c)/2\}-2r^2-\eta}{2}
\le
\mathcal R_{n,q}(\epsilon).
\]
Apply this with
\[
s=\Delta/2,
\qquad
r=\Delta/8,
\qquad
c=1/4,
\qquad
\eta=\Delta^2/32 .
\]
The numerical identity
\[
\frac{(\Delta/2)^2\{(1-1/4)/2\}-2(\Delta/8)^2-\Delta^2/32}{2}
=\frac{\Delta^2}{64}
\]
gives
\[
\frac{\Delta^2}{64}
\le
\mathcal R_{n,q}(\epsilon).
\]
The calibrated signal inequality
\[
\frac{\kappa^7}{153600000000000\, nD_n}
\le s_n\rho_{\kappa,n}\gamma_\kappa
\]
and \(D_n\le20\log n\) imply
\[
\frac{\kappa^{14}}{25600(153600000000000)^2}
\frac{m^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\le \mathcal R_{n,q}(\epsilon).
\]
Since \(m\ge q/2\), this proves
\[
A_\epsilon\frac{q^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\le \mathcal R_{n,q}(\epsilon)
\]
throughout the range \(K_\epsilon(\log n)^4\le q\le n\log n\).
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmCalibratedSignal_lower
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArmHighDimensional_rate_of_calibrated_signal

The parametric component supplies the fixed-sample term.  Fix \(n\ge1\) and \(d\ge1\), and put
\[
\eta_n:=\frac{2/5}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Since \(\eta_n^2=4/(25n)\) and \(\eta_n\le2/5\), the two endpoint binary laws with propensity \(1/2\), treated means \(1/2\) and \(1/2+\eta_n\), and baseline control mean \(1/2\) are valid.  Deterministically replacing every control-arm outcome by zero preserves the treated functional and contracts total variation; it yields two control-zero laws \(Q_0,Q_1\) with overlap in \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), treated functionals
\[
\psi(Q_0)=\frac12,
\qquad
\psi(Q_1)=\frac12+\eta_n,
\]
and product total variation at most \(1/2\).  The total-variation bound is calibrated by
\[
n\frac{(1/2)\eta_n^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}
=\frac{8}{25}
\le \log 2 .
\]
Applying the two-point testing inequality with half-separation \(\eta_n/2\) and total variation \(1/2\) gives
\[
\frac{1}{100n}
=
\left(\frac{\eta_n}{2}\right)^2\frac{1-1/2}{2}
\le \mathcal R_{n,d}(\epsilon).
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.oneArm_parametric_lower

Now choose \(N_\epsilon\) so large that \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) implies \(n\ge N_{0,\epsilon}\), \(n\ge3\), and
\[
(\log n)^6\le \frac{n}{K_\epsilon^2+1}.
\]
Set
\[
a_\epsilon:=\min\{1/200,A_\epsilon/2\},
\qquad
b_\epsilon:=1 .
\]
Then \(a_\epsilon,b_\epsilon>0\).  Fix \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), \(d\ge1\), and \(d\le b_\epsilon n\log n=n\log n\).  If
\[
K_\epsilon(\log n)^4\le d,
\]
the high-dimensional bound and the parametric bound give
\[
a_\epsilon\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\le \frac12\mathcal R_{n,d}(\epsilon),
\qquad
\frac{a_\epsilon}{n}
\le \frac12\mathcal R_{n,d}(\epsilon),
\]
and hence \(a_\epsilon r_{n,d}\le\mathcal R_{n,d}(\epsilon)\).  In the complementary branch, \(d<K_\epsilon(\log n)^4\).  The choice of \(N_\epsilon\) gives
\[
K_\epsilon^2(\log n)^6\le n,
\]
so
\[
\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}
\le \frac1n,
\qquad
r_{n,d}\le \frac2n .
\]
Since \(a_\epsilon\le1/200\), the parametric lower bound yields
\[
a_\epsilon r_{n,d}
\le \frac{1}{100n}
\le \mathcal R_{n,d}(\epsilon).
\]
Thus for all \(n\ge N_\epsilon\), \(d\ge1\), and \(d\le b_\epsilon n\log n\),
\[
a_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2(\log n)^2}\right\}
\le \mathcal R_{n,d}(\epsilon),
\]
which is the asserted one-arm lower bound.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.zengOneArmMinimaxLower_of_highDimensional
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.DiscreteAteMinimaxLoggap.zengOneArmMinimaxLower
\end{proof}
