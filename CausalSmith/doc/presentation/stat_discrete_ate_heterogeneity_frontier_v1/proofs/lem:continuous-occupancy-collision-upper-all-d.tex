\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:continuous-occupancy-collision-upper-all-d}]
Fix \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\).  Let \(b_\epsilon,B_\epsilon>0\) be the constants in \cref{obj:lem:zeng-usable-occupancy-reciprocal}, and set
\[
K_\epsilon:=\frac{16}{\epsilon^2(1-\epsilon)},\qquad
C_\epsilon:=2K_\epsilon B_\epsilon+
\frac{2K_\epsilon B_\epsilon+4}{b_\epsilon}+2 .
\]
These constants are positive.  Now fix \(n,d\ge1\), real parameters \(M,\sigma\), and \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-class}; let \(Q_P^{(n)}\) be the \(n\)-fold observed-data law. % lean: continuous_occupancy_collision_upper_all_d

Write the unclipped statistic in \cref{obj:def:collision-handle} with its zero fallback as
\[
\widetilde T_n
:=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac1{R_n}
\sum_{k:I_k=1}N_k(\overline Y_{1k}-\overline Y_{0k}),& R_n>0,\\[1ex]
0,& R_n=0,
\end{cases}
\]
and write its design-centered version, totalized in the same way, as
\[
C_n
:=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac1{R_n}
\sum_{k:I_k=1}N_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}),& R_n>0,\\[1ex]
0,& R_n=0.
\end{cases}
\]
On \(\{R_n>0\}\), the difference is the finite-design weighted sum of supported arm-cell residuals
\[
\widetilde T_n-C_n
=
\sum_{k:I_k=1}\sum_{i=1}^n
\frac{N_k}{R_n}
\left\{
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=1\}}{N_{1k}}(Y_i-\mu_{1k})
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=0\}}{N_{0k}}(Y_i-\mu_{0k})
\right\},
\]
with value \(0\) on \(\{R_n=0\}\).  The coefficients in this display depend only on the finite design \(((X_i,A_i))_{i=1}^n\).  Expanding the square therefore gives products of two supported residuals multiplied by a product of finite-design coefficients.  When the two sample coordinates are distinct, the product-law integral is zero after conditioning on the finite design of the other coordinates and integrating one coordinate over its arm-cell fibers; the centering used is exactly
\[
\int_{\{X=k,A=a\}}(Y-\mu_{ak})\,dP=0.
\]
When the two factors use the same coordinate but different arm-cell labels, their supports are disjoint, so the product is pointwise zero.  The remaining same-coordinate, same-label terms are bounded by the arm-cell second central moments from \cref{obj:ass:second-central-moment}, and summing the coordinate indicators gives the arm-cell counts.  Hence
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[(\widetilde T_n-C_n)^2\right]
\le
M^2\,
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\mathbf 1\{R_n>0\}\frac1{R_n^2}
\sum_{k:I_k=1}N_k^2
\left(\frac1{N_{1k}}+\frac1{N_{0k}}\right)
\right].
\]

It remains to compare the design factor in the last display with reciprocal usable occupancy.  Push the sample to the finite design \(((X_i,A_i))_{i=1}^n\).  In each positive-mass cell, the overlap condition in \cref{obj:ass:overlap} gives both arm probabilities at least \(\epsilon p_k\), equivalently \(\pi_k\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\).  A cell-label configuration that places an observation in a zero-mass cell has zero joint design weight, so it contributes zero before any conditional arm probability is formed.

Fix a positive-mass cell \(k\), a cell-label configuration, and the arm assignments outside \(k\).  Let \(m=N_k\) for this fixed cell-label configuration, and let \(r\) be the usable count contributed by all other cells under the fixed outside arm assignments.  For \(m\le1\), the cell cannot be crossed and its local variance and local reciprocal-occupancy share are both zero.  For \(m\ge2\), summing only over the \(m\) arm assignments inside cell \(k\) gives the local finite-binomial comparison
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\pi_k^j(1-\pi_k)^{m-j}
\frac{m^2}{(r+m)^2}
\mathbf 1\{0<j<m\}
\left(\frac1j+\frac1{m-j}\right) \\
&\qquad\le
\frac{4}{\epsilon^2(1-\epsilon)}
\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\pi_k^j(1-\pi_k)^{m-j}
\frac{m}{(r+m)^2}
\mathbf 1\{0<j<m\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The denominator \((r+m)^2\) is the squared global usable count that applies when cell \(k\) is crossed; it is kept inside the local term throughout the comparison.  The displayed inequality follows from the binomial inverse-arm bound
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\pi_k^j(1-\pi_k)^{m-j}
\mathbf 1\{0<j<m\}
\left(\frac1j+\frac1{m-j}\right)
\le
\frac{4}{(m+1)\epsilon}
\]
and the interior-mass lower bound
\[
2\epsilon(1-\epsilon)
\le
\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\pi_k^j(1-\pi_k)^{m-j}
\mathbf 1\{0<j<m\}.
\]
Summing the local comparison over cells and outside assignments gives the finite-design inequality with constant \(4/\{\epsilon^2(1-\epsilon)\}\).  We retain the weaker constant \(K_\epsilon=16/\{\epsilon^2(1-\epsilon)\}\), obtaining
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\mathbf 1\{R_n>0\}\frac1{R_n^2}
\sum_{k:I_k=1}N_k^2
\left(\frac1{N_{1k}}+\frac1{N_{0k}}\right)
\right]
\le
K_\epsilon\,
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\mathbf 1\{R_n>0\}\frac1{R_n}
\right].
\]
Combining the last two displays yields
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[(\widetilde T_n-C_n)^2\right]
\le
K_\epsilon M^2\,
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\mathbf 1\{R_n>0\}\frac1{R_n}
\right].
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.integral_occupancyWeightedResidual_sq_le_reciprocal

The centered design bias obeys
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[(C_n-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
M^2\left\{\sigma^2+Q_P^{(n)}(R_n=0)\right\}.
\]
Indeed, because \(Q_P^{(n)}\) is the product observed-data law, every empirically usable cell has positive population mass almost surely: if \(p_k=0\), then no observation falls in cell \(k\) almost surely.  On this full-probability event and on \(\{R_n>0\}\),
\[
C_n-\tau(P)
=
\frac1{R_n}\sum_{k:I_k=1}N_k\delta_k,
\qquad
\delta_k:=\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}-\tau(P),
\]
so \(C_n-\tau(P)\) is a convex average of deviations for positive-mass cells.  The homogeneity condition in \cref{obj:ass:approximate-homogeneity} gives \(|C_n-\tau(P)|\le\sigma M\) there.  On \(\{R_n=0\}\), the totalized definition gives \(C_n=0\), and \(|\tau(P)|\le M\) by \cref{obj:lem:scale-sanity}. % lean: integral_collisionDesignCenter_bias_sq_le

Put
\[
x:=\frac{n\vee d}{n^2}.
\]
Since \(n\ge1\), \(x>0\), and \cref{obj:lem:zeng-usable-occupancy-reciprocal} gives
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\mathbf 1\{R_n>0\}\frac1{R_n}
\right]
\le
B_\epsilon\{x+\exp(-b_\epsilon/x)\},
\qquad
Q_P^{(n)}(R_n=0)\le 2\exp(-b_\epsilon/x).
\]
The elementary inequality
\[
\exp(-b_\epsilon/x)\le b_\epsilon^{-1}x,
\qquad x>0,
\]
follows from \(y\le e^y\) with \(y=b_\epsilon/x\).  Also
\[
x=\frac{n\vee d}{n^2}\le \frac1n+\frac d{n^2}.
\]
% lean: exp_neg_div_absorbed_by_linear_rate
% lean: occupancy_max_rate_le

The clipping interval contains \(\tau(P)\) by \cref{obj:lem:scale-sanity}, so clipping the inner statistic to \([-M,M]\) is a contraction of squared distance from \(\tau(P)\).  Together with \((u+v)^2\le2u^2+2v^2\), the preceding bounds give
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
2K_\epsilon B_\epsilon M^2\{x+\exp(-b_\epsilon/x)\}
+
2M^2\{\sigma^2+2\exp(-b_\epsilon/x)\}.
\]
Using the two preceding displays and the definition of \(C_\epsilon\),
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}-\tau(P))^2\right]
\le
C_\epsilon M^2
\left(\frac1n+\sigma^2+\frac d{n^2}\right),
\]
which is the asserted bound. % lean: continuous_occupancy_collision_upper_all_d
\end{proof}
