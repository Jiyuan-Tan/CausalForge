\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:fixed-interior-tightness-and-shrinking-radius-gap-all-d}]
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  The proof starts from the all-alphabet bracket in \cref{obj:thm:two-sided-minimax-bracket-all-d}; write
\[
\omega_{n,d,\sigma}
:=
\frac1n+\min\left(1,\frac d{n^2}\right)
+\sigma^2\min(1,u_{n,d})
\]
for the capped converse benchmark appearing there.

In the triangular region \(L_n^2<d<nL_n\), the cap is inactive and the selector benchmark is comparable to
\[
b_{n,d}+\min(u_{n,d},\sigma^2),
\]
while the product benchmark is exactly \(b_{n,d}+\sigma^2u_{n,d}\).  This proves the triangular benchmark clause and identifies \(\ell_{n,d,\sigma}\). % lean: selectorBenchmark_triangular

For every radius floor \(\underline\sigma>0\), the lower benchmark \(\omega_{n,d,\sigma}\) controls the selector benchmark \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\) up to a constant depending on \(\underline\sigma\).  Combining this comparison with the all-alphabet bracket gives constants \(c,C\) such that
\[
cM^2r_{n,d,\sigma}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
\le
CM^2r_{n,d,\sigma}
\]
whenever \(\underline\sigma\le\sigma\le2\). % lean: fixed_radius_minimax_equivalence

The same bracket gives the matching-elbow clause once \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\) is compared with \(\omega_{n,d,\sigma}\) in the three stated cases: saturation \(1\le u_{n,d}\), polynomial dominance \(u_{n,d}\le Kb_{n,d}\), and radius dominance \(\sigma^2\le Kb_{n,d}\). % lean: matching_elbow_minimax_equivalence

The exact algebraic elbow follows by expanding \(u_{n,d}\le b_{n,d}\), multiplying by the positive denominator \(n^2L_n^2\), and collecting terms:
\[
u_{n,d}\le b_{n,d}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d^2\le nL_n^2+dL_n^2 .
\]
% lean: polynomialComponent_le_baseRate_iff

The sufficient elbows are the two displayed consequences of this algebra.  If \(d\le\sqrt n\,L_n\), then \(d^2\le nL_n^2\); if \(d\le L_n^2\), then \(d^2\le dL_n^2\).  In either case the exact algebraic elbow gives \(u_{n,d}\le b_{n,d}\). % lean: polynomialComponent_le_baseRate_of_le_sqrt; polynomialComponent_le_baseRate_of_le_log_sq

For the residual-wedge implication, assume the ratio \(r_{n,d_n,\sigma_n}/\omega_{n,d_n,\sigma_n}\) diverges.  The matching-elbow comparison contrapositive forces \(b_{n,d_n}/u_{n,d_n}\to0\), \(b_{n,d_n}/\sigma_n^2\to0\), \(u_{n,d_n}\to0\), and \(\sigma_n^2\to0\); this is exactly membership in the residual wedge. % lean: benchmark_divergence_implies_residual_wedge

Finally, substituting \(d_n=n\) and \(\sigma_n=L_n^{-1/2}\) gives \(b_{n,n}\asymp n^{-1}\), \(u_{n,n}=L_n^{-2}\), and \(\sigma_n^2=L_n^{-1}\).  These relations give the residual-wedge membership of the diagonal sequence. % lean: diagonal_residual_wedge

Along the same sequence, the selector benchmark is of order \(L_n^{-2}\) and the product benchmark is of order \(L_n^{-3}\), up to the shared negligible baseline, so their ratio is eventually of order \(L_n\) and diverges. % lean: diagonal_benchmark_eventually_same_order; diagonal_benchmark_ratio_tendsto_atTop
\end{proof}
