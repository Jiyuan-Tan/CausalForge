\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:fixed-interior-tightness-and-shrinking-radius-gap-all-d}]
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  The proof starts from the all-alphabet bracket in \cref{obj:thm:two-sided-minimax-bracket-all-d}; write
\[
\omega_{n,d,\sigma}
:=
\frac1n+\min\left(1,\frac d{n^2}\right)
+\sigma^2\min(1,u_{n,d})
\]
for the capped converse benchmark appearing there.

In the triangular region \(L_n^2<d<nL_n\), set \(a=1/n\), \(t=d/n^2\), \(u=u_{n,d}\), and \(s=\sigma^2\).  The two triangular inequalities give \(t\le u<1\), and the selector benchmark is
\[
q_{n,d,\sigma}=a+\min\{u,s+t\}.
\]
For nonnegative \(a,t,u,s\) with \(t\le u\), the lower bound follows by splitting according as \(u\le s+t\) or \(s+t<u\), while the upper bound follows by splitting according as \(u\le s\) or \(s<u\).  These two checks give
\[
\frac12\{a+t+\min(u,s)\}
\le a+\min\{u,s+t\}
\le a+t+\min(u,s).
\]
Thus
\[
\frac12\left\{b_{n,d}+\min(u_{n,d},\sigma^2)\right\}
\le q_{n,d,\sigma}
\le b_{n,d}+\min(u_{n,d},\sigma^2),
\]
and the product benchmark is, by definition,
\[
\ell_{n,d,\sigma}=b_{n,d}+\sigma^2u_{n,d}.
\]
% lean: selectorBenchmark_triangular

For every radius floor \(\underline\sigma>0\), put
\[
D_{\underline\sigma}:=\max\{1,\underline\sigma^{-2}\}.
\]
If \(\underline\sigma\le\sigma\le2\), then \(\sigma^2\ge\underline\sigma^2\), and the frontier and converse benchmarks satisfy
\[
r_{n,d,\sigma}
\le \frac1n+\min(1,u_{n,d})
\le D_{\underline\sigma}
\left\{\frac1n+\sigma^2\min(1,u_{n,d})\right\}
\le D_{\underline\sigma}\omega_{n,d,\sigma}.
\]
Combining this comparison with \cref{obj:thm:two-sided-minimax-bracket-all-d}, which gives
\[
c_0M^2\omega_{n,d,\sigma}
\le \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
\le C_0M^2r_{n,d,\sigma},
\]
yields the fixed-radius constants \(c=c_0/D_{\underline\sigma}\) and \(C=C_0\):
\[
cM^2r_{n,d,\sigma}
\le
\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
\le
CM^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\]
% lean: fixed_radius_minimax_equivalence

For the matching elbows, the benchmark comparison used with the same bracket is
\[
r_{n,d,\sigma}\le (1+K)\omega_{n,d,\sigma}.
\]
When \(u_{n,d}\ge1\),
\[
r_{n,d,\sigma}\le \frac1n+\min\left\{1,\sigma^2+\frac d{n^2}\right\}\le \omega_{n,d,\sigma},
\]
where the last inequality is checked by the two cases \(d/n^2\le1\) and \(d/n^2>1\).  When \(u_{n,d}\le Kb_{n,d}\), the case \(d/n^2\le1\) gives
\[
r_{n,d,\sigma}\le \frac1n+u_{n,d}\le (1+K)b_{n,d}\le(1+K)\omega_{n,d,\sigma},
\]
and the case \(d/n^2>1\) gives \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\le n^{-1}+1\le\omega_{n,d,\sigma}\).  When \(\sigma^2\le Kb_{n,d}\), the case \(d/n^2\le1\) gives
\[
r_{n,d,\sigma}\le b_{n,d}+\sigma^2\le(1+K)b_{n,d}\le(1+K)\omega_{n,d,\sigma},
\]
and the case \(d/n^2>1\) is again \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\le n^{-1}+1\le\omega_{n,d,\sigma}\).  The all-alphabet bracket therefore gives the matching-elbow minimax equivalence with lower constant \(c_0/(1+K)\) and upper constant \(C_0\).
% lean: matching_elbow_minimax_equivalence

For \(n>0\), the logarithmic factor is positive.  Expanding \(u_{n,d}\le b_{n,d}\), multiplying by the positive denominator \(n^2L_n^2\), and collecting terms gives
\[
u_{n,d}\le b_{n,d}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d^2\le nL_n^2+dL_n^2 .
\]
% lean: polynomialComponent_le_baseRate_iff

The sufficient elbows are the two displayed consequences of this algebra.  If \(d\le\sqrt n\,L_n\), then \(d^2\le nL_n^2\); if \(d\le L_n^2\), then \(d^2\le dL_n^2\).  In either case the exact algebraic elbow gives \(u_{n,d}\le b_{n,d}\).
% lean: polynomialComponent_le_baseRate_of_le_sqrt; polynomialComponent_le_baseRate_of_le_log_sq

For the residual-wedge implication, write along the sequence \(b_j=b_{j,d_j}\), \(u_j=u_{j,d_j}\), \(s_j=\sigma_j^2\), \(r_j=r_{j,d_j,\sigma_j}\), and \(\omega_j=\omega_{j,d_j,\sigma_j}\).  Since \(r_j/\omega_j\to\infty\), the matching-elbow comparison implies that, for every fixed \(K\ge0\), eventually
\[
u_j<1,\qquad Kb_j<u_j,\qquad Kb_j<s_j.
\]
Taking \(K=\eta^{-1}\) for arbitrary \(\eta>0\) gives \(b_j/u_j\to0\) and \(b_j/s_j\to0\).  To prove \(s_j\to0\), fix \(\alpha>0\), put \(\rho=\sqrt\alpha\), and set \(D=\max\{1,\rho^{-2}\}\).  Divergence makes \(r_j/\omega_j>D\) eventually; on the same tail, \(\sigma_j\ge\rho\) would give \(r_j\le D\omega_j\) by the fixed-radius comparison.  Hence \(0\le\sigma_j<\rho\) eventually, so \(s_j<\alpha\) eventually.

It remains to show \(u_j\to0\).  Fix \(\alpha>0\).  If \(\alpha\ge1\), the eventual inequality \(u_j<1\) gives \(u_j<\alpha\).  If \(0<\alpha<1\), use the eventual bounds \(b_j/u_j<1/2\), \(s_j<\alpha/2\), \(u_j<1\), and \(r_j/\omega_j>\alpha^{-1}\).  At any point on this tail with \(u_j\ge\alpha\), the inequality \(d_j/j^2\le b_j<u_j/2\) and \(s_j<u_j/2\) give \(s_j+d_j/j^2<u_j\) and \(d_j/j^2\le1\).  Consequently
\[
r_j=b_j+s_j,\qquad \omega_j=b_j+s_ju_j,
\]
and \(u_j\ge\alpha\) with \(\alpha<1\) implies \(r_j/\omega_j\le\alpha^{-1}\), a contradiction.  Thus \(u_j<\alpha\) eventually.  Together with the assumed eventual admissibility, these four limits are exactly the residual-wedge conditions.
% lean: benchmark_divergence_implies_residual_wedge

For the diagonal sequence \(d_n=n\) and \(\sigma_n=L_n^{-1/2}\), eventual admissibility follows from \(L_n\ge1\).  Direct simplification gives, for \(n\ge1\),
\[
b_{n,n}=\frac2n,\qquad
u_{n,n}=\frac1{L_n^2},\qquad
\sigma_n^2=\frac1{L_n}.
\]
The logarithmic facts \(L_n\to\infty\) and \(L_n^k/n\to0\) for each fixed \(k\) yield
\[
\frac{b_{n,n}}{u_{n,n}}=\frac{2L_n^2}{n}\to0,\qquad
u_{n,n}=\frac1{L_n^2}\to0,\qquad
\frac{b_{n,n}}{\sigma_n^2}=\frac{2L_n}{n}\to0,\qquad
\sigma_n^2=\frac1{L_n}\to0.
\]
Hence the diagonal sequence lies in the residual wedge.
% lean: diagonal_residual_wedge

Along the same sequence, the exact benchmark formulas are
\[
q_{n,n,L_n^{-1/2}}=\frac1n+\frac1{L_n^2},\qquad
\ell_{n,n,L_n^{-1/2}}=\frac2n+\frac1{L_n^3}.
\]
Since \(L_n^3/n\to0\), these formulas give eventually
\[
\frac13 L_n
\le
\frac{q_{n,n,L_n^{-1/2}}}{\ell_{n,n,L_n^{-1/2}}}
\le
2L_n.
\]
Thus the diagonal benchmark ratio is of order \(L_n\), and because \(L_n\to\infty\), the ratio diverges.
% lean: diagonal_benchmark_eventually_same_order; diagonal_benchmark_ratio_tendsto_atTop
\end{proof}
