\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:ate-identification}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item
For each cell \(k=1,\ldots,d\), set
\[
p_k=P(X=k);
\]
when \(d=0\), all sums below are empty.  When \(p_k>0\), let \(\pi_k=P(A=1\mid X=k)\).  The finite-cell law carries an arm-cell outcome law \(\nu_{ak}\) for every arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and every cell, including cells with \(p_k=0\), and its totalized mean is
\[
\mu_{ak}:=\int y\,d\nu_{ak}(y).
\]
For every supported cell and every Borel set \(\mathfrak A\subseteq\mathbb R\), the observed arm-cell factorization is
\[
P_O(X=k,A=a,Y\in\mathfrak A)
=
p_k
\begin{cases}
\pi_k, & a=1,\\
1-\pi_k, & a=0
\end{cases}
\nu_{ak}(\mathfrak A).
\]
If \(p_k>0\), \cref{obj:ass:overlap} and \(\epsilon>0\) make the displayed scalar positive for both arms.  Hence, on supported cells, \(\nu_{ak}\) is the observed conditional law of \(Y\) given \((A=a,X=k)\), and \(\mu_{ak}\) is the corresponding observed conditional mean.  If \(p_k=0\), then \(P_O(X=k)=0\); the totalized law \(\nu_{ak}\) and mean \(\mu_{ak}\) enter the finite formula below only through terms multiplied by \(p_k\). % lean: observed_arm_outcome_measure

\item
Fix \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and a supported cell \(k\), so \(p_k>0\).  By \cref{obj:ass:consistency}, on \(\{X=k,A=a\}\),
\[
Y(a)=Y
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Together with the observed arm-cell factorization, this gives, for every Borel \(\mathfrak A\),
\[
P(X=k,A=a,Y(a)\in\mathfrak A)
=
p_k
\begin{cases}
\pi_k, & a=1,\\
1-\pi_k, & a=0
\end{cases}
\nu_{ak}(\mathfrak A).
\]
Applying \cref{obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability} in cell \(k\) to the potential-outcome event \(\{Y(a)\in\mathfrak A\}\) yields
\[
P(X=k,A=a,Y(a)\in\mathfrak A)\,P(X=k)
=
P(X=k,A=a)\,P(X=k,Y(a)\in\mathfrak A).
\]
Here \(P(X=k)=p_k\), and \(P(X=k,A=a)\) is \(p_k\pi_k\) when \(a=1\) and \(p_k(1-\pi_k)\) when \(a=0\).  The same overlap argument makes this arm-cell probability positive, so cancellation gives
\[
P(X=k,Y(a)\in\mathfrak A)=p_k\nu_{ak}(\mathfrak A).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mathcal L_P(Y(a);\,X=k)=p_k\,\nu_{ak},
\]
where the left side is the pushforward of the full-data law restricted to \(\{X=k\}\). % lean: full_cell_potential_measure

\item
The preceding restricted-law identity supplies the full-data integrability.  On supported cells, \cref{obj:ass:second-central-moment} gives integrability of \(y\mapsto (y-\mu_{ak})^2\) under \(\nu_{ak}\).  Thus \(y-\mu_{ak}\) is square-integrable, hence integrable, and adding the finite constant \(\mu_{ak}\) gives first-moment integrability of \(y\mapsto y\) under \(\nu_{ak}\).  The restricted-law identity transfers this integrability to \(Y(a)\) on each supported cell.  If \(p_k=0\), the full-data mass of \(\{X=k\}\) is \(0\), so the corresponding restricted measure is the zero measure.  Since there are finitely many cells,
\[
Y(1)\in L^1(P),\qquad Y(0)\in L^1(P).
\]
% lean: full_potential_integrable

\item
For every \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and every cell \(k\),
\[
\int_{\{X=k\}}Y(a)\,dP
=
p_k\int y\,d\nu_{ak}(y)
=
p_k\mu_{ak}.
\]
On supported cells this is the integral form of the restricted-law identity in the second step and the defining integral for \(\mu_{ak}\).  On zero-mass cells, the restricted measure is zero and \(p_k=0\), so both sides vanish. % lean: full_potential_cell_integral

\item
Apply the finite-fiber decomposition of the integral to the measurable finite-valued map \(X\).  Using the integrability just established,
\[
\int Y(1)\,dP
=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\{X=k\}}Y(1)\,dP,
\qquad
\int Y(0)\,dP
=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\{X=k\}}Y(0)\,dP.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\,dP
&=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}
\left\{
\int_{\{X=k\}}Y(1)\,dP
-
\int_{\{X=k\}}Y(0)\,dP
\right\}\\
&=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}).
\end{aligned}
\]
By \cref{obj:def:ate-functional},
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k}),
\]
so
\[
\tau(P)
=
\int\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\,dP.
\]
On supported cells, the first step identifies \(\mu_{ak}\) with \(E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=k]\); cells with \(p_k=0\) contribute \(0\) to the totalized sum.  Thus, when conditional expectations on zero-mass arm-cell events are chosen to equal the totalized means \(\mu_{ak}\), the same equality is written as
\[
\tau(P)
=
\int\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\,dP
=
\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k
\left\{
E_P[Y\mid A=1,X=k]-E_P[Y\mid A=0,X=k]
\right\}.
\]
The observed margin determines this finite-cell formula: \(p_k=P_O(X=k)\), and on every supported cell \cref{obj:ass:overlap} gives positive observed mass in both arms, so the observed conditional laws determine the two arm-cell means.  Zero-mass cells have weight \(p_k=0\). % lean: ate_identification
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
