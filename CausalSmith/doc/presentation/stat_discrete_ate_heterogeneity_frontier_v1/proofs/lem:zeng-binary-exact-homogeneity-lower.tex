\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:zeng-binary-exact-homogeneity-lower}]
Let the overlap-dependent collision constant be
\[
u_\epsilon:=\min\left\{\frac12,\,8\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)^2\right\},
\qquad
a_\epsilon:=\min\left\{\frac1{200},\,\frac{u_\epsilon^2}{128}\right\},
\qquad
b_\epsilon:=1,
\qquad
N_\epsilon:=1 .
\]
Because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), we have \(u_\epsilon>0\), and therefore \(a_\epsilon>0\); also \(b_\epsilon>0\).
% lean: zengBinaryExactHomogeneityLower

Fix \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) with \(d>0\), \(N_\epsilon\le n\), and \(d\le b_\epsilon n^2\).  Then \(n>0\) and, since \(b_\epsilon=1\),
\[
d\le n^2 .
\]
Write
\[
R_{n,d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{bin}}
:=
\inf_{\widehat\psi}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\mathrm{bin}}(\epsilon)}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\widehat\psi-\psi(P))^2\right]
\]
for the fixed-sample binary exact-homogeneity minimax risk.

First, set
\[
\Delta:=\frac{2}{5\sqrt n},
\qquad
\Delta>0,
\qquad
\Delta^2=\frac{4}{25n}.
\]
Since \(n\ge1\), \(\Delta\le2/5\), so \(1/2+\Delta\le1\).  Define two endpoint binary laws \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) by uniform cell masses \(p_k=1/d\) and propensities \(\pi_k=1/2\) in every cell, with conditional response means
\[
\mu_{0k}(P_0)=\mu_{1k}(P_0)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{0k}(P_1)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1k}(P_1)=\frac12+\Delta
\]
for every cell \(k\).  The inequalities \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) put \(\pi_k=1/2\) in \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), and the preceding bound on \(\Delta\) puts all displayed conditional means in \([0,1]\).  Thus both laws have uniform cell masses, overlap at level \(\epsilon\), and a cell effect constant across cells.  Hence
\[
P_0,P_1\in\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\mathrm{bin}}(\epsilon),
\qquad
\psi(P_0)=0,
\qquad
\psi(P_1)=\Delta .
\]
For this endpoint pair, the Bernoulli \(\chi^2\) calculation gives total-variation distance at most \(1/2\) for the two \(n\)-fold product laws because
\[
n\,
\frac{(1/2)\Delta^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}
=
\frac{8}{25}
\le \log 2 .
\]
Le Cam's two-point inequality therefore gives
\[
\frac{\Delta^2}{16}
\le
R_{n,d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{bin}},
\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
\frac{1}{100n}
\le
R_{n,d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{bin}} .
\]
% lean: binaryExactMinimaxRisk_parametric_lower

Second, \cref{obj:lem:binary-exact-collision-lower}, applied with \(n\ge1\), \(d\ge1\), \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and \(d\le n^2\), gives
\[
\frac{u_\epsilon^2}{64}\frac{d}{n^2}
\le
R_{n,d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{bin}} .
\]
% lean: binaryExactMinimaxRisk_collision_lower

If \(d\le n\), then
\[
\frac{d}{n^2}\le \frac1n,
\qquad
\frac1n+\frac{d}{n^2}\le \frac2n .
\]
Using \(a_\epsilon\le1/200\), we get
\[
a_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d}{n^2}\right)
\le
\frac1{200}\frac2n
=
\frac1{100n}
\le
R_{n,d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{bin}} .
\]
% lean: zengBinaryExactHomogeneityLower

Otherwise \(n\le d\), and hence
\[
\frac1n\le \frac{d}{n^2},
\qquad
\frac1n+\frac{d}{n^2}\le 2\frac{d}{n^2}.
\]
Using \(a_\epsilon\le u_\epsilon^2/128\), we get
\[
a_\epsilon\left(\frac1n+\frac{d}{n^2}\right)
\le
\frac{u_\epsilon^2}{128}\,2\frac{d}{n^2}
=
\frac{u_\epsilon^2}{64}\frac{d}{n^2}
\le
R_{n,d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{bin}} .
\]
% lean: zengBinaryExactHomogeneityLower
The two cases cover all \(d>0\), and the stated lower bound follows.
\end{proof}
