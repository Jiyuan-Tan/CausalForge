\section{Main results}

The main results characterize the design-based mean squared error in terms of the complete-block score energy \(A_d\) and the neighborhood-overlap factor \(d\). Before the formal statements, the theorem hierarchy is:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Coefficient-mass minimax frontier.} \Cref{obj:thm:degree-frontier} gives matching lower and clipped-SNIPE upper bounds over \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\) at the exposed-binomial scale.
\item \textbf{Bounded-outcome extension.} \Cref{obj:thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier} compares the coefficient-mass and uniformly bounded-outcome risks and gives the attaining clipped estimator for the bounded-outcome envelope.
\item \textbf{Exact complete-block SNIPE risk.} \Cref{obj:thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier} also evaluates canonical unprojected SNIPE exactly on complete directed blocks and globally for that estimator when \(d\mid n\).
\item \textbf{Local-linear benchmark.} \Cref{obj:thm:sharp-local-linear-constant-and-representers} solves the block-local unbiased linear problem on fixed complete directed blocks and characterizes its blockwise risk criterion.
\end{itemize}

The following map keeps those roles visible while also locating the fair-coin specialization.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:degree-frontier}[Degree frontier bounds]
Fix an interaction order \(\beta\in\mathbb N\) and an assignment probability \(p\in\mathbb R\). Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Order.)} \(1\le \beta\).
\item \textbf{(Design probability.)} \(0<p<1\).
\end{itemize}
Then there exist constants \(c_{\beta,p},C_{\beta,p}\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
0<c_{\beta,p}\le C_{\beta,p}.
\]
For every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) and \(B\in\mathbb R\) satisfying \(1\le n\), \(d\le n\), and \(0\le B\), with \(k_\star(d,\beta,p)\) as in \cref{obj:def:exposed-order} and \(R_n^\star(d,\beta,p,B)\) as in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}, the following hold:
\[
c_{\beta,p}B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}}{n}\right\}
\le
R_n^\star(d,\beta,p,B).
\]
Moreover,
\[
R_n^\star(d,\beta,p,B)
\le
\sup_{(G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{up}}-\tau_n\right)^2
\right]
\le
C_{\beta,p}B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}}{n}\right\},
\]
where \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\) is the model class in \cref{obj:def:model-class} and \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{up}}\) is SNIPE projected to \([-B,B]\). The unprojected SNIPE estimator also satisfies
\[
\sup_{(G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n\right)^2
\right]
\le
C_{\beta,p}B^2
\frac{d\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}}{n}.
\]

For every \((G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\) and unit \(i\), writing \(N_i=\{j:(j,i)\in G\}\),
\[
\sum_{r=1}^{\min\{\beta,|N_i|\}}
\binom{|N_i|}{r}
\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{\{p(1-p)\}^r}
\le
A_d,
\]
where
\[
A_d
=
\sum_{r=1}^{\min\{\beta,d\}}
\binom{d}{r}
\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{\{p(1-p)\}^r}.
\]
For every \((G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\), every \(r\in\mathbb N\), and every unit \(i\), if \(1\le r\), then
\[
\sum_{\ell\in V_n}
\binom{|N_i\cap N_\ell|}{r}
=
\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq N_i\\ |S|=r}}
\#\{\ell\in V_n:S\subseteq N_\ell\}
\le
d\binom{|N_i|}{r}
\le
d\binom{d}{r}.
\]

Finally, if \(0<B\) and \(1\le d\), let
\[
m=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{d}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
\rho=\frac{md}{n},
\qquad
\delta=\delta(n,d,\beta,B,p).
\]
For every \(U:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to\mathbb R\) satisfying \(|U_b|\le B/2\) for all \(b\), there are models \(M_+\) and \(M_-\) in \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\) whose graph is the complete \(d\)-block graph, whose coefficient schedules are the corresponding block schedules with signs \(+1\) and \(-1\), and whose total treatment effects obey
\[
\tau_n(M_+)=\rho\delta,
\qquad
\tau_n(M_-)=-\rho\delta.
\]
The two block-prior densities with signs \(+1\) and \(-1\), relative to the block dominating measure, satisfy
\[
H^2(\Pi_+,\Pi_-)
\le
\frac{4\pi^2m\delta^2}{B^2A_d}.
\]
The active fraction and block energy satisfy
\[
\frac12\le \rho\le 1,
\qquad
\frac{A_d}{m}
=
\frac{dA_d/n}{\rho}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

The rate in \cref{obj:thm:degree-frontier} separates two forces. The local Bernoulli part is the complete-block energy \(A_d\), equivalently the exposed binomial scale in the theorem statement; the global network part is the single additional overlap charge \(d\), coming from the number of unit scores that can share a given \(r\)-fold assignment set. The projection onto \([-B,B]\) supplies the saturated branch of the bound, while the unprojected estimator carries the linear-regime variance scale.

The next theorem states the simultaneous comparison between the coefficient-mass class and the uniformly bounded-outcome class. It uses the two minimax risks from \cref{obj:def:two-class-minimax-risks} and the bounded-outcome projection from \cref{obj:def:bounded-outcome-clipped-snipe}. The statement should be read in five parts: inclusion of the coefficient-mass class in the bounded-outcome class, the two-class minimax frontier, SNIPE unbiasedness and risk bounds, exact complete-block risks for unprojected SNIPE, and the block lower-bound construction with the exposed-binomial energy comparison.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier}[Bounded-outcome degree frontier]
Fix an interaction order \(\beta\ge 1\) and a treatment probability \(p\in(0,1)\).  Then there are constants \(c_{\beta,p}\) and \(C_{\beta,p}\) such that \(0<c_{\beta,p}\le C_{\beta,p}\) and the following statements hold for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\), every \(B\in\mathbb R\), and every finite design \(D\) on \(\{0,1\}^{V_n}\) satisfying the itemized conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Population and degree.)} The population has size \(n\ge1\), \(V_n=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and the degree index satisfies \(d\le n\).
\item \textbf{(Radius.)} The radius satisfies \(B\ge0\).
\item \textbf{(Design.)} The design \(D\) is the product Bernoulli design with common treatment probability \(p\), as in \cref{obj:ass:bernoulli-design}.
\end{itemize}
Let
\[
A_d
=
\sum_{r=1}^{\bar\beta_d}
\binom{d}{r}
\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{\{p(1-p)\}^r},
\]
with \(\bar\beta_d\) and \(k_\star(d,\beta,p)\) as in \cref{obj:def:exposed-order}.  The coefficient-mass model \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class} is contained in the uniformly bounded-outcome model \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)\) of \cref{obj:def:bounded-outcome-model-class}.  If \(B>0\) and \(d\ge1\), this inclusion is strict: some graph and bounded-outcome coefficient schedule in \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)\) has no realization with the same graph and coefficient schedule in \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\).

The minimax risks of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} satisfy
\[
c_{\beta,p} B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}
\le
R_{n,\ell_1}^{\star}(d,\beta,p,B)
=
R_n^{\star}(d,\beta,p,B)
\le
R_{n,\infty}^{\star}(d,\beta,p,B).
\]
For the bounded-outcome clipped SNIPE estimator \(\widehat\tau_{n,\infty}^{\mathrm{up}}\) of \cref{obj:def:bounded-outcome-clipped-snipe},
\[
R_{n,\infty}^{\star}(d,\beta,p,B)
\le
\sup_{(G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)}
\mathbb E_D\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_{n,\infty}^{\mathrm{up}}-\tau_n(G,c)\bigr)^2
\right]
\le
C_{\beta,p} B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.
\]
The coefficient-class clipped SNIPE estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{up}}\) also satisfies
\[
\sup_{(G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}
\mathbb E_D\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{up}}-\tau_n(G,c)\bigr)^2
\right]
\le
C_{\beta,p} B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.
\]

For every \((G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\), the unprojected SNIPE estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}\) is \(D\)-unbiased for \(\tau_n(G,c)\).  The same \(D\)-unbiasedness holds for every \((G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)\).  Its worst-case mean squared error obeys
\[
\sup_{(G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}
\mathbb E_D\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n(G,c)\bigr)^2
\right]
\le
\frac{B^2 d A_d}{n},
\]
and
\[
\sup_{(G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)}
\mathbb E_D\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n(G,c)\bigr)^2
\right]
\le
\frac{B^2 d A_d}{n}.
\]
For every unit \(i\), on both \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\) and \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)\), the local score energy is at most \(A_d\).

The complete-block energy is comparable to the exposed binomial term:
\[
c_{\beta,p}\, d\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}
\le
dA_d
\le
C_{\beta,p}\, d\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}.
\]

If \(d\ge1\) and \(d\mid n\), let \(m=n/d\) and let the graph be the disjoint union of \(m\) complete directed \(d\)-blocks, including loops.  Then canonical unprojected SNIPE has exact worst-case risk on the fixed block graph and globally on both classes:
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\text{ on the fixed block graph}}
\mathbb E_D\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n\bigr)^2
\right]
=
\frac{B^2A_d}{m},
\]
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)\text{ on the fixed block graph}}
\mathbb E_D\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n\bigr)^2
\right]
=
\frac{B^2A_d}{m},
\]
and
\[
\sup_{(G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}
\mathbb E_D\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n(G,c)\bigr)^2
\right]
=
\sup_{(G,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)}
\mathbb E_D\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n(G,c)\bigr)^2
\right]
=
\frac{B^2A_d}{m}
=
\frac{B^2dA_d}{n}.
\]

If \(B>0\) and \(d\ge1\), put
\[
m=\bigl\lfloor n/d\bigr\rfloor,
\qquad
\rho=\frac{md}{n},
\qquad
\delta=\delta(n,d,\beta,B,p)\ \text{ as in \cref{obj:def:block-family}}.
\]
For every vector \(U=(U_b)_{b=1}^m\) with \(|U_b|\le B/2\) for all \(b\), there are two coefficient-mass models on the block graph, with signs \(+1\) and \(-1\), whose coefficient schedules are the corresponding block schedules and whose total treatment effects are
\[
\tau_n^{+}=\rho\delta,
\qquad
\tau_n^{-}=-\rho\delta.
\]
For the two associated block priors \(\Pi_+\) and \(\Pi_-\),
\[
H^2(\Pi_+,\Pi_-)
\le
\frac{4\pi^2 m\delta^2}{B^2A_d},
\]
and the active share satisfies
\[
\frac12\le \rho\le 1,
\qquad
\frac{A_d}{m}
=
\frac{dA_d/n}{\rho}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier} shows that the outcome-bounded envelope shares the same design difficulty as the coefficient-mass envelope, up to constants depending only on \((\beta,p)\). The strict inclusion clause clarifies that this is a genuine comparison between different bounded classes. The complete-block equality identifies the variance contribution \(B^2A_d/m=B^2dA_d/n\) exactly for canonical unprojected SNIPE, so the global rate can be read as complete-block energy multiplied by the block-to-population conversion.

The block-local benchmark restricts attention to fixed complete directed block graphs, coefficient-mass schedules on those graphs, and linear block-measurable weights satisfying exact design-unbiasedness equations. Fix a sequence indexed by \(t\) with positive integers \(n_t\) and \(d_t\) satisfying \(d_t\mid n_t\), put \(m_t:=n_t/d_t\), and let \(G_t\) be the complete directed \(d_t\)-block graph on \(V_{n_t}\). The following definitions name the fixed-graph coefficient class, the admissible local linear estimators, and their minimax risk before the sharp benchmark theorem.

\begin{definitionv}{def:local-linear-class}[Local class \(\mathcal M_t(G_t)\)]
Under \cref{obj:ass:low-order,obj:ass:bounded-coefficient-mass}, on the fixed graph \(G_t\), define
\[
\mathcal M_t(G_t)
:=
\left\{
c:
c_{i,S}=0\ \text{unless } S\subseteq N_i^{G_t}\text{ and } |S|\le \bar\beta_{d_t},
\quad
\sum_{S\subseteq N_i^{G_t}} |c_{i,S}|\le B
\text{ for every } i\in V_{n_t}
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The class in \cref{obj:def:local-linear-class} fixes the complete-block graph and varies only the admissible coefficient schedule. This isolates the cost of block-local unbiased weighting from graph selection or graph heterogeneity.

\begin{definitionv}{def:local-linear-estimator-class}[Block-local estimators $\mathcal E_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}$]
For each unit \(i\in V_{n_t}\), let \(b(i)\) be the index of the complete block containing \(i\), and let \(Z_{b(i)}\) be the assignment vector restricted to that block:
\[
b(i)
:=
\text{the unique } b \text{ such that } i\in V_b,
\qquad
Z_{b(i)}
:=
(Z_j)_{j\in V_{b(i)}}.
\]
Let \(L^2(P_Z)\) denote the finite-design square-integrable functions under \(P_Z\). Define
\[
\mathcal E_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}
:=
\left\{
\widehat\tau_{w,t}
=
\frac{1}{n_t}\sum_{i\in V_{n_t}} w_{i,t}(Z_{b(i)})Y_i(Z):
\begin{array}{l}
w_{i,t}\in L^2(P_Z)\text{ is measurable with respect to }Z_{b(i)},\\
\mathbb E_Z[w_{i,t}]=0,\\
\mathbb E_Z\left[w_{i,t}\prod_{j\in S}Z_j\right]=1
\text{ for every nonempty }S\subseteq N_i^{G_t}
\text{ with }|S|\le\bar\beta_{d_t}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The class in \cref{obj:def:local-linear-estimator-class} encodes block-locality and unbiasedness through moment equations against every nonempty raw monomial up to \(\bar\beta_{d_t}\) in the unit's block. The finite-design space \(L^2(P_Z)\) is the natural Hilbert space for these block weights under the Bernoulli assignment law from \cref{obj:ass:bernoulli-design}.

\begin{definitionv}{def:local-linear-risk}[Local linear risk $R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}$]
\[
R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}
:=
\inf_{\widehat\tau_{w,t}\in\mathcal E_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}}
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[
\left(\widehat\tau_{w,t}-\tau_{n_t}\right)^2
\right].
\]
The supremum ranges over coefficient schedules in \(\mathcal M_t(G_t)\) on the fixed graph \(G_t\).
\end{definitionv}

The risk in \cref{obj:def:local-linear-risk} is the complete-block linear benchmark against which SNIPE-type weights can be compared.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:sharp-local-linear-constant-and-representers}[Sharp local linear representers]
Fix an integer \(\beta\), a common Bernoulli treatment probability \(p\), and a radius \(B\).  Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Smoothness order.)} \(\beta\ge 1\).
\item \textbf{(Assignment probability.)} \(p\in(0,1)\).
\item \textbf{(Radius.)} \(B>0\).
\item \textbf{(Block sequence.)} For each index \(t\), \(n_t\) and \(d_t\) are positive integers with \(d_t\mid n_t\), and
\[
m_t:=\frac{n_t}{d_t}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Complete-block graph.)} For each \(t\), \(G_t\) is the directed graph on \(V_{n_t}\) in which \(j\to i\) exactly when \(j\) and \(i\) are active units in the same quotient class after division by \(d_t\); equivalently, the active units form complete directed blocks with self-loops and inactive units are isolated.
\item \textbf{(Assignment design.)} The assignment design \(D_t\) is the product Bernoulli design with common probability \(p\), as in \cref{obj:ass:bernoulli-design}.
\end{itemize}
Define the complete-block score energy by
\[
A_d
:=
\sum_{r=1}^{\bar\beta_d}
\binom{d}{r}\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{\{p(1-p)\}^r}.
\]
Then, for every \(t\),
\[
R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}
=
\frac{B^2A_{d_t}}{m_t}
=
\frac{B^2d_tA_{d_t}}{n_t}.
\]

For local linear weights \(w\in\mathcal E_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}\), put \(Z_S:=\prod_{j\in S}Z_j\).  For each active block \(V_b\), define
\[
\Psi_{b,t}(w)
:=
\max_{\substack{(\varepsilon_i,S_i)_{i\in V_b}\\
\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\},\ S_i\subseteq V_b,\ |S_i|\le \bar\beta_{d_t}}}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[
\left(
\sum_{i\in V_b}
\varepsilon_i
\left\{
w_{i,t}(Z_{b(i)})Z_{S_i}
-
\mathbf 1\{S_i\ne\varnothing\}
\right\}
\right)^2
\right].
\]
For every \(t\) and every \(w\in\mathcal E_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}\),
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{w,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2\right]
=
\frac{B^2}{n_t^2}\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}\Psi_{b,t}(w),
\]
and, for every active block \(V_b\),
\[
d_t^2A_{d_t}\le \Psi_{b,t}(w).
\]

For any sequence \(w_t\in\mathcal E_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}\),
\[
\frac{
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{w_t,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2\right]
}{
B^2A_{d_t}/m_t
}
\longrightarrow 1
\]
if and only if
\[
\frac{1}{m_td_t^2A_{d_t}}
\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}
\left\{\Psi_{b,t}(w_t)-d_t^2A_{d_t}\right\}
\longrightarrow 0.
\]
Moreover, the representer condition
\[
\frac{1}{n_tA_{d_t}}
\sum_{i\in V_{n_t}}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[
\left\{
w_{i,t}(Z_{b(i)})-g_{d_t}(Z_{b(i)})
\right\}^2
\right]
\longrightarrow 0
\]
implies
\[
\frac{
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{w_t,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2\right]
}{
B^2A_{d_t}/m_t
}
\longrightarrow 1.
\]

If \(d_t\to\infty\), then there exists a sequence \(w_t\in\mathcal E_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}\) such that
\[
\frac{
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{w_t,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2\right]
}{
B^2A_{d_t}/m_t
}
\longrightarrow 1,
\]
and, for all sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
\frac{1}{n_tA_{d_t}}
\sum_{i\in V_{n_t}}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[
\left\{
w_{i,t}(Z_{b(i)})-g_{d_t}(Z_{b(i)})
\right\}^2
\right]
=2.
\]
For every permutation \(\pi:V_{n_t}\to V_{n_t}\) that preserves block membership, meaning \(b(\pi(i))=b(i)\) for every \(i\), the corresponding relabeled normalized average squared distance from the complete-block SNIPE score is also equal to \(2\) for all sufficiently large \(t\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:sharp-local-linear-constant-and-representers} gives an exact finite benchmark for local linear rules on complete blocks. \(\Psi_{b,t}\) is the exact blockwise worst-case quadratic form: averaging it across blocks reproduces the worst-case risk, and its lower bound \(d_t^2A_{d_t}\) yields the sharp risk \(B^2A_{d_t}/m_t\). The representer condition shows that average \(L^2(P_Z)\) closeness to the complete-block SNIPE score is sufficient for asymptotic optimality. The distance-\(2\) construction matters because optimality can occur through the blockwise quadratic form even away from the representer in normalized \(L^2(P_Z)\) distance.

The fair-coin specialization makes the exposed-order calculation transparent. At \(p=1/2\), even-order Bernoulli contrasts cancel and odd orders determine the energy; for first-order interference this gives the particularly simple \(d^2/n\) rate.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:fair-coin-energy-frontier}[Fair-coin energy frontier]
For the fair-coin design \(p=1/2\), the following two conclusions hold.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Exact fair-coin contrasts.)} For every \(n,d,\beta\in\mathbb N\) and \(B\in\mathbb R\) with \(n\ge 1\), \(d\ge 1\), \(d\le n\), \(\beta\ge 1\), and \(B\ge 0\), the Bernoulli contrast coefficients satisfy, for every \(r\ge 1\),
\[
\Delta_r(1/2)
=
\begin{cases}
2^{1-r}, & r \text{ odd},\\
0, & r \text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently the complete-block score energy at interaction order \(\beta\) is
\[
A_d
=
4\sum_{\substack{1\le r\le \bar\beta_d\\ r\ \mathrm{odd}}}\binom dr,
\qquad
\bar\beta_d=\min\{\beta,d\},
\]
and the exposed order \(k_\star(d,\beta,1/2)\) from \cref{obj:def:exposed-order} is the largest odd integer at most \(\bar\beta_d\), with value \(0\) when no such odd integer exists.

\item \textbf{(First-order frontier.)} There exist constants \(c_{\mathrm{lower}},c_{\mathrm{upper}}\in\mathbb R\) with
\[
0<c_{\mathrm{lower}}\le c_{\mathrm{upper}}
\]
such that, for every \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) and \(B\in\mathbb R\) with \(n\ge 1\), \(d\ge 1\), \(d\le n\), and \(B\ge 0\), the order-one energy is
\[
A_d=4d,
\]
and the minimax risks of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} satisfy
\[
c_{\mathrm{lower}}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n}\right\}
\le
R_n^\star(d,1,1/2,B),
\]
\[
R_n^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
=
R_{n,\ell_1}^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
\le
R_{n,\infty}^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
\le
c_{\mathrm{upper}}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n}\right\}.
\]
If additionally \(d\mid n\), then the unprojected SNIPE estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}\) has exact worst-case mean squared error
\[
\sup_{M\in\mathcal M_{n,d,1}(B)}
\mathbb E_{1/2,M}\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n\bigr)^2
\right]
=
\frac{4B^2d^2}{n},
\]
and, over the uniformly bounded-outcome class,
\[
\sup_{M\in\mathcal M_{n,d,1}^{\infty}(B)}
\mathbb E_{1/2,M}\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n\bigr)^2
\right]
=
\frac{4B^2d^2}{n}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:fair-coin-energy-frontier} gives the most legible additive-interference calibration of the general theory. For \(p=1/2\) and \(\beta=1\), the energy identity \(A_d=4d\) turns the general scale \(B^2\min\{1,dA_d/n\}\) into \(B^2\min\{1,d^2/n\}\), with exact unprojected SNIPE risk \(4B^2d^2/n\) on complete blocks when \(d\mid n\). This benchmark is the known-graph Bernoulli counterpart to problems where the interference structure, clustering, or adjustment information is part of the statistical design \citep{YuAiroldiBorgsChayes2022,EichhornKhanUganderYu2026,WangLi2026}.
