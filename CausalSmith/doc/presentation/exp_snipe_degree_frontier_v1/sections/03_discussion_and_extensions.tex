\section{Discussion and extensions}

The results above identify the design-based cost of total-effect estimation through a small set of finite-population quantities. The common feature in \cref{obj:thm:degree-frontier,obj:thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier,obj:thm:fair-coin-energy-frontier} is the product of an overlap factor and a block energy. Under the bounded-degree condition, each treatment coordinate can enter at most \(d\) unit-level scores, and the overlap count in \cref{obj:thm:degree-frontier} turns this graph fact into the variance scale \(dA_d/n\). The energy \(A_d\) then records how much Bernoulli design information is required to recover the all-treated-versus-all-control contrast from low-order centered monomials.

This interpretation connects the rate to the exposure and overlap structure emphasized in network-experiment analyses \citep{Leung2022,Savje2021,Hu2022,Gao2025}. In the present model, the known graph fixes the neighborhoods and the low-order polynomial restriction fixes which assignment interactions are relevant. The exposed order \(k_\star(d,\beta,p)\) summarizes the maximum positive contrast order visible under Bernoulli assignment when such an order exists, with \(k_\star=0\) under the degree-zero convention, and \cref{obj:thm:degree-frontier} converts that order into the binomial scale \(d\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}/n\). For fixed \(\beta\) and \(p\), the rate therefore separates the sample-size contribution \(1/n\), the graph-overlap contribution \(d\), and the interaction-order contribution through the number of exposed subsets inside a degree-\(d\) neighborhood.

The closest estimator-level antecedent, \citet{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}, develops SNIPE for low-order neighborhood interference under known-graph Bernoulli assignment and analyzes the resulting design variance through exposure overlap. The minimax frontier here turns that variance scale into a matched decision-theoretic characterization: the complete-block lower bound, the clipped SNIPE upper bound, and the binomial degree formula \(dA_d/n\asymp_{\beta,p}d\binom{d}{k_\star}/n\) all use the same block energy. The bounded-outcome result and the local-linear complete-block theorem provide the corresponding bounded-envelope and graph-local benchmarks.

The complete-block benchmark clarifies why the same quantity appears in both upper and lower bounds. In the least-favourable construction of \cref{obj:thm:degree-frontier}, complete directed blocks align all within-block neighborhoods, so the block representer in \cref{obj:def:block-score-energy} generates the hardest local contrast at energy \(A_d\). On this graph, \cref{obj:thm:sharp-local-linear-constant-and-representers} gives the exact local linear value \(B^2A_{d_t}/m_t\), equivalently \(B^2d_tA_{d_t}/n_t\). The complete-block calculation matches the global upper bound's degree dependence and gives the exact unprojected-SNIPE worst-case risk on the block graph; on arbitrary graphs, the bounded out-degree condition controls how often local scores can share assignment coordinates.

The local linear characterization also separates risk optimality from a unique choice of weights. The canonical complete-block score is sufficient for attaining the benchmark, and \cref{obj:thm:sharp-local-linear-constant-and-representers} gives the exact blockwise quadratic criterion whose average excess characterizes asymptotic minimaxity inside the local linear class. Thus the complete-block calculation is more than a lower-bound device: it identifies the precise variance target that any asymptotically optimal local linear rule must match, while allowing multiple weight sequences to attain that target. This perspective complements recent work on low-order and degree-sensitive interference estimators \citep{WangLi2026,EichhornKhanUganderYu2026}.

The bounded-outcome extension in \cref{obj:thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier} shows that the same degree dependence is calibrated by \(A_d\) under a uniform potential-outcome envelope. The projection interval changes from the coefficient-mass scale to the natural total-effect range, but the unprojected SNIPE variance remains governed by \(B^2dA_d/n\), and clipping converts that variance calculation into the bounded risk comparison. The fair-coin specialization in \cref{obj:thm:fair-coin-energy-frontier} makes this calibration explicit: for \(p=1/2\), odd interaction orders determine \(A_d\), and first-order interference yields the rate \(B^2\min\{1,d^2/n\}\).

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
The analysis is developed for known bounded-degree interference graphs, independent Bernoulli assignment with common treatment probability, and noiseless finite-population potential outcomes represented by low-order polynomial schedules. Natural extensions include alternative randomization schemes, covariate adjustment, graph uncertainty, clustered assignment, and sampling or outcome noise. These directions would connect the minimax calculation here with broader design and exposure-mapping frameworks for network experiments \citep{Leung2022,Savje2021,Hu2022,Gao2025}.
