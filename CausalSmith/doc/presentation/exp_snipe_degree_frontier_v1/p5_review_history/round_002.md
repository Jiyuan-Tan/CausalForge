# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7/10 — The verified mathematical contribution is substantial, but the manuscript needs significant prose and positioning repair before publication because several claims and presentation choices exceed or blur the formal scope.

The paper establishes a finite-population minimax MSE frontier for known bounded-degree low-order polynomial interference under common-probability Bernoulli assignment, with matching clipped SNIPE upper bounds, complete-block lower constructions, exact complete-block SNIPE constants, and a local linear block benchmark. The contribution is meaningful for design-based network experiments and gives a sharp calibration for a low-order known-graph Bernoulli setting. The formal statements are verified, but the manuscript as written sometimes presents interpretive or comparative claims too broadly and contains avoidable scope, cross-reference, and positioning problems. I recommend major revision focused on claim fidelity, related-work precision, and clearer separation between verified theorem content and interpretation.

## Strengths
- The main minimax frontier is a clear and useful decision-theoretic contribution for low-order known-graph Bernoulli interference models.
- The paper treats both coefficient-mass and uniformly bounded-outcome classes and gives a useful comparison between them.
- The complete-block construction and exact unprojected SNIPE risk provide strong calibration for the degree dependence.
- The local linear benchmark adds a finite-dimensional Riesz/representer interpretation that helps explain the estimator geometry.
- The fair-coin specialization makes the role of assignment probability especially transparent.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] related work** — The comparison to Cortez-Rodriguez, Eichhorn, and Yu is close but still potentially misleading. The manuscript says, "Relative to that upper-bound result, \cref{obj:thm:degree-frontier,obj:thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier} add bounded-class minimax lower bounds, the uniformly bounded-outcome extension, the exposed-binomial overlap factor, and complete-block constants." The honest-scope charter says the paper tightens the stated worst-case variance bound of that work, not SNIPE's actual variance, and it does not claim to beat their Theorem 2 on its unrestricted class.
  - *Fix:* Revise the comparison to state precisely that the paper gives a matched bounded-class minimax frontier in the present low-order known-graph Bernoulli model, tightens the stated worst-case variance calibration in this notation, and leaves the unrestricted-class comparison to the cited theorem outside the claimed contribution.
- **[major·prose] setup and assumptions** — The prose describes the polynomial model restriction and noise-free design setting outside the Limitations section using negative framing: "there is no idiosyncratic outcome noise" and later "An additive mean-zero unit noise extension would add a variance floor..." This violates the affirmative contribution-framing contract outside the explicitly titled limitations/future-work section.
  - *Fix:* Move the outcome-noise discussion to the Limitations and future work subsection, or rewrite the setup affirmatively as: "The model is an exact finite-population polynomial schedule, so the reported risk is the assignment-design MSE for that schedule."
- **[major·prose] discussion and extensions** — The sentence "The saturated branch \(dA_d/n\gtrsim1\) marks regimes where Bernoulli assignment is weak for all-treated-versus-all-control contrasts across the stated bounded classes" is an interpretive regime claim that goes beyond the formal frontier unless carefully tied to the minimax constant-scale statement. The verified theorem establishes the minimax risk scale, not a general qualitative weakness criterion across designs or estimands.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite as a theorem-tethered statement: "In the stated bounded classes, when \(dA_d/n\) is bounded away from zero at the saturation scale, the minimax MSE remains of order \(B^2\) up to constants depending on \((\beta,p)\)."
- **[major·prose] discussion and extensions** — The sentence "consistency at the minimax rate requires the degree bound to grow no faster than roughly \(n^{1/(k_\star+1)}\)" is too compressed because \(k_\star(d,\beta,p)\) can depend on \(d\) at small degrees and constants can deteriorate with \((\beta,p)\), especially near zeros of contrasts. It is directionally right for fixed exposed order but should be stated under the same fixed-\((\beta,p)\), eventual-\(k_\star\) conditions as the theorem.
  - *Fix:* State the growth implication with explicit conditions: fixed \((\beta,p)\), eventual positive degree with stable exposed order, and constants interpreted only for that fixed pair. Alternatively, present it as a heuristic corollary of \(d\binom d{k_\star}=o(n)\).
- **[minor·structure] main results** — Theorem title "Bounded-outcome degree frontier" undersells that the theorem also contains coefficient-class inclusion, strictness, unprojected SNIPE unbiasedness, complete-block exact risks, exposed-binomial comparison, and lower-bound block-prior clauses. This makes the result hard to parse and blurs which clauses are central.
  - *Fix:* Split the theorem or retitle and reorganize it into labelled clauses: two-class frontier, SNIPE risk and unbiasedness, complete-block exact risk, block lower-bound construction, and binomial energy comparison.
- **[minor·structure] setup and assumptions** — Several reader-facing cross-references use `\leanref{...}{...}` rather than cleveref, for example "the \leanref{S-1}{finite-population Bernoulli randomization framework}" and "\leanref{sym:p}{\ensuremath{p}}". The cross-reference contract requires cleveref exclusively for reader-facing cross-references.
  - *Fix:* Use ordinary prose for formalization-scope tags or convert manuscript object references to `\cref{...}` where they refer to paper environments. Reserve Lean identifiers for the verification appendix if needed.
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The abstract is accurate but too dense for a leading econometrics journal: it introduces \(A_d\), \(g_d\), \(h_d\), \(k_\star\), block constructions, exact constants, and fair-coin cancellations in one paragraph. The contribution is hard to absorb despite being important.
  - *Fix:* Recast the abstract into three short movements: setting and main frontier; estimator/lower-bound construction; exact complete-block and fair-coin implications. Keep only the definitions needed to understand the rate.
- **[minor·citation] related work** — The related-work section cites many relevant strands but gives limited detail on the exact assumptions and rates in the nearest local-interference and SNIPE papers. The brief supplied to the authors includes richer context on Fisher, Cox, Rosenbaum, Toulis and Kao, Baird, Basse, Vazquez-Bare, and others that is not fully integrated.
  - *Fix:* Add a concise comparison paragraph specifying the target, design, interference restriction, and rate/bound form in the closest SNIPE and local-interference results, then state exactly how the present bounded-class minimax frontier differs.
- **[minor·prose] verification note** — The verification note says the Lean development checks broad items including "the design-unbiasedness and variance calculations underlying the risk bounds" and "the continuous-prior integrated-risk step and affinity-based total variation bound." Since this section is a trust-boundary statement, readers need a clearer distinction between verified formal theorem statements, published-input citations, and econometric interpretation.
  - *Fix:* Revise the verification note into a scoped list: verified finite-design algebra and theorem statements; cited published inputs used through theorem-local interfaces; unverified scholarly positioning and interpretation. Avoid implying broader empirical or bibliographic validation.
- **[nit·prose] global** — Some prose repeats the same explanation of exact polynomial outcomes and design-only randomness in both Setup and the assumptions discussion, which slows the manuscript.
  - *Fix:* Keep one concise setup paragraph and move extended scope discussion to Limitations and future work.

## Questions for authors
- Can you state explicitly how the Cortez-Rodriguez, Eichhorn, and Yu bound being tightened differs from their actual SNIPE variance and from their unrestricted-class theorem?
- Would you consider splitting the bounded-outcome theorem into a frontier theorem plus separate corollaries for complete blocks and block priors to improve readability?
- Can the verification appendix include a concise map from each main theorem clause to the verified declaration and clarify which cited literature enters only as scholarly positioning?
