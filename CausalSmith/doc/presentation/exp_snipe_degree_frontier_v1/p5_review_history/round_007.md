# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.8/10 — The verified mathematical contribution is substantial, but the manuscript needs sharper positioning, cleaner exposition, and several claim-fidelity repairs before it reads as a publishable econometrics paper.

The paper establishes a design-based minimax MSE frontier for known bounded-degree low-order polynomial network interference under common Bernoulli assignment, with clipped SNIPE attaining the bounded-class rate and complete blocks supplying the lower calibration. The formal claims appear faithful to the verification contract, and the contribution is potentially useful as a benchmark for network-experiment inference. The manuscript is currently too theorem-heavy and internally repetitive, and some prose overstates interpretation or underspecifies the comparison with the closest SNIPE work.

## Strengths
- The main frontier is clean, finite-population, and directly tied to interpretable quantities: complete-block score energy and out-degree overlap.
- The two-envelope comparison between coefficient-mass and uniformly bounded-outcome classes is useful and clearly stated in the formal layer.
- The complete-block exact risk and block-local linear benchmark add valuable calibration beyond a rate statement.
- The verification note and theorem-local audit trail materially improve trust in the algebraic and minimax claims.

## Findings
- **[major·citation] related work** — The closest comparison to Cortez-Rodriguez, Eichhorn, and Yu is still too compressed for a leading-journal reader. The sentence "The comparison to the unrestricted class studied in \citet{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023} is through that paper's stated theorem, while the present frontier supplies the bounded-class calibration in the notation and model class used here" signals the boundary but does not spell out the competitor's formal rate, class, and assumptions enough to establish novelty.
  - *Fix:* Add a short dedicated paragraph comparing the exact CREY theorem used: state its outcome class or envelope, degree/order dependence, assignment conditions, and whether the present paper tightens a stated worst-case bound, changes the model class, or proves a matched lower bound. Keep the claim affirmative: describe the present bounded-class frontier and complete-block calibration rather than implying dominance over CREY's unrestricted setting.
- **[major·structure] main results** — The theorem section is difficult to evaluate because \cref{obj:thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier} combines inclusion, two minimax frontiers, unbiasedness, upper bounds, exact complete-block risk, energy comparison, and lower-bound construction in one very long theorem. The prose says it has five parts, but the displayed statement is still too dense for readers to identify which conclusion supports which claim.
  - *Fix:* Split the theorem into two or three reader-facing results, for example: two-envelope minimax frontier; unprojected SNIPE risk and complete-block equality; block lower construction and energy comparison. If the formal environment must remain unchanged, add a proposition-style corollary immediately after it that isolates the publishable frontier in one display.
- **[major·prose] intro** — The paper states "The rate separates two sources of design difficulty" and later treats the factorization into local energy and an out-degree overlap charge as a substantive interpretation. The formal upper bound verifies the overlap count and the complete-block construction verifies sharpness at the combined scale, but the prose can read as a decomposition theorem for every graph rather than a worst-case bounded-degree frontier.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite the interpretation to say that the worst-case frontier over \(\mathcal G_{n,d}\) is calibrated by complete-block local energy multiplied by the bounded out-degree overlap charge, and that graph-specific risks are controlled through local energies and overlap counts. Avoid suggesting an exact additive or multiplicative decomposition for each fixed graph unless a separate graph-specific theorem is stated.
- **[major·prose] discussion** — The sentence "For fixed \((\beta,p)\) sequences with eventual positive degree and stable exposed order \(k_\star\), the comparison \(dA_d\asymp_{\beta,p} d\binom d{k_\star}\) identifies a sufficient and rate-equivalent vanishing condition..." is not a formal theorem as stated and introduces asymptotic language beyond the main finite-population theorem.
  - *Fix:* Either label this explicitly as a corollary and state its conditions carefully, including fixed \((\beta,p)\), eventual \(d\ge1\), and stable exposed order, or soften it to an interpretation of the displayed finite-sample bound. If retained as a corollary, give a one-line proof from the binomial comparison.
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The abstract uses \(A_d\), SNIPE, and \(k_\star(d,\beta,p)\) before a nontechnical reader has any context. It eventually apposes \(A_d\) and \(k_\star\), but the first sentence is overloaded and the acronym SNIPE is introduced only after the acronym appears in parentheses.
  - *Fix:* Introduce SNIPE by its full phrase before the acronym and consider moving the plain-word description of \(A_d\) before the first rate display. Keep the first-use glosses concise.
- **[minor·structure] setup and assumptions** — Several definitions are followed by prose that repeats the formal statement rather than explaining its role. This makes the setup long and delays the main result.
  - *Fix:* Compress repeated explanatory paragraphs after elementary definitions, especially graph class, coefficient class, potential outcomes, and two-class risk. Preserve motivation for bounded degree, coefficient mass, and bounded outcomes.
- **[minor·structure] main results** — The theorem hierarchy table helps, but it repeats the preceding bullet list and still leaves the reader without a minimal takeaway theorem.
  - *Fix:* Replace either the bullet list or the table with a single compact roadmap, then add a short corollary display stating the central minimax equivalence for the two classes.
- **[minor·prose] appendix** — The appendix proofs are written in a very verbose pedagogical style, with many elementary algebra steps that obscure the high-level proof architecture.
  - *Fix:* Retain enough detail for auditability, but move routine binomial and projection facts into shorter lemmas or compress them into paragraphs. A proof sketch before each long proof would help readers navigate the verified details.
- **[minor·prose] verification note** — The verification note says "The Lean 4 development verifies the finite-design algebra, displayed formal statements, and displayed proofs used in the paper". This is broadly consistent with the contract, but a reader could take it to include scholarly positioning and literature claims.
  - *Fix:* Revise the first sentence to specify that Lean verification covers the formal mathematical statements and their displayed proofs, while econometric interpretation and literature positioning are manuscript claims supported by citations.
- **[nit·prose] global** — Some prose uses nearly identical formulations of the same contribution in the abstract, introduction, main-results commentary, and discussion.
  - *Fix:* Deduplicate repeated statements of the frontier and reserve each location for a distinct purpose: abstract for result, introduction for motivation, main results for formal map, discussion for interpretation.

## Questions for authors
- Which exact theorem from Cortez-Rodriguez, Eichhorn, and Yu is being used as the comparison point, and what are its model-class and envelope assumptions relative to the present bounded classes?
- Do the authors intend the local-energy-times-overlap explanation as a worst-case calibration over bounded-degree graphs or as a graph-specific decomposition? The current prose should make that boundary explicit.
- Would the authors be willing to add a short corollary that states the central two-class minimax frontier in one place, separate from the full verified theorem bundle?
