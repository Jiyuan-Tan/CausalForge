# Referee review

**Recommendation:** minor_revision
**Overall score:** 8/10 — The paper delivers a clear verified minimax frontier with sharp complete-block calibrations, but several presentation and claim-fidelity issues should be corrected before publication.

The submission establishes a finite-population design-based minimax MSE frontier for low-order polynomial network interference under common Bernoulli assignment, with clipped SNIPE attaining the rate and complete directed blocks supplying the lower-bound calibration. The verified statements support the main mathematical contribution and the paper is potentially valuable for researchers studying design-based causal inference under interference. The manuscript is generally coherent, but it needs tightening around scope, closest-prior-work positioning, and a few stale or misleading prose claims.

## Strengths
- The main contribution is substantively meaningful: a matched bounded-class minimax frontier with explicit dependence on the Bernoulli block energy and overlap degree.
- The paper gives a useful bridge between SNIPE variance analysis, Riesz representer geometry, and finite-population network-experiment risk.
- The verified formal layer strongly supports the central theorems, including the block representer identities, overlap count, lower-bound construction, and fair-coin specialization.
- The exposition keeps theorem conditions visible and usually states fixed-\((\beta,p)\) constants and bounded-class scope carefully.

## Findings
- **[minor·prose] Verification note** — The verification note appears stale relative to the current verification contract. It says: "Several proof ingredients enter through theorem-local interfaces in the Lean development. These include ... the block-prior Hellinger calculation in \cref{obj:lem:block-prior-hellinger-bound}, and the block Riesz programs..." The current contract reports empty external dependencies and faithful proofs for the named Hellinger lemma and representer/program results, so this wording makes verified ingredients sound like maintained assumptions.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite the verification note to distinguish verified internal lemmas from theorem-local published inputs. State affirmatively that the listed internal algebraic, Hellinger, and block-program statements are Lean-verified, and reserve the theorem-local interface language only for genuine cited published inputs, if any remain.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The closest-prior-work comparison is directionally useful but not yet precise enough for a journal reader. The manuscript says the present analysis "characterizes" the bounded-class MSE and that the comparison to Cortez-Rodriguez, Eichhorn, and Yu is through their stated theorem, but it gives little detail on that theorem's estimator class, outcome class, assignment probabilities, and degree/rate expression.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact paragraph or table in the Related work section comparing the present theorem to Cortez-Rodriguez, Eichhorn, and Yu: target, model class, boundedness condition, known-graph status, estimator, upper/lower status, and exact rate. Keep the wording aligned with the charter: the paper tightens the stated worst-case variance bound, and the present result is the bounded-class calibration.
- **[minor·prose] Main results** — Theorem labels and prose use broad phrases such as "Degree frontier bounds" and "Bounded-outcome degree frontier" before the reader has absorbed that the frontier is for fixed \(\beta\), fixed common \(p\in(0,1)\), known graph, bounded-degree low-order polynomial schedules, and design MSE. The formal statements include the conditions, but the labels and surrounding prose are broad enough to invite overreading.
  - *Fix:* Adjust the theorem lead-in or theorem titles to include the positive scope, for example "Fixed-\((\beta,p)\) Bernoulli bounded-degree frontier" and "Two bounded-class Bernoulli frontier." Add one sentence before the first theorem stating that all constants are fixed-\((\beta,p)\) constants and the frontier is for the known-graph common-probability Bernoulli experiment.
- **[minor·prose] Discussion and extensions** — The paragraph deriving the heuristic degree-growth condition could be read as a general asymptotic phase boundary even though it relies on fixed \((\beta,p)\), eventual positive degree, and stable exposed order. The current text includes those qualifiers, but the phrase "degree-growth regime in which the minimax scale tends to zero" is broader than the immediately stated heuristic.
  - *Fix:* Rephrase the opening sentence to tie the claim directly to the displayed fixed-pair heuristic, for example: "For fixed \((\beta,p)\) sequences with eventual positive degree and stable exposed order, the comparison identifies a sufficient and rate-equivalent vanishing condition for the displayed minimax scale."
- **[minor·prose] Main results** — The local linear benchmark is introduced as "The paper also solves a finite-dimensional local linear benchmark" and later as a broader "Gauss--Markov-type statement." The formal theorem is confined to block-local linear weights on fixed complete directed block graphs under the \(\ell_1\) coefficient class, with a separate sufficient representer-closeness condition.
  - *Fix:* In the introduction and main-results lead-in, state the benchmark's scope in one sentence before giving the theorem: fixed complete directed blocks, block-local linear unbiased weights, and the coefficient-mass schedule class. Keep the Gauss--Markov analogy but attach it to those exact restrictions.
- **[minor·structure] global** — The manuscript is dense and theorem-heavy, and the logical path from local representer energy to global minimax risk would be easier to follow with a reader-facing roadmap or schematic. As written, readers must piece together \(A_d\), overlap, clipping, and block lower bounds from long prose paragraphs.
  - *Fix:* Add a short result map after the setup or at the start of Main results: one row each for upper bound, lower bound, exact complete-block risk, local linear benchmark, and fair-coin specialization, listing the relevant object and theorem reference.
- **[nit·prose] abstract** — The phrase "exposed nonzero Bernoulli contrast order" appears before the formal definition and is somewhat specialized for an abstract, even with the appositive for \(k_\star\).
  - *Fix:* Add a few words of plain-language gloss, for example: "where \(k_\star(d,\beta,p)\), the largest interaction order with nonzero all-one-versus-all-zero Bernoulli contrast, ..."
- **[nit·citation] Related work** — The related-work brief includes Fisher and Cox, but the manuscript's dedicated Related work section starts with Horvitz-Thompson and Rubin. That is acceptable substantively, but the opening sentence says "baseline language" and omits the randomization-test lineage that many readers will expect in a design-based paper.
  - *Fix:* Either cite Fisher and Cox in the first paragraph or revise the opening to say the paper uses the Horvitz-Thompson/potential-outcome strand of the broader design-based tradition.

## Questions for authors
- Can you provide a concise comparison table against Cortez-Rodriguez, Eichhorn, and Yu that separates their stated worst-case SNIPE variance result from the present bounded-class minimax frontier?
- Is the verification note intended to describe current Lean-verified internal lemmas or remaining theorem-local assumptions? The present wording seems to mix those categories.
