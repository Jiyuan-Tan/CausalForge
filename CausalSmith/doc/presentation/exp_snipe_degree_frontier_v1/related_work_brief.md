Modern design-based causal inference traces to randomized experimentation and finite-population estimation, including Fisher's randomization logic, Cox's discussion of interference, Rubin's potential-outcomes formulation, the Horvitz--Thompson estimator, and the textbook synthesis of Imbens and Rubin \citep{Fisher1935,Cox1958,Rubin1978,HorvitzThompson1952,ImbensRubin2015}. Interference enters this tradition through work defining estimands and randomization analyses when outcomes depend on other units' assignments, including Sobel's housing-mobility critique, Rosenbaum's randomization tests, Hudgens and Halloran's partial-interference framework, and subsequent IPW and causal-network formulations \citep{Sobel2006,Rosenbaum2007,HudgensHalloran2008,TchetgenTchetgenVanderWeele2012,Liu2016,vanDerLaan2014,Ogburn2017}. Econometric work on social interactions supplies a parallel identification lineage through the reflection problem, network-based peer-effect identification, and treatment-response models with social interactions \citep{Manski1993,Graham2008,Bramoulle2009,Manski2013}.

A large methodological strand develops exposure mappings, graph-aware designs, and randomization-based estimators for experiments on networks. Aronow and Samii formalize design, exposure, and estimand components for general interference and derive randomization-based IPW estimators with large-sample theory \citep{Aronow2017}. Bowers, Fredrickson, and Panagopoulos frame interference as a testable modeling opportunity; Toulis and Kao study peer-influence estimands; Ugander, Karrer, Backstrom, and Kleinberg introduce graph-cluster randomization and network exposure probabilities; and Eckles, Karrer, and Ugander analyze design and analysis choices for reducing interference bias \citep{Bowers2013,Toulis2013,Ugander2013,Eckles2017}. Two-stage and saturation-design contributions study direct and spillover effects under group or cluster structure, including optimal saturation designs and finite-sample estimators \citep{Baird2018,Basse2018b,VazquezBare2023}. Design work for network-correlated outcomes and network interference further includes model-assisted allocation, quasi-coloring designs, and randomization tests \citep{Basse2018a,Jagadeesan2020,Athey2018}.

The nearest econometrics and statistics frontier studies sparse or local interference, total-treatment-effect targets, and regression or covariate-adjusted analyses. Leung establishes asymptotic theory under approximate neighborhood interference \citep{Leung2022}. Sävje, Aronow, and Hudgens characterize average effects under unknown limited interference \citep{Savje2021}. Yu, Airoldi, Borgs, and Chayes study total treatment effect estimation when the interference network is unknown, using an additive-interference structure \citep{YuAiroldiBorgsChayes2022}. Hu, Li, and Wager define average direct and indirect effects tied to Bernoulli policy perturbations \citep{Hu2022}. Gao and Ding connect exposure-mapping Hájek estimators to weighted least squares and develop design-based covariance theory for network experiments \citep{Gao2025}. Lin's covariate-adjustment result in ordinary randomized experiments provides the no-interference benchmark for recent covariate-adjusted interference estimators \citep{Lin2013,WangLi2026}.

The closest direct antecedent is the low-order neighborhood-interference model and SNIPE estimator of Cortez-Rodriguez, Eichhorn, and Yu \citep{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}. Their estimator is also related to the pseudoinverse estimator for slate off-policy evaluation and to the general Riesz-estimator framework for randomized experiments \citep{Swaminathan2017,HarshawSavjeWang2025}. The present paper sharpens this low-order, known-graph, unit-Bernoulli line by giving a matched bounded-class minimax MSE frontier with exact degree dependence \(dA_d/n \asymp_{\beta,p} d\binom{d}{k_\star}/n\), constructing both a least-favourable family and an attaining clipped SNIPE estimator, and resolving the complete-block local linear benchmark. In relation to the active frontier, the result provides the unit-randomized calibration for the singleton-cluster corner of clustered-design work \citep{EichhornKhanUganderYu2026} and the unadjusted benchmark around which covariate-adjusted SNIPE refinements can be evaluated \citep{WangLi2026}.
