\begin{abstract}
This paper studies design-based estimation of the finite-population all-treated-versus-all-control effect under known bounded-degree interference and low-order polynomial potential-outcome schedules. Under common-probability Bernoulli assignment, coefficient mass bounded by \(B\), graph in- and out-degree bounded by \(d\), and fixed interaction order \(\beta\), the minimax mean squared error is characterized up to constants depending only on \((\beta,p)\):
\[
B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}}{n}\right\}.
\]
Here \(k_\star(d,\beta,p)\) is the maximum \(r\in\{1,\ldots,\min\{\beta,d\}\}\) whose Bernoulli all-treated-versus-all-control contrast is nonzero when this set is nonempty, with \(k_\star=0\) under the degree-zero convention. For each fixed \((\beta,p)\), the implied constants are uniform in \(n\), \(d\), and \(B\). The upper bound is achieved by the clipped SNIPE estimator, and the lower bound is obtained from complete-block least-favourable priors built from the block representer. The same block energy \(A_d\) governs a uniformly bounded-outcome model class, yielding the equivalent frontier \(B^2\min\{1,dA_d/n\}\). On complete-block graph sequences, the local linear benchmark has exact finite-sample value \(B^2A_d/m\), and asymptotic optimality is characterized by a blockwise quadratic excess criterion. For fair-coin assignment, only odd interaction orders contribute to \(A_d\); for first-order interactions \((\beta=1)\), the frontier becomes \(B^2\min\{1,d^2/n\}\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Network experiments routinely target contrasts between global treatment policies while observing outcomes under randomized assignments that expose only a small portion of the potential-outcome schedule. When one unit's outcome may depend on nearby units' assignments, the variance of a graph-aware estimator is shaped both by the size of each exposure neighborhood and by the number of other outcomes that share the same treatment coordinates. This paper gives a finite-population, design-based minimax calculation for that tradeoff under known bounded-degree interference and low-order polynomial response schedules.

The estimand is the average all-treated-versus-all-control contrast. The population, graph, and potential outcomes are fixed, and randomness comes from independent Bernoulli assignment with common treatment probability \(p\in(0,1)\). Each unit's outcome is a polynomial in the assignments in its graph neighborhood, with interaction order bounded by a fixed \(\beta\) and coefficient mass bounded by \(B\). The graph has maximum in-degree and out-degree at most \(d\), with self-loops counted. This is the coefficient-mass model class in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, and risk is the design mean squared error in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.

The main result, \cref{obj:thm:degree-frontier}, establishes the minimax frontier
\[
R_n^\star(d,\beta,p,B)
\asymp_{\beta,p}
B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}}{n}\right\}.
\]
The index \(k_\star(d,\beta,p)\), defined in \cref{obj:def:exposed-order}, is the maximum \(r\in\{1,\ldots,\min\{\beta,d\}\}\) whose Bernoulli all-treated-versus-all-control contrast is nonzero when this set is nonempty, with \(k_\star=0\) under the degree-zero convention. Thus the rate separates three components: the sample-size factor \(1/n\), the graph-overlap factor \(d\), and the number of exposed interaction subsets inside a degree-\(d\) neighborhood. The clipped SNIPE estimator in \cref{obj:def:snipe-estimator} attains the upper bound, while the unprojected SNIPE estimator has worst-case mean squared error bounded by the corresponding untruncated variance scale.

The proof identifies two finite-design objects behind the rate. The first is the complete-block score energy \(A_d\) introduced in \cref{obj:def:block-score-energy}. \Cref{obj:lem:block-energy-representer} proves that, for complete-block product Bernoulli designs on \(\{0,1\}^d\) and the low-order polynomial space \(\mathcal P_d\), the block score represents the all-treated-versus-all-control contrast, \(A_d\) is the minimum energy of an unbiased local weight, and \(A_d^{-1}\) is the minimum energy of a normalized local perturbation. The second is the overlap count in \cref{obj:thm:degree-frontier}, which shows that each \(r\)-way treatment subset in one neighborhood can be reused by at most \(d\) outcome scores. Together these facts convert local Bernoulli information into the global variance scale \(dA_d/n\), and \cref{obj:lem:block-energy-representer} relates \(A_d\) to \(\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}\).

The lower bound uses complete directed blocks as least-favourable carriers. Within each block, the perturbation is aligned with the normalized Riesz representer \(h_d\), and random baseline coefficients are drawn from a smooth compactly supported density. The two signed priors shift the target by \(\rho\delta\) and \(-\rho\delta\), while their squared Hellinger distance is controlled by \(m\delta^2/(B^2A_d)\). This construction yields the lower frontier at the same scale as the SNIPE upper bound.

The complete-block calculation also gives an exact benchmark for local linear estimators. In \cref{obj:thm:sharp-local-linear-constant-and-representers}, for a fixed complete-block graph sequence with block size \(d_t\), \(m_t=n_t/d_t\) blocks, and the moment restrictions in \cref{obj:def:local-linear-estimator-class}, the local linear minimax risk is
\[
R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}=\frac{B^2A_{d_t}}{m_t}.
\]
The theorem gives a finite-sample blockwise quadratic criterion \(\Psi_{b,t}\), proves that its excess over \(d_t^2A_{d_t}\) exactly characterizes asymptotic attainment of the benchmark, and shows that risk-optimal local linear weights can remain separated from the canonical complete-block SNIPE score in normalized \(L^2\) distance when \(d_t\to\infty\).

The coefficient-mass model has a bounded-outcome analogue in \cref{obj:def:bounded-outcome-model-class}. For this class, \cref{obj:thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier} characterizes the minimax order through the same energy scale:
\[
R_{n,\infty}^{\star}(d,\beta,p,B)
\asymp_{\beta,p}
B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.
\]
The projected estimator is clipped to the natural total-effect range \([-2B,2B]\), and the unprojected SNIPE estimator remains unbiased with worst-case mean squared error at most \(B^2dA_d/n\). On complete blocks with \(d\mid n\), canonical SNIPE has exact worst-case risk \(B^2A_d/m\) for both the coefficient-mass and bounded-outcome classes.

The fair-coin specialization in \cref{obj:thm:fair-coin-energy-frontier} makes the exposure calculation explicit. At \(p=1/2\), even-order Bernoulli contrasts cancel and odd orders determine the energy:
\[
A_d=4\sum_{\substack{1\le r\le \bar\beta_d\\ r\text{ odd}}}\binom{d}{r}.
\]
For first-order interactions \((\beta=1)\), \(A_d=4d\), so the minimax frontier is of order \(B^2\min\{1,d^2/n\}\). This case gives a transparent calibration of the quadratic degree cost induced by first-order interference under fair Bernoulli assignment.

The results build on the design-based potential-outcomes tradition for randomized experiments \citep{Rubin1978,HorvitzThompson1952,ImbensRubin2015} and on interference frameworks based on exposure mappings, local dependence, and network randomization \citep{Sobel2006,HudgensHalloran2008,Aronow2017,Ugander2013,Eckles2017,Leung2022,Savje2021,Hu2022,Gao2025}. The closest antecedent is the SNIPE analysis for low-order neighborhood interference in \citet{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}, which supplies the known-graph Bernoulli estimator and its overlap-aware design variance calculation. The present paper characterizes the matched minimax mean-squared-error frontier in that setting, with explicit degree dependence \(dA_d/n\asymp_{\beta,p}d\binom{d}{k_\star}/n\). Its lower bound is generated by a complete-block least-favourable family aligned with the Riesz representer, and its extensions give the bounded-outcome frontier and the exact complete-block local linear benchmark. The calculation also supplies a unit-randomized reference point for recent work on low-order, clustered, and covariate-adjusted interference estimators \citep{HarshawSavjeWang2025,EichhornKhanUganderYu2026,WangLi2026}. A Lean 4 development accompanies the paper; the verification appendix states the precise formal scope.
