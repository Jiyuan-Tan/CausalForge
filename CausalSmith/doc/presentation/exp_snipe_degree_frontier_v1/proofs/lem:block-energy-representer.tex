\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:block-energy-representer}]
Fix \(\beta\ge1\) and \(p\in(0,1)\).  For \(1\le r\le\beta\), write
\[
e_r(p):=\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{[p(1-p)]^r},
\qquad
m_r(p):=\frac{|\Delta_r(p)|}{[p(1-p)]^r}(1+p)^r .
\]
Let \(c_1\) be the minimum of \(e_r(p)\) over the exposed orders \(1\le r\le\beta\) with \(\Delta_r(p)\ne0\), let \(c_2:=2^\beta\sum_{r=1}^{\beta}e_r(p)\), and let
\[
H:=c_1+\frac{2^\beta\sum_{r=1}^{\beta}m_r(p)}{c_1}.
\]
Since \(\Delta_1(p)=1\) and \(p(1-p)>0\), this exposed set contains \(1\), and hence \(c_1>0\).  Applying the uniform comparison at \(d=1\), together with
\(\binom{1}{k_\star(1,\beta,p)}>0\), gives \(c_1\le c_2\).  The displayed formula for \(H\) is \(c_1\) plus a nonnegative term, so \(H>0\). % lean: blockLowerConst_spec, blockEnergy_uniform_compare, kStar_mem_exposedOrder, rawMassCoeff

Now fix \(d\ge1\).  The energy comparison is obtained by taking \(k=k_\star(d,\beta,p)\): the lower bound isolates the \(k\)-term in \(A_d\), while the upper bound writes \(A_d\) as the finite sum over \(1\le r\le\bar\beta_d\) and uses the fixed-order domination
\[
\binom{d}{r}\le 2^\beta\binom{d}{k}
\]
for every exposed \(r\le k\), with zero contrast terms contributing zero. % lean: blockEnergy_uniform_compare, eligibleOrder_le_kStar, choose_le_pow_mul_choose

Let \(D\) be any common-\(p\) product Bernoulli design on \(\{0,1\}^d\).  The score \(g_d\) pairs to one with every eligible nonconstant raw monomial and has mean zero on the constant monomial; by linearity over the raw-monomial span \(\mathcal P_d\), for every \(f\in\mathcal P_d\),
\[
\mathbb E_D[g_d(Z)f(Z)]=L_d(f).
\]
% lean: blockScore_raw_moment, blockScore_mean_zero, blockScore_represents
The same identities give \(L_d(g_d)=A_d\) and \(\mathbb E_D[g_d(Z)^2]=A_d\), and the positivity of \(A_d\) follows from the \(r=1\) summand.  Since \(h_d=g_d/A_d\), this yields
\[
L_d(h_d)=1,
\qquad
\mathbb E_D[h_d(Z)^2]=A_d^{-1}.
\]
% lean: blockScore_contrast, blockScore_sq_expectation, blockEnergy_pos, blockRepresenter_contrast_energy

The centered-monomial expansion of \(g_d\) in the raw basis places \(g_d\), and therefore \(h_d\), in \(\mathcal P_d\); together with \(L_d(h_d)=1\), this proves feasibility for the perturbation program. % lean: centeredMonomial_raw_expansion, blockScore_mem_polySpace, blockRepresenter_perturbFeasible
If \(h\in\mathcal P_d\) is feasible, then \(\mathbb E_D[g_d(Z)h(Z)]=L_d(h)=1\) and \(\mathbb E_D[g_d(Z)^2]=A_d\).  Expanding the nonnegative square gives
\[
\mathbb E_D\!\left[(A_dh(Z)-g_d(Z))^2\right]
=
A_d^2\mathbb E_D[h(Z)^2]-A_d,
\]
so \(A_d^{-1}\le \mathbb E_D[h(Z)^2]\).  Equality forces the displayed square to have expectation zero; the strict product Bernoulli law has positive mass on every assignment, so \(A_dh=g_d\) pointwise, equivalently \(h=h_d\).  The converse equality follows from the preceding identity for \(h_d\). % lean: perturbFeasible_energy_unique, blockDesign_sq_eq_zero_iff

For the weight program, feasibility extends from the raw constraints to all of \(\mathcal P_d\), and \(g_d\in\mathcal P_d\).  Hence every feasible \(w\) satisfies \(\mathbb E_D[w(Z)g_d(Z)]=L_d(g_d)=A_d\), while \(\mathbb E_D[g_d(Z)^2]=A_d\).  Expanding
\[
\mathbb E_D\!\left[(w(Z)-g_d(Z))^2\right]
=
\mathbb E_D[w(Z)^2]-A_d
\]
gives \(A_d\le\mathbb E_D[w(Z)^2]\), with equality exactly when \(w=g_d\) by the same positive-mass argument. % lean: weightFeasibleAt_represents, blockScore_mem_polySpace, weightFeasibleAt_energy_unique, blockDesign_sq_eq_zero_iff

The canonical feasible perturbation \(h_d\) and canonical feasible weight \(g_d\) give the matching attainable values in the two finite-dimensional programs, and the two optimality inequalities just proved give the reverse inequalities for every admissible pair of endpoint witnesses \(0\le p\) and \(p\le1\).  Thus
\[
\mathsf{PerturbProg}_{d,\beta,p}=A_d^{-1},
\qquad
\mathsf{WeightProg}_{d,\beta,p}=A_d .
\]
% lean: blockPrograms_exact

Finally, expand each centered product in \(g_d\) into raw monomials and apply the triangle inequality.  This bounds the raw coefficient mass of \(h_d\) by
\[
A_d^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^{\bar\beta_d}\binom{d}{r}m_r(p).
\]
The same fixed-order binomial comparison and the lower energy bound \(c_1\binom{d}{k_\star(d,\beta,p)}\le A_d\) reduce this quantity to at most \(H\). % lean: blockRawCoef_mass_estimate, blockRawCoef_mass_uniform
\end{proof}
