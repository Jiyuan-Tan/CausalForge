\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:overlap-count}]
Let
\[
\mathcal F:=\{S\subseteq N_i: |S|=r\}.
\]
For each \(l\in V_n\), the binomial coefficient \(\binom{|N_i\cap N_l|}{r}\) counts the \(r\)-subsets of \(N_i\cap N_l\), equivalently
\[
\binom{|N_i\cap N_l|}{r}
=
\sum_{S\in\mathcal F}\mathbf 1\{S\subseteq N_l\}.
\]
% lean: hchoose, Finset.card_powersetCard
Summing over \(l\) and commuting the two finite sums gives
\[
\sum_{l\in V_n}\binom{|N_i\cap N_l|}{r}
=
\sum_{S\in\mathcal F}\#\{l\in V_n:S\subseteq N_l\}.
\]
% lean: Finset.sum_comm, containingNeighborhoods

Fix \(S\in\mathcal F\).  Since \(|S|=r\) and \(1\le r\), choose \(j\in S\).  Whenever \(S\subseteq N_l\), this \(j\) belongs to \(N_l\), so the set of \(l\)'s with \(S\subseteq N_l\) is contained in the out-neighborhood of \(j\).  The out-degree part of \cref{obj:ass:bounded-degree} therefore gives
\[
\#\{l\in V_n:S\subseteq N_l\}\le d .
\]
% lean: hcontain
Summing this bound over \(S\in\mathcal F\) and using \(|\mathcal F|=\binom{|N_i|}{r}\) yields
\[
\sum_{S\in\mathcal F}\#\{l\in V_n:S\subseteq N_l\}
\le
d\binom{|N_i|}{r}.
\]
% lean: hcardF, Finset.sum_le_sum
Finally, the in-degree part of \cref{obj:ass:bounded-degree} gives \(|N_i|\le d\), and monotonicity of binomial coefficients in the upper argument gives
\[
d\binom{|N_i|}{r}\le d\binom{d}{r}.
\]
% lean: Nat.choose_le_choose, Nat.mul_le_mul_left
Combining the displayed identities and inequalities proves the claim. % lean: overlap_count_le
\end{proof}
