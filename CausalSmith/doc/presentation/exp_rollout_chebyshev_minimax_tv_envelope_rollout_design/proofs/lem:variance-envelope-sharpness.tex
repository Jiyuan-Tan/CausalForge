\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:variance-envelope-sharpness}]
Write
\[
s=\frac{\sigma_0^2}{(n:\mathbb R)},
\]
interpreting the quotient as the total real division used in the statement; in particular the quotient is defined also when \(n=0\). Since \(\sigma_0^2\ge0\) and the real cast of a natural number is nonnegative, this gives \(s\ge0\).

\emph{Covariance bound.}
For real random variables \(U,V\) on \(\Omega\), the design covariance is the finite sum
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(U,V)
=
\sum_{z\in\Omega}\pi(z)\bigl(U(z)-\mathbb E_\pi U\bigr)\bigl(V(z)-\mathbb E_\pi V\bigr).
\]
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the vectors with entries \(\sqrt{\pi(z)}(U(z)-\mathbb E_\pi U)\) and \(\sqrt{\pi(z)}(V(z)-\mathbb E_\pi V)\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(U,V)
\le
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(U)}
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(V)}.
\]
Applying the same bound to \(-U\) and using \(\operatorname{Var}_\pi(-U)=\operatorname{Var}_\pi(U)\) gives
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(U,V)|
\le
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(U)}
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(V)}.
\] % lean: variance_envelope_sharpness
For each \(i,j\), \cref{obj:ass:round-mean-variance-envelope} gives
\(\operatorname{Var}_\pi(X_i)\le s\) and \(\operatorname{Var}_\pi(X_j)\le s\). Hence
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(X_i,X_j)|
\le
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(X_i)}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(X_j)}
\le
\sqrt{s}\sqrt{s}
=
s,
\]
where the last equality uses \(s\ge0\). % lean: variance_envelope_sharpness

\emph{Variance bound.}
By bilinearity of the design covariance, the variance of the weighted sum expands as
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\pi\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^k w_jX_j\right)
=
\sum_{i=0}^k\sum_{j=0}^k
w_iw_j\,\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(X_i,X_j).
\]
Termwise,
\[
w_iw_j\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(X_i,X_j)
\le |w_iw_j\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(X_i,X_j)|
= |w_i|\,|w_j|\,|\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(X_i,X_j)|
\le |w_i|\,|w_j|\,s.
\]
Summing and factoring the double sum therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\pi\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^k w_jX_j\right)
\le
\sum_{i=0}^k\sum_{j=0}^k |w_i|\,|w_j|\,s
=
s\left(\sum_{j=0}^k |w_j|\right)^2.
\] % lean: variance_envelope_sharpness
Substituting \(s=\sigma_0^2/(n:\mathbb R)\) gives the stated variance bound.

\emph{Sharpness.}
Define
\[
\varepsilon_j=
\begin{cases}
1,& w_j\ge0,\\
-1,& w_j<0,
\end{cases}
\qquad j=0,\ldots,k,
\]
and set
\[
\Gamma_{ij}=s\,\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j.
\]
This matrix is positive semidefinite, since it is \(s\) times the rank-one outer product \(\varepsilon\varepsilon^\prime\) and \(s\ge0\). Equivalently, for every \(v\in\mathbb R^{k+1}\),
\[
\sum_{i=0}^k\sum_{j=0}^k v_i\Gamma_{ij}v_j
=
s\left(\sum_{j=0}^k v_j\varepsilon_j\right)^2
\ge0.
\]
Since \(\varepsilon_j^2=1\), each diagonal entry satisfies
\[
\Gamma_{jj}=s\varepsilon_j^2=s=\frac{\sigma_0^2}{(n:\mathbb R)},
\]
and hence obeys the required diagonal bound. Finally, the definition of \(\varepsilon_j\) gives \(w_j\varepsilon_j=|w_j|\) for every \(j\), so
\[
\sum_{i=0}^k\sum_{j=0}^k w_i\Gamma_{ij}w_j
=
s\left(\sum_{j=0}^k w_j\varepsilon_j\right)^2
=
s\left(\sum_{j=0}^k |w_j|\right)^2.
\] % lean: variance_envelope_sharpness
Substituting \(s=\sigma_0^2/(n:\mathbb R)\) gives the desired positive semidefinite matrix and equality. % lean: variance_envelope_sharpness
\end{proof}
