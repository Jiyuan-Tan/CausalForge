\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:amplification-dual-norm}]
Throughout write
\[
\mathcal N=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^k |w_j|:w\in W_\beta(p)\right\},
\qquad
\mathcal A=\left\{\left(\sum_{j=0}^k |w_j|\right)^2:w\in W_\beta(p)\right\},
\]
and
\[
D=
\sup\left\{
|r(1)-r(0)|:
r\in\mathbb R[x],\ \deg(r)\le\beta,\ \max_{0\le j\le k}|r(p_j)|\le1
\right\}.
\]
Call a polynomial \(r\) \emph{admissible} if \(\deg(r)\le\beta\) and \(\max_{0\le j\le k}|r(p_j)|\le1\), so that \(D\) is the supremum of \(|r(1)-r(0)|\) over admissible \(r\).

\emph{Step 1: the moment form of unbiasedness.}
Rewrite the unbiasedness equations in the single moment form
\[
\sum_{j=0}^k w_jp_j^\ell=
\begin{cases}
0,&\ell=0,\\
1,&1\le \ell\le\beta,
\end{cases}
\qquad 0\le\ell\le\beta .
\]
This is equivalent to \(w\in W_\beta(p)\): the case \(\ell=0\) is the zeroth-moment equation in \cref{obj:def:unbiased-weight-set}, while each positive \(\ell\le\beta\) is one of its remaining moment equations. % lean: key
Consequently \(\mathcal N\) is exactly the set of attainable \(\ell^1\) norms for this moment system, and the assumed nonemptiness of \(W_\beta(p)\) gives primal feasibility: \(\mathcal N\ne\emptyset\) and \(\mathcal A\ne\emptyset\). % lean: hprimal_set

\emph{Step 2: representation identity.}
For every \(w\in W_\beta(p)\) and every real polynomial \(r\) with \(\deg(r)\le\beta\),
\[
r(1)-r(0)=\sum_{j=0}^k w_j\,r(p_j).
\]
Indeed, writing \(r(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\beta}c_\ell x^\ell\) and exchanging the two finite sums,
\[
\sum_{j=0}^k w_j\,r(p_j)
=
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\beta}c_\ell\sum_{j=0}^k w_jp_j^\ell
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}c_\ell
=
r(1)-r(0),
\]
where the middle equality is the moment form of Step 1 and the last uses \(r(1)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\beta}c_\ell\) and \(r(0)=c_0\). % lean: repr_identity

\emph{Step 3: weak duality.}
Let \(w\in W_\beta(p)\) and let \(r\) be admissible. By Step 2 and the triangle inequality,
\[
|r(1)-r(0)|
=
\left|\sum_{j=0}^k w_j\,r(p_j)\right|
\le
\sum_{j=0}^k |w_j|\,|r(p_j)|
\le
\sum_{j=0}^k |w_j| ,
\]
the last step because \(|r(p_j)|\le1\) for every node. % lean: dual_le_primal
Thus every value \(|r(1)-r(0)|\) is bounded above by every element of \(\mathcal N\). Since \(\mathcal N\ne\emptyset\), the set of dual values is bounded above, so \(D\) is a finite real number, and
\[
D\le \inf\mathcal N .
\] % lean: sSup_dual_le_sInf_primal_of_momentSol_nonempty
Moreover the zero polynomial is admissible with \(|r(1)-r(0)|=0\), so \(0\) belongs to the dual value set and
\[
D\ge 0 .
\] % lean: dual_nonneg_of_momentSol_nonempty

\emph{Step 4: strong duality.}
There exists \(w^\star\in W_\beta(p)\) with
\[
\sum_{j=0}^k |w^\star_j|\le D .
\]
Indeed, by Step 2 the endpoint value \(r(1)-r(0)\) depends on \(r\) only through the node-value vector \(\left(r(p_j)\right)_{j=0}^k\), so it defines a linear functional on the subspace of \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) spanned by such vectors; by the definition of \(D\) that functional is dominated there by the sublinear majorant \(x\mapsto D\max_{0\le j\le k}|x_j|\). Extending it to all of \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) under the same majorant, its coordinate values \(w^\star_j\) again satisfy the moment equations of Step 1, hence \(w^\star\in W_\beta(p)\); and evaluating the extension at the sign vector \(\varepsilon_j=\operatorname{sign}(w^\star_j)\) gives \(\sum_j|w^\star_j|\le D\max_j|\varepsilon_j|=D\). % lean: exists_moment_le_dual_of_momentSol_nonempty
In particular \(\inf\mathcal N\le D\), which combined with Step 3 gives the first assertion,
\[
\inf_{w\in W_\beta(p)}\sum_{j=0}^k |w_j|
=
\sup\left\{
|r(1)-r(0)|:
\deg(r)\le\beta,\ \max_{0\le j\le k}|r(p_j)|\le1
\right\}.
\] % lean: l1_repr_eq_sup_dual_of_momentSol_nonempty

\emph{Step 5: the squared identity.}
The set \(\mathcal A\) is nonempty by Step 1 and bounded below by \(0\), so \(\inf\mathcal A\) is a well-defined real number.
For every \(w\in W_\beta(p)\) we have \(0\le D\le \sum_j|w_j|\) by Steps 3 and 4, hence \(D^2\le\left(\sum_j|w_j|\right)^2\); taking the infimum over \(\mathcal A\) gives
\[
D^2\le\inf\mathcal A .
\] % lean: hAmp_lower
Conversely, the weight \(w^\star\) of Step 4 satisfies \(0\le\sum_j|w^\star_j|\le D\), and therefore
\[
\inf\mathcal A\le \left(\sum_{j=0}^k|w^\star_j|\right)^2\le D^2 .
\] % lean: exists_moment_le_dual_of_momentSol_nonempty
Thus \(\inf\mathcal A=D^2\). Since \cref{obj:def:amplification-criterion} defines \(A_\beta(p)\) as precisely this infimum, the claimed squared formula follows. % lean: amplification_dual_norm
\end{proof}
