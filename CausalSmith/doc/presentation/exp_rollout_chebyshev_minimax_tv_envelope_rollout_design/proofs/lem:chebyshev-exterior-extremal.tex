\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:chebyshev-exterior-extremal}]
Let \(s=\{0,\ldots,\beta\}\), and let \(v_i\), \(i\in s\), be the Chebyshev extremal nodes
\[
v_i=\cos\frac{\pi i}{\beta},
\qquad i=0,\ldots,\beta .
\]
The node map \(i\mapsto v_i\) is injective on \(s\), so the interpolation nodes are distinct. % lean: hvs
For \(i=0,\ldots,\beta\), let \(L_i\) be the Lagrange basis polynomial for these nodes,
\[
L_i(x)=\prod_{\substack{0\le j\le\beta\\ j\ne i}}\frac{x-v_j}{v_i-v_j}.
\]
Since \(|s|=\beta+1\), any polynomial \(Q\) with \(\deg(Q)\le\beta\) has degree strictly less than the number of nodes, and the same is true for \(T_\beta\). Hence both polynomials are recovered from their node values by Lagrange interpolation.

First prove the one-sided claim: if \(\deg(Q)\le\beta\) and \(|Q(x)|\le1\) for every \(x\in[-1,1]\), then
\[
Q(x_0)\le T_\beta(x_0).
\]
Interpolating \(Q\) at the nodes and evaluating at \(x_0\) gives
\[
Q(x_0)=\sum_{i=0}^{\beta}Q(v_i)L_i(x_0).
\] % lean: hQeval
The same interpolation argument for \(T_\beta\) gives
\[
T_\beta(x_0)=\sum_{i=0}^{\beta}T_\beta(v_i)L_i(x_0).
\] % lean: hTeval
For \(x_0>1\), the signed exterior coefficients satisfy
\[
(-1)^iL_i(x_0)\ge0,
\qquad i=0,\ldots,\beta .
\]
Indeed, every numerator factor obeys \(x_0-v_j>0\), because each Chebyshev node lies in \([-1,1]\) and \(x_0>1\). For the denominator, the Chebyshev-node monotonicity used in this sign calculation gives \(v_i<v_j\) when \(j<i\) and \(v_j<v_i\) when \(i<j\). Thus the denominator has exactly \(i\) negative factors, and multiplying by \((-1)^i\) makes the denominator sign positive. Combining these signs in the product formula for \(L_i\) yields the displayed inequality. % lean: chebyshev_exterior_lagrange_coeff_nonneg

Since \(v_i\in[-1,1]\), the interval bound on \(Q\) gives \(|Q(v_i)|\le1\), and multiplying by the sign \((-1)^i\) preserves this absolute-value bound. Therefore
\[
(-1)^iQ(v_i)\le1.
\] % lean: neg_one_pow_mul_le_of_abs_le_one
The Chebyshev extremal-node identity gives
\[
T_\beta(v_i)=(-1)^i,
\qquad i=0,\ldots,\beta .
\] % lean: Polynomial.Chebyshev.eval_T_real_node
Inserting the factor \((-1)^i(-1)^i=1\) and multiplying the last inequality by the nonnegative coefficient \((-1)^iL_i(x_0)\) yields, for each \(i\),
\[
Q(v_i)L_i(x_0)
=
\left((-1)^iQ(v_i)\right)\left((-1)^iL_i(x_0)\right)
\le
(-1)^iL_i(x_0)
=
T_\beta(v_i)L_i(x_0).
\]
Summing over \(i=0,\ldots,\beta\) and using the two interpolation displays proves the one-sided claim. % lean: upper

Apply this claim first to \(Q=P\), obtaining \(P(x_0)\le T_\beta(x_0)\). Apply it again to \(Q=-P\); the degree bound and interval bound are unchanged, so \(-P(x_0)\le T_\beta(x_0)\). % lean: hneg_upper
The two inequalities give
\[
-T_\beta(x_0)\le P(x_0)\le T_\beta(x_0),
\]
and hence
\[
|P(x_0)|\le T_\beta(x_0).
\] % lean: chebyshev_exterior_extremal
\end{proof}
