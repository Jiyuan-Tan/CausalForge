\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:chebyshev-exterior-extremal}]
Let \(v_i\), \(i=0,\ldots,\beta\), be the Chebyshev extremal nodes
\[
v_i=\cos\frac{\pi i}{\beta},
\qquad i=0,\ldots,\beta .
\]
Since cosine is strictly decreasing on \([0,\pi]\) and \(0\le \pi i/\beta\le\pi\), these nodes are distinct and strictly ordered,
\[
1=v_0>v_1>\cdots>v_\beta=-1,
\qquad
v_i\in[-1,1] .
\]
Moreover \(T_\beta(\cos\theta)=\cos(\beta\theta)\), so evaluating at \(\theta=\pi i/\beta\) gives
\[
T_\beta(v_i)=\cos(\pi i)=(-1)^i,
\qquad i=0,\ldots,\beta .
\] % lean: hvs
Let \(L_i\) be the Lagrange basis polynomial for these \(\beta+1\) distinct nodes,
\[
L_i(x)=\prod_{\substack{0\le j\le\beta\\ j\ne i}}\frac{x-v_j}{v_i-v_j},
\qquad i=0,\ldots,\beta .
\]
Both \(T_\beta\) and any polynomial \(Q\) with \(\deg(Q)\le\beta\) have degree strictly less than the number \(\beta+1\) of nodes, so both are recovered from their node values by Lagrange interpolation.

First prove the one-sided claim: if \(\deg(Q)\le\beta\) and \(|Q(x)|\le1\) on \([-1,1]\), then
\[
Q(x_0)\le T_\beta(x_0).
\]
Interpolating \(Q\) and \(T_\beta\) at the nodes and evaluating at \(x_0\) gives
\[
Q(x_0)=\sum_{i=0}^{\beta}Q(v_i)L_i(x_0),
\qquad
T_\beta(x_0)=\sum_{i=0}^{\beta}T_\beta(v_i)L_i(x_0).
\] % lean: hQeval
For \(x_0>1\), the signed exterior coefficients satisfy
\[
(-1)^iL_i(x_0)\ge0.
\]
Indeed, every numerator factor obeys \(x_0-v_j>0\) because \(v_j\le1<x_0\), so
\[
\prod_{j\ne i}(x_0-v_j)>0 ;
\]
and splitting the denominator at \(i\), the strict ordering \(v_0>\cdots>v_\beta\) gives \(v_i-v_j<0\) for each of the \(i\) indices \(j<i\) and \(v_i-v_j>0\) for each index \(j>i\), so
\[
(-1)^i\prod_{j\ne i}(v_i-v_j)>0 .
\]
Dividing the first display by the second yields the asserted sign. % lean: chebyshev_exterior_lagrange_coeff_nonneg
Since \(v_i\in[-1,1]\), the interval bound on \(Q\) gives \(|Q(v_i)|\le1\), and \(\left|(-1)^iQ(v_i)\right|=|Q(v_i)|\), hence
\[
(-1)^iQ(v_i)\le1.
\] % lean: neg_one_pow_mul_le_of_abs_le_one
Inserting the factor \((-1)^i(-1)^i=1\) and multiplying the last inequality by the nonnegative coefficient \((-1)^iL_i(x_0)\) yields
\[
Q(v_i)L_i(x_0)
=
\left((-1)^iQ(v_i)\right)\left((-1)^iL_i(x_0)\right)
\le
(-1)^iL_i(x_0)
=
T_\beta(v_i)L_i(x_0).
\]
Summing over \(i=0,\ldots,\beta\) and using the two interpolation displays proves the one-sided claim. % lean: upper

Apply this claim first to \(Q=P\), obtaining \(P(x_0)\le T_\beta(x_0)\). Apply it again to \(Q=-P\); the degree bound and interval bound are unchanged, so \(-P(x_0)\le T_\beta(x_0)\). % lean: hneg_upper
The two inequalities are exactly
\[
-T_\beta(x_0)\le P(x_0)\le T_\beta(x_0),
\]
and hence
\[
|P(x_0)|\le T_\beta(x_0).
\] % lean: chebyshev_exterior_extremal
\end{proof}
