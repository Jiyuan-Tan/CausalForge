# Chebyshev Rollouts for Learning the Full-Adoption Effect

Under polynomial rollout means and a sharp variance envelope, Chebyshev-Lobatto measurement fractions attain the minimax exponential variance-amplification rate for extrapolating from partial rollout \(q<1\) to full adoption.

---

## Punchline

- The design choice is where to measure along a rollout path from \(0\) to \(q\).
- The target is the full-adoption contrast, the difference between the mean at full treatment and baseline.
- Under a degree-\(\beta\) rollout mean curve, unbiased estimation becomes polynomial extrapolation from \([0,q]\) to \(1\).
- Under the variance envelope, risk is exactly controlled by the total variation of the unbiased weights.
- Chebyshev-Lobatto nodes solve the schedule problem up to constants in the low-budget regime.
- Amplification means the factor by which unbiased extrapolation inflates the round-mean variance scale.
- The payoff is a design rule with the minimax amplification base \(\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-q})^2}{q}\).

---

## Why This Question Matters

- Rollout experiments often stop before full adoption because treatment is costly, risky, or operationally constrained.
- With interference, a unit’s outcome can depend on who else is treated.
- The estimand can still be the full-adoption contrast: what happens moving the whole population from untreated to treated.
- The experiment observes only partial treated fractions, so the endpoint must be inferred from the response curve.
- Running example: a platform rolls out a network feature to at most fraction \(q\) of users, then wants the global adoption effect.

---

## The Econometric Problem

- Each measurement round gives a population mean at treated fraction \(p_j\).
- The schedule \(p=(p_0,\ldots,p_k)\) is chosen before seeing outcomes.
- The budget fixes \(p_k=q\), while the target is at \(1\).
- A linear estimator averages round means with weights \(w_j\).
- The central question is: which treated fractions make unbiased extrapolation least variable?

## From Rollout Design to Polynomial Weights

- The unbiased weight set \(W_\beta(p)\) collects the weights that reproduce the endpoint contrast for every degree-\(\beta\) polynomial mean curve.
- The intercept is removed, and each nonconstant monomial contributes one unit to the contrast.
- The amplification criterion \(A_\beta(p)\) is the squared minimum total variation, that is the \(\ell^1\) norm, over \(W_\beta(p)\).
- The schedule problem is to minimize this amplification over admissible treated-fraction placements.

@informal prop:rollout-polynomial-identity: Under static rollout consistency and a degree-\(\beta\) rollout mean, the full-adoption contrast equals the sum of the nonconstant polynomial coefficients.

## Key Idea

- Extrapolation to \(1\) asks the estimator to control a polynomial outside the observed interval \([0,q]\).
- Large alternating weights are the statistical price of making this control exact.
- Chebyshev-Lobatto placement concentrates measurements near \(0\) and \(q\), where endpoint control is most sensitive.
- Oversampling with \(k\ge c\beta\) makes the discrete rollout grid behave like the continuous Chebyshev problem.

## Main Result 1: The Envelope Risk Criterion

@informal thm:tv-envelope-design: Under the rollout law-class restrictions, every unbiased weight vector has variance at most \(\frac{\sigma_0^2}{n}\) times its squared \(\ell^1\) norm, and this bound is sharp over the positive-semidefinite diagonal-envelope covariance class.

@formal thm:tv-envelope-design

## Benchmarks

@informal prop:equal-spacing-benchmark: With \(\beta+1\) equally spaced nodes on \([0,q]\), the amplification is at most \(9(\beta/q)^{2\beta}\).

@informal prop:no-extrapolation-boundary: At full budget \(q=1\), the endpoint-difference rule has amplification at most \(4\).

- Equal spacing gives a simple feasible reference scale.
- Full rollout turns the target into an observed endpoint contrast.
- The low-budget design problem is the extrapolation case between these two reference points.

---

## Main Result 2: Chebyshev Minimax Amplification

@informal thm:chebyshev-minimax: For \(q\le q_{\max}<1\) and \(k\ge c\beta\), every admissible schedule has amplification at least a constant times \(\left(\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-q})^2}{q}\right)^{2\beta}\), and the shifted Chebyshev-Lobatto schedule has amplification at most a constant times the same expression.

@formal thm:chebyshev-minimax

## Why the Novelty Wins

- The naive equal-spacing bound carries the symbolic base \(\beta/q\).
- Chebyshev-Lobatto placement achieves the \(\beta\)-independent base \(\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-q})^2}{q}\).
- The improvement comes from shrinking the extrapolation amplification built into the unbiased weights.
- The variance scale \(\sigma_0^2/n\) stays fixed; the schedule works by controlling the multiplier \(A_\beta(p)\).
- In the platform example, the same rollout budget supports a more stable extrapolation to global adoption.

---

## Why It Is True: Duality

- The minimum total variation of unbiased weights has a dual interpretation.
- Instead of choosing weights, ask how large a degree-\(\beta\) polynomial’s endpoint contrast can be while its values at the measured fractions stay bounded.
- A difficult schedule is one that lets such a polynomial stay quiet at measurements and grow at the endpoint.
- A good schedule blocks that growth.

@informal lem:amplification-dual-norm: The minimum \(\ell^1\) norm of unbiased weights equals the largest endpoint contrast among degree-\(\beta\) polynomials bounded at the schedule nodes.

---

## Why It Is True: Chebyshev Geometry

- Map the observed interval \([0,q]\) to \([-1,1]\).
- The full-adoption point becomes an exterior point \(x_q=2/q-1>1\), with growth factor \(\lambda(q)=x_q+\sqrt{x_q^2-1}\).
- Chebyshev polynomials are extremal for controlling growth at exterior points.
- Oversampled Lobatto nodes make boundedness on the discrete schedule control boundedness over the full interval.
- The endpoint bound supplies the exponential base in \(q\).

@informal lem:chebyshev-exterior-extremal: A degree-\(\beta\) polynomial bounded by one on \([-1,1]\) has absolute value at most \(T_\beta(x_0)\) at any exterior point \(x_0>1\).

@informal lem:oversampled-chebyshev-lobatto-norming: Under \(k\ge c\beta\), bounded values on the Lobatto mesh control the polynomial uniformly on \([-1,1]\) up to a constant \(K(c)\).

@informal lem:continuous-chebyshev-endpoint-bound: For \(0<q\le q_{\max}<1\), every polynomial bounded by one on \([-1,1]\) has endpoint contrast at most \(C(q_{\max})\lambda(q)^\beta\), while the Chebyshev witness contributes at least \(c(q_{\max})\lambda(q)^\beta\).

## Exact Nested Rollout Risk

- The envelope result solves the sharp diagonal-envelope design problem.
- The exact nested risk \(R_{\mathrm{exact}}(\beta,k,q)\) keeps the true covariance matrix \(\Gamma_P(p)\) inside the risk instead of the envelope.
- The paper records the covariance-specific exact optimality question separately.
- The proved exact-risk implication is rate feasibility for Chebyshev-Lobatto schedules through the envelope upper bound.

@informal lem:exact-risk-envelope-upper: At every admissible schedule, the exact nested risk is at most the envelope risk \(\frac{\sigma_0^2}{n}A_\beta(p)\).

@informal oeq:exact-nested-minimax: Under the stated low-budget and oversampling hypotheses, exact Chebyshev-Lobatto optimality asks whether the fixed Chebyshev-Lobatto exact risk equals \(R_{\mathrm{exact}}(\beta,k,q)\).

@informal lem:exact-chebyshev-rate-feasible: With \(k=\lceil c\beta\rceil\), the shifted Chebyshev-Lobatto schedule has worst-case exact variance at most \(\frac{\sigma_0^2}{n}C_+(c,q_{\max})\left(\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-q})^2}{q}\right)^{2\beta}\).

## Takeaways

- Partial rollout with low-order interference leads to a polynomial endpoint extrapolation problem.
- The variance-envelope design criterion is the squared minimum total variation of unbiased weights.
- Chebyshev-Lobatto schedules attain the minimax low-budget amplification rate up to constants.
- The key scale is \(\left(\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-q})^2}{q}\right)^{2\beta}\).
- For exact nested rollout covariance, the same schedule is proved rate-feasible through the envelope comparison.
