\section{Comparison with ACE and Gaussian benchmarks}

The fixed-separation theorem in \cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax} gives a contour guarantee on the broad spectral class and on the fixed-code ACE class. This section records the comparison with the published order-\(r\) ACE guarantee of \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} on the same clipped-code experiment. The comparison has two parts: first, the class relations align the cumulant-separated spectral and ACE restrictions; second, the two generalized-quantile upper bounds can be read on the common class under the same JMS eligibility condition.

The following proposition states the alignment and the resulting upper-guarantee comparison. The constant \(C_{\mathrm{spec}}\) is fixed by the primitive constants in the contour construction, while \(C_{\gamma}\) and the estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}\) come from the published ACE handle. The displayed quantity \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\) is the order-\(r\) generalized-quantile upper guarantee from \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. The sequence clause compares the displayed upper bounds on the common clipped-code class.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:jms-ace-alignment}[ACE and spectral alignment]*
Fix an ACE order \(r\ge2\) and positive constants
\(\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\).  There is a constant
\(C_{\mathrm{spec}}>0\), depending only on these fixed constants, such that
the following statements hold.

Let \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), let \(C_{\gamma}>0\) be the constant in the
published order-\(r\) ACE generalized-quantile guarantee, and write
\(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}\) for the estimator supplied by that
published ACE handle.  Let \(p_0\) be a parameter record whose
\(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\), and \(\gamma\)
coordinates equal the displayed constants, and fix the certified primitive
bank and range records associated with \(p_0\).  For any parameter record
\(p\) sharing the same fixed experiment constants as \(p_0\) and the same
probability level \(\gamma\), and for any supplied code sequences whose clipped
versions are \(\bar g_n\) and \(\bar q_n\), the following hold, under the
usual measurability and integrability conditions.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Class relations.} At the sample size \(n\) of \(p\),
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n},
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}
 \subseteq \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}.
\]
If the comparison exponent of \(p\) satisfies \(s=r\), then the common clipped-code convention gives
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 =\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}.
\]
Here the classes and the comparison subclass are those of
\cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class,obj:def:non-gaussian-class,obj:def:ace-comparison-subclass}.

\item \textbf{ACE upper guarantee.} If a law \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\)
uses the clipped supplied treatment and outcome codes \(\bar g_n,\bar q_n\),
if the JMS eligibility condition \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) of
\cref{obj:def:jms-eligibility,obj:def:jms-eligibility-quantities} holds,
and if \(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\ge0\), then
\[
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \bigl(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\bigr)
 \le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma},
\]
where \(Q_{P,1-\gamma}\) is the generalized quantile in
\cref{obj:def:generalized-quantile} and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}
 &= C_{\gamma} r!16^r\delta^{-1}
 \Big[
   \varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
   + C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}  \\
 &\qquad\qquad
   +64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
   \{r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\}
   (\gamma n)^{-1/2}
 \Big].
\end{aligned}
\]

\item \textbf{Spectral upper guarantee.} If
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) contains at least one law using
the clipped supplied codes, \(n\ge2\), \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) holds,
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\), and
\[
 \varepsilon_{1,n}
 \le
 \{4R_1\exp(2C_gR_1)\}^{-1},
\]
where \(R_1\) is the outer contour radius of \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle} determined by the parameter record in force,
then the certified contour statistic
\(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) of
\cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}, built from the fixed primitive
records, satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \bigl(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|\bigr)
 \le
 \sqrt{\frac{C_{\mathrm{spec}}}{\gamma n}},
\]
where the supremum ranges over laws in the published ACE class whose clipped
supplied treatment and outcome codes are \(\bar g_n\) and \(\bar q_n\).

\item \textbf{Upper-guarantee separation along sequences.} For any sequence
\((p_n)\) of parameter records whose sample size at index \(n\) is \(n\), with the
same fixed experiment constants as \(p_0\),
the same radius sequences \(\varepsilon_{1,\cdot},\varepsilon_{2,\cdot}\),
and \(p_n.\gamma=\gamma\), suppose that eventually
\(n\ge2\), \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) holds,
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\), \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\ge0\),
\[
 \varepsilon_{1,n}
 \le
 \{4R_1\exp(2C_gR_1)\}^{-1},
\]
and the published ACE class contains a law using the clipped supplied codes.
If
\[
 \frac{
   \varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
   + C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
 }{n^{-1/2}}
 \longrightarrow \infty,
\]
then
\[
 \frac{
   \sqrt{C_{\mathrm{spec}}/(\gamma n)}
 }{
   B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}
 }
 \longrightarrow 0 .
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment} places the spectral and ACE guarantees on a shared statistical domain. The class relations use the fact that the ACE \(L^r(P_X)\) code control implies the \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code control needed by the spectral class, while the \(L^s(P_X)\)-restricted spectral subclass matches the published ACE code restrictions when \(s=r\). Under JMS eligibility, the ACE bound retains its finite-order nuisance terms, and the contour bound is governed by the fixed-separation sampling term \((\gamma n)^{-1/2}\). Along the displayed nuisance-dominance sequences, the ratio of the contour upper guarantee to the imported finite-order ACE upper guarantee tends to zero. The conclusion is an upper-bound comparison on the common clipped-code class; the ACE-related lower-bound statement used in the paper is the class-minimax lower bound over all measurable estimators on the fixed-code class.

For later reference, the class relation used inside \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment} is also recorded separately. This isolates the comparison-class bookkeeping from the estimator-specific upper bounds.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:jms-ace-class-relations}[ACE class relations]
Fix a measurable covariate space and a parameter record \(p\) with \(r,k\in\mathbb N\), \(k=r+1\), \(r\ge2\), \(s\in[0,\infty]\) satisfying \(s\ge r\), \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), positive constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},\delta,\sigma\), and nonnegative nonincreasing code-radius sequences \(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\) on positive indices.  Then, for every class index \(n\),
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(JMS inclusion.)} For every model \(m\), if \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) as defined in \cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class}, then \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) as defined in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}.
\item \textbf{(Comparison inclusion.)} For every model \(m\), if \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) as defined in \cref{obj:def:ace-comparison-subclass}, then \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\).
\item \textbf{(Equal-exponent equivalence.)} If \(s=r\), then for every model \(m\),
\[
m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}} .
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:jms-ace-class-relations} is the set-inclusion component of the comparison. It identifies which restrictions are shared by the broad cumulant-separated class and the published ACE class, and it pins down the exact equality case under the common clipped-code convention. This makes the comparison in \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment} an upper-guarantee comparison on the same law class and the same clipped-code experiment.

The second benchmark is the bounded-outcome Gaussian comparison class of \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class}. \Cref{obj:prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy} gives a bounded-outcome Gaussian diagnostic for the intersection of the exact partially linear conditional mean, bounded observed outcome, and nondegenerate Gaussian treatment innovation. In every nonempty fixed-code intersection of that class, the decision-theoretic mean-squared and generalized-quantile risks are zero because the member restrictions determine the target value itself.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy}[Bounded Gaussian degeneracy]
Fix a measurable covariate space and a parameter record \(p\) with sample size \(n\ge1\), orders \(r,k\in\mathbb N\) satisfying \(k=r+1\) and \(r\ge2\), exponent \(s\ge r\), probability level \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), positive constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},\delta,\sigma\), and nonnegative nonincreasing code-radius sequences \(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\) on positive indices. Then every model \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) satisfying \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class} has
\[
\theta_0(P)=0.
\]
Moreover, for any deterministic supplied code sequences \(\widehat g,\widehat q:\mathbb N\to\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\), if there exists at least one \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) whose clipped codes at sample size \(n\) satisfy
\[
\bar g_n(x)=\min\{\max\{\widehat g_n(x),-C_g\},C_g\},
\qquad
\bar q_n(x)=\min\{\max\{\widehat q_n(x),-C_q\},C_q\}
\]
for every \(x\), then the fixed-code Gaussian minimax risks are both zero:
\[
\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)=0,
\qquad
\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)=0.
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy} gives a diagnostic for the source-stated Gaussian comparison class. A bounded observed outcome has bounded conditional support, while the partially linear term \(\theta_0\eta\) is driven by nondegenerate Gaussian treatment noise under \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class}; the proposition records the resulting target value and the induced zero minimax risks on each nonempty fixed-code intersection. Thus the Gaussian benchmark characterizes that simultaneous set of restrictions, whereas the fixed-separation comparison with ACE in \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment} is the active finite-sample upper-guarantee comparison on the cumulant-separated class.

Together, \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment,obj:lem:jms-ace-class-relations,obj:prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy} separate the two comparison roles. The ACE alignment places the published finite-order guarantee of \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} and the contour guarantee on a common cumulant-separated domain, with the sequence clause identifying the regime in which the ratio of displayed upper guarantees tends to zero. The Gaussian proposition explains the bounded-outcome Gaussian class by its implied target and the corresponding decision-theoretic risks, matching the diagnostic role already summarized in \cref{obj:thm:common-experiment-dichotomy}.
