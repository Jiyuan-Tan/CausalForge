\section{Explicit mixture reductions and local benchmarks}

The preceding comparison keeps the cumulant separation level fixed. This section records two constructive weak-non-Gaussian benchmarks that make the transform-zero mechanism explicit in one dimension. The first benchmark treats a symmetric two-component Gaussian mixture, where the relevant zero is known and the contour search collapses to a sine moment. The second follows a Gaussian--Rademacher path indexed by a mixture amplitude, so the denominator scale and the fourth cumulant can be read from the same parameter. These examples connect the contour construction to non-Gaussian identification ideas in independent-component and structural-shock settings \citep{Comon1994,HyvarinenOja1997,HyvarinenKarhunenOja2001,LanneMeitzSaikkonen2017,LeeMesters2024,HoeschLeeMesters2024,ReizingerEtAl2025}.

The first construction is the clipped sine-ratio estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}\). It uses the known imaginary zero of the symmetric mixture transform and estimates the corresponding ratio directly from the empirical moments of \(Y\sin(\pi Z_n/2)\) and \(Z_n\sin(\pi Z_n/2)\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:sine-estimator}[Sine-ratio estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}\)]
Write \(\Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}(u)=\min\{\max\{u,-C_{\theta}\},C_{\theta}\}\) for the projection of a real number onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\).  For \(n\ge 1\) observed triples \(O_i=(X_i,T_i,Y_i)\), let \(\mathbb P_n f=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n f(O_i)\) and let \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\), where \(\bar g_n\) is the supplied treatment code clipped to \([-C_g,C_g]\). The clipped sine-ratio estimator is
\[
\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}
\left\{
\frac{\mathbb P_n[Y\sin(\pi Z_n/2)]}
{\mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(\pi Z_n/2)]}
\right\},
&\displaystyle \mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(\pi Z_n/2)]\ge e^{-\pi^2/8}/4,\\[3mm]
0,
&\displaystyle \mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(\pi Z_n/2)]< e^{-\pi^2/8}/4.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{definitionv}

The threshold in \cref{obj:def:sine-estimator} is tied to the population denominator scale that appears in the next proposition. In the symmetric mixture experiment, the treatment innovation has a known transform zero at \(i\pi/2\), and \(L^1(P_X)\) control of the supplied treatment code keeps the sine denominator bounded away from zero.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:symmetric-mixture-reduction}[Symmetric mixture reduction]
For any real constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\), there is a constant \(C>0\), depending only on these four constants, such that the following holds.  Let \(p\) be a parameter record whose constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\) are the ones just fixed, write \(n\) for its sample size, and let \(P\) be a law in the corresponding model.  Assume the usual measurability and integrability conditions, and suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sampling.)} \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are drawn according to the i.i.d. product law in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}.
\item \textbf{(Symmetric mixture.)} The treatment innovation \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\) has law
\[
\tfrac12 N(-1,1)+\tfrac12 N(1,1).
\]
\item \textbf{(Independence and ranges.)} The treatment innovation is independent of \(X\) as in \cref{obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise}, the outcome innovation satisfies \cref{obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence}, and the range and tail conditions in \cref{obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian} hold.
\item \textbf{(Treatment code.)} The supplied treatment code obeys
\[
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^1(P_X)}\le \varepsilon_{1,n},
\qquad
\varepsilon_{1,n}\le \frac1\pi .
\]
\end{itemize}
Then the fourth cumulant of \(\eta\) is
\[
\kappa_4(\eta)=-2,
\]
the treatment-innovation moment-generating function \(M(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}]\) satisfies
\[
M(i\pi/2)=0,
\]
and, writing \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) and \(D_n=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\),
\[
E_P\!\left[
Z_n\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi Z_n}{2}\right)
\right]
=
e^{-\pi^2/8}
E_P\!\left[
\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi D_n}{2}\right)
\right],
\]
with
\[
\frac{e^{-\pi^2/8}}{2}
\le
E_P\!\left[
Z_n\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi Z_n}{2}\right)
\right].
\]
Moreover, for the sine estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}\) in \cref{obj:def:sine-estimator},
\[
E_P\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}-\theta_0(P)\bigr)^2
\right]
\le
\frac{C}{n}.
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:symmetric-mixture-reduction} gives a fully explicit root-\(n\) benchmark for a symmetric non-Gaussian treatment innovation. The mixture has fourth cumulant \(-2\), its transform vanishes at \(i\pi/2\), and the sine denominator equals a positive contamination factor times \(e^{-\pi^2/8}\). The resulting empirical ratio attains \(C/n\) mean-squared error under the stated range, tail, independence, and \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code conditions.

The next elementary inequality is the algebraic device behind the sine-risk calculations. It separates numerator fluctuation from denominator fluctuation after clipping, using only a lower bound on the population denominator mean.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:clipped-ratio-risk-decomposition}[Clipped ratio risk decomposition]
Use the empirical-average notation \(\mathbb P_n\) from \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}. Let \(W\) and \(R\) be real-valued functions of one observation, let \(\theta,C_{\theta},A,\mu_W\in\mathbb R\), and let \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) be any sample. Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\ge 1\).
\item \textbf{(Clipping range.)} The target value \(\theta\) lies inside the clipping bound \(C_{\theta}\), in the sense that \(|\theta|\le C_{\theta}\).
\item \textbf{(Denominator scale.)} The scale \(A\) is strictly positive.
\item \textbf{(Population denominator level.)} The denominator mean satisfies \(\mu_W\ge A/2\).
\end{itemize}
Define the clipped ratio statistic at threshold \(A/4\) by
\[
\widehat\theta
=
\begin{cases}
\min\!\left\{\max\!\left\{\dfrac{\theta\,\mathbb P_n W+\mathbb P_n R}{\mathbb P_n W},-C_{\theta}\right\},C_{\theta}\right\},
& \text{if } A/4\le \mathbb P_n W,\\
0,
& \text{if } \mathbb P_n W<A/4.
\end{cases}
\]
Then, pathwise on this sample,
\[
(\widehat\theta-\theta)^2
\le
\frac{16}{A^2}
\left\{
(\mathbb P_n R)^2
+
C_{\theta}^2\bigl(\mathbb P_n(W-\mu_W)\bigr)^2
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:clipped-ratio-risk-decomposition} is deterministic. In applications, \(W\) is the sine-weighted residualized treatment, \(R\) is the centered outcome-noise contribution to the sine numerator, and \(A\) is the population denominator scale. Once the sine moment gives \(\mu_W\ge A/2\), the ratio error is controlled by two empirical averages.

The Gaussian--Rademacher path provides a local family approaching Gaussian treatment noise through an explicit fourth-cumulant parameter. Let \(\eta_a\) denote the centered Gaussian--Rademacher treatment innovation in the next statement. Its first positive transform zero, denominator scale, and cumulant magnitude are all explicit functions of \(a\).

\begin{lemmav}{lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark}[Gaussian--Rademacher sine benchmark]
Fix real constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\).  There is a constant \(C>0\), depending only on these four constants, such that the following statement holds.  Let \(p\) be an admissible parameter record whose constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\) are the ones just fixed and whose cumulant order is \(k=4\), let \(a\in(0,1]\), and let \(P\) be a law in the model \(m\).  Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Gaussian--Rademacher path.)} The treatment innovation \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\) has the law of
\[
\eta_a=\sqrt{1-a^2}\,G_{\circ}+aS,
\]
where \(G_{\circ}\sim N(0,1)\), \(S\) is symmetric Rademacher, and \(G_{\circ}\) and \(S\) are independent.
\item \textbf{(Sampling and model restrictions.)} The law satisfies \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling,obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise,obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence,obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian}.
\item \textbf{(Current treatment-code radius.)} For the clipped supplied treatment code \(\bar g_n\),
\[
\int |\,\bar g_n(x)-g_0(x)\,|\,dP_X(x)\le \varepsilon_{1,n},
\]
under the usual integrability condition for this \(L^1(P_X)\) quantity.
\end{itemize}
Define
\[
t_a=\frac{\pi}{2a},\qquad
A_a=a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\},\qquad
\Delta_a=2a^4.
\]
Then the treatment-innovation moment-generating function satisfies
\[
M_a(z)=\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az),
\]
the fourth cumulant satisfies \(\kappa_4(\eta_a)=-2a^4\), and \(t_a>0\) is the first positive zero of the characteristic transform: \(M_a(it_a)=0\) and \(M_a(iu)\ne0\) for every \(u\in(0,t_a)\).  Moreover, for every \(d\in\mathbb R\),
\[
E_P[\sin\{t_a(\eta+d)\}]=0.
\]

Let \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) and \(D_n=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\).  If \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le 1/2\), then
\[
E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]
=A_aE_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
\]
and
\[
E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]\ge A_a/2.
\]
With \(\mathbb P_n\) denoting empirical averaging over \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), define
\[
 \widehat\theta_{a,n}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}
 \frac{\mathbb P_n[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]}
      {\mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]},
 &\displaystyle \mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]\ge A_a/4,\\[3mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\]
where \(\Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}\) clips to \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\).  Its mean squared error obeys
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \le
 C\min\!\left\{1,\frac1n a^{-2}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right)\right\}.
\]
The same smallness condition also gives
\[
\varepsilon_{1,n}\le \frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi}
\]
and the equivalent cumulant-parameterized bound
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \le
 C\min\!\left\{1,\frac1n\Delta_a^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}\right)\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark} shows how the explicit sine reduction behaves as the treatment-noise law moves toward Gaussianity. The first zero is \(t_a=\pi/(2a)\), while the denominator scale \(A_a\) decays exponentially as \(a\) decreases. Expressing the same bound through \(\Delta_a=2a^4\) makes the dependence on fourth-cumulant magnitude explicit. The condition \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le1/2\) is exactly the treatment-code accuracy gate used by this pathwise calculation.

The section closes with a local benchmark statement. It combines the Gaussian--Rademacher path just described with a localized version of the published ACE oracle envelope from \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. The sequence \((\delta_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}\) supplies the shrinking cumulant threshold, and the ACE clause evaluates the published bound after replacing the fixed separation level by the local value \(\Delta=\delta_n\).

\begin{theoremv}{thm:local-to-gaussian-partial-benchmarks}[Local Gaussian benchmarks]
Let \((\delta_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}\) be a real sequence with \(\delta_n>0\) for every \(n\), nonincreasing in \(n\), and \(\delta_n\to0\).  Fix \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\in\mathbb R\).  The following two assertions hold.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Gaussian--Rademacher path.)} There is a constant \(C>0\), depending only on \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\), such that the following holds.  Let \(p\) be any parameter record whose constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\) are the ones just fixed and whose orders are \(k=4\) and \(r=3\).  For any \(a\in(0,1]\) and any law \(P\) in a model \(m\), assume that the treatment noise satisfies
\[
\eta\sim \sqrt{1-a^2}\,G_{\circ}+aS,
\]
where \(G_{\circ}\sim N(0,1)\), \(S\) is symmetric Rademacher, and \(G_{\circ}\perp S\).  Assume also \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling,obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise,obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence,obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian}.  At the sample size \(n\) of \(p\), assume the supplied clipped treatment code \(\bar g_n\) satisfies
\[
x\mapsto |\bar g_n(x)-g_0(x)|\in L^1(P_X),
\qquad
\int |\bar g_n(x)-g_0(x)|\,dP_X(x)\le \varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
Then the conclusion of \cref{obj:lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark} — the explicit mean-squared-error bound along the Gaussian--Rademacher path — holds with constant \(C\) for \(p,m,a\).

\item \textbf{(Local ACE oracle envelope.)} For every published ACE handle, every \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), and every \(C_{\gamma}\in\mathbb R\), if the handle satisfies the conclusion of Theorem~5.4 of \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} at \((\gamma,C_{\gamma})\), then \(C_{\gamma}>0\) and the following local ACE statement holds.  For every parameter record \(p\) whose probability level is \(\gamma\), writing \(n\) for its sample size, if \(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\), then for every deterministic treatment code \(\widehat g\), deterministic outcome code \(\widehat q\), and every model, whenever
\[
\bar g_n=\Pi_{[-C_g,C_g]}\widehat g_n,
\qquad
\bar q_n=\Pi_{[-C_q,C_q]}\widehat q_n,
\]
and, writing \(r\) and \(k\) for the orders of \(p\) and \(\Delta=\delta_n\), the local ACE class conditions
\[
1\le n,
\qquad
|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge \Delta,
\]
hold together with \cref{obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise,obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence,obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian}, the treatment-noise exponential envelope
\[
o\mapsto \exp\{\eta(o)^2/\psi_{\eta}^2\}\in L^1(P),
\qquad
\int \exp\{\eta(o)^2/\psi_{\eta}^2\}\,dP(o)\le 2,
\]
and the nuisance-radius bounds
\[
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \varepsilon_{1,n},
\qquad
\|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \varepsilon_{2,n},
\]
and whenever the local JMS eligibility quantities
\[
a_{1,n}=2\log\!\left(6(C_g+\psi_{\eta})\varepsilon_{1,n}^{-1}\right),
\qquad
b_{1,n}=\log(\gamma n/9),
\]
\[
a_2=4(C_g+\psi_{\eta}),
\qquad
b_{2,n}(\Delta)=
\frac{200\min\{1,C_{\theta}\}\Delta}{\max\{\varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n},(\gamma n)^{-1/2}(\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta})\}}
\]
satisfy
\[
a_{1,n}\ne0,
\qquad
0<\frac{b_{1,n}}{a_{1,n}},
\qquad
0<a_2b_{2,n}(\Delta),
\qquad
a_2\log\{a_2b_{2,n}(\Delta)\}\ne0,
\]
and
\[
r\le
\min\left\{
\frac{b_{1,n}}{a_{1,n}}-a_{1,n}^{-1}\log\!\left(\frac{b_{1,n}}{a_{1,n}}\right),
\frac{b_{2,n}(\Delta)}{a_2\log\{a_2b_{2,n}(\Delta)\}}
\right\},
\]
the published order-\(r\) ACE estimator obeys
\[
Q_{P,1-\gamma}
\!\left(\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)\right|\right)
\le
B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}(\delta_n;C_{\gamma}),
\]
where
\[
B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}(\Delta;C_{\gamma})
=
C_{\gamma}\,r!\,16^r\,\Delta^{-1}
\left(
\varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
+C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
+64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
\bigl(r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\bigr)
(\gamma n)^{-1/2}
\right).
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:local-to-gaussian-partial-benchmarks} gives two procedure-specific local upper benchmarks. The Gaussian--Rademacher clause reuses \cref{obj:lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark} for the \(k=4,r=3\) path under the stated \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code condition. The ACE clause specializes the published order-\(r\) generalized-quantile guarantee of \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} to a local cumulant threshold \(\Delta=\delta_n\), with the displayed eligibility quantities and the resulting envelope \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}(\delta_n;C_{\gamma})\). The last algebraic clause records how an oracle upper bound that is valid under two sets of hypotheses can be summarized by the smaller of the two displayed upper inputs.

These local statements complement the fixed-separation results by making the dependence on the non-Gaussian signal explicit in tractable submodels. For the symmetric mixture, the sine denominator is bounded by a fixed positive scale and the risk is \(C/n\). Along the Gaussian--Rademacher path, the same sine construction has a denominator scale \(A_a\) and a cumulant magnitude \(\Delta_a\), yielding the explicit exponential dependence displayed in \cref{obj:lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark}. The localized ACE envelope gives the corresponding published finite-order benchmark under its stated eligibility and nuisance-radius conditions.
