\section{Appendix B: empirical process, stability, and lower-bound lemmas}

This appendix collects the auxiliary statistical statements used by the fixed-separation contour theorem. The first two results control the analytic transforms that enter the contour statistic: one gives uniform \(L^2\) concentration of the empirical residual and outcome transforms on a fixed disk, and the other gives a deterministic \(L^1(P_X)\) stability bound for the nuisance factor. Together they supply the stochastic and population inputs behind the contour-risk bounds in \cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}. The concentration statement uses the same sub-Gaussian scale conventions and product-sample structure as \cref{obj:def:model-parameters,obj:ass:iid-sampling}, in line with standard empirical-process and high-dimensional probability arguments \citep{VanDerVaart1998,VanDerVaartWellner1996,Vershynin2018,Wainwright2019}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}[Uniform transform concentration]
Fix \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},R_1\in\mathbb R\). There is a constant \(K>0\), depending on these displayed constants, such that the following holds. Let \(p\) be a parameter tuple and let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) be represented by a model in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}. Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Primitive constants.)} The primitive constants of \(p\) are \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\), and its search radius is \(R_1\).
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\ge 2\).
\item \textbf{(Sampling.)} \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) follow the i.i.d. product law specified in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}.
\item \textbf{(Fold.)} \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and the corresponding inference fold \(I_a\) is nonempty.
\end{itemize}
With \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\), define
\[
\widehat F_{a,n}(z)=\frac{1}{|I_a|}\sum_{i\in I_a}\exp(zZ_{n,i}),
\qquad
\widehat G_{a,n}(z)=\frac{1}{|I_a|}\sum_{i\in I_a}Y_i\exp(zZ_{n,i}),
\]
and
\[
F_n(z)=E_P[\exp(zZ_n)],
\qquad
G_n(z)=E_P[Y\exp(zZ_n)].
\]
Then
\[
E\!\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}\left|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)\right|^2\right]
+
E\!\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}\left|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)\right|^2\right]
\le \frac{K}{|I_a|}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2} is the stochastic input for the selected-contour risk proof. The proof below supplies the disk-uniform \(L^2\) bound used after the pilot fold selects a circle.

The next statement is the population stability input. It uses the direct \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code radius in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} to keep the nuisance transform close to one on the contour search region.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}[Zero-Free Nuisance Factor]
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) satisfying \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}, the associated treatment-code discrepancy \(D_n\) obeys
\[
 |D_n|\le 2C_g\quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad
 E_P|D_n|\le \varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
Consequently, for the corresponding factor \(H_n\) and radius \(R_1\),
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|H_n(z)-1|
 \le R_1 e^{2C_gR_1}\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le (4R_1 e^{2C_gR_1})^{-1}\), then \(|H_n(z)|\ge 3/4\) for every \(|z|\le R_1\), and \(F_n\) and \(M\) have the same zeros in \(\{|z|\le R_1\}\), counted with multiplicity.
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free} is the deterministic transfer from treatment-transform zeros to observable residual-transform zeros. The proof below uses the clipping bound and the \(L^1(P_X)\) radius to control the nuisance factor \(H_n\) on the search disk.

A separate tail calculation is used when Gaussian outcome innovations are embedded in lower-bound and benchmark paths. The following elementary scale bound matches the Luxemburg convention fixed in \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:gaussian-exponential-square-quarter-scale}[Gaussian square exponential bound]
Use the Gaussian-law notation of \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}. For every positive scale \(\psi>0\), let \(\zeta\sim N(0,(\psi/4)^2)\). Then \(\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)\) is integrable under the law of \(\zeta\), and
\[
\mathbb E\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)\le 2 .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:gaussian-exponential-square-quarter-scale} is the tail-scale normalization used in the lower-bound construction below.

The final auxiliary result supplies the decision-theoretic lower-bound path. It constructs one-dimensional submodels in which the observed treatment law, regression functions, and supplied clipped codes remain fixed while the coefficient varies locally at \(n^{-1/2}\) scale. Such paths are the standard input for parametric lower bounds in semiparametric experiments \citep{BickelKlaassenRitovWellner1993,VanDerVaart1998}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}[One-dimensional hard submodel]
Fix \(r\in\mathbb N\) and real constants
\(\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\) with \(\psi_{\xi}>0\). There exist constants
\(c_0,C_0,\tau,c_{\mathrm{ACE}}\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
0<c_0,\qquad 0<C_0,\qquad 0<\tau\le \psi_{\xi},\qquad 0<c_{\mathrm{ACE}} .
\]
For every parameter record \(p\) whose constants
\(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\) are the ones just fixed, and every \(n\ge 1\), if the non-Gaussian class in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} is nonempty, then there are a base law \(P^{\circ}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and a family \(\{P_{n,\vartheta}:|\vartheta|\le c_0\}\) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Class membership and target.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), \(P_{n,\vartheta}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and \(\theta_0(P_{n,\vartheta})=\vartheta\).
\item \textbf{(Fixed observed treatment law.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the law of \((X,T)\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}\) equals its law under \(P^{\circ}\).
\item \textbf{(Fixed regressions and supplied codes.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the functions \(g_0,q_0\) and the clipped supplied codes \(\bar g_n,\bar q_n\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}\) equal those under \(P^{\circ}\).
\item \textbf{(Gaussian outcome innovation.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the outcome innovation \(\xi=Y-q_0(X)-\theta_0\eta\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}\) has law \(N(0,\tau^2)\) and is independent of \((X,T)\).
\item \textbf{(Outcome equation.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\),
\[
Y=q_0(X)+\vartheta\,\eta+\xi
\]
almost surely under \(P_{n,\vartheta}\).
\item \textbf{(Product relative entropy.)} For all real \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1,h\) with \(|\vartheta_0|\le c_0\), \(|\vartheta_1|\le c_0\), and
\[
|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=\frac{h}{\sqrt n},
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n}\,\middle\|\,P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n}\right)
\le C_0 h^2 .
\]
\end{itemize}
Moreover, for every starting law \(P^{\circ}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) in the class of \cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class}, there is a family \(\{P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}:|\vartheta|\le c_0\}\) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(JMS ACE membership and target.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) and \(\theta_0(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}})=\vartheta\).
\item \textbf{(Fixed observed treatment law.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the law of \((X,T)\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) equals its law under \(P^{\circ}\).
\item \textbf{(Fixed nuisance objects.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the functions \(g_0,q_0\), the clipped supplied codes \(\bar g_n,\bar q_n\), and the treatment innovation \(\eta\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) equal those under \(P^{\circ}\), and the treatment and outcome code-radius conditions in \cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class} hold at \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\).
\item \textbf{(Gaussian outcome innovation.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the outcome innovation \(\xi=Y-q_0(X)-\theta_0\eta\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) has law \(N(0,\tau^2)\) and is independent of \((X,T)\).
\item \textbf{(Outcome equation.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\),
\[
Y=q_0(X)+\vartheta\,\eta+\xi
\]
almost surely under \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\).
\item \textbf{(Product relative entropy.)} For all real \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1,h\) with \(|\vartheta_0|\le c_0\), \(|\vartheta_1|\le c_0\), and
\[
|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=\frac{h}{\sqrt n},
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left((P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\mathrm{ACE}})^{\otimes n}\,\middle\|\,(P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\mathrm{ACE}})^{\otimes n}\right)
\le C_0 h^2 .
\]
\end{itemize}
Finally, for every pair of clipped-code functions \(g_{\star},q_{\star}:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\), if there is a law \(P^{\circ}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) with \(\bar g_n=g_{\star}\) and \(\bar q_n=q_{\star}\), then the fixed-code JMS ACE minimax risk satisfies
\[
\frac{c_{\mathrm{ACE}}}{n}
\le
\inf_{\widetilde\theta(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\bar g_n,\bar q_n)}
\sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}:\\ \bar g_n=g_{\star},\ \bar q_n=q_{\star}}}
E_P\!\left[\{\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P)\}^2\right].
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel} is the lower-bound ingredient for the \(n^{-1}\) minimax statements. The proof below builds the fixed-code local path and applies the relative-entropy calculation used in the testing-to-estimation step.

The ACE branch of the proof verifies the same fixed-code path inside \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\), with the treatment innovation and clipped nuisance codes preserved along the path.
