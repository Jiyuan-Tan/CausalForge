# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.2/10 — The verified core is substantial and potentially publishable, but the manuscript needs serious tightening of scope, naming, exposition, and several claim-fidelity repairs before it reads like a journal submission.

The paper establishes a Lean-verified fixed-code contour construction for partially linear models with cumulant-separated non-Gaussian treatment innovations, including contour identification, fixed-separation MSE bounds, ACE comparison statements, Gaussian diagnostics, and explicit mixture benchmarks. The mathematical contribution is novel and interesting, especially the transform-zero contour mechanism and the fixed-code alignment with ACE. As written, however, the manuscript is very difficult to parse, frequently foregrounds implementation and verification machinery over statistical insight, and contains several prose and presentation claims that need to be made more precise or renamed to match the verified scope.

## Strengths
- The core fixed-separation result is strong: a total Borel contour statistic attains matched order n^{-1} MSE on the stated fixed-code non-Gaussian class.
- The identification mechanism through MGF zeros, nuisance zero-freeness, and contour averages is original in the partially linear model setting and is clearly connected to transform methods.
- The paper is unusually explicit about fixed-code conditioning, class alignment, imported ACE guarantees, and represented-data reproducibility.
- The ACE comparison is mostly honest as an upper-guarantee comparison, and the Gaussian result is correctly framed as a diagnostic rather than as an active estimator comparison.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Main fixed-separation result** — The title and repeated shorthand "Adaptive root-n minimaxity" overstate the delivered result. The verified theorem is fixed-code and fixed-separation, with a fixed contour bank and a current L1 treatment-code radius gate; it does not establish adaptation over shrinking non-Gaussianity, tuning-free adaptation across regimes, or uniform inference.
  - *Fix:* Rename the theorem and surrounding prose to something like "Fixed-separation contour minimaxity" or "Fixed-code fixed-separation contour risk bound" and reserve the word adaptive for the finite bank selection within the fixed theorem conditions.
- **[major·prose] abstract** — The abstract says "a finite translated-dyadic contour bank and a total Borel statistic attain matched c/n and C/n mean-squared-error bounds on the non-Gaussian spectral class." This is accurate only under fixed primitive constants, nonempty fixed-code class, i.i.d. sampling, and the displayed current L1(P_X) treatment-code gate, but the sentence is long enough that the fixed-code and gate restrictions are easy to miss.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite the sentence to foreground the exact domain: "Under fixed primitive constants, nonempty fixed-code classes, i.i.d. product sampling, and the current L1(P_X) treatment-code gate, the constructed statistic attains..."
- **[major·prose] Comparison with ACE and Gaussian benchmarks** — The prose says "Under JMS eligibility, the ACE bound retains its finite-order nuisance terms, and the contour bound is governed by the fixed-separation sampling term (gamma n)^{-1/2)." The verified comparison establishes a contour upper bound of that order, but not that the actual contour error distribution is governed by that term in every eligible regime.
  - *Fix:* Change to an upper-bound formulation, e.g. "Under JMS eligibility, the imported ACE upper bound contains the displayed finite-order nuisance terms, while the contour theorem supplies the displayed fixed-separation upper bound of order (gamma n)^{-1/2)."
- **[major·prose] Related work** — The sentence "in that regime the ratio of the contour upper guarantee to the imported finite-order ACE upper guarantee tends to zero on the common clipped-code class" risks being read as a performance dominance result for estimators, although the verified proposition only compares displayed upper guarantees.
  - *Fix:* Keep the upper-bound qualifier at every occurrence and add one clarifying sentence that no lower bound on the ACE estimator's achieved error is asserted by this comparison.
- **[major·statement] Setup and assumptions** — Several code-radius assumptions are stated as "for every n" while the central decision problem and verified classes use current-index restrictions. This creates ambiguity about whether class membership requires a full sequence-wise accuracy path or only the fixed finite-n gate.
  - *Fix:* Separate sequence-level supplied-code assumptions from current-index class gates. In the main law-class definitions and discussion, state explicitly that risk theorems use the current clipped pair at the sample index; reserve "for every n" only for deterministic radius-sequence conventions.
- **[major·structure] Certified construction and executable correspondence** — The represented-data construction occupies a very large share of the paper but remains conditional on a compiled bounded spectral adapter. The manuscript claims a "conditional reproducibility layer" accurately, but the statistical reader is left without a concrete implementation artifact, API, or verification recipe showing how this condition can be checked in practice.
  - *Fix:* Either provide an actual implementation artifact and instructions for compiling/checking the adapter, or move most of the finite-arithmetic specification to an appendix and state in the main text that the computational result is a conditional output-correspondence theorem for implementations satisfying the displayed contract.
- **[minor·structure] global** — House typography is violated by formal titles such as "Common Experiment Dichotomy", "Local Gaussian Benchmarks", and "Zero-Free Nuisance Factor"; section and subsection titles are sentence case under the manuscript contract.
  - *Fix:* Change these titles to sentence case: "Common experiment dichotomy", "Local Gaussian benchmarks", and "Zero-free nuisance factor".
- **[minor·prose] Introduction** — The sentence "The appendix records proof and deferred-detail information for the paper's formal statements" undersells the fact that extensive proofs and construction details are in the appendices and makes the main text feel detached from the argument.
  - *Fix:* Replace with a more informative roadmap sentence identifying which appendices contain analytic proofs, empirical-process bounds, lower bounds, certified construction, and verification scope.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The related-work section is adequate in breadth but sometimes catalogs literatures instead of sharply distinguishing the paper's theorem from the closest ACE and higher-order orthogonality results.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact paragraph after the ACE discussion that states the exact contrast: fixed-code direct L1 treatment-code stability and transform-zero contours versus finite-order ACE nuisance products and eligibility conditions, with both claims restricted to their stated upper guarantees.
- **[minor·prose] Explicit mixture reductions and local benchmarks** — The text says "The last algebraic clause records how an oracle upper bound that is valid under two sets of hypotheses can be summarized by the smaller of the two displayed upper inputs," but no such algebraic clause appears in the displayed theorem statement provided.
  - *Fix:* Delete this sentence or add the corresponding formal statement if it is intended to be part of the theorem.
- **[minor·prose] Verification scope and crosswalk** — The prose says "The theorem statements and proofs corresponding to the displayed formal environments are machine-checked in Lean 4..." This is generally consistent with the contract, but several definitions have no proof component and the ACE result relies on an external published theorem.
  - *Fix:* Revise to: "The displayed formal environments are matched to Lean declarations; theorem and lemma proofs are checked under the stated assumptions, and the ACE comparison uses the theorem-local imported JMS conclusion."
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The abstract uses symbols before or near first use in a dense way: "For gamma in (1/2,1)" is glossed, but r, delta, and sigma appear in the abstract without the same plain-word appositive treatment.
  - *Fix:* Add brief appositives at first abstract use, e.g. "the ACE order r", "the cumulant-separation level delta", and "the Gaussian innovation variance sigma^2", or replace the symbols with words.
- **[nit·prose] Setup and assumptions** — The fold definition uses I_0={i:i<floor(n/2)} and I_1={i:i>=floor(n/2)} without making the indexing convention visually explicit; later text alternates between 0-based and 1-based sample descriptions.
  - *Fix:* State once whether sample indices are 0-based or 1-based in the statistical prose, and make the fold notation consistent with that convention.
- **[nit·prose] global** — The manuscript repeatedly uses very long labels and names such as "compiled bounded spectral adapter satisfying the full canonical build-and-compilation specification" in the main narrative, which obscures the statistical contribution.
  - *Fix:* Introduce a short defined phrase after the full definition, then use that phrase in the main text while keeping the full condition in theorem statements.

## Questions for authors
- Can the authors provide a short numerical or simulation illustration showing how the contour selector behaves for the symmetric mixture and Gaussian--Rademacher path? This is not needed for validity, but it would make the construction much easier to evaluate empirically.
- Is there an actual compiled bounded spectral adapter corresponding to the displayed contract, or is the represented-data result currently only a conditional specification?
- Can the constants in the contour bank be summarized in a reader-facing way, for example by reporting their dependence on the primitive parameters and explaining whether the construction is computationally practical for moderate r and delta?
