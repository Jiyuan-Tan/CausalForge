# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.5/10 — The paper has a novel and formally verified fixed-separation contour construction, but the current manuscript is too opaque and contains several scope, notation, and presentation issues that would block publication as written.

The submission develops a complex-transform contour estimator for partially linear models with cumulant-separated treatment innovations and proves fixed-code root-n/minimax guarantees under explicit stability gates. The contribution is potentially interesting for econometricians working on non-Gaussian identification and higher-order orthogonal learning. The verified formal layer appears to support the main mathematical claims, but the manuscript needs substantial revision to make the contribution understandable, accurately scoped, and conventionally assessable by econometrics readers.

## Strengths
- The core idea of using zeros of the treatment-innovation MGF to build instruments for residualized partially linear estimation is original and mathematically interesting.
- The manuscript states explicit conditions for identification, risk, ACE comparison, Gaussian degeneracy, and finite represented execution.
- The comparison with ACE is mostly framed honestly as an upper-guarantee comparison rather than an estimator dominance result.
- The verification crosswalk is unusually detailed and gives useful confidence that the formal claims match the stated theorem layer.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] Main fixed-separation result** — The central statistical theorem is presented as “Adaptive root-n minimaxity,” but the result is a fixed-separation, fixed-code guarantee under a direct population L1 treatment-code stability gate and a fixed certified contour bank. The title and surrounding language can be read as adaptation over unknown or shrinking non-Gaussianity, which the manuscript itself later treats as future work.
  - *Fix:* Retitle the theorem and nearby prose to something like “Fixed-separation fixed-code root-n minimaxity” and state in the first explanatory paragraph that the adaptation is contour selection within a fixed bank under fixed primitive constants, not adaptation to shrinking cumulant separation or unknown local regimes.
- **[major·statement] Setup and assumptions** — The fold definition is ambiguous and appears inconsistent with the sample notation. The paper writes O_1,...,O_n but defines I_0={i:i<floor(n/2)} and I_1={i:i>=floor(n/2)}; with ordinary 1-based indexing I_0 is empty for n=2, while the theorem later relies on nonempty folds and n>=2.
  - *Fix:* Define the sample index set explicitly as either {0,...,n-1}, matching the formal layer, or rewrite the folds in 1-based notation, for example I_0={1,...,floor(n/2)} and I_1={floor(n/2)+1,...,n}. Then propagate that convention everywhere empirical averages over folds are used.
- **[major·prose] Main fixed-separation result** — The theorem bullets contain the phrase “Non-Gaussian fixed-code u.” and “ACE fixed-code u.” This is not just a typo: it appears in a central formal result and obscures whether the intended label is upper bound, utility, or another condition.
  - *Fix:* Replace these bullet labels with precise labels such as “Non-Gaussian fixed-code risk bound” and “ACE fixed-code risk bound.” Check the rest of the manuscript for similar leftover fragments, including “mean squared u obeys” in the Gaussian--Rademacher benchmark.
- **[major·structure] Main fixed-separation result** — The paper’s main risk statement is hard to interpret because the fixed-code convention, base-model dependence, transported certified records, and ordinary Borel statistic are introduced inside a long theorem rather than as a clean statistical experiment before the theorem.
  - *Fix:* Before the theorem, add a short subsection defining the fixed-code experiment, what the estimator observes, what is fixed across the supremum, and which conditions are statistical versus certified-execution conditions. Then restate the theorem with the statistical risk result separated from the represented-execution clause.
- **[major·structure] Clarity and presentation** — The manuscript is far longer and more formal-mechanical than needed for a journal article. Many paragraphs restate every symbol after each definition, and the certified-computation details dominate the statistical exposition.
  - *Fix:* Move most implementation substrate details, Lean crosswalk material, and line-by-line proof commentary to an online appendix. In the main text, keep the definitions needed to understand the estimator, theorem conditions, and comparison, and provide a concise proof sketch for each major result.
- **[major·prose] Soundness of exposition** — The assumptions motivating the contour stability gate are mathematically stated but not economically or statistically motivated. The main gate can be extremely stringent, as the manuscript’s own mixture calculation gives epsilon_{1,n} about 1.3e-5 under one calibration, yet the implications for practical relevance are not discussed until much later and only indirectly.
  - *Fix:* Add a focused discussion immediately after the main theorem explaining when the L1(P_X) treatment-code gate is plausible, how it compares with ordinary first-stage rates, and what the constants mean for moderate samples. This can remain affirmative by describing the fixed-overlap/fixed-separation operating regime rather than listing absent regimes.
- **[major·prose] Comparison with ACE and Gaussian benchmarks** — The ACE comparison is formally an upper-bound comparison, but some surrounding language risks implying method superiority. For example, “Along sequences where the ACE nuisance terms dominate the sampling term, the displayed contour upper guarantee is asymptotically smaller than the displayed ACE upper guarantee” is correct only as a comparison of displayed bounds, not performance.
  - *Fix:* Wherever this comparison is discussed, repeat “upper guarantee” or “bound” in the subject of the sentence and avoid verbs such as “outperforms,” “dominates,” or “smaller than ACE” unless the object is explicitly the bound. Add one sentence stating that no lower bound for the ACE estimator is claimed.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The related-work brief includes Engle1986, Speckman1988, HardleLiangGao2000, Marcinkiewicz1939, Lukacs1970, Andrews2017, and ShimizuHoyerHyvarinenKerminen2006, but these are absent from the manuscript’s related-work discussion despite being natural anchors for PLM, characteristic functions, completeness/testing, and non-Gaussian causal structure.
  - *Fix:* Add these references where they naturally belong, especially Engle, Speckman, and Hardle--Liang--Gao in the PLM paragraph, Marcinkiewicz and Lukacs in the transform-zero paragraph, Andrews in the observable-zero/testing discussion, and Shimizu et al. in the non-Gaussian causal-identification paragraph.
- **[minor·prose] Global** — Several formal-environment titles violate the house typography rule requiring sentence case. Examples include “Common Experiment Dichotomy,” “Local Gaussian Benchmarks,” and “Zero-Free Nuisance Factor.”
  - *Fix:* Change these to sentence case: “Common experiment dichotomy,” “Local Gaussian benchmarks,” and “Zero-free nuisance factor.” Audit all theorem, lemma, proposition, and section titles for the same rule.
- **[minor·prose] Global** — Some symbols are used before a plain-language first-use gloss. For example, the abstract uses theta_0, P_X, and gamma; the introduction uses eta and Z_n with glosses, but the notation load remains high for an abstract.
  - *Fix:* At first use in the abstract, write phrases such as “the coefficient theta_0,” “the covariate law P_X,” and “the probability level gamma,” or use words instead of symbols until the setup section.
- **[minor·structure] Setup and assumptions** — The assumptions named Treatment code radius and Outcome code radius appear twice with different exponents, first L^s and later L^r, creating avoidable reader confusion.
  - *Fix:* Rename the displayed titles to “L^s treatment code radius,” “L^s outcome code radius,” “JMS L^r treatment code radius,” and “JMS L^r outcome code radius.”
- **[minor·structure] Explicit mixture reductions and local benchmarks** — The statement “The final elementary inequality records how two valid oracle upper inputs combine by taking their minimum” introduces U_DML even though the theorem provides no DML construction or bound in the manuscript.
  - *Fix:* Either remove the final elementary inequality involving U_DML from the theorem or explain it as a purely algebraic placeholder outside the theorem environment. The current theorem should focus on the delivered Gaussian--Rademacher and ACE upper benchmarks.
- **[minor·prose] Verification scope and crosswalk** — The paragraph “The theorem statements and proofs corresponding to the displayed formal environments are machine-checked in Lean 4...” is stale relative to the verification contract for objects with external_dependencies: the ACE alignment uses a published theorem through a citation boundary.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite the paragraph to say that formal environments are checked under their stated assumptions and citation interfaces, and that the ACE-alignment theorem verifies the reduction/application conditional on the cited Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis conclusion, with the theorem-local footnote identifying that boundary.
- **[nit·prose] Appendix A** — In the finite contour bank bookkeeping display, the manuscript writes “nu_star=...” but the surrounding definition and proof use “u_star.” This is likely a typography error and can confuse the dyadic certificate.
  - *Fix:* Replace nu_star by u_star in the bookkeeping display for the finite contour bank.
- **[nit·structure] Global** — The manuscript uses many internal labels and Lean-specific payloads, such as leanref calls and theorem-local code comments, in a way that interrupts reader-facing prose.
  - *Fix:* Keep Lean references in the verification appendix or margin notes, and remove most leanref insertions from the main narrative unless they clarify a symbol that cannot otherwise be tracked.

## Questions for authors
- Can the authors give one or two realistic first-stage scenarios in which the displayed L1(P_X) stability gate is satisfied at useful sample sizes?
- Is the intended sample indexing throughout the paper zero-based, as in the formalization, or one-based, as in the O_1,...,O_n notation?
- Do the authors intend the certified represented execution to be a reproducibility contribution in this paper, or should it be framed as an appendix-level implementation contract supporting the Borel statistic?
