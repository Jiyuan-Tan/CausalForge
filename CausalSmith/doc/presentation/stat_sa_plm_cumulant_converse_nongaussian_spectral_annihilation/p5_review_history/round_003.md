# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.6/10 — The paper contains a substantial verified fixed-separation construction, but as written its scope, computational reproducibility, and several reader-facing claims need tightening before it earns publication.

The submission develops a contour-instrument estimator for partially linear models with cumulant-separated non-Gaussian treatment noise, proving fixed-code \(\Theta(n^{-1})\) MSE bounds and an upper-bound comparison with finite-order ACE under stated conditions. The verified formal layer appears strong and the paper is ambitious, especially in connecting transform-zero geometry, supplied-code stability, and a certified arithmetic interface. The main weaknesses are expository: the central contribution is hard to isolate, the computational artifact is only conditionally described, and several prose passages need sharper conditioning to match the verified claims.

## Strengths
- The main fixed-separation theorem is mathematically substantive: it converts cumulant separation and direct \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code control into a finite-bank contour statistic with matched fixed-code MSE order.
- The paper carefully distinguishes broad spectral, ACE comparison, and bounded-outcome Gaussian diagnostic classes in the formal statements.
- The known-zero, contour-identification, and explicit sine-mixture benchmarks give useful intuition for the transform-zero mechanism.
- The verification crosswalk and theorem-local ACE citation interface are valuable for auditing which claims are proved internally and which use published inputs.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] global** — The manuscript is dominated by formal definitions and certified-construction details before the reader gets a compact view of the econometric object, assumptions, and payoff. As written, it is difficult to tell which parts are the statistical contribution, which parts are comparison bookkeeping, and which parts are conditional executable correspondence.
  - *Fix:* Add an early roadmap table or subsection summarizing each main result with columns for law class, supplied-code condition, separation regime, statistic, risk criterion, and computational status. Move low-level arithmetic details out of the main narrative and keep the main text focused on the statistical theorem and its conditions.
- **[major·other] Appendix D: verification scope and crosswalk** — The paper claims Lean verification and a represented-data execution interface, but the manuscript does not give a reader enough reproducibility information to inspect the formal artifact or determine whether a compiled bounded spectral adapter actually exists for the submitted version.
  - *Fix:* Add a reproducibility paragraph giving the public repository or archive, exact commit, Lean/toolchain versions, build command, names of the top-level declarations, and the status of any compiled bounded spectral adapter. If the adapter is only a conditional interface, state affirmatively that the paper establishes conditional executable correspondence for adapters satisfying the displayed build specification.
- **[major·prose] Related work** — The sentence "That is why an innovation carrying a separated nonzero higher cumulant cannot have a zero-free transform" overstates and misattributes the zero conclusion. The verified result is the paper's sub-Gaussian fixed-cumulant zero-localization lemma, not a blanket consequence of Marcinkiewicz's theorem in the form stated.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite the passage to say that under the paper's centered sub-Gaussian envelope and fixed cumulant-separation condition, \cref{obj:lem:zero-localization} gives an explicit disk containing an MGF zero; Marcinkiewicz should be presented only as background motivation for the Gaussian/non-Gaussian transform distinction.
- **[major·statement] Main fixed-separation result** — The theorem title "Adaptive root-n minimaxity" risks suggesting adaptation across nuisance, ACE, DML, or local-to-Gaussian regimes, while the delivered result is a fixed-separation, fixed-code finite-contour-bank guarantee under fixed primitive constants and a current \(L^1(P_X)\) radius gate.
  - *Fix:* Retitle the theorem and surrounding prose to emphasize fixed-separation contour minimaxity. If the authors retain "adaptive," explicitly tie it only to finite contour selection within the fixed bank and not to adaptive switching or shrinking-separation inference.
- **[major·prose] Comparison with ACE and Gaussian benchmarks** — The Gaussian comparison is mathematically correct but could be read as a substantive comparison with Gaussian PLM estimation rather than a diagnostic for a simultaneous bounded-outcome Gaussian class. The surrounding prose should prevent readers from interpreting the zero minimax risk as a general property of Gaussian treatment residual settings.
  - *Fix:* At the start of the Gaussian benchmark discussion, state affirmatively that the proposition characterizes the intersection of exact PLM conditional mean, bounded observed outcome, and nondegenerate Gaussian treatment innovation, and that the decision-theoretic zero risk concerns that nonempty fixed-code intersection.
- **[minor·prose] Setup and assumptions** — The sentence "no procedure for estimating \(g_0\) from the sample is modelled" violates the affirmative contribution-framing rule outside a Limitations/Future Work/Open Questions section.
  - *Fix:* Replace it with an affirmative scope statement such as: "All risk statements condition on deterministic supplied clipped treatment codes and evaluate fixed-code performance at the current sample index."
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The phrase "The same statistic has the corresponding generalized-quantile upper bound" is hard to parse because the paper uses a lower \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile with \(\gamma>1/2\), so the controlled quantile level is below one half.
  - *Fix:* In the abstract and first theorem discussion, write explicitly that the statistic controls the generalized lower \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile of absolute error by order \((\gamma n)^{-1/2}\), with \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\).
- **[minor·structure] Introduction** — The introduction states many theorem consequences before giving a compact explanation of the estimator's operational form, so readers encounter contour-bank, Borel statistic, represented execution, and ACE alignment terminology before seeing the basic sample split and contour-selection logic.
  - *Fix:* Add a short "Estimator in words" paragraph before the theorem tour: pilot fold selects a bank circle using empirical residual transform modulus and winding count; evaluation fold computes the normalized contour ratio; clipping makes the statistic total.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The non-Gaussian identification discussion would be fairer with an explicit citation to non-Gaussian linear causal discovery, which is present in the authors' related-work context but absent from the manuscript's related-work paragraph.
  - *Fix:* Add \citet{ShimizuHoyerHyvarinenKerminen2006} or an equivalent reference when discussing ICA, non-Gaussianity, and causal/structural identification.
- **[nit·prose] global** — Several explanatory sentences use negative contrast outside the limitations section, for example "rather than a quantity tied to a single prespecified zero." These are not mathematical negations, but they conflict with the requested affirmative prose style.
  - *Fix:* Replace such contrasts with direct positive descriptions, e.g. "The normalization averages over the enclosed zero structure."

## Questions for authors
- Is there an actual compiled bounded spectral adapter accompanying the paper, or is the executable correspondence entirely conditional on future adapters satisfying the displayed substrate?
- Can the authors provide the Lean repository, exact commit, and build instructions corresponding to the verification scope stated in Appendix D?
- In the intended econometric use case, are the supplied treatment and outcome codes external fixed inputs, cross-fitted first stages treated conditionally, or outputs of a separate learning procedure outside this paper's statistical experiment?
