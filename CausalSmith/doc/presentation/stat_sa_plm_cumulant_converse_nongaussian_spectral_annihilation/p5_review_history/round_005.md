# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.2/10 — The verified fixed-separation contour construction is novel and potentially important, but the manuscript needs substantial revision to make the econometric contribution, assumptions, comparisons, and computability claims intelligible and publication-ready.

The paper develops a contour-instrument estimator for partially linear models with independent cumulant-separated treatment innovations and proves fixed-code parametric MSE and generalized-quantile guarantees under direct treatment-code control. The formal layer appears closely aligned with the main claims, and the ACE comparison is mostly framed as an upper-guarantee comparison. The submission is nevertheless difficult to evaluate as an econometrics paper because the exposition is dominated by verification and implementation machinery, several contribution claims are broader than the operational result, and the assumptions and practical payoff need sharper motivation.

## Strengths
- The core idea of using MGF zeros and contour ratios to handle treatment-code residual shifts is original and mathematically interesting.
- The main statistical theorem states a clear fixed-separation fixed-code MSE characterization and an accompanying quantile guarantee.
- The comparison with Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis ACE states the imported theorem-local scope and largely keeps the comparison at the level of displayed upper guarantees.
- The paper gives explicit mixture benchmarks that make the contour mechanism more concrete than the abstract bank construction.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] abstract** — The abstract compresses many conditional and class-specific statements into a broad publication-facing contribution. In particular, the sentence "Under fixed cumulant separation \(\delta\), the cumulant-separation level, and sufficiently accurate direct \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code control, where \(P_X\) is the covariate marginal, a finite translated-dyadic contour bank and a total Borel statistic attain matched \(c/n\) and \(C/n\) mean-squared-error bounds on the fixed-code non-Gaussian spectral class" omits the i.i.d. product-law, nonempty fixed-code class, fixed primitive records, bounded ranges, sub-Gaussian tails, independent treatment noise, and small-radius gate that make the theorem operational.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite the abstract result sentence to state the exact class and gates in one compact form, for example: "For fixed primitive constants, i.i.d. sampling, independent sub-Gaussian cumulant-separated treatment innovations, bounded coefficient/regression/noise envelopes, a nonempty fixed-code class, and the displayed current \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code radius gate, the constructed total Borel statistic attains matching \(c/n\) and \(C/n\) fixed-code MSE bounds."
- **[major·prose] introduction** — The roadmap table and surrounding text call the estimator an "Ordinary sample statistic; conditional represented execution" and the computation layer a "certified-computation layer," but the verified statements give represented execution only conditional on a compiled bounded spectral adapter satisfying a displayed build specification. This can read as an implemented or independently reproducible computational claim rather than a conditional correspondence theorem.
  - *Fix:* Separate the statistical estimator from the represented-data correspondence in the roadmap. State that the paper defines an ordinary Borel statistic and proves a conditional output-correspondence theorem for any compiled adapter satisfying the specified finite-arithmetic contract; add a short note that the manuscript supplies the specification, not an empirical software validation.
- **[major·statement] main fixed-separation result** — The phrase "adaptive root-n minimaxity" overstates the delivered adaptation. The theorem is adaptive over a finite contour bank under fixed primitive constants and a current treatment-code gate; it is not adaptive over shrinking cumulant separation, estimator families, unknown local regimes, or inference procedures.
  - *Fix:* Rename the theorem and surrounding prose to emphasize fixed-separation finite-bank selection, for example "Fixed-separation contour minimaxity" or "Finite-bank fixed-code root-n guarantee." Reserve "adaptive" for the bank-selection mechanism and explicitly tie it to fixed \(\delta\) and fixed primitive records.
- **[major·prose] related work** — The paper states that the contour guarantee is "informative exactly along the sequences satisfying the nuisance-dominance condition" but gives limited econometric interpretation of when the required direct \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code gate is plausible relative to ACE's \(L^r\) treatment and outcome radii. The closest competitor comparison is algebraically present but not yet translated into conditions a PLM reader can assess.
  - *Fix:* Add a dedicated paragraph after the ACE bound explaining the relation between the direct \(L^1(P_X)\) gate and the JMS code-radius/eligibility conditions, including examples of nuisance-rate regimes where the displayed separation applies and regimes where the ACE and contour upper bounds are both sampling dominated.
- **[major·structure] setup and assumptions** — The supplied-code setup is central but under-motivated. The paper assumes deterministic fixed supplied codes and evaluates fixed-code risks, while many readers will expect data-trained nuisance estimates, cross-fitting, or sample-dependent learners in partially linear models.
  - *Fix:* Add an early subsection explaining the decision problem: supplied codes are deterministic inputs at the current sample index; risks condition on the current clipped code pair; the theorem applies after any external training procedure only through a deterministic fixed-code interpretation or conditioning argument that the paper explicitly states.
- **[major·prose] comparison with ACE and Gaussian benchmarks** — The sentence "Along sequences where the ACE nuisance terms dominate the sampling term, the displayed spectral upper bound is asymptotically smaller than the displayed ACE upper bound" is faithful to the proposition as an upper-bound ratio, but it can be misread as estimator dominance or minimax dominance over ACE procedures.
  - *Fix:* Revise the sentence to: "Along the displayed nuisance-dominance sequences, the contour upper guarantee is asymptotically smaller than the imported finite-order ACE upper guarantee; this is a comparison of available guarantees on the common clipped-code class." Repeat this qualification near the proposition, not only once in the introduction.
- **[major·structure] global** — The manuscript is much longer and more formal-mechanical than a typical econometrics submission, with proof details, certified arithmetic, and Lean crosswalk material interrupting the statistical narrative. This makes it difficult to locate the economic model, the identifying variation, and the main comparison.
  - *Fix:* Move the certified arithmetic substrate, executable correspondence, verification crosswalk, and most proof-level constants to an online appendix. Keep in the main text a concise construction, the main assumptions, the identification theorem, the fixed-separation theorem, and the ACE/Gaussian comparison.
- **[minor·prose] introduction** — The sentence "The Gaussian comparison has a different role" is followed by a degeneracy result, but the manuscript should make clearer that this is a diagnostic for a simultaneous bounded-outcome Gaussian assumption set rather than evidence about Gaussian PLM difficulty more broadly.
  - *Fix:* Revise the paragraph to state affirmatively that the proposition characterizes the bounded-outcome Gaussian intersection and gives zero risks on that intersection; avoid wording that invites comparison with unconstrained Gaussian PLM models.
- **[minor·statement] explicit mixture reductions and local benchmarks** — The title "Local Gaussian Benchmarks" is broad relative to the verified theorem, which gives a Gaussian--Rademacher sine upper benchmark and a conditional ACE oracle-envelope specialization. It does not establish a unified local Gaussian theory.
  - *Fix:* Retitle the theorem to "Partial local-to-Gaussian upper benchmarks" and adjust the first paragraph of the section to emphasize the two procedure-specific benchmark envelopes.
- **[minor·structure] appendices** — Several appendix proofs and construction definitions repeat reader-facing claims already stated in the main text, which increases the chance of stale prose if the formal declarations change.
  - *Fix:* Convert repeated prose summaries in appendices into brief pointers to the formal environments, and keep only proof-specific intuition or implementation-specific details that are not duplicated elsewhere.
- **[minor·prose] global** — Section titles mostly follow sentence case, but theorem titles such as "Common Experiment Dichotomy" and "Local Gaussian Benchmarks" use title case inside formal displays, conflicting with the stated house typography.
  - *Fix:* Change formal title arguments to sentence case, e.g. "Common experiment dichotomy" and "Local Gaussian benchmarks" or the more scoped titles suggested above.
- **[nit·prose] appendix C** — The algorithmic prose says "The integrand \(V_j\) is NOT normalized at this stage". The all-caps negative phrasing is visually jarring and unnecessary.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite affirmatively: "The normalization by \(N_j\) is applied after the mesh enclosure is computed."

## Questions for authors
- Can the authors give a concise econometric example where the direct current \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code gate is natural and easier to satisfy than the ACE nuisance conditions?
- Is there an actual compiled bounded spectral adapter corresponding to the certified arithmetic specification, or is the computation result intended solely as a conditional realizability statement?
- How should readers choose the supplied treatment code in applications, and what conditioning argument transfers a sample-trained nuisance estimate into the fixed-code theorem?
