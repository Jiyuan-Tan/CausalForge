# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.2/10 — The verified fixed-separation contour result is novel and potentially important, but the manuscript needs substantial tightening of scope, exposition, positioning, and practical interpretation before it would read as a publishable econometrics paper.

The paper develops a complex-contour estimator for a partially linear coefficient under independent cumulant-separated treatment noise and fixed supplied treatment codes, with Lean-verified identification, fixed-code minimax MSE, ACE upper-guarantee alignment, and mixture benchmarks. The formal core appears faithful to the verification contract, and the contribution is real: transform zeros are used in a new way for residualized PLM identification and estimation. As written, however, the paper is much more a verified theorem dossier than a journal article, and several prose choices blur the fixed-code, fixed-separation, conditional-computation scope that makes the results true.

## Strengths
- The central contour-identification idea is original and technically interesting in the PLM/non-Gaussian residual setting.
- The main fixed-separation theorem gives a clean fixed-code \(n^{-1}\) MSE characterization under explicit stability gates.
- The ACE comparison is mostly careful about being an upper-guarantee comparison rather than a dominance theorem for estimators.
- The Gaussian and mixture calculations help locate the target map and give interpretable special cases.
- The verification crosswalk and theorem-local citation scope make the formal trust boundary unusually explicit.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] Main fixed-separation result** — The discussion imports ordinary first-stage language that is not delivered by the fixed-code theorem: "Since \(L^2(P_X)\) control implies \(L^1(P_X)\) control on a probability space, ordinary first-stage rates can feed this gate once their constants place the current sample in the fixed-separation operating regime." The verified theorem conditions on deterministic supplied codes and does not analyze random first-stage training, sample splitting for nuisance learning, or conditioning/unconditioning over a training sample.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite this passage to state the delivered condition affirmatively: the theorem applies to any deterministic current clipped treatment code whose population \(L^1(P_X)\) error satisfies the displayed gate. If the authors want to discuss learned first stages, add a separate theorem or corollary that conditions on or integrates over the training procedure and states the required high-probability or in-expectation code event.
- **[major·prose] global** — The paper repeatedly calls \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) a "learned residual" and refers to "learned residuals" in the roadmap. In the formal results, \(\bar g_n\) is a deterministic supplied current-index clipped code and the risk is fixed-code; no learning procedure for \(\widehat g_n\) is modeled.
  - *Fix:* Define the phrase once as shorthand for a residual formed with a supplied deterministic code, or replace it throughout with "code residual" or "current clipped-code residual." In the introduction and theorem discussions, foreground the fixed-code convention whenever risk claims are stated.
- **[major·statement] Main fixed-separation result** — The theorem title and surrounding prose use "Adaptive root-n minimaxity." Although the text later narrows "adaptive" to contour selection from a fixed certified bank, the natural econometric reading is adaptation over smoothness, nuisance rates, or local non-Gaussianity, none of which is established.
  - *Fix:* Rename the theorem to something like "Fixed-bank root-n minimaxity under fixed cumulant separation" and reserve "adaptive" only for the finite bank-selection step, with the fixed primitive constants and direct \(L^1(P_X)\) gate stated in the same sentence as the rate claim.
- **[major·citation] Related work** — The related-work section is directionally adequate but omits several foundational and closely relevant references identified by the manuscript's own context: Engle, Speckman, Hardle-Liang-Gao for PLM; Marcinkiewicz and Lukacs for transform/cumulant foundations; Shimizu-Hoyer-Hyvarinen-Kerminen and Andrews for non-Gaussian/observable-zero links. This weakens novelty calibration for a leading econometrics audience.
  - *Fix:* Add a concise early paragraph covering the PLM origins and monograph treatment, and expand the transform/non-Gaussian paragraphs to include the missing foundational and closest-context references, explaining how the present contour-zero PLM construction differs from them.
- **[major·structure] global** — The manuscript is written as a formal-verification artifact rather than a referee-readable econometrics article. Long proof bodies include Lean trace comments such as "% lean: ...", extensive implementation details, and repeated formal bookkeeping in the main paper. This makes the main economic/statistical argument hard to evaluate despite the verified core.
  - *Fix:* Move Lean trace comments and most finite-arithmetic implementation details to a verification or reproducibility appendix/supplement. In the main text, keep theorem statements, assumptions, intuition, and short proof sketches that explain why cumulant separation, \(L^1(P_X)\) stability, and finite-bank concentration imply the rate.
- **[major·prose] Setup and assumptions** — The generalized lower quantile \(Q_{P,1-\gamma}\) is defined with \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), so the controlled quantile level \(1-\gamma\) is below one half. Several passages call \(\gamma\) a "probability level" without reminding readers that the result is not a high-confidence upper error bound in the usual sense.
  - *Fix:* Where the quantile bound is first stated and again in the ACE comparison, explicitly say that the result controls the lower \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile as defined in \(\cref{obj:def:generalized-quantile}\). Avoid language that could be read as a \(1-\gamma\) coverage or high-probability guarantee unless a separate upper-tail statement is proved.
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The author footnote says "Machine-generated and Lean-verified by the CausalSmith research pipeline." Read literally, this can be taken to certify the entire article, including prose interpretation and the external JMS theorem, whereas the verification contract verifies formal statements and reductions, with one cited theorem imported through a theorem-local scope note.
  - *Fix:* Revise the footnote to say that the displayed formal statements and proofs are Lean-verified under the stated citation interface, and that the ACE comparison uses the cited JMS theorem as an external published input.
- **[minor·prose] Comparison with ACE and Gaussian benchmarks** — The sentence "This comparison is most informative when the ACE nuisance terms dominate the sampling term" is plausible but qualitative. The formal separation is only an upper-guarantee ratio under the displayed sequence condition, not a general performance comparison.
  - *Fix:* Tie the sentence directly to the proposition: "The displayed upper-guarantee ratio is informative along sequences satisfying the nuisance-dominance condition in \(\cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment}\)."
- **[minor·other] Main fixed-separation result** — The paper includes a numerical gate example with \(R_1\approx222.7\) and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\approx1.3\times10^{-5}\), but gives no simulation or numerical demonstration that the contour selector behaves reasonably at such scales. This leaves the econometric usefulness of the certified constants unclear.
  - *Fix:* Add a small simulation or numerical benchmark for the symmetric mixture and Gaussian--Rademacher path showing denominator behavior, contour selection, and MSE against the sine/ACE/DML benchmarks, or clearly label the constants as certification-scale constants rather than practical tuning advice.
- **[minor·prose] Main fixed-separation result** — Two theorem bullets are labeled "Non-Gaussian fixed-code u." and "ACE fixed-code u.", and the Gaussian--Rademacher lemma says "Its mean squared u obeys". These appear to be corrupted words and undermine polish in key formal statements.
  - *Fix:* Replace the corrupted "u" occurrences with the intended term, probably "risk" or "upper bound," throughout the theorem and lemma statements.
- **[nit·structure] appendices** — Several proof paragraphs repeat definitions and assumptions verbatim immediately after the formal statement, making the appendix much longer than necessary for readers who are not auditing Lean correspondence.
  - *Fix:* Compress repetitive proof openings and keep the verification crosswalk as the place where formal-object alignment is documented.

## Questions for authors
- Can the authors state a clear conditional-on-training corollary for random first-stage treatment codes, or is the intended econometric interpretation strictly fixed-code throughout?
- Is there an implemented compiled bounded spectral adapter corresponding to the certified substrate, or is the construction currently a specification only?
- How sensitive is the contour estimator in finite samples to the conservative boundary certificate \(a_\star\) and the very small \(L^1(P_X)\) gate shown in the mixture calibration?
