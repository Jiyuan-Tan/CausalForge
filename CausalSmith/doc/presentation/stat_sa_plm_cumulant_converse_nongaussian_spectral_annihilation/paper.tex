\begin{abstract}
This paper studies estimation of the partially linear coefficient \(\theta_0\), the treatment coefficient, when the treatment innovation is independent of covariates, sub-Gaussian, and separated from Gaussianity by a fixed nonzero cumulant. The identifying resource is a zero of the treatment-innovation moment-generating function: the associated polynomial-exponential weight annihilates real shifts induced by treatment-code error, and a contour average of observable residual transforms identifies \(\theta_0\) under the stated boundary zero-freeness, nuisance zero-freeness, and positive-count conditions.

The statistical theorem is a fixed-code result. For fixed primitive constants, i.i.d. product sampling, the partially linear conditional-mean restrictions, bounded coefficient and regression ranges, sub-Gaussian treatment and outcome-noise envelopes, a nonempty fixed-code class, and the displayed current \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code radius gate, where \(P_X\) is the covariate marginal, a finite translated-dyadic contour bank and a total Borel statistic attain matched \(c/n\) and \(C/n\) mean-squared-error bounds on the non-Gaussian spectral class. For \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), where \(\gamma\) is the probability level, the same statistic controls the generalized lower \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile of absolute error by order \((\gamma n)^{-1/2}\).

The same fixed-separation rate holds on the aligned Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis ACE comparison class. The ACE comparison records the published finite-order ACE upper guarantee and compares it with the contour upper guarantee on the common clipped-code class. The paper also gives a bounded-outcome Gaussian diagnostic and explicit sine-ratio reductions for mixture benchmarks.

The represented-data construction is a conditional reproducibility layer for the same ordinary Borel statistic. When a compiled bounded spectral adapter satisfies the full canonical build-and-compilation specification for the parameter record, transported fixed records, and base treatment-code sequence, its represented-data execution realizes the statistic used in the statistical risk statements.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Partially linear models are a standard way to separate a low-dimensional structural coefficient from flexible covariate regressions. In the model studied here, the observed data are triples \(O=(X,T,Y)\), the treatment regression is \(g_0(X)=E[T\mid X]\), the outcome regression is \(q_0(X)=E[Y\mid X]\), and the target is the partially linear coefficient \(\theta_0\) in the conditional mean
\[
E[Y\mid X,T]=q_0(X)+\theta_0\{T-g_0(X)\}.
\]
Classical semiparametric theory and modern double/debiased machine learning estimate this coefficient by orthogonalizing treatment and outcome variation around nuisance regressions \citep{Robinson1988,Newey1990,Newey1994,NeweyMcFadden1994,BickelKlaassenRitovWellner1993,VanDerVaart1998,VanDerVaartWellner1996,BelloniChernozhukovHansen2014,ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML}. Higher-order orthogonality and ACE methods refine this idea by exploiting additional structure in nuisance errors and treatment residuals \citep{RobinsLiTchetgenVanDerVaart2008,LiuMukherjeeNeweyRobins2017,NeweyRobins2018,LiuMukherjeeRobinsTchetgen2021,MackeySyrgkanisZadik2018,JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. This paper develops a complementary fixed-separation construction based on non-Gaussian treatment innovations.

The statistical decision problem fixes the supplied clipped treatment and outcome codes at the current sample size. The risk bounds compare laws compatible with that same code pair; if the codes are obtained from an external training stage, the theorem applies after conditioning on the training output. The finite-arithmetic material supplies a conditional reproducibility clause for represented-data executions that satisfy the displayed build contract.

Four statements organize the paper. First, the contour identification theorem recovers \(\theta_0\) from an observable population contour ratio under the displayed zero-free and positive-count conditions. Second, under the theorem's fixed-code nonemptiness, sampling, and radius conditions, the fixed-code minimax theorem gives matched \(c/n\) and \(C/n\) mean-squared-error bounds on the broad non-Gaussian spectral class and the aligned Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis ACE class. Under the same fixed-code hypotheses, the same contour statistic also has a generalized lower \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile upper bound of order \((\gamma n)^{-1/2}\). Third, the ACE alignment proposition compares the published finite-order ACE upper guarantee with the contour upper guarantee on the common clipped-code class. Fourth, the bounded-outcome Gaussian diagnostic characterizes the comparison class by its implied target and fixed-code risks. Here \(P_X\) denotes the covariate marginal, \(r\) the ACE order, \(\delta\) the cumulant-separation level, \(\gamma\) the probability level, and \(\sigma^2\) the Gaussian innovation variance.

The identifying resource is a zero of the treatment-innovation moment-generating function. Let \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\) denote the treatment innovation and let \(M(z)=E[e^{z\eta}]\). When \(M\) has a zero \(z_0\) of multiplicity \(\ell\), the polynomial-exponential weight \(J_{z_0,\ell}(w)=w^{\ell-1}e^{z_0w}\) annihilates every shifted innovation \(\eta+d\). This shift invariance is useful for code residuals: if \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)=\eta+\{g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\}\), where \(\bar g_n\) is the clipped supplied treatment code, then the same zero generates conditional moments for \(Z_n\). \Cref{obj:thm:known-zero-instrument} gives the resulting known-zero ratio, and \cref{obj:lem:observable-factorization,obj:thm:exact-contour-identification} turn the ratio into an observable contour identity.

The contour identity is the paper's population mechanism. The residual transform \(F_n(z)=E[e^{zZ_n}]\) factors as \(M(z)H_n(z)\), where \(H_n(z)=E[e^{z\{g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\}}]\) is the nuisance transform. The outcome-weighted transform \(G_n(z)=E[Ye^{zZ_n}]\) decomposes into a derivative term carrying \(\theta_0\) and an analytic contamination term. On any circle where \(F_n\) is zero-free on the boundary, \(H_n\) is zero-free on the closed disk, and the enclosed zero count is positive, the normalized contour integral of \(G_n/F_n\) equals \(\theta_0\). This places the argument in the transform-based identification tradition \citep{FeuervergerMcDunnough1981,Singleton2001,ChackoViceira2003,CarrascoFlorensRenault2007,Carrasco2017,KaganLinnikRao1973,Mattner1992,DHaultfoeuille2011,DarollesFanFlorensRenault2011,HuShiu2022}.

\section{Related work}

The partially linear model originates in semiparametric regression, where a parametric coefficient is estimated alongside a nonparametric function of covariates \citep{Engle1986,Speckman1988}; \citet{Robinson1988} gave the root-\(n\) consistent residualized estimator that remains the reference construction, \citet{HardleMammen1993} developed the associated specification testing, and \citet{HardleLiangGao2000} give the monograph treatment. Partially linear models are a central workhorse for semiparametric estimation of low-dimensional structural coefficients in the presence of high-dimensional or otherwise flexible nuisance functions. The classical formulation and efficiency theory connect the coefficient \(\theta_0\), the partially linear coefficient, to residualized treatment variation after removing the treatment regression and outcome regression \citep{Hansen1982,Newey1990,Newey1994,NeweyMcFadden1994,BickelKlaassenRitovWellner1993,VanDerVaart1998,VanDerVaartWellner1996}. Modern double/debiased machine learning builds on the same orthogonalization principle by allowing flexible first-stage estimates while preserving first-order stability of the target moment \citep{BelloniChernozhukovHansen2014,ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML}. Within that tradition, the present paper focuses on a partially linear coefficient estimated from supplied treatment and outcome codes, and studies the additional identifying information carried by non-Gaussian treatment innovations.

A closely related line of work studies higher-order orthogonality and influence-function expansions for nuisance-robust semiparametric estimation. Higher-order constructions refine the first-order orthogonal-score idea by exploiting additional smoothness or distributional structure in nuisance errors \citep{RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,VanDerLaanRubin2006,RobinsLiTchetgenVanDerVaart2008,LiuMukherjeeNeweyRobins2017,NeweyRobins2018,LiuMukherjeeRobinsTchetgen2021,ChernozhukovEscancianoIchimuraNeweyRobins2022}. For the partially linear model, \citet{MackeySyrgkanisZadik2018} identify the role of non-Gaussian treatment residuals in higher-order orthogonal learning. \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} give the closest finite-order comparison point: their published order-\(r\) ACE guarantee, with ACE order \(r\), controls the \(1-\gamma\) error quantile, where \(\gamma\) is the probability level, by a constant multiple of
\[
\varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
+
C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
+
(\gamma n)^{-1/2},
\]
where \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\) are the treatment- and outcome-code radii, \(C_{\theta}\) is the coefficient-range bound, and \(n\) is the sample size. The explicit prefactor \(r!\,16^r\delta^{-1}\), with \(\delta\) denoting the cumulant-separation level, makes the comparison sensitive to the finite order and to the amount of non-Gaussian separation.

The direct \(L^1(P_X)\) gate in the contour theorem plays a different operational role from the JMS pair of \(L^r(P_X)\) treatment and outcome radii. The contour gate controls the treatment-code perturbation of the residual transform and preserves the zero geometry; the ACE eligibility quantities in \cref{obj:def:jms-eligibility-quantities,obj:def:jms-eligibility} also use the outcome-code radius and the order-\(r\) finite-difference scale. On bounded covariate-regression ranges, an \(L^r(P_X)\) treatment radius at level \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\) gives the corresponding \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment control, so the common ACE class lies inside the contour class through \cref{obj:lem:jms-ace-class-relations}. The contour result therefore asks the treatment code to meet the displayed small-radius gate, while the published ACE guarantee asks the treatment and outcome radii to meet the JMS eligibility inequalities.

This translation gives concrete nuisance-rate regimes for the comparison in \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment}. If \(\varepsilon_{1,n}=n^{-a}\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}=n^{-b}\), the ACE nuisance part dominates sampling when \(\min\{ra+b,(r+1)a\}<1/2\), subject to the JMS eligibility and contour small-radius gates; in that regime the ratio of the contour upper guarantee to the imported finite-order ACE upper guarantee tends to zero on the common clipped-code class. If \(\min\{ra+b,(r+1)a\}\ge1/2\), the ACE upper guarantee and the contour upper guarantee are both governed by the sampling term up to constants and eligibility factors. These comparisons concern the available upper bounds on the aligned class.

The contour approach developed here represents the same source of identifying variation through zeros of the treatment-innovation moment-generating function and converts those zeros into instruments and contour ratios. Transform methods have a long history in econometrics and statistics, including characteristic-function estimation, empirical transform methods, and moment-generating-function arguments \citep{FeuervergerMcDunnough1981,Singleton2001,ChackoViceira2003,CarrascoFlorensRenault2007,Carrasco2017}. The classical foundation for reading distributional structure off a transform is Marcinkiewicz's theorem, that a characteristic function of the form \(\exp(P)\) with \(P\) a polynomial forces \(\deg P\le2\) and hence a Gaussian law \citep{Marcinkiewicz1939}. In this paper's centered sub-Gaussian envelope, the fixed cumulant-separation condition yields the explicit zero-localization conclusion in \cref{obj:lem:zero-localization}: the treatment-innovation MGF has a zero inside the displayed disk. The zero-based step also connects to classical results on distributions, transforms, and completeness \citep{KaganLinnikRao1973,Mattner1992,DHaultfoeuille2011,DarollesFanFlorensRenault2011,HuShiu2022}. In the present setting, those tools are applied to residualized treatment variation formed with a supplied treatment code, so the analytic question becomes stability of transform zeros under \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code perturbations.

Non-Gaussianity is also a recurring identifying resource in independent component analysis, structural shock recovery, and related econometric models \citep{Comon1994,HyvarinenOja1997,HyvarinenKarhunenOja2001,LanneMeitzSaikkonen2017,LeeMesters2024,HoeschLeeMesters2024,ReizingerEtAl2025}. Relatedly, \citet{Andrews2017} exhibits families that are \(L^2\)-complete or boundedly complete, the property that makes a vanishing transform functional informative under the relevant class. The construction here uses non-Gaussianity through localized transform-zero geometry: it localizes a transform zero and integrates the observable ratio on a selected circle around the enclosed zero structure. The paper applies that resource in a partially linear estimation problem: cumulant separation supplies a transform zero, and the contour construction converts the resulting analytic geometry into a coefficient estimator. This places the contribution between higher-order semiparametric learning and non-Gaussian identification, with the comparison to ACE made on the common cumulant-separated class where both sets of assumptions are imposed.

The fixed-separation statistical statement is expressed in minimax terms. Under fixed cumulant separation and stable supplied treatment codes, the contour estimator attains the parametric \(n^{-1}\) mean-squared-error order over the non-Gaussian spectral class, while the comparison with \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} records the published ACE generalized-quantile upper guarantee on the aligned ACE class. The sequence comparison in \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment} is an upper-guarantee comparison on the common clipped-code class: when the displayed ACE nuisance terms dominate \(n^{-1/2}\), the ratio of the fixed-separation contour upper guarantee to the imported finite-order ACE upper guarantee tends to zero.

Finally, the finite-construction component draws on computable analysis and interval arithmetic. The relevant background treats real-number computation, constructive numerical representations, and certified interval enclosures \citep{Weihrauch2000,Ko1991,Moore1966}. Those tools provide the arithmetic substrate for the contour bank and represented-data statistic, while the statistical exposition keeps the identification and risk arguments in standard econometric form.

The basic experiment is a partially linear model with deterministic supplied codes for the treatment and outcome regressions. The first definition records the numerical constants, the split convention, and the exact sub-Gaussian norm convention used throughout.

\subsection{Fixed-code decision problem}

The supplied treatment and outcome codes are inputs to the current finite-\(n\) decision problem. At sample size \(n\), the estimator uses the clipped pair \((\bar g_n,\bar q_n)\), and the risks below compare laws whose current clipped code pair agrees with that same input. The data used in the risk bound are then the i.i.d. product sample from the law under evaluation, with the code pair treated as fixed for that experiment.

This convention is the one used by the minimax criteria in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risks} and by the fixed-code classes in \cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}. When the supplied codes come from an external training stage, the theorem applies through the deterministic code pair obtained after conditioning on that training output, or through any deterministic code sequence fixed before the product-law sample is drawn. The theorem's code-radius assumptions are assumptions about that current clipped pair under the covariate marginal \(P_X\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:model-parameters}[Parameter record and standing conventions]
A parameter record fixes a sample size \(n\ge 1\), an order pair \(r,k\in\mathbb N\) with
\[
 k=r+1,\qquad r\ge 2,
\]
a comparison exponent \(s\in[0,\infty]\) with \(s\ge r\), a probability level \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), positive constants
\[
 C_{\theta},\ C_g,\ C_q,\ \psi_{\eta},\ \psi_{\xi},\ \delta,\ \sigma>0,
\]
and two code-radius sequences \(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\) that are nonnegative and nonincreasing on positive indices; their values at \(n\) are written \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\).

Two conventions are used throughout: \(N(m,v)\) denotes the Gaussian law on \(\mathbb R\) with mean \(m\) and variance \(v\), and \(\mathbb P_n f=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(O_i)\) denotes the empirical average of \(f\) over the sample.  The sample is split into the two deterministic folds
\[
 I_0=\{i:i<\lfloor n/2\rfloor\},\qquad I_1=\{i:i\ge\lfloor n/2\rfloor\},
\]
written \(I_a\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\).

For a real random variable \(W\) and \(t>0\), the Luxemburg sub-Gaussian convention is that \(\lVert W\rVert_{\psi_2}\le t\) means \(E[\exp(W^2/t^2)]\le 2\), and its conditional form \(\lVert W\rVert_{\psi_2\mid X}\le t\) means \(E[\exp(W^2/t^2)\mid X]\le 2\) almost surely.
\end{definitionv}

The observation-level model follows the usual partially linear decomposition, with \(g_0\) and \(q_0\) denoting the treatment and outcome regressions and with supplied code sequences clipped to the fixed ranges in \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}.

\begin{definitionv}{def:plm-model}[Observed-data partially linear model]
An observation is the triple \(O=(X,T,Y)\), where \(X\) takes values in a measurable covariate space \(\mathcal X\) and \(T,Y\in\mathbb R\).  A model over a parameter record consists of a probability law \(P\) for \(O\) under which \(T\) and \(Y\) are integrable, a real coefficient \(\theta_0\), measurable regression functions \(g_0,q_0\) on the covariate space with
\[
 g_0(X)=E_P[T\mid X],\qquad q_0(X)=E_P[Y\mid X]
\]
\(P\)-almost surely, and deterministic measurable supplied code sequences \(\widehat g=(\widehat g_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}\) and \(\widehat q=(\widehat q_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}\) for \(g_0\) and \(q_0\).  The treatment and outcome innovations are
\[
 \eta=T-g_0(X),\qquad \xi=Y-q_0(X)-\theta_0\eta,
\]
and the clipped supplied codes at index \(n\) are
\[
 \bar g_n(x)=\min\{\max\{\widehat g_n(x),-C_g\},C_g\},\qquad
 \bar q_n(x)=\min\{\max\{\widehat q_n(x),-C_q\},C_q\}.
\]
The covariate marginal of \(P\) is written \(P_X\).  A model is written \(m\), and when the law has to be displayed the coefficient is written \(\theta_0(P)\).
\end{definitionv}

The sampling and structural restrictions are collected next. They separate the finite-sample product experiment, the partially linear conditional mean, the bounded ranges, and the tail and cumulant conditions that define the statistical classes.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iid-sampling}[I.i.d. sampling]
Under \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the observations satisfy
\[
O_1,\ldots,O_n \stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim} P .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The i.i.d. sampling condition is the standard finite-sample product-law setup for orthogonal and semiparametric procedures \citep{ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML}. It fixes the probability space on which all estimators and generalized quantiles below are evaluated.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:independent-treatment-noise}[Independent treatment noise]
Under \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the treatment innovation is independent of the covariates:
\[
\eta \perp X .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

Independent treatment noise is the standard residual independence condition used in the cumulant-based partially linear comparison \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. In this paper it also makes the treatment-innovation transform a law-level object separate from the covariate distribution.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:outcome-mean-independence}[Outcome mean independence]
Under \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the outcome innovation has conditionally mean zero:
\[
E_P[\xi \mid X,T]=0
\]
almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

Outcome mean independence is the standard partially linear conditional mean restriction \citep{Robinson1988}. It pins down the coefficient through the conditional mean of \(Y\) given \(X\) and \(T\).

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:theta-range}[Coefficient range]
Under \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the partially linear coefficient satisfies
\[
|\theta_0|\le C_{\theta}.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The coefficient range is the standard bounded target range condition in the JMS comparison framework \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. It supplies a common compact parameter range for minimax risks and clipped estimators.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:g-range}[Treatment-regression range]
Under \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the treatment regression satisfies
\[
\|g_0\|_{\infty}\le C_g .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The bounded treatment regression condition is standard in the same comparison class \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. It also aligns the supplied treatment code with the clipping radius used in \cref{obj:def:plm-model}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:q-range}[Outcome-regression range]
Under \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the outcome regression satisfies
\[
\|q_0\|_{\infty}\le C_q .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The bounded outcome regression condition is the standard common sub-Gaussian PLM range condition \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. Together with the treatment and coefficient bounds, it fixes the envelope in which the supplied outcome code is compared.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:bounded-gaussian-outcome}[Bounded outcome]
In the setting of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the outcome \(Y\) satisfies
\[
|Y|\le C_q
\]
almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

The bounded observed-outcome restriction is the standard bounded-\(Y\) condition in the simultaneous Gaussian comparison experiment \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. It is used only for that Gaussian intersection class.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:eta-subgaussian}[Treatment-noise sub-Gaussianity]
In the setting of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, with the Luxemburg convention
\[
\lVert W\rVert_{\psi_2}
=
\inf\{u>0:E_P[\exp(W^2/u^2)]\le2\},
\]
the treatment innovation \(\eta\) satisfies
\[
\lVert \eta\rVert_{\psi_2}\le\psi_{\eta}.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The treatment-tail condition is the standard uniform sub-Gaussian treatment-noise requirement under the exact Luxemburg convention \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. The convention matches the concentration and transform-envelope bounds used later; background sub-Gaussian facts follow the usual conventions in \citet{Vershynin2018} and \citet{Wainwright2019}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:xi-subgaussian}[Outcome-noise sub-Gaussianity]
In the setting of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the outcome innovation \(\xi\) satisfies
\[
\lVert \xi\rVert_{\psi_2\mid X}\le\psi_{\xi}
\]
almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

The outcome-tail condition is the standard uniform conditional sub-Gaussian outcome-noise restriction \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. It provides the conditional exponential-square control needed for empirical outcome-weighted transforms.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:cumulant-separation}[Cumulant separation]
In the setting of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the \(k\)th cumulant of the treatment innovation \(\eta\) satisfies
\[
|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

Cumulant separation is the standard Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis nonzero cumulant condition with a uniform separation level \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. In the contour analysis, the same lower bound supplies the non-Gaussian signal that later yields transform zeros.

The supplied-code restrictions specify how the deterministic first-stage codes enter the statistical classes. The \(L^s(P_X)\) conditions give a common language for comparison with ACE, the \(L^r(P_X)\) versions match the published order-\(r\) condition, the Gaussian condition defines the comparison experiment, and the \(L^1(P_X)\) condition is the stability requirement used by the contour class.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:treatment-code-radius}[Treatment-code radius]
In the setting of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, for every \(n\),
\[
\lVert \bar g_n-g_0\rVert_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The \(L^s(P_X)\) treatment-code radius is a standard structure-agnostic supplied-code uncertainty set \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. It measures the current-index treatment-code error under the covariate marginal.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:outcome-code-radius}[Outcome-code radius]
In the setting of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, for every \(n\),
\[
\lVert \bar q_n-q_0\rVert_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The matching \(L^s(P_X)\) outcome-code radius is the standard supplied-code uncertainty set for the outcome regression \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. It places the outcome code and treatment code on the same comparison scale.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:jms-treatment-code-radius}[Treatment-code \(L^r\) radius]
In the notation of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, for every \(n\ge 1\),
\[
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The \(L^r(P_X)\) treatment-code radius is the standard Theorem 5.4 treatment-code uncertainty set in the JMS ACE guarantee \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. It is stated separately because the published finite-order bound is indexed by the ACE order \(r\).

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:jms-outcome-code-radius}[Outcome-code \(L^r\) radius]
In the notation of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, for every \(n\ge 1\),
\[
\|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \varepsilon_{2,n}.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The \(L^r(P_X)\) outcome-code radius is the corresponding standard Theorem 5.4 outcome-code uncertainty set \citep{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. It is paired with \cref{obj:ass:jms-treatment-code-radius} in the ACE comparison class.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:gaussian-treatment-noise}[Gaussian treatment noise]
In the notation of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the treatment innovation satisfies
\[
\eta\sim N(0,\sigma^2).
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The Gaussian treatment-noise condition is the standard known-variance Gaussian experiment used in Theorem 3.2 of \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. It defines the Gaussian benchmark class against which the non-Gaussian contour class is compared.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:l1-treatment-code-radius}[Treatment-code \(L^1\) radius]
In the notation of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, for every \(n\ge 1\),
\[
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^1(P_X)}\le \varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment radius is specific to this analysis. It is the stability condition that controls the code-residual perturbation of the treatment transform in the contour argument. Throughout, a \emph{code residual} means \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) formed with the deterministic supplied clipped code \(\bar g_n\). All risk statements condition on deterministic supplied clipped treatment and outcome codes and evaluate fixed-code performance at the current sample index.

We now package these restrictions into the law classes used by the main theorem and by the ACE and Gaussian comparisons. The broad spectral class keeps the cumulant-separated treatment noise and the \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code control that are needed for the contour construction.

\begin{definitionv}{def:non-gaussian-class}[Non-Gaussian class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\)]
Using the notation of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, and with the structural conditions recorded in \cref{obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise,obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence,obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:eta-subgaussian,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian,obj:ass:cumulant-separation}, define
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}
=
\left\{
P:\;
\begin{array}{l}
g_0(x)=E_P[T\mid X=x],\quad q_0(x)=E_P[Y\mid X=x],\\
E_P[Y\mid X,T]=q_0(X)+\theta_0(P)\{T-g_0(X)\},\\
\eta=T-g_0(X),\quad
\xi=Y-q_0(X)-\theta_0(P)\eta,\\
\eta\perp X,\quad E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0,\\
|\theta_0(P)|\le C_{\theta},\quad
\|g_0\|_{\infty}\le C_g,\quad
\|q_0\|_{\infty}\le C_q,\\
\|\eta\|_{\psi_2}\le\psi_{\eta},\quad
\|\xi\|_{\psi_2\mid X}\le\psi_{\xi},\\
|\kappa_{r+1}(\eta)|\ge\delta,\quad
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^1(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
Thus membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) is exactly determined by the displayed law, range, tail, cumulant-separation, and treatment-code radius conditions.
\end{definitionv}

The bounded-outcome Gaussian class uses the same partially linear residual functionals but imposes Gaussian treatment noise and the supplied-code restrictions at exponent \(s\). It is the source-stated Gaussian comparison class for the diagnostic and minimax criteria below.

\begin{definitionv}{def:gaussian-class}
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}:=\left\{P:\begin{array}{l}1\le n,\ E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0\ P\text{-a.s.},\ \xi\in L^1(P),\ |\theta_0|\le C_{\theta},\ |Y|\le C_q\ P\text{-a.s.},\\ \eta\perp X,\ \eta\sim N(0,\sigma^2),\ |g_0(x)|\le C_g\ P_X\text{-a.e.},\\ \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n},\ \|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}\end{array}\right\}.
\]
Here \(g_0,q_0,\eta,\xi\) are the residual functionals determined by \(P\) in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}.
\end{definitionv}

The ACE comparison class records the published cumulant-separated setup at order \(r\). Its member conditions mirror the preceding assumptions while using the \(L^r(P_X)\) code radii that enter the JMS finite-order guarantee.

\begin{definitionv}{def:jms-ace-class}[JMS ACE comparison class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\)]
Fix an admissible parameter record with positive sample-size coordinate and
\[
k=r+1,\qquad 2\le r,\qquad r\le s,\qquad \gamma\in(1/2,1),
\]
with
\[
0<C_{\theta},\quad 0<C_g,\quad 0<C_q,\quad
0<\psi_{\eta},\quad 0<\psi_{\xi},\quad 0<\delta,
\quad 0<\sigma,
\]
and with radius sequences satisfying
\[
0\le \varepsilon_{j,b}\le \varepsilon_{j,a}
\qquad (j\in\{1,2\},\ 1\le a\le b).
\]
At any positive class index \(n\), the JMS ACE comparison class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) consists of all model records \(m\) whose observed-data component \(P\) is a probability law on \(O=X\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\), carrying a real \(\theta_0\), measurable functions \(g_0,q_0:X\to\mathbb R\), and deterministic measurable supplied code sequences whose clipped versions are denoted \(\bar g_\ell\) and \(\bar q_\ell\). The coordinates \(T\) and \(Y\) are \(P\)-integrable, and the conditional-mean identities
\[
g_0(X)=E_P[T\mid X],\qquad q_0(X)=E_P[Y\mid X]
\]
hold \(P\)-almost surely. Writing \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\) and \(\xi=Y-q_0(X)-\theta_0\eta\), the member conditions in \cref{obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise,obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence,obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:eta-subgaussian,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian,obj:ass:cumulant-separation} hold in the following form:
\[
\xi\in L^1(P),\qquad E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0,\qquad \eta\perp X,
\]
with the conditional-mean identity for \(\xi\) holding \(P\)-almost surely;
\[
|\theta_0|\le C_{\theta},\qquad |g_0(x)|\le C_g,
\qquad |q_0(x)|\le C_q,
\]
with the displayed bounds on \(g_0\) and \(q_0\) holding for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\);
\[
\exp(\eta^2/\psi_{\eta}^2)\in L^1(P),\qquad
\int \exp(\eta^2/\psi_{\eta}^2)\,dP\le 2,
\]
\[
\exp(\xi^2/\psi_{\xi}^2)\in L^1(P),\qquad
E_P[\exp(\xi^2/\psi_{\xi}^2)\mid X]\le 2,
\]
with the conditional tail bound holding \(P\)-almost surely; and
\[
|\kappa_{r+1}(\eta)|\ge\delta,\qquad
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n},\qquad
\|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

Risk comparisons use a generalized lower quantile for absolute estimation error. Because \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), the controlled level \(1-\gamma\) lies below one half, so \(Q_{P,1-\gamma}\) is a lower quantile of the absolute error and every quantile bound below is to be read on that scale. The definition is stated for an arbitrary real sample statistic so it can be applied both to estimator errors and to the published ACE quantile bound.

\begin{definitionv}{def:generalized-quantile}[Generalized quantile \(Q_{P,1-\gamma}(W)\)]
In the sampling setup of \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling} and the notation of \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, let \(P\) be an observed-data law, let \(n\ge 1\), and let \(W=W(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\) be a real-valued sample statistic. Under the product law \(P^{\otimes n}\), write
\[
F_W(t)=\Pr\{W\le t\}.
\]
For \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), the generalized lower \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile of \(W\) is
\[
Q_{P,1-\gamma}(W)
=
\inf\{t\in\mathbb R:F_W(t)\ge 1-\gamma\}.
\]
This is the left-continuous generalized inverse of \(F_W\) evaluated at probability level \(1-\gamma\).
\end{definitionv}

The contour factorization in the next section is expressed through the covariate-level error made by the supplied treatment code. The following notation isolates that error and the corresponding outcome-contamination transform.

\begin{definitionv}{def:outcome-contamination}[Treatment-code error \(D_n\), outcome contamination \(b_n\), and its transform \(B_n\)]
Fix a law \(P\), an index \(n\), and the clipped supplied treatment code \(\bar g_n\).  The treatment-code error and the outcome contamination are the covariate functions
\[
 D_n(X)=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X),
 \qquad
 b_n(X)=q_0(X)-\theta_0\{g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\}=q_0(X)-\theta_0 D_n(X),
\]
and the contamination transform is their weighted exponential transform
\[
 B_n(z)=E_P\bigl[b_n(X)e^{zD_n(X)}\bigr],\qquad z\in\mathbb C.
\]
\end{definitionv}

For comparison with \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}, we also record the eligibility quantities that enter the published order-\(r\) ACE condition. They combine code radii, sample size, tail scales, coefficient range, and cumulant separation into the finite-order admissibility check.

\begin{definitionv}{def:jms-eligibility-quantities}[JMS eligibility quantities \(a_{1,n},b_{1,n},a_2,b_{2,n}\)]
Under the notation and conditions in \cref{obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:eta-subgaussian,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian,obj:ass:cumulant-separation,obj:ass:jms-treatment-code-radius,obj:ass:jms-outcome-code-radius,obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:model-parameters}, the four quantities entering the Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis order-\(r\) eligibility condition at index \(n\) are
\[
 a_{1,n}=2\log\!\left(\frac{6(C_g+\psi_{\eta})}{\varepsilon_{1,n}}\right),
 \qquad
 b_{1,n}=\log\!\left(\frac{\gamma n}{9}\right),
 \qquad
 a_2=4(C_g+\psi_{\eta}),
\]
\[
 b_{2,n}=
 \frac{200\min\{1,C_{\theta}\}\,\delta}
      {\max\bigl\{\varepsilon_{1,n},\ \varepsilon_{2,n},\ (\gamma n)^{-1/2}(\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta})\bigr\}} .
\]
\end{definitionv}

The minimax criteria are class-indexed and fix the supplied codes at the current sample size. This keeps the decision problem focused on laws compatible with the same clipped code pair.

\begin{definitionv}{def:minimax-risks}[Minimax risks \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}},\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G},\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}\)]
For fixed supplied code sequences, let \(\bar g_n\) and \(\bar q_n\) denote their current-index clippings to \([-C_g,C_g]\) and \([-C_q,C_q]\). All infima below range over measurable estimators based on \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), and each supremum is restricted to laws in the displayed class whose clipped treatment and outcome codes at index \(n\) are \(\bar g_n\) and \(\bar q_n\). Given a parameter record \(p\) and supplied code sequences \(\widehat g,\widehat q\), a class \(\mathcal C\) of laws carries the notation \(\mathcal C(p;\bar g_n,\bar q_n)\) for the subset cut out by this fixed-code restriction, and we write \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)\), \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)\), and \(\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)\) for the three risks defined next whenever their dependence on the parameter record and the supplied codes has to be displayed. Define the non-Gaussian minimax mean-squared risk by
\[
\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
=
\inf_{\widetilde\theta(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\bar g_n,\bar q_n)}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
E_P\!\left[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2\right].
\]
Define the Gaussian JMS minimax mean-squared risk by
\[
\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}
=
\inf_{\widetilde\theta(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\bar g_n,\bar q_n)}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
E_P\!\left[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2\right].
\]
For \(1/2<\gamma<1\), define the Gaussian JMS minimax generalized lower-quantile risk by
\[
\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}
=
\inf_{\widetilde\theta}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
Q_{P,1-\gamma}\!\left(|\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P)|\right).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The order-\(r\) ACE guarantee is invoked in later comparisons through its eligibility predicate. The predicate below is the finite-sample condition formed from the four quantities in \cref{obj:def:jms-eligibility-quantities}.

\begin{definitionv}{def:jms-eligibility}[JMS eligibility condition \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\)]
With \(a_{1,n},b_{1,n},a_2,b_{2,n}\) defined as in \cref{obj:def:jms-eligibility-quantities}, the JMS order-\(r\) eligibility condition is
\[
\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r\le
\min\left\{
\frac{b_{1,n}}{a_{1,n}}
-\frac{1}{a_{1,n}}\log\left(\frac{b_{1,n}}{a_{1,n}}\right),
\frac{b_{2,n}}{a_2\log(a_2b_{2,n})}
\right\},
\]
on the indices for which the displayed expressions are defined.
\end{definitionv}

Finally, the common ACE comparison class is obtained by adding the \(L^s(P_X)\) treatment- and outcome-code restrictions to the broad spectral class. This subclass is the domain on which the contour and ACE upper guarantees are aligned in the comparison section.

\begin{definitionv}{def:ace-comparison-subclass}[ACE comparison subclass \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\)]
The ACE comparison subclass of the non-Gaussian class in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} is
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}
=
\left\{
P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}:
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n},
\ 
\|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}
\right\}.
\]
It consists of laws satisfying the broad spectral-class conditions together with the displayed supplied-code restrictions.
\end{definitionv}


\section{Zero instruments and contour identification}

The analytic mechanism starts from a zero of the treatment-innovation transform and turns it into an instrument for the residualized treatment. In the partially linear model, the supplied treatment code enters through the residual \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\); the point of the construction is that a transform zero of the innovation \(\eta\) continues to generate usable population moments after the residual has been shifted by the treatment-code error. The use of transform zeros follows a classical identification tradition for moment-generating and characteristic functions \citep{KaganLinnikRao1973,Mattner1992,DHaultfoeuille2011,DarollesFanFlorensRenault2011,HuShiu2022}, while the contour formulation below is aligned with transform-based estimation in econometrics \citep{FeuervergerMcDunnough1981,Singleton2001,CarrascoFlorensRenault2007,Carrasco2017}.

The first object is the treatment-innovation transform \(M(z)\) and its zero-based instrument. If \(z_0\) is a zero of multiplicity \(\ell\), differentiating the exponential tilt at that zero produces the polynomial-exponential weight used throughout this section.

\begin{definitionv}{def:zero-instrument}[Treatment-innovation transform \(M\)]
Let \(M:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\), let \(z_0\in\mathbb C\), and let \(\ell\in\mathbb N\) satisfy \(1\le \ell\), \(M(z_0)=0\), and analytic order \(\ell\) for \(M\) at \(z_0\). Define
\[
J_{z_0,\ell}(w)=w^{\ell-1}e^{z_0w},\qquad w\in\mathbb C.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The instrument \(J_{z_0,\ell}(w)\) is indexed by the complex zero \(z_0\) and the positive multiplicity \(\ell\). Its role is local in the transform variable but global in the residual shift: the same weight will be evaluated at shifted innovations and at the code residual.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:known-zero-instrument}[Zero-based instrument identities]
Fix a model-parameter record \(p\), a model \(m\) with observed-data law \(P\), and an integer \(n\ge 1\). Let
\[
M(z)=E_P\!\left[e^{z\eta}\right],
\qquad
\eta=T-g_0(X),
\qquad
Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X),
\qquad
H_n(z)=E_P\!\left[e^{z\{g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\}}\right].
\]
Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Non-Gaussian class.)} The model \(m\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) as specified in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}.
\item \textbf{(Zero and multiplicity.)} For some \(z_0\in\mathbb C\) and integer \(\ell\ge 1\), \(M(z_0)=0\) and the analytic order of \(M\) at \(z_0\) is \(\ell\).
\item \textbf{(Instrument.)} \(J_{z_0,\ell}\) is the zero-based instrument from \cref{obj:def:zero-instrument}, equivalently
\[
J_{z_0,\ell}(w)=w^{\ell-1}e^{z_0w}.
\]
\end{itemize}
Then, for every \(d\in\mathbb R\),
\[
E_P\!\left[J_{z_0,\ell}(\eta+d)\right]=0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
E_P\!\left[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\mid X\right]=0
\quad P\text{-almost surely},
\]
and
\[
E_P\!\left[Z_n J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\right]
=
M^{(\ell)}(z_0)H_n(z_0).
\]
Whenever the denominator is nonzero,
\[
E_P\!\left[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\right]\neq 0,
\]
the coefficient is identified by
\[
\theta_0
=
\frac{E_P\!\left[YJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\right]}
     {E_P\!\left[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\right]}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:known-zero-instrument} gives the moment-ratio form of the argument when a valid zero is known. The identity \(E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(\eta+d)]=0\) applies uniformly over real shifts \(d\), so conditioning on \(X\) converts the residualized treatment into a valid zero moment. The denominator identity identifies the relevant scale as the product of the nonzero derivative at the transform zero and the nuisance transform \(H_n(z)\); when that scale is nonzero, the partially linear coefficient is the displayed population ratio.

For data-driven contour selection, the paper uses observable transforms of the code residual. The treatment-residual transform \(F_n(z)\) carries the zero geometry, the outcome-weighted transform \(G_n(z)\) carries the numerator information, and a contour average of \(G_n/F_n\) extracts the coefficient over all enclosed zeros at once.

\begin{definitionv}{def:contour-functional}[Contour functional \(\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n)\)]
For \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\), define
\[
F_n(z)=E_P[e^{zZ_n}],
\qquad
G_n(z)=E_P[Ye^{zZ_n}],
\qquad
H_n(z)=E_P[e^{z\{g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\}}].
\]
Let \(C_j\) be a positively oriented circle centered at the origin with positive radius, and suppose \(F_n\) is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk bounded by \(C_j\). Let \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\) denote the number of zeros of \(F_n\) strictly inside \(C_j\), counted with multiplicity. When \(F_n\) is zero-free on \(C_j\) and \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\ge1\), define
\[
\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n)
=
\{N_{C_j}(F_n)2\pi i\}^{-1}
\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz .
\]
\end{definitionv}

Here \(C_j\) is the positively oriented circle used for the contour integral, \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\) is the enclosed zero count, and \(\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n)\) is the normalized contour functional. The normalization by the zero count makes the functional an average over the enclosed zero structure.

The next identity links this observable contour world to the latent treatment innovation. It factors the residual transform through the innovation transform and isolates the covariate-only contamination term that enters the outcome-weighted transform.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:observable-factorization}[Observable transform factorization]
Let \(m\) be a model with observed-data law \(P\) in the non-Gaussian class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) of \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}.  For
\[
Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X),\qquad
D_n(X)=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X),\qquad
\eta=T-g_0(X),
\]
define, for \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
F_n(z)=E_P[e^{zZ_n}],\qquad
M(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}],\qquad
H_n(z)=E_P[e^{zD_n(X)}],
\]
and
\[
G_n(z)=E_P[Ye^{zZ_n}],\qquad
B_n(z)=E_P[b_n(X)e^{zD_n(X)}],
\]
where \(b_n(X)\) is the outcome-contamination function in \cref{obj:def:outcome-contamination}.  Then, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
F_n(z)=M(z)H_n(z)
\]
and
\[
G_n(z)=\theta_0(P)F_n'(z)+M(z)B_n(z).
\]
\end{lemmav}

The proof is deferred to \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.

\Cref{obj:lem:observable-factorization} is the bridge from residual contamination to contour identification. The factorization \(F_n=MH_n\) states that the observable residual transform inherits zeros from the treatment-innovation transform whenever the nuisance transform stays away from zero. The second display decomposes \(G_n/F_n\) into the derivative term carrying \(\theta_0\) and a nuisance ratio; integrated around a valid contour, the latter contribution is analytic inside the contour and the former contributes through the enclosed zero count.

The population identification statement packages these ingredients into the contour ratio used later by the statistical construction. The contour must be valid for the observable residual transform and for the nuisance transform on the same disk.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:exact-contour-identification}[Exact contour identification]
Fix \(n\ge 1\), a law \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) as in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}, and a certified contour-bank input \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) as in \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle}. For any bank index \(j\in\{0,\ldots,J_{\mathrm{base}}\}\), let \(C_j\) be the positively oriented circle with radius \(\rho_j\), and let
\[
F_n(z)=E_P[e^{zZ_n}],\qquad
G_n(z)=E_P[Ye^{zZ_n}],\qquad
H_n(z)=E_P[e^{z\{g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\}}].
\]
Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Residual zero-freeness.)} \(F_n(z)\ne 0\) for every \(z\in C_j\).
\item \textbf{(Nuisance zero-freeness.)} \(H_n(z)\ne 0\) for every \(z\) on or inside \(C_j\).
\item \textbf{(Positive contour count.)} The zero count \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\), counted with multiplicity inside \(C_j\), satisfies \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\ge 1\).
\end{itemize}
Then the contour functional of \cref{obj:def:contour-functional} identifies the partially linear coefficient and is given by the normalized contour integral:
\[
(\theta_0(P):\mathbb C)
=
\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n)
=
\{N_{C_j}(F_n)\,2\pi i\}^{-1}
\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz .
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:exact-contour-identification} establishes the population contour identity on any bank circle satisfying the stated zero-free and positive-count conditions. Residual zero-freeness makes \(G_n/F_n\) well defined on the integration path; nuisance zero-freeness keeps the zeros enclosed by the residual transform aligned with the innovation-zero geometry supplied by \cref{obj:lem:observable-factorization}; and the positive count gives a nontrivial normalization. Under those conditions, the contour average of the observable ratio is exactly the partially linear coefficient.

The section therefore reduces identification with code residuals to a geometric task: find a circle on which the observable residual transform is separated from zero, whose interior contains at least one residual-transform zero, and on whose closed disk the nuisance transform is separated from zero. The next section supplies fixed-separation conditions and a finite contour bank under which such circles are available with enough empirical stability to yield the stated fixed-code risk bounds.


\section{Main fixed-separation result}

The preceding section gave the population contour identity. The fixed-separation result turns that identity into a uniform statistical construction by combining three ingredients: an explicit zero-localization radius from cumulant separation, an \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code gate that preserves the relevant zero geometry, and empirical transform control on a finite contour bank. The empirical-process component follows the same uniform-supremum logic used in semiparametric and double/debiased learning analyses \citep{BickelKlaassenRitovWellner1993,VanDerVaart1998,VanDerVaartWellner1996,ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML}, while the contour bank uses finite analytic localization of zeros of exponential transforms \citep{MichelenSahasrabudhe2019,EremenkoFryntov2021,DinhGhoshTranTran2021}.

The first auxiliary bound fixes the Luxemburg normalization used throughout the paper. It converts the sub-Gaussian convention in \cref{obj:def:model-parameters} into a concrete moment-generating-function envelope, so the constants entering the zero-localization radius remain explicit.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:luxemburg-mgf-envelope}[Luxemburg MGF envelope]
Following the Luxemburg sub-Gaussian convention in \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}, let \(\psi>0\) be a Luxemburg scale and let \(W\) be a real-valued measurable random variable on a probability space with law \(P\). Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Integrability.)} \(W\in L^1(P)\) and \(\exp(W^2/\psi^2)\in L^1(P)\).
\item \textbf{(Centeredness.)} \(E_P W=0\).
\item \textbf{(Luxemburg envelope.)}
\[
E_P\exp(W^2/\psi^2)\le 2.
\]
\end{itemize}
Then, for every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
E_P e^{tW}\le \exp(4\psi^2t^2).
\]
\end{lemmav}

The proof is deferred to \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.

\Cref{obj:lem:luxemburg-mgf-envelope} supplies the analytic tail envelope used to control treatment-innovation transforms on complex disks. With the exact convention fixed in \cref{obj:ass:eta-subgaussian}, the bound carries the displayed factor \(4\), which then feeds directly into the localization constants.

Cumulant separation gives the treatment-innovation transform a zero inside an explicit disk. This step is the bridge from the population identification formulas in \cref{obj:thm:known-zero-instrument,obj:thm:exact-contour-identification} to a finite search region.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:zero-localization}
Fix a primitive parameter tuple \(p\) and a law \(P\) for \(O=(X,T,Y)\). Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(k=r+1\) with \(r\ge2\);
\item \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\) is centered;
\item \(\|\eta\|_{\psi_2}\le\psi_{\eta}\) under the convention in \cref{obj:ass:eta-subgaussian}; and
\item \(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta\) as in \cref{obj:ass:cumulant-separation}.
\end{itemize}
Define
\[
A_k=(2^{k+4}k!)^{1/(k-2)},\qquad
R_0=A_k(\psi_{\eta}^2/\delta)^{1/(k-2)},\qquad
M(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}].
\]
Then there exists \(z_0\in\mathbb C\) such that \(|z_0|\le R_0\) and \(M(z_0)=0\).
\end{lemmav}

The proof is deferred to \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.

The radius \leanref{sym:R0}{\ensuremath{R_0}} is determined by the fixed cumulant order, the treatment-noise Luxemburg scale, and the separation level. The contour construction expands this disk to the outer radius \leanref{sym:R1}{\ensuremath{R_1}}, allowing the finite bank to place candidate circles around the localized zero while retaining a buffer for perturbation control.

The numerator transform also needs a fixed envelope on the search region. The next bound records a population scale for the observable outcome-weighted transform on the disk used by the contour bank.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:population-numerator-envelope}[Population numerator envelope]
Let \(p\) be a parameter record as in \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}, and let \(m\) be a model with observed-data law \(P\) as in \cref{obj:def:plm-model}. Write \(n=p.n\), let \(R_1\) be the outer search radius from \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle}, and define the population numerator envelope
\[
C_G(p)
=
\left[
64\{C_q^4+4C_{\theta}^4\psi_{\eta}^4+4\psi_{\xi}^4\}
+
2\exp\{16R_1C_g+16R_1^2\psi_{\eta}^2\}
\right]^{1/2}.
\]
Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Non-Gaussian class.)} The model \(m\), equivalently its law \(P\), lies in the non-Gaussian class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) of \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}.
\item \textbf{(Search window.)} The complex point \(z\in\mathbb C\) satisfies \(|z|\le R_1\).
\end{itemize}
Then the observable outcome-weighted transform \(G_n(z)=E_P[Ye^{zZ_n}]\) from \cref{obj:def:contour-functional} satisfies
\[
|G_n(z)|\le C_G(p).
\]
\end{lemmav}

The proof is deferred to \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.

\Cref{obj:lem:population-numerator-envelope} keeps the outcome side of the contour ratio on the same fixed scale as the denominator search region. The envelope depends on the primitive bounds and \(R_1\), so the later risk constants can be indexed by the fixed class constants.

The estimator is a finite, split-sample contour program. The pilot fold selects a circle using the empirical winding number and a denominator lower-modulus certificate; the evaluation fold computes the normalized contour ratio on that selected circle and clips the real midpoint to the parameter range. The certified-record clauses state the corresponding finite-rational implementation contract for the same statistic.

\begin{algorithmv}{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}[Adaptive contour estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\)]
The estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) is the packaged adaptive contour estimator on the public domain where the current supplied treatment-regression code underlying \(\bar g_n\) is measurable. It takes as inputs \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), the supplied treatment-regression code sequence through its current clipped values \(\bar g_n\), the parameter record \(p\), and fixed certified records \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) and \(\mathfrak c_{\theta,\star}\) representing \(k,\delta,\psi_{\eta},R_0,R_1\), and \(C_{\theta}\), with
\[
R_0=A_k\left(\frac{\psi_{\eta}^2}{\delta}\right)^{(k-2)^{-1}},
\qquad
R_1=R_0+1.
\]
Here \(p\) contains \(n\ge1\), \(r,k\in\mathbb N\) with \(k=r+1\) and \(r\ge2\), \(s\in[0,\infty]\) with \(r\le s\), \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), positive constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},\delta,\sigma\), and nonnegative nonincreasing sequences \(\varepsilon_{1n},\varepsilon_{2n}:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\) on positive indices.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Define the deterministic folds
\[
I_0=\{i:i<\lfloor n/2\rfloor\},
\qquad
I_1=\{i:i\ge\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}.
\]
For each real observation coordinate \(x\) and integer \(m\ge0\), define the floor-dyadic interval
\[
\mathcal I_m(x)=
\left[
2^{-(m+1)}\lfloor2^{m+1} x\rfloor,
2^{-(m+1)}\{\lfloor2^{m+1} x\rfloor+1\}
\right].
\]

\item Form the residualized treatment values
\[
Z_{n,i}=T_i-\bar g_n(X_i),
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.
\]
For each fold \(a\in\{0,1\}\), derivative order \(p\), and complex \(z\), define
\[
\widehat F_{a,n}^{(p)}(z)
=
\bigl(\max\{|I_a|,1\}\bigr)^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_a}Z_{n,i}^{p}e^{zZ_{n,i}},
\qquad p\in\{0,1,2\},
\]
and
\[
\widehat G_{a,n}^{(p)}(z)
=
\bigl(\max\{|I_a|,1\}\bigr)^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_a}Y_iZ_{n,i}^{p}e^{zZ_{n,i}},
\qquad p\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Thus an empty fold contributes the empty sum and is normalized by one.

\item For represented observation data, define
\[
\widetilde{\mathfrak s}
=
\bigl((\mathfrak t_i,\mathfrak y_i,\mathfrak g_i)_{i=1}^n\bigr),
\]
where \(\mathfrak t_i\), \(\mathfrak y_i\), and \(\mathfrak g_i\) are certified-real records naming \(T_i\), \(Y_i\), and \(\bar g_n(X_i)\). The full represented-data input is
\[
\mathfrak s_{\star}
=
\bigl((\mathfrak t_i,\mathfrak y_i,\mathfrak g_i)_{i=1}^n,
\mathfrak p_{\star},\mathfrak c_{\theta,\star}\bigr).
\]
For \(i=1,\ldots,n\), put
\[
\mathfrak z_i=\mathfrak t_i-\mathfrak g_i.
\]

\item Invoke the contour bank determined by \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\). For the \(j\)th circle, define
\[
C_j=\{z_j(t):0\le t\le1\},
\qquad
z_j(t)=\rho_je^{2\pi it}.
\]
At error tolerance one, refine \(\mathfrak z_i\), \(\mathfrak y_i\), and \(\mathfrak\rho_j\) using their own executable moduli, and let \(U_{Z,i}\), \(U_{Y,i}\), and \(U_{\rho,j}\) be the largest absolute endpoint of the corresponding rational intervals. For a canonical observation name this is the interval \(\mathcal I_{\mu(1)}\) with the convention above; for \(\mathfrak z_i\) it is the certified subtraction interval obtained from \(\mathfrak t_i\) and \(\mathfrak g_i\), whose modulus uses the two half-error input moduli. Using \(e^x\le3^{\lceil x\rceil}\) for \(x\ge0\) and \(\pi<4\), form rational upper bounds for the empirical derivative sums on each \(C_j\).

\item For each \(j\), compute a rational bracket of width at most \(a_{\star}/64\) enclosing
\[
\inf_{z\in C_j}|\widehat F_{0,n}(z)|.
\]
Let \(m_j\) be the lower endpoint of this bracket. When \(m_j>0\), define
\[
W_{0j}(t)=
\rho_je^{2\pi it}
\frac{\widehat F'_{0,n}(z_j(t))}
{\widehat F_{0,n}(z_j(t))}.
\]
Use the rational Lipschitz bound
\[
|W'_{0j}(t)|\le
64\left[
\rho_j\frac{\sup|\widehat F'_{0,n}|}{m_j}
+\rho_j^2\left\{
\frac{\sup|\widehat F''_{0,n}|}{m_j}
+\frac{\sup|\widehat F'_{0,n}|^2}{m_j^2}
\right\}\right].
\]

\item Compute a rectangle enclosure, with coordinate widths strictly below \(1/4\), for
\[
\int_0^1W_{0j}(t)\,dt
=
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}
\frac{\widehat F'_{0,n}(z)}
{\widehat F_{0,n}(z)}\,dz.
\]
Define
\[
N_j=
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}
\frac{\widehat F'_{0,n}(z)}
{\widehat F_{0,n}(z)}\,dz
\]
when the real coordinate contains a unique nonnegative integer and the imaginary coordinate contains zero. A circle is admissible when
\[
m_j\ge a_{\star}/2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
N_j\ge1.
\]
Let
\[
j_{\star}
=
\min\left\{
j:
j\in\arg\max_{\ell:\,m_{\ell}\ge a_{\star}/2,\ N_{\ell}\ge1}m_{\ell}
\right\}.
\]
If the admissible set is empty, set
\[
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}=0.
\]

\item On the evaluation fold for the selected circle \(C_{j_{\star}}\), compute a lower endpoint
\[
m_{j_{\star}}^{(1)}
\le
\inf_{z\in C_{j_{\star}}}|\widehat F_{1,n}(z)|.
\]
If
\[
m_{j_{\star}}^{(1)}<a_{\star}/4,
\]
set
\[
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}=0.
\]

\item Otherwise define the evaluation-fold contour integrand
\[
V_{j_{\star}}(t)=
\rho_{j_{\star}}e^{2\pi it}
\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z_{j_{\star}}(t))}
{\widehat F_{1,n}(z_{j_{\star}}(t))}.
\]
Use the rational Lipschitz bound
\[
|V'_{j_{\star}}(t)|\le
64\left[
\rho_{j_{\star}}\frac{\sup|\widehat G_{1,n}|}{m_{j_{\star}}^{(1)}}
+\rho_{j_{\star}}^2\left\{
\frac{\sup|\widehat G'_{1,n}|}{m_{j_{\star}}^{(1)}}
+\frac{\sup|\widehat G_{1,n}|\sup|\widehat F'_{1,n}|}{(m_{j_{\star}}^{(1)})^2}
\right\}\right].
\]
For each rational tolerance \(e>0\) and rational Lipschitz bound \(L\), the schedule spends one third of the tolerance on discretization and uses
\[
N_{\mathrm{mesh}}=\left\lceil\frac{3L}{e}\right\rceil+1,
\]
so that \(L/N_{\mathrm{mesh}}\le e/3\).

\item Enclose the unnormalized contour integral
\[
\int_0^1V_{j_{\star}}(t)\,dt
=
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_{j_{\star}}}
\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}
{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}\,dz.
\]
Divide the resulting rectangle by \(N_{j_{\star}}\) to enclose
\[
\frac{1}{N_{j_{\star}}2\pi i}
\oint_{C_{j_{\star}}}
\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}
{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}\,dz
\]
with coordinate radius at most \(1/n\). Take \(y\in\mathbb Q\) to be the real midpoint of that enclosure, so that \(y\) approximates the normalized contour integral to within \(1/n\):
\[
\left|\,y-\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{N_{j_{\star}}2\pi i}
\oint_{C_{j_{\star}}}\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}\,dz\right)\right|\le\frac1n.
\]
Only rational quantities are ever formed, so the estimator returns this certified rational approximation rather than the integral itself; the \(1/n\) tolerance is negligible beside the statistical error.

\item Output
\[
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}
=
\operatorname{clip}_{C_{\theta}}(y)
=
\frac{|y+C_{\theta}|-|y-C_{\theta}|}{2}
=
\Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}(y).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithmv}

The statistic \leanref{sym:thetahatSpec}{\ensuremath{\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}}} is a total Borel rule because every branch has a displayed output, including the fallback value used when the empirical admissibility or evaluation-fold lower-modulus checks fail. The represented-data clauses give an executable correspondence for implementations satisfying the bounded contour-arithmetic specification of \cref{obj:def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate}; the statistical risk statements below concern the same ordinary-sample statistic.

The main theorem gives the fixed-code minimax statement under fixed cumulant separation. The fixed-code restriction keeps the supplied clipped treatment and outcome codes common across the laws in the risk criterion, matching the decision-theoretic convention in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risks}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}[Fixed-code root-\(n\) minimaxity under fixed cumulant separation]
Fix \(r\ge 2\) and positive constants \(\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\).  Then there are constants \(c>0\) and \(C>0\), depending only on these fixed primitive constants, such that the following statements hold.

For every measurable covariate space, let \(p_0\) be a parameter record whose primitive entries \(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\) equal the fixed constants above, and fix one experiment-wide record consisting of the certified contour-bank input and the represented range input for \(p_0\).  For every parameter record \(p\) with the same primitive entries as \(p_0\), and for every base model satisfying the usual measurability, conditional-mean, and integrability requirements, write \(n\) for the sample size of \(p\).  Let \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) be the total Borel statistic of \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}, constructed from the transported fixed records and the base treatment-code sequence.  Define the fixed-code non-Gaussian class
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}(p;\mathrm{base})
=
\{P:\ P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\text{ of }\cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class},\ 
\bar g_n(P)=\bar g_n(\mathrm{base}),\ 
\bar q_n(P)=\bar q_n(\mathrm{base})\}.
\]
Define the fixed-code JMS ACE class
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}(p;\mathrm{base})
=
\{P:\ P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\text{ of }\cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class},\ 
\bar g_n(P)=\bar g_n(\mathrm{base}),\ 
\bar q_n(P)=\bar q_n(\mathrm{base})\}.
\]

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Measurable statistic.)}  The statistic \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) is measurable.
\item \textbf{(Represented execution.)}  For every compiled bounded spectral adapter satisfying the full canonical build-and-compilation specification for \(p\), the transported fixed records, and the base treatment-code sequence, the represented-data execution realizes the same \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) as in \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}.
\item \textbf{(Class relations.)}  Every law in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  If \(s=r\), then \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) coincides with the \(L^s(P_X)\)-restricted subclass \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) of \cref{obj:def:ace-comparison-subclass}.
\item \textbf{(Non-Gaussian fixed-code risk.)}  If the sample law is the i.i.d. product law of \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}, \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}(p;\mathrm{base})\) is nonempty, \(n\ge 2\), and
\[
\varepsilon_{1,n}\le \{4R_1\exp(2C_gR_1)\}^{-1},
\]
where \(R_1\) is the search radius from the contour-bank construction in \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}, then
\[
\frac{c}{n}
\le
\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}(p;\mathrm{base})}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2\right]
\le
\frac{C}{n},
\]
and
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}(p;\mathrm{base})}
Q_{P,1-\gamma}\!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
\le
\sqrt{\frac{C}{\gamma n}},
\]
with generalized lower quantiles as in \cref{obj:def:generalized-quantile}.
\item \textbf{(ACE fixed-code risk.)}  If \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}(p;\mathrm{base})\) is nonempty, \(n\ge 2\), and
\[
\varepsilon_{1,n}\le \{4R_1\exp(2C_gR_1)\}^{-1},
\]
then
\[
\frac{c}{n}
\le
\inf_{\widetilde\theta}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}(p;\mathrm{base})}
E_P\!\left[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2\right]
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}(p;\mathrm{base})}
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2\right]
\le
\frac{C}{n},
\]
where the infimum is over measurable real-valued estimators of \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\).  Moreover,
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}(p;\mathrm{base})}
Q_{P,1-\gamma}\!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
\le
\sqrt{\frac{C}{\gamma n}}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax} establishes the fixed-separation statistical guarantee. On the non-Gaussian fixed-code class, the minimax mean-squared risk is sandwiched between constants times \(n^{-1}\), and the contour statistic attains the upper bound. The same statistic also attains the \(n^{-1}\) MSE order and the displayed generalized-quantile bound on the fixed-code JMS ACE class whenever that class is nonempty and the treatment-code radius satisfies the stated small-radius gate.

The intuition is the same as in the population contour identity, with sampling error added. Cumulant separation localizes a zero of \(M\); the \(L^1(P_X)\) radius controls the perturbation from \(\eta\) to \(Z_n\), preserving a usable contour inside the finite bank; and the split empirical transforms approximate the population transforms on the selected circle at the usual \(n^{-1/2}\) stochastic scale. Squaring the coefficient error gives the \(n^{-1}\) MSE order, while the lower bound records the matching parametric difficulty inside the same fixed-separation experiment.

The sequence-level statement packages the same construction for common primitive constants. It also records the bounded-outcome Gaussian diagnostic under the JMS comparison class, separating the fixed-separation non-Gaussian rate statement from the Gaussian benchmark risk calculation.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:common-experiment-dichotomy}[Common experiment dichotomy]
Fix \(r\ge 2\) and positive constants \(\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\). For every measurable covariate space, the following hold.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Non-Gaussian sequence.)} Let \(p_0\) have these fixed values of \(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\), and fix the primitive records \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) and \(\mathfrak c_{\theta,\star}\) of \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle,obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}. Let \((p_j)\) be any parameter sequence with the same fixed values of \(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\) as \(p_0\), and with common supplied radii \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\). Let \((\widehat g_n,\widehat q_n)\) be any measurable deterministic code pair, and write \((\bar g_n,\bar q_n)\) for the clipped pair. Suppose that \(n_j=p_j.n\to\infty\) and, for all sufficiently large \(j\),
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n_j}(p_j;\bar g_{n_j},\bar q_{n_j})\neq\varnothing,\qquad
n_j\ge 2,\qquad
\varepsilon_{1,n_j}\le
\Bigl(4R_1(p_j)\exp\{2C_gR_1(p_j)\}\Bigr)^{-1}.
\]
Then there are constants \(c,C>0\) such that, with
\(B\) the translated-dyadic contour bank generated from \(p_0\) and \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\), for all sufficiently large \(j\) the transported primitive bank for \(p_j\) generates the same bank \(B\), the statistic \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n_j}\) of \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator} is measurable, every compiled bounded spectral adapter satisfying the full canonical build and compilation specification is represented by the corresponding execution on the same transported primitive records, and
\[
\frac{c}{n_j}\le
\mathfrak R_{n_j}^{\mathrm{NG}}(p_j;\widehat g,\widehat q)
\le
\frac{C}{n_j}.
\]
Moreover, the same statistic witnesses the upper bound:
\[
\sup_{P\in \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n_j}(p_j;\bar g_{n_j},\bar q_{n_j})}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n_j}-\theta_0(P)\bigr)^2
\right]
\le
\frac{C}{n_j}.
\]

\item \textbf{(Gaussian class.)} For every parameter record \(p\), writing \(n\) for its sample size, every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) in \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class} satisfies
\[
\theta_0(P)=0.
\]
For every measurable deterministic code pair \((\widehat g_n,\widehat q_n)\), whenever the clipped-code intersection of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) is nonempty,
\[
\mathfrak R_{n}^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)=0,
\qquad
\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)=0.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:common-experiment-dichotomy} gives two common-experiment conclusions. Along any sequence with the same fixed primitive constants and common supplied radii, the translated-dyadic contour bank stabilizes and the non-Gaussian minimax MSE remains of order \(n_j^{-1}\), with the contour statistic attaining the upper bound. On the bounded-outcome Gaussian JMS class, the partially linear target is identically zero, so both the fixed-code MSE risk and the fixed-code generalized-quantile risk equal zero whenever the clipped-code intersection is nonempty.

The non-Gaussian sequence clause reflects the finite nature of the contour bank: fixed primitive constants determine the same search geometry for all sufficiently large indices satisfying the displayed radius gate. The Gaussian clause records a diagnostic property of the simultaneous bounded-outcome and Gaussian treatment-noise restrictions in \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class}; under those restrictions, the target itself is pinned to zero, and the corresponding minimax risks inherit that degeneracy. Together, \cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax,obj:thm:common-experiment-dichotomy} provide the fixed-separation rate statement used for the ACE comparison in the next section.


\section{Comparison with ACE and Gaussian benchmarks}

The fixed-separation theorem in \cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax} gives a contour guarantee on the broad spectral class and on the fixed-code ACE class. This section records the comparison with the published order-\(r\) ACE guarantee of \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} on the same clipped-code experiment. The comparison has two parts: first, the class relations align the cumulant-separated spectral and ACE restrictions; second, the two generalized-quantile upper bounds can be read on the common class under the same JMS eligibility condition.

The following proposition states the alignment and the resulting upper-guarantee comparison. The constant \(C_{\mathrm{spec}}\) is fixed by the primitive constants in the contour construction, while \(C_{\gamma}\) and the estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}\) come from the published ACE handle. The displayed quantity \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\) is the order-\(r\) generalized-quantile upper guarantee from \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. The sequence clause compares the displayed upper bounds on the common clipped-code class.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:jms-ace-alignment}[ACE and spectral alignment]*
Fix an ACE order \(r\ge2\) and positive constants
\(\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\).  There is a constant
\(C_{\mathrm{spec}}>0\), depending only on these fixed constants, such that
the following statements hold.

Let \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), let \(C_{\gamma}>0\) be the constant in the
published order-\(r\) ACE generalized-quantile guarantee, and write
\(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}\) for the estimator supplied by that
published ACE handle.  Let \(p_0\) be a parameter record whose
\(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\), and \(\gamma\)
coordinates equal the displayed constants, and fix the certified primitive
bank and range records associated with \(p_0\).  For any parameter record
\(p\) sharing the same fixed experiment constants as \(p_0\) and the same
probability level \(\gamma\), and for any supplied code sequences whose clipped
versions are \(\bar g_n\) and \(\bar q_n\), the following hold, under the
usual measurability and integrability conditions.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Class relations.} At the sample size \(n\) of \(p\),
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n},
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}
 \subseteq \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}.
\]
If the comparison exponent of \(p\) satisfies \(s=r\), then the common clipped-code convention gives
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 =\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}.
\]
Here the classes and the comparison subclass are those of
\cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class,obj:def:non-gaussian-class,obj:def:ace-comparison-subclass}.

\item \textbf{ACE upper guarantee.} If a law \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\)
uses the clipped supplied treatment and outcome codes \(\bar g_n,\bar q_n\),
if the JMS eligibility condition \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) of
\cref{obj:def:jms-eligibility,obj:def:jms-eligibility-quantities} holds,
and if \(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\ge0\), then
\[
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \bigl(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\bigr)
 \le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma},
\]
where \(Q_{P,1-\gamma}\) is the generalized quantile in
\cref{obj:def:generalized-quantile} and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}
 &= C_{\gamma} r!16^r\delta^{-1}
 \Big[
   \varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
   + C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}  \\
 &\qquad\qquad
   +64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
   \{r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\}
   (\gamma n)^{-1/2}
 \Big].
\end{aligned}
\]

\item \textbf{Spectral upper guarantee.} If
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) contains at least one law using
the clipped supplied codes, \(n\ge2\), \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) holds,
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\), and
\[
 \varepsilon_{1,n}
 \le
 \{4R_1\exp(2C_gR_1)\}^{-1},
\]
where \(R_1\) is the outer contour radius of \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle} determined by the parameter record in force,
then the certified contour statistic
\(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) of
\cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}, built from the fixed primitive
records, satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \bigl(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|\bigr)
 \le
 \sqrt{\frac{C_{\mathrm{spec}}}{\gamma n}},
\]
where the supremum ranges over laws in the published ACE class whose clipped
supplied treatment and outcome codes are \(\bar g_n\) and \(\bar q_n\).

\item \textbf{Upper-guarantee separation along sequences.} For any sequence
\((p_n)\) of parameter records whose sample size at index \(n\) is \(n\), with the
same fixed experiment constants as \(p_0\),
the same radius sequences \(\varepsilon_{1,\cdot},\varepsilon_{2,\cdot}\),
and \(p_n.\gamma=\gamma\), suppose that eventually
\(n\ge2\), \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) holds,
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\), \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\ge0\),
\[
 \varepsilon_{1,n}
 \le
 \{4R_1\exp(2C_gR_1)\}^{-1},
\]
and the published ACE class contains a law using the clipped supplied codes.
If
\[
 \frac{
   \varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
   + C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
 }{n^{-1/2}}
 \longrightarrow \infty,
\]
then
\[
 \frac{
   \sqrt{C_{\mathrm{spec}}/(\gamma n)}
 }{
   B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}
 }
 \longrightarrow 0 .
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment} places the spectral and ACE guarantees on a shared statistical domain. The class relations use the fact that the ACE \(L^r(P_X)\) code control implies the \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code control needed by the spectral class, while the \(L^s(P_X)\)-restricted spectral subclass matches the published ACE code restrictions when \(s=r\). Under JMS eligibility, the ACE bound retains its finite-order nuisance terms, and the contour bound is governed by the fixed-separation sampling term \((\gamma n)^{-1/2}\). Along the displayed nuisance-dominance sequences, the ratio of the contour upper guarantee to the imported finite-order ACE upper guarantee tends to zero. The conclusion is an upper-bound comparison on the common clipped-code class; the ACE-related lower-bound statement used in the paper is the class-minimax lower bound over all measurable estimators on the fixed-code class.

For later reference, the class relation used inside \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment} is also recorded separately. This isolates the comparison-class bookkeeping from the estimator-specific upper bounds.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:jms-ace-class-relations}[ACE class relations]
Fix a measurable covariate space and a parameter record \(p\) with \(r,k\in\mathbb N\), \(k=r+1\), \(r\ge2\), \(s\in[0,\infty]\) satisfying \(s\ge r\), \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), positive constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},\delta,\sigma\), and nonnegative nonincreasing code-radius sequences \(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\) on positive indices.  Then, for every class index \(n\),
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(JMS inclusion.)} For every model \(m\), if \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) as defined in \cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class}, then \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) as defined in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}.
\item \textbf{(Comparison inclusion.)} For every model \(m\), if \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) as defined in \cref{obj:def:ace-comparison-subclass}, then \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\).
\item \textbf{(Equal-exponent equivalence.)} If \(s=r\), then for every model \(m\),
\[
m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}} .
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

The proof is deferred to \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.

\Cref{obj:lem:jms-ace-class-relations} is the set-inclusion component of the comparison. It identifies which restrictions are shared by the broad cumulant-separated class and the published ACE class, and it pins down the exact equality case under the common clipped-code convention. This makes the comparison in \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment} an upper-guarantee comparison on the same law class and the same clipped-code experiment.

The second benchmark is the bounded-outcome Gaussian comparison class of \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class}. \Cref{obj:prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy} gives a bounded-outcome Gaussian diagnostic for the intersection of the exact partially linear conditional mean, bounded observed outcome, and nondegenerate Gaussian treatment innovation. In every nonempty fixed-code intersection of that class, the decision-theoretic mean-squared and generalized-quantile risks are zero because the member restrictions determine the target value itself.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy}[Bounded Gaussian degeneracy]
Fix a measurable covariate space and a parameter record \(p\) with sample size \(n\ge1\), orders \(r,k\in\mathbb N\) satisfying \(k=r+1\) and \(r\ge2\), exponent \(s\ge r\), probability level \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), positive constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},\delta,\sigma\), and nonnegative nonincreasing code-radius sequences \(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\) on positive indices. Then every model \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) satisfying \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class} has
\[
\theta_0(P)=0.
\]
Moreover, for any deterministic supplied code sequences \(\widehat g,\widehat q:\mathbb N\to\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\), if there exists at least one \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) whose clipped codes at sample size \(n\) satisfy
\[
\bar g_n(x)=\min\{\max\{\widehat g_n(x),-C_g\},C_g\},
\qquad
\bar q_n(x)=\min\{\max\{\widehat q_n(x),-C_q\},C_q\}
\]
for every \(x\), then the fixed-code Gaussian minimax risks are both zero:
\[
\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)=0,
\qquad
\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)=0.
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy} gives a diagnostic for the source-stated Gaussian comparison class. A bounded observed outcome has bounded conditional support, while the partially linear term \(\theta_0\eta\) is driven by nondegenerate Gaussian treatment noise under \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class}; the proposition records the resulting target value and the induced zero minimax risks on each nonempty fixed-code intersection. Thus the Gaussian benchmark characterizes that simultaneous set of restrictions, whereas the fixed-separation comparison with ACE in \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment} is the active finite-sample upper-guarantee comparison on the cumulant-separated class.

Together, \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment,obj:lem:jms-ace-class-relations,obj:prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy} separate the two comparison roles. The ACE alignment places the published finite-order guarantee of \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} and the contour guarantee on a common cumulant-separated domain, with the sequence clause identifying the regime in which the ratio of displayed upper guarantees tends to zero. The Gaussian proposition explains the bounded-outcome Gaussian class by its implied target and the corresponding decision-theoretic risks, matching the diagnostic role already summarized in \cref{obj:thm:common-experiment-dichotomy}.


\section{Explicit mixture reductions and local benchmarks}

The preceding comparison keeps the cumulant separation level fixed. This section records two constructive weak-non-Gaussian benchmarks that make the transform-zero mechanism explicit in one dimension. The first benchmark treats a symmetric two-component Gaussian mixture, where the relevant zero is known and the contour search collapses to a sine moment. The second follows a Gaussian--Rademacher path indexed by a mixture amplitude, so the denominator scale and the fourth cumulant can be read from the same parameter. These examples connect the contour construction to non-Gaussian identification ideas in independent-component and structural-shock settings \citep{Comon1994,HyvarinenOja1997,HyvarinenKarhunenOja2001,LanneMeitzSaikkonen2017,LeeMesters2024,HoeschLeeMesters2024,ReizingerEtAl2025}.

The first construction is the clipped sine-ratio estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}\). It uses the known imaginary zero of the symmetric mixture transform and estimates the corresponding ratio directly from the empirical moments of \(Y\sin(\pi Z_n/2)\) and \(Z_n\sin(\pi Z_n/2)\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:sine-estimator}[Sine-ratio estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}\)]
Write \(\Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}(u)=\min\{\max\{u,-C_{\theta}\},C_{\theta}\}\) for the projection of a real number onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\).  For \(n\ge 1\) observed triples \(O_i=(X_i,T_i,Y_i)\), let \(\mathbb P_n f=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n f(O_i)\) and let \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\), where \(\bar g_n\) is the supplied treatment code clipped to \([-C_g,C_g]\). The clipped sine-ratio estimator is
\[
\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}
\left\{
\frac{\mathbb P_n[Y\sin(\pi Z_n/2)]}
{\mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(\pi Z_n/2)]}
\right\},
&\displaystyle \mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(\pi Z_n/2)]\ge e^{-\pi^2/8}/4,\\[3mm]
0,
&\displaystyle \mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(\pi Z_n/2)]< e^{-\pi^2/8}/4.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{definitionv}

The threshold in \cref{obj:def:sine-estimator} is tied to the population denominator scale that appears in the next proposition. In the symmetric mixture experiment, the treatment innovation has a known transform zero at \(i\pi/2\), and \(L^1(P_X)\) control of the supplied treatment code keeps the sine denominator bounded away from zero.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:symmetric-mixture-reduction}[Symmetric mixture reduction]
For any real constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\), there is a constant \(C>0\), depending only on these four constants, such that the following holds.  Let \(p\) be a parameter record whose constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\) are the ones just fixed, write \(n\) for its sample size, and let \(P\) be a law in the corresponding model.  Assume the usual measurability and integrability conditions, and suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sampling.)} \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are drawn according to the i.i.d. product law in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}.
\item \textbf{(Symmetric mixture.)} The treatment innovation \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\) has law
\[
\tfrac12 N(-1,1)+\tfrac12 N(1,1).
\]
\item \textbf{(Independence and ranges.)} The treatment innovation is independent of \(X\) as in \cref{obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise}, the outcome innovation satisfies \cref{obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence}, and the range and tail conditions in \cref{obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian} hold.
\item \textbf{(Treatment code.)} The supplied treatment code obeys
\[
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^1(P_X)}\le \varepsilon_{1,n},
\qquad
\varepsilon_{1,n}\le \frac1\pi .
\]
\end{itemize}
Then the fourth cumulant of \(\eta\) is
\[
\kappa_4(\eta)=-2,
\]
the treatment-innovation moment-generating function \(M(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}]\) satisfies
\[
M(i\pi/2)=0,
\]
and, writing \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) and \(D_n=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\),
\[
E_P\!\left[
Z_n\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi Z_n}{2}\right)
\right]
=
e^{-\pi^2/8}
E_P\!\left[
\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi D_n}{2}\right)
\right],
\]
with
\[
\frac{e^{-\pi^2/8}}{2}
\le
E_P\!\left[
Z_n\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi Z_n}{2}\right)
\right].
\]
Moreover, for the sine estimator \(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}\) in \cref{obj:def:sine-estimator},
\[
E_P\!\left[
\bigl(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}-\theta_0(P)\bigr)^2
\right]
\le
\frac{C}{n}.
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:symmetric-mixture-reduction} gives a fully explicit root-\(n\) benchmark for a symmetric non-Gaussian treatment innovation. The mixture has fourth cumulant \(-2\), its transform vanishes at \(i\pi/2\), and the sine denominator equals a positive contamination factor times \(e^{-\pi^2/8}\). The resulting empirical ratio attains \(C/n\) mean-squared error under the stated range, tail, independence, and \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code conditions.

The next elementary inequality is the algebraic device behind the sine-risk calculations. It separates numerator fluctuation from denominator fluctuation after clipping, using only a lower bound on the population denominator mean.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:clipped-ratio-risk-decomposition}[Clipped ratio risk decomposition]
Use the empirical-average notation \(\mathbb P_n\) from \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}. Let \(W\) and \(R\) be real-valued functions of one observation, let \(\theta,C_{\theta},A,\mu_W\in\mathbb R\), and let \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) be any sample. Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\ge 1\).
\item \textbf{(Clipping range.)} The target value \(\theta\) lies inside the clipping bound \(C_{\theta}\), in the sense that \(|\theta|\le C_{\theta}\).
\item \textbf{(Denominator scale.)} The scale \(A\) is strictly positive.
\item \textbf{(Population denominator level.)} The denominator mean satisfies \(\mu_W\ge A/2\).
\end{itemize}
Define the clipped ratio statistic at threshold \(A/4\) by
\[
\widehat\theta
=
\begin{cases}
\min\!\left\{\max\!\left\{\dfrac{\theta\,\mathbb P_n W+\mathbb P_n R}{\mathbb P_n W},-C_{\theta}\right\},C_{\theta}\right\},
& \text{if } A/4\le \mathbb P_n W,\\
0,
& \text{if } \mathbb P_n W<A/4.
\end{cases}
\]
Then, pathwise on this sample,
\[
(\widehat\theta-\theta)^2
\le
\frac{16}{A^2}
\left\{
(\mathbb P_n R)^2
+
C_{\theta}^2\bigl(\mathbb P_n(W-\mu_W)\bigr)^2
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

The proof is deferred to \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.

\Cref{obj:lem:clipped-ratio-risk-decomposition} is deterministic. In applications, \(W\) is the sine-weighted residualized treatment, \(R\) is the centered outcome-noise contribution to the sine numerator, and \(A\) is the population denominator scale. Once the sine moment gives \(\mu_W\ge A/2\), the ratio error is controlled by two empirical averages.

The Gaussian--Rademacher path provides a local family approaching Gaussian treatment noise through an explicit fourth-cumulant parameter. Let \(\eta_a\) denote the centered Gaussian--Rademacher treatment innovation in the next statement. Its first positive transform zero, denominator scale, and cumulant magnitude are all explicit functions of \(a\).

\begin{lemmav}{lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark}[Gaussian--Rademacher sine benchmark]
Fix real constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\).  There is a constant \(C>0\), depending only on these four constants, such that the following statement holds.  Let \(p\) be an admissible parameter record whose constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\) are the ones just fixed and whose cumulant order is \(k=4\), let \(a\in(0,1]\), and let \(P\) be a law in the model \(m\).  Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Gaussian--Rademacher path.)} The treatment innovation \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\) has the law of
\[
\eta_a=\sqrt{1-a^2}\,G_{\circ}+aS,
\]
where \(G_{\circ}\sim N(0,1)\), \(S\) is symmetric Rademacher, and \(G_{\circ}\) and \(S\) are independent.
\item \textbf{(Sampling and model restrictions.)} The law satisfies \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling,obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise,obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence,obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian}.
\item \textbf{(Current treatment-code radius.)} For the clipped supplied treatment code \(\bar g_n\),
\[
\int |\,\bar g_n(x)-g_0(x)\,|\,dP_X(x)\le \varepsilon_{1,n},
\]
under the usual integrability condition for this \(L^1(P_X)\) quantity.
\end{itemize}
Define
\[
t_a=\frac{\pi}{2a},\qquad
A_a=a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\},\qquad
\Delta_a=2a^4.
\]
Then the treatment-innovation moment-generating function satisfies
\[
M_a(z)=\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az),
\]
the fourth cumulant satisfies \(\kappa_4(\eta_a)=-2a^4\), and \(t_a>0\) is the first positive zero of the characteristic transform: \(M_a(it_a)=0\) and \(M_a(iu)\ne0\) for every \(u\in(0,t_a)\).  Moreover, for every \(d\in\mathbb R\),
\[
E_P[\sin\{t_a(\eta+d)\}]=0.
\]

Let \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) and \(D_n=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\).  If \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le 1/2\), then
\[
E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]
=A_aE_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
\]
and
\[
E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]\ge A_a/2.
\]
With \(\mathbb P_n\) denoting empirical averaging over \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), define
\[
 \widehat\theta_{a,n}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}
 \frac{\mathbb P_n[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]}
      {\mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]},
 &\displaystyle \mathbb P_n[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]\ge A_a/4,\\[3mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\]
where \(\Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}\) clips to \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\).  Its mean squared error obeys
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \le
 C\min\!\left\{1,\frac1n a^{-2}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right)\right\}.
\]
The same smallness condition also gives
\[
\varepsilon_{1,n}\le \frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi}
\]
and the equivalent cumulant-parameterized bound
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \le
 C\min\!\left\{1,\frac1n\Delta_a^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}\right)\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

The proof is deferred to \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.

\Cref{obj:lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark} shows how the explicit sine reduction behaves as the treatment-noise law moves toward Gaussianity. The first zero is \(t_a=\pi/(2a)\), while the denominator scale \(A_a\) decays exponentially as \(a\) decreases. Expressing the same bound through \(\Delta_a=2a^4\) makes the dependence on fourth-cumulant magnitude explicit. The condition \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le1/2\) is exactly the treatment-code accuracy gate used by this pathwise calculation.

The section closes with a local benchmark statement. It combines the Gaussian--Rademacher path just described with a localized version of the published ACE oracle envelope from \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}. The sequence \((\delta_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}\) supplies the shrinking cumulant threshold, and the ACE clause evaluates the published bound after replacing the fixed separation level by the local value \(\Delta=\delta_n\).

\begin{theoremv}{thm:local-to-gaussian-partial-benchmarks}[Local Gaussian benchmarks]
Let \((\delta_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}\) be a real sequence with \(\delta_n>0\) for every \(n\), nonincreasing in \(n\), and \(\delta_n\to0\).  Fix \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\in\mathbb R\).  The following two assertions hold.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Gaussian--Rademacher path.)} There is a constant \(C>0\), depending only on \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\), such that the following holds.  Let \(p\) be any parameter record whose constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\) are the ones just fixed and whose orders are \(k=4\) and \(r=3\).  For any \(a\in(0,1]\) and any law \(P\) in a model \(m\), assume that the treatment noise satisfies
\[
\eta\sim \sqrt{1-a^2}\,G_{\circ}+aS,
\]
where \(G_{\circ}\sim N(0,1)\), \(S\) is symmetric Rademacher, and \(G_{\circ}\perp S\).  Assume also \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling,obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise,obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence,obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian}.  At the sample size \(n\) of \(p\), assume the supplied clipped treatment code \(\bar g_n\) satisfies
\[
x\mapsto |\bar g_n(x)-g_0(x)|\in L^1(P_X),
\qquad
\int |\bar g_n(x)-g_0(x)|\,dP_X(x)\le \varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
Then the conclusion of \cref{obj:lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark} — the explicit mean-squared-error bound along the Gaussian--Rademacher path — holds with constant \(C\) for \(p,m,a\).

\item \textbf{(Local ACE oracle envelope.)} For every published ACE handle, every \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), and every \(C_{\gamma}\in\mathbb R\), if the handle satisfies the conclusion of Theorem~5.4 of \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} at \((\gamma,C_{\gamma})\), then \(C_{\gamma}>0\) and the following local ACE statement holds.  For every parameter record \(p\) whose probability level is \(\gamma\), writing \(n\) for its sample size, if \(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\), then for every deterministic treatment code \(\widehat g\), deterministic outcome code \(\widehat q\), and every model, whenever
\[
\bar g_n=\Pi_{[-C_g,C_g]}\widehat g_n,
\qquad
\bar q_n=\Pi_{[-C_q,C_q]}\widehat q_n,
\]
and, writing \(r\) and \(k\) for the orders of \(p\) and \(\Delta=\delta_n\), the local ACE class conditions
\[
1\le n,
\qquad
|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge \Delta,
\]
hold together with \cref{obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise,obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence,obj:ass:theta-range,obj:ass:g-range,obj:ass:q-range,obj:ass:xi-subgaussian}, the treatment-noise exponential envelope
\[
o\mapsto \exp\{\eta(o)^2/\psi_{\eta}^2\}\in L^1(P),
\qquad
\int \exp\{\eta(o)^2/\psi_{\eta}^2\}\,dP(o)\le 2,
\]
and the nuisance-radius bounds
\[
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \varepsilon_{1,n},
\qquad
\|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \varepsilon_{2,n},
\]
and whenever the local JMS eligibility quantities
\[
a_{1,n}=2\log\!\left(6(C_g+\psi_{\eta})\varepsilon_{1,n}^{-1}\right),
\qquad
b_{1,n}=\log(\gamma n/9),
\]
\[
a_2=4(C_g+\psi_{\eta}),
\qquad
b_{2,n}(\Delta)=
\frac{200\min\{1,C_{\theta}\}\Delta}{\max\{\varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n},(\gamma n)^{-1/2}(\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta})\}}
\]
satisfy
\[
a_{1,n}\ne0,
\qquad
0<\frac{b_{1,n}}{a_{1,n}},
\qquad
0<a_2b_{2,n}(\Delta),
\qquad
a_2\log\{a_2b_{2,n}(\Delta)\}\ne0,
\]
and
\[
r\le
\min\left\{
\frac{b_{1,n}}{a_{1,n}}-a_{1,n}^{-1}\log\!\left(\frac{b_{1,n}}{a_{1,n}}\right),
\frac{b_{2,n}(\Delta)}{a_2\log\{a_2b_{2,n}(\Delta)\}}
\right\},
\]
the published order-\(r\) ACE estimator obeys
\[
Q_{P,1-\gamma}
\!\left(\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)\right|\right)
\le
B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}(\delta_n;C_{\gamma}),
\]
where
\[
B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}(\Delta;C_{\gamma})
=
C_{\gamma}\,r!\,16^r\,\Delta^{-1}
\left(
\varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
+C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
+64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
\bigl(r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\bigr)
(\gamma n)^{-1/2}
\right).
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:local-to-gaussian-partial-benchmarks} gives two procedure-specific local upper benchmarks. The Gaussian--Rademacher clause reuses \cref{obj:lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark} for the \(k=4,r=3\) path under the stated \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code condition. The ACE clause specializes the published order-\(r\) generalized-quantile guarantee of \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} to a local cumulant threshold \(\Delta=\delta_n\), with the displayed eligibility quantities and the resulting envelope \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}(\delta_n;C_{\gamma})\). The last algebraic clause records how an oracle upper bound that is valid under two sets of hypotheses can be summarized by the smaller of the two displayed upper inputs.

These local statements complement the fixed-separation results by making the dependence on the non-Gaussian signal explicit in tractable submodels. For the symmetric mixture, the sine denominator is bounded by a fixed positive scale and the risk is \(C/n\). Along the Gaussian--Rademacher path, the same sine construction has a denominator scale \(A_a\) and a cumulant magnitude \(\Delta_a\), yielding the explicit exponential dependence displayed in \cref{obj:lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark}. The localized ACE envelope gives the corresponding published finite-order benchmark under its stated eligibility and nuisance-radius conditions.


\section{Limitations and future work}

The fixed-separation results in \cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax,obj:thm:common-experiment-dichotomy} use a cumulant-separation constant that is fixed across the experiment. The local benchmarks in \cref{obj:thm:local-to-gaussian-partial-benchmarks} point to a complementary triangular-array program in which the non-Gaussian signal is allowed to shrink with the sample size. This section records that agenda separately from the fixed-\(\delta\) contour theorem and from the procedure-specific local upper bounds of the preceding section.

A useful starting point for that program is a finite, sample-facing object that stores the contour information available at a given library depth. The following definition names the empirical handle used to organize candidate contours, empirical winding information, contour moments, and angular variability.

\begin{definitionv}{def:local-gaussian-handle}[Local Gaussian handle \(\mathscr C_m\)]
Fix a parameter tuple with positive sample size \(n\), fixed ACE order \(r\ge2\), cumulant order \(k=r+1\), \(s\ge r\), \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), strictly positive \(C_\theta,C_g,C_q,\psi_\eta,\psi_\xi,\delta,\sigma\), and nonnegative nonincreasing sequences \(\epsilon_{1n}\) and \(\epsilon_{2n}\) on positive indices. Work on a measurable covariate space with observations \(O=(X,T,Y)\), and fix a probability law \(P\) with real functional \(\theta_0(P)\), measurable conditional means \(g_0(X)=E_P[T\mid X]\) and \(q_0(X)=E_P[Y\mid X]\), integrable \(T\) and \(Y\), and deterministic measurable supplied regression-code sequences for treatment and outcome.

For each library depth \(d\), let \(\mathscr C_d\) be the deterministic finite library of rational circles represented by the positive rational radii \(\rho=j+1\), \(0\le j<d\). For a realized sample indexed by \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), form the empirical transforms \(\widehat F_{a,n}\) and \(\widehat G_{a,n}\) on the prespecified deterministic fold \(a\in\{0,1\}\), using indices below \(n/2\) for \(a=0\) and indices at least \(n/2\) for \(a=1\). For a circle \(C\in\mathscr C_d\), \(C=\{\rho e^{it}:0\le t\le2\pi\}\), on which the empirical boundary modulus is certified positive, the candidate record consists of quantities definitionally computed from the empirical contour integrands
\[
 W_{a,C}(t)=\rho e^{it}
 \frac{\widehat F'_{a,n}(\rho e^{it})}
      {\widehat F_{a,n}(\rho e^{it})},
 \qquad
 V_{a,C}(t)=\rho e^{it}
 \frac{\widehat G_{a,n}(\rho e^{it})}
      {\widehat F_{a,n}(\rho e^{it})}.
\]
Specifically, the record contains a rational interval enclosure of the boundary modulus \(\inf_{z\in C}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)|\), a complex rational interval enclosure for \((2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}W_{a,C}(t)\,dt\), a complex rational interval enclosure for \((2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}V_{a,C}(t)\,dt\), and a rational interval enclosure of the angular variance
\[
 \widehat v_{a,C}
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
 \left|V_{a,C}(t)-
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}V_{a,C}(u)\,du\right|^2dt.
\]
Each enclosure is accompanied by its soundness certificate for the displayed empirical quantity, and the actual empirical transforms on the stated deterministic fold are the transform inputs of the schema. These records are candidate data for one realized sample, one deterministic inference fold, and one rational circle.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:local-gaussian-handle} provides a concrete data structure for studying adaptive contour selection in local regimes. It keeps the deterministic library, fold-specific empirical transforms, certified boundary separation, contour moments, and angular variance in one record. For shrinking cumulant separation, such records give a way to describe what a selector observes before choosing among contour-based procedures and more classical alternatives.

The open statistical question is naturally triangular. Let \((\delta_n)\) denote shrinking positive cumulant-separation thresholds. The fixed-separation theorem controls risk when the primitive separation constant is fixed, while the local benchmarks in \cref{obj:thm:local-to-gaussian-partial-benchmarks} describe particular upper envelopes when the non-Gaussian signal is weak. The remaining problem is to identify the sharp joint rate and the corresponding inference theory across the ordinary DML, finite-order ACE, and contour regimes \citep{ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML,NeweyRobins2018,MackeySyrgkanisZadik2018,JinMackeySyrgkanis2025,LeeMesters2024,HoeschLeeMesters2024}.

\begin{remarkv}{oeq:local-to-gaussian-frontier}[Local-to-Gaussian frontier]
A natural next question concerns triangular treatment-noise laws satisfying
\[
|\kappa_k(\eta_n)|\ge\delta_n
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
\delta_n\downarrow0
\]
under the same supplied code sequences. The target is a data-driven selector among ordinary DML, finite-order ACE, and global-contour procedures that attains the sharp minimax mean-squared-error rate as a function of
\[
(n,\varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n},\delta_n),
\]
supports uniformly valid inference across these regimes, and admits a matching local minimax lower bound. Future work could determine the sharp-rate functional, the corresponding uniform-inference criterion, and the local lower-bound construction for this frontier.
\end{remarkv}

\Cref{obj:oeq:local-to-gaussian-frontier} states the future-work target as an open research agenda rather than as an identification or risk theorem. Its rate arguments are the sample size, the two supplied-code radii, and the local cumulant-separation threshold. The agenda also includes a uniform-inference criterion and a local minimax lower-bound construction, so any completed theory would need to align estimation, coverage, selection, and lower-bound witnesses on the same triangular experiment.

This separation clarifies the scope of the paper's established results. The fixed-separation contour theorem gives the \(\Theta(n^{-1})\) MSE characterization under fixed cumulant separation and the stated treatment-code stability condition. The local mixture and ACE benchmarks give constructive upper comparisons under their own displayed hypotheses. The shrinking-separation program in \cref{obj:oeq:local-to-gaussian-frontier} is the natural next step for a unified adaptive theory across Gaussian-adjacent and fixed non-Gaussian regimes.

\section{Proofs for identification and analytic localization}

This appendix collects the analytic statements that support the identification argument in \cref{obj:thm:known-zero-instrument,obj:lem:observable-factorization,obj:thm:exact-contour-identification} and the fixed-bank localization used in \cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}. The common theme is that zeros of moment-generating functions can be controlled by classical complex-analytic arguments and then transferred to residualized-treatment transforms. The zero and completeness perspective is standard in identification arguments based on transforms \citep{KaganLinnikRao1973,Mattner1992}, and the finite-bank construction uses the same analytic geometry that underlies modern zero-localization bounds for moment-generating functions \citep{MichelenSahasrabudhe2019,EremenkoFryntov2021,DinhGhoshTranTran2021}.

For \cref{obj:thm:known-zero-instrument}, the analytic order of a zero of \(M\) determines which polynomial-exponential weight annihilates shifted treatment innovations. Independence of the treatment innovation from \(X\), as imposed in \cref{obj:ass:independent-treatment-noise}, then turns the shifted identity into a conditional moment for the code residual \(Z_n\). The denominator calculation is the corresponding differentiated transform identity, and the partially linear conditional-mean restriction in \cref{obj:ass:outcome-mean-independence} supplies the numerator relation.

The factorization in \cref{obj:lem:observable-factorization} isolates the effect of treatment-code contamination. Since \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\), independence separates the residual transform into the treatment-innovation transform and the nuisance transform. The outcome-weighted transform has the derivative term carrying \(\theta_0\) plus the covariate-only contamination transform from \cref{obj:def:outcome-contamination}. This decomposition is the algebraic input for \cref{obj:thm:exact-contour-identification}: on a contour where the residual transform is separated from zero and the nuisance transform has no zero on the closed disk, the logarithmic-derivative contribution counts the enclosed zeros and the analytic contamination contribution integrates to zero.

The remaining analytic step is uniform localization. \Cref{obj:lem:zero-localization} gives a disk, determined by the fixed cumulant order, sub-Gaussian scale, and separation constant, that contains at least one zero of the treatment-innovation transform. The finite-bank statement below turns that disk-level information into a deterministic list of circles, with explicit dyadic bookkeeping and a positive boundary-modulus certificate. Its role is to provide the law-blind contour geometry used later by the empirical statistic.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:finite-contour-bank}[Finite contour bank]
Let \(p\) be a primitive parameter record with \(k=r+1\), \(r\ge2\), \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), \(s\ge r\), positive constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},\delta,\sigma\), and nonnegative nonincreasing code-radius sequences \(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\). Let \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) be a certified-bank input record for \(p\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle}, so that
\[
R_0=A_k\left(\frac{\psi_{\eta}^2}{\delta}\right)^{(k-2)^{-1}},
\qquad
R_1=R_0+1 .
\]
For any sample size \(n\ge1\), any model \(m\) with observed-data law \(P\), and any membership certificate \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) as defined in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}, form the bank data \(B\) of \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle} for \(p\) and \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\). Then \(a_{\star}>0\), and the components of \(B\) satisfy
\[
N_{\mathrm{cert}}=4U_{\psi}^{2}(U_R+2)^3,\qquad
m_{\star}=N_{\mathrm{cert}}+3,\qquad
J=2^{m_{\star}-1}+1,
\]
\[
d_{\star}=\left(\frac12\right)^{m_{\star}},\qquad
h_{\star}=\frac{d_{\star}}{3},\qquad
e_{\star}=8U_RU_{\psi}^{2}(U_R+1)^2,
\]
\[
d_{\star}^{\mathrm{den}}=3(2U_R+1),\qquad
u_{\star}=2e_{\star}+N_{\mathrm{cert}}\bigl(m_{\star}+d_{\star}^{\mathrm{den}}\bigr),\qquad
a_{\star,\mathbb Q}=\left(\frac12\right)^{u_{\star}}.
\]
The bank radii are strictly increasing and lie in the search annulus:
\[
R_0<\rho_j<R_1
\quad\text{for every }j\in\{1,\ldots,J\}.
\]
Writing \(C_j=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=\rho_j\}\) and \(M(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}]\) for the treatment-innovation moment-generating function, there exists an index \(j\in\{1,\ldots,J\}\) such that
\[
N_{C_j}(M)\ge1
\quad\text{and}\quad
a_{\star}\le |M(z)|\ \text{for every }z\in C_j .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:finite-contour-bank}]
Let \(B\) be the bank produced from \((p,\mathfrak p_\star)\).  The construction in \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle} gives
\[
N_{\mathrm{cert}}=4U_\psi^2(U_R+2)^3,\qquad
m_\star=N_{\mathrm{cert}}+3,\qquad
J=2^{m_\star-1}+1,
\]
\[
d_\star=2^{-m_\star},\qquad h_\star=d_\star/3,
\]
\[
e_\star=8U_RU_\psi^2(U_R+1)^2,\qquad
d_\star^{\mathrm{den}}=3(2U_R+1),
\]
and
\[
u_\star=2e_\star+N_{\mathrm{cert}}(m_\star+d_\star^{\mathrm{den}}),
\qquad
a_\star=2^{-u_\star}.
\]
Thus \(a_\star>0\) and the displayed bookkeeping identities hold. % lean: finite_contour_bank

The radii have the explicit form
\[
\rho_j=R_0+\frac14+j\,2^{-m_\star},
\qquad 0\le j\le 2^{m_\star-1}.
\]
Hence \(j\mapsto\rho_j\) is strictly increasing.  Since
\[
\frac14+j2^{-m_\star}\le \frac14+2^{m_\star-1}2^{-m_\star}=\frac34<1,
\]
and \(R_1=R_0+1\), each bank radius satisfies
\[
R_0<\rho_j<R_1.
\]
% lean: finite_contour_bank

It remains to prove the treatment-noise certificate.  The error-one certified refinements and the ceiling construction give
\[
\psi_\eta\le U_\psi,\qquad R_1\le U_R.
\]
Let
\[
M(z)=E_Pe^{z\eta},\qquad R_{\mathrm{fac}}=R_1+1,
\qquad R_{\mathrm{out}}=R_1+2.
\]
The conditional-mean identity for \(g_0\) centers \(\eta\).  The treatment-tail condition in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), together with \cref{obj:lem:luxemburg-mgf-envelope}, gives for every \(t\in\mathbb R\)
\[
E_P e^{t\eta}\le \exp(4\psi_\eta^2t^2).
\]
The same real exponential integrability gives the entire complex moment-generating function, normalization at the origin follows from the probability law, and the complex modulus estimate yields
\[
M(0)=1,\qquad M\text{ is entire},\qquad
|M(z)|\le \exp(4\psi_\eta^2|z|^2)
\quad (z\in\mathbb C).
\]
% lean: treatmentMGF_entire_normalized_bound

Let
\[
N_0=N_{\{|z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\}}(M)
\]
be the number of zeros of \(M\) in the open disk of radius \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), counted with multiplicity.  Jensen's zero-counting bound applied on \(R_{\mathrm{out}}\) gives
\[
N_0\log\!\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{out}}}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}}\right)
\le 4\psi_\eta^2R_{\mathrm{out}}^2.
\]
% lean: jensen_zeroMultiplicityCount_bound
Since \(R_{\mathrm{out}}=R_{\mathrm{fac}}+1\),
\[
\frac1{R_{\mathrm{out}}}
\le
\log\!\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{out}}}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}}\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
N_0
\le 4\psi_\eta^2R_{\mathrm{out}}^3
\le 4U_\psi^2(U_R+2)^3
=N_{\mathrm{cert}}.
\]
% lean: finite_contour_bank

List the \(N_0\) zeros in \(\{|z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\}\), with multiplicity, as
\[
a_1,\ldots,a_{N_0}.
\]
The finite Blaschke factorization on the disk of radius \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\) gives a holomorphic function \(g\), zero-free in \(\{|z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\}\), such that
\[
M(z)=Q(z)g(z),\qquad |z|\le R_{\mathrm{fac}},
\]
where
\[
Q(z)=
\prod_{\nu=1}^{N_0}
\frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\nu)}
     {R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline{a_\nu}z}.
\]
% lean: exists_complete_pointwise_blaschke_factorization

The grid spacing is \(d_\star\), and the mesh identities give \(d_\star>0\), \(h_\star=d_\star/3\), and \(2h_\star<d_\star\).  Thus one listed zero modulus \(|a_\nu|\) can be within distance \(<h_\star\) of at most one grid radius: two distinct grid radii are separated by at least \(d_\star\), while two distances \(<h_\star\) would put them less than \(2h_\star\) apart.  Also
\[
N_0\le N_{\mathrm{cert}}<2^{N_{\mathrm{cert}}+2}+1
=2^{m_\star-1}+1=J.
\]
The pigeonhole argument for this separated dyadic grid gives an index \(j\) such that
\[
h_\star\le \bigl||a_\nu|-\rho_j\bigr|
\qquad (1\le \nu\le N_0).
\]
% lean: exists_translated_grid_radius_separated

By \cref{obj:lem:zero-localization}, \(M\) has a zero \(z_0\) with \(|z_0|\le R_0\).  Since \(R_0<\rho_j\), this zero lies strictly inside \(C_j=\{|z|=\rho_j\}\).  If \(M\) had a zero \(z\) on \(C_j\), then \(z\in\{|z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\}\), and the factorization \(M=Qg\) together with the zero-freeness of \(g\) would force \(z=a_\nu\) for some \(\nu\).  This would give
\[
\bigl||a_\nu|-\rho_j\bigr|=0<h_\star,
\]
contradicting the separation of the chosen grid point.  Hence the contour has positive zero count:
\[
N_{C_j}(M)\ge1.
\]
% lean: zeroMultiplicityCount_pos_of_exists_zero

It remains to lower-bound \(|M|\) on this circle.  Put
\[
A=4\psi_\eta^2R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2.
\]
On \(|w|=R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), the Blaschke product has \(|Q(w)|=1\), so the boundary envelope for \(M\) gives \(|g(w)|\le e^A\).  Also \(M(0)=1\) and \(|Q(0)|\le1\), hence \(|g(0)|\ge1\).  Applying Harnack's inequality to the positive harmonic function \(A-\log|g|\) gives, for every \(|z|\le R_1\),
\[
\exp\!\left\{-\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+R_1}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}-R_1}-1\right)A\right\}|Q(z)|
\le |M(z)|.
\]
Since \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}=R_1+1\),
\[
\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+R_1}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}-R_1}-1\right)A
=2R_1\cdot 4\psi_\eta^2(R_1+1)^2
=8\psi_\eta^2R_1(R_1+1)^2.
\]
Thus, for every \(|z|\le R_1\),
\[
\exp\{-8\psi_\eta^2R_1(R_1+1)^2\}\,|Q(z)|
\le |M(z)|.
\]
% lean: norm_lower_of_blaschke_factorization
Since
\[
8\psi_\eta^2R_1(R_1+1)^2\le
8U_RU_\psi^2(U_R+1)^2=e_\star,
\]
the elementary dyadic-exponential comparison \(2^{-2E}\le e^{-E}\) for natural \(E\), applied to \(E=e_\star\), gives
\[
2^{-2e_\star}
\le \exp\{-8\psi_\eta^2R_1(R_1+1)^2\}.
\]
% lean: dyadic_two_mul_le_exp_neg

Now take \(z\in C_j\).  For each zero \(a_\nu\),
\[
|z-a_\nu|
\ge \bigl||z|-|a_\nu|\bigr|
=\bigl|\rho_j-|a_\nu|\bigr|
\ge h_\star.
\]
Also \(|z|\le R_1\), and therefore
\[
R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|\le 2R_1+1\le 2U_R+1.
\]
For each Blaschke factor,
\[
\left|
\frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\nu)}
     {R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline{a_\nu}z}
\right|
\ge
\frac{|z-a_\nu|}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|}
\ge
\frac{h_\star}{2U_R+1}
=
\frac{d_\star}{d_\star^{\mathrm{den}}}.
\]
% lean: norm_blaschkeFactor_ge
Multiplying over the at most \(N_{\mathrm{cert}}\) factors and using
\((d_\star^{\mathrm{den}})^{-1}\ge2^{-d_\star^{\mathrm{den}}}\) gives
\[
|Q(z)|
\ge
2^{-N_{\mathrm{cert}}(m_\star+d_\star^{\mathrm{den}})}.
\]
% lean: dyadic_product_certificate

Combining the last two lower bounds,
\[
|M(z)|
\ge
2^{-2e_\star}\,
2^{-N_{\mathrm{cert}}(m_\star+d_\star^{\mathrm{den}})}
=
2^{-u_\star}
=a_\star.
\]
Thus for this bank index \(j\),
\[
a_\star\le |M(z)|\qquad (z\in C_j),
\]
and the asserted uniform treatment-noise certificate follows. % lean: finite_contour_bank
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:finite-contour-bank} is the population geometric certificate used by the empirical selector in \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}. The proof below records the dyadic bookkeeping and the zero-localization argument behind that certificate.


\section{Empirical process, stability, and lower-bound lemmas}

This appendix collects the auxiliary statistical statements used by the fixed-separation contour theorem. The first two results control the analytic transforms that enter the contour statistic: one gives uniform \(L^2\) concentration of the empirical residual and outcome transforms on a fixed disk, and the other gives a deterministic \(L^1(P_X)\) stability bound for the nuisance factor. Together they supply the stochastic and population inputs behind the contour-risk bounds in \cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}. The concentration statement uses the same sub-Gaussian scale conventions and product-sample structure as \cref{obj:def:model-parameters,obj:ass:iid-sampling}, in line with standard empirical-process and high-dimensional probability arguments \citep{VanDerVaart1998,VanDerVaartWellner1996,Vershynin2018,Wainwright2019}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}[Uniform transform variance]
Fix real numbers \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\) and \(R_1\). There is a constant \(K>0\) such that the following holds. Let \(p\) be a parameter record with
\[
p.C_{\theta}=C_{\theta},\qquad
p.C_g=C_g,\qquad

and sample size \(n=p.n\ge2\). Let \(m\) be a model in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), as in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}, and let \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) be sampled under the i.i.d. product law \(P^{\otimes n}\), as in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}. \begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}]

\[
A_F=2\exp\{8R_1C_g+4R_1^2\psi_\eta^2\},

\[
A_G=

+

and define

The constants \(A_F\) and \(A_G\) are strictly positive, so \(K>0\); it
depends only on the displayed primitive constants and \(R_1\). % lean: empiricalTransformL2Constant empiricalTransformL2Constant_pos

Fix a fold for which \(I_a\) is nonempty, and let \(I=I_a\),
\(m_I=|I|\), and \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\). Since the contour-bank radius in
\cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle} satisfies \(R_1=R_0+1\) with
\(R_0\ge0\), we have \(R_1>0\). Write
\(D_n(X)=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\), so \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\). The range bound for
\(g_0\) in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} and the clipping of
\(\bar g_n\) in \cref{obj:def:plm-model} give \(|D_n(X)|\le 2C_g\) almost

\[
4R_1|Z_n|
\le 4R_1|\eta|+8R_1C_g
\le \frac{\eta^2}{\psi_\eta^2}+4R_1^2\psi_\eta^2+8R_1C_g,
\]
where the second inequality is the Young inequality obtained by expanding
\((|\eta|/\psi_\eta-2R_1\psi_\eta)^2\ge0\). Therefore
\[
\{\exp(2R_1|Z_n|)\}^2
\le
\exp\{8R_1C_g+4R_1^2\psi_\eta^2\}
\exp(\eta^2/\psi_\eta^2).
\]
Integrating and using the Luxemburg treatment-tail bound from
\cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} yields
\[
E_P\left[\exp\{2R_1|Z_n|\}^2\right]
\le A_F.
\]
% lean: learnedResidual_exp_abs_sq_lintegral_le

The outcome-weighted envelope is

almost every observation, the algebraic bound

\le

\(|q_0(X)|\le C_q\) almost surely and \(|\theta_0|\le C_\theta\), to give
\[
|Y|^4
\le
64\{C_q^4+C_\theta^4|\eta|^4+|\xi|^4\}

almost surely. The Luxemburg moment bounds give
\(E_P|\eta|^4\le4\psi_\eta^4\) and \(E_P|\xi|^4\le4\psi_\xi^4\), and hence
\[
E_P|Y|^4
\le
64\{C_q^4+4C_\theta^4\psi_\eta^4+4\psi_\xi^4\}.

Together with

\{|Y|\exp(2R_1|Z_n|)\}^2
\le
|Y|^4+
\{\exp(4R_1|Z_n|)\}^2,
\]
and the preceding residual envelope applied at radius \(2R_1\), this gives the
stated bound. % lean: outcome_exp_abs_sq_lintegral_le

We use the following coefficient-series bound. Let \(J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\)
be a nonempty deterministic finite subset of sample indices, and write
\(m_J=|J|\). Let \(U,V\) be measurable real functions, let \(R>0\), let
\(C\ge0\), and suppose
\[
E_P\left[\{|U|\exp(2R|V|)\}^2\right]\le C^2.
\]
Define
\[
c_{J,k}(O_1,\ldots,O_n)
=
m_J^{-1}\sum_{i\in J}
\left\{
\frac{U(O_i)V(O_i)^k}{k!}
-
E_P\frac{UV^k}{k!}
\right\}.
\]
Then, under the i.i.d. product law for \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\),
\[
E\left[
\sup_{|z|\le R}
\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_{J,k} z^k\right|^2
\right]
\le
\left(\frac{2C}{\sqrt{m_J}}\right)^2.
\]
The envelope gives
\[
\left\|
\frac{UV^k}{k!}
\right\|_{L^2(P)}
\le
\frac{C}{(2R)^k},
\]
because \(x^k/k!\le e^{2Rx}/(2R)^k\) for \(x\ge0\); the hypotheses
\(R>0\) and \(C\ge0\) make the displayed majorant nonnegative. Under the
product sample law, centering over the block \(J\) gives
\[
\|c_{J,k}\|_{L^2(P^{\otimes n})}
\le
\frac{C}{(2R)^k\sqrt{m_J}}.
\]
The series of majorants is summable, and the centered analytic-series supremum
bound yields
\[
\left(
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{C}{(2R)^k\sqrt{m_J}}R^k
\right)^2
=
\left(\frac{2C}{\sqrt{m_J}}\right)^2.
\]

\[
m_J^{-1}\sum_{i\in J}U(O_i)e^{zV(O_i)}
-
E_P[Ue^{zV}]
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{J,k} z^k,
\]
with the exchange of summation, finite averaging, and integration justified by
the doubled exponential envelope. % lean: weighted_exp_integral_eq_moment_tsum weighted_empirical_sub_eq_centered_factorial_series

Apply the preceding bound first with the block \(J=I_a\), \(U\equiv1\),
\(V=Z_n\), \(R=R_1\), and \(C=\sqrt{A_F}\). Since \(A_F>0\), this choice has
\(C\ge0\), and

E\left[
\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|^2
\right]
\le
\frac{4A_F}{m_I}.
\]
% lean: empirical_transform_uniform_l2_explicit

Apply it again with the block \(J=I_a\), \(U=Y\), \(V=Z_n\), \(R=R_1\), and
\(C=\sqrt{A_G}\). Since \(A_G>0\), this choice has \(C\ge0\), and
\[
E\left[
\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|^2
\right]
\le
\frac{4A_G}{m_I}.
\]
% lean: empirical_transform_uniform_l2_explicit

Adding the two displays yields

E\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|^2\right]
+
E\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|^2\right]
\le
\frac{4(A_F+A_G)}{|I_a|}

\frac{K}{|I_a|}.

This proves the assertion. % lean: empirical_transform_uniform_l2

The next statement is the population stability input. It uses the direct \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code radius in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} to keep the nuisance transform close to one on the contour search region.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}[Zero-free nuisance factor]
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) satisfying \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}, the associated treatment-code discrepancy \(D_n\) obeys
\[
 |D_n|\le 2C_g\quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad
 E_P|D_n|\le \varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
Consequently, for the corresponding factor \(H_n\) and radius \(R_1\),
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|H_n(z)-1|
 \le R_1 e^{2C_gR_1}\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le (4R_1 e^{2C_gR_1})^{-1}\), then \(|H_n(z)|\ge 3/4\) for every \(|z|\le R_1\), and \(F_n\) and \(M\) have the same zeros in \(\{|z|\le R_1\}\), counted with multiplicity.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}]
Let
\[
D_n(X)=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X),
\qquad
H_n(z)=E_P[e^{zD_n(X)}].
\]
Since \(|g_0(X)|\le C_g\) almost surely and \(|\bar g_n(X)|\le C_g\) by
clipping,
\[
|D_n(X)|\le |g_0(X)|+|\bar g_n(X)|\le2C_g
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
% lean: l1_nuisance_zero_free

The treatment-code condition in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) gives
\[
E_P|D_n(X)|
=
\int |g_0(x)-\bar g_n(x)|\,dP_X(x)
\le
\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
% lean: l1_nuisance_zero_free

For \(|z|\le R_1\), the exponential remainder bound gives, with
\(w=zD_n(X)\),
\[
|e^{zD_n(X)}-1|
\le
|z|\,|D_n(X)|\,e^{|z|\,|D_n(X)|}.
\]
Together with \(|D_n(X)|\le2C_g\), this yields
\[
|H_n(z)-1|
\le
E_P|e^{zD_n(X)}-1|
\le
R_1 e^{2C_gR_1}E_P|D_n(X)|
\le
R_1 e^{2C_gR_1}\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(|z|\le R_1\) gives the displayed uniform bound. % lean: l1_nuisance_zero_free

Assume
\[
\varepsilon_{1,n}\le (4R_1e^{2C_gR_1})^{-1}.
\]
Then the preceding display gives \(|H_n(z)-1|\le1/4\) throughout the disk, and
therefore
\[
|H_n(z)|\ge 1-|H_n(z)-1|\ge3/4
\qquad(|z|\le R_1).
\]
% lean: l1_nuisance_zero_free

It remains to record why the multiplicity comparison is legitimate at each
point of the disk.  The almost-sure bound \(|D_n(X)|\le2C_g\) implies that
\(E_P\exp\{tD_n(X)\}<\infty\) for every real \(t\), so the complex MGF
\(H_n\) is analytic in a neighborhood of every \(|z|\le R_1\).  The treatment-tail
condition in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), under the Luxemburg convention of
\cref{obj:def:model-parameters}, gives the same local exponential-moment
condition for \(\eta\): for every real \(t\), completing the square bounds
\(\exp\{t\eta\}\) by a constant multiple of
\(\exp\{\eta^2/\psi_\eta^2\}\), which is integrable.  Hence the treatment-noise
MGF \(M(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}]\) is analytic in a neighborhood of every point of the
disk as well. % lean: l1_nuisance_zero_free

By \cref{obj:lem:observable-factorization},
\[
F_n(z)=M(z)H_n(z).
\]
Fix \(|z|\le R_1\).  The preceding lower bound gives \(H_n(z)\ne0\), so the
analytic order of \(H_n\) at \(z\) is zero.  Applying additivity of analytic
order to the displayed factorization gives
\[
\operatorname{ord}_z(F_n)
=
\operatorname{ord}_z(M)+\operatorname{ord}_z(H_n)
=
\operatorname{ord}_z(M).
\]
Thus \(F_n\) and \(M\) have exactly the same zeros in \(\{|z|\le R_1\}\), counted
with multiplicity. % lean: residualMGF_eq_treatmentMGF_mul_nuisanceMGF analyticOrderAt_mul l1_nuisance_zero_free
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free} is the deterministic transfer from treatment-transform zeros to observable residual-transform zeros. The proof below uses the clipping bound and the \(L^1(P_X)\) radius to control the nuisance factor \(H_n\) on the search disk.

A separate tail calculation is used when Gaussian outcome innovations are embedded in lower-bound and benchmark paths. The following elementary scale bound matches the Luxemburg convention fixed in \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:gaussian-exponential-square-quarter-scale}[Gaussian square exponential bound]
Use the Gaussian-law notation of \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}. For every positive scale \(\psi>0\), let \(\zeta\sim N(0,(\psi/4)^2)\). Then \(\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)\) is integrable under the law of \(\zeta\), and
\[
\mathbb E\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)\le 2 .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:gaussian-exponential-square-quarter-scale}]
Let
\[
v=\left(\frac{\psi}{4}\right)^2.
\]
Since \(\psi>0\), this variance is positive.  Under \(N(0,v)\), the density identity is, for every \(x\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2v}\right)
\exp\!\left(\frac{x^2}{\psi^2}\right)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{7x^2}{\psi^2}\right),
\]
because \(1/(2v)=8/\psi^2\). % lean: gaussian_exp_sq_integrable_and_integral_le_two

Since \(7/\psi^2>0\), the function \(x\mapsto\exp\{-7x^2/\psi^2\}\) is integrable on \(\mathbb R\).  The density identity therefore transfers this integrability to \(x\mapsto\exp(x^2/\psi^2)\) under \(N(0,v)\), so \(\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)\) is integrable under the law of \(\zeta\). % lean: integrable_exp_neg_mul_sq

For the expectation, the same density identity rewrites the Gaussian-law integral as
\[
\mathbb E\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}
\int_{\mathbb R}\exp\!\left(-\frac{7x^2}{\psi^2}\right)\,dx.
\]
The Gaussian integral formula applied with coefficient \(7/\psi^2\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{7/\psi^2}}.
\]
% lean: integral_gaussian

Finally,
\[
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{7/\psi^2}}
\le
2\sqrt{2\pi v},
\]
since after squaring both sides this is
\[
\frac{\pi\psi^2}{7}\le 8\pi v=\frac{\pi\psi^2}{2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)\le2.
\]
% lean: gaussian_exp_sq_integrable_and_integral_le_two
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:gaussian-exponential-square-quarter-scale} is the tail-scale normalization used in the lower-bound construction below.

The final auxiliary result supplies the decision-theoretic lower-bound path. It constructs one-dimensional submodels in which the observed treatment law, regression functions, and supplied clipped codes remain fixed while the coefficient varies locally at \(n^{-1/2}\) scale. Such paths are the standard input for parametric lower bounds in semiparametric experiments \citep{BickelKlaassenRitovWellner1993,VanDerVaart1998}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}[One-dimensional hard submodel]
Fix \(r\in\mathbb N\) and real constants
\(\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\) with \(\psi_{\xi}>0\). There exist constants
\(c_0,C_0,\tau,c_{\mathrm{ACE}}\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
0<c_0,\qquad 0<C_0,\qquad 0<\tau\le \psi_{\xi},\qquad 0<c_{\mathrm{ACE}} .
\]
For every parameter record \(p\) whose constants
\(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\) are the ones just fixed, and every \(n\ge 1\), if the non-Gaussian class in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} is nonempty, then there are a base law \(P^{\circ}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and a family \(\{P_{n,\vartheta}:|\vartheta|\le c_0\}\) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Class membership and target.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), \(P_{n,\vartheta}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and \(\theta_0(P_{n,\vartheta})=\vartheta\).
\item \textbf{(Fixed observed treatment law.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the law of \((X,T)\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}\) equals its law under \(P^{\circ}\).
\item \textbf{(Fixed regressions and supplied codes.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the functions \(g_0,q_0\) and the clipped supplied codes \(\bar g_n,\bar q_n\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}\) equal those under \(P^{\circ}\).
\item \textbf{(Gaussian outcome innovation.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the outcome innovation \(\xi=Y-q_0(X)-\theta_0\eta\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}\) has law \(N(0,\tau^2)\) and is independent of \((X,T)\).
\item \textbf{(Outcome equation.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\),
\[
Y=q_0(X)+\vartheta\,\eta+\xi
\]
almost surely under \(P_{n,\vartheta}\).
\item \textbf{(Product relative entropy.)} For all real \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1,h\) with \(|\vartheta_0|\le c_0\), \(|\vartheta_1|\le c_0\), and
\[
|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=\frac{h}{\sqrt n},
\]
one has, with \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}\) the Kullback--Leibler divergence,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n}\,\middle\|\,P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n}\right)
\le C_0 h^2 .
\]
\end{itemize}
Moreover, for every starting law \(P^{\circ}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) in the class of \cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class}, there is a family \(\{P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}:|\vartheta|\le c_0\}\) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(JMS ACE membership and target.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) and \(\theta_0(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}})=\vartheta\).
\item \textbf{(Fixed observed treatment law.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the law of \((X,T)\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) equals its law under \(P^{\circ}\).
\item \textbf{(Fixed nuisance objects.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the functions \(g_0,q_0\), the clipped supplied codes \(\bar g_n,\bar q_n\), and the treatment innovation \(\eta\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) equal those under \(P^{\circ}\), and the treatment and outcome code-radius conditions in \cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class} hold at \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\).
\item \textbf{(Gaussian outcome innovation.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), the outcome innovation \(\xi=Y-q_0(X)-\theta_0\eta\) under \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) has law \(N(0,\tau^2)\) and is independent of \((X,T)\).
\item \textbf{(Outcome equation.)} For every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\),
\[
Y=q_0(X)+\vartheta\,\eta+\xi
\]
almost surely under \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\).
\item \textbf{(Product relative entropy.)} For all real \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1,h\) with \(|\vartheta_0|\le c_0\), \(|\vartheta_1|\le c_0\), and
\[
|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=\frac{h}{\sqrt n},
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left((P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\mathrm{ACE}})^{\otimes n}\,\middle\|\,(P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\mathrm{ACE}})^{\otimes n}\right)
\le C_0 h^2 .
\]
\end{itemize}
Finally, for every pair of clipped-code functions \(g_{\star},q_{\star}:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\), if there is a law \(P^{\circ}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) with \(\bar g_n=g_{\star}\) and \(\bar q_n=q_{\star}\), then the fixed-code JMS ACE minimax risk satisfies
\[
\frac{c_{\mathrm{ACE}}}{n}
\le
\inf_{\widetilde\theta(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\bar g_n,\bar q_n)}
\sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}:\\ \bar g_n=g_{\star},\ \bar q_n=q_{\star}}}
E_P\!\left[\{\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P)\}^2\right].
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}]
Set
\[
c_0=
\begin{cases}
\min\{C_{\theta},1\},& C_{\theta}>0,\\
1,& C_{\theta}\le 0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\tau=\frac{\psi_{\xi}}4,
\qquad
C_0=\max\left\{1,\frac{16\psi_{\eta}^2}{\psi_{\xi}^2}\right\}.
\]
Then \(c_0>0\), \(C_0>0\), and \(0<\tau\le\psi_{\xi}\).  For any admissible choice of parameters whose displayed constants agree with the fixed constants, \cref{obj:def:model-parameters} gives \(C_{\theta}>0\), and hence \(c_0=\min\{C_{\theta},1\}\le C_{\theta}\). % lean: non_gaussian_hard_submodel

The two-point testing bound at relative-entropy budget \(C_0c_0^2\) supplies a constant \(c_{\mathrm{LC}}>0\), depending only on that budget, such that whenever two \(n\)-sample laws have relative entropy at most \(C_0c_0^2\), every measurable sample statistic \(\widehat\alpha\) with finite quadratic risks satisfies
\[
c_{\mathrm{LC}}(\theta_1-\theta_0)^2
\le
\max\left\{
E_0[(\widehat\alpha-\theta_0)^2],
E_1[(\widehat\alpha-\theta_1)^2]
\right\}.
\]
The minimax reduction applies this bound after symmetrically clipping an arbitrary estimator to a range containing both target values.  Define
\[
c_{\mathrm{ACE}}=c_{\mathrm{LC}}c_0^2>0 .
\]
% lean: minimaxRiskOn_two_point_lower

Fix now an admissible choice of parameters \(p\) with the displayed primitive constants and a class index \(n\ge1\).  Assume first that \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) is nonempty, and choose \(P_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), represented by a base model \(m_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ\).  For each \(\vartheta\in\mathbb R\), define \(P_{n,\vartheta}\) by preserving the joint law of \((X,T)\) under \(P_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ\), drawing an independent
\[
\xi_\star\sim N(0,\tau^2),
\]
and setting
\[
Y=q_0(X)+\vartheta\{T-g_0(X)\}+\xi_\star .
\]
Equivalently, the path is the product of the retained \((X,T)\)-law and the centered Gaussian innovation, transported through this affine outcome map. % lean: affineGaussianLaw; affineGaussianModel

Along this path,
\[
\theta_0(P_{n,\vartheta})=\vartheta,
\qquad
g_0(P_{n,\vartheta})=g_0(P_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ),
\qquad
q_0(P_{n,\vartheta})=q_0(P_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ),
\]
and the clipped supplied codes satisfy
\[
\bar g_n(P_{n,\vartheta})=\bar g_n(P_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ),
\qquad
\bar q_n(P_{n,\vartheta})=\bar q_n(P_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ).
\]
The covariate-treatment marginal is also retained:
\[
(P_{n,\vartheta})_{X,T}=(P_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ)_{X,T}.
\]
% lean: affineGaussianModel_theta0; affineGaussianModel_g0; affineGaussianModel_q0; affineGaussianModel_map_xt

The treatment innovation is unchanged along the path:
\[
\eta_{P_{n,\vartheta}}=\eta_{P_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ}.
\]
The outcome innovation is the fresh Gaussian coordinate:
\[

so

\qquad
\xi_{P_{n,\vartheta}}\perp (X,T).

% lean: affineGaussianModel_eta; affineGaussianModel_map_xi; affineGaussianModel_indep_xi_xt

All treatment-side assumptions, the regression ranges, the cumulant separation, and the treatment \(L^1(P_X)\) code-radius condition are inherited from \(P_{\mathrm{NG}}^\circ\).  The conditional mean condition follows from the displayed outcome equation, the independence of \(\xi_\star\) from \((X,T)\), and its centering. \exp\{\xi_{P_{n,\vartheta}}^2/\psi_\xi^2\}\in L^1(P_{n,\vartheta}),

\le 2
\quad P_{n,\vartheta}\text{-a.s.}

\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

The same construction gives the asserted outcome equation:

% lean: non_gaussian_hard_submodel

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}\|P_{n,\vartheta_1})

The moment bound used here is the Luxemburg consequence

n\,\frac{(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0)^2\,2\psi_{\eta}^2}{2\tau^2}

\le C_0h^2 .

Thus the required entropy display holds. % lean: affineGaussianModel_iid_kl_le; non_gaussian_hard_submodel

and

% lean: affineGaussianModel_theta0; affineGaussianModel_map_xt; affineGaussianModel_eta

Consequently the two JMS ACE code-radius inequalities are transported unchanged:

\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n},
\qquad
\|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}.

Together with the transported treatment-side and range conditions, the coefficient bound \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\le C_{\theta}\), and the Gaussian conditional Luxemburg calculation above, this gives

|\vartheta|\le c_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P_{n,\vartheta}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\in

\]
whenever \(|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=h/\sqrt n\). % lean: affineGaussianModel_jmsAceClass_quarter; affineGaussianModel_iid_kl_le

\qquad
\vartheta_+=\frac{c_0}{2\sqrt n},

and let \(P_-:=P_{n,\vartheta_-}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\), \(P_+:=P_{n,\vartheta_+}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\), using \(P_\star^\circ\) as the starting law.  Since \(n\ge1\),

|\vartheta_-|\le c_0,
\qquad |\vartheta_+|\le c_0,
\qquad
|\vartheta_+-\vartheta_-|=\frac{c_0}{\sqrt n}.
\]

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_-^{\otimes n}\|P_+^{\otimes n})
\le C_0c_0^2.

\end{proof}

\section{Certified construction and executable correspondence}

This appendix records the finite arithmetic contract behind the contour statistic in \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}. The statistical arguments in the main text use ordinary empirical transforms and contour ratios; the material here specifies a rational interval substrate, a deterministic contour-bank record, and the deterministic perturbation statement that connects a selected represented contour calculation to the population ratio. The construction follows the computable-analysis and interval-arithmetic convention that real inputs are accessed through nested rational enclosures with effective moduli \citep{Weihrauch2000,Ko1991,Moore1966}.

The first definition fixes the elementary arithmetic layer. It gives represented real and positive-real records, complex rectangles, guarded division, elementary functions on bounded intervals, circle-node construction, and certified mesh integration. These operations are the only numerical primitives used by the represented contour program.

\begin{definitionv}{def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate}[Certified contour arithmetic substrate \(\mathsf{CCIA}_{\mathrm{build}}\)]
The bounded-build specification \(\mathsf{CCIA}_{\mathrm{build}}\) consists of the following finite rational arithmetic operations and their stated enclosure properties.

A represented real number \(x\) is given by rational closed intervals \(I_m=[I_m^-,I_m^+]\) and an executable modulus \(\mu:\mathbb Q_{>0}\to\mathbb N\) such that
\[
x\in I_{m+1}\subseteq I_m,
\qquad
I_{\mu(e)}^+-I_{\mu(e)}^-\le e .
\]
A positive represented real number additionally supplies a rational lower bound \(p>0\) with \(p\le x\). The executable data are the interval sequence, the modulus, and the positive lower bound.

For rational intervals \(A=[a_-,a_+]\) and \(B=[b_-,b_+]\), interval addition, subtraction, and multiplication are
\[
A+B=[a_-+b_-,a_++b_+],
\qquad
A-B=[a_--b_+,a_+-b_-],
\]
\[
AB=
\left[
\min\{a_-b_-,a_-b_+,a_+b_-,a_+b_+\},
\max\{a_-b_-,a_-b_+,a_+b_-,a_+b_+\}
\right].
\]
A complex rectangle is \(A+iB\). Complex addition, subtraction, conjugation, and multiplication use these endpoint operations together with
\[
(A+iB)(C+iD)=(AC-BD)+i(AD+BC).
\]
For \(A=[a_-,a_+]\), define
\[
\operatorname{sq}_{-}(A)=
\begin{cases}
0,&a_-\le0\le a_+,\\
\min\{a_-^2,a_+^2\},&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\operatorname{sq}_{+}(A)=\max\{a_-^2,a_+^2\}.
\]
The squared modulus of \(A+iB\) is enclosed by adding the corresponding lower and upper square bounds. Rational bisection between nonnegative rational endpoints gives lower and upper square-root enclosures whose width after \(q\) bisections is at most the initial width times \(2^{-q}\). Division is the guarded operation
\[
\frac{A+iB}{C+iD}
=(A+iB)(C-iD)(C^2+D^2)^{-1},
\]
performed when the modulus routine returns a rational lower bound \(m>0\), with outward division by the positive rational interval enclosing \(C^2+D^2\).

The represented value of \(\pi\) is constructed from Machin's identity
\[
\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239).
\]
For \(0<x<1\),
\[
\arctan x=\sum_{j=0}^{N}(-1)^j\frac{x^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_N(x),
\qquad
|R_N(x)|\le\frac{x^{2N+3}}{2N+3}.
\]
Intersecting the first \(m+1\) requested enclosures gives rational nested enclosures. If \(e=p/q>0\) is in lowest terms, the choice \(N=5q+5\) makes the propagated Machin remainder smaller than \(e\), giving an effective modulus.

For a rational interval contained in \([-B,B]\), with rational \(B\ge0\), the Taylor polynomials for \(\exp\), \(\sin\), and \(\cos\) are evaluated by the preceding interval operations, with Lagrange remainders bounded by
\[
3^{\lceil B\rceil}\frac{B^{N+1}}{(N+1)!},
\qquad
\frac{B^{N+1}}{(N+1)!},
\qquad
\frac{B^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}.
\]
For a requested rational error \(e=p/q>0\), take
\[
N=\lceil2B\rceil+q+2\lceil B\rceil+2.
\]
After index \(\lceil2B\rceil\), successive absolute Taylor terms decrease by at least a factor \(1/2\); since \(3^{\lceil B\rceil}<2^{2\lceil B\rceil}\) and \(2^{-q}\le q^{-1}\le e\), the displayed \(N\) gives error at most \(e\). Finite intersections make the output rectangles nested. Consequently,
\[
\exp(x+iy)=\exp(x)\{\cos y+i\sin y\}
\]
has a nested complex-rectangle extension with an effective modulus on every bounded input rectangle. Combining the constructed representation of \(\pi\) with these trigonometric operations gives, from a positive radius representation \(\mathfrak r\) and integers \(N\ge1\) and \(0\le q<N\), represented circle-node values
\[
r\exp(2\pi iq/N)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2\pi i r\exp(2\pi iq/N).
\]

Finite rectangle sums and products are primitive recursive. If a complex-valued function on \([0,1]\) has a supplied rational Lipschitz bound \(L\), then a uniform mesh with
\[
N_{\mathrm{mesh}}=\lceil L/e\rceil+1
\]
has oscillation at most \(e\) on each cell. Evaluating every node to a rational error budget divided by the displayed finite number of operations yields sound finite infimum, supremum, and trapezoidal-integral enclosures. The required finite computation consists of \(N_{\mathrm{mesh}}+1\) node evaluations, the displayed Taylor cutoffs, square-root bisection cutoffs, and finitely many endpoint operations. Absolute value of a real interval is implemented by the square-free endpoint rule implicit in \(\operatorname{sq}_{-}\) and \(\operatorname{sq}_{+}\), with the same compositional modulus property.

A concrete implementation satisfies \(\mathsf{CCIA}_{\mathrm{build}}\) when it implements these algorithms, exports their soundness and effective-modulus contracts, and compiles the resulting module. Later executable conclusions are conditional on a compiled implementation satisfying this bounded-build specification.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate} supplies the finite representation interface used throughout the executable clauses. Nested intervals and moduli give a standard Type-2-style name for real values, while the endpoint operations and guarded division give sound complex-rectangle enclosures for the contour integrands. The mesh rule turns a supplied Lipschitz bound into finite enclosures for boundary moduli and contour integrals, matching the interval-analysis approach of \citet{Moore1966} within the computable-real framework of \citet{Weihrauch2000,Ko1991}.

The next definition specializes that arithmetic substrate to the contour bank. The primitive record stores certified names for the fixed separation, treatment-noise scale, zero-localization radius, and outer search radius. From those names and the parameter record, the bank construction produces a deterministic translated dyadic list of circles and a positive dyadic lower-modulus certificate.

\begin{definitionv}{def:contour-bank-handle}[Certified contour-bank handle $\mathfrak p_{\star}$]
Let \(p\) be a parameter record with sample size \(n\in\mathbb N\), \(1\le n\), orders \(r,k\in\mathbb N\) satisfying \(k=r+1\) and \(2\le r\), exponent \(s\in[0,\infty]\) satisfying \(r\le s\), probability level \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), constants \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},\delta,\sigma>0\), and sequences \(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\) satisfying \(\varepsilon_\ell(t)\ge0\) for \(t\ge1\) and \(\varepsilon_\ell(j)\le\varepsilon_\ell(i)\) whenever \(1\le i\le j\), for \(\ell=1,2\). Let \(\operatorname{Name}(x)\) denote a supplied certified-real record with value \(x\), and let \(\operatorname{Name}_{+}(x)\) denote such a record together with a rational lower-bound witness \(\ell>0\) satisfying \(\ell\le x\). As part of the experiment convention, fix the supplied record
\[
 \mathfrak p_{\star}=
 \bigl(k_{\star},\mathfrak d_{\star},\mathfrak p_{\eta,\star},
       \mathfrak r_{0,\star},\mathfrak r_{1,\star}\bigr),
\]
where \(k_{\star}\) is exact with \(k_{\star}=k\) and
\[
 \mathfrak d_{\star}:\operatorname{Name}_{+}(\delta),\quad
 \mathfrak p_{\eta,\star}:\operatorname{Name}_{+}(\psi_{\eta}),\quad
 \mathfrak r_{0,\star}:\operatorname{Name}_{+}(R_0),\quad
 \mathfrak r_{1,\star}:\operatorname{Name}_{+}(R_1).
\]
The value contracts are
\[
 R_0=(2^{k+4}k!)^{1/(k-2)}
       (\psi_{\eta}^2/\delta)^{1/(k-2)},
 \qquad R_1=R_0+1.
\]
Writing \(A_k=(2^{k+4}k!)^{1/(k-2)}\) for the numerical factor, this reads \(R_0=A_k(\psi_{\eta}^2/\delta)^{1/(k-2)}\). The search radius of the record is this outer radius: \(\operatorname{searchRadius}(p)=R_1\).
The record \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) is the primitive certified input shared by the bank and the estimator for this parameter record.  The bank data assembled below from \(p\) and \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) is written \(B\).

Invoke the supplied moduli of \(\mathfrak p_{\eta,\star}\) and \(\mathfrak r_{1,\star}\) at error one. If the returned intervals have rational upper endpoints \(u_{\psi}\) and \(u_R\), define
\[
 U_{\psi}=\left\lceil\max\{1,u_{\psi}\}\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 U_R=\left\lceil\max\{1,u_R\}\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 N_{\mathrm{cert}}=4U_{\psi}^2(U_R+2)^3.
\]
Set
\[
 m_{\star}=N_{\mathrm{cert}}+3,
 \qquad d_{\star}=2^{-m_{\star}},
 \qquad h_{\star}=d_{\star}/3,
 \qquad J_{\mathrm{base}}=2^{m_{\star}-1}.
\]
The bank therefore contains
\[
 J=J_{\mathrm{base}}+1
\]
circles, indexed by \(j\in\{0,\ldots,J_{\mathrm{base}}\}\).
For each \(j=0,\ldots,J_{\mathrm{base}}\), use certified addition on the fixed record \(\mathfrak r_{0,\star}\) and the rational point record for \(1/4+j2^{-m_{\star}}\) to form
\[
 \mathfrak\rho_j
 =\mathfrak r_{0,\star}+\operatorname{Name}(1/4+j2^{-m_{\star}}).
\]
The associated real-valued radius field is
\[
 \rho_j=(\mathfrak\rho_j).\operatorname{value}
       =R_0+1/4+j2^{-m_{\star}}.
\]

Define the integers
\[
 e_{\star}=8U_RU_{\psi}^2(U_R+1)^2,
 \qquad
 d_{\star}^{\mathrm{den}}=3(2U_R+1),
\]
\[
 u_{\star}=2e_{\star}
 +N_{\mathrm{cert}}(m_{\star}+d_{\star}^{\mathrm{den}}).
\]
Define the rational dyadic certificate and its real value by
\[
 a_{\star,\mathbb Q}=2^{-u_{\star}}>0,
 \qquad
 a_{\star}=(a_{\star,\mathbb Q}:\mathbb R).
\]
Every output datum is obtained by the displayed finite modulus calls, rational endpoint operations, certified additions with rational point records, and primitive recursion over the displayed integer bounds. The construction is a finite certified procedure in the supplied record \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\).
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle} converts the analytic radius from \cref{obj:lem:zero-localization} into a finite bank used by \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}. The radii fill a translated dyadic grid outside the zero-localization disk and inside the outer search region, and the certificate \(a_{\star}\) gives the deterministic denominator scale used by the selector. Because the construction depends on fixed primitive records and finite modulus calls, the same bank can be transported across the common-experiment sequences described in \cref{obj:thm:common-experiment-dichotomy}.

The represented-data transducer in \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator} applies \cref{obj:def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate} to the sample records \(\mathfrak t_i,\mathfrak y_i,\mathfrak g_i\). It first forms certified residual records, then evaluates the pilot-fold winding integrand on each bank circle, and finally evaluates the selected contour ratio on the evaluation fold. The fallback branches are part of the total rule: a failed admissibility check or a failed evaluation-fold modulus check returns the displayed value \(0\), while a successful run returns the clipped midpoint of a certified real interval. Thus the ordinary statistic and the represented execution have the same branch structure and the same clipped value whenever the compiled arithmetic implementation satisfies the bounded-build contract.

The final lemma is the deterministic accuracy statement used by the statistical risk proof. It compares the evaluation-fold empirical transforms with arbitrary population maps \(F\) and \(G\) on the selected circle, assumes the selected contour identifies a real target \(\vartheta\), and turns uniform transform errors into a bound for the clipped contour statistic.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:selected-contour-perturbation-bound}[Selected contour perturbation]
Fix a parameter record \(p\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-parameters}, the certified primitive and range records \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) and \(\mathfrak c_{\theta,\star}\) of \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle,obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}, a supplied treatment-regression code sequence, and observations \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) with \(O=(X,T,Y)\) as in \cref{obj:def:plm-model}.  Let \(B\) be the contour bank generated by \(p\) and \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\), let \(a_{\star}\) be its dyadic lower-modulus certificate, let \(C_j\) have radius \(\rho_j\), and write \(R_1=\operatorname{searchRadius}(p)\).  Let \(F,G:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\), let \(d,e,C_G,\mu,\nu\in\mathbb R\), and let \(\vartheta\in\mathbb R\) be the target value.

Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Target range.)} The target satisfies
\[
|\vartheta|\le C_{\theta}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Selector event.)} The canonical represented input built from the clipped current code and the sample satisfies
\[
d\ge0,\qquad e\ge0,\qquad C_G\ge0,\qquad \mu>0,\qquad \nu>0,
\]
\[
\mu=\frac{31a_{\star}}{64},\qquad \nu=\frac{23a_{\star}}{64},\qquad d\le \mu-\nu,
\]
and there are a bank index \(j\) and an integer \(N_j\) such that the certified selector of \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator} selects \(C_j\), the pilot winding output on \(C_j\) decodes to \(N_j\), \(N_j\ge1\), the evaluation-fold modulus enclosure on \(C_j\) has lower endpoint at least \(a_{\star}/4\), and every \(z\in C_j\) satisfies
\[
\mu\le |F(z)|,\qquad |G(z)|\le C_G,\qquad
|\widehat F_{1,n}(z)-F(z)|\le d,\qquad
|\widehat G_{1,n}(z)-G(z)|\le e,
\]
where \(\widehat F_{1,n}\) and \(\widehat G_{1,n}\) are the split-fold empirical transforms of \cref{obj:def:local-gaussian-handle}.
\item \textbf{(Contour integrability.)} For every bank index \(j\) selected by the canonical selector, both contour ratios
\[
z\mapsto \frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
z\mapsto \frac{G(z)}{F(z)}
\]
are integrable on the circle \(C_j\).
\item \textbf{(Exact identification.)} For every bank index \(j\) and every integer \(N\), if the canonical selector selects \(C_j\) and the pilot winding output on \(C_j\) decodes to \(N\), then
\[
\vartheta
=
\Re\left[
\{N\,2\pi i\}^{-1}
\oint_{C_j}\frac{G(z)}{F(z)}\,dz
\right].
\]
\end{itemize}
Then the total Borel contour statistic \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) of \cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator}, computed on the same canonicalized sample, satisfies
\[
\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\vartheta\right|
\le
R_1\max\left\{\nu^{-1},\,C_G(\mu\nu)^{-1}\right\}(d+e)
+
\frac{1}{\max\{n,1\}}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:selected-contour-perturbation-bound}]
On the selector event, choose the selected bank index \(j\) and the decoded integer
\(N\ge1\).  Write \(C_j\) for the selected circle and set
\[
\widehat T_j
=
\Re\left[
\{N\,2\pi i\}^{-1}
\oint_{C_j}
\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}\,dz
\right],
\qquad
T_j
=
\Re\left[
\{N\,2\pi i\}^{-1}
\oint_{C_j}
\frac{G(z)}{F(z)}\,dz
\right].
\]
The exact-identification hypothesis for the selected decoded contour gives
\(\vartheta=T_j\).

For each \(z\in C_j\), the selector event gives
\[
\mu\le |F(z)|,
\qquad
|G(z)|\le C_G,
\qquad
|\widehat F_{1,n}(z)-F(z)|\le d,
\qquad
|\widehat G_{1,n}(z)-G(z)|\le e .
\]
Together with \(d\le\mu-\nu\), the triangle inequality yields the empirical
denominator margin
\[
|\widehat F_{1,n}(z)|
\ge |F(z)|-|\widehat F_{1,n}(z)-F(z)|
\ge \mu-d
\ge \nu .
\]
Since \(\mu>0\) and \(\nu>0\), both denominators are nonzero on \(C_j\).  Hence
\[
\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}}{\widehat F_{1,n}}-
\frac{G}{F}
=
\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}-G}{\widehat F_{1,n}}
+
\frac{G(F-\widehat F_{1,n})}{\widehat F_{1,n}F},
\]
and the two terms have moduli bounded by \(e/\nu\) and
\(C_Gd/(\mu\nu)\), respectively.  Thus, pointwise on \(C_j\),
\[
\left|
\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}-
\frac{G(z)}{F(z)}
\right|
\le
\frac{e}{\nu}+\frac{C_Gd}{\mu\nu}.
\]

The contour-integrability hypothesis for the selected index permits integration
of this pointwise bound around the circle of radius \(\rho_j\).  Therefore
\[
\left|
\oint_{C_j}
\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}\,dz
-
\oint_{C_j}
\frac{G(z)}{F(z)}\,dz
\right|
\le
2\pi\rho_j
\left(\frac{e}{\nu}+\frac{C_Gd}{\mu\nu}\right).
\]
Multiplication by \(\{N2\pi i\}^{-1}\) and taking real parts give
\[
|\widehat T_j-\vartheta|
\le
\frac{\rho_j}{N}
\left(\frac{e}{\nu}+\frac{C_Gd}{\mu\nu}\right).
\]

It remains to relate this contour value to the returned statistic.  For the same
selected \(j\) and decoded \(N\), the construction of
\(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) in
\cref{obj:def:adaptive-contour-estimator} uses the rational midpoint \(y\) of the
real coordinate of the certified evaluation rectangle.  That rectangle contains
the normalized empirical contour value whose real part is \(\widehat T_j\), and
its real-coordinate width is at most \(2/\max\{n,1\}\).  Hence
\[
|y-\widehat T_j|\le \frac{1}{\max\{n,1\}}.
\]
The statistic is the clipping of this rational value to
\([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\).  Since \(|\vartheta|\le C_{\theta}\), projection
onto this interval is contractive relative to \(\vartheta\), so
\[
|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\vartheta|
\le |y-\vartheta|
\le |y-\widehat T_j|+|\widehat T_j-\vartheta|.
\]

By the bank construction in \cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle}, the selected
radius satisfies \(\rho_j<R_1\), and the decoded count satisfies \(N\ge1\).  Using
\(d,e,C_G\ge0\) and \(\mu,\nu>0\),
\[
\frac{\rho_j}{N}
\left(\frac{e}{\nu}+\frac{C_Gd}{\mu\nu}\right)
\le
R_1\max\{\nu^{-1},\,C_G(\mu\nu)^{-1}\}(d+e).
\]
Combining the preceding displays proves the asserted bound.
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:selected-contour-perturbation-bound} is the deterministic bridge between certified execution and the risk calculation in \cref{obj:thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}. The selector event supplies a lower bound for the population denominator and an evaluation-fold lower-modulus check; the exact-identification clause supplies the population target represented by the same selected contour. The conclusion then bounds the statistic by the uniform denominator and numerator errors on the selected circle, with the final \(1/\max\{n,1\}\) term coming from the certified interval radius used when the real contour moment is converted into the clipped rational midpoint.

Combining \cref{obj:def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate,obj:def:contour-bank-handle,obj:lem:selected-contour-perturbation-bound} gives the executable correspondence needed by the main contour theorem. \begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:known-zero-instrument}]
Put
\[
D_n(X)=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X),\qquad
b_n(X)=q_0(X)-\theta_0D_n(X),
\]
as in \cref{obj:def:outcome-contamination}, and write
\[
J(w)=J_{z_0,\ell}(w)=w^{\ell-1}e^{z_0w},\qquad w\in\mathbb C,
\]
as in \cref{obj:def:zero-instrument}.  From the definitions of \(\eta\) and \(Z_n\) in \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:contour-functional},
\[
Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)=\eta+D_n(X).
\]
The definitions of \(\xi\) in \cref{obj:def:plm-model} and \(b_n\) above also give, by algebra,
\[
Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi .
\]
Membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), together with the clipping convention in \cref{obj:def:plm-model}, supplies the integrability and boundedness used below.  Pulled back to the observation space, \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} gives
\[
|g_0(X)|\le C_g,\qquad |q_0(X)|\le C_q,\qquad |\theta_0|\le C_{\theta}
\qquad P\text{-a.s.},
\]
and the clipping gives \(|\bar g_n(X)|\le C_g\).  Hence
\[
|D_n(X)|\le 2C_g,
\qquad
|b_n(X)|\le C_q+C_{\theta}(2C_g)
\qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
The treatment and outcome sub-Gaussian clauses in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} imply real exponential moments of every order for \(\eta\) and \(\xi\); the displayed bound on \(D_n\) then gives the corresponding real exponential moments for \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\).  These facts justify the differentiated transforms, conditional expectations, and products appearing below.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Since \(M\) is analytic at \(z_0\) and has analytic order \(\ell\ge1\) there,
\[
M^{(\alpha)}(z_0)=0\qquad\text{for every }0\le\alpha<\ell .
\]
Moreover, for each such \(\alpha\),
\[
M^{(\alpha)}(z_0)
=
E_P\!\left[\eta^\alpha e^{z_0\eta}\right],
\]
by differentiating the moment-generating function under the expectation. Hence, for any \(d\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[J(\eta+d)]
&=
E_P\!\left[(\eta+d)^{\ell-1}e^{z_0(\eta+d)}\right]  \\
&=
e^{z_0d}
\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\ell-1}
\binom{\ell-1}{\alpha}d^{\ell-1-\alpha}
E_P\!\left[\eta^\alpha e^{z_0\eta}\right] \\
&=
e^{z_0d}
\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\ell-1}
\binom{\ell-1}{\alpha}d^{\ell-1-\alpha}
M^{(\alpha)}(z_0)
=0 .
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the shifted annihilation identity. % lean: zeroInstrument_integral_add_eq_zero

\item We next show the conditional identity. Let \(\mathcal A_X\) be any measurable covariate event. Since \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\), the binomial expansion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_{\{X\in\mathcal A_X\}} J(Z_n)\,dP  \\
&\quad =
\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\ell-1}
\binom{\ell-1}{\alpha}
\int
\eta^\alpha e^{z_0\eta}
D_n(X)^{\ell-1-\alpha}e^{z_0D_n(X)}
\mathbf 1_{\mathcal A_X}(X)\,dP .
\end{aligned}
\]
The treatment-noise independence in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} factors each summand as
\[
\binom{\ell-1}{\alpha}
E_P\!\left[\eta^\alpha e^{z_0\eta}\right]\,
E_P\!\left[
D_n(X)^{\ell-1-\alpha}e^{z_0D_n(X)}
\mathbf 1_{\mathcal A_X}(X)
\right],
\]
and the first factor is \(M^{(\alpha)}(z_0)=0\). Thus the integral over every covariate event is zero, which characterizes
\[
E_P[J(Z_n)\mid X]=0
\qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
% lean: zeroInstrument_condExp_learnedResidual_eq_zero

\item Define, for \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
F_n(z)=E_P[e^{zZ_n}].
\]
Since \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\), the same independence gives the factorization
\[
F_n(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}]\,E_P[e^{zD_n(X)}]=M(z)H_n(z),
\]
with \(H_n\) as in \cref{obj:def:contour-functional}. Also,
\[
E_P[Z_nJ(Z_n)]
=
E_P[Z_n^\ell e^{z_0Z_n}]
=
F_n^{(\ell)}(z_0).
\]
Differentiating \(F_n=MH_n\) \(\ell\) times at \(z_0\) yields
\[
F_n^{(\ell)}(z_0)
=
\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\ell}
\binom{\ell}{\alpha}
M^{(\alpha)}(z_0)H_n^{(\ell-\alpha)}(z_0).
\]
All terms with \(\alpha<\ell\) vanish by the analytic-order calculation above, and the remaining term is
\[
F_n^{(\ell)}(z_0)=M^{(\ell)}(z_0)H_n(z_0).
\]
Therefore
\[
E_P[Z_nJ(Z_n)]
=
M^{(\ell)}(z_0)H_n(z_0).
\]
% lean: zeroInstrument_learnedResidual_integral_eq

\item Finally, using the algebraic decomposition \(Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi\),
\[
E_P[YJ(Z_n)]
=
\theta_0E_P[Z_nJ(Z_n)]
+
E_P[b_n(X)J(Z_n)]
+
E_P[\xi J(Z_n)] .
\]
The second term is zero because \(b_n(X)\) is covariate-measurable and the conditional identity above gives
\[
E_P[b_n(X)J(Z_n)]
=
E_P\!\left[b_n(X)E_P[J(Z_n)\mid X]\right]
=0 .
\]
The third term is zero because \(J(Z_n)\) is measurable with respect to \((X,T)\), while \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} gives \(E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0\):
\[
E_P[\xi J(Z_n)]
=
E_P\!\left[J(Z_n)E_P[\xi\mid X,T]\right]
=0 .
\]
Thus
\[
E_P[YJ(Z_n)]
=
\theta_0E_P[Z_nJ(Z_n)] .
\]
Whenever \(E_P[Z_nJ(Z_n)]\ne0\), division gives
\[
\theta_0
=
\frac{E_P[YJ(Z_n)]}{E_P[Z_nJ(Z_n)]}.
\]
% lean: outcome_zeroInstrument_integral_eq_theta_mul
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:observable-factorization}]
Fix \(z\in\mathbb C\).  The definitions give
\[
Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)=\eta+\{g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\}=\eta+D_n(X).
\]
Moreover, \cref{obj:def:plm-model,obj:def:non-gaussian-class} give the pointwise clipping bound \(|\bar g_n(X)|\le C_g\) and the treatment-regression range bound \(|g_0(X)|\le C_g\) almost surely, hence
\[
 |D_n(X)|=|g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)|
 \le |g_0(X)|+|\bar g_n(X)|
 \le 2C_g
 \qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
Together with the Luxemburg treatment-tail condition for \(\eta\), this bound gives every real exponential moment of \(Z_n\), and hence the exponential integrability used below. % lean: learnedResidual_integrable_exp
Since \(D_n(X)\) is a function of \(X\) and \(\eta\) is independent of \(X\) under \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class},
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_n(z)
&=E_P\exp\{z(\eta+D_n(X))\}  \\
&=E_P\bigl[\exp(z\eta)\exp\{zD_n(X)\}\bigr] \\
&=E_P\exp(z\eta)\;E_P\exp\{zD_n(X)\}
=M(z)H_n(z).
\end{aligned}
\]
% lean: residualMGF_eq_treatmentMGF_mul_nuisanceMGF

For the second identity, first note the pointwise residual decomposition
\[
Y=\theta_0 Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi,
\]
because \cref{obj:def:outcome-contamination} gives \(b_n(X)=q_0(X)-\theta_0D_n(X)\), while \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\) and \(\xi=Y-q_0(X)-\theta_0\eta\).  The preceding exponential-moment envelope for \(Z_n\) also gives differentiation under the expectation:
\[
F_n'(z)
=
E_P\!\left[Z_n e^{zZ_n}\right].
\]
% lean: hasDerivAt_complexMGF

Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_n(z)
&=E_P\!\left[Y e^{zZ_n}\right] \\
&=\theta_0 E_P\!\left[Z_ne^{zZ_n}\right]
  +E_P\!\left[b_n(X)e^{zZ_n}\right]
  +E_P\!\left[\xi e^{zZ_n}\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
The contamination weight is bounded almost surely by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |b_n(X)|
 &=|q_0(X)-\theta_0D_n(X)| \\
 &\le |q_0(X)|+|\theta_0|\,|D_n(X)| \\
 &\le C_q+C_{\theta}(2C_g),
\end{aligned}
\qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
where the last line uses the outcome-regression and coefficient range bounds in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} together with the displayed bound on \(D_n\).  This boundedness and the same exponential envelope for \(Z_n\) give integrability of the contamination term. % lean: contamination_weighted_exp_integrable

The outcome-noise term is integrable as well.  The \(\xi\)-tail condition in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} gives \(E_P[|\xi|^2]<\infty\), and the learned-residual envelope gives
\[
E_P\!\left[\left|e^{zZ_n}\right|^2\right]
=E_P\!\left[e^{2\operatorname{Re}(z)Z_n}\right]<\infty.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz, \(E_P[|\xi e^{zZ_n}|]<\infty\). % lean: outcomeNoise_weighted_exp_integrable
Since \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\), the real and imaginary parts of \(e^{zZ_n}\) are measurable with respect to \((X,T)\).  Applying \(E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0\) from \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} separately to these integrable real products yields
\[
E_P\!\left[\xi e^{zZ_n}\right]=0.
\]
% lean: outcomeNoise_weighted_exp_eq_zero

Finally, independence of \(\eta\) and \(X\) factors the contamination term:
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P\!\left[b_n(X)e^{zZ_n}\right]
&=E_P\!\left[b_n(X)e^{zD_n(X)}e^{z\eta}\right] \\
&=E_Pe^{z\eta}\;E_P\!\left[b_n(X)e^{zD_n(X)}\right]
=M(z)B_n(z).
\end{aligned}
\]
% lean: contamination_weighted_factorization

Substituting the last three displays and the derivative identity gives
\[
G_n(z)=\theta_0F_n'(z)+M(z)B_n(z).
\]
Since \(z\) was arbitrary, both identities hold for every \(z\in\mathbb C\). % lean: observable_factorization
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:exact-contour-identification}]
Write \(\rho_j\) for the radius of the selected positively oriented bank circle \(C_j=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=\rho_j\}\).  The bank construction gives \(\rho_j>0\).  Put
\[
D_n(X)=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X),\qquad
b_n(X)=q_0(X)-\theta_0(P)D_n(X),
\]
and
\[
B_n(z)=E_P\!\left[b_n(X)e^{zD_n(X)}\right],\qquad z\in\mathbb C .
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free},
\[
|D_n(X)|\le 2C_g\qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
The clipping defining \(\bar g_n\), the range conditions in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and \(|\theta_0(P)|\le C_\theta\) give
\[
|b_n(X)|
\le |q_0(X)|+|\theta_0(P)|\,|D_n(X)|
\le C_q+2C_\theta C_g
\qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
Consequently \(H_n\) and \(B_n\) are complex differentiable at every point.  Indeed, on the unit neighborhood of any \(z_0\in\mathbb C\),
\[
|D_n(X)e^{zD_n(X)}|
\le 2C_g\exp\{(|z_0|+1)2C_g\},
\]
and
\[
|b_n(X)D_n(X)e^{zD_n(X)}|
\le (C_q+2C_\theta C_g)(2C_g)
   \exp\{(|z_0|+1)2C_g\},
\]
so dominated differentiation under the integral applies to the weighted transforms.  Separately, the sub-Gaussian exponential-moment condition for the treatment innovation \(\eta\), together with \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\) and the almost-sure bound on \(D_n\), gives \(E_P e^{tZ_n}<\infty\) for every real \(t\).  The standard moment-generating-function analyticity criterion therefore gives analyticity of \(F_n\) on a neighborhood of the closed disk \(\{|z|\le \rho_j\}\).

On the closed disk \(\{|z|\le\rho_j\}\), the nuisance zero-freeness assumption gives \(H_n(z)\ne0\).  Hence the quotient \(B_n/H_n\) is well-defined on the closed disk, differentiable in the open disk, and continuous on the closed disk.  Cauchy's theorem on the circle gives
\[
\oint_{C_j}\frac{B_n(z)}{H_n(z)}\,dz=0 .
\]

For \(z\in C_j\), \cref{obj:lem:observable-factorization} gives
\[
F_n(z)=M(z)H_n(z),
\qquad
G_n(z)=\theta_0(P)F_n'(z)+M(z)B_n(z),
\]
where \(M(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}]\).  Since \(F_n(z)\ne0\) and \(H_n(z)\ne0\) on \(C_j\), also \(M(z)\ne0\) on \(C_j\).  Therefore, pointwise for \(z\in C_j\),
\[
\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}
=
\theta_0(P)\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}
+
\frac{B_n(z)}{H_n(z)} .
\]
Integrating around \(C_j\) and using the Cauchy identity for the quotient \(B_n/H_n\) yields
\[
\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
=
\theta_0(P)
\oint_{C_j}\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz .
\]

The function \(F_n\) is analytic near the closed disk and zero-free on \(C_j\), so the argument principle gives
\[
\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\oint_{C_j}\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
=
N_{C_j}(F_n).
\]
Thus
\[
\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
=
\theta_0(P)\,N_{C_j}(F_n)\,2\pi i .
\]
The positive-count assumption gives \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\ge1\), so \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\,2\pi i\ne0\).  Dividing by this nonzero factor gives
\[
\theta_0(P)
=
\{N_{C_j}(F_n)\,2\pi i\}^{-1}
\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz .
\]

By \cref{obj:def:contour-functional}, the right-hand side is \(\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n)\).  Therefore
\[
(\theta_0(P):\mathbb C)
=
\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n)
=
\{N_{C_j}(F_n)\,2\pi i\}^{-1}
\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz .
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:luxemburg-mgf-envelope}]
Fix \(t\in\mathbb R\) and put
\[
\lambda=t\psi,\qquad v=\frac{W}{\psi}.
\]
The square-exponential envelope first gives integrability of \(e^{tW}\): for every outcome point,
\[
tW
\le \frac{W^2}{\psi^2}+\frac{t^2\psi^2}{4},
\]
because \((W/\psi-t\psi/2)^2\ge0\).  Hence \(e^{tW}\) is dominated by
\(e^{t^2\psi^2/4}e^{W^2/\psi^2}\). % lean: integrable_exp_mul_of_luxemburg_sq

Consider first the case \(|\lambda|\le1\).  For every real \(u\),
\[
e^u\le 1+u+u^2e^{|u|}.
\]
Indeed, when \(|u|\le1\), the Taylor-remainder bound
\(|e^u-1-u|\le u^2\) gives \(e^u-1-u\le u^2\le u^2e^{|u|}\).
When \(|u|>1\) and \(u\ge0\), one has \(u>1\), so
\(e^u\le u^2e^{|u|}\le 1+u+u^2e^{|u|}\).  When \(|u|>1\) and
\(u<0\), one has \(u\le-1\), whence \(e^u\le1\le 1+u+u^2\le
1+u+u^2e^{|u|}\). % lean: exp_le_one_add_add_sq_mul_exp_abs
With \(u=\lambda v\),
\[
|\lambda v|\le \frac{\lambda^2+v^2}{2}\le \frac{1+v^2}{2}.
\]
Also,
\[
v^2e^{(1+v^2)/2}
=e^{1/2}v^2e^{v^2/2}
\le e^{1/2}e^{v^2}
\le 2e^{v^2},
\]
where the two inequalities use \(v^2\le e^{v^2/2}\) and \(e^{1/2}\le2\).  Combining these estimates with monotonicity of the exponential gives
\[
(\lambda v)^2e^{|\lambda v|}
\le 2\lambda^2e^{v^2}.
\]
% lean: sq_le_exp_half_sq, exp_half_le_two
Therefore
\[
e^{tW}=e^{\lambda v}
\le 1+tW+2\lambda^2\exp(W^2/\psi^2).
\]
Integrating and using \(E_PW=0\) and \(E_P\exp(W^2/\psi^2)\le2\),
\[
E_Pe^{tW}
\le 1+4\lambda^2
\le \exp(4\lambda^2)
=\exp(4\psi^2t^2).
\]
% lean: integral_exp_mul_le_exp_four_mul_sq_of_luxemburg

It remains to treat \(|\lambda|>1\).  Completing the square gives
\[
e^{tW}\le \exp(\lambda^2/4)\exp(W^2/\psi^2).
\]
After integration,
\[
E_Pe^{tW}
\le 2\exp(\lambda^2/4).
\]
Since \(2\le e\) and \(|\lambda|>1\) implies \(1<\lambda^2\),
\[
2\exp(\lambda^2/4)
\le \exp(1+\lambda^2/4)
\le \exp(4\lambda^2)
=\exp(4\psi^2t^2).
\]
% lean: integral_exp_mul_le_exp_four_mul_sq_of_luxemburg

The two cases prove the asserted bound for every \(t\in\mathbb R\). % lean: integral_exp_mul_le_exp_four_mul_sq_of_luxemburg
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:zero-localization}]
Let
\[
M(z)=E_Pe^{z\eta}.
\]
From \(k=r+1\) and \(r\ge2\), one has \(k\ge3\).  The treatment-tail assumption gives
\[
E_P\exp(\eta^2/\psi_\eta^2)\le2,
\]
together with the corresponding integrability, and the conditional-mean identity for \(g_0\) gives
\[
E_P\eta=E_P\{T-g_0(X)\}=0.
\]
Applying \cref{obj:lem:luxemburg-mgf-envelope} to \(\eta\) gives, for every real \(t\),
\[
E_Pe^{t\eta}\le \exp(4\psi_\eta^2t^2).
\]
Consequently, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
|M(z)|
\le E_Pe^{\operatorname{Re}(z)\eta}
\le \exp\{4\psi_\eta^2|z|^2\}.
\]
The same exponential-integrability envelope gives analyticity of the complex MGF on all of \(\mathbb C\), and normalization at the origin gives \(M(0)=1\).

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(M(z)\ne0\) for every \(|z|\le R_0\).  Then \(\log |M|\) is harmonic on the open disk \(\{|z|<R_0\}\).  Choose an analytic function \(F\) on this disk whose real part is \(\log|M|\), and set
\[
f(z)=F(z)-F(0).
\]
Then \(f(0)=0\) and, for \(|z|<R_0\),
\[
\operatorname{Re} f(z)=\log |M(z)|\le 4\psi_\eta^2R_0^2.
\]

The Borel--Carath\'eodory estimate on the disk of radius \(R_0\), applied to the normalized function \(f\), gives on the sphere \(|z|=R_0/2\)
\[
|f(z)|\le 8\psi_\eta^2R_0^2.
\]
Cauchy's estimate on the disk of radius \(R_0/2\) therefore yields
\[
|f^{(k)}(0)|
\le
\frac{k!\,8\psi_\eta^2R_0^2}{(R_0/2)^k}
=
2^{k+3}k!\,\psi_\eta^2R_0^{2-k}.
\]

Near the origin, \(M(0)=1\), so \(M\) takes values in the slit plane on a small disk and the principal logarithm \(\log M\) is analytic there.  The analytic function
\[
z\mapsto f(z)-\log M(z)
\]
has identically zero real part on that small connected disk and vanishes at \(0\); hence it is identically zero there.  Thus
\[
f^{(k)}(0)=\left.\frac{d^k}{dz^k}\log M(z)\right|_{z=0}.
\]
Since \(\kappa_k(\eta)\) is the real part of this derivative and \(|\operatorname{Re} w|\le |w|\),
\[
|\kappa_k(\eta)|
\le 2^{k+3}k!\,\psi_\eta^2R_0^{2-k}.
\]

By the definition
\[
R_0=(2^{k+4}k!)^{1/(k-2)}
       \left(\frac{\psi_\eta^2}{\delta}\right)^{1/(k-2)},
\]
we have
\[
R_0^{k-2}
=
2^{k+4}k!\,\frac{\psi_\eta^2}{\delta}.
\]
Substitution in the preceding bound gives
\[
|\kappa_k(\eta)|
\le
2^{k+3}k!\,\psi_\eta^2
\left(2^{k+4}k!\,\frac{\psi_\eta^2}{\delta}\right)^{-1}
=\frac{\delta}{2}
<\delta.
\]

This contradicts the cumulant separation condition \(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta\).  Therefore there exists \(z_0\in\mathbb C\) with \(|z_0|\le R_0\) and \(M(z_0)=0\).
\end{proof}

hence the asserted equality of the two classes under the common clipped-code
convention.

\item Fix a model \(m\) with observed-data law
\(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) using the clipped supplied
codes \(\bar g_n,\bar q_n\), and suppose \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\),
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\), and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\ge0\).  If
\(\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\), the positive-radius part of the assumed published
order-\(r\) ACE generalized-quantile guarantee, with the objects and gates
stated in \cref{obj:prop:jms-ace-alignment}, applied with the supplied handle
and the same clipped codes, gives
\[
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \bigl(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\bigr)
 \le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}.

It remains to treat the boundary case \(\varepsilon_{2,n}=0\).  For any
\(\chi>0\), let \(p^{(\chi)}\) be the parameter record obtained from \(p\) by
replacing the whole second radius sequence by
\[
  \varepsilon_{2,j}^{(\chi)}=\max\{\varepsilon_{2,j},\chi\},
  \qquad j\ge1,
\]
and leaving every other coordinate unchanged.  This sequence is again
nonnegative and nonincreasing.  Read the same observed-data law, regressions,
and supplied code sequences as a model \(m^{(\chi)}\) over \(p^{(\chi)}\); its
observed-data law is still \(P\).  If \(0<\chi<\varepsilon_{1,n}\), then the
class membership is preserved: every condition in
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) is unchanged except the
outcome-code radius, and at index \(n\)
\[
  \varepsilon_{2,n}^{(\chi)}=\chi
  \quad\text{with}\quad
  0\le \varepsilon_{2,n}\le \chi .
\]
Moreover, with
\[
  \omega_n=(\gamma n)^{-1/2}(\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}),
\]
the denominator entering the second JMS eligibility quantity is unchanged:
\[
 \max\{\varepsilon_{1,n},\max\{\varepsilon_{2,n}^{(\chi)},\omega_n\}\}
 =
 \max\{\varepsilon_{1,n},\max\{\varepsilon_{2,n},\omega_n\}\},
\]
because \(\varepsilon_{2,n}=0\) and \(0<\chi<\varepsilon_{1,n}\).  All other
eligibility quantities are unchanged by construction, so
\(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) holds for \(p^{(\chi)}\) at the same index.

Let
\[
  \Lambda_n
  =
  C_{\gamma} r!16^r\delta^{-1}\varepsilon_{1,n}^r .
\]
The assumed ACE guarantee includes \(C_{\gamma}>0\), and the displayed hypotheses
give \(\Lambda_n>0\).  For any real \(\beta>B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\), set
\[
  \chi_{\beta}
  =
  \min\!\left\{
    \frac{\varepsilon_{1,n}}{2},
    \frac{\beta-B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}}{2\Lambda_n}
  \right\}.
\]
Then \(0<\chi_{\beta}<\varepsilon_{1,n}\), and the positive-radius result
applied to \(p^{(\chi_{\beta})}\) gives
\[
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \bigl(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\bigr)
 \le
 B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}+\Lambda_n\chi_{\beta}.
\]
By the definition of \(\chi_{\beta}\),
\[
  \Lambda_n\chi_{\beta}
  \le
  \frac{\beta-B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}}{2},
\]
so the same quantile is at most \(\beta\).  Since this holds for every
\(\beta>B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\), the desired boundary inequality follows:
\[
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \bigl(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\bigr)
 \le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}.
\]

 \right\}.

supplied treatment-code sequence agrees pointwise with the statistic computed

\[
  T_i-\bar g_n(X_i),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n .

\[
 \varepsilon_{1,n}
 \le
 \{4R_1\exp(2C_gR_1)\}^{-1},

 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \bigl(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|\bigr)
 \le
 \sqrt{\frac{C_{\mathrm{spec}}}{\gamma n}} .

Since \(\mathcal A_n^{\star}\) is exactly the class of laws in the statement
with the clipped supplied codes \(\bar g_n,\bar q_n\), this is the asserted
spectral upper guarantee.

last item of the proposition.  Define
\[
  \mathcal L_n
  =
  \varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
  +C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
\]
and
\[
  \lambda_{\mathrm{ACE}}
  =
  C_{\gamma}r!16^r\delta^{-1}.
\]
The assumed ACE guarantee includes \(C_{\gamma}>0\), so
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{ACE}}>0\).  Along the sequence, the fixed-constant
identities in the hypotheses rewrite the ACE bound as
\[
 B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}
 =
 \lambda_{\mathrm{ACE}}
 \left[
   \mathcal L_n
   +
   64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
   \{r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\}
   (\gamma n)^{-1/2}
 \right].
\]
The second summand in brackets is nonnegative, hence eventually
\[
  \lambda_{\mathrm{ACE}}\mathcal L_n
  \le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}.
\]
The assumed divergence is
\[
  \frac{\mathcal L_n}{n^{-1/2}}\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
For \(n\ge1\),
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{C_{\mathrm{spec}}}{\gamma n}}
 =
 \sqrt{\frac{C_{\mathrm{spec}}}{\gamma}}\;n^{-1/2}.
\]
Once \(\mathcal L_n>0\) and
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{ACE}}\mathcal L_n\le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\), we have
\[
 0\le
 \frac{\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{spec}}/(\gamma n)}}{B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}}
 \le
 \frac{\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{spec}}/\gamma}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{ACE}}}
 \left(\frac{\mathcal L_n}{n^{-1/2}}\right)^{-1}.
\]
The right-hand side tends to \(0\), so the squeeze theorem gives
\[
 \frac{
   \sqrt{C_{\mathrm{spec}}/(\gamma n)}
 }{
   B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}
 }
 \longrightarrow 0 .
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:symmetric-mixture-reduction}]

t=\frac{\pi}{2},\qquad
A_{\sin}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{8}\right),

and
\[
K_{\sin}
=
4(C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g)^2+4\psi_{\xi}^2
+C_{\theta}^2(8+16C_g^2).

Choose
\[
C=1+\frac{16}{A_{\sin}^2}K_{\sin}.

\(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\). Fix \(p\), \(P\), and the model objects satisfying the

&=\frac12\exp\!\left(-z+\frac{z^2}{2}\right)
  +\frac12\exp\!\left(z+\frac{z^2}{2}\right)  \\
&=\exp\!\left(\frac{z^2}{2}\right)\cosh z .
\end{aligned}

Therefore
\[
M(it)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{8}\right)\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=0.
\]
Near zero,
\[
\frac{d}{dz}\log M(z)=z+\tanh z.
\]
The third derivative at zero of \(z\) is zero, while
\[
(\tanh z)'=\operatorname{sech}^2 z,\qquad
(\tanh z)''=-2\tanh z\,\operatorname{sech}^2 z,
\qquad
(\tanh z)'''(0)=-2.
\]
Hence the fourth logarithmic derivative of \(M\) at zero is \(-2\), which is the asserted
fourth cumulant:
\[
\kappa_4(\eta)=-2 .

All differentiations are justified by the Gaussian mixture's finite exponential moments. % lean: treatmentMGF_eq_symmetricGaussianMixture

\item The same transform computation gives the weighted characteristic moment used for the

M'(z)=\exp(z^2/2)\,z\cosh z+\exp(z^2/2)\sinh z,

evaluating at \(z=it\) yields
\[
E_P\!\left[\eta\,e^{it\eta}\right]
=M'(it)
=iA_{\sin}.

Using \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\), the independence of \(\eta\) and \(X\), and the identity

\[

\ge

The conditional mean restriction for the outcome innovation gives

Thus
\[

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:clipped-ratio-risk-decomposition}]
Write
\[
\bar W=\mathbb P_n W,\qquad
\bar R=\mathbb P_n R,\qquad
\Delta_W=\mathbb P_n(W-\mu_W).
\]
Since \(n\ge1\),
\[
\Delta_W=\bar W-\mu_W.
\]
% lean: clippedRatioFromScores_sq_le

Consider first the branch \(\bar W\ge A/4\). Then \(\bar W>0\), and the
unclipped ratio obeys
\[
\frac{\theta\bar W+\bar R}{\bar W}-\theta=\frac{\bar R}{\bar W}.
\]
The projection onto \([-C_\theta,C_\theta]\) fixes \(\theta\), because
\(|\theta|\le C_\theta\), and is \(1\)-Lipschitz. Hence
\[
|\widehat\theta-\theta|
\le
\left|\frac{\bar R}{\bar W}\right|
\le
\frac{4|\bar R|}{A}.
\]
Squaring and adding the nonnegative term involving \(\Delta_W\) gives
\[
(\widehat\theta-\theta)^2
\le
\frac{16}{A^2}\bar R^2
\le
\frac{16}{A^2}
\left\{\bar R^2+C_\theta^2\Delta_W^2\right\}.
\]
% lean: clippedRatioFromScores_sq_le

It remains to handle the branch \(\bar W<A/4\), where \(\widehat\theta=0\).
Since \(\mu_W\ge A/2\),
\[
\mu_W-\bar W\ge A/4,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
A^2\le16(\bar W-\mu_W)^2=16\Delta_W^2.
\]
Also \(|\theta|\le C_\theta\), hence \(\theta^2\le C_\theta^2\). Therefore
\[
(\widehat\theta-\theta)^2
=
\theta^2
\le
C_\theta^2
\le
\frac{16}{A^2}C_\theta^2\Delta_W^2
\le
\frac{16}{A^2}\left\{\bar R^2+C_\theta^2\Delta_W^2\right\}.
\]
The two branches give the claimed pathwise bound. % lean: clippedRatioFromScores_sq_le
\end{proof}

=
C_{\gamma}\,r!\,16^r\,\Delta^{-1}
\left(
\varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
+C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
+64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
\bigl(r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\bigr)
(\gamma n)^{-1/2}
\right).
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SaPlmCumulantConverse.local_to_gaussian_partial_benchmarks
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fix a model \(m\) in the displayed Gaussian class, and write \(P\) for its observed-data law. Define
\[
\eta=T-g_0(X),
\qquad
\xi=Y-q_0(X)-\theta_0(P)\eta .
\]
The conditional mean restrictions in \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class,obj:def:plm-model} give
\[
E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0,
\]
and \(q_0(X)\) and \(\theta_0(P)\eta\) are measurable with respect to \((X,T)\). Hence conditional-expectation linearity yields
\[
E_P[Y\mid X,T]
=
q_0(X)+\theta_0(P)\eta
\qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
The bounded-outcome condition gives \(-C_q\le Y\le C_q\) \(P\)-a.s. Conditioning the two inequalities on \((X,T)\) gives
\[
-C_q\le E_P[Y\mid X,T]\le C_q
\qquad P\text{-a.s.},
\]
and therefore
\[
\bigl|q_0(X)+\theta_0(P)\eta\bigr|\le C_q
\qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
Conditioning the same bounded-outcome inequalities on \(X\), and using
\[
E_P[Y\mid X]=q_0(X),
\]
gives
\[
|q_0(X)|\le C_q
\qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
Combining the two displayed bounds,
\[
|\theta_0(P)\eta|
=
\bigl|(q_0(X)+\theta_0(P)\eta)-q_0(X)\bigr|
\le
\bigl|q_0(X)+\theta_0(P)\eta\bigr|+|q_0(X)|
\le 2C_q
\qquad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SaPlmCumulantConverse.gaussianClass_theta_eq_zero

\item Suppose, for contradiction, that \(\theta_0(P)\ne0\), and define
\[
\Lambda_\theta=\frac{2C_q}{|\theta_0(P)|}\in\mathbb R .
\]
The previous step implies
\[
|\eta|\le \Lambda_\theta
\qquad P\text{-a.s.},
\]
so
\[
P\{\eta>\Lambda_\theta\}=0.
\]
The Gaussian membership in \cref{obj:def:gaussian-class} gives \(\eta\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\), and the parameter record in \cref{obj:def:model-parameters} gives \(\sigma>0\). Thus the variance is nonzero and
\[
N(0,\sigma^2)\bigl((\Lambda_\theta,\infty)\bigr)=0.
\]
For a Gaussian law with positive variance, Lebesgue measure is absolutely continuous with respect to that Gaussian law. Hence the last display implies
\[
\lambda\bigl((\Lambda_\theta,\infty)\bigr)=0.
\]
But
\[
\lambda\bigl((\Lambda_\theta,\infty)\bigr)=\infty .
\]
This contradiction proves
\[
\theta_0(P)=0 .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SaPlmCumulantConverse.gaussianClass_theta_eq_zero

\item Now fix deterministic code sequences \(\widehat g,\widehat q\) and assume the fixed-code Gaussian class in the statement is inhabited. Consider the estimator
\[
\widetilde\theta_0(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\widehat g_n,\widehat q_n)=0 .
\]
For every model in that fixed-code class, the first part gives \(\theta_0(P)=0\), and hence
\[
E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
\{\widetilde\theta_0(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\widehat g_n,\widehat q_n)-\theta_0(P)\}^2
\right]=0 .
\]
Taking the supremum over the fixed-code class and then the infimum over all estimators in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risks} gives
\[
\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)\le0.
\]
Since this risk is nonnegative by definition,
\[
\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)=0 .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SaPlmCumulantConverse.bounded_outcome_gaussian_degeneracy

\item It remains to evaluate the generalized-quantile criterion for the same zero estimator. Since \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\),
\[
0<1-\gamma<1.
\]
For every model in the fixed-code Gaussian class,
\[
\bigl|\widetilde\theta_0(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\widehat g_n,\widehat q_n)-\theta_0(P)\bigr|=0
\qquad P^{\otimes n}\text{-a.s.}
\]
Thus the law of this loss is the point mass at zero. Its distribution function is \(0\) for negative thresholds and \(1\) at every nonnegative threshold, so the generalized lower \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile of \cref{obj:def:generalized-quantile} is
\[
Q_{P,1-\gamma}(0)=0 .
\]
Consequently the supremum of the fixed-code quantile loss for the zero estimator is \(0\), and the minimax infimum is at most \(0\). Nonnegativity in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risks} gives
\[
\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}(p;\widehat g,\widehat q)=0 .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SaPlmCumulantConverse.generalizedQuantile_zero
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:jms-ace-class-relations}]
Fix the parameter record, an index \(n\), and a model \(m\).  For every code index \(j\), the clipped-code discrepancies
\[
x\longmapsto \bar g_j(x)-g_0(x),
\qquad
x\longmapsto \bar q_j(x)-q_0(x)
\]
are \(P_X\)-measurable, because the supplied codes and the regressions are measurable and clipping preserves measurability.  The covariate marginal \(P_X\) is a probability law, since it is the image of the observed-data probability law under the covariate map. % lean: jms_ace_class_relations

Assume first that \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\).  The positive class-index condition and the structural, range, tail, and cumulant requirements of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) are the corresponding member conditions in \cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class}.  The treatment-code requirement in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} follows from the JMS ACE treatment radius.  Put
\[
f(x)=\bar g_n(x)-g_0(x).
\]
The JMS ACE radius bound gives a finite \(L^r(P_X)\) bound for \(f\).  Since \(P_X\) is a probability law and \(2\le r\), exponent monotonicity gives \(f\in L^1(P_X)\) and
\[
\|f\|_{L^1(P_X)}
\le
\|f\|_{L^r(P_X)} .
\]
Together with \(\|f\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}\) and the nonnegativity of \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\) at the positive index \(n\), this yields
\[
\int |\bar g_n(x)-g_0(x)|\,dP_X(x)\le \varepsilon_{1,n},
\]
with the displayed integrand integrable.  Thus \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\). % lean: jms_ace_class_relations

Next assume \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\).  The inherited non-Gaussian member conditions supply the positive class-index condition and the common structural, range, tail, and cumulant requirements appearing in \cref{obj:def:jms-ace-class}.  The subclass restrictions in \cref{obj:def:ace-comparison-subclass} give
\[
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n},
\qquad
\|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}.
\]
Because \(r\le s\) and \(P_X\) is a probability law, exponent monotonicity gives
\[
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}
\le
\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^s(P_X)},
\qquad
\|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}
\le
\|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^s(P_X)} .
\]
These are the two JMS ACE code-radius requirements, so
\(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\). % lean: jms_ace_class_relations

Finally suppose \(s=r\).  The preceding paragraph gives
\[
m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}} .
\]
Conversely, if \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\), the first inclusion gives \(m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and the two JMS ACE \(L^r(P_X)\) code bounds become exactly the two \(L^s(P_X)\) subclass bounds in \cref{obj:def:ace-comparison-subclass}.  Hence
\[
m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
m\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}} .
\]
% lean: jms_ace_class_relations
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark}]
Choose
\[
K=
4(C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g)^2+4\psi_\xi^2
+
C_{\theta}^2(8+16C_g^2),
\qquad
C=1+64K+4C_{\theta}^2 .
\]
Then \(C>0\).  Fix \(p,a,m\) satisfying the assumptions, and write
\[
t=t_a=\frac{\pi}{2a},
\qquad
A=A_a=a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\}.
\]
Since \(0<a\le1\), \(t>0\) and \(A>0\). % lean: gaussian_rademacher_l1_benchmark

Independence of \(G_\circ\) and \(S\) gives the product transform
\[
M_a(z)
=
E\exp\{z\sqrt{1-a^2}G_\circ\}\,
E\exp\{zaS\}
=
\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az).
\]
The fourth logarithmic derivative at zero of this transform is the Rademacher contribution, hence
\[
\kappa_4(\eta_a)=-2a^4 .
\]
% lean: treatmentMGF_eq_gaussianRademacher; model_gaussianRademacher_kappa_four

On the imaginary axis,
\[
M_a(iu)=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{1-a^2}{2}u^2\right)\cos(au).
\]
The exponential factor is nonzero.  Therefore the first positive zero occurs when \(au=\pi/2\), namely
\[
t_a=\frac{\pi}{2a},
\qquad
M_a(it_a)=0,
\qquad
M_a(iu)\ne0\quad(0<u<t_a).
\]
% lean: gaussianRademacher_first_characteristic_zero

For any \(d\in\mathbb R\),
\[
E_P[\sin\{t_a(\eta+d)\}]
=
\Im\left(e^{it_ad}E_P[e^{it_a\eta}]\right)
=
\Im\left(e^{it_ad}M_a(it_a)\right)
=
0 .
\]
% lean: integral_sin_shift_eq_zero

Let
\[
Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X),
\qquad
D_n=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X),
\]
so that
\[
Z_n=\eta+D_n(X).
\]
The derivative of the transform at the first zero is
\[
M_a'(it_a)
=
iA_a,
\]
equivalently
\[
E_P\!\left[\eta\,e^{it_a\eta}\right]=iA_a .
\]
% lean: eta_cexp_moment_at_first_zero

Using \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\), independence of \(\eta\) and \(X\), and \(M_a(it_a)=0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[Z_ne^{it_aZ_n}]
&=
E_P[\eta e^{it_a\eta}]\,E_P[e^{it_aD_n(X)}]
+
E_P[e^{it_a\eta}]\,E_P[D_n(X)e^{it_aD_n(X)}]  \\
&=
iA_a\,E_P[e^{it_aD_n(X)}].
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking imaginary parts gives
\[
E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]
=
A_aE_P[\cos(t_aD_n)] .
\]
% lean: learnedResidual_sine_denominator_identity

Assume now that \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le1/2\).  The \(L^1(P_X)\) condition gives
\[
E_P|D_n(X)|\le\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
Since \(1-\cos u\le |u|\),
\[
E_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
\ge
1-t_aE_P|D_n|
\ge
1-t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}
\ge
\frac12 .
\]
Together with the preceding identity and \(A_a>0\), this yields
\[
E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]\ge A_a/2 .
\]
% lean: learnedResidual_sine_denominator_lower

Define the two scores
\[
W=Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n),
\qquad
R=(Y-\theta_0Z_n)\sin(t_aZ_n).
\]
The zero-shift identity and the conditional mean condition imply
\[
E_P[R]=0.
\]
Indeed, writing
\[
Y-\theta_0Z_n
=
q_0(X)-\theta_0D_n(X)+\xi,
\]
the covariate term is killed by the sine-shift identity after conditioning on \(X\), while
\[
E_P[\xi\sin(t_aZ_n)]
=
E_P\!\left[\sin(t_aZ_n)E_P(\xi\mid X,T)\right]
=
0 .
\]
Thus
\[
E_P[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]
=
\theta_0\,E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)] .
\]
% lean: sine_remainder_centered_of_mgf_zero

The Gaussian--Rademacher path has the second-moment envelope
\[
E_P[\eta^2]\le4.
\]
With \(|g_0|\le C_g\) and \(|\bar g_n|\le C_g\), we have
\[
|D_n|\le2C_g,
\]
and therefore
\[
E_P[W^2]\le 8+16C_g^2.
\]
% lean: gaussianRademacher_second_lintegral_le; sine_score_memLp_of_eta_second_lintegral_le

Similarly, the range conditions give
\[
|q_0(X)-\theta_0D_n(X)|
\le
C_q+2C_\theta C_g,
\]
and the conditional Luxemburg bound for \(\xi\) gives
\[
E_P[\xi^2]\le 2\psi_\xi^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
E_P[R^2]
\le
4(C_q+2C_\theta C_g)^2+4\psi_\xi^2.
\]
The two displayed square-moment bounds are exactly the components entering \(K\). % lean: sine_score_memLp_of_eta_second_lintegral_le

Let
\[
\overline W=\mathbb P_n W,
\qquad
\overline R=\mathbb P_n R,
\qquad
\mu_W=E_PW .
\]
The estimator in the statement is the clipped ratio based on
\[
\mathbb P_n[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]
=
\theta_0\overline W+\overline R
\]
with threshold \(A_a/4\).  Since \(\mu_W\ge A_a/2\), \cref{obj:lem:clipped-ratio-risk-decomposition} gives the pathwise inequality
\[
(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2
\le
\frac{16}{A_a^2}
\left\{\overline R^2+C_\theta^2(\overline W-\mu_W)^2\right\}.
\]
Taking expectation under the i.i.d. product law and using \(E_PR=0\) gives
\[
E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
\le
\frac{16}{nA_a^2}
\left(E_P[R^2]+C_\theta^2E_P[W^2]\right)
\le
\frac{16K}{nA_a^2}.
\]
% lean: thetaHatAt_eq_clippedRatioFromScores; clippedRatioFromScores_lintegral_le

The clipping range also gives the deterministic bound
\[
(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2\le4C_\theta^2 .
\]
Combining this with the preceding display and using
\[
A_a^{-2}
=
a^{-2}
\exp\!\left(\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right)
\]
yields
\[
E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
\le
C\min\!\left\{
1,\frac1n a^{-2}
\exp\!\left(\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right)
\right\}.
\]
% lean: clippedRatioFromScores_sq_le_global; gaussian_rademacher_l1_benchmark

The smallness condition is equivalent to the stated radius bound.  Since
\[
t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac12
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac{a}{\pi},
\]
and
\[
\Delta_a=2a^4,
\qquad
\Delta_a^{1/4}=2^{1/4}a,
\]
we obtain
\[
\varepsilon_{1,n}
\le
\frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi}.
\]
% lean: gaussian_rademacher_l1_benchmark

Finally,
\[
\Delta_a^{-1/2}
=
(2a^4)^{-1/2}
=
(\sqrt2\,a^2)^{-1}.
\]
The first risk factor is bounded by the cumulant-parametrized factor:
\[
a^{-2}\exp\!\left(\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right)
\le
\Delta_a^{-1/2}
\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}\right).
\]
Substituting this inequality into the previous risk display gives
\[
E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
\le
C\min\!\left\{
1,\frac1n\Delta_a^{-1/2}
\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}\right)
\right\}.
\]
% lean: gaussian_rademacher_l1_benchmark
\end{proof}
