\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:gaussian-exponential-square-quarter-scale}]
Let
\[
v=\left(\frac{\psi}{4}\right)^2.
\]
Since \(\psi>0\), this variance is positive.  Under \(N(0,v)\), the density identity is, for every \(x\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2v}\right)
\exp\!\left(\frac{x^2}{\psi^2}\right)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{7x^2}{\psi^2}\right),
\]
because \(1/(2v)=8/\psi^2\). % lean: gaussian_exp_sq_integrable_and_integral_le_two

Since \(7/\psi^2>0\), the function \(x\mapsto\exp\{-7x^2/\psi^2\}\) is integrable on \(\mathbb R\).  The density identity therefore transfers this integrability to \(x\mapsto\exp(x^2/\psi^2)\) under \(N(0,v)\), so \(\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)\) is integrable under the law of \(\zeta\). % lean: integrable_exp_neg_mul_sq

For the expectation, the same density identity rewrites the Gaussian-law integral as
\[
\mathbb E\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}
\int_{\mathbb R}\exp\!\left(-\frac{7x^2}{\psi^2}\right)\,dx.
\]
The Gaussian integral formula applied with coefficient \(7/\psi^2\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{7/\psi^2}}.
\]
% lean: integral_gaussian

Finally,
\[
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{7/\psi^2}}
\le
2\sqrt{2\pi v},
\]
since after squaring both sides this is
\[
\frac{\pi\psi^2}{7}\le 8\pi v=\frac{\pi\psi^2}{2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E\exp(\zeta^2/\psi^2)\le2.
\]
% lean: gaussian_exp_sq_integrable_and_integral_le_two
\end{proof}
