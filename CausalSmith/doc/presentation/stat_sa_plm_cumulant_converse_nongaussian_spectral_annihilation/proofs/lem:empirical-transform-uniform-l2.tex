\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}]
Set
\[
A_F=2\exp\{8R_1C_g+4R_1^2\psi_\eta^2\},
\]
\[
A_G=
64\{C_q^4+4C_\theta^4\psi_\eta^4+4\psi_\xi^4\}
+
2\exp\{16R_1C_g+16R_1^2\psi_\eta^2\},
\]
and define
\[
K=4(A_F+A_G).
\]
The constants \(A_F\) and \(A_G\) are strictly positive, so \(K>0\); it
depends only on the displayed primitive constants and \(R_1\). % lean: empiricalTransformL2Constant empiricalTransformL2Constant_pos

Fix a fold for which \(I_a\) is nonempty, and let \(I=I_a\),
\(m_I=|I|\), and \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\). Since the contour-bank radius in
\cref{obj:def:contour-bank-handle} satisfies \(R_1=R_0+1\) with
\(R_0\ge0\), we have \(R_1>0\). Write
\(D_n(X)=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\), so \(Z_n=\eta+D_n(X)\). The range bound for
\(g_0\) in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} and the clipping of
\(\bar g_n\) in \cref{obj:def:plm-model} give \(|D_n(X)|\le 2C_g\) almost
surely. Hence, for almost every observation,
\[
4R_1|Z_n|
\le 4R_1|\eta|+8R_1C_g
\le \frac{\eta^2}{\psi_\eta^2}+4R_1^2\psi_\eta^2+8R_1C_g,
\]
where the second inequality is the Young inequality obtained by expanding
\((|\eta|/\psi_\eta-2R_1\psi_\eta)^2\ge0\). Therefore
\[
\{\exp(2R_1|Z_n|)\}^2
\le
\exp\{8R_1C_g+4R_1^2\psi_\eta^2\}
\exp(\eta^2/\psi_\eta^2).
\]
Integrating and using the Luxemburg treatment-tail bound from
\cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class} yields
\[
E_P\left[\exp\{2R_1|Z_n|\}^2\right]
\le A_F.
\]
% lean: learnedResidual_exp_abs_sq_lintegral_le

The outcome-weighted envelope is
\[
E_P\left[\{|Y|\exp(2R_1|Z_n|)\}^2\right]\le A_G.
\]
Indeed, \cref{obj:def:plm-model} gives \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\). For
almost every observation, the algebraic bound
\[
|q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi|^4
\le
64\{|q_0(X)|^4+|\theta_0|^4|\eta|^4+|\xi|^4\}
\]
combines with the bounds in \cref{obj:def:non-gaussian-class}, namely
\(|q_0(X)|\le C_q\) almost surely and \(|\theta_0|\le C_\theta\), to give
\[
|Y|^4
\le
64\{C_q^4+C_\theta^4|\eta|^4+|\xi|^4\}
\]
almost surely. The Luxemburg moment bounds give
\(E_P|\eta|^4\le4\psi_\eta^4\) and \(E_P|\xi|^4\le4\psi_\xi^4\), and hence
\[
E_P|Y|^4
\le
64\{C_q^4+4C_\theta^4\psi_\eta^4+4\psi_\xi^4\}.
\]
Together with
\[
\{|Y|\exp(2R_1|Z_n|)\}^2
\le
|Y|^4+
\{\exp(4R_1|Z_n|)\}^2,
\]
and the preceding residual envelope applied at radius \(2R_1\), this gives the
stated bound. % lean: outcome_exp_abs_sq_lintegral_le

We use the following coefficient-series bound. Let \(U,V\) be measurable real
functions, let \(R>0\), let \(C\ge0\), and suppose
\[
E_P\left[\{|U|\exp(2R|V|)\}^2\right]\le C^2.
\]
For a nonempty index set \(I\), define
\[
c_k(O_1,\ldots,O_n)
=
m_I^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}
\left\{
\frac{U(O_i)V(O_i)^k}{k!}
-
E_P\frac{UV^k}{k!}
\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
E\left[
\sup_{|z|\le R}
\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k z^k\right|^2
\right]
\le
\left(\frac{2C}{\sqrt{m_I}}\right)^2.
\]
The envelope gives
\[
\left\|
\frac{UV^k}{k!}
\right\|_{L^2(P)}
\le
\frac{C}{(2R)^k},
\]
because \(x^k/k!\le e^{2Rx}/(2R)^k\) for \(x\ge0\); the hypotheses
\(R>0\) and \(C\ge0\) make the displayed majorant nonnegative. Under the
product sample law, centering over the block \(I\) gives
\[
\|c_k\|_{L^2(P^{\otimes n})}
\le
\frac{C}{(2R)^k\sqrt{m_I}}.
\]
The series of majorants is summable, and the centered analytic-series supremum
bound yields
\[
\left(
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{C}{(2R)^k\sqrt{m_I}}R^k
\right)^2
=
\left(\frac{2C}{\sqrt{m_I}}\right)^2.
\]
% lean: factorial_coefficient_memLp_two pi_finset_centered_average_sq_lintegral_le centered_real_series_diskSupNorm_sq_lintegral_le centered_factorial_empirical_disk_l2

The same coefficient construction represents the empirical transform errors
exactly. For \(|z|\le R\),
\[
m_I^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}U(O_i)e^{zV(O_i)}
-
E_P[Ue^{zV}]
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_k z^k,
\]
with the exchange of summation, finite averaging, and integration justified by
the doubled exponential envelope. % lean: weighted_exp_integral_eq_moment_tsum weighted_empirical_sub_eq_centered_factorial_series

Apply the preceding bound first with \(U\equiv1\), \(V=Z_n\), \(R=R_1\), and
\(C=\sqrt{A_F}\). Since \(A_F>0\), this choice has \(C\ge0\), and
\[
E\left[
\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|^2
\right]
\le
\frac{4A_F}{m_I}.
\]
% lean: empirical_transform_uniform_l2_explicit

Apply it again with \(U=Y\), \(V=Z_n\), \(R=R_1\), and
\(C=\sqrt{A_G}\). Since \(A_G>0\), this choice has \(C\ge0\), and
\[
E\left[
\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|^2
\right]
\le
\frac{4A_G}{m_I}.
\]
% lean: empirical_transform_uniform_l2_explicit

Adding the two displays yields
\[
E\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|^2\right]
+
E\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|^2\right]
\le
\frac{4(A_F+A_G)}{|I_a|}
=
\frac{K}{|I_a|}.
\]
This proves the assertion. % lean: empirical_transform_uniform_l2
\end{proof}
