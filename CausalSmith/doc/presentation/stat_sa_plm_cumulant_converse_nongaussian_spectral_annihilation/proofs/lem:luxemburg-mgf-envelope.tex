\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:luxemburg-mgf-envelope}]
Fix \(t\in\mathbb R\) and put
\[
\lambda=t\psi,\qquad v=\frac{W}{\psi}.
\]
The square-exponential envelope first gives integrability of \(e^{tW}\): for every outcome point,
\[
tW
\le \frac{W^2}{\psi^2}+\frac{t^2\psi^2}{4},
\]
because \((W/\psi-t\psi/2)^2\ge0\).  Hence \(e^{tW}\) is dominated by
\(e^{t^2\psi^2/4}e^{W^2/\psi^2}\). % lean: integrable_exp_mul_of_luxemburg_sq

Consider first the case \(|\lambda|\le1\).  For every real \(u\),
\[
e^u\le 1+u+u^2e^{|u|}.
\]
Indeed, when \(|u|\le1\), the Taylor-remainder bound
\(|e^u-1-u|\le u^2\) gives \(e^u-1-u\le u^2\le u^2e^{|u|}\).
When \(|u|>1\) and \(u\ge0\), one has \(u>1\), so
\(e^u\le u^2e^{|u|}\le 1+u+u^2e^{|u|}\).  When \(|u|>1\) and
\(u<0\), one has \(u\le-1\), whence \(e^u\le1\le 1+u+u^2\le
1+u+u^2e^{|u|}\). % lean: exp_le_one_add_add_sq_mul_exp_abs
With \(u=\lambda v\),
\[
|\lambda v|\le \frac{\lambda^2+v^2}{2}\le \frac{1+v^2}{2}.
\]
Also,
\[
v^2e^{(1+v^2)/2}
=e^{1/2}v^2e^{v^2/2}
\le e^{1/2}e^{v^2}
\le 2e^{v^2},
\]
where the two inequalities use \(v^2\le e^{v^2/2}\) and \(e^{1/2}\le2\).  Combining these estimates with monotonicity of the exponential gives
\[
(\lambda v)^2e^{|\lambda v|}
\le 2\lambda^2e^{v^2}.
\]
% lean: sq_le_exp_half_sq, exp_half_le_two
Therefore
\[
e^{tW}=e^{\lambda v}
\le 1+tW+2\lambda^2\exp(W^2/\psi^2).
\]
Integrating and using \(E_PW=0\) and \(E_P\exp(W^2/\psi^2)\le2\),
\[
E_Pe^{tW}
\le 1+4\lambda^2
\le \exp(4\lambda^2)
=\exp(4\psi^2t^2).
\]
% lean: integral_exp_mul_le_exp_four_mul_sq_of_luxemburg

It remains to treat \(|\lambda|>1\).  Completing the square gives
\[
e^{tW}\le \exp(\lambda^2/4)\exp(W^2/\psi^2).
\]
After integration,
\[
E_Pe^{tW}
\le 2\exp(\lambda^2/4).
\]
Since \(2\le e\) and \(|\lambda|>1\) implies \(1<\lambda^2\),
\[
2\exp(\lambda^2/4)
\le \exp(1+\lambda^2/4)
\le \exp(4\lambda^2)
=\exp(4\psi^2t^2).
\]
% lean: integral_exp_mul_le_exp_four_mul_sq_of_luxemburg

The two cases prove the asserted bound for every \(t\in\mathbb R\). % lean: integral_exp_mul_le_exp_four_mul_sq_of_luxemburg
\end{proof}
