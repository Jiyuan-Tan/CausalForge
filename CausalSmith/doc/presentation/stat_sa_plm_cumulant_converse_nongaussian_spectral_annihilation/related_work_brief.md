The paper sits in the semiparametric partially linear model tradition initiated by \citep{Engle1986,Robinson1988,Speckman1988} and consolidated in \citep{HardleLiangGao2000}. Its statistical risk and sample-splitting language draw on the general semiparametric and GMM canon, including \citep{Hansen1982,Newey1990,Newey1994,NeweyMcFadden1994,BickelKlaassenRitovWellner1993,VanDerVaart1998,VanDerVaartWellner1996}. The high-dimensional causal-inference lineage is represented by post-selection and orthogonal-score methods \citep{BelloniChernozhukovHansen2014,ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML,ChernozhukovEscancianoIchimuraNeweyRobins2022}, with cross-fitting and fast-remainder ideas from \citep{NeweyRobins2018} and related doubly robust and targeted-learning foundations in \citep{RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,VanDerLaanRubin2006}.

Methodologically, the closest econometric antecedents are higher-order influence and orthogonality constructions. \citet{RobinsLiTchetgenVanDerVaart2008}, \citet{LiuMukherjeeRobinsTchetgen2021}, and \citet{LiuMukherjeeNeweyRobins2017} develop higher-order influence-function estimators and minimax-adaptive functional estimation. \citet{MackeySyrgkanisZadik2018} introduces higher-order orthogonal machine learning and identifies the PLM role of non-Gaussian treatment residuals. \citet{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} gives the finite-order ACE comparator, including cumulant-separated non-Gaussian guarantees and the Gaussian benchmark assumptions used here. The present paper positions its fixed-separation result as an effective contour-based PLM construction with exact learned-residual factorization, direct \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code stability, and a once-for-all conditioned contour bank.

The transform and zero-geometry strand combines classical characteristic-function theory \citep{Marcinkiewicz1939,Lukacs1970,KaganLinnikRao1973}, empirical-characteristic-function estimation \citep{FeuervergerMcDunnough1981,Singleton2001,Carrasco2017}, spectral GMM \citep{ChackoViceira2003}, and inverse-problem regularization \citep{CarrascoFlorensRenault2007}. Location-family completeness and observable-zero testing enter through \citep{Mattner1992,DHaultfoeuille2011,DarollesFanFlorensRenault2011,Andrews2017,HuShiu2022}. Quantitative zero-localization and stability context comes from Marcinkiewicz-type and polynomial-root CLT results \citep{MichelenSahasrabudhe2019,EremenkoFryntov2021,DinhGhoshTranTran2021}. In this lineage, the paper's use of complex MGF zeros is tied to PLM residual contamination and contour identification, while the zero technology itself belongs to an established analytic-probability toolkit.

The non-Gaussian identification frontier includes ICA and structural-econometric work. \citet{Comon1994}, \citet{HyvarinenOja1997}, and \citet{HyvarinenKarhunenOja2001} provide the ICA foundations, and \citet{ShimizuHoyerHyvarinenKerminen2006} shows how non-Gaussianity can identify linear causal structure. In econometrics, \citet{LanneMeitzSaikkonen2017}, \citet{LeeMesters2024}, and \citet{HoeschLeeMesters2024} study non-Gaussian simultaneous-equation and SVAR inference, including weak-non-Gaussian regimes. \citet{ReizingerEtAl2025} connects ICA and treatment-effect estimation directly. The effective and certified-computation language is naturally situated against computable and interval analysis \citep{Weihrauch2000,Ko1991,Moore1966}, while the concentration inputs align with standard sub-Gaussian references \citep{Vershynin2018,Wainwright2019}.
