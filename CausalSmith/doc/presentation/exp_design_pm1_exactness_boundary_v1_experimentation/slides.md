# Exact Randomized Designs for Two-Block Interference Experiments

This paper pinpoints when a covariance relaxation is an exact randomized \(\{\pm1\}\) design, and computes the finite loss when parity blocks exact implementation.

---

## The relaxation is sometimes literal and sometimes only a benchmark

- The relaxed design problem optimizes a covariance matrix for network-interference assignment.
- An implementable design must come from an actual randomized sign vector \(Z\in\{-1,1\}^n\).
- In a two-block homophily model, the paper fully reduces this comparison to two block-covariance parameters.
- The cut design is exactly optimal in a strict region.
- The iid design is exactly optimal on one affine-balanced locus and is the large-robustness limit elsewhere.
- For odd block size, parity creates an open region with strictly positive implementability loss.

---

## The running example is a two-community experiment

- Think of \(n=2m\) units split into equal communities \(A_m\) and \(B_m\).
- Within-community exposure has weight \(a/m\).
- Across-community exposure has weight \(b/m\).
- Homophily means \(a>b>0\): ties inside a community carry more exposure than ties across communities.
- The design question is how to randomize treatment signs when interference follows this graph.

## The object being optimized is a covariance of assignments

- A randomized design induces \(X(P)=\mathbb E[ZZ^\top]\).
- Under sign symmetry, this is the covariance of treatment signs.
- The relaxation optimizes over block-symmetric correlation matrices \(X(u,v)\).
- The parameter \(u\) is the common within-block covariance.
- The parameter \(v\) is the common across-block covariance.
- Exact implementation asks whether the optimal \(X(u,v)\) is generated by a finite assignment law.

---

## The design criterion balances exposure, robustness, and aggregate balance

- The graph Laplacian term rewards alignment of signs across high-weight edges.
- The pseudoinverse term weights low-frequency graph directions.
- The Schatten--2 penalty uses \(\kappa\), the robustness weight, to favor smaller covariance norm.
- The aggregate balance penalty discourages large total sign imbalance.
- The pseudoinverse weight is \(r\).

@formal def:design-objective

---

## Symmetry turns the problem into two dimensions

- Averaging any relaxed covariance over the two-block automorphism group weakly improves the objective.
- Averaging any sign-symmetric assignment law over the same group also weakly improves the objective.
- Therefore the relaxed optimum lives in the block elliptope \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\).
- The implementable optimum lives in the block-exchangeable sign-design class \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\).
- The reduction preserves the relaxed and implementable infima exactly.

@formal prop:symmetry-reduction

---

## The finite obstruction is visible in block sums

- A block-exchangeable law is summarized, for implementation purposes, by the two block sums \(S_A\) and \(S_B\).
- Even \(m\) allows a block sum of zero.
- Odd \(m\) forces each realized block sum away from zero.
- In spectral coordinates, this parity restriction becomes the lower bound \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\).
- Here \(d_m=0\) for even \(m\) and \(d_m=2/m\) for odd \(m\).

## Three covariances organize the story

- \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\): all signs in one community oppose all signs in the other community.
- \(I_n\): independent fair assignment, with zero off-diagonal covariance.
- \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}\): the relaxed spread point in the within-block spectral direction.
- The paper characterizes when these points solve the relaxed problem and when implementation changes the optimum.
- The next question is which of these covariances is actually selected by \(F_{r,\kappa}\).

---

## The cut design is exactly optimal in a strict region

@informal thm:cut-corner-exactness: Under two-block homophily and the stated strict cut-region bound on \(r\), \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\) is the unique relaxed and implementable optimum, so the implementability loss is zero.

@formal thm:cut-corner-exactness

---

## The cut result has a simple design interpretation

- Small enough \(r\) keeps the graph-and-balance preference at the cut corner.
- The implementing law is the two-point law on the cut assignment and its global sign reversal.
- In the running two-community experiment, the design assigns opposite signs across the two homophilous communities.
- The covariance relaxation is literal here: solving the relaxed problem gives an actual randomized design.

---

## Iid assignment is exact only on one affine-balanced locus

@informal thm:robust-corner-exactness: Under two-block homophily and \(r\ge0\), finite-robustness relaxed optimality of \(I_n\) occurs exactly on \(a+3b=2m\) and \(r=2b(a+b)\), while relaxed minimizers converge entrywise to \(I_n\) as \(\kappa\to\infty\) elsewhere.

@formal thm:robust-corner-exactness

---

## Robustness explains why iid appears as a limit

- Independent fair assignment is always implementable in the symmetric design class.
- The Frobenius penalty pulls the relaxed solution toward the center \(I_n\).
- At the affine-balanced locus, every positive robustness weight makes \(I_n\) the unique relaxed minimizer.
- Away from that locus, increasing \(\kappa\) makes the relaxed minimizer converge entrywise to \(I_n\).
- Exact finite-\(\kappa\) optimality and large-\(\kappa\) limiting behavior are distinct design phenomena.

---

## Odd block size creates a positive-loss window

@informal thm:gap-window: Under two-block homophily, \(a+3b<2m\), odd \(m\), and the stated robustness range, there is a nonempty interval of \(r\) where \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}\) is the unique relaxed optimum, lies outside \(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\), and yields strictly positive implementability loss.

@formal thm:gap-window

---

## The gap is a finite parity effect

- In the running example, odd community size means each community has an unavoidable residual sign imbalance in every realization.
- The spread covariance asks the relaxed problem to put all mass in the within-block spectral direction.
- Odd parity requires positive mass in the remaining directions.
- The implementable optimum therefore moves to the nearest feasible parity-truncated slice in objective terms.
- For even \(m\), the theorem states that every covariance in the block elliptope is implementable.

---

## The exact loss is computed by an active-set formula

@informal thm:sharp-rho-star: Under two-block homophily, the active-set quantity \(\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)\) equals the implementability loss \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\), is always nonnegative, and is zero exactly when a relaxed minimizer satisfies the parity truncation \(d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\).

@formal thm:sharp-rho-star

---

## The computation is three-dimensional after reduction

- Spectral coordinates reduce the block elliptope to a simplex \(T_m\).
- The relaxed objective becomes a linear term plus a weighted Euclidean norm.
- For \(\kappa>0\), one active support and multiplier identify the unique relaxed minimizer.
- If that point satisfies \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\), the loss is zero.
- If it falls below the parity threshold, the implementable value is found on the truncation segment.
- This gives a finite calculation for every admissible \(m,a,b,r,\kappa\).

---

## A small odd example shows all regimes

- Take \(m=3\), \(a=2\), \(b=1\), and \(\kappa=0\).
- Homophily holds, and the low-scale condition is \(a+3b=5<6=2m\).
- For \(r<6\), the cut covariance is uniquely optimal and implementable.
- For \(r\in(7.5,9)\), the spread covariance is uniquely relaxed-optimal and unattainable.
- At \(r=8\), the active-set calculation gives \(\Delta_3^{\pm}(8,0)=2/9\).
- The example illustrates how exactness, positive loss, and active-set correction appear in one finite design.

---

## The contribution is an exact finite implementability theory

- The symmetry reduction converts the covariance relaxation and the randomized-design problem to the same two-block geometry.
- The cut theorem gives a strict exactness region implemented by a two-point randomized design.
- The robust-corner theorem characterizes finite-robustness optimality of iid assignment and its large-\(\kappa\) limit.
- The gap theorem identifies an odd-parity region with a unique relaxed optimum and strictly positive implementability loss.
- The active-set theorem computes the exact finite loss \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\) across the admissible parameter space.
