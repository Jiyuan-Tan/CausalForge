# Exact Designs in Two-Block Network Experiments

This paper shows exactly when a covariance relaxation for a homophilous two-block interference experiment is attainable by a finite randomized \(\{\pm1\}\) assignment law, and computes the finite loss when attainability fails.

---

## Punchline

- The design problem starts as a covariance optimization problem.
- A randomized experiment must produce that covariance from an actual sign assignment law.
- In two equal homophilous blocks, symmetry reduces the question to two covariance parameters.
- The relaxation is exact at the cut design in a strict cut region: the tradeoff weight \(r\) sits below an explicit threshold \(r_{\mathrm{cut}}(m,a,b,\kappa)\).
- Independent fair assignment is exactly optimal on one affine-balanced locus and is the large-robustness target elsewhere.
- Odd block size creates a positive implementability loss in an explicit parameter window.
- A finite active-set formula computes the loss for every admissible \(m,a,b,r,\kappa\).

---

## Why Care?

- Network interference makes the assignment covariance part of the experimental design.
- A covariance relaxation can recommend correlations that look statistically attractive.
- The experimentalist still needs a finite randomization law on \(\{-1,1\}^n\).
- The central question is exact and finite: when is the relaxed covariance actually implementable?
- The paper answers this question in a two-community homophily model.

---

## Running Example

- Think of two equal communities, such as two schools, villages, platforms, or neighborhoods.
- Units interact more strongly within a community than across communities.
- The design chooses a randomized treatment-control sign vector \(Z\).
- A cut design assigns opposite signs to the two communities, up to global sign reversal.
- An iid design assigns independent fair signs to all units.
- A spread covariance is the extreme point that puts all of its mass on within-block contrasts and none on the two aggregate directions.

## Setup: Two Homophilous Blocks

- There are \(n=2m\) units split into communities \(A_m\) and \(B_m\).
- The within-block edge weight is \(a/m\), and the across-block edge weight is \(b/m\).
- Homophily means \(a\), the within-block intensity, is larger than \(b\), the across-block intensity.
- The assignment law is sign-symmetric: treatment and control labels are balanced at the law level.
- The graph Laplacian \(L_m\) and its pseudoinverse \(L_m^\dagger\) define the design criterion.

## The Optimization Problem

- The relaxed object is a block-symmetric correlation matrix \(X(u,v)\); the block elliptope is the set of such matrices that are valid covariances, and it contains every implementable one.
- The within-block covariance parameter \(u\) captures common correlation inside each community.
- The across-block covariance parameter \(v\) captures common correlation between communities.
- The implementable object is a covariance \(X(P)\) generated by a block-exchangeable sign design.
- The design objective combines graph disagreement, a pseudoinverse term, Frobenius robustness, and aggregate balance.
- The implementability loss \(\Delta_m^\pm(r,\kappa)\) is the relaxed-to-implementable objective gap.

## Key Idea: Geometry Plus Parity

- The block covariance has three spectral coordinates: \(x\) for within-block contrasts, \(y\) for between-block contrast, and \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}\) for the aggregate direction.
- The relaxed feasible set becomes a simplex in these three coordinates.
- Implementability adds a finite sign-assignment restriction.
- For even \(m\), every covariance in the block elliptope slice is generated by a block-exchangeable sign design.
- For odd \(m\), every realized block sum has odd parity, so implementability imposes the lower bound \(d_m=2/m\) on \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\).
- Positive loss appears exactly when the relaxed optimum sits beyond that parity boundary.

## Where the Literature Stands

- Design-based causal inference supplies the finite-population randomization viewpoint.
- Network-interference work explains why assignment covariance matters when outcomes depend on neighbors’ assignments.
- Covariance-based SDP design, especially Thiyageswaran et al., optimizes over relaxed assignment covariances.
- Elliptope and cut-polytope theory explain why a convex covariance relaxation can exceed finite sign-design feasibility.
- This paper bridges those strands by characterizing exact implementability and exact rounding loss in a homophilous two-block design.

@informal thm:cut-corner-exactness: In the stated strict cut region, the cut covariance is the unique relaxed minimizer, is generated by a two-point randomized design, and has implementability loss equal to zero.

## Main Result 2: Iid Exactness and Large-Robustness Target

@informal thm:robust-corner-exactness: Independent fair assignment is implementable, and it is a finite-robustness relaxed optimum exactly on the affine-balanced locus \(a+3b=2m\) and \(r=2b(a+b)\), while relaxed minimizers converge entrywise to \(I_n\) as \(\kappa\to\infty\) elsewhere.

## Main Result 3: Odd-Parity Positive Loss

@informal thm:gap-window: Under two-block homophily, the low-scale condition, odd block size, and the stated robustness range, the spread covariance is the unique relaxed minimizer over an explicit \(r\)-interval and the implementability loss is strictly positive.

## Why the Novelty Wins

- The naive covariance relaxation optimizes over the whole block elliptope.
- In the odd-block gap window, that relaxation puts all relevant mass on the spread vertex.
- A finite sign design with odd block size must keep at least \(d_m=2/m\) mass in the \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\) directions.
- The active-set correction moves the solution only as far as the parity boundary requires.
- The loss calculation therefore balances the relaxed objective improvement against the exact finite implementability constraint.

## Main Result 4: Sharp Finite Loss

@informal thm:sharp-rho-star: For every admissible \(m,a,b,r,\kappa\), the active-set expression \(\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)\) equals the implementability loss \(\Delta_m^\pm(r,\kappa)\), is nonnegative, and is zero exactly when a relaxed minimizer satisfies the parity truncation.

## Why It Is True: Reduction

- First, symmetrize the relaxed covariance and the assignment law.
- The objective is invariant under within-block permutations and block swapping.
- Convexity and averaging make the symmetrized object weakly better.
- The original relaxed problem reduces to the block elliptope.
- The original sign-design problem reduces to block-exchangeable laws.
- The remaining distinction is geometric: relaxed simplex versus implementable parity-truncated simplex.

---

## Why It Is True: Certificates

- At the cut vertex, coefficient separation makes any movement away from the cut direction increase the reduced objective.
- At the iid point, the Frobenius term selects the center only when the linear coefficients are balanced.
- At the spread vertex, the \(y\) and \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}\) directions become costly enough that the relaxed optimum concentrates on \(x\).
- For odd \(m\), that spread point violates the block-sum parity restriction.
- The active-set formula solves the unconstrained simplex problem and then solves the one-dimensional boundary correction when truncation binds.

---

## Takeaways

- The covariance relaxation is exactly implementable in the strict cut region via the two-point cut design.
- Independent fair assignment is exactly optimal on the affine-balanced locus and is the limiting robust target as \(\kappa\) grows.
- Odd community size creates an explicit open region with strictly positive implementability loss.
- Even community size gives zero loss throughout the block-elliptope slice.
- The active-set formula computes the exact finite loss for every admissible parameter choice.
