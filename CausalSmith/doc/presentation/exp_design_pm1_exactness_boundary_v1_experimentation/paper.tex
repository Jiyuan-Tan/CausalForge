\begin{abstract}
This paper studies when a covariance relaxation for interference-aware randomized design is exactly implementable by a finite \(\{\pm1\}\) assignment law. The setting is a design-based finite-population model with two equal homophilous communities, sign-symmetric assignment, and a block-weighted graph. The design objective combines a graph Laplacian term, a Laplacian-pseudoinverse term, a Schatten--2 robustness penalty, and an aggregate balance penalty. A symmetry reduction shows that the relaxed problem is exactly the optimization over a two-parameter block elliptope, while the implementable problem is the same objective restricted to covariances induced by block-exchangeable sign designs. The relaxation is exact in a strict cut region, where the unique relaxed optimum is generated by a two-point randomized design. Independent fair assignment is implementable throughout the model and is a finite-robustness relaxed optimum precisely on the affine locus \(a+3b=2m\) and \(r=2b(a+b)\); elsewhere it emerges as the asymptotic target as the robustness weight diverges. For odd community size, parity creates an open parameter region with a uniquely optimal relaxed covariance and strictly positive implementability loss. Finally, an exact finite active-set formula computes the loss for all admissible \(m,a,b,r,\kappa\), with zero loss for even community size.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Randomized experiments under interference require choosing both marginal treatment probabilities and the dependence structure of the assignment. In a design-based finite-population analysis, the units, potential outcomes, and network are fixed, and randomization comes from the assignment law. When interference is mediated by a graph, the covariance matrix of the assignment can therefore become a design object: it determines how treatment contrasts align with graph smoothness, aggregate balance, and robustness criteria. This perspective is closely related to the randomization-based tradition in causal inference \citep{horvitz1952,rubin1974}, to design and inference under interference \citep{hudgens2008,aronow2013,savje2017,leung2019,li2020}, and to recent covariance-based design formulations for network experiments \citep{thiyageswaran2026}.

A covariance relaxation is attractive because it replaces a finite randomized assignment problem by a convex matrix problem. Its feasible set can be strictly larger than the covariances generated by actual \(\{\pm1\}\) assignment laws, producing the familiar gap between semidefinite or elliptope relaxations and discrete correlation structures \citep{goemans1995,deza1997,boyd2004}. In experimental design, this gap has an operational meaning: implementation requires a covariance prescription induced by a randomization mechanism.

This paper isolates that issue in a finite two-community model. There are \(n=2m\) fixed units, split into two equal communities. The graph has within-community edge weight \(a/m\) and across-community edge weight \(b/m\), with \(a>b>0\). Assignments are sign vectors \(Z\in\{-1,1\}^n\), and assignment laws are required to be sign-symmetric. The objective \(F_{r,\kappa}\) in \cref{obj:def:design-objective} combines four terms: graph Laplacian exposure, a Laplacian-pseudoinverse component weighted by \(r\), a Schatten--2 robustness penalty weighted by \(\kappa\), and an aggregate balance penalty. The object of interest is the implementability loss \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\), the difference between the best implementable block-exchangeable sign design and the relaxed block-elliptope benchmark. \Cref{obj:prop:symmetry-reduction} establishes equality with the infimum over the broader sign-symmetric class \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}\).

The first step is a symmetry reduction. Under \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}, \cref{obj:prop:symmetry-reduction} shows that averaging over graph symmetries weakly decreases the objective for both relaxed covariance matrices and sign-symmetric assignment laws. Hence the relaxed infimum is exactly the infimum over \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\), and the implementable infimum is exactly the infimum over \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\). This reduction makes the implementability question finite-dimensional.

The paper then gives two exactness results. Under \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}, \cref{obj:thm:cut-corner-exactness} shows that, for \(r,\kappa\ge0\) satisfying the displayed strict cut-region inequality, the cut covariance \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\) is the unique relaxed minimizer. It is also generated by the symmetric two-point cut design, so the implementability loss is zero throughout that region. This exact finite-sample result supplies a transparent sufficient region for literal implementation of the relaxed optimum.

The second exactness result concerns iid assignment. Under \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} and \(r\ge0\), \cref{obj:thm:robust-corner-exactness} shows that \(I_n\) is a finite-robustness relaxed minimizer precisely when \(a+3b=2m\) and \(r=2b(a+b)\). On that affine-balanced locus, every positive \(\kappa\) makes \(I_n\) the unique relaxed minimizer. Elsewhere, relaxed minimizers converge entrywise to \(I_n\) as \(\kappa\to\infty\). Thus iid assignment is always implementable in this symmetric class, with exact finite-\(\kappa\) optimality characterized by the affine-balanced locus.

The main positive-loss result is arithmetic. When \(m\) is odd, each realized block sum has odd parity and therefore absolute value at least one. Under the homophily, low-scale, and odd-community conditions, \cref{obj:thm:gap-window} identifies a nonempty robustness range and an open interval of pseudoinverse weights in which the spread covariance \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}\) is the unique relaxed minimizer, lies outside \(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\), and yields \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)>0\). The same theorem also shows that every covariance in the block elliptope is implementable when \(m\) is even.

For computation outside that local positive-gap window, \cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star} gives an exact finite formula. Under \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}, for every \(r,\kappa\ge0\), the loss equals a sharp reduced quantity \(\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)\). The relaxed problem becomes a weighted simplex problem in three spectral coordinates, and implementability adds only the parity truncation \(d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\), where \(d_m=0\) for even \(m\) and \(d_m=2/m\) for odd \(m\). With positive robustness, the relaxed minimizer is unique and is obtained by an active-set calculation; if it violates the parity truncation, the implementable value is obtained on a one-dimensional boundary segment. With \(\kappa=0\), the theorem gives the corresponding exposed-face condition. The formal statements and proofs are machine-checked in Lean~4 to the extent described in the verification appendix.

The contribution is an exact finite-sample study of the covariance criterion \(F_{r,\kappa}\) in a homophilous two-block design. It characterizes literal implementability and positive loss and computes the exact objective-value price of returning from the relaxation to a feasible randomized assignment. Every statement in the formal layer is machine-checked in Lean~4 (Appendix~B).

\section{Setup and assumptions}

We work in a \leanref{S-1}{design-based finite-population assignment model}: the units are fixed, and the only random object is the treatment assignment. There are \(n=2m\) units partitioned into two equal communities. An assignment is represented by the sign vector \leanref{sym:Z}{\ensuremath{Z}}\(\in\{-1,1\}^n\), with a realized assignment denoted by \(z\), and an assignment law \leanref{sym:P}{\ensuremath{P}} is a probability mass function on this finite assignment space. This formulation follows the randomization-based tradition in which the assignment mechanism is the source of sampling variation \citep{horvitz1952,rubin1974}, while the network structure enters through the design criterion rather than through a superpopulation model.

The graph used by the criterion is a two-community weighted graph. The analysis rests first on the following homophily and scale conventions.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:two-block-homophily}[Two-Block Homophily]
The block size and edge intensities satisfy
\[
m\ge 2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a>b>0.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The constants \leanref{sym:a}{\ensuremath{a}} and \leanref{sym:b}{\ensuremath{b}} encode stronger interaction intensity within a community than across communities. This homophily restriction is specific to this analysis: it gives the two-block model a nondegenerate within-versus-between contrast while keeping both edge intensities positive.

\begin{definitionv}{def:two-block-graph}[Two-Block Graph Weights \(W_{ij}\)]
The vertex set is the disjoint union \(A_m\cup B_m\), where
\[
|A_m|=|B_m|=m.
\]
The graph weights \(W_{ij}\) are given as follows: if \(i\ne j\) and \(i\) and \(j\) lie in the same block, then
\[
W_{ij}=\frac{a}{m};
\]
if \(i\) and \(j\) lie in opposite blocks, then
\[
W_{ij}=\frac{b}{m};
\]
and for every vertex \(i\),
\[
W_{ii}=0.
\]
\end{definitionv}

Thus the communities \leanref{sym:A_m}{\ensuremath{A_m}} and \leanref{sym:B_m}{\ensuremath{B_m}} have equal size, and all heterogeneity in exposure strength is summarized by whether a pair lies within or across communities. Let \leanref{sym:L_m}{\ensuremath{L_m}} denote the graph Laplacian associated with these weights, with diagonal entries equal to weighted degrees and off-diagonal entries \(-W_{ij}\). We write \leanref{sym:L_m^dagger}{\ensuremath{L_m^\dagger}} for its Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse. Network-interference applications often use such weighted graphs to encode exposure or spillover structure \citep{hudgens2008,aronow2013,savje2017,leung2019,li2020}; here the two-block graph isolates the exact finite-sample geometry of a homophilous special case.

The sign representation is invariant to a global relabeling of treatment and control. We impose that invariance directly on the assignment law.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:balanced-sign-design}[Balanced Sign Symmetry]
The assignment law is sign-symmetric: for every \(z\in\{-1,1\}^n\),
\[
P(Z=z)=P(Z=-z).
\]
\end{assumptionv}

This symmetry condition is specific to this analysis. It expresses balance between the two treatment labels at the law level, yields zero one-point margins, and permits many forms of dependence and varying treated counts across realizations.

\begin{definitionv}{def:balanced-design-class}[Balanced sign class $\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}$]
Under \cref{obj:ass:balanced-sign-design}, the balanced sign class is
\[
\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}
=
\left\{
P \text{ on } \{-1,1\}^n :
P(Z=z)=P(Z=-z)\ \text{for every } z\in\{-1,1\}^n
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The class \leanref{sym:P_m^bal}{\ensuremath{\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}}} contains all sign-symmetric laws. For the two-block graph, the relevant symmetric subclass also treats units within a block exchangeably and treats the two equal blocks symmetrically.

\begin{definitionv}{def:block-exchangeable-design-class}[Block-exchangeable class $\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}$]
The block-exchangeable class is the set of all assignment laws
\[
P\in \mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}
\]
that are invariant under permutations within $A_m$, invariant under permutations within $B_m$, and invariant under swapping the two equal communities.
\end{definitionv}

For optimization of $F_{r,\kappa}$ over the sign-symmetric class $\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}$, the block-exchangeable class \leanref{sym:P_m^sym}{\ensuremath{\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}}} preserves the optimum: averaging over the two-block automorphism group weakly decreases this invariant objective. This reduction makes the maintained implementability question finite-dimensional.

For any assignment law, write \(X(P)\) for the second-moment matrix,
\[
X(P)=\mathbb E_P[ZZ^\top].
\]
This is a unit-diagonal second-moment (correlation) matrix; under the sign symmetry of \cref{obj:ass:balanced-sign-design} the one-point margins vanish, so for implementable laws it coincides with the covariance of \(Z\). We refer to \(X\) as a second-moment or correlation matrix throughout, reserving ``covariance'' for the sign-symmetric case; the relaxed matrices below are unit-diagonal correlation matrices that need not arise from any law. The covariance relaxation replaces such implementable second moments by block-symmetric correlation matrices. For scalars \(u\) and \(v\), \(X(u,v)\) denotes the matrix with diagonal entries equal to one, common off-diagonal covariance \(u\) within each block, and common covariance \(v\) across the two blocks.

\begin{definitionv}{def:block-elliptope}[Block Elliptope \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\)]
Under \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}, the block elliptope is
\[
\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}
=
\left\{
X(u,v):
1-u\ge 0,\ 
1+(m-1)u-mv\ge 0,\ 
1+(m-1)u+mv\ge 0
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The relaxed covariance set \leanref{sym:E_m^blk}{\ensuremath{\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}}} is the block-symmetric slice of the elliptope. The three displayed inequalities are the positive-semidefiniteness restrictions in the invariant directions induced by the two equal blocks. This convex relaxation is the object optimized before imposing that the covariance arise from an actual \(\{\pm1\}\) assignment law \citep{boyd2004,thiyageswaran2026}.

\begin{definitionv}{def:implementable-covariance-class}[Implementable covariance class $\mathcal C_m^{\pm}$]
The implementable covariance class is
\[
\mathcal C_m^{\pm}
=
\left\{
X(P): P\in \mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The set \leanref{sym:C_m^pm}{\ensuremath{\mathcal C_m^{\pm}}} records the discrete feasibility constraint that remains after symmetry reduction: a relaxed covariance matrix is useful for exact randomized design only when it is generated by some block-exchangeable sign law.

The objective combines graph smoothness, a pseudoinverse term, robustness, and a balance penalty. The scalar \leanref{sym:r}{\ensuremath{r}} weights the pseudoinverse term, \(\kappa\) weights the Schatten--2 norm penalty, and \leanref{sym:J_n}{\ensuremath{J_n}} denotes the \(n\times n\) all-ones matrix.

\begin{definitionv}{def:design-objective}[Design objective $F_{r,\kappa}$]
For $r,\kappa\ge 0$, the design objective evaluated at a covariance matrix $X$ is
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X)
=
\operatorname{tr}(L_m X)
+
r\,\operatorname{tr}(L_m^\dagger X)
+
\kappa \|X\|_{S_2}
+
\operatorname{tr}(J_n X).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The first term is the expected weighted disagreement of the sign assignment across graph edges: because $\operatorname{tr}(L_m X)=\sum_{i<j}W_{ij}\,\mathbb E\bigl[(Z_i-Z_j)^2\bigr]=2\sum_{i<j}W_{ij}\bigl(1-X_{ij}\bigr)$ for a unit-diagonal second moment, minimizing it penalizes sign dispersion across high-weight edges and rewards positive treatment-sign alignment there. The second term uses the Laplacian pseudoinverse to weight low-frequency graph directions, the third is a Frobenius-norm robustness penalty, and the final term $\operatorname{tr}(J_n X)=\mathbb E\bigl[(\sum_i Z_i)^2\bigr]$ penalizes imbalance in the aggregate sign vector. We treat $F_{r,\kappa}$ as a covariance design criterion in the spirit of the network-experiment SDP formulation of \citet{thiyageswaran2026}. The paper compares this criterion over the relaxed block elliptope and implementable sign designs and characterizes exactly when the optima coincide. The implementability loss measures their objective-value gap.

\begin{definitionv}{def:implementability-gap}[Implementability loss $\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)$]
The implementability loss is
\[
\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)
=
\inf_{P\in \mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}}
F_{r,\kappa}(X(P))
-
\inf_{X\in \mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}}
F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The quantity \ensuremath{\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)} is therefore the exact price of requiring a covariance matrix to be induced by a finite randomized assignment law rather than merely satisfying the block elliptope constraints.

A useful handle on implementability is provided by the two block sums. For a given law \(P\), these sums retain the information relevant to the block-symmetric second moments.

\begin{definitionv}{def:block-sum-handle}[Block-sum law $\mathcal L_P(S_A,S_B)$]
For an assignment law $P$, the block-sum law is the law under $P$ of
\[
(S_A,S_B)
=
\left(
\sum_{i\in A_m} Z_i,\,
\sum_{i\in B_m} Z_i
\right).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The block-sum law \(\mathcal L_P(S_A,S_B)\) will be used to express the discrete restrictions that separate \(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\) from \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\).

Two additional restrictions are stated here because later exact-loss results invoke them explicitly.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:low-scale-two-block}[Low-Scale Two-Block Regime]
The two-block edge intensities satisfy
\[
a+3b<2m.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The low-scale regime is specific to this analysis. It is a finite-sample scale restriction comparing graph intensity with block size; for fixed \(a\) and \(b\), it is automatically satisfied once \(m\) is large enough.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:odd-community-size}[Odd Community Size]
The block size \(m\) is odd.
\end{assumptionv}

The odd-block condition is also specific to this analysis. It isolates the parity case in which every realized block sum has absolute value at least one, which is exactly where the later implementability loss result has content.

The following reduction justifies working with the block elliptope and the block-exchangeable assignment class while preserving the relevant optima.

\begin{lemmav}{prop:symmetry-reduction}[Symmetry Reduction Principle]
Assume \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}. Let \(r,\kappa\in[0,\infty)\). Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Relaxed matrices.)} For every positive semidefinite matrix \(X\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) with \(X_{ii}=1\) for every \(i\), let \(u\) and \(v\) be the averages of the off-diagonal within-community and across-community entries of \(X\), respectively. The block matrix \(X(u,v)\) of \cref{obj:def:block-elliptope} has feasible block-elliptope parameters, and
\[
F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(u,v)\bigr)\le F_{r,\kappa}(X),
\]
where \(F_{r,\kappa}\) is \cref{obj:def:design-objective}.
\item \textbf{(Assignment laws.)} For every assignment law \(P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:balanced-design-class}, its orbit average over the full two-block automorphism group belongs to \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:block-exchangeable-design-class}, has zero one-point margins,
\[
\mathbb E[Z_i]=0\qquad\text{for every }i,
\]
and satisfies
\[
F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(P^{\mathrm{orb}})\bigr)\le F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(P)\bigr).
\]
\item \textbf{(Relaxed infimum.)}
\[
\inf_{\substack{X\succeq0\\ X_{ii}=1\ \forall i}} F_{r,\kappa}(X)
=
\inf_{X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}} F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]
\item \textbf{(Implementable infimum.)}
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}} F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(P)\bigr)
=
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}} F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(P)\bigr).
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

The proof is deferred to \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.

\Cref{obj:prop:symmetry-reduction} is the bridge between the original finite assignment problem and the two-parameter geometry used in the rest of the paper. It says that, for the objective in \cref{obj:def:design-objective}, symmetrizing either a relaxed covariance matrix or an assignment law is weakly improving. Consequently, the relaxed benchmark is exactly the infimum over \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\), and the implementable benchmark is exactly the infimum over \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\).

\section{Exactness at implementable designs}

The symmetry reduction in \cref{obj:prop:symmetry-reduction} leaves a precise question: when does the relaxed optimum over \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) coincide with a covariance matrix generated by an actual block-exchangeable sign design? This section records two exact cases. We use three block covariances: the cut covariance \leanref{sym:X_cut}{\ensuremath{X_{\mathrm{cut}}}}\(=X(1,-1)\), the identity \(I_n=X(0,0)\) corresponding to independent fair Bernoulli signs, and the spread covariance \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}=X\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{m-1},0\right)\). The implementing laws are the two-point cut law \(P_{\mathrm{cut}}\) and the independent fair-sign law \(P_{\mathrm{iid}}\). These designs represent different design instincts: one separates the homophilous communities, while the other spreads randomization independently across all units \citep{efron1971,morgan2012,harshaw2019}.

The cut design is optimal in a strict small-\(r\) region. There, the graph and balance terms favor separating the two blocks strongly enough to keep the relaxed optimum at the cut corner after including the pseudoinverse and robustness terms.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:cut-corner-exactness}[Cut-Corner Exactness]
Assume the two-block homophily condition in \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}. Let \(r,\kappa\in\mathbb R\) satisfy
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Nonnegative tradeoff.)} \(0\le r\).
\item \textbf{(Nonnegative robustness.)} \(0\le \kappa\).
\item \textbf{(Cut region.)}
\[
r< r_{\mathrm{cut}}(m,a,b,\kappa)
:= \max\!\left\{0,\,
\min\!\left(
2b(a+b)\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{a-b}\right),
2b(2m-2b-\kappa)
\right)\right\}.
\]
\end{itemize}
Then \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\), and for every
\(X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) with \(X\ne X_{\mathrm{cut}}\),
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})<F_{r,\kappa}(X),
\]
where \(F_{r,\kappa}\) is the objective in \cref{obj:def:design-objective}. Moreover,
\[
\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)=0,
\]
with \(\Delta_m^{\pm}\) as in \cref{obj:def:implementability-gap}. Finally,
\(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\), and for every
\(X\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\) with \(X\ne X_{\mathrm{cut}}\),
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})<F_{r,\kappa}(X),
\]
where \(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\) is the class in \cref{obj:def:implementable-covariance-class}; the cut design satisfies
\[
P_{\mathrm{cut}}\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
X(P_{\mathrm{cut}})=X_{\mathrm{cut}},
\]
with \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\) as in \cref{obj:def:block-exchangeable-design-class}.
\end{theoremv}

\cref{obj:thm:cut-corner-exactness} is an exact attainability statement. The displayed strict inequality involving \(r_{\mathrm{cut}}(m,a,b,\kappa)\) gives a sufficient region in which the relaxed block-elliptope optimum is unique, implementable, and has zero implementability loss. The implementing law \leanref{sym:P_cut}{\ensuremath{P_{\mathrm{cut}}}} is the symmetric two-point law on the cut assignment and its global sign reversal. This result characterizes optimality for the stated covariance criterion \(F_{r,\kappa}\).

The second exactness result concerns the robust corner \(I_n\). Independent fair assignment is always implementable, but the theorem below shows that its relaxed optimality is much more restrictive: a finite positive robustness weight can make \(I_n\) optimal only on a particular affine relation among the graph scale and the pseudoinverse weight.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:robust-corner-exactness}[Robust Corner Exactness]
Let \(m\in\mathbb N\) and \(a,b,r\in\mathbb R\). Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Two-block homophily.)} \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} holds for \((m,a,b)\).
\item \textbf{(Nonnegative pseudoinverse weight.)} \(r\ge 0\).
\end{itemize}
For the objective \(F_{r,\kappa}\) of \cref{obj:def:design-objective}, the following statements hold.

First, there exists a finite nonnegative robustness weight \(\kappa\) such that \(I_n\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) and
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(I_n)\le F_{r,\kappa}(X)
\qquad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}},
\]
if and only if
\[
a+3b=2m
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
r=2b(a+b).
\]

Second, on this affine-balanced locus, every positive robustness weight makes \(I_n\) the unique relaxed minimizer: if
\[
a+3b=2m
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
r=2b(a+b),
\]
then for every \(\kappa>0\),
\[
I_n\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}
\]
and, for every \(X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) with \(X\ne I_n\),
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(I_n)<F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]

Third, the iid design satisfies
\[
P_{\mathrm{iid}}\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}
\]
for the block-exchangeable class of \cref{obj:def:block-exchangeable-design-class}, and
\[
X(P_{\mathrm{iid}})=I_n.
\]

Finally, off the affine-balanced locus, \(I_n\) is never a minimizer at any positive finite robustness weight, and relaxed minimizers converge entrywise to \(I_n\) as \(\kappa\to\infty\). Precisely, if
\[
\neg\bigl(a+3b=2m\ \text{and}\ r=2b(a+b)\bigr),
\]
then for every \(\kappa>0\) it is not the case that
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(I_n)\le F_{r,\kappa}(X)
\qquad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}.
\]
Moreover, for any family \((X_\kappa)_{\kappa\in\mathbb R}\) such that, for every \(\kappa>0\),
\[
X_\kappa\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
F_{r,\kappa}(X_\kappa)\le F_{r,\kappa}(X)
\ \text{ for every }X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}},
\]
each matrix entry converges to the corresponding entry of \(I_n\):
\[
(X_\kappa)_{ij}\longrightarrow (I_n)_{ij}
\qquad\text{as }\kappa\to\infty
\]
for every \(i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\).
\end{theoremv}

\cref{obj:thm:robust-corner-exactness} separates implementability from optimality. The iid law \leanref{sym:P_iid}{\ensuremath{P_{\mathrm{iid}}}} belongs to the block-exchangeable design class and attains \(I_n\), but this implementable covariance is a relaxed minimizer at finite robustness only when \(a+3b=2m\) and \(r=2b(a+b)\). Off that affine-balanced locus, relaxed minimizers converge entrywise to \(I_n\) as \(\kappa\to\infty\), yet no positive finite value of \(\kappa\) makes \(I_n\) itself optimal. Thus robustness can make iid assignment an asymptotic limiting target without making it an exact finite-\(\kappa\) solution.

Together, \cref{obj:thm:cut-corner-exactness} and~\cref{obj:thm:robust-corner-exactness} identify two implementable covariances whose roles are sharply different. The cut covariance is exactly optimal throughout the stated strict cut region, yielding zero implementability loss there. The iid covariance is implementable everywhere under the maintained homophily conditions, with exact relaxed optimality on the stated affine-balanced locus and limiting optimality elsewhere as robustness diverges. The next section turns to parameter regions with strictly positive implementability loss.

\section{Positive loss and sharp computation}

The preceding section identifies two implementable covariances that can solve the relaxed problem exactly. We now study relaxed points in \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) lying beyond the covariances attainable by balanced \(\{\pm1\}\) assignment laws. This is the same basic tension that appears in correlation and semidefinite relaxations more broadly: the convex benchmark can contain exposed points beyond the original discrete objects \citep{goemans1995,deza1997,boyd2004}. In the present two-block design problem, the obstruction is finite and arithmetical. When \(m\) is odd, each realized block sum has odd parity, imposing a positive-mass restriction away from the pure within-block spectral direction.

The next result gives a parameter region in which this obstruction is binding. It uses two-block homophily from \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}, the low-scale condition from \cref{obj:ass:low-scale-two-block}, and the odd-block condition from \cref{obj:ass:odd-community-size}. For robustness weights in the stated range, the relevant relaxed covariance is the spread covariance \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}\), the vertex with spectral coordinates \((m/(m-1),0,0)\). The theorem states that, in a nonempty interval of pseudoinverse weights, this relaxed point is uniquely optimal but not implementable.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:gap-window}[Positive-Gap Window]
Suppose \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} and \cref{obj:ass:low-scale-two-block} hold for the two-block model with block size \(m\) and weights \(a,b\). Let
\[
q=2(m-1),\qquad
\kappa_{\mathrm{gap}}(m,a,b)
=
\frac{(2m-a-3b)(a-b)\sqrt q}{a+b},
\]
and
\[
r_{\mathrm{cut,gap}}(m,a,b,\kappa)
=
\max\left\{0,\;2b(a+b)\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{a-b}\right)\right\}.
\]
Define
\[
R_x^-(m,a,b,\kappa)
=
2b(a+b)\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{(a-b)\sqrt q}\right),
\qquad
R_x^+(m,a,b,\kappa)
=
(a+b)\left(2m-a-b-\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt q}\right),
\]
and
\[
r_{\mathrm{gap}}^-(m,a,b,\kappa)
=
\frac{R_x^-(m,a,b,\kappa)+R_x^+(m,a,b,\kappa)}{2},
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+(m,a,b,\kappa)
=
R_x^+(m,a,b,\kappa).
\]

Then the following two statements hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Odd block size.)} If \cref{obj:ass:odd-community-size} holds, then for every \(\kappa\) satisfying
\[
0\le \kappa < \kappa_{\mathrm{gap}}(m,a,b),
\]
one has
\[
r_{\mathrm{cut,gap}}(m,a,b,\kappa)
<
r_{\mathrm{gap}}^-(m,a,b,\kappa)
<
r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+(m,a,b,\kappa).
\]
Moreover, for every
\[
r_{\mathrm{gap}}^-(m,a,b,\kappa)
<
r
<
r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+(m,a,b,\kappa),
\]
the spread covariance \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}\) belongs to \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\), and it is the unique strict minimizer of the design objective \(F_{r,\kappa}\) from \cref{obj:def:design-objective} over \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\): for every \(X\in \mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) with \(X\ne X_{\mathrm{spread}}\),
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{spread}})<F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]
At the same time,
\[
X_{\mathrm{spread}}\notin \mathcal C_m^{\pm},
\]
where \(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\) is the implementable covariance class of \cref{obj:def:implementable-covariance-class}, and the implementability loss of \cref{obj:def:implementability-gap} satisfies
\[
\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)>0.
\]

\item \textbf{(Even block size.)} If \(m\) is even, then every covariance in the block elliptope is implementable:
\[
\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\subseteq \mathcal C_m^{\pm}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\cref{obj:thm:gap-window} is the paper's positive-loss statement. Under its two-block homophily, low-scale, and odd-community hypotheses, the upper bound \(\kappa_{\mathrm{gap}}(m,a,b)\) defines a robustness range in which the interval \((r_{\mathrm{gap}}^-,r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+)\) is nonempty and lies strictly beyond the cut-side comparison threshold \(r_{\mathrm{cut,gap}}\). Inside that interval, the relaxed optimum is not merely one unattainable minimizer among many; it is the unique strict minimizer over \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\). Since \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}\notin\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\), imposing implementability necessarily raises the objective value, giving \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)>0\).

The even-\(m\) clause marks the opposite parity case. When each block can have zero sign sum, every relaxed covariance in the block elliptope slice is generated by some block-exchangeable sign law. Thus \cref{obj:thm:gap-window} ties positive loss specifically to the odd-block parity restriction.

The interval result is deliberately local. It identifies one open region where the spread covariance is the relaxed optimum and the loss is strictly positive, but it does not assert a closed-form global boundary for exact attainability outside that region. For computation at arbitrary \(r\) and \(\kappa\), the relevant object is instead the sharp finite active-set formula below.

The formula works in the reduced spectral simplex \(T_m\), obtained from \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}: the block symmetry diagonalizes every \(X(u,v)\) into three eigendirections, with coordinates \(x=1-u\) on the within-block contrast space (multiplicity \(q=2(m-1)\)), \(y=1+(m-1)u-mv\) on the between-block contrast direction, and \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}=1+(m-1)u+mv\) on the all-ones (aggregate) direction; the subscript ``\(\mathrm{sp}\)'' marks this last, spread-across-all-units coordinate and distinguishes it from an ambient sign index. In these coordinates \(F_{r,\kappa}\) becomes the scalar objective \(\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})=c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}\), and implementability adds only the parity truncation \(d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\), with \(d_m=0\) for even block sizes and \(d_m=2/m\) for odd block sizes. The theorem's remaining ingredients are the following finite objects, established in \cref{sec:deferred-proofs} (\cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-active-set} and~\cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-truncation}). For linear coefficients \(\alpha^\sharp=(c_x/q,c_y,c_z)\) and norm weights \(\beta^\sharp=(1/q,1,1)\), the weighted-simplex objective is \(\Phi_{\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa}(t)=\sum_i\alpha^\sharp_i t_i+\kappa\sqrt{\sum_i\beta^\sharp_i t_i^2}\) on the simplex of mass \(2m\); an \emph{admissible} active support–multiplier pair \((S,\lambda)\) is a nonempty \(S\subseteq\{x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\}\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\) with \(\sum_{i\in S}(\lambda-\alpha^\sharp_i)^2/\beta^\sharp_i=\kappa^2\), \(\alpha^\sharp_i<\lambda\) on \(S\), and \(\lambda\le\alpha^\sharp_j\) off \(S\); the associated \emph{active-set point} \(t^{S,\lambda}\) puts mass \(2m\,((\lambda-\alpha^\sharp_i)/\beta^\sharp_i)/\sum_{h\in S}(\lambda-\alpha^\sharp_h)/\beta^\sharp_h\) on each \(i\in S\) and zero off \(S\); \(\operatorname{truncSelector}\) returns the endpoint or interior optimizer of \(\Phi\) restricted to the truncation segment \(\{y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=d_m\}\); and \(\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)\) is the resulting constrained-minus-unconstrained reduced value, i.e.\ the minimum of \(\phi_{r,\kappa}\) over \(T_m\cap\{y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\}\) minus its minimum over \(T_m\). The theorem shows this \(\rho_\star\) equals the implementability loss and gives its finite case split.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:sharp-rho-star}[Sharp Rounding Loss]
Under \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}, fix $r,\kappa\in[0,\infty)$.  Let
\[
q=2(m-1),\qquad
c_x=q\Bigl((a+b)+\frac{r}{a+b}\Bigr),\qquad
c_y=2b+\frac{r}{2b},\qquad
c_z=2m,
\]
let
\[
T_m=\{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}):x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0,\ qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\},
\]
let
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
=c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2},
\]
and let $d_m=0$ if $m$ is even and $d_m=2/m$ if $m$ is odd.  Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sharp equality and range.)}
\[
\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)=\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa),
\qquad
\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)\ge0,
\qquad
\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\ge0,
\]
where $\Delta_m^{\pm}$ is the implementability gap of \cref{obj:def:implementability-gap}.

\item \textbf{(Zero loss.)}
\[
\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)=0
\]
if and only if there exists $(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m$ such that
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
\le
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x',y',z'_{\mathrm{sp}})
\quad\text{for every }(x',y',z'_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m,
\]
and
\[
d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}.
\]

\item \textbf{(Strict robustness.)}
If $\kappa>0$, then there is a unique relaxed minimizer of $\phi_{r,\kappa}$ over $T_m$.  Moreover,
\[
\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)=0
\]
if and only if every relaxed minimizer $(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m$ satisfies
\[
d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}.
\]

\item \textbf{(Linear case.)}
If $\kappa=0$, then for every $(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m$, the point $(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})$ minimizes $\phi_{r,0}$ over $T_m$ if and only if
\[
x\ne0\Rightarrow \frac{c_x}{q}\le c_y\ \text{and}\ \frac{c_x}{q}\le c_z,
\]
\[
y\ne0\Rightarrow c_y\le \frac{c_x}{q}\ \text{and}\ c_y\le c_z,
\]
and
\[
z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ne0\Rightarrow c_z\le \frac{c_x}{q}\ \text{and}\ c_z\le c_y.
\]

\item \textbf{(Active-set formula.)}
If $\kappa>0$, then there exist an active support $S\subseteq\{x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\}$ and a multiplier $\lambda\in\mathbb R$ such that $S$ and $\lambda$ are admissible for
\[
\alpha^\sharp=\Bigl(\frac{c_x}{q},c_y,c_z\Bigr),
\qquad
\beta^\sharp=\Bigl(\frac1q,1,1\Bigr),
\qquad
\kappa.
\]
Writing $t^{S,\lambda}=(t_x^{S,\lambda},t_y^{S,\lambda},t_z^{S,\lambda})$ for the associated active-set point on the simplex of mass $2m$, the reduced point
\[
\Bigl(\frac{t_x^{S,\lambda}}{q},t_y^{S,\lambda},t_z^{S,\lambda}\Bigr)
\]
belongs to $T_m$, minimizes $\phi_{r,\kappa}$ over $T_m$, and the relaxed reduced value is
\[
2m\,\lambda.
\]

\item \textbf{(Truncation correction.)}
If no relaxed minimizer $(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m$ satisfies $d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}$, then the implementable reduced value equals the weighted-simplex objective with coefficients $\alpha^\sharp$, weights $\beta^\sharp$, and robustness weight $\kappa$, evaluated at the point on the truncation segment
\[
H_{d_m}=\{(2m-d_m,s,d_m-s):0\le s\le d_m\}
\]
selected by $\operatorname{truncSelector}(2m,d_m,\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa)$:
\[
\operatorname{val}_{\mathrm{impl}}(m,a,b,r,\kappa)
=
\Phi_{\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa}
\!\left(
2m-d_m,\,
s_\star,\,
d_m-s_\star
\right),
\]
where
\[
s_\star=\operatorname{truncSelector}(2m,d_m,\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa).
\]

\item \textbf{(Even block size.)}
If $m$ is even, then
\[
\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)=0.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star} turns the implementability question into a finite calculation. The sharp loss \ensuremath{\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)} is exactly the implementability loss \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\), so the formula is not an approximation to the rounding penalty. Zero loss occurs precisely when at least one relaxed minimizer satisfies the parity-induced lower bound \(d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\). When \(\kappa>0\), the active-set result yields a unique relaxed minimizer, and the zero-loss condition can be checked at that point alone.

The active-set part supplies the promised finite case split. The coefficients \(\alpha^\sharp=(c_x/q,c_y,c_z)\) and weights \(\beta^\sharp=(1/q,1,1)\) convert the reduced objective into a weighted simplex problem over total mass \(2m\). For positive robustness, an admissible support \(S\) and multiplier \(\lambda\) identify the relaxed minimizer and its value. If that minimizer violates the implementability truncation, the remaining optimization is one-dimensional: the implementable value is obtained on the segment \(H_{d_m}\) by the selector appearing in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star}. The theorem thereby gives a complete finite procedure for computing \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\) for any admissible \(m,a,b,r,\kappa\).

The two main results in this section therefore play complementary roles. \Cref{obj:thm:gap-window} provides an interpretable open region in which the relaxation has a strictly unattainable optimum for odd block sizes. \Cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star} gives the exact loss everywhere under \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}, including the linear case \(\kappa=0\), the unique-minimizer case \(\kappa>0\), and the even-block case where the loss vanishes. For interference-aware randomized design, the practical implication is a two-step calculation: solve the covariance relaxation, then check its optimizer against the finite sign-assignment restrictions and apply the exact active-set adjustment when needed.

\paragraph{A worked example and parameter map.} Take \(m=3\), \(a=2\), \(b=1\), and \(\kappa=0\); these satisfy homophily (\(a>b>0\)) and the low-scale regime (\(a+3b=5<2m=6\)). Here \(q=4\), the parity truncation is \(d_3=2/3\), and the reduced coefficients are \(c_x=q\bigl((a+b)+r/(a+b)\bigr)=4\bigl(3+r/3\bigr)\), \(c_y=2b+r/(2b)=2+r/2\), and \(c_z=2m=6\). The gap-window robustness bound is \(\kappa_{\mathrm{gap}}=(2m-a-3b)(a-b)\sqrt q/(a+b)=2/3\), so \(\kappa=0\) lies in the admissible range. Mapping over \(r\ge0\) then exhibits all three regimes of the theory. For \(r<r_{\mathrm{cut}}=\min\{2b(a+b),\,2b(2m-2b)\}=\min\{6,8\}=6\), the cut covariance \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}=(0,6,0)\) is the unique relaxed optimum and is implementable, so \(\Delta_3^{\pm}=0\) (\cref{obj:thm:cut-corner-exactness}). For \(r\) in the gap window \((r_{\mathrm{gap}}^-,r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+)=(7.5,\,9)\), the spread covariance \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}=(3/2,0,0)\) is the unique relaxed optimum but is \emph{not} implementable (\cref{obj:thm:gap-window}). At \(r=8\), for instance, the unconstrained reduced optimum is at \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}\) with value \(c_x\cdot\tfrac32=34\), while the implementable optimum lies on the parity slice \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2/3\) at \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})=(4/3,\,2/3,\,0)\) with value \(308/9\); hence
\[
\Delta_3^{\pm}(8,0)=\tfrac{308}{9}-34=\tfrac29\approx0.222,
\]
a strictly positive but, in this instance, modest price relative to the optimal value \(34\) (about \(0.65\%\)). For even community size the picture collapses: \(d_m=0\), the parity truncation is vacuous, and \(\Delta_m^{\pm}\equiv0\) throughout. This single example only illustrates the three regimes for one parameter choice; it does not establish that the loss is generally small or generally negligible, and \cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star} should be used to compute \(\Delta_m^{\pm}\) at any specific \((m,a,b,r,\kappa)\) of interest. How large the loss can become as \(a,b,r,\kappa\) scale is not settled here and is left open.

\section{Discussion and extensions}

\paragraph{Relation to covariance-based network design.} The design criterion and elliptope relaxation build on covariance-based experimental design for network interference, particularly the semidefinite formulation of \citet{thiyageswaran2026}, and on the broader elliptope-versus-cut-polytope literature \citep{goemans1995,deza1997,boyd2004}. This paper contributes an exact implementability theory for the homophilous two-block family: a joint symmetry reduction of relaxed covariances and implementable laws, an odd-parity obstruction producing positive loss, and a finite active-set correction computing \(\Delta_m^{\pm}\) exactly.

Viewed abstractly, \(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\) is a moment set for block-exchangeable Rademacher vectors and a two-coordinate projection of the correlation polytope. The paper pins down the exact boundary of this low-dimensional exchangeable slice through the odd-block parity truncation \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge 2/m\), then converts that boundary into the exact rounding loss. Future work can connect this family-specific characterization more fully to general correlation-polytope and exchangeable-moment results.

The results above give a finite-sample account of when a covariance relaxation can be read literally as a randomized design. In the two-block homophily model, the relaxed block elliptope \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) serves both as a computational device and as a benchmark for exact implementability by a sign-symmetric, block-exchangeable \(\{\pm1\}\) assignment law. \Cref{obj:thm:cut-corner-exactness} gives a region in which the relaxed optimum is generated by an assignment law, whereas \cref{obj:thm:gap-window} gives a region with a uniquely optimal relaxed covariance and strictly positive implementability loss.

For experimental design under interference, the covariance matrix is a property of the assignment mechanism, and implementation requires a covariance induced by a randomization law. The implementability loss \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\) therefore has a direct design interpretation: it is the exact objective-value price of returning from the relaxed covariance problem to feasible randomized assignment. \Cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star} computes that price through a finite active-set calculation.

The iid result points in the opposite direction. Independent fair assignment is always available as a randomized design and has a familiar role in design-based inference. \Cref{obj:thm:robust-corner-exactness} identifies exact finite-robustness optimality on the affine-balanced locus \(a+3b=2m\) and \(r=2b(a+b)\), while relaxed minimizers elsewhere converge to \(I_n\) as \(\kappa\) grows. Thus robustness rationalizes iid assignment either as an exact solution on the locus or as a limiting target elsewhere.

The positive-loss result is arithmetic. For odd \(m\), each realized block sum has absolute value at least one. Under the hypotheses and robustness range stated in \cref{obj:thm:gap-window}, this parity restriction binds and places the uniquely optimal spread covariance outside \(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\). For even \(m\), every relaxed block covariance is implementable in the maintained symmetric model. The theorem therefore isolates odd-block parity as the source of positive loss.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

The two-block structure is deliberately stylized, but it captures a common empirical feature of networked populations: ties and exposures are often stronger within social groups than between them. Homophily and community structure are central themes in social network analysis \citep{mcpherson2001,newman2002}, and stochastic block models provide a canonical probabilistic language for such clustered networks \citep{holland1983,abbe2017}. Field and policy experiments with interference often face analogous clustering through villages, schools, households, online neighborhoods, or other exposure groups \citep{banerjee2013,baird2018,ugander2020,li2021}. The present model abstracts from those empirical details in order to isolate an exact design question: after symmetry reduction, which relaxed covariance matrices are generated by actual sign assignments?

Several extensions follow naturally, but they require additional arguments beyond the statements in this paper. The present results do not cover unequal block sizes, where both the spectral reduction and the block-sum lattice would require new arguments. The present results also do not cover more than two communities; the three-coordinate reduction \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\) and the implementability characterization would require modification. In those settings, one should not expect the parity correction \(d_m\) or the active-set formula in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star} to carry over without modification.

A second extension concerns additional restrictions on the randomization scheme. The class \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\) imposes sign symmetry and block exchangeability, but it does not require a fixed treated count in every realization, nor does it impose covariate balance, rerandomization acceptance rules, or cluster-level assignment constraints. Such restrictions are common in empirical practice and can be important for finite-sample precision and credibility \citep{savje2021,zigler2018}. They would shrink the implementable covariance class and could introduce losses even in cases where \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\subseteq\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\) under the present assumptions.

A third issue is that the graph itself is treated as fixed. In applications, measured networks may be incomplete, exposure weights may be estimated, and the relevant interference neighborhood may be uncertain. The objective \(F_{r,\kappa}\) can be interpreted conditionally on the chosen graph weights, but the results here do not address robustness to network measurement error or to alternative exposure mappings. Those questions are important for empirical deployment because different plausible graphs can imply different design priorities, especially when homophily is strong.

Finally, the analysis is finite-sample throughout. The exact formulas are indexed by \(m,a,b,r,\kappa\), and the odd-block obstruction is stated at the finite block size used by the design. Although the parity truncation becomes numerically smaller as \(m\) grows, the consequences for objective values depend on the full set of coefficients in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star}. Any asymptotic simplification should therefore be derived from the finite active-set expression rather than assumed from the geometry alone.

\section{Reduced geometry and proof ingredients}

This appendix makes visible the auxiliary geometry behind the two main optimization statements: the reduced coordinates, vertex certificates, parity restriction, and finite active-set calculation used in \cref{obj:thm:gap-window} and~\cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star}. The reduction is a low-dimensional convex-analytic one: the block symmetry turns the covariance problem into a weighted simplex problem, in the same general spirit as elliptope and semidefinite relaxations for discrete optimization \citep{goemans1995,deza1997,boyd2004}.

The first ingredient identifies the spectral coordinates in which the block elliptope becomes a simplex and the objective becomes a scalar function.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:block-spectral-coordinates}[Block Spectral Coordinates]
For any real numbers $r,\kappa,u,v$, assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Homophily.)} \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} holds for the block size $m$ and weights $a,b$.
\item \textbf{(Spectral coordinates.)} Set
\[
q=2(m-1),\qquad
x=1-u,\qquad
y=1+(m-1)u-mv,\qquad
z_{\mathrm{sp}}=1+(m-1)u+mv .
\]
\item \textbf{(Reduced coefficients.)} Set
\[
c_x=q\left((a+b)+\frac{r}{a+b}\right),\qquad
c_y=2b+\frac{r}{2b},\qquad
c_z=2m .
\]
\end{itemize}
Then the block covariance matrix $X(u,v)$ belongs to the block elliptope $\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}$ if and only if
\[
x\ge 0,\qquad y\ge 0,\qquad z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge 0,\qquad
qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m .
\]
Moreover, for the design objective of \cref{obj:def:design-objective},
\[
F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(u,v)\bigr)
=
c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}.
\]
Finally,
\[
X_{\mathrm{cut}}=X(1,-1)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(1-1,\;1+(m-1)\cdot 1-m(-1),\;1+(m-1)\cdot 1+m(-1))
=(0,2m,0),
\]
while
\[
I_n=X(0,0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(1-0,\;1+(m-1)\cdot 0-m\cdot 0,\;1+(m-1)\cdot 0+m\cdot 0)
=(1,1,1).
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}]
Throughout write \(n=2m\), \(q=2(m-1)\), and
\[
x=1-u,\qquad
y=1+(m-1)u-mv,\qquad
z_{\mathrm{sp}}=1+(m-1)u+mv .
\]
\cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} gives \(m\ge2\), hence \(q>0\) and \(m>0\), and it gives \(a>b>0\), hence \(a+b>0\) and \(2b>0\); these are the only nonvanishing denominators used below.

\emph{Step 1 (the two parameters are read off from the matrix).}  Since \(|A_m|=m\ge2\), we may pick two distinct indices \(i,j\in A_m\), and since \(|B_m|=m\ge1\) we may pick \(k\in B_m\).  By the entry description of \(X(u,v)\),
\[
X(u,v)_{ij}=u,
\qquad
X(u,v)_{ik}=v .
\]
Consequently \(X(u,v)=X(u',v')\) forces \(u'=u\) and \(v'=v\): the block-symmetric parameterization is injective.  Therefore the only representation of \(X(u,v)\) as a member of \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:block-elliptope} is the one with parameters \((u,v)\), and
\[
X(u,v)\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}
\iff
1-u\ge0,\quad
1+(m-1)u-mv\ge0,\quad
1+(m-1)u+mv\ge0,
\]
that is, \(x\ge0\), \(y\ge0\), \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0\).  Moreover the affine identity
\[
qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}
=2(m-1)(1-u)+\bigl(1+(m-1)u-mv\bigr)+\bigl(1+(m-1)u+mv\bigr)
=2m
\]
holds for all \(u,v\), the \(u\)-terms and the \(v\)-terms cancelling.  Hence block-elliptope membership is equivalent to \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\), which is the first assertion. % lean: blockSymMatrix_mem_blockElliptope_iff_reducedTriangle

\emph{Step 2 (two pair-counting identities).}  Call a matrix \(w\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) \emph{block-patterned with data \((d,\sigma,c)\)} if \(w_{ii}=d\) for every \(i\), \(w_{ij}=\sigma\) whenever \(i\ne j\) lie in the same community, and \(w_{ij}=c\) whenever \(i\) and \(j\) lie in opposite communities.  Among the \(n^2\) ordered index pairs there are \(n=2m\) diagonal pairs, \(2m(m-1)\) same-community off-diagonal pairs, and \(2m\cdot m\) opposite-community pairs, so
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}
=2m\,d+2m(m-1)\,\sigma+2m^2\,c .
\]
% lean: block_pair_sum
With the community sign vector \(s\in\mathbb R^{n}\) defined by \(s_i=1\) for \(i\in A_m\) and \(s_i=-1\) for \(i\in B_m\), the sign product \(s_is_j\) equals \(+1\) on diagonal and same-community pairs and \(-1\) on opposite-community pairs, so the same count with the sign weight gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_is_j\,w_{ij}
=2m\,d+2m(m-1)\,\sigma-2m^2\,c .
\]
% lean: block_signed_pair_sum

\emph{Step 3 (the four terms of the objective).}  The matrix \(X(u,v)\) is block-patterned with data \((1,u,v)\).  Applying the unsigned count with \((d,\sigma,c)=(1,u,v)\) gives the balance term
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(J_nX(u,v)\bigr)
=\sum_{i,j}X(u,v)_{ij}
=2m+2m(m-1)u+2m^2v
=2m\bigl(1+(m-1)u+mv\bigr)
=2m\,z_{\mathrm{sp}} .
\]
% lean: block_pair_sum
The entrywise square of \(X(u,v)\) is block-patterned with data \((1,u^2,v^2)\), so the same count gives \(\|X(u,v)\|_{S_2}^2=2m+2m(m-1)u^2+2m^2v^2\); on the other hand, expanding the three spectral coordinates,
\[
qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2
=2(m-1)(1-u)^2+2\bigl(1+(m-1)u\bigr)^2+2m^2v^2
=2m+2m(m-1)u^2+2m^2v^2 ,
\]
the linear-in-\(u\) terms \(-4(m-1)u\) and \(+4(m-1)u\) cancelling.  Hence
\[
\|X(u,v)\|_{S_2}=\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}.
\]
% lean: block_pair_sum
Next, \(\operatorname{tr}X(u,v)=n=2m\) because the diagonal is constant equal to one.  Let
\[
\Pi_1=\frac{1}{n}J_n,
\qquad
\Pi_s=\frac{1}{n}\,ss^\top
\]
be the orthogonal projections onto the all-ones direction and onto the community-sign direction.  Dividing the two displays of Step 2 (applied with \((d,\sigma,c)=(1,u,v)\)) by \(n=2m\) gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\Pi_1X(u,v)\bigr)=z_{\mathrm{sp}},
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\Pi_sX(u,v)\bigr)
=\frac{2m+2m(m-1)u-2m^2v}{2m}
=y .
\]
% lean: block_signed_pair_sum
The pseudoinverse \(L_m^\dagger\) enters the objective only through its spectral action, which on the two-block graph is
\[
L_m^\dagger
=\frac{1}{a+b}\bigl(I_n-\Pi_1-\Pi_s\bigr)+\frac{1}{2b}\,\Pi_s ,
\]
i.e.\ eigenvalue \(1/(a+b)\) on the within-community contrast space, \(1/(2b)\) on the community-sign direction, and \(0\) on the all-ones direction.  Therefore, using \(\operatorname{tr}X(u,v)=2m\) and the affine identity \(2m-y-z_{\mathrm{sp}}=qx\) of Step 1,
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(L_m^\dagger X(u,v)\bigr)
=\frac{2m-z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y}{a+b}+\frac{y}{2b}
=\frac{qx}{a+b}+\frac{y}{2b}.
\]
For the graph term, every vertex has the same weighted degree
\[
\sum_{j}W_{ij}=(m-1)\frac{a}{m}+m\,\frac{b}{m}=\frac{(m-1)a}{m}+b ,
\]
because each vertex has \(m-1\) same-community neighbours of weight \(a/m\) and \(m\) opposite-community neighbours of weight \(b/m\).  Thus \(L_m\) is block-patterned with data \(\bigl((m-1)a/m+b,\,-a/m,\,-b/m\bigr)\), and the matrix with entries \((L_m)_{ij}X(u,v)_{ij}\) is block-patterned with data \(\bigl((m-1)a/m+b,\,-(a/m)u,\,-(b/m)v\bigr)\).  Since \(X(u,v)\) is symmetric, the unsigned count gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(L_mX(u,v)\bigr)
=2m\Bigl(\frac{(m-1)a}{m}+b\Bigr)-2m(m-1)\frac{a}{m}u-2m^2\frac{b}{m}v
=2(m-1)a(1-u)+2mb(1-v),
\]
% lean: block_pair_sum
and expanding both sides shows that this equals the reduced form
\[
2(m-1)a(1-u)+2mb(1-v)=q(a+b)x+2b\,y .
\]

\emph{Step 4 (assembling the objective).}  Substituting the four displays of Step 3 into \cref{obj:def:design-objective},
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(u,v)\bigr)
&=\bigl(q(a+b)x+2b\,y\bigr)
+r\Bigl(\frac{qx}{a+b}+\frac{y}{2b}\Bigr)
+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}
+2m\,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\\
&=q\Bigl((a+b)+\frac{r}{a+b}\Bigr)x
+\Bigl(2b+\frac{r}{2b}\Bigr)y
+2m\,z_{\mathrm{sp}}
+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}\\
&=c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2},
\end{aligned}
\]
which is the asserted reduced form. % lean: block_spectral_coordinates

\emph{Step 5 (the two corner readings).}  The cut covariance is \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}=ss^\top\), whose entries are \(s_i^2=1\) on the diagonal, \(+1\) on same-community pairs, and \(-1\) on opposite-community pairs; hence \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}=X(1,-1)\), and substituting \(u=1\), \(v=-1\),
\[
\bigl(1-1,\;1+(m-1)-m(-1),\;1+(m-1)+m(-1)\bigr)=(0,2m,0).
\]
% lean: cutCovariance_eq_blockSym
Likewise \(I_n\) has entries \(1\) on the diagonal and \(0\) off it, so \(I_n=X(0,0)\), and substituting \(u=v=0\),
\[
\bigl(1-0,\;1+(m-1)\cdot0-m\cdot0,\;1+(m-1)\cdot0+m\cdot0\bigr)=(1,1,1).
\]
% lean: identity_eq_blockSym
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates} is the coordinate change used throughout the main text. It replaces the two covariance parameters \(u,v\) by nonnegative spectral coordinates satisfying one affine constraint. In these coordinates, the cut covariance is the vertex \((0,2m,0)\), the iid covariance is the center-like point \((1,1,1)\), and the objective separates into a linear part plus a weighted Euclidean norm.

The next two statements are vertex certificates. They give simple coefficient inequalities under which either the cut vertex or the spread vertex is the unique minimizer of the reduced problem.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:cut-vertex-certificate}[Cut Vertex Certificate]
Let \(m\) be fixed and write \(q=2(m-1)\). Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Positive spectral multiplicity.)} \(q>0\).
\item \textbf{(Nonnegative robustness.)} \(\kappa\ge 0\).
\item \textbf{(Cut-side coefficient separation.)} The coefficients satisfy
\[
\frac{c_x}{q}>c_y+\kappa
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c_z>c_y+\kappa .
\]
\end{itemize}
Then \((0,2m,0)\in T_m\), where
\[
T_m=\{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}): x\ge 0,\ y\ge 0,\ z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge 0,\ qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\}.
\]
Moreover, for every \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) with
\((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\neq (0,2m,0)\),
\[
c_x\cdot 0+c_y(2m)+c_z\cdot 0
+\kappa\sqrt{q\cdot 0^2+(2m)^2+0^2}
<
c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}.
\]
Thus \((0,2m,0)\) is the unique minimizer on \(T_m\) of the reduced objective.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:cut-vertex-certificate}]
Write \(q=2(m-1)\) and \(M=2m\), and abbreviate the reduced objective by
\[
\phi(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
=c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2},
\]
so that the claim is that \((0,M,0)\) is the unique minimizer of \(\phi\) on \(T_m\).  The hypothesis \(q>0\) means \(m>1\), so \(M=2m>0\).

\emph{Step 1 (feasibility).}  The point \((0,M,0)\) has nonnegative coordinates and satisfies \(q\cdot0+M+0=M=2m\); hence \((0,M,0)\in T_m\). % lean: cut_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 2 (the value at the cut vertex).}  Since \(M>0\), \(\sqrt{q\cdot0^2+M^2+0^2}=\sqrt{M^2}=M\), so
\[
\phi(0,M,0)=c_yM+\kappa M=(c_y+\kappa)M .
\]
% lean: cut_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 3 (a lower bound for the norm term).}  Let \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\).  Because \(q>0\), both \(qx^2\ge0\) and \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2\ge0\), so
\[
y^2\le qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
y\le\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2},
\]
and multiplying by \(\kappa\ge0\) gives \(\kappa y\le\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}\).  Therefore
\[
c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa y\le\phi(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
% lean: cut_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 4 (the coefficient separation in cleared form).}  Multiplying the hypothesis \(c_x/q>c_y+\kappa\) by \(q>0\), and taking the hypothesis \(c_z>c_y+\kappa\) as it stands, gives
\[
c_x-qc_y-q\kappa>0,
\qquad
c_z-c_y-\kappa>0 .
\]
% lean: cut_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 5 (some off-\(y\) mass is present).}  Suppose now in addition \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\ne(0,M,0)\).  If \(x=0\) and \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}=0\), the simplex equation \(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=M\) would force \(y=M\), i.e.\ the point would be \((0,M,0)\); hence \(x>0\) or \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}>0\).  Since \(x\ge0\) and \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0\) and both coefficients in Step 4 are strictly positive, the sum of the two products has a strictly positive term and a nonnegative one:
\[
0<(c_x-qc_y-q\kappa)x+(c_z-c_y-\kappa)z_{\mathrm{sp}} .
\]
% lean: cut_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 6 (the difference identity).}  Substituting \(M=qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\) into \((c_y+\kappa)M\) and cancelling the \(y\)-terms gives the identity
\[
\bigl(c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa y\bigr)-(c_y+\kappa)M
=(c_x-qc_y-q\kappa)x+(c_z-c_y-\kappa)z_{\mathrm{sp}} .
\]
Combining it with Step 5,
\[
(c_y+\kappa)M<c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa y .
\]
% lean: cut_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 7 (conclusion).}  Chaining Steps 2, 6 and 3,
\[
\phi(0,M,0)=(c_y+\kappa)M
<c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa y
\le\phi(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}),
\]
which is the asserted strict inequality
\[
c_x\cdot0+c_y(2m)+c_z\cdot0+\kappa\sqrt{q\cdot0^2+(2m)^2+0^2}
<c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}
\]
for every \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) other than \((0,2m,0)\).  In particular no other point of \(T_m\) attains the value \(\phi(0,2m,0)\), so the cut vertex is the unique minimizer. % lean: cut_vertex_certificate
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:cut-vertex-certificate} is the reduced certificate underlying the cut exactness result. The coefficient separation says that moving mass away from the \(y\)-coordinate toward either the within-block spectral coordinate or the aggregate coordinate is too costly, even after accounting for the robustness term.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:spread-vertex-certificate}[Spread Vertex Certificate]
Let \(m\in\mathbb N\), let \(c_x,c_y,c_z,\kappa\in\mathbb R\), and set \(q=2(m-1)\). Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Positive multiplicity.)} \(q>0\).
\item \textbf{(Nonnegative robustness.)} \(\kappa\ge 0\).
\item \textbf{(The \(y\)-coefficient is large.)} \(c_y>c_x/q+\kappa/\sqrt q\).
\item \textbf{(The \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}\)-coefficient is large.)} \(c_z>c_x/q+\kappa/\sqrt q\).
\end{itemize}
Define
\[
T_m=\{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}):x\ge0,\ y\ge0,\ z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0,\ qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\}
\]
and
\[
\phi(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
=
c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}.
\]
Then \((2m/q,0,0)\in T_m\), and for every \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) with
\((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\neq (2m/q,0,0)\),
\[
\phi(2m/q,0,0)<\phi(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:spread-vertex-certificate}]
Put \(q=2(m-1)\), \(M=2m\) and \(\sigma=\sqrt q\).  Since \(q>0\) we have \(\sigma>0\), \(\sigma^2=q\), and \(M=q+2>0\).

\emph{Step 1 (feasibility of the spread vertex).}  The point \((M/q,0,0)\) has nonnegative coordinates and satisfies \(q(M/q)+0+0=M=2m\), so \((M/q,0,0)\in T_m\). % lean: spread_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 2 (the value at the spread vertex).}  Since \(M/q\ge0\),
\[
\sqrt{q(M/q)^2+0^2+0^2}=\sqrt{\bigl(\sigma\,(M/q)\bigr)^2}=\sigma\,\frac{M}{q},
\]
hence
\[
\phi\Bigl(\frac Mq,0,0\Bigr)=c_x\frac Mq+\kappa\sigma\frac Mq .
\]
% lean: spread_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 3 (a lower bound for the norm term).}  Let \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\).  From \(\sigma^2=q\) and \(y^2,z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2\ge0\),
\[
(\sigma x)^2=qx^2\le qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\sigma x\le\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2},
\]
and multiplying by \(\kappa\ge0\) gives \(\kappa\sigma x\le\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}\).  Therefore
\[
c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa\sigma x\le\phi(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
% lean: spread_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 4 (the coefficient separation in cleared form).}  Multiplying the hypotheses \(c_y>c_x/q+\kappa/\sigma\) and \(c_z>c_x/q+\kappa/\sigma\) by \(\sigma>0\) gives
\[
\Bigl(c_y-\frac{c_x}{q}\Bigr)\sigma-\kappa>0,
\qquad
\Bigl(c_z-\frac{c_x}{q}\Bigr)\sigma-\kappa>0 .
\]
% lean: spread_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 5 (some off-\(x\) mass is present).}  Assume in addition \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\ne(M/q,0,0)\).  If \(y=z_{\mathrm{sp}}=0\), the simplex equation \(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=M\) would force \(x=M/q\), i.e.\ the point would be the spread vertex; hence \(y>0\) or \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}>0\).  Since \(y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0\) and both coefficients of Step 4 are strictly positive,
\[
0<\Bigl(\bigl(c_y-\tfrac{c_x}{q}\bigr)\sigma-\kappa\Bigr)y
+\Bigl(\bigl(c_z-\tfrac{c_x}{q}\bigr)\sigma-\kappa\Bigr)z_{\mathrm{sp}} .
\]
% lean: spread_vertex_certificate

\emph{Step 6 (the difference identity).}  Write \(M/q=x+(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}})/q\), which is the simplex equation divided by \(q\).  Then
\[
\bigl(c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa\sigma x\bigr)
-\Bigl(c_x\frac Mq+\kappa\sigma\frac Mq\Bigr)
=c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}-\frac{c_x}{q}(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}})-\frac{\kappa\sigma}{q}(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}),
\]
and multiplying by \(\sigma\) and using \(\sigma^2=q\) turns the last term into \(\kappa(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}})\), giving
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Bigl(c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa\sigma x
-c_x\frac Mq-\kappa\sigma\frac Mq\Bigr)\sigma\\
&\qquad=\Bigl(\bigl(c_y-\tfrac{c_x}{q}\bigr)\sigma-\kappa\Bigr)y
+\Bigl(\bigl(c_z-\tfrac{c_x}{q}\bigr)\sigma-\kappa\Bigr)z_{\mathrm{sp}} .
\end{aligned}
\]
% lean: spreadObjectiveDiff_mul_sqrt
By Step 5 the right-hand side is strictly positive, and \(\sigma>0\), so the bracket on the left is strictly positive:
\[
c_x\frac Mq+\kappa\sigma\frac Mq<c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa\sigma x .
\]

\emph{Step 7 (conclusion).}  Chaining Steps 2, 6 and 3,
\[
\phi\Bigl(\frac Mq,0,0\Bigr)
=c_x\frac Mq+\kappa\sigma\frac Mq
<c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa\sigma x
\le\phi(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
\]
for every \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) other than \((2m/q,0,0)\), which is the asserted strict inequality. % lean: spread_vertex_certificate
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:spread-vertex-certificate} plays the corresponding role for the spread vertex. In the notation of \cref{obj:thm:gap-window}, this vertex is the reduced representation of \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}\). The coefficient inequalities make the \(x\)-direction uniquely cheapest, so the relaxed optimizer concentrates all simplex mass there.

The iid result uses a different geometric fact: the point \((1,1,1)\) is the unique minimizer of the Frobenius part on the simplex, but it minimizes the full objective only when the linear coefficients are balanced across the three spectral directions.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:frobenius-center-certificate}[Frobenius Center Certificate]
Let \(m\in\mathbb N\), set \(q=2(m-1)\), and suppose \(q>0\).  For any real coefficients \(c_x,c_y,c_z\) and any real \(\kappa\), define
\[
T_m=\{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}): x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge 0,\ qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\}
\]
and
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
=
c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
+\kappa\sqrt{q x^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}.
\]
Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Frobenius center.)} The point \((1,1,1)\) belongs to \(T_m\), and for every \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) with \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\ne(1,1,1)\),
\[
\sqrt{q\cdot 1^2+1^2+1^2}
<
\sqrt{q x^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Certificate necessity.)} If \((1,1,1)\) minimizes \(\phi_{r,\kappa}\) over \(T_m\), that is, if
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}(1,1,1)\le
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
\quad\text{for all }(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m,
\]
then
\[
\frac{c_x}{q}=c_y
\quad\text{and}\quad
c_y=c_z.
\]
\item \textbf{(Strict optimality.)} If \(\kappa>0\), \(\frac{c_x}{q}=c_y\), and \(c_y=c_z\), then for every \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) with \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\ne(1,1,1)\),
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}(1,1,1)
<
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:frobenius-center-certificate}]
Throughout \(q=2(m-1)>0\), so that
\[
q+2=2m .
\]

\emph{Step 1 (the center is feasible).}  The point \((1,1,1)\) has nonnegative coordinates and satisfies \(q\cdot1+1+1=q+2=2m\), hence \((1,1,1)\in T_m\). % lean: frobenius_center_certificate

\emph{Step 2 (a bias--variance decomposition of the weighted norm on \(T_m\)).}  Let \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\).  Expanding the squares,
\[
q(x-1)^2+(y-1)^2+(z_{\mathrm{sp}}-1)^2
=\bigl(qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2\bigr)
-2\bigl(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\bigr)+(q+2),
\]
and substituting the simplex equation \(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\) together with \(q+2=2m\) gives \(-2(2m)+(q+2)=-(q+2)\).  Hence
\[
qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2
=(q\cdot1^2+1^2+1^2)+q(x-1)^2+(y-1)^2+(z_{\mathrm{sp}}-1)^2 .
\]
% lean: frobenius_center_certificate

\emph{Step 3 (strict minimality of the center for the norm).}  Suppose \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) with \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\ne(1,1,1)\).  Each of \(q(x-1)^2\), \((y-1)^2\), \((z_{\mathrm{sp}}-1)^2\) is nonnegative, and if their sum vanished then, since \(q>0\), each would vanish and the point would be \((1,1,1)\).  Therefore
\[
0<q(x-1)^2+(y-1)^2+(z_{\mathrm{sp}}-1)^2 ,
\]
so by Step 2, \(q\cdot1^2+1^2+1^2<qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2\), and taking square roots (both arguments being nonnegative and the square root strictly increasing)
\[
\sqrt{q\cdot1^2+1^2+1^2}<\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}.
\]
This is the Frobenius-center assertion. % lean: frobenius_center_certificate

\emph{Step 4 (necessity of \(c_x/q=c_y\)).}  Assume \(\phi_{r,\kappa}(1,1,1)\le\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\) for all \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\), and consider the curve
\[
t\longmapsto(1+t,\,1-qt,\,1).
\]
It is feasible for \(|t|<\min\{1,1/q\}\): the coordinates \(1+t\) and \(1-qt\) are then positive, the third is \(1\), and
\[
q(1+t)+(1-qt)+1=q+2=2m
\]
for every \(t\), so the curve stays in \(T_m\).  Consequently \(t=0\) is a local minimum of
\[
g(t)=\phi_{r,\kappa}(1+t,1-qt,1)
=c_x(1+t)+c_y(1-qt)+c_z
+\kappa\sqrt{q(1+t)^2+(1-qt)^2+1}.
\]
The radicand has derivative
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl(q(1+t)^2+(1-qt)^2+1\Bigr)\Big|_{t=0}
=2q(1+t)-2q(1-qt)\Big|_{t=0}=2q-2q=0,
\]
and its value at \(t=0\) is \(q+2>0\), so the chain rule makes the derivative of the square-root term vanish at \(t=0\).  Hence \(g'(0)=c_x-qc_y\), and local minimality at an interior point forces
\[
c_x-qc_y=0,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\frac{c_x}{q}=c_y .
\]
% lean: frobeniusCenterTangentXY_deriv

\emph{Step 5 (necessity of \(c_y=c_z\)).}  Consider likewise
\[
t\longmapsto(1,\,1+t,\,1-t),
\]
feasible for \(|t|<1\) since then all three coordinates are nonnegative and \(q\cdot1+(1+t)+(1-t)=q+2=2m\).  Writing \(h(t)=\phi_{r,\kappa}(1,1+t,1-t)\), the radicand \(q+(1+t)^2+(1-t)^2\) has derivative \(2(1+t)-2(1-t)\), which vanishes at \(t=0\), and its value there is \(q+2>0\); so again the square-root term contributes nothing to \(h'(0)\) and
\[
h'(0)=c_y-c_z=0,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
c_y=c_z .
\]
% lean: frobeniusCenterTangentYZ_deriv

\emph{Step 6 (strict optimality under the balance conditions).}  Suppose finally \(\kappa>0\), \(c_x/q=c_y\) and \(c_y=c_z\); thus \(c_x=qc_y\) and \(c_z=c_y\).  For every \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) the linear part is then constant:
\[
c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
=c_y\bigl(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\bigr)
=c_y\cdot 2m
=c_y(q+2)
=c_x\cdot1+c_y\cdot1+c_z\cdot1 .
\]
For \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\ne(1,1,1)\), Step 3 gives a strictly larger norm term, and multiplying that strict inequality by \(\kappa>0\) preserves strictness.  Adding the equal linear parts to the strict norm comparison yields
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}(1,1,1)<\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}),
\]
so \((1,1,1)\) is the strict minimizer. % lean: frobenius_center_certificate
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:frobenius-center-certificate} explains why the iid covariance has a knife-edge exactness condition in \cref{obj:thm:robust-corner-exactness}. The norm term alone favors the point \((1,1,1)\), but the linear coefficients must be equal after accounting for the \(q\)-multiplicity of the \(x\)-coordinate. When that equality fails, the linear part tilts the objective away from the iid point at every finite positive robustness weight.

The next statement is the implementability restriction in reduced form. It is where the parity of the block size enters the geometry.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:pm-reduced-slice-characterization}[Parity-Truncated Reduced Slice]
Let \(m\ge 2\) be an integer and let \(u,v\in\mathbb R\). Write
\[
x=1-u,\qquad
y=1+(m-1)u-mv,\qquad
z_{\mathrm{sp}}=1+(m-1)u+mv,
\]
and let \(d_m=0\) when \(m\) is even and \(d_m=2/m\) when \(m\) is odd. Then the block covariance matrix \(X(u,v)\) belongs to the implementable covariance class \(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\) of \cref{obj:def:implementable-covariance-class} if and only if
\[
x\ge 0,\qquad y\ge 0,\qquad z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge 0,\qquad
2(m-1)x+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m,
\]
and
\[
y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:pm-reduced-slice-characterization}]
Write \(q=2(m-1)\) and keep the abbreviations \(x=1-u\), \(y=1+(m-1)u-mv\), \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}=1+(m-1)u+mv\) of the statement, so that
\[
y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2+2(m-1)u,
\qquad
z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y=2mv,
\qquad
qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m ,
\]
the last identity holding for all \(u,v\) because the \(u\)- and \(v\)-terms cancel.  Throughout, \((S_A,S_B)\) are the two community sums of \cref{obj:def:block-sum-handle}.


\emph{Part I: necessity.}  Suppose \(X(u,v)\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\), i.e.\ \(X(u,v)=X(P)=\mathbb E_P[ZZ^\top]\) for some \(P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\).  Reading the entries of \(X(P)\) off the block pattern of \(X(u,v)\) gives \(\mathbb E[Z_iZ_j]=1\) if \(i=j\), \(=u\) if \(i\ne j\) lie in the same community, and \(=v\) if they lie in opposite communities.

\emph{(I.1) The three second-moment identities.}  Expanding \(S_A^2=\sum_{i,j\in A_m}Z_iZ_j\), there are \(m\) diagonal pairs and \(m(m-1)\) off-diagonal same-community pairs, and likewise for \(S_B^2\); expanding \(S_AS_B=\sum_{i\in A_m}\sum_{j\in B_m}Z_iZ_j\), all \(m^2\) pairs are cross-community.  Hence
\[
\mathbb E[S_A^2]=\mathbb E[S_B^2]=m+m(m-1)u,
\qquad
\mathbb E[S_AS_B]=m^2v .
\]
% lean: E_sumAr_sq % lean: E_sumBr_sq % lean: E_sumAr_sumBr

\emph{(I.2) Nonnegativity of \(y\) and \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}\).}  Combining the three identities,
\[
\mathbb E\bigl[(S_A-S_B)^2\bigr]
=2m+2m(m-1)u-2m^2v
=2m\,y,
\qquad
\mathbb E\bigl[(S_A+S_B)^2\bigr]
=2m+2m(m-1)u+2m^2v
=2m\,z_{\mathrm{sp}} .
\]
Both left-hand sides are expectations of squares, hence nonnegative, and \(m>0\); therefore \(y\ge0\) and \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0\). % lean: pm_slice_forward

\emph{(I.3) Nonnegativity of \(x\).}  Since \(|A_m|=m\ge2\), pick distinct \(i,j\in A_m\).  Because \(Z_i^2=Z_j^2=1\) pointwise,
\[
0\le\mathbb E\bigl[(Z_i-Z_j)^2\bigr]=2-2\,\mathbb E[Z_iZ_j]=2-2u,
\]
so \(u\le1\), i.e.\ \(x=1-u\ge0\). % lean: u_le_one

Together with the identity \(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\) recorded above, this gives the reduced-triangle conditions
\[
x\ge0,\qquad y\ge0,\qquad z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0,\qquad
2(m-1)x+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m .
\]

\emph{(I.4) The parity bound.}  If \(m\) is even then \(d_m=0\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\) by (I.2), and there is nothing more to prove.  If \(m\) is odd, then \(S_A\) is a sum of \(m\) terms each equal to \(\pm1\) with \(m\) odd, so \(S_A\) is an odd integer; in particular \(S_A\ne0\) and, being an integer, \(S_A^2\ge1\).  The same holds for \(S_B\).  Taking expectations,
\[
\mathbb E[S_A^2]\ge1,
\qquad
\mathbb E[S_B^2]\ge1 .
\]
% lean: blockSumA_odd % lean: one_le_E_sumAr_sq % lean: one_le_E_sumBr_sq
On the other hand, by (I.1),
\[
\mathbb E[S_A^2]+\mathbb E[S_B^2]=2m+2m(m-1)u=m\,(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}),
\]
so \(m(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}})\ge2\) and hence \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge 2/m=d_m\). % lean: pm_slice_forward


\emph{Part II: sufficiency.}  Conversely, assume \(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0\), \(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\) and \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\).  We exhibit a law in \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\) whose second moment is \(X(u,v)\).

Two general facts are used.  First, the block parameterization is affine: for weights \(w_k\ge0\) with \(\sum_kw_k=1\),
\[
\sum_k w_k\,X(u_k,v_k)=X\Bigl(\sum_k w_ku_k,\ \sum_k w_kv_k\Bigr),
\]
because every diagonal entry of the left side is \(\sum_kw_k=1\) and the within- and across-community entries average separately. % lean: sum_blockSymMatrix
Second, if \(P=\sum_kw_kP_k\) is a convex mixture of laws \(P_k\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\), then \(P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\) (sign symmetry and invariance under the two-block automorphism group are preserved by convex combinations) and, by linearity of expectation, \(X(P)=\sum_kw_kX(P_k)\). % lean: mixtureDesign_mem % lean: mixtureDesign_secondMoment

The building blocks are uniform laws on supports that are invariant under global sign reversal and under the two-block automorphism group; each such law lies in \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\), and its second moment is therefore block-symmetric, say \(X(u_\bullet,v_\bullet)\), with \(u_\bullet,v_\bullet\) determined through (I.1) by \(\mathbb E[S_A^2]\) and \(\mathbb E[S_AS_B]\):
\[
u_\bullet=\frac{\mathbb E[S_A^2]-m}{m(m-1)},
\qquad
v_\bullet=\frac{\mathbb E[S_AS_B]}{m^2}.
\]
% lean: secondMoment_blockSym_of_exchangeable

\emph{(II.1) Even \(m\): a three-vertex mixture.}  Take
\begin{itemize}
\item \(P_{\mathrm{cut}}\), uniform on the cut assignment (\(+1\) on \(A_m\), \(-1\) on \(B_m\)) and its global sign reversal, for which \(S_A^2=m^2\) and \(S_AS_B=-m^2\), hence \(X(P_{\mathrm{cut}})=X(1,-1)\); % lean: cutVDesign_secondMoment
\item \(P_{\mathrm{all}}\), uniform on the two constant assignments (all \(+1\), all \(-1\)), for which \(S_A^2=m^2\) and \(S_AS_B=m^2\), hence \(X(P_{\mathrm{all}})=X(1,1)\); % lean: allVDesign_secondMoment
\item \(P_{\mathrm{spr}}\), uniform on \(\{z:S_A(z)=S_B(z)=0\}\), a nonempty support when \(m\) is even, for which \(\mathbb E[S_A^2]=\mathbb E[S_AS_B]=0\) and hence \(X(P_{\mathrm{spr}})=X\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{m-1},0\right)\). % lean: spreadVDesign_secondMoment
\end{itemize}
Assign the weights
\[
w_{\mathrm{cut}}=\frac{y}{2m},
\qquad
w_{\mathrm{all}}=\frac{z_{\mathrm{sp}}}{2m},
\qquad
w_{\mathrm{spr}}=\frac{(m-1)x}{m}.
\]
They are nonnegative because \(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0\) and \(m\ge2\), and they sum to one because
\[
\frac{y}{2m}+\frac{z_{\mathrm{sp}}}{2m}+\frac{(m-1)x}{m}
=\frac{y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}+2(m-1)x}{2m}
=\frac{2m}{2m}=1 .
\]
Their barycentre in the \((u,v)\) parameters is
\[
w_{\mathrm{cut}}\cdot1+w_{\mathrm{all}}\cdot1+w_{\mathrm{spr}}\cdot\Bigl(-\frac{1}{m-1}\Bigr)
=\frac{y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}}{2m}-\frac{x}{m}
=\frac{2m-2(m-1)x}{2m}-\frac{x}{m}
=1-x=u
\]
and
\[
w_{\mathrm{cut}}\cdot(-1)+w_{\mathrm{all}}\cdot1+w_{\mathrm{spr}}\cdot0
=\frac{z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y}{2m}=v .
\]
Hence the mixture \(P=w_{\mathrm{cut}}P_{\mathrm{cut}}+w_{\mathrm{all}}P_{\mathrm{all}}+w_{\mathrm{spr}}P_{\mathrm{spr}}\) lies in \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\) and has \(X(P)=X(u,v)\), so \(X(u,v)\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\). % lean: pm_slice_backward_even

\emph{(II.2) Odd \(m\): a four-vertex mixture.}  For odd \(m\) the support \(\{S_A=S_B=0\}\) is empty, so \(P_{\mathrm{spr}}\) is unavailable and the two parity vertices replace it:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(P^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathrm{cut}}\), uniform on \(\{z:(S_A,S_B)=(1,-1)\text{ or }(-1,1)\}\) --- a nonempty support for odd \(m\) --- for which \(S_A^2=1\) and \(S_AS_B=-1\), hence
\[
X\bigl(P^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathrm{cut}}\bigr)=X\Bigl(-\frac1m,\,-\frac1{m^2}\Bigr);
\]
% lean: pcutVDesign_secondMoment
\item \(P^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathrm{all}}\), uniform on \(\{z:(S_A,S_B)=(1,1)\text{ or }(-1,-1)\}\), for which \(S_A^2=1\) and \(S_AS_B=1\), hence
\[
X\bigl(P^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathrm{all}}\bigr)=X\Bigl(-\frac1m,\,\frac1{m^2}\Bigr).
\]
% lean: pallVDesign_secondMoment
\end{itemize}
In reduced coordinates these two points are \(\bigl(\tfrac{m+1}{m},\tfrac2m,0\bigr)\) and \(\bigl(\tfrac{m+1}{m},0,\tfrac2m\bigr)\), i.e.\ they sit on the parity boundary \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2/m\), whereas \(X(1,-1)\) and \(X(1,1)\) are the outer vertices \((0,2m,0)\) and \((0,0,2m)\).

Put \(s=y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\) and
\[
\mu=\frac{y}{s},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{s-2/m}{2m-2/m},
\]
which are well defined because \(s\ge2/m>0\) and \(2m-2/m>0\) for \(m\ge2\).  Then \(0\le\mu\le1\) since \(0\le y\le s\), and \(0\le\lambda\le1\) since \(2/m\le s\le 2m\), the upper bound coming from \(s=2m-qx\le2m\) with \(qx\ge0\).  Take the four weights
\[
\bigl(\lambda\mu,\ \lambda(1-\mu),\ (1-\lambda)\mu,\ (1-\lambda)(1-\mu)\bigr)
\]
on \(\bigl(P_{\mathrm{cut}},P_{\mathrm{all}},P^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathrm{cut}},P^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathrm{all}}\bigr)\); they are nonnegative and sum to one.  Their barycentre in the \(u\)-parameter is
\[
\lambda\cdot1+(1-\lambda)\Bigl(-\frac1m\Bigr)
=\frac{(m+1)\lambda-1}{m}
=u,
\]
where the last equality is the definition of \(\lambda\) rewritten through \(s=2m-2(m-1)(1-u)\), which gives \(\lambda=(1+mu)/(m+1)\).  For the \(v\)-parameter, the two \(\mu\)-pairs collapse to
\[
\lambda\mu(-1)+\lambda(1-\mu)(1)
+(1-\lambda)\mu\Bigl(-\frac1{m^2}\Bigr)+(1-\lambda)(1-\mu)\frac1{m^2}
=(1-2\mu)\Bigl(\lambda+\frac{1-\lambda}{m^2}\Bigr),
\]
and the two factors evaluate to
\[
1-2\mu=\frac{z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y}{s},
\qquad
\lambda+\frac{1-\lambda}{m^2}
=\frac{1}{m^2}+\lambda\,\frac{m^2-1}{m^2}
=\frac{s}{2m},
\]
the second using \(\lambda=(ms-2)/\bigl(2(m^2-1)\bigr)\).  Their product is \((z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y)/(2m)=v\).  Hence the four-point mixture lies in \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\) and has second moment \(X(u,v)\), so \(X(u,v)\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\). % lean: pm_slice_backward_odd


Parts I and II are the two implications of the asserted equivalence. % lean: pm_reduced_slice_characterization
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:pm-reduced-slice-characterization} gives implementability a transparent low-dimensional form. Relative to the relaxed simplex, the implementable slice adds the lower bound \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\). For even \(m\), \(d_m=0\), so this lower bound is already implied by nonnegativity. For odd \(m\), the positive lower bound records the positive squared magnitude of the realized block sums.

It remains to solve the reduced optimization problem. The next lemma gives the unconstrained weighted-simplex solution as a finite active-set calculation.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:weighted-simplex-active-set}[Weighted Simplex Active Set]
Let \(m\ge 2\), let \(M>0\) satisfy \(M=2m\), and let
\(\mathcal I=\{x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\}\). Let
\(\alpha=(\alpha_i)_{i\in\mathcal I}\), let \(\kappa\ge 0\), and let
\(\beta=(\beta_i)_{i\in\mathcal I}\) have strictly positive entries with
\[
  \beta_x=\frac{1}{2(m-1)},\qquad
  \beta_y=\beta_{z_{\mathrm{sp}}}=1 .
\]
For \(t\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal I}\), write
\[
  \Delta_M=\Bigl\{t:\ t_i\ge 0\ \text{for all }i\in\mathcal I,\ 
  \sum_{i\in\mathcal I}t_i=M\Bigr\},
\]
and
\[
  \Phi(t)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\alpha_i t_i
  +\kappa\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\beta_i t_i^2}.
\]
Then the following hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Positive robustness.)} If \(\kappa>0\), there is a unique pair
\((S,\lambda)\), where \(S\subseteq\mathcal I\) is nonempty and
\(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), such that
\[
  \sum_{i\in S}\frac{(\lambda-\alpha_i)^2}{\beta_i}=\kappa^2,\qquad
  \alpha_i<\lambda\ \text{for every }i\in S,\qquad
  \lambda\le \alpha_j\ \text{for every }j\notin S .
\]
For this unique pair, define
\[
  t_i^\star=
  \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle
  M\,\frac{(\lambda-\alpha_i)/\beta_i}
  {\sum_{h\in S}(\lambda-\alpha_h)/\beta_h}, & i\in S,\\[1.1em]
  0, & i\notin S .
  \end{cases}
\]
Then \(t^\star\in\Delta_M\), and \(t^\star\) is the unique minimizer of
\(\Phi\) over \(\Delta_M\) in the strict sense that, for every
\(s\in\Delta_M\) with \(s\ne t^\star\),
\[
  \Phi(t^\star)<\Phi(s).
\]
Its value is
\[
  \Phi(t^\star)=M\lambda .
\]

\item \textbf{(Zero robustness.)} If \(\kappa=0\), then for every
\(t\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal I}\),
\[
  \Bigl(t\in\Delta_M\ \text{and}\ 
  \Phi(t)\le \Phi(s)\ \text{for every }s\in\Delta_M\Bigr)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  t\in\Delta_M\ \text{and whenever }t_i\ne 0,\ 
  \alpha_i\le \alpha_j\ \text{for every }j\in\mathcal I .
\]
Equivalently, the minimizer set is exactly the exposed
\(\alpha\)-minimizing face of \(\Delta_M\).
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-active-set}]
Throughout \(M=2m>0\) and every \(\beta_i>0\).

\emph{Part A: \(\kappa>0\).}

\emph{Step A1 (an admissible pair exists).}  Define the threshold function
\[
G(\lambda)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\frac{\bigl(\max\{\lambda-\alpha_i,0\}\bigr)^2}{\beta_i},
\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb R,
\]
which is continuous because it is a finite sum of continuous functions of \(\lambda\).  Let \(\alpha_{\min}=\min_i\alpha_i\) and \(\alpha_{\max}=\max_i\alpha_i\), attained at indices \(k_{\min}\) and \(k_{\max}\).  At \(\lambda=\alpha_{\min}\) every positive part vanishes, so
\[
G(\alpha_{\min})=0\le\kappa^2 .
\]
At \(\lambda_{+}=\alpha_{\max}+\kappa\sqrt{\beta_{k_{\max}}}\) the single summand at \(k_{\max}\) already equals
\[
\frac{\bigl(\kappa\sqrt{\beta_{k_{\max}}}\bigr)^2}{\beta_{k_{\max}}}=\kappa^2,
\]
and all summands are nonnegative, so \(G(\lambda_{+})\ge\kappa^2\).  Since \(\alpha_{\min}\le\lambda_{+}\), the intermediate value theorem yields \(\lambda\in[\alpha_{\min},\lambda_{+}]\) with \(G(\lambda)=\kappa^2\).  Put
\[
S=\{i\in\mathcal I:\alpha_i<\lambda\}.
\]
Off \(S\) the positive part \(\max\{\lambda-\alpha_i,0\}\) vanishes, so
\[
\sum_{i\in S}\frac{(\lambda-\alpha_i)^2}{\beta_i}=G(\lambda)=\kappa^2 ,
\]
and \(S\) is nonempty (otherwise the left side would be \(0\ne\kappa^2\)).  By construction \(\alpha_i<\lambda\) on \(S\) and \(\lambda\le\alpha_j\) off \(S\), so \((S,\lambda)\) is admissible. % lean: exists_admissible

\emph{Step A2 (the active-set point lies in \(\Delta_M\)).}  Write
\[
D=\sum_{h\in S}\frac{\lambda-\alpha_h}{\beta_h}.
\]
Each summand is strictly positive (\(\lambda>\alpha_h\) on \(S\), \(\beta_h>0\)) and \(S\ne\emptyset\), so \(D>0\).  Hence each \(t_i^\star=M\bigl((\lambda-\alpha_i)/\beta_i\bigr)/D\) with \(i\in S\) is nonnegative --- indeed strictly positive --- while \(t_j^\star=0\) off \(S\); and
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}t_i^\star
=\frac{M}{D}\sum_{i\in S}\frac{\lambda-\alpha_i}{\beta_i}
=\frac{M}{D}\cdot D=M .
\]
Thus \(t^\star\in\Delta_M\), and moreover
\[
t_i^\star>0\iff i\in S .
\]
% lean: activeSetPoint_mem % lean: activeSetPoint_pos_iff

\emph{Step A3 (norm and stationarity at \(t^\star\)).}  Using \(t_i^\star=0\) off \(S\) and the admissibility equation,
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\beta_i(t_i^\star)^2
=\frac{M^2}{D^2}\sum_{i\in S}\beta_i\frac{(\lambda-\alpha_i)^2}{\beta_i^2}
=\frac{M^2}{D^2}\sum_{i\in S}\frac{(\lambda-\alpha_i)^2}{\beta_i}
=\Bigl(\frac{M\kappa}{D}\Bigr)^2 ,
\]
so, writing \(N=\sqrt{\sum_i\beta_i(t_i^\star)^2}\),
\[
N=\frac{M\kappa}{D}>0 .
\]
% lean: activeSetPoint_normSq
Consequently, for every \(i\in S\),
\[
\frac{\kappa\,\beta_i t_i^\star}{N}
=\frac{\kappa\,\beta_i}{N}\cdot\frac{M(\lambda-\alpha_i)}{\beta_iD}
=\frac{\kappa M}{ND}(\lambda-\alpha_i)
=\lambda-\alpha_i ,
\]
which is the stationarity relation on the active support.

\emph{Step A4 (the optimal value).}  Multiplying the stationarity relation of Step A3 by \(t_i^\star\), summing over \(i\in S\) (equivalently over all of \(\mathcal I\), since \(t^\star\) vanishes off \(S\)), and using \(\sum_i\beta_i(t_i^\star)^2=N^2\) and \(\sum_it_i^\star=M\),
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\alpha_it_i^\star+\kappa N
=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\alpha_it_i^\star+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\beta_i(t_i^\star)^2
=\lambda\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}t_i^\star=M\lambda ,
\]
i.e.
\[
\Phi(t^\star)=M\lambda .
\]
% lean: activeSetPoint_value

\emph{Step A5 (strict optimality).}  Let \(s\in\Delta_M\) with \(s\ne t^\star\), and write \(N(s)=\sqrt{\sum_i\beta_is_i^2}\).  Since \(\beta\) is strictly positive, \(\sum_is_i=\sum_it_i^\star=M\ne0\) and \(s\ne t^\star\), the strict weighted Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\beta_it_i^\star s_i<N\,N(s).
\]
% lean: weighted_cs_simplex_strict
Subtracting \(\sum_i\beta_i(t_i^\star)^2=N^2\) from both sides and multiplying by \(\kappa/N>0\),
\[
\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\beta_it_i^\star\,(s_i-t_i^\star)
<\kappa\bigl(N(s)-N\bigr).
\]
By the stationarity relation of Step A3 (and \(t_j^\star=0\) off \(S\)) the left side equals \(\sum_{i\in S}(\lambda-\alpha_i)(s_i-t_i^\star)\), so
\[
\Phi(s)-\Phi(t^\star)
=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\alpha_i(s_i-t_i^\star)+\kappa\bigl(N(s)-N\bigr)
>\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\alpha_i(s_i-t_i^\star)
+\sum_{i\in S}(\lambda-\alpha_i)(s_i-t_i^\star).
\]
Setting \(\gamma_i=\lambda\) for \(i\in S\) and \(\gamma_i=\alpha_i\) for \(i\notin S\), the right-hand side is \(\sum_i\gamma_i(s_i-t_i^\star)\); since \(\gamma_it_i^\star=\lambda t_i^\star\) for every \(i\) (on \(S\) by definition of \(\gamma\), off \(S\) because \(t_i^\star=0\)) and \(\sum_it_i^\star=M\), it equals
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\gamma_is_i-\lambda M .
\]
Finally \(\gamma_i\ge\lambda\) for every \(i\) --- with equality on \(S\) and by admissibility \(\alpha_j\ge\lambda\) off \(S\) --- and \(s_i\ge0\), so \(\sum_i\gamma_is_i\ge\lambda\sum_is_i=\lambda M\) and the last display is nonnegative.  Hence
\[
\Phi(t^\star)<\Phi(s)
\qquad\text{for every }s\in\Delta_M\text{ with }s\ne t^\star .
\]
% lean: activeSetPoint_strict_min

\emph{Step A6 (uniqueness of \((S,\lambda)\)).}  Let \((S',\lambda')\) be another admissible pair, with active-set point \(t^{\star\prime}\).  Steps A2 and A5 apply to it as well, so both \(t^\star\) and \(t^{\star\prime}\) are strict minimizers of \(\Phi\) over \(\Delta_M\); if they differed, each would have strictly smaller value than the other, which is impossible.  Hence \(t^\star=t^{\star\prime}\).  By the support identity of Step A2,
\[
i\in S\iff t_i^\star>0\iff t_i^{\star\prime}>0\iff i\in S',
\]
so \(S=S'\).  By Step A4, \(M\lambda=\Phi(t^\star)=\Phi(t^{\star\prime})=M\lambda'\), and \(M>0\) gives \(\lambda=\lambda'\). % lean: activeSetPoint_strict_min % lean: activeSetPoint_pos_iff % lean: activeSetPoint_value

\emph{Part B: \(\kappa=0\).}  Here \(\Phi(t)=\sum_i\alpha_it_i\) is linear.  Fix \(k\) with \(\alpha_k=\min_j\alpha_j\).

If \(t\in\Delta_M\) minimizes \(\Phi\) over \(\Delta_M\), compare it with the vertex of \(\Delta_M\) that places all the mass on coordinate \(k\), i.e.\ the point \(w\) with \(w_k=M\) and \(w_j=0\) for \(j\ne k\), whose value is \(\Phi(w)=M\alpha_k\):
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\alpha_it_i\le M\alpha_k=\alpha_k\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}t_i,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}(\alpha_i-\alpha_k)t_i\le0 .
\]
Every summand is nonnegative (\(\alpha_i\ge\alpha_k\), \(t_i\ge0\)), so every summand vanishes; hence \(t_i\ne0\) forces \(\alpha_i=\alpha_k\le\alpha_j\) for every \(j\).  That is exactly membership in the exposed \(\alpha\)-minimizing face.

Conversely, if \(t\in\Delta_M\) is supported only on \(\alpha\)-minimizing coordinates, then \(\alpha_it_i=\alpha_kt_i\) for every \(i\) and
\[
\Phi(t)=\alpha_k\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}t_i=M\alpha_k ,
\]
whereas every \(s\in\Delta_M\) satisfies \(\Phi(s)=\sum_i\alpha_is_i\ge\alpha_k\sum_is_i=M\alpha_k\).  So \(t\) is a minimizer.  The two implications give the stated equivalence, and the minimizer set is precisely the exposed \(\alpha\)-minimizing face of \(\Delta_M\). % lean: kappa_zero_face
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-active-set} is the finite case split behind the active-set formula in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star}. For \(\kappa>0\), the support and multiplier determine both the optimizer and the value. For \(\kappa=0\), the norm term drops out and the minimizers form the exposed face associated with the smallest linear coefficient.

The implementable problem may add the parity lower bound. The following truncation statement records how the solution changes when the unconstrained active-set point lies outside that truncated simplex.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:weighted-simplex-truncation}[Weighted Simplex Truncation]
Let \(m\ge 2\), let \(M,d\in\mathbb R\), and let \(\alpha=(\alpha_x,\alpha_y,\alpha_z)\), \(\beta=(\beta_x,\beta_y,\beta_z)\), and \(\kappa\in\mathbb R\). Suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Mass and truncation.)} \(M>0\), \(M=2m\), and \(0\le d\le M\).
\item \textbf{(Weights.)} Each \(\beta_i\) is strictly positive and
\[
\beta=\left(\frac{1}{2(m-1)},1,1\right),
\]
so in particular \(\beta_y=\beta_z=1\).
\item \textbf{(Robustness.)} \(\kappa\ge 0\).
\end{itemize}
Write
\[
K_d=\{t\in\Delta_M:t_y+t_z\ge d\},
\qquad
\Phi(t)=\sum_i\alpha_i t_i+\kappa\sqrt{\sum_i\beta_i t_i^2}.
\]
For \(\kappa>0\), for every nonempty active support \(S\subseteq\{x,y,z\}\) and multiplier \(\lambda\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{i\in S}\frac{(\lambda-\alpha_i)^2}{\beta_i}=\kappa^2,\qquad
\alpha_i<\lambda\ \text{for }i\in S,\qquad
\lambda\le \alpha_j\ \text{for }j\notin S,
\]
let \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\) be the active-set point
\[
t^{\mathrm{rel}}_i=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{M((\lambda-\alpha_i)/\beta_i)}
{\sum_{h\in S}(\lambda-\alpha_h)/\beta_h}, & i\in S,\\[1em]
0, & i\notin S .
\end{cases}
\]
If \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\in K_d\), then
\[
\Phi(t^{\mathrm{rel}})\le \Phi(s)
\qquad\text{for every }s\in K_d.
\]
If \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}_y+t^{\mathrm{rel}}_z<d\), then
\[
t^\star=(M-d,s^\star,d-s^\star)\in K_d
\]
and
\[
\Phi(t^\star)\le \Phi(s)
\qquad\text{for every }s\in K_d,
\]
where \(s^\star=\operatorname{truncSelector}(M,d,\alpha,\beta,\kappa)\), namely with
\[
\delta=\alpha_y-\alpha_z,\qquad A=\beta_x(M-d)^2,
\]
\[
s^\star=
\begin{cases}
0, & \displaystyle \delta\ge \frac{\kappa d}{\sqrt{A+d^2}},\\[0.8em]
d, & \displaystyle \delta\le -\frac{\kappa d}{\sqrt{A+d^2}},\\[0.8em]
\displaystyle
\frac{d-\delta\sqrt{(A+d^2/2)/(\kappa^2-\delta^2/2)}}{2},
& \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]

For \(\kappa=0\), let \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\) be a point of the exposed \(\alpha\)-minimizing face
\[
\{t\in\Delta_M:\ t_i\ne 0\Rightarrow \alpha_i\le \alpha_j\ \text{for every }j\}.
\]
Assume the tie convention that, if this exposed face contains at least one point of \(K_d\), then the selected point \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\) lies in \(K_d\). If \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\in K_d\), then
\[
\Phi(t^{\mathrm{rel}})\le \Phi(s)
\qquad\text{for every }s\in K_d.
\]
If \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}_y+t^{\mathrm{rel}}_z<d\), then
\[
t^\star=(M-d,s^\star,d-s^\star)\in K_d
\]
and
\[
\Phi(t^\star)\le \Phi(s)
\qquad\text{for every }s\in K_d,
\]
where \(s^\star=\operatorname{truncSelector}(M,d,\alpha,\beta,0)\), equivalently, in the ordered selector convention, \(s^\star=0\) if \(\alpha_y-\alpha_z\ge 0\) and \(s^\star=d\) if \(\alpha_y-\alpha_z<0\).
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-truncation}]
Write \(\delta=\alpha_y-\alpha_z\), \(A=\beta_x(M-d)^2\ge0\), and for \(\sigma\in\mathbb R\) let
\[
h(\sigma)=(M-d,\sigma,d-\sigma)
\]
be the point of the boundary segment \(H_d\) with second coordinate \(\sigma\).  Since \(\beta_y=\beta_z=1\), evaluating the objective there gives the one-dimensional slice
\[
g_d(\sigma):=\Phi\bigl(h(\sigma)\bigr)
=\alpha_x(M-d)+\alpha_y\sigma+\alpha_z(d-\sigma)
+\kappa\,r(\sigma),
\qquad
r(\sigma)=\sqrt{A+\sigma^2+(d-\sigma)^2}.
\]
% lean: wsObj_truncSeg_eq

\emph{Step 1 (two-point convexity of \(\Phi\)).}  For \(u,w\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal I}\) and \(\theta\in[0,1]\),
\[
\Phi\bigl((1-\theta)u+\theta w\bigr)\le(1-\theta)\Phi(u)+\theta\Phi(w).
\]
Indeed the linear part is exactly additive, and \(t\mapsto\sqrt{\sum_i\beta_it_i^2}\) is the Euclidean norm of the vector with coordinates \(\sqrt{\beta_i}\,t_i\), so it obeys the triangle inequality and positive homogeneity; multiplying by \(\kappa\ge0\) preserves the inequality. % lean: weighted_sqrt_segment_le % lean: wsObj_segment_le

\emph{Step 2 (the dichotomy from a relaxed minimizer).}  Let \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\in\Delta_M\) satisfy \(\Phi(t^{\mathrm{rel}})\le\Phi(s)\) for every \(s\in\Delta_M\).

If \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\in K_d\), then since \(K_d\subseteq\Delta_M\) the same inequality holds for every \(s\in K_d\); this is the first alternative in both robustness regimes. % lean: trunc_from_minimizer

Assume from now on \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}_y+t^{\mathrm{rel}}_z<d\).  Because \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}_y,t^{\mathrm{rel}}_z\ge0\), this forces \(d>0\).

\emph{Step 3 (every feasible point is dominated on the face \(H_d\)).}  Let \(s\in K_d\) and put \(\Sigma=s_y+s_z\ge d\) and \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}=t^{\mathrm{rel}}_y+t^{\mathrm{rel}}_z<d\).  If \(\Sigma=d\) then \(s\) already lies on \(H_d\): its first coordinate is \(M-d\) by the mass constraint, so \(s=h(s_y)\) with \(0\le s_y\le d\), and we may take \(\sigma=s_y\).  If \(\Sigma>d\), set
\[
\theta=\frac{\Sigma-d}{\Sigma-\Sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}}\in[0,1],
\qquad
t^{\mathrm b}=(1-\theta)s+\theta\,t^{\mathrm{rel}} ,
\]
which is well defined since \(\Sigma-\Sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}>0\).  Then \(t^{\mathrm b}\in\Delta_M\) (a convex combination of simplex points) and
\[
t^{\mathrm b}_y+t^{\mathrm b}_z=(1-\theta)\Sigma+\theta\Sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}=\Sigma-\theta(\Sigma-\Sigma^{\mathrm{rel}})=d,
\]
so \(t^{\mathrm b}=h(\sigma)\) with \(\sigma=t^{\mathrm b}_y\in[0,d]\).  By Step 1 and \(\Phi(t^{\mathrm{rel}})\le\Phi(s)\),
\[
\Phi\bigl(h(\sigma)\bigr)=\Phi(t^{\mathrm b})
\le(1-\theta)\Phi(s)+\theta\Phi(t^{\mathrm{rel}})
\le\Phi(s).
\]
In either case there is \(\sigma\in[0,d]\) with \(\Phi(h(\sigma))\le\Phi(s)\). % lean: truncSeg_reduction

\emph{Step 4 (the selector lies in \([0,d]\)).}  In the two endpoint branches \(s^\star\in\{0,d\}\subseteq[0,d]\) because \(d>0\).  In the interior branch both guards fail,
\[
|\delta|<\frac{\kappa d}{\sqrt{A+d^2}},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\delta^2(A+d^2)<\kappa^2d^2 ,
\]
which forces \(\kappa>0\) and, since \(\delta^2d^2\le\delta^2(A+d^2)\), also \(\delta^2<\kappa^2\), so \(\kappa^2-\delta^2/2>0\) and
\[
W:=\sqrt{\frac{A+d^2/2}{\kappa^2-\delta^2/2}}
\]
is well defined with \(s^\star=(d-\delta W)/2\).  Moreover
\[
\delta^2W^2=\frac{2\delta^2A+\delta^2d^2}{2\kappa^2-\delta^2}<d^2
\iff
\delta^2(A+d^2)<\kappa^2d^2 ,
\]
so \(|\delta W|<d\) and hence \(0<s^\star<d\).  In all branches \(h(s^\star)=(M-d,s^\star,d-s^\star)\) has nonnegative coordinates (using \(d\le M\)), total mass \(M\), and \(s^\star+(d-s^\star)=d\); therefore
\[
t^\star=h(s^\star)\in K_d .
\]
% lean: truncSelector_mem

\emph{Step 5 (the selector's sign condition).}  We claim that for every \(\sigma\in[0,d]\),
\[
0\le(\sigma-s^\star)\Bigl(r(s^\star)\,\delta+\kappa\,(2s^\star-d)\Bigr).
\]
If \(s^\star=0\), the branch guard reads \(\delta\sqrt{A+d^2}\ge\kappa d\), and \(r(0)=\sqrt{A+d^2}\), so the bracket \(\delta\sqrt{A+d^2}-\kappa d\) is nonnegative while \(\sigma-0\ge0\).  If \(s^\star=d\), the guard reads \(\delta\sqrt{A+d^2}\le-\kappa d\), and \(r(d)=\sqrt{A+d^2}\), so the bracket \(\delta\sqrt{A+d^2}+\kappa d\) is nonpositive while \(\sigma-d\le0\).  In the interior branch the bracket vanishes: with \(W\) as in Step 4 we have \(2s^\star-d=-\delta W\) and
\[
r(s^\star)^2=A+(s^\star)^2+(d-s^\star)^2
=A+\frac{d^2}{2}+\frac{\delta^2W^2}{2}
=\Bigl(\kappa^2-\frac{\delta^2}{2}\Bigr)W^2+\frac{\delta^2W^2}{2}
=(\kappa W)^2 ,
\]
using \(W^2(\kappa^2-\delta^2/2)=A+d^2/2\); both \(r(s^\star)\) and \(\kappa W\) are nonnegative, so \(r(s^\star)=\kappa W\) and
\[
r(s^\star)\,\delta+\kappa(2s^\star-d)=\kappa W\delta-\kappa\delta W=0 .
\]
% lean: truncSelector_interior_stationary % lean: truncSelector_sign_core

\emph{Step 6 (the selector minimizes the slice).}  Fix \(\sigma\in[0,d]\).  By Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the vectors \((\sqrt A,s^\star,d-s^\star)\) and \((\sqrt A,\sigma,d-\sigma)\),
\[
A+s^\star\sigma+(d-s^\star)(d-\sigma)\le r(s^\star)\,r(\sigma).
\]
% lean: truncSeg_cs_sqrt
Subtracting \(r(s^\star)^2=A+(s^\star)^2+(d-s^\star)^2\) from both sides and using the algebraic identity
\[
A+s^\star\sigma+(d-s^\star)(d-\sigma)-r(s^\star)^2=(\sigma-s^\star)(2s^\star-d),
\]
we obtain \((\sigma-s^\star)(2s^\star-d)\le r(s^\star)\bigl(r(\sigma)-r(s^\star)\bigr)\); multiplying by \(\kappa\ge0\) and adding \(r(s^\star)\delta(\sigma-s^\star)\) to both sides gives, together with Step 5,
\[
0\le(\sigma-s^\star)\Bigl(r(s^\star)\delta+\kappa(2s^\star-d)\Bigr)
\le r(s^\star)\Bigl(\delta(\sigma-s^\star)+\kappa\bigl(r(\sigma)-r(s^\star)\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
Since \(r(s^\star)>0\) (because \(d>0\) makes \((s^\star)^2+(d-s^\star)^2>0\)),
\[
\delta(\sigma-s^\star)+\kappa\bigl(r(\sigma)-r(s^\star)\bigr)\ge0,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
g_d(s^\star)\le g_d(\sigma),
\]
because \(g_d(\sigma)-g_d(s^\star)=\delta(\sigma-s^\star)+\kappa(r(\sigma)-r(s^\star))\). % lean: truncSeg_selector_le

Combining Steps 3 and 6: for every \(s\in K_d\) there is \(\sigma\in[0,d]\) with \(\Phi(t^\star)=g_d(s^\star)\le g_d(\sigma)=\Phi(h(\sigma))\le\Phi(s)\), so
\[
t^\star=(M-d,s^\star,d-s^\star)\in K_d
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Phi(t^\star)\le\Phi(s)\ \text{ for every }s\in K_d .
\]
% lean: trunc_from_minimizer

\emph{Step 7 (the two robustness regimes).}  For \(\kappa>0\), an admissible pair \((S,\lambda)\) has active-set point \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\) lying in \(\Delta_M\) and minimizing \(\Phi\) over \(\Delta_M\) by \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-active-set}; Steps 2--6 then give exactly the two stated alternatives. % lean: activeSetPoint_isMinimizer % lean: weighted_simplex_truncation

For \(\kappa=0\), a point \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\) of the exposed \(\alpha\)-minimizing face minimizes the linear objective \(\Phi\) over \(\Delta_M\), again by \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-active-set}, so Steps 2--6 apply verbatim.  Under the stated tie convention the first alternative is selected whenever the face meets \(K_d\).  Finally, at \(\kappa=0\) the two selector guards read \(0\le\delta\) and \(\delta\le0\), so the interior branch is never taken and
\[
s^\star=\begin{cases}0,&\alpha_y-\alpha_z\ge0,\\ d,&\alpha_y-\alpha_z<0,\end{cases}
\]
which is the ordered endpoint rule. % lean: exposedMinFace_isMinimizer % lean: truncSelector
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-truncation} reduces the constrained implementable calculation to a one-dimensional boundary segment whenever the relaxed optimizer violates the lower bound. The selector chooses either an endpoint of that segment or the unique interior point identified by the displayed expression. This is the source of the truncation correction in \cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star}.

The final ingredient ties the reduced optimization problems back to the implementability loss defined in the main text.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:rounding-gap-reduction}[Reduced Gap Identity]
For any real $r$ and any $\kappa\ge 0$, under \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily}, the implementability loss of \cref{obj:def:implementability-gap} satisfies
\[
\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)
=
\inf_{\substack{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\\ y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m}}
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
-
\inf_{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m}
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:rounding-gap-reduction}]
Set \(q=2(m-1)\); \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} gives \(m\ge2\), so \(m\ne0\) and \(q>0\).  By \cref{obj:def:implementability-gap} it suffices to identify the two image sets
\[
F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\bigr)
=\bigl\{F_{r,\kappa}(X):X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\bigr\},
\qquad
F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\bigr)
=\bigl\{F_{r,\kappa}(X):X\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\bigr\}
\]
with the corresponding sets of reduced objective values, since an infimum depends only on the set of attained values.

\emph{Step 1 (the coordinate change is invertible on \(T_m\)).}  Given \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\), put
\[
u=1-x,
\qquad
v=\frac{z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y}{2m}.
\]
Then \(1-u=x\) by construction.  The simplex equation \(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\) gives \((m-1)x=m-\tfrac{1}{2}(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}})\), hence \(1+(m-1)u=m-(m-1)x=\tfrac12(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}})\), and therefore
\[
1+(m-1)u-mv=\frac{y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}}{2}-\frac{z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y}{2}=y,
\qquad
1+(m-1)u+mv=\frac{y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}}{2}+\frac{z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y}{2}=z_{\mathrm{sp}} .
\]
So every point of \(T_m\) is the spectral-coordinate image of the block matrix \(X(u,v)\) with these parameters. % lean: coord_inv

\emph{Step 2 (the relaxed image).}  We claim
\[
\bigl\{F_{r,\kappa}(X):X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\bigr\}
=\bigl\{\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}):(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\bigr\}.
\]
For the inclusion \(\subseteq\): every \(X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) is of the form \(X(u,v)\) by \cref{obj:def:block-elliptope}, and \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates} places its spectral coordinates in \(T_m\) and identifies \(F_{r,\kappa}(X(u,v))=\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\).  For \(\supseteq\): given \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\), Step 1 produces \((u,v)\) with these spectral coordinates, and \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates} then gives both \(X(u,v)\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) and \(F_{r,\kappa}(X(u,v))=\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\). % lean: hRel

\emph{Step 3 (the implementable image).}  We claim
\[
\bigl\{F_{r,\kappa}(X):X\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\bigr\}
=\bigl\{\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}):(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m,\ y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\bigr\}.
\]
For \(\subseteq\): let \(X=X(P)\) with \(P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\).  Because \(P\) is invariant under the two-block automorphism group and \(m\ge2\), all within-community off-diagonal second moments coincide and all across-community ones coincide, so there are \(u,v\) with
\[
X(P)=X(u,v);
\]
% lean: secondMoment_blockSym_of_exchangeable
\cref{obj:lem:pm-reduced-slice-characterization} then gives \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) together with \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\), and \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates} identifies \(F_{r,\kappa}(X)=\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\).  For \(\supseteq\): given \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) with \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\), Step 1 produces \((u,v)\) realizing these coordinates, \cref{obj:lem:pm-reduced-slice-characterization} places \(X(u,v)\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\), and \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates} again matches the objective values. % lean: pm_reduced_slice_characterization

\emph{Step 4 (conclusion).}  Substituting the two image identities of Steps 2 and 3 into \cref{obj:def:implementability-gap},
\[
\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)
=
\inf_{\substack{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\\ y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m}}
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
-
\inf_{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m}
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}),
\]
as claimed. % lean: rounding_gap_reduction
\end{proof}

\cref{obj:lem:rounding-gap-reduction} is the bridge from geometry back to design. The relaxed benchmark is the infimum over \(T_m\), while the implementable benchmark is the same reduced objective with the parity truncation imposed. Consequently, the loss is exactly the value difference between these two scalar problems, which is the quantity evaluated by the active-set and truncation formulas above.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:symmetry-reduction}]
Write \(n=2m\) and, for a matrix \(X\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\), introduce the two off-diagonal block sums and the two pair counts
\[
S_{\mathrm{w}}(X)=\sum_{\substack{i\ne j\\ i,j\text{ same community}}}X_{ij},
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{a}}(X)=\sum_{\substack{i,j\\ \text{opposite communities}}}X_{ij},
\]
\[
N_{\mathrm{w}}=2m(m-1),
\qquad
N_{\mathrm{a}}=2m^2 ,
\]
the counts being the numbers of ordered index pairs of each kind.  Call \(G(d,w,c)\) the \emph{block-constant} matrix with \(d\) on the diagonal, \(w\) on within-community off-diagonal entries, and \(c\) on across-community entries.  \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} gives \(m\ge2\), so \(N_{\mathrm{w}},N_{\mathrm{a}}>0\).

\emph{Step 1 (a master trace identity).}  If \(X\) is symmetric, then grouping the \(n^2\) ordered pairs into diagonal, within-community, and across-community pairs gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(G(d,w,c)\,X\bigr)
=d\sum_{i}X_{ii}+w\,S_{\mathrm{w}}(X)+c\,S_{\mathrm{a}}(X).
\]
% lean: trace_blockConstMat_mul
The three matrices \(L_m\), \(L_m^\dagger\) and \(J_n\) are block-constant: \(L_m\) has the constant weighted degree \((m-1)a/m+b\) on its diagonal and entries \(-a/m\), \(-b/m\) off it; \(J_n=G(1,1,1)\); and \(L_m^\dagger\) is a linear combination of the identity and the two rank-one projections onto the all-ones and community-sign directions, all three of which are block-constant.  Consequently, if \(X\) and \(Y\) are symmetric with unit diagonal and
\[
S_{\mathrm{w}}(Y)=S_{\mathrm{w}}(X),
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{a}}(Y)=S_{\mathrm{a}}(X),
\]
then \(\operatorname{tr}(L_mY)=\operatorname{tr}(L_mX)\), \(\operatorname{tr}(L_m^\dagger Y)=\operatorname{tr}(L_m^\dagger X)\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(J_nY)=\operatorname{tr}(J_nX)\); if in addition \(\|Y\|_{S_2}\le\|X\|_{S_2}\), then, since \(\kappa\ge0\),
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(Y)\le F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]
% lean: trace_symmetrize % lean: designObjective_le_of_blockSums
This comparison is the common engine of the two symmetrization claims.

\emph{Step 2 (relaxed matrices: the block average is feasible).}  Let \(X\) be positive semidefinite with \(X_{ii}=1\) for all \(i\), and set
\[
u=\frac{S_{\mathrm{w}}(X)}{N_{\mathrm{w}}},
\qquad
v=\frac{S_{\mathrm{a}}(X)}{N_{\mathrm{a}}},
\]
the averages of the within- and across-community off-diagonal entries.  By construction \(X(u,v)\) is symmetric with unit diagonal and
\[
S_{\mathrm{w}}\bigl(X(u,v)\bigr)=N_{\mathrm{w}}u=S_{\mathrm{w}}(X),
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{a}}\bigl(X(u,v)\bigr)=N_{\mathrm{a}}v=S_{\mathrm{a}}(X).
\]

Membership in \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) requires the three inequalities of \cref{obj:def:block-elliptope}.  First, testing positive semidefiniteness against \(e_i-e_j\) and \(e_i+e_j\) and using the unit diagonal gives
\[
0\le(e_i-e_j)^\top X(e_i-e_j)=2-2X_{ij},
\qquad
0\le(e_i+e_j)^\top X(e_i+e_j)=2+2X_{ij},
\]
so \(|X_{ij}|\le1\) for all \(i,j\). % lean: abs_entry_le_one
Averaging the bound \(X_{ij}\le1\) over the \(N_{\mathrm{w}}\) within-community off-diagonal pairs gives \(u\le1\), i.e.\ \(1-u\ge0\).  Second, with \(s\) the community sign vector (\(+1\) on \(A_m\), \(-1\) on \(B_m\)) the sign product \(s_is_j\) is \(+1\) on diagonal and within-community pairs and \(-1\) on across-community pairs, so
\[
0\le s^\top Xs=\sum_iX_{ii}+S_{\mathrm{w}}(X)-S_{\mathrm{a}}(X)
=2m+2m(m-1)u-2m^2v
=2m\bigl(1+(m-1)u-mv\bigr),
\]
and dividing by \(2m>0\) gives \(1+(m-1)u-mv\ge0\).  Third, testing against the all-ones vector, whose quadratic form is the total entry sum,
\[
0\le\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}=\sum_iX_{ii}+S_{\mathrm{w}}(X)+S_{\mathrm{a}}(X)
=2m\bigl(1+(m-1)u+mv\bigr),
\]
giving \(1+(m-1)u+mv\ge0\).  With \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} this is exactly \(X(u,v)\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\). % lean: blockElliptopeMem_symmetrize

\emph{Step 3 (relaxed matrices: the objective does not increase).}  The block sums already match by Step 2, so by Step 1 only the Frobenius term has to be compared.  Squaring entrywise and grouping pairs as before,
\[
\|X\|_{S_2}^2=2m+\sum_{\text{within}}X_{ij}^2+\sum_{\text{across}}X_{ij}^2,
\qquad
\|X(u,v)\|_{S_2}^2=2m+N_{\mathrm{w}}u^2+N_{\mathrm{a}}v^2 .
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz over each of the two pair families gives \(\bigl(\sum_{\text{within}}X_{ij}\bigr)^2\le N_{\mathrm{w}}\sum_{\text{within}}X_{ij}^2\), i.e.
\[
N_{\mathrm{w}}u^2\le\sum_{\text{within}}X_{ij}^2,
\qquad\text{and likewise}\qquad
N_{\mathrm{a}}v^2\le\sum_{\text{across}}X_{ij}^2 .
\]
Hence \(\|X(u,v)\|_{S_2}\le\|X\|_{S_2}\), % lean: frobeniusNorm_symmetrize_le
and Step 1 yields
\[
F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(u,v)\bigr)\le F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]
% lean: symmetrize_objective_le

\emph{Step 4 (assignment laws: the orbit average).}  Let \(H\) be the (finite, nonempty) group of two-block automorphisms: permutations \(\sigma\) of the units that either preserve both communities setwise or swap them.  For \(P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}\) define the orbit average
\[
P^{\mathrm{orb}}(Z=z)=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{\sigma\in H}P\bigl(Z=z\circ\sigma\bigr).
\]
It is a probability law (an average of such), it is sign-symmetric because each summand is, and it is \(H\)-invariant because precomposing \(z\) with \(\tau\in H\) permutes the summation over \(H\); hence \(P^{\mathrm{orb}}\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\). % lean: orbitAvgDesign_mem_class
Sign symmetry gives zero one-point margins: replacing \(z\) by \(-z\) leaves the law unchanged and flips the sign of \(Z_i\), so \(\mathbb E[Z_i]=-\mathbb E[Z_i]\) and therefore
\[
\mathbb E[Z_i]=0
\qquad\text{for every }i .
\]
% lean: balanced_zero_margin

\emph{Step 5 (assignment laws: the objective does not increase).}  The same change of variables in the expectation gives the entrywise orbit-averaging identity
\[
X\bigl(P^{\mathrm{orb}}\bigr)_{ij}
=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{\sigma\in H}X(P)_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)} .
\]
% lean: orbitAvg_secondMoment_entry
Every \(\sigma\in H\) maps within-community ordered pairs to within-community ordered pairs and across-community pairs to across-community pairs, and does so bijectively; hence summing the display over each family gives
\[
S_{\mathrm{w}}\bigl(X(P^{\mathrm{orb}})\bigr)=S_{\mathrm{w}}\bigl(X(P)\bigr),
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{a}}\bigl(X(P^{\mathrm{orb}})\bigr)=S_{\mathrm{a}}\bigl(X(P)\bigr).
\]
% lean: Ssame_orbitAvg % lean: Scross_orbitAvg
For the Frobenius term, Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the average over \(H\) gives entrywise
\[
X\bigl(P^{\mathrm{orb}}\bigr)_{ij}^2
\le\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{\sigma\in H}X(P)_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}^2 ,
\]
and summing over all \((i,j)\), each inner sum being a permutation of the full square sum, yields
\[
\bigl\|X(P^{\mathrm{orb}})\bigr\|_{S_2}\le\bigl\|X(P)\bigr\|_{S_2}.
\]
% lean: frobeniusNorm_orbitAvg_le
Both second moments are symmetric with unit diagonal (\(X(P)_{ii}=\mathbb E[Z_i^2]=1\)), so Step 1 applies and gives
\[
F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(P^{\mathrm{orb}})\bigr)\le F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(P)\bigr).
\]
% lean: design_symmetrize

\emph{Step 6 (an infimum-reduction principle).}  Let \(f\) be a real function on a set \(\mathcal S\), let \(\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal S\) be nonempty, suppose \(f\) is bounded below on \(\mathcal S\), and suppose every \(x\in\mathcal S\) admits \(y\in\mathcal T\) with \(f(y)\le f(x)\).  Then
\[
\inf_{\mathcal S}f=\inf_{\mathcal T}f .
\]
Indeed \(\inf_{\mathcal S}f\le\inf_{\mathcal T}f\) because \(\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal S\), and conversely each value \(f(x)\), \(x\in\mathcal S\), dominates some \(f(y)\ge\inf_{\mathcal T}f\). % lean: sInf_image_reduce

\emph{Step 7 (the two infimum identities).}  On the relaxed side take \(\mathcal S=\{X\succeq0:X_{ii}=1\ \forall i\}\) and \(\mathcal T=\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\).  Then \(\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal S\), since each \(X(u,v)\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) has unit diagonal and, by the three nonnegative spectral coordinates, is positive semidefinite; % lean: blockElliptope_subset_elliptope
\(\mathcal T\ne\emptyset\) because \(I_n=X(0,0)\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\); \(F_{r,\kappa}\) is bounded below on \(\mathcal S\), since the three block-constant matrices have bounded entries and \(|X_{ij}|\le1\) throughout \(\mathcal S\) while the Frobenius term is nonnegative; % lean: designObjective_bddBelow
and Steps 2--3 supply the dominating point.  Step 6 gives
\[
\inf_{\substack{X\succeq0\\ X_{ii}=1\ \forall i}}F_{r,\kappa}(X)
=\inf_{X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}}F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]

On the implementable side take \(\mathcal S=\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}\) and \(\mathcal T=\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\), with \(f(P)=F_{r,\kappa}(X(P))\).  Then \(\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal S\) by \cref{obj:def:block-exchangeable-design-class}; \(\mathcal T\ne\emptyset\) because the iid fair-sign law is block-exchangeable; % lean: iidDesign_mem_blockExchangeable
\(f\) is bounded below because every second moment \(X(P)=\mathbb E_P[ZZ^\top]\) is a Gram matrix, hence positive semidefinite, with unit diagonal, so it lies in the set \(\mathcal S\) of the previous paragraph and inherits the same lower bound; % lean: assignmentSecondMoment_posSemidef
and Steps 4--5 supply the dominating law.  Step 6 gives
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{bal}}}F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(P)\bigr)
=\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}}F_{r,\kappa}\bigl(X(P)\bigr).
\]
% lean: symmetry_reduction
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:cut-corner-exactness}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item
By the two-block homophily assumption, \(m\ge2\), \(0<b<a\). Hence
\[
q=2(m-1)>0,\qquad a-b>0,\qquad a+b>0,\qquad 2b>0 .
\]
Set
\[
A=2b(a+b)\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{a-b}\right),
\qquad
B=2b(2m-2b-\kappa).
\]
From \(r<\max\{0,\min(A,B)\}\) and \(0\le r\), we first get
\[
r<\min(A,B).
\]
Indeed, if \(\min(A,B)\le0\), then the outer maximum is \(0\), contradicting
\(0\le r<0\). Otherwise the outer maximum is \(\min(A,B)\). Thus
\[
r<A,\qquad r<B .
\]
% lean: cut_corner_exactness

\item
We now convert these two inequalities into the coefficient separations needed at the cut vertex.
From \(r<A\), division by the positive number \(2b(a+b)\) gives
\[
\frac{r}{2b(a+b)}
<
1-\frac{\kappa}{a-b}.
\]
Multiplying by \(a-b>0\) and rearranging gives
\[
\kappa
<
(a-b)\left(1-\frac{r}{2b(a+b)}\right).
\]
Using
\[
(a+b)+\frac{r}{a+b}
-\left(2b+\frac{r}{2b}+\kappa\right)
=
(a-b)\left(1-\frac{r}{2b(a+b)}\right)-\kappa
\]
and \(c_x/q=(a+b)+r/(a+b)\), \(c_y=2b+r/(2b)\), we obtain
\[
\frac{c_x}{q}>c_y+\kappa .
\]
Similarly, from \(r<B\), division by \(2b>0\) gives
\[
\frac{r}{2b}<2m-2b-\kappa,
\]
and hence
\[
2b+\frac{r}{2b}+\kappa<2m.
\]
Since \(c_z=2m\), this is
\[
c_z>c_y+\kappa .
\]
% lean: cut_corner_exactness

\item
The hypotheses of \cref{obj:lem:cut-vertex-certificate} are therefore satisfied: \(q>0\), \(\kappa\ge0\), and the two coefficient separations above hold. Hence
\[
(0,2m,0)\in T_m,
\]
and every other point of \(T_m\) has strictly larger reduced objective value.

By \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}, the cut covariance is
\[
X_{\mathrm{cut}}=X(1,-1),
\]
and its spectral coordinates are
\[
(1-1,\;1+(m-1)\cdot1-m(-1),\;1+(m-1)\cdot1+m(-1))
=(0,2m,0).
\]
Thus \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\), and
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})
=
\phi_{r,\kappa}(0,2m,0).
\]
% lean: cut_vertex_certificate % lean: cutCovariance_eq_blockSym

\item
Let \(X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) and suppose \(X\ne X_{\mathrm{cut}}\). Write \(X=X(u,v)\). By \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}, the associated coordinates
\[
x=1-u,\qquad
y=1+(m-1)u-mv,\qquad
z_{\mathrm{sp}}=1+(m-1)u+mv
\]
belong to \(T_m\), and
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X)=\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
These coordinates cannot equal \((0,2m,0)\): if \(x=0\), then \(u=1\), and if also
\(z_{\mathrm{sp}}=0\), then
\[
0=1+(m-1)\cdot1+mv=m(1+v),
\]
so \(v=-1\) since \(m>0\), giving \(X=X_{\mathrm{cut}}\), a contradiction. The strict part of \cref{obj:lem:cut-vertex-certificate} therefore gives
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})
=
\phi_{r,\kappa}(0,2m,0)
<
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
=
F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]
Thus \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\) is the unique minimizer over \(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\).
% lean: block_spectral_coordinates

\item
The cut design \(P_{\mathrm{cut}}\) puts mass \(1/2\) on the cut assignment \(z^+\) (\(+1\) on \(A_m\), \(-1\) on \(B_m\)) and mass \(1/2\) on its global sign reversal \(z^-=-z^+\).  It is therefore sign-symmetric, i.e.\ \(P_{\mathrm{cut}}(Z=z)=P_{\mathrm{cut}}(Z=-z)\) for all \(z\).  Its two-point support is invariant under the two-block automorphism group: a permutation preserving both communities fixes each of \(z^+\) and \(z^-\), and the community swap exchanges them.  Hence
\[
P_{\mathrm{cut}}\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}} .
\]
% lean: cutDesign_eq_cutVDesign % lean: cutVDesign_mem
Moreover \(z^+_iz^+_j=z^-_iz^-_j\) for all \(i,j\), so both atoms contribute the same product and
\[
X(P_{\mathrm{cut}})_{ij}
=\mathbb E\bigl[Z_iZ_j\bigr]
=z^+_iz^+_j
=(X_{\mathrm{cut}})_{ij},
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
X(P_{\mathrm{cut}})=X_{\mathrm{cut}} .
\]
% lean: cutVDesign_secondMoment
Consequently \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\).

Next, every \(X\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\) is the second moment of a design \(P\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}\).  Because \(P\) is invariant under the two-block automorphism group and \(m\ge2\), all within-community off-diagonal second moments coincide and all across-community ones coincide, so
\[
X=X(u,v)
\qquad\text{for some }u,v ,
\]
% lean: secondMoment_blockSym_of_exchangeable
and \cref{obj:lem:pm-reduced-slice-characterization} places its spectral coordinates in the reduced triangle \(T_m\).  By \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates} this membership is exactly block-elliptope membership, so
\[
\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\subseteq \mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}.
\]
% lean: pm_slice_forward
Therefore, if \(X\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\) and \(X\ne X_{\mathrm{cut}}\), the strict relaxed optimality already proved yields
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})<F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]
Thus \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\) is also the unique minimizer over \(\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\).

\item
It remains to identify the gap. From the strict inequalities above, with equality allowed only at
\(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\), we have the non-strict minimizing inequalities
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})\le F_{r,\kappa}(X)
\quad\text{for all }X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}},
\]
and
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})\le F_{r,\kappa}(X)
\quad\text{for all }X\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}.
\]
Since \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\) belongs to both feasible classes, each of the two infima is attained at \(X_{\mathrm{cut}}\):
\[
\inf_{X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}}F_{r,\kappa}(X)
=F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})
=\inf_{X\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}}F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]
Hence, by the definition of the implementability gap in \cref{obj:def:implementability-gap},
\[
\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)
=
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})
-
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{cut}})
=0.
\]
This proves all asserted conclusions.
% lean: sInf_image_eq_of_minimizer % lean: cut_corner_exactness
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:robust-corner-exactness}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item We first prove the finite-weight equivalence.  Write
\[
q=2(m-1),\qquad
c_x=q\left((a+b)+\frac r{a+b}\right),\qquad
c_y=2b+\frac r{2b},\qquad
c_z=2m .
\]
Suppose that some \(\kappa\ge 0\) makes \(I_n\) a relaxed minimizer over
\(\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\).  For an arbitrary
\((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\), set
\[
u=1-x,\qquad v=\frac{z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y}{2m}.
\]
The simplex equation \(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\) gives \(1+(m-1)u=\tfrac12(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}})\), so these formulas recover
\[
1-u=x,\qquad
1+(m-1)u-mv=y,\qquad
1+(m-1)u+mv=z_{\mathrm{sp}} .
\]
% lean: reduced_coord_inverse
Using \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}, this inverse change of variables sends
\((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\) to a feasible block matrix \(X(u,v)\), and the matrix
objective equals the reduced objective at \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\).  Since
\(I_n=X(0,0)\) corresponds to \((1,1,1)\), so that
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(I_n)=\phi_{r,\kappa}(1,1,1),
\]
% lean: identity_objective_eq_reduced_center
the assumed matrix minimality says that
\((1,1,1)\) minimizes
\[
c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
+\kappa\sqrt{q x^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}
\]
on \(T_m\).  The certificate-necessity part of
\cref{obj:lem:frobenius-center-certificate} therefore gives
\[
\frac{c_x}{q}=c_y,\qquad c_y=c_z .
\]
% lean: center_coeffs_of_identity_relaxed_min
The first equality is
\[
a+b+\frac r{a+b}=2b+\frac r{2b},
\]
and by the identity
\[
\Bigl(a+b+\frac r{a+b}\Bigr)-\Bigl(2b+\frac r{2b}\Bigr)
=(a-b)\left(1-\frac{r}{2b(a+b)}\right)
\]
this is equivalent to
\[
(a-b)\left(1-\frac{r}{2b(a+b)}\right)=0.
\]
By two-block homophily, \(a>b>0\), so \(a-b\ne0\) and the second factor vanishes:
\[
r=2b(a+b).
\]
% lean: robust_coeff_r_eq_of_center
Substituting this into \(c_y=c_z\) gives
\[
2b+\frac{2b(a+b)}{2b}=2m,
\]
that is \(a+3b=2m\).  Thus the affine-balanced locus holds. % lean: robust_locus_of_center_coeffs
Conversely, if
\[
a+3b=2m,\qquad r=2b(a+b),
\]
then substituting \(r=2b(a+b)\) into the three coefficients gives
\[
\frac{c_x}{q}=(a+b)+\frac{2b(a+b)}{a+b}=a+3b,
\qquad
c_y=2b+\frac{2b(a+b)}{2b}=a+3b,
\qquad
c_z=2m=a+3b,
\]
so \(c_x/q=c_y=c_z\). % lean: robust_center_coeffs_of_locus
Taking \(\kappa=1\), the strict
optimality part of \cref{obj:lem:frobenius-center-certificate}, transported through
\cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}, gives \(I_n\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\)
and strict optimality against every \(X\ne I_n\).  For \(X=I_n\) the desired inequality
is equality, while for \(X\ne I_n\) it follows from strict optimality.  Hence a finite
nonnegative robustness weight exists exactly on the stated locus. % lean: robust_corner_exactness

\item On the affine-balanced locus, let \(\kappa>0\).  As above,
\[
\frac{c_x}{q}=c_y=c_z .
\]
The point \(I_n=X(0,0)\) has reduced coordinates \((1,1,1)\) by
\cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}.  The Frobenius-center certificate, \cref{obj:lem:frobenius-center-certificate}, gives strict reduced optimality of
\((1,1,1)\) on \(T_m\).  Translating back by
\cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}, \(I_n\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\), and
for any \(X=X(u,v)\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\) with \(X\ne I_n\), the associated
reduced coordinates are not \((1,1,1)\): if they were, then \(x=1\) gives \(u=0\), and then \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}=1\) reads \(1+mv=1\), so \(v=0\) since \(m>0\), i.e.\ \(X=I_n\).  Therefore
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(I_n)<F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\] % lean: identity_strict_relaxed_min_of_locus % lean: identity_mem_blockElliptope

\item The iid law assigns mass \(2^{-n}\) to every sign vector, so it is invariant under
sign flip, under within-block permutations, and under the block swap, whence
\[
P_{\mathrm{iid}}\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sym}}.
\] % lean: iidDesign_mem_blockExchangeable
Its second moments are diagonal: the diagonal entries are \(\mathbb E[Z_i^2]=1\), and for \(i\ne j\)
the involution \(z\mapsto z'\) that flips only the \(i\)-th sign is a bijection of the
assignment space preserving the uniform law and sending \(Z_iZ_j\) to \(-Z_iZ_j\), so
\[
\mathbb E[Z_iZ_j]=-\mathbb E[Z_iZ_j]=0
\qquad (i\ne j).
\]
Thus all off-diagonal moments vanish and
\[
X(P_{\mathrm{iid}})=I_n .
\] % lean: iidDesign_secondMoment

\item Now assume the affine-balanced locus fails.  If for some \(\kappa>0\) the matrix
\(I_n\) were a relaxed minimizer, then the same center-coefficient necessity argument
from the first step would give
\[
\frac{c_x}{q}=c_y,\qquad c_y=c_z,
\]
and the same algebra would force
\[
a+3b=2m,\qquad r=2b(a+b),
\]
contradicting the assumption.  Hence \(I_n\) is not a relaxed minimizer at any positive
finite robustness weight off the locus. % lean: robust_corner_exactness

\item Finally, let \((X_\kappa)_{\kappa\in\mathbb R}\) be any family of relaxed
minimizers for positive \(\kappa\).  Since \(r\ge0\), \(a>b>0\) and \(m\ge2\), the reduced linear
coefficients satisfy \(c_x,c_y,c_z\ge0\); and every point of \(T_m\) has nonnegative coordinates, so the linear part \(c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}\) is nonnegative on \(T_m\).  Put
\[
L=c_x+c_y+c_z .
\]
The reduced coordinates of \(I_n\) are \((1,1,1)\), whose weighted squared norm is \(q\cdot1+1+1=q+2=2m\); hence, by \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates},
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(I_n)=L+\kappa\sqrt{2m}
\qquad\text{for every }\kappa .
\]
Comparing each minimizer with the feasible point \(I_n\) gives
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_\kappa)\le F_{r,\kappa}(I_n)=L+\kappa\sqrt{2m}.
\]
Writing \(X_\kappa=X(u_\kappa,v_\kappa)\) and setting
\[
N_{\mathrm{w}}=2m(m-1)>0,
\qquad
N_{\mathrm{a}}=2m^2>0,
\]
the reduced squared norm decomposes as
\[
q(1-u_\kappa)^2+
\bigl(1+(m-1)u_\kappa-mv_\kappa\bigr)^2+
\bigl(1+(m-1)u_\kappa+mv_\kappa\bigr)^2
=
2m+N_{\mathrm{w}}u_\kappa^2+N_{\mathrm{a}}v_\kappa^2 .
\]
% lean: reduced_norm_sq_block_coords
Suppose some within-community off-diagonal entry stayed at distance at least \(\varepsilon>0\) from its target for arbitrarily large \(\kappa\); that entry equals \(u_\kappa\), so \(|u_\kappa|\ge\varepsilon\) and the displayed squared norm is at least \(2m+N_{\mathrm{w}}\varepsilon^2\).  Setting
\[
\delta=\sqrt{2m+N_{\mathrm{w}}\varepsilon^2}-\sqrt{2m}>0,
\]
the robustness term at \(X_\kappa\) is therefore at least \(\kappa(\sqrt{2m}+\delta)\), and, the linear part being nonnegative,
\[
\kappa\bigl(\sqrt{2m}+\delta\bigr)\le F_{r,\kappa}(X_\kappa)
\le L+\kappa\sqrt{2m},
\]
which forces \(\kappa\delta\le L\); this fails for every \(\kappa>L/\delta\), a contradiction.  Hence \(u_\kappa\to0\).  The same argument with \(N_{\mathrm{a}}\) in place of \(N_{\mathrm{w}}\), applied to an across-community entry (which equals \(v_\kappa\)), shows \(v_\kappa\to0\).  Diagonal entries of \(X_\kappa\) are identically \(1\), matching \(I_n\) exactly, while off-diagonal entries
are either \(u_\kappa\) or \(v_\kappa\) and the corresponding entries of \(I_n\) are \(0\). Hence every entry of \(X_\kappa\) converges to
the corresponding entry of \(I_n\) as \(\kappa\to\infty\). % lean: robust_minimizers_tendsto_identity_entries
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:gap-window}]
1.  \Cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} gives \(m\ge2\), \(b<a\), and \(b>0\). Hence
\[
q=2(m-1)>0,\qquad \sqrt q>0,\qquad a+b>0,\qquad a-b>0.
\]
\cref{obj:ass:low-scale-two-block} gives
\[
2m-a-3b>0.
\]
These inequalities are the standing sign facts used below for division and multiplication by positive quantities.  % lean: gap_window

2.  Assume now that \cref{obj:ass:odd-community-size} holds, and fix
\[
0\le \kappa<\kappa_{\mathrm{gap}}(m,a,b)=\frac{(2m-a-3b)(a-b)\sqrt q}{a+b}.
\]
Multiplying this strict inequality by the positive factor \((a+b)/((a-b)\sqrt q)\),
\[
\frac{\kappa(a+b)}{(a-b)\sqrt q}<2m-a-3b.
\]
Next, from \(1+\dfrac{2b}{a-b}=\dfrac{a+b}{a-b}\) we get the identity
\[
R_x^+-R_x^-
=(a+b)\Bigl(2m-a-b-\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt q}\Bigr)-2b(a+b)\Bigl(1+\frac{\kappa}{(a-b)\sqrt q}\Bigr)
=(a+b)\left((2m-a-3b)-\frac{\kappa(a+b)}{(a-b)\sqrt q}\right),
\]
whose right-hand side is positive by the previous display and \(a+b>0\).  Thus \(R_x^-<R_x^+=r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+\), and since
\[
r_{\mathrm{gap}}^-=\frac{R_x^-+R_x^+}{2}
\]
is the midpoint of \(R_x^-\) and \(R_x^+\),
\[
R_x^-<r_{\mathrm{gap}}^-<r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+.
\]
Also \(R_x^-\ge0\), because \(2b(a+b)>0\) and \(\kappa/((a-b)\sqrt q)\ge0\); and since \(1-\kappa/(a-b)\le1\le1+\kappa/((a-b)\sqrt q)\) and \(2b(a+b)>0\),
\[
2b(a+b)\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{a-b}\right)\le R_x^-,
\]
so, the maximum of two quantities each at most \(R_x^-\) being at most \(R_x^-\),
\[
r_{\mathrm{cut,gap}}(m,a,b,\kappa)
=\max\left\{0,\;2b(a+b)\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{a-b}\right)\right\}
\le R_x^-.
\]
Combining these inequalities yields
\[
r_{\mathrm{cut,gap}}(m,a,b,\kappa)
<
r_{\mathrm{gap}}^-(m,a,b,\kappa)
<
r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+(m,a,b,\kappa).
\]  % lean: gap_window

3.  Let \(r_{\mathrm{gap}}^-<r<r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+\). From the preceding step,
\[
R_x^-<r.
\]
After division by \(2b(a+b)>0\), this says
\[
1+\frac{\kappa}{(a-b)\sqrt q}<\frac{r}{2b(a+b)}.
\]
Multiplying by \(a-b>0\) gives
\[
\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt q}
<
(a-b)\left(\frac{r}{2b(a+b)}-1\right).
\]
Using the identity
\[
2b+\frac r{2b}-\left(a+b+\frac r{a+b}\right)
=
(a-b)\left(\frac{r}{2b(a+b)}-1\right),
\]
and \(c_x/q=a+b+r/(a+b)\), this proves
\[
c_y>\frac{c_x}{q}+\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt q}.
\]
Similarly, the upper bound \(r<r_{\mathrm{gap}}^+=R_x^+=(a+b)\bigl(2m-a-b-\kappa/\sqrt q\bigr)\), divided by \(a+b>0\), gives
\[
\frac r{a+b}<2m-a-b-\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt q},
\]
hence, since \(c_z=2m\) and \(c_x/q=a+b+r/(a+b)\),
\[
c_z>\frac{c_x}{q}+\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt q}.
\]
By the spread-vertex certificate, \cref{obj:lem:spread-vertex-certificate}, the point
\[
\left(\frac{2m}{q},0,0\right)=\left(\frac{m}{m-1},0,0\right)
\]
belongs to \(T_m\) and is the strict unique minimizer of the reduced objective on \(T_m\).  % lean: spread_vertex_certificate

4.  The spread covariance is \(X(-1/(m-1),0)\), whose spectral coordinates are
\[
x=1-\left(-\frac1{m-1}\right)=\frac{m}{m-1}=\frac{2m}{q},\qquad
y=1-\frac{m-1}{m-1}=0,\qquad z_{\mathrm{sp}}=0.
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}, the membership of this triple in \(T_m\) gives
\[
X_{\mathrm{spread}}\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}},
\]
and the value of \(F_{r,\kappa}\) at \(X_{\mathrm{spread}}\) is the reduced objective at
\((2m/q,0,0)\). If \(X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\), write \(X=X(u,v)\). \Cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates} sends it to a point of \(T_m\) and identifies \(F_{r,\kappa}(X)\) with the reduced objective there. If this reduced point equals \((2m/q,0,0)\), then \(y=z_{\mathrm{sp}}=0\), so
\[
z_{\mathrm{sp}}-y=2mv=0
\quad\text{and}\quad
y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2+2(m-1)u=0,
\]
forcing \(v=0\) and \(u=-1/(m-1)\), hence \(X=X_{\mathrm{spread}}\). Therefore every \(X\ne X_{\mathrm{spread}}\) corresponds to a different point of \(T_m\), and the strict reduced optimality from Step 3 gives
\[
F_{r,\kappa}(X_{\mathrm{spread}})<F_{r,\kappa}(X).
\]  % lean: spread_certificate_relaxed_minimizer_and_gap

5.  Since \(m\) is odd, \cref{obj:lem:pm-reduced-slice-characterization} imposes the implementability condition
\[
y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m=\frac2m>0 .
\]
The spread point has \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=0\), so
\[
X_{\mathrm{spread}}\notin\mathcal C_m^{\pm}.
\]
For the loss, pass from \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) to the simplex variable
\[
t=(qx,\,y,\,z_{\mathrm{sp}}),
\qquad
t_x+t_y+t_z=qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m=M,
\]
a bijection between \(T_m\) and \(\Delta_M\) with inverse \((t_x,t_y,t_z)\mapsto(t_x/q,t_y,t_z)\).  With the coefficients and weights
\[
\alpha=\Bigl(\frac{c_x}{q},c_y,c_z\Bigr),
\qquad
\beta=\Bigl(\frac1q,1,1\Bigr),
\]
this bijection matches the two objectives exactly:
\[
\sum_i\alpha_it_i=\frac{c_x}{q}\,qx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
=c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}},
\qquad
\sum_i\beta_it_i^2=\frac{(qx)^2}{q}+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2=qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2,
\]
so \(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\kappa}(t)=\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\), and the parity constraint reads \(t_y+t_z\ge d_m\).  Under this dictionary the relaxed minimizer of Step 3 is
\[
t^{\mathrm{rel}}=(2m,0,0),
\qquad
t^{\mathrm{rel}}_y+t^{\mathrm{rel}}_z=0<d_m ,
\]
so it is infeasible for the truncated simplex \(K_{d_m}\).  We check the data required by \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-truncation}: the mass is \(M=2m>0\), the weights \(\beta=(1/q,1,1)\) are strictly positive with \(\beta_x=1/q=1/(2(m-1))\) and \(\beta_y=\beta_z=1\), the robustness weight satisfies \(\kappa\ge0\), and \(0<d_m=2/m\le1\le 2m=M\) since \(m\ge2\).  Its remaining input is the relaxed datum, namely the optimality of \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\): transporting the strict uniqueness of Step 3 through the objective-matching bijection above, \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\) is the strict unique minimizer of \(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\kappa}\) on \(\Delta_M\).  If \(\kappa>0\), \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-active-set} supplies an admissible support--multiplier pair \((S,\lambda)\) whose active-set point minimizes \(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\kappa}\) over \(\Delta_M\), hence coincides with \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\) by that uniqueness, so the truncation lemma may be applied to \((S,\lambda)\).  If \(\kappa=0\), the same lemma identifies the minimizers of the then-linear \(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\kappa}\) over \(\Delta_M\) with the exposed \(\alpha\)-minimizing face, so \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\) is a point of that face; uniqueness makes the face the single point \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\), which misses \(K_{d_m}\), so the tie convention attached to the \(\kappa=0\) branch is vacuous.  In either regime \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}_y+t^{\mathrm{rel}}_z=0<d_m\) puts us in the second alternative, which supplies a point \(t^\star\in K_{d_m}\) minimizing \(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\kappa}\) over \(K_{d_m}\); translating back, the corresponding \((x^\star,y^\star,z^\star_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) satisfies \(y^\star+z^\star_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\) and minimizes \(\phi_{r,\kappa}\) over the parity-truncated slice.  In particular both infima in \cref{obj:lem:rounding-gap-reduction} are attained:
\[
\inf_{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m}\phi_{r,\kappa}
=\phi_{r,\kappa}\Bigl(\frac{2m}{q},0,0\Bigr),
\qquad
\inf_{\substack{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\\ y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m}}\phi_{r,\kappa}
=\phi_{r,\kappa}(x^\star,y^\star,z^\star_{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
The truncated minimizer is feasible for the relaxed problem but differs from the relaxed minimizer, because \(y^\star+z^\star_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m>0\) while the relaxed minimizer has \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=0\); the strict uniqueness from Step 3 therefore gives
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}\Bigl(\frac{2m}{q},0,0\Bigr)<\phi_{r,\kappa}(x^\star,y^\star,z^\star_{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
Finally \cref{obj:lem:rounding-gap-reduction} identifies \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\) with the truncated reduced infimum minus the relaxed reduced infimum, so
\[
\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)>0.
\]  % lean: spread_certificate_relaxed_minimizer_and_gap % lean: pos_gap_of_unique_min_outside_slice

6.  It remains to prove the even case. Assume \(m\) is even and let \(X\in\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\). Write \(X=X(u,v)\), and let \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\) be its spectral coordinates. By \cref{obj:lem:block-spectral-coordinates}, this point lies in \(T_m\), so \(y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0\). For even \(m\), the parity threshold in \cref{obj:lem:pm-reduced-slice-characterization} is \(d_m=0\), and hence \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0\) automatically. The same lemma therefore gives \(X\in\mathcal C_m^{\pm}\). Thus
\[
\mathcal E_m^{\mathrm{blk}}\subseteq\mathcal C_m^{\pm}.
\]  % lean: blockElliptope_subset_implementable_of_even
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:sharp-rho-star}]
Throughout \cref{obj:ass:two-block-homophily} gives \(m\ge2\), hence \(q=2(m-1)>0\), \(a+b>0\) and \(2b>0\); with \(r\ge0\) this makes all three reduced coefficients nonnegative,
\[
c_x=q\Bigl((a+b)+\frac{r}{a+b}\Bigr)\ge0,
\qquad
c_y=2b+\frac{r}{2b}\ge0,
\qquad
c_z=2m\ge0 .
\]
Write \(M=2m\) and record once the change of variables used repeatedly below:
\[
t=(qx,\,y,\,z_{\mathrm{sp}}),
\qquad
\alpha^\sharp=\Bigl(\frac{c_x}{q},c_y,c_z\Bigr),
\qquad
\beta^\sharp=\Bigl(\frac1q,1,1\Bigr).
\]
Since \(q>0\), this is a bijection from \(T_m\) onto the simplex \(\Delta_M\) of mass \(M\), with inverse \(t\mapsto(t_x/q,t_y,t_z)\); it carries \(qx+y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2m\) to \(t_x+t_y+t_z=M\), it carries the parity constraint \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\) to \(t_y+t_z\ge d_m\), and it matches the two objectives:
\[
\sum_i\alpha^\sharp_it_i=\frac{c_x}{q}\,qx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}
=c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}},
\qquad
\sum_i\beta^\sharp_it_i^2=\frac{(qx)^2}{q}+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2
=qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2,
\]
so that
\[
\Phi_{\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa}(t)=\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
% lean: reducedTriangle_to_simplex % lean: simplex_to_reducedTriangle % lean: wsObj_eq_reducedObjective

\textbf{1. Equality and range.}
By definition, \(\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)\) is the difference between the implementable reduced value and the relaxed reduced value,
\[
\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)
=\inf_{\substack{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\\ y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m}}\phi_{r,\kappa}
-\inf_{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m}\phi_{r,\kappa}.
\]
The reduced gap identity in \cref{obj:lem:rounding-gap-reduction} identifies this same difference with \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\).  Hence
\[
\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)=\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa).
\]
% lean: rounding_gap_reduction
For the range, note first that on \(T_m\) all coordinates are nonnegative and \(\kappa\ge0\), so with the nonnegative coefficients recorded above
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
=c_xx+c_yy+c_zz_{\mathrm{sp}}+\kappa\sqrt{qx^2+y^2+z_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}\ge0 ,
\]
so both infima are over nonempty sets of reals bounded below by \(0\); the truncated set is nonempty because \((1,1,1)\in T_m\) satisfies \(y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}=2\ge2/m\ge d_m\) for \(m\ge2\).  Since the truncated feasible set is contained in \(T_m\), its infimum is at least the unconstrained one, i.e.
\[
\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)\ge0 ,
\]
and by the identity just proved also \(\Delta_m^{\pm}(r,\kappa)\ge0\). % lean: roundingLossCertificate_nonneg % lean: implementabilityGap_nonneg

\textbf{2. Zero loss.}
The set \(T_m\) is closed and bounded and \(\phi_{r,\kappa}\) is continuous on it, and the same holds for the truncated slice \(T_m\cap\{y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\}\), which is nonempty by Step 1; hence both infima are attained.  Fix minimizers: \(\xi^{\mathrm{rel}}\in T_m\) with
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}(\xi^{\mathrm{rel}})\le\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})
\quad\text{for all }(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m,
\]
and \(\xi^{\mathrm{impl}}\) in the truncated slice minimizing \(\phi_{r,\kappa}\) there; then
\[
\rho_\star=\phi_{r,\kappa}(\xi^{\mathrm{impl}})-\phi_{r,\kappa}(\xi^{\mathrm{rel}}).
\]
% lean: exists_relaxed_reduced_minimizer % lean: exists_implementable_reduced_minimizer % lean: relaxedReducedValue_eq_of_min % lean: implementableReducedValue_eq_of_min
If \(\rho_\star=0\), then \(\phi_{r,\kappa}(\xi^{\mathrm{impl}})=\phi_{r,\kappa}(\xi^{\mathrm{rel}})\), so \(\xi^{\mathrm{impl}}\) is itself a minimizer of \(\phi_{r,\kappa}\) over all of \(T_m\), and by construction it satisfies \(d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\); this is the asserted existence.  Conversely, if some \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\) minimizes \(\phi_{r,\kappa}\) over \(T_m\) and satisfies \(d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\), then it is feasible for the truncated problem, so
\[
\phi_{r,\kappa}(\xi^{\mathrm{rel}})\le\phi_{r,\kappa}(\xi^{\mathrm{impl}})\le\phi_{r,\kappa}(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})=\phi_{r,\kappa}(\xi^{\mathrm{rel}}),
\]
forcing equality throughout and \(\rho_\star=0\).  This is the stated zero-loss equivalence. % lean: sharp_roundingLoss_zero_iff_argmin_meets_slice % lean: zero_gap_iff_argmin_meets_slice

\textbf{3. Strict robustness.}
Assume \(\kappa>0\).  Under the change of variables recorded at the start, the positive-robustness part of \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-active-set} gives a point of \(\Delta_M\) that is the \emph{strictly} unique minimizer of \(\Phi_{\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa}\) there; transporting it back by the bijection, \(\phi_{r,\kappa}\) has exactly one minimizer on \(T_m\). % lean: sharp_reduced_active_set_and_unique
With uniqueness in hand, ``some relaxed minimizer satisfies \(d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\)'' and ``every relaxed minimizer satisfies \(d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\)'' are the same statement, so the criterion of Step 2 may be restated in the universal form:
\[
\rho_\star=0
\iff
\text{every minimizer }(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\text{ of }\phi_{r,\kappa}\text{ on }T_m\text{ has }d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}} .
\]
% lean: sharp_roundingLoss_zero_iff_unique_argmin_subset_slice

\textbf{4. Linear case.}
Assume \(\kappa=0\), and fix \((x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m\).  Passing to \(t=(qx,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\), the reduced objective is the linear weighted-simplex objective \(\sum_i\alpha^\sharp_it_i\).  The zero-robustness part of \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-active-set} says that \(t\) minimizes it over \(\Delta_M\) exactly when
\[
t_i\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ \alpha^\sharp_i\le\alpha^\sharp_j\ \text{ for every }j .
\]
Since \(q>0\), \(t_x\ne0\) is equivalent to \(x\ne0\), while \(t_y=y\) and \(t_z=z_{\mathrm{sp}}\); and \(\alpha^\sharp=(c_x/q,c_y,c_z)\).  Reading the three coordinates in turn, this condition is exactly
\[
x\ne0\Rightarrow\frac{c_x}{q}\le c_y\ \text{and}\ \frac{c_x}{q}\le c_z,
\qquad
y\ne0\Rightarrow c_y\le\frac{c_x}{q}\ \text{and}\ c_y\le c_z,
\qquad
z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ne0\Rightarrow c_z\le\frac{c_x}{q}\ \text{and}\ c_z\le c_y,
\]
as asserted. % lean: sharp_kappa_zero_reduced_min_iff

\textbf{5. Active-set formula.}
Assume \(\kappa>0\).  The same active-set construction from Step 3 supplies an admissible support \(S\subseteq\{x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\}\) and multiplier \(\lambda\) for \(\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa\).  The corresponding simplex point \(t^{S,\lambda}\) is nonnegative with \(t^{S,\lambda}_x+t^{S,\lambda}_y+t^{S,\lambda}_z=M=2m\); therefore its preimage has nonnegative coordinates and satisfies \(q\bigl(t^{S,\lambda}_x/q\bigr)+t^{S,\lambda}_y+t^{S,\lambda}_z=2m\), i.e.
\[
\Bigl(\frac{t_x^{S,\lambda}}{q},t_y^{S,\lambda},t_z^{S,\lambda}\Bigr)\in T_m .
\]
The weighted-simplex strict minimizer property transfers back through the objective-matching identity, so this reduced point minimizes \(\phi_{r,\kappa}\) over \(T_m\).  The value formula in \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-active-set} states \(\Phi_{\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa}(t^{S,\lambda})=M\lambda\), so the relaxed reduced value is
\[
\inf_{(x,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\in T_m}\phi_{r,\kappa}=2m\,\lambda .
\]
% lean: sharp_reduced_active_set_and_unique

\textbf{6. Truncation correction.}
Suppose no relaxed minimizer in \(T_m\) satisfies \(d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\).  By Step 2 the relaxed minimizer \(\xi^{\mathrm{rel}}\) exists; pass to its image \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}\in\Delta_M\) under the change of variables \(t=(qx,y,z_{\mathrm{sp}})\) recorded in the preamble, which minimizes \(\Phi_{\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa}\) over \(\Delta_M\) but has \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}_y+t^{\mathrm{rel}}_z<d_m\) by hypothesis.  (When \(\kappa=0\) the hypothesis also makes the tie convention of \cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-truncation} vacuous, since the exposed relaxed face is then disjoint from \(K_{d_m}\).)  Also \(0\le d_m\le 2m=M\), because \(d_m\in\{0,2/m\}\) and \(2/m\le 2\le 2m\) for \(m\ge2\).  \Cref{obj:lem:weighted-simplex-truncation} therefore puts a minimizer of \(\Phi_{\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa}\) over \(K_{d_m}=\{t\in\Delta_M:t_y+t_z\ge d_m\}\) on the boundary segment
\[
H_{d_m}=\{(2m-d_m,s,d_m-s):0\le s\le d_m\}
\]
at
\[
s_\star=\operatorname{truncSelector}(2m,d_m,\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa).
\]
Since the bijection of the preamble carries \(K_{d_m}\) onto the parity-truncated slice \(T_m\cap\{y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge d_m\}\) and preserves objective values, the implementable reduced value equals the objective at that boundary point:
\[
\operatorname{val}_{\mathrm{impl}}(m,a,b,r,\kappa)
=
\Phi_{\alpha^\sharp,\beta^\sharp,\kappa}
(2m-d_m,s_\star,d_m-s_\star).
\]
% lean: sharp_truncation_value_of_no_reduced_argmin_in_slice

\textbf{7. Even block size.}
If \(m\) is even, then \(d_m=0\).  A relaxed minimizer exists by Step 2, and every point of \(T_m\) has \(y,z_{\mathrm{sp}}\ge0\), hence \(0=d_m\le y+z_{\mathrm{sp}}\).  So that minimizer meets the slice, and the zero-loss criterion from Step 2 gives
\[
\rho_\star(m,a,b,r,\kappa)=0 .
\]
% lean: sharp_roundingLoss_zero_of_even
\end{proof}
