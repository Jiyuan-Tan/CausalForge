\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:le-cam-two-point-mse-source}]
Write
\[
\mathrm{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
=
\sup_{S\ \mathrm{measurable}}\bigl|Q_0(S)-Q_1(S)\bigr|
\]
for the total variation distance between the two \(n\)-sample laws. Let \(K_+=\max\{K,0\}\) and
define, before any law is chosen,
\[
c_K=\frac{\exp(-K_+)}{32}>0 ,
\]
so that \(c_K\) depends on the budget \(K\) alone. Fix any measurable estimator \(T\) for which the
two squared losses below are integrable, and put
\[
r=\frac{|\theta_1-\theta_0|}{2}=\frac{\Delta}{2}\ \ge 0 .
\]

\emph{Step 1 (estimation to testing).} Since \(2r=|\theta_0-\theta_1|\), the two error events
\[
S_0=\{|T-\theta_0|\ge r\},
\qquad
S_1=\{|T-\theta_1|\ge r\}
\]
cover the sample space: on the complement of \(S_0\) one has \(|T-\theta_0|<r\), whence
\(|T-\theta_1|\ge|\theta_0-\theta_1|-|T-\theta_0|>2r-r=r\), so \(S_0^{c}\subseteq S_1\). Therefore
\[
Q_0(S_0)+Q_1(S_1)
\ \ge\
Q_0(S_0)+Q_1(S_0^{c})
=
1-\bigl\{Q_1(S_0)-Q_0(S_0)\bigr\}
\ \ge\
1-\mathrm{TV}(Q_0,Q_1),
\]
and a sum of two numbers is at most twice their maximum, so
\[
\frac{1-\mathrm{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)}{2}
\le
\max\Bigl\{
Q_0(|T-\theta_0|\ge r),
Q_1(|T-\theta_1|\ge r)
\Bigr\}.
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.half_one_sub_tvDist_le_max_error

\emph{Step 2 (a testing floor valid for every finite budget).} The budget assumption
\(\mathrm{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)\le K\) is an inequality against the finite extended-real number attached to
\(K\); hence the divergence is finite, which in particular forces \(Q_0\ll Q_1\) (the
absolute-continuity side condition of the next inequality), and its real value satisfies
\(\mathrm{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)\le K_+\). The Bretagnolle--Huber inequality gives
\[
\frac12\exp\bigl\{-\mathrm{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)\bigr\}
\le
1-\mathrm{TV}(Q_0,Q_1);
\]
it holds because, with \(\varrho=\int\sqrt{\mathrm dQ_0/\mathrm dQ_1}\,\mathrm dQ_1\) the
Bhattacharyya affinity, Jensen's inequality gives
\(\exp\{-\tfrac12\mathrm{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)\}\le\varrho\), while Cauchy--Schwarz together with the
Scheff\'e identity gives \(\varrho^{2}\le 2\{1-\mathrm{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}\). Combining this with
\(\mathrm{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)\le K_+\) and Step 1,
\[
\frac{\exp(-K_+)}{4}
\le
\frac{1-\mathrm{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)}{2}
\le
\max\Bigl\{
Q_0(|T-\theta_0|\ge r),
Q_1(|T-\theta_1|\ge r)
\Bigr\}.
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.bretagnolle_huber_affinity

\emph{Step 3 (testing back to squared error).} For \(i=0,1\) the event \(|T-\theta_i|\ge r\) is the
event \((T-\theta_i)^2\ge r^2\), and the squared loss is nonnegative and integrable under \(Q_i\), so
Markov's inequality in integral form gives
\[
r^2\,Q_i(|T-\theta_i|\ge r)
\le
E_{Q_i}\bigl\{(T-\theta_i)^2\bigr\},
\qquad i=0,1 .
\]
Write
\[
p_i=Q_i(|T-\theta_i|\ge r),
\qquad
m_i=E_{Q_i}\bigl\{(T-\theta_i)^2\bigr\} .
\]
To pass from these two separate bounds to the maximum, distinguish which error probability is
larger. If \(p_0\le p_1\), then \(r^2\max\{p_0,p_1\}=r^2p_1\le m_1\le\max\{m_0,m_1\}\); if
\(p_1\le p_0\), the same argument with the indices reversed gives
\(r^2\max\{p_0,p_1\}\le\max\{m_0,m_1\}\). Therefore
\[
r^2\max\Bigl\{
Q_0(|T-\theta_0|\ge r),
Q_1(|T-\theta_1|\ge r)
\Bigr\}
\le
\max_{i=0,1}E_{Q_i}\bigl\{(T-\theta_i)^2\bigr\} .
\]

\emph{Step 4 (conclusion).} Multiplying the display of Step 2 by \(r^2\ge 0\) and inserting Step 3,
\[
\max_{i=0,1}E_{Q_i}\bigl\{(T-\theta_i)^2\bigr\}
\ \ge\
r^2\,\frac{\exp(-K_+)}{4} .
\]
Finally \(r^2=(\theta_1-\theta_0)^2/4=\Delta^2/4\), so the right-hand side equals
\(\exp(-K_+)\Delta^2/16\), and since \(\exp(-K_+)\Delta^2/16\ge \exp(-K_+)\Delta^2/32\),
\[
\max_{i=0,1}E_{Q_i}\bigl\{(T-\theta_i)^2\bigr\}
\ \ge\
\frac{\exp(-K_+)}{32}\,\Delta^2
=
c_K\Delta^2 .
\]
As \(T\) was an arbitrary such estimator and \(c_K\) was fixed in advance of \(Q_0,Q_1\), this is the
assertion. % lean: Causalean.Stat.Minimax.le_cam_two_point_mse
\end{proof}
