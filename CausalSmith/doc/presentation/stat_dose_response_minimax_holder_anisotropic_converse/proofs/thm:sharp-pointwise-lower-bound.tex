\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:sharp-pointwise-lower-bound}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The hypotheses match those of the appendix lower-bound engine.} The three hypotheses
assumed here are, item by item, the three hypotheses of
\cref{obj:lem:oracle-dose-regression-lower-all-beta} for the same covariate dimension \(d\) and the
same tuple of constants:
\begin{itemize}
\item the positive-smoothness bullet gives \(\alpha>0\), \(\beta>0\) and \(s>0\), which is the
lemma's positive-smoothness hypothesis;
\item the regime-constants bullet gives \(M>0\), \(c_0>0\), \(t_0\in(0,1)\),
\(\varepsilon_0\in(0,1/2)\) and \([t_0-\varepsilon_0,t_0+\varepsilon_0]\subset(0,1)\), which is the
lemma's regime-constants hypothesis, the last clause being the interior-window requirement of
\cref{obj:ass:interior-dose};
\item the strict-slack bullet is \cref{obj:ass:baseline-submodel-slack} for the same tuple
\((d,\beta,s,M,c_0,\varepsilon_0,t_0)\), which is the lemma's slack-baseline hypothesis.
\end{itemize}
In particular no hypothesis of the lemma is left unverified, and none is strengthened: the
treatment-density smoothness index \(\beta\) enters only through the class index
\(\mathcal P_{\alpha,\beta,s}(M,c_0,\varepsilon_0,t_0)\) and through the feasibility of the slack
baseline for that same \(\beta\), both of which are supplied by the hypotheses above for the given
fixed \(\beta>0\).

\item \emph{Applying the lemma.} \Cref{obj:lem:oracle-dose-regression-lower-all-beta} therefore
supplies a constant \(c_{\mathrm{or}}>0\), not depending on \(n\), and a threshold \(n_0\) such that
\[
R_n\!\left(
    \mathcal P_{\alpha,\beta,s}(M,c_0,\varepsilon_0,t_0),
    t_0
  \right)
\ge
c_{\mathrm{or}}\,n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge n_0 .
\]
The constant is the explicit one produced by that lemma's two-point construction: with outcome
scale \(B=M/2\), the amplitude \(\lambda\in(0,M/4]\) of its Hölder gate, the divergence budget
\(K=16\lambda^2(M-\eta_0)/B^2\), and the associated Le Cam constant \(c_K>0\) of
\cref{obj:lem:le-cam-two-point-mse-source},
\[
c_{\mathrm{or}}=4c_K\lambda^2>0 ,
\]
so it depends only on \(\alpha\), \(M\), and the slack \(\eta_0\) of
\cref{obj:ass:baseline-submodel-slack}, and in particular is positive because \(\lambda>0\) and
\(c_K>0\). The exponent \(2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\) is the one produced by that construction's
calibration of the bandwidth against a fixed divergence budget; it does not involve \(\beta\),
because the two least favourable laws of the construction perturb only the treatment regression and
leave the covariate and treatment densities at the common slack baseline \((p_0,q_0)\).
% lean: oracle_dose_regression_lower_all_beta

\item \emph{Conclusion.} Take \(c=c_{\mathrm{or}}\). Then \(c>0\), and reading the display of Step 2
in the orientation of the statement gives
\[
  c\, n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
  \le
  R_n\!\left(
    \mathcal P_{\alpha,\beta,s}(M,c_0,\varepsilon_0,t_0),
    t_0
  \right)
\]
for every \(n\ge n_0\), that is, for all sufficiently large sample sizes. This is the asserted
positive constant together with the desired eventual lower bound over the model class of
\cref{obj:def:holder-dose-class} for the minimax risk of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}.
% lean: sharp_pointwise_lower_bound
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
