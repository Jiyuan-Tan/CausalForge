\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:rho-oracle-regime-algebra}]
Throughout, \(\rho_n\) is the maximum of the two power terms in \cref{obj:def:published-hoif-rate},
so the claim is that the first term dominates.

\emph{Case \(n=1\).} Every real power of \(1\) equals \(1\), so both terms coincide,
\[
1^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=1=1^{-2/(1+d/(4s)+1/\alpha)},
\]
and hence \(\rho_1=\max\{1,1\}=1=1^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\), which is the asserted identity.
% lean: rho_oracle_regime_algebra

\emph{Case \(n>1\).} Since \(s>0\) we have \(4s>0\), so the smooth-covariate hypothesis \(d\le 4s\) can
be divided through:
\[
\frac{d}{4s}\le 1 .
\]
Adding \(1+1/\alpha\) to both sides,
\[
1+\frac d{4s}+\frac1\alpha
\le
2+\frac1\alpha .
\]
Both sides are strictly positive, since \(d/(4s)\ge0\) and \(1/\alpha>0\) by \(\alpha>0\). Dividing
the constant \(2>0\) by the two sides therefore reverses the inequality:
\[
\frac{2}{2+1/\alpha}
\le
\frac{2}{1+d/(4s)+1/\alpha}.
\]
% lean: rho_oracle_regime_algebra
Multiplying numerator and denominator of the left-hand side by \(\alpha>0\) gives the elementary
identity
\[
\frac{2}{2+1/\alpha}
=
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1},
\]
so that
\[
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}
\le
\frac{2}{1+d/(4s)+1/\alpha}.
\]
Since \(n>1\), the map \(x\mapsto n^{x}\) is strictly increasing; negating the two exponents reverses
the last inequality and then applying \(x\mapsto n^{x}\) preserves it, so
\[
n^{-2/(1+d/(4s)+1/\alpha)}
\le
n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)} .
\]
Thus the maximum in \cref{obj:def:published-hoif-rate} is attained by the treatment-regression
term, that is,
\[
\rho_n=n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)} .
\]
% lean: rho_oracle_regime_algebra
\end{proof}
