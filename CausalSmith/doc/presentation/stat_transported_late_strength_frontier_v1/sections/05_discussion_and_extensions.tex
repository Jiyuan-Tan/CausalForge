\section{Discussion and extensions}

The preceding sections characterize the expected length of honest confidence sets for the transported complier ratio in terms of the effective identification strength \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\). Combining \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} gives the oracle order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\), and \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} shows that the same order holds uniformly after conditioning on any admissible deterministic transport geometry. The parameter \(t_n\) is therefore the scalar comparison that joins the transported first stage with the dispersion of the target-to-source weights.

The order-one elbow has a simple interpretation. When the effective-strength threshold \(t_0\) is small, the compact parameter space \(\Theta=[-1,1]\) from \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range,obj:def:oracle-honesty} sets the natural length scale: honest procedures can always return a set whose length is bounded by the diameter of the causal range, and \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse} gives a matching order-one lower bound. As \(t_0\) increases, the score-inversion radius in \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} converts stronger transported first-stage information into the square-root improvement \(t_0^{-1/2}\). This is the transported analogue of the identification-robust ratio logic behind Anderson--Rubin and Fieller-style confidence sets, with honest expected length evaluated over the strength-indexed classes in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk}.

The compact causal range is central to the weak-first-stage comparison. \Cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} identifies the target complier-conditional contrast as \(\mu_{Y,n}/\mu_n\) and places it in \([-1,1]\) using bounded outcomes and monotone binary treatment receipt. That compactness supplies a meaningful fallback scale at weak effective strength and keeps score inversion inside the causal parameter space. The length criterion in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk} therefore evaluates uncertainty about a bounded transported causal contrast rather than an unrestricted real-valued ratio.

Fixed instrument overlap and Kish weight dispersion play distinct roles. The overlap condition in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap} controls the inverse-propensity source score used throughout \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment}. The second moment \(\kappa_n\) in \cref{obj:ass:weight-second-moment} records how much the target covariate law concentrates relative to the source covariate law. In the no-shift specialization of \cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction}, \(w(X)=1\) gives \(\kappa_n=1\) and reduces the strength index to \(n\mu_n^2\). Under covariate shift, the same first-stage mean produces less effective information when target mass is carried by high-dispersion weights.

The oracle and finite-cell feasible constructions have the same score-inversion structure. The oracle rule in \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} uses known \(w\) and \(e\) to form transported reduced-form and first-stage scores. The finite-cell rules in \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} replace the unknown target weights by empirical target cell frequencies and estimate the same transported contrasts cell by cell. Their matching upper and lower bounds show that, in the stated finite-cell designs, learning target cell masses from the covariate-only target sample preserves the oracle expected-length order.

The results also separate geometry from implementation. \Cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} treats an admissible deterministic geometry as fixed and gives constants uniform over such geometries. \Cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} then give sample-only or regular-cell procedures whose information sets match their model classes. This separation clarifies which part of the rate comes from weak transported-ratio information and which part comes from estimating the target covariate distribution in a finite-cell experiment. The focus on honest expected length is in the spirit of uniform inference criteria for weak and irregular problems, including the decision-theoretic perspective in \citet{ArmstrongKolesar2021}.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

Several extensions remain natural beyond the formal scope of the results above. First, feasible weight learning is developed for the uniform and regular finite-cell classes in \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class,obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class}. Extending the construction to broader weight-learning schemes would require conditions that replace the finite-cell frequency and U-statistic controls in \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}.

Second, the oracle and regular-cell procedures use the assignment propensity as an input, and the uniform finite-cell result fixes the balanced propensity through \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}. Propensity learning would add another nuisance-estimation component to the inverse-propensity score and would require coverage and length control that accounts for that component uniformly under weak transported first stages.

Third, continuous-covariate approximations are represented here through finite-cell and regular-cell designs with \(k_n=o(n^{1/2})\). A sieve or partition sequence for genuinely continuous covariates would need approximation conditions linking the target-to-source density ratio, the source contrasts, and the cell-scale growth to the effective-strength index.

Fourth, the minimax comparisons fix the threshold \(t_0>0\) in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk,obj:def:oracle-value,obj:def:fixed-geometry-value}. Moving thresholds \(t_0=t_{0,n}\) would change the local comparison between the order-one range and the square-root regime and would require a separate formulation of the length criterion.

Finally, the displayed constants in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment,obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier,obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} deliver rate-sharp upper and lower bounds. Sharp constants for particular designs, loss functions, or calibrated critical values remain an open refinement of the minimax expected-length characterization.
