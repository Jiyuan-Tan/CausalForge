\section{Related Literature and Discussion}

This paper connects three econometric literatures: complier-effect identification, weak-identification inference, and transportability.  The starting point is the LATE representation of \citet{ImbensAngrist1994} and \citet{AngristImbensRubin1996}, with antecedents in encouragement designs such as \citet{Bloom1984}.  Principal-stratification formulations give the corresponding language for compliance types and complier contrasts \citep{FrangakisRubin2002,ImbensRubin1997,ImbensRosenbaum2005}, while partial-identification analyses clarify the role of bounded outcomes and monotonicity in instrumental-variable models \citep{BalkePearl1997}.  In this paper, \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} gives the transported analogue of the complier-ratio identification step: the source inverse-propensity contrasts, after target weighting, identify a target complier-conditional contrast \(\theta_T\in\Theta=[-1,1]\).  The compact range is central for expected length because every honest set is measured on a fixed causal scale.

Weak-instrument inference supplies the ratio-inference perspective.  Anderson--Rubin and Fieller methods treat weak denominators through score inversion rather than plug-in division \citep{AndersonRubin1949,Fieller1954}; later work develops finite-sample, asymptotic, and many-design refinements for weakly identified instrumental-variable models \citep{GleserHwang1987,Dufour1997,BoundJaegerBaker1995,StaigerStock1997,StockWrightYogo2002,Moreira2003,AndrewsMoreiraStock2006,Mikusheva2010,AndrewsStockSun2019,BertanhaMoreira2020,Ma2023,SmuclerLanniMasip2025}.  The present analysis keeps the weak-denominator logic but places it inside a transported complier problem.  The relevant denominator is the transported first-stage mean \(\mu_n\), and the sampling variance is enlarged by the transport-weight second moment \(\kappa_n\).  Thus \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} index the minimax expected-length order by \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\), so weak first stages and covariate-shift dispersion enter through a single effective-strength scalar.

The transportability and generalizability literatures motivate the two-population structure.  Work on generalizing treatment effects across samples emphasizes overlap, density-ratio weighting, and target-population interpretation \citep{HotzImbensMortimer2005,ColeStuart2010,StuartColeBradshawLeaf2011,Tipton2013,Dahabreh2020,DahabrehPetitoRobertson2020,Nguyen2017}.  Graphical and selection-diagram approaches give complementary conditions for moving causal information across populations \citep{PearlBareinboim2011,BareinboimPearl2013,BareinboimPearl2016}.  Here the transported object is a complier ratio rather than an average treatment effect: \cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport} moves the conditional assignment-outcome contrast, while \cref{obj:ass:receipt-transport} moves the target-average receipt contrast.  That split matches the Wald structure in \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} and makes the target complier effect identifiable under the stated source-IV and transport restrictions.

Recent work on transported noncompliance is closest in estimand and design.  \citet{RudolphVanderLaan2017} study transportability for encouragement and adherence settings, and \citet{ChenHuang2025} develop transported CACE identification and estimation under related two-sample conditions.  Recent contributions also study transported or site-generalized causal effects with noncompliance and heterogeneity \citep{ChoiGuShen2018,ChoiShen2019,ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2024,Rudolph2025,Ross2026,Ren2025,Aronow2026}.  The present paper adds a weak-first-stage expected-length characterization to this line of work: \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} gives a fixed-geometry minimax comparison, while \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} construct feasible cell-weight score inversions attaining the same order in finite-cell experiments.

Semiparametric efficiency provides a complementary large-sample benchmark for weighting and transport estimation \citep{RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,HiranoImbensRidder2003,VanDerLaanRubin2006,Chernozhukov2018}.  Those methods clarify how nuisance estimation and covariate weighting affect first-order distributional approximations under regular identification.  The results here focus on honest confidence-set length when the transported first stage is local to zero.  The score radius in \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle} and its finite-cell analogues in \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} use the same weighting logic, but the target criterion is minimax expected length over \(t_n\ge t_0\), rather than point-estimator variance at a fixed regular law.

The comparison with linear two-sample IV is also useful.  Two-sample IV designs combine information from separate samples through shared covariates or moments, and weak-identification issues arise when the first-stage relation is small \citep{BoundJaegerBaker1995,StaigerStock1997,AndrewsStockSun2019}.  In the present transported complier setting, the two samples have distinct roles: source observations identify reduced-form and first-stage contrasts, while target covariates determine the transported averaging law.  The finite-cell constructions make that division explicit by estimating target cell masses from \((X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\) and source cell contrasts from \((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n\).

Together, the results characterize a finite honest expected-length tradeoff for transported complier effects under bounded outcomes, fixed instrument overlap, domination, envelope control, and second-moment weight dispersion.  The compact causal range in \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} fixes the maximal length scale, and the lower and upper bounds in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment,obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier,obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} show how expected length contracts at order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) once effective strength exceeds \(t_0\).
