\section{Appendix: Proofs, Auxiliary Arguments, and Verification Note}

This appendix collects the formal arguments supporting the statements used in the main text.  The organization follows the order of invocation: identification and compact range, oracle score inversion and oracle lower bounds, fixed-geometry comparisons, finite-cell weight learning, and the regular-cell extension.  Each argument works under the hypotheses displayed in the corresponding main-text statement, with notation inherited from \cref{obj:def:model-class,obj:def:frontier-risk,obj:def:admissible-geometry-class}.

The identification argument for \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} combines the source-IV restrictions with the two transport restrictions.  Conditional randomization and overlap turn the inverse-propensity score into the source assignment contrast for \(Y\) and \(D\).  Exclusion and monotonicity then express those contrasts through complier potential outcomes, while outcome-contrast transport and target-average receipt-contrast transport move the two components to the target covariate law.  The resulting transported Wald ratio is the target complier-conditional contrast \(\theta_T\), and bounded outcomes place that contrast in \(\Theta=[-1,1]\).

The oracle upper-bound argument for \cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} treats the score in \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle} as an Anderson--Rubin moment indexed by \(\vartheta\) \citep{AndersonRubin1949,Fieller1954}.  At \(\vartheta=\theta_T\), the transported mean of the score is zero.  The overlap and bounded-outcome conditions give a variance bound proportional to \(\kappa_n/n\), and the empirical dispersion \(\widehat\kappa_n\) provides the corresponding random scale.  Inverting the score over the compact set \(\Theta\) yields honesty, and the length calculation uses the same score inequality together with the denominator lower bound implied by \(t_n\ge t_0\).

The oracle converse in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse} uses a two-point weak-identification comparison.  The construction holds the sampling ratio, overlap, envelope, and second-moment restrictions fixed while varying the transported complier contrast over alternatives whose statistical distance remains bounded.  Coverage at both nearby laws forces an honest confidence set to contain separated parameter values with positive probability, giving a lower bound on limiting expected length.  This is the transported counterpart of the weak-IV length logic developed around score and ratio inference \citep{GleserHwang1987,Dufour1997,Moreira2003,AndrewsMoreiraStock2006,Mikusheva2010,ArmstrongKolesar2021}.

For the fixed-geometry results, \cref{obj:def:least-favorable-witness} and \cref{obj:def:geometry-handle} supply the local family and the distance calculation.  The complier probability \(p_n(x)\) is calibrated so that the transported first-stage mean has effective strength \(t_0\), while the tilt radius \(H(t_0)\) moves the target complier contrast by the order \(t_0^{-1/2}\).  The chi-square and total-variation bounds in \cref{obj:def:geometry-handle} convert that calibration into the lower bound in \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}.  The upper bound in the same theorem follows by applying the oracle score-inversion construction within the fixed slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\).  In the unit-weight specialization, \cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction} records \(\kappa_n=1\), \(P_T=P_S^X\), and \(t_n=n\mu_n^2\), so the transported comparison reduces to the single-population weak-first-stage scale.

The finite-cell proof for \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} separates target-weight estimation from source contrast estimation.  Uniform source cells make \(\widehat p_{x,n}\) an empirical estimate of the target cell mass, and \(\widehat m_{G,n}(x)\) estimates the corresponding source contrast.  The plug-in contrasts \(\widehat M_{Y,n}\) and \(\widehat M_{D,n}\) therefore estimate the transported reduced form and first stage directly from the two samples.  The U-statistic \(\widehat\kappa_{U,n}\) controls the target-sample contribution to weight dispersion, and the inflation through \(\widehat K_n\) supplies the variance bound used for honesty and expected-length control.

The regular-cell extension in \cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} repeats this argument with known nonuniform source-cell probabilities.  Normalization by \(q_{x,n}\) converts each cell contrast into the source-scale quantity, and the collision statistic weighted by \(q_{x,n}^{-1}\) estimates the corresponding dispersion.  The constants \(c_-\), \(c_+\), \(\varepsilon\), and \(c\) keep the variance terms uniformly controlled, yielding the same \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) expected-length order for the regular-cell feasible class in \cref{obj:def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty}.

The theorem statements and proofs in this appendix are machine-checked in Lean 4.  The cited weak-identification references provide the econometric context for the score-inversion and ratio-inference arguments used in the main text.
