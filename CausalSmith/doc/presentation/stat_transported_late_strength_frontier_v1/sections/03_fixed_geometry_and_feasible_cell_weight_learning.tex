\section{Fixed Geometry and Feasible Cell Weight Learning}

The oracle result in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} evaluates the score with the row-specific transport weight and propensity.  This section separates two additional questions.  First, by holding the transport geometry fixed, it records a least-favorable family and a fixed-geometry minimax comparison whose constants are uniform over admissible geometries.  Second, in finite-cell designs, it replaces oracle weights with target-sample cell frequencies and shows that the same expected-length order is attainable from the observed source and target samples.

The fixed-geometry analysis treats the covariate marginal, density ratio, and propensity as a deterministic triangular array.  The admissible class below packages the carrier conditions needed for the local lower-bound family and for the oracle score rule under a common geometry.

\begin{definitionv}{def:admissible-geometry-class}[Admissible geometry class \(\mathcal G\)]
\(\mathcal G\) is the class of deterministic arrays \(\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}\) satisfying the following conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Source law.)} \(P_S^X\) is a probability law on \(\mathcal X\).
\item \textbf{(Instrument overlap.)} \(e:\mathcal X\to[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\).
\item \textbf{(Transport weights.)} \(w:\mathcal X\to[0,2k_n]\), \(\mathbb E_S[w(X)]=1\), and
\[
\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]\le k_n .
\]
\item \textbf{(Target law.)} For every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
P_T(A)=\mathbb E_S\!\left[w(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in A\}\right].
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} collects the deterministic source law, overlap, transport-weight envelope, second moment, and induced target law.  The class is the fixed-carrier counterpart of the transport and rate restrictions in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:transport-domination,obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment}: it preserves the same dominated target representation while making the geometry common across the laws being compared.

Given such a geometry, the fixed-geometry slice restricts the full transported model to laws with that row's \(P_S^X\), \(w\), and \(e\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:fixed-geometry-slice}[Fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\)]
For \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\),
\[
\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)
=
\left\{
P\in\mathcal P_n:
(P_S^X,w,e)\text{ equals the index-}n\text{ component of }\mathfrak g
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} turns the oracle comparison into a conditional minimax problem indexed by \(\mathfrak g\).  All variation inside the slice comes from the causal full-data law subject to the restrictions of \(\mathcal P_n\), while the covariate-shift and assignment-propensity geometry remain fixed.

The corresponding oracle class uses the same inputs as \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}, with honesty imposed uniformly over the fixed slice.

\begin{definitionv}{def:fixed-geometry-honesty}[Fixed-geometry oracle honesty \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\)]
For \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) as in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class}, \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\) is the class of sequences \((C_n)_{n\ge1}\) such that
\[
C_n
=
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right)
\subseteq \Theta
\]
and
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}
P(\theta_T\in C_n)
\ge 1-\alpha .
\]
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-honesty} keeps the oracle information set fixed within the slice and requires asymptotic coverage for every transported law compatible with that geometry.  This is the natural benchmark for asking how much of the expected-length difficulty is generated by weak effective strength once the covariate geometry is held constant.

\begin{definitionv}{def:fixed-geometry-value}[Fixed-geometry value \(V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\)]
For \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) as in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class}, the fixed-geometry minimax expected-length value at threshold \(t_0\) is
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
=
\inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)] .
\]
\end{definitionv}

The value in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} measures the best limiting worst-case expected length over the fixed slice above the effective-strength threshold \(t_0\).  Because \(\lambda(C_n)\) is evaluated on the compact parameter space \(\Theta\), the comparison retains the same length scale as \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk}.

A useful benchmark is the single-population case in which the target and source covariate laws coincide.  With unit transport weights, effective strength reduces to the usual first-stage sample-size scaling, and the fixed-geometry value has the same rate as the transported oracle value.

\begin{theoremv}{prop:no-shift-reduction}[Single-population frontier reduction]
On a measurable covariate space \(\mathcal X\), let \(\mathfrak g\) be a deterministic geometry array satisfying \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} with scale \(k_n\) and overlap constant \(\varepsilon\). Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sampling ratio.)} \(c>0\) and \(N_n/n \to c\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap and noncoverage.)} \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).
\item \textbf{(Cell scale.)} \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\), \(k_n\to\infty\), and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to 0\).
\item \textbf{(Unit transport weight.)} For every \(n\), \(w_n(X)=1\) \(P_{S,n}^X\)-almost surely under \(\mathfrak g\).
\end{itemize}
Then, for every \(n\),
\[
\kappa_n=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
P_{T,n}=P_{S,n}^X .
\]
Moreover, for every transported array \(P\) and every \(n\), if \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}, then its effective strength satisfies
\[
t_n
=
n\mu_n^2 .
\]
Finally, for every \(t_0>0\), let
\[
L_\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}},
\qquad
c_\alpha=\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16},
\qquad
C_\alpha=\max\left\{2,\;4L_\alpha+\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2}\right\}.
\]
The fixed-geometry minimax value \(V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\) from \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} obeys
\[
c_\alpha \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
\le
C_\alpha \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction} identifies the no-shift specialization of the transported problem.  When \(w=1\), the Kish dispersion is \(\kappa_n=1\), the target law equals the source covariate marginal, and the index \(t_n\) becomes \(n\mu_n^2\).  The same \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) expected-length rate then describes the fixed-geometry value, linking the transported formulation to the familiar weak-first-stage scaling in a single covariate population.

The fixed-geometry lower bound is generated by a local family that tilts the complier outcome mean while preserving the geometry.  The construction uses the same conditional full-data law in both populations, so the variation in the transported ratio is carried by the complier outcome contrast.

\begin{definitionv}{def:least-favorable-witness}[Least-favorable witness \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\)]
Fix \(\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}\in\mathcal G\) and \(t_0>0\). Set
\[
\mu_n=\left(\frac{t_0\kappa_n}{n}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
p_n(x)=\frac{\mu_n w(x)}{\kappa_n},
\qquad
\rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{8},
\qquad
H(t_0)=\min\{1/4,\rho t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
For each \(|h|\le H(t_0)\), conditional on \(X=x\), assign compliance types by
\[
\Pr\{(D(0),D(1))=(0,1)\mid X=x\}=p_n(x),
\]
\[
\Pr\{(D(0),D(1))=(1,1)\mid X=x\}
=
\Pr\{(D(0),D(1))=(0,0)\mid X=x\}
=
\frac{1-p_n(x)}{2}.
\]
Set \(Y(0)=0\). For compliers, let \(Y(1)\) be Bernoulli with mean \(1/2+h\); for always-takers and never-takers, let \(Y(1)\) be Bernoulli with mean \(1/2\). Use this same conditional full-data law in both populations, draw source encouragement with propensity \(e(X)\), and write \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) for the resulting joint observed-data law together with the fixed oracle inputs.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:least-favorable-witness} calibrates the conditional complier probability to make the transported first stage equal to \(\mu_n=(t_0\kappa_n/n)^{1/2}\).  The local tilt \(h\) moves the target complier contrast within \(\Theta\), while the bound \(H(t_0)\) keeps the Bernoulli means inside the bounded-outcome support.  This family is the fixed-geometry analogue of the local alternatives used in weak-identification lower bounds.

The next definition records the separation calculation for the same family.  It connects the local tilt scale to the total variation distance used in the change-of-measure comparison.

\begin{definitionv}{def:geometry-handle}[Geometry handle \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\)]
Fix an admissible deterministic array \(\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}\). For \(t_0>0\), set
\[
\mu_n=\left(\frac{t_0\kappa_n}{n}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
p_n(x)=\frac{\mu_n w(x)}{\kappa_n}.
\]
For \(0<\alpha<1\), let \(\rho=(1-\alpha)/8\). As the complier outcome mean varies over \(h\in\mathbb R\) with
\[
|h|\le \min\{1/4,\rho t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
using the chi-square divergence and total variation distance defined in \cref{obj:synth_3,obj:synth_4}, the corresponding least-favorable laws satisfy
\[
1+\chi^2(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\Vert Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g})
\le \exp(8t_0h^2),
\qquad
\|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le 2\rho .
\]
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:geometry-handle} shows that the local experiments remain statistically close when \(h\) is of order \(t_0^{-1/2}\).  The chi-square bound supplies the quantitative separation control, and the total variation bound translates that control into the coverage-to-length tradeoff underlying the fixed-geometry lower bound.

The fixed-geometry theorem combines this least-favorable comparison with the oracle score-inversion upper bound.  Its constants are independent of the particular admissible geometry.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}[Fixed-geometry frontier]
Let \(c>0\), \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), and \(0<\alpha<1\). Assume the following conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Target-sample ratio.)} The target sample sizes satisfy \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Envelope scale.)} The sequence \(k_n\) is positive for every \(n\), satisfies \(k_n\to\infty\), and obeys \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\).
\item \textbf{(Geometry.)} The deterministic geometry array \(\mathfrak g\) satisfies \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} with scale \(k_n\) and overlap constant \(\varepsilon\).
\item \textbf{(Fixed-geometry slices.)} For every \(n\) and every transported array whose \(n\)th law lies in the fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}, the conditions in
\cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array,obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment,obj:ass:degrading-array}
hold.
\end{itemize}

For every \(t_0>0\), define
\[
L_\alpha=\left(\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
c_\alpha=\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16},
\qquad
C_\alpha=\max\left\{2,\,4L_\alpha+\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2}\right\}.
\]
Then the fixed-geometry minimax value of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} satisfies
\[
c_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)
\le
C_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
The constants \(c_\alpha\) and \(C_\alpha\) are uniform over admissible deterministic geometries \(\mathfrak g\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} establishes the fixed-geometry minimax expected-length order.  The lower constant comes from the least-favorable family in \cref{obj:def:least-favorable-witness,obj:def:geometry-handle}, while the upper constant follows from the score-inversion construction evaluated with the fixed oracle inputs.  Uniformity over \(\mathfrak g\) means that the same rate expression covers covariate-shift designs with different admissible weight profiles, provided the overlap, envelope, second-moment, and sub-root scale conditions hold.

The remaining results replace oracle weights by quantities estimated from the target covariate sample.  In the uniform finite-cell class \(\mathcal N_n\), the source cell mass is \(1/k_n\) and the propensity is \(1/2\), so the target cell frequencies supply a direct empirical estimate of the transport weights.  This yields a sample-only analogue of the oracle score inversion.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}[Finite-cell sample-only score-inversion attainment]
Let \(N_n\) and \(k_n\) be positive integer sequences, let \(c,\varepsilon,\alpha\in\mathbb R\), and suppose the following conditions hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sampling ratio.)} \(c>0\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap and coverage levels.)} \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).
\item \textbf{(Cell growth.)} \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\), \(k_n\to\infty\), and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\).
\item \textbf{(Ambient carrier.)} For every \(n\), the measurable covariate space contains \(k_n\) distinct measurable singleton cells.
\item \textbf{(Finite-cell rows.)} For every row \(n\) and every transported array \(P\in\mathcal N_n\), the row satisfies the two-sample condition in \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}, the uniform finite-cell source condition in \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}, and the degrading-array condition in \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array}, with \(\mathcal N_n\) as in \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}.
\end{itemize}
Put
\[
B_c=32(1+c^{-1}),\qquad
L_{\alpha,c}=\left(\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2},\qquad
C_0=\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B_c\}.
\]
Then \(C_0>0\), and there exists a sample-only finite-cell confidence-set sequence \(C=(C_n)\) in the feasible class \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-feasible-honesty} such that, for every \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal N_n\), \(C_n\) agrees \(P\)-almost surely with the finite-cell score-inversion rule using critical constant \(L_{\alpha,c}\). Equivalently, on the finite-cell experiment, this rule is given by
\[
H_i=2(2Z_i-1),\qquad
\widehat p_{x,n}=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{j=1}^{N_n}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=x\},
\]
and, for \(G\in\{Y,D\}\),
\[
\widehat m_{G,n}(x)=\frac{k_n}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i,\qquad
\widehat M_{G,n}=\sum_{x=1}^{k_n}\widehat p_{x,n}\widehat m_{G,n}(x).
\]
For \(N_n\ge2\), define
\[
\widehat\kappa_{U,n}
=\frac{k_n}{N_n(N_n-1)}
\sum_{j\ne\ell}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\},\qquad
\widehat K_n=1+\widehat\kappa_{U,n},
\]
and set
\[
C_n=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|
\le L_{\alpha,c}\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\right\},
\]
with \(C_n=\Theta\) at indices for which \(N_n<2\). For every \(t_0>0\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Moreover, for every \(t_0>0\), the feasible finite-cell honest minimax risk satisfies
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
\inf_{\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)].
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} constructs a feasible confidence set whose inputs are exactly the source observations and target covariates.  The statistic \(\widehat p_{x,n}\) estimates the target mass of cell \(x\), while \(\widehat m_{G,n}(x)\) estimates the source contrast for \(G\in\{Y,D\}\) in that cell.  Their product aggregates to the transported plug-in contrast \(\widehat M_{G,n}\).  The U-statistic \(\widehat\kappa_{U,n}\) estimates the weight-dispersion contribution from repeated target cells, and the rule inflates the score radius through \(\widehat K_n\).  The theorem therefore gives both honesty and the attainable \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) expected-length bound for a sample-only finite-cell procedure, together with the matching lower order over \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\).

The uniform finite-cell design is a transparent weight-learning benchmark.  Many transported samples have source cell probabilities that are known from design or from a fixed sampling frame but are unequal across cells.  The regular-cell extension covers that case by allowing source masses to vary within fixed constants around \(1/k_n\), while retaining known cell probabilities and known propensity scores as procedure inputs.

\begin{definitionv}{def:regular-finite-cell-class}[Regular finite-cell class $\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}$]
For fixed constants \(0<c_-\le 1\le c_+<\infty\), define
\[
\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}
=
\left\{
P\in\mathcal P_n:
\begin{array}{l}
P_S^X \text{ is supported on exactly } k_n \text{ measurable cells},\\
q_{x,n}=P_S^X(X \text{ lies in cell } x),\\
c_-/k_n\le q_{x,n}\le c_+/k_n \text{ for every } x
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class} preserves the finite-cell structure but replaces exact uniformity with regular cell masses.  The constants \(c_-\) and \(c_+\) keep the source sampling variance comparable across cells, so the same target-frequency idea can be combined with known source-cell probabilities.

The feasible class for this extension records the additional design inputs explicitly.

\begin{definitionv}{def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty}[Regular-cell feasible class $\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}$]
For a noncoverage level \(0<\alpha<1\), define \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) as the class of regular-cell procedures \((C_n)_{n\ge 1}\) with the following properties. For each \(n\), \(C_n\) is a \(\Theta\)-valued random set whose inputs are \((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n\), \((X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\), the realized regular-cell support, and the known cell-indexed vectors \((q_{x,n})_{x=1}^{k_n}\) and \((e_{x,n})_{x=1}^{k_n}\). For every realized regular-cell support and every pair of real \(k_n\)-vectors \((q_{x,n})_{x=1}^{k_n}\) and \((e_{x,n})_{x=1}^{k_n}\), the sample-by-\(\theta\) graph of \(C_n\), restricted to source and target samples on that support, is measurable. These procedures satisfy
\[
1-\alpha\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}}
P(\theta_T\in C_n),
\]
with the rowwise infimum evaluated as \(1\) when \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) is empty.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty} is the regular-cell analogue of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-feasible-honesty}.  It makes the known source-cell probabilities and known propensity vector part of the admissible procedure input, while maintaining the same asymptotic honesty requirement over the regular finite-cell class.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}[Regular-cell weight attainment]
Fix \(c,\varepsilon,\alpha,c_-,c_+\in\mathbb R\) and sequences \(N_n\) and \(k_n\).  Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} \(0<c\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap and coverage levels.)} \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).
\item \textbf{(Regular cell constants.)} \(0<c_-\le 1\) and \(1\le c_+\).
\item \textbf{(Cell growth.)} \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\), \(k_n\to\infty\), and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\).
\item \textbf{(Measurable carrier.)} For every \(n\), the covariate carrier contains \(k_n\) injected measurable singleton cells.
\item \textbf{(Model restrictions.)} Every array \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) from \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class} with constants \(c,\varepsilon,c_-,c_+\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}, \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}, and \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\end{itemize}
On \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), let \(q_{x,n}=P_S^X(X=x)\) and define
\[
H_i=\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)}.
\]
For \(G\in\{Y,D\}\), set
\[
\widehat p_{x,n}=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{j=1}^{N_n}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=x\},
\qquad
\widehat m_{G,n}(x)=\frac1{nq_{x,n}}\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i,
\]
and
\[
\widehat M_{G,n}=\sum_{x=1}^{k_n}\widehat p_{x,n}\widehat m_{G,n}(x).
\]
For \(N_n\ge2\), set
\[
\widehat\kappa_{U,n}
=
\frac1{N_n(N_n-1)}\sum_{j\ne\ell}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}}{q_{X_j^T,n}},
\qquad
\widehat K_n=1+\widehat\kappa_{U,n},
\]
and put \(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}=\Theta\) when \(N_n<2\).  Let
\[
B_{\varepsilon,c}=8(\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1}).
\]
For every fixed \(L\ge\{2B_{\varepsilon,c}/\alpha\}^{1/2}\), the sequence
\[
C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}
=
\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|
\le L\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\right\}
\]
for \(N_n\ge2\), together with the preceding convention for \(N_n<2\), belongs to \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) from \cref{obj:def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty} and is constructed from the known regular source-cell probabilities, the known propensity, and the source and target samples.  Moreover, for every fixed \(t_0>0\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})]
\le
\max\{2,4\sqrt2L+8B_{\varepsilon,c}\}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
The corresponding minimax risk over \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) is bounded below by
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Thus, with known regular source-cell probabilities and a known cell-varying propensity satisfying the stated overlap, the feasible regular-cell construction attains the oracle frontier order on \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} extends the sample-only construction to regular unequal cell masses.  The source contrast estimator now normalizes by \(q_{x,n}\), and the target-sample U-statistic weights cell coincidences by the corresponding source-cell probability.  Under the stated regularity constants, overlap, sampling ratio, and sub-root cell growth, the resulting score-inversion set belongs to \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) and has expected length bounded by a constant multiple of \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\).  Together with \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}, this shows how target-sample weight learning preserves the transported weak-first-stage rate in both uniform and regular finite-cell experiments.
