\section{Oracle Frontier and Score Inversion}

The transported Wald estimand in \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} reduces honest inference to a ratio problem with a denominator that may be small.  The weak-IV literature treats this feature through score or moment inequalities rather than direct division, beginning with Anderson--Rubin and Fieller-type constructions and extending through modern weak-identification methods \citep{AndersonRubin1949,Fieller1954,StaigerStock1997,StockWrightYogo2002,Moreira2003,AndrewsMoreiraStock2006,Mikusheva2010,AndrewsStockSun2019}.  In the transported setting, the relevant score is the source inverse-propensity contrast weighted by the target-to-source density ratio.  The resulting radius is scaled by the empirical second moment of the oracle weights, so first-stage weakness and covariate-shift dispersion enter through the same effective-strength index \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:inversion-handle}[Score-inversion set $C_n$]
For each \(\vartheta\in\Theta\), define the source score
\[
w(X)\left\{\frac{Z}{e(X)}-\frac{1-Z}{1-e(X)}\right\}(Y-\vartheta D).
\]
Define the empirical second moment of the oracle weights by
\[
\widehat\kappa_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n w(X_i)^2.
\]
With oracle-known \(w\) and \(e\), the score-inversion confidence set is
\[
C_n
=
\left\{
\vartheta\in\Theta:
\left|
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
w(X_i)\left\{\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)}\right\}(Y_i-\vartheta D_i)
\right|
\le
L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\widehat\kappa_n}{n}}
\right\}.
\]
For oracle-known \(w\) and \(e\), this score is a source-sample statistic.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:inversion-handle} is the transported analogue of an Anderson--Rubin inversion.  For a candidate value \(\vartheta\), the centered moment \(Y-\vartheta D\) has zero transported mean at the target complier contrast, while the inverse-propensity score converts source assignment variation into the reduced-form and first-stage contrasts from \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range}.  The scale \(L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\) reflects bounded outcomes, fixed instrument overlap, and the Kish dispersion of the oracle transport weights.

The minimax lower bound shows that this scaling is intrinsic to honest expected length over the transported model.  Its statement applies to the full transported class and to the uniform finite-cell submodel, so the benchmark covers both the oracle setting and the finite-cell environment used later for feasible weight learning.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:oracle-converse}[Oracle frontier converse]
Fix \(c>0\), \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\), and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  There exist constants \(c_0>0\) and \(t_c>0\), depending on \(c\), \(\varepsilon\), and \(\alpha\), such that the following holds.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Array size.)} The target sample sizes satisfy \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Cell scale.)} The sequence \(k_n\) is positive, satisfies \(k_n\to\infty\), and satisfies \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\).
\item \textbf{(Measurable finite carriers.)} For each \(n\), the covariate space contains \(k_n\) distinct measurable singleton carrier cells.
\item \textbf{(Transported-IV class.)} Every array in the transported model class \(\mathcal P_n\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class} satisfies \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support,obj:ass:population-presence,obj:ass:two-sample-array,obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:source-observation,obj:ass:iv-randomization,obj:ass:iv-exclusion,obj:ass:iv-monotonicity,obj:ass:outcome-transport,obj:ass:receipt-transport,obj:ass:target-complier-positivity,obj:ass:transport-domination,obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment,obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\item \textbf{(Finite-cell submodel.)} Every array in the finite-cell class \(\mathcal N_n\) of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class} satisfies \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}.
\end{itemize}

Then, for every \(t_0>0\), the oracle values of \cref{obj:def:oracle-value,obj:def:finite-cell-oracle-value} obey
\[
c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\le V^\star(t_0)
\]
and
\[
c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\le V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0).
\]
Moreover, for every \(t_0\in(0,t_c]\),
\[
c_0\le V^\star(t_0).
\]
The same constants \(c_0\) and \(t_c\) apply uniformly over all decompositions of the effective strength \(t_0\) into the transported first-stage mean \(\mu_n\) and the weight dispersion \(\kappa_n\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse} establishes the lower half of the oracle frontier.  Above a fixed threshold \(t_0\), every honest oracle procedure has limiting worst-case expected length at least a constant multiple of \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\).  The small-strength clause gives the corresponding constant lower bound for \(t_0\le \leanref{sym:t_c}{$t_c$}\), with the positive constant \(\leanref{sym:c_0}{$c_0$}\) depending on the sampling ratio, overlap, and noncoverage level.  The final uniformity clause is the key transported feature: the lower bound is indexed by \(t_0\) itself, whether weak effective strength arises through a small transported first stage, high weight dispersion, or both.

The attainable procedure uses the score in \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle} with the row-specific oracle inputs.  The admissible-geometry condition supplies a common ambient carrier for evaluating the density ratio and propensity, while the score statistic is computed under each law using that law's oracle \(w\) and \(e\).

\begin{theoremv}{thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment}[Oracle score attainment]
Fix sequences \(N_n\) and \(k_n\), constants \(c,\varepsilon,\alpha\), the transported-IV model class \(\mathcal P_n\) in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, and the score-inversion construction in \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle}.  Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sampling ratio.)} \(c>0\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap and noncoverage.)} \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).
\item \textbf{(Envelope scale.)} \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\), \(k_n\to\infty\), and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\).
\item \textbf{(Nonempty admissible carrier.)} There is an admissible deterministic geometry \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) satisfying \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} at scale \(k_n\) and overlap constant \(\varepsilon\).
\item \textbf{(Uniform model restrictions.)} Every \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) satisfies the two-sample condition in \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}, the overlap condition in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}, the weight envelope in \cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope}, the weight second-moment bound in \cref{obj:ass:weight-second-moment}, and the degrading-array condition in \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\end{itemize}
Let
\[
L_\alpha=\left(\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
For each \(n\), define
\[
H_i=\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)},\qquad
\widehat A_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n w(X_i)H_iY_i,\qquad
\widehat B_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n w(X_i)H_iD_i,
\]
and
\[
\widehat\kappa_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n w(X_i)^2,\qquad
C_n=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
\left|\widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n\right|
\le L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\widehat\kappa_n}{n}}\right\}.
\]
Then the score-inversion sequence \((C_n)\) belongs to the oracle honest class \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\) of \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}.  Moreover, with
\[
C_0=\max\left\{2,\ 4L_\alpha+\frac8{\varepsilon^2}\right\},
\]
for every \(t_0>0\), the frontier risk from \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk} satisfies
\[
R((C_n),t_0)\le C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Consequently the oracle value in \cref{obj:def:oracle-value} satisfies the same attainable frontier order over fixed positive effective-strength thresholds.
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} gives an honest oracle confidence-set sequence whose expected length matches the lower bound in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse} up to constants.  The statistic \(\widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n\) is the transported reduced-form score minus \(\vartheta\) times the transported first-stage score, and inversion over \(\Theta\) retains the compact causal range from \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range}.  The constant \(\leanref{sym:C_0}{$C_0$}\) depends on the noncoverage calibration and overlap constant, while the dependence on transported identification strength is summarized by \(t_0\).

Combining \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment}, the oracle minimax expected length over the transported model has order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\).  This statement aligns the transported complier-ratio problem with weak-IV robust score inversion, while making the transport contribution explicit: \(\kappa_n\) converts covariate-shift dispersion into the same effective-strength scale as the first-stage mean.  The next section fixes and then learns the geometry, showing how the oracle rate is carried into finite-cell procedures that use the observed source and target samples.
