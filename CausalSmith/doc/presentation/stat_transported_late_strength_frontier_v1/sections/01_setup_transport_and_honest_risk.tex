\section{Setup, Transport, and Honest Risk}

The analysis begins with a \leanref{S-1}{two-population full-data potential-outcome world} indexed by \leanref{sym:n}{$n$}.  The full-data law \leanref{sym:P_n^F}{$P_n^F$} is defined on the population indicator \leanref{sym:S}{$S$}, covariates \leanref{sym:X}{$X$}, treatment-receipt potential outcomes \(D(0),D(1)\), and treatment potential outcomes \(Y(0),Y(1)\).  The source population, \(S=1\), supplies randomized encouragement data, while the target population, \(S=0\), supplies the covariate distribution to which the complier contrast is transported.  The measurable covariate space \leanref{sym:\mathcal X}{$\mathcal X$} is otherwise unrestricted in the main model.

We impose the support and population-presence conditions first because all later transport and ratio objects are conditional on these two population states.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:full-data-support}[Full-data support]
The full-data law satisfies
\[
P_n^F\{S\in\{0,1\},\ D(0),D(1)\in\{0,1\},\ Y(0),Y(1)\in[0,1]\}=1.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:full-data-support} is specific to this analysis.  It fixes the binary receipt and bounded-outcome scale that makes the transported complier contrast live in a compact parameter space and gives expected length a uniform unit of measurement.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:population-presence}[Population presence]
For each \(s\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
P_n^F(S=s)>0.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:population-presence} is the standard two-population support condition: both source and target conditional laws are well defined, as in transported noncompliance designs \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

The observed sampling experiment is a \leanref{S-2}{two-sample triangular-array sampling world}.  We observe i.i.d. source records \leanref{sym:O_i^S}{$O_i^S$} with coordinates \leanref{sym:Z}{$Z$}, \leanref{sym:D}{$D$}, and \leanref{sym:Y}{$Y$}, and an independent target covariate sample \leanref{sym:X_j^T}{$X_j^T$}.  The source observed law is \leanref{sym:P_S}{$P_S$}, its covariate marginal is \leanref{sym:P_S^X}{$P_S^X$}, and the target covariate law is \leanref{sym:P_T}{$P_T$}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:two-sample-array}[Two-sample array]
For a transported triangular array on the measurable covariate space \(\mathcal X\), let \(P_S\) denote the index-\(n\) observed source law induced on \(O_i^S=(X_i,Z_i,D_i,Y_i)\), and let \(P_T\) denote the index-\(n\) target covariate law on \(X_j^T\). The two sample factors satisfy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Source factor.)} For every \(n\), \(P_S\) is a probability measure.
\item \textbf{(Target factor.)} For every \(n\), \(P_T\) is a probability measure.
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} \(0<c\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\end{itemize}
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array} is the standard independent two-sample triangular-array sampling condition, with target size \leanref{sym:N_n}{$N_n$} comparable to source size through \leanref{sym:c}{$c$} \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  This sampling scheme separates estimation of source contrasts from empirical representation of the target covariate distribution.

The causal-IV block combines overlap, source consistency, random assignment conditional on covariates, exclusion, and monotonicity.  These restrictions are the source-side ingredients that identify conditional reduced-form and first-stage contrasts.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:instrument-overlap}[Instrument overlap]
The instrument-overlap constant satisfies \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), and the source propensity score satisfies
\[
\varepsilon\le e(X)\le 1-\varepsilon
\]
\(P_S^X\)-almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap} is the standard instrument positivity condition, stated with the overlap constant \leanref{sym:\varepsilon}{$\varepsilon$} and source propensity \leanref{sym:e}{$e(X)$} \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  It keeps the inverse-propensity contrast stable over the source covariate support.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:source-observation}[Source observation law]
Conditional on \(S=1\), the observed source law satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Assignment propensity.)} \(P(Z=1\mid X,S=1)=e(X)\).
\item \textbf{(Treatment receipt.)} \(D=D(Z)\).
\item \textbf{(Observed outcome.)} \(Y=Y(D)\).
\end{itemize}
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:source-observation} is the standard source assignment and consistency condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  It links the observed receipt to the assignment-indexed receipt potential outcome \leanref{sym:D(z)}{$D(z)$} and the observed outcome to the treatment potential outcome \leanref{sym:Y(d)}{$Y(d)$}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iv-randomization}[Instrument randomization]
The instrument satisfies
\[
Z\perp (Y(0),Y(1),D(0),D(1))\mid (X,S=1).
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:iv-randomization} is the standard conditional instrument exchangeability condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  Together with overlap, it makes the source assignment contrasts interpretable as covariate-specific causal reduced forms.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iv-exclusion}[Instrument exclusion]
For every \(z,s\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
Y^Z(z)=Y(D(z))
\]
\(P_n^F\)-almost surely conditional on \(S=s\).
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:iv-exclusion} is the standard instrument exclusion restriction \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  The assignment-potential outcome \leanref{sym:Y^Z(z)}{$Y^Z(z)$} therefore changes with encouragement through received treatment.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iv-monotonicity}[Instrument monotonicity]
For every \(s\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
D(1)\ge D(0)
\]
\(P_n^F\)-almost surely conditional on \(S=s\).
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:iv-monotonicity} is the standard no-defiers monotonicity condition for LATE analysis \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  It makes the first-stage contrast a complier-share object rather than a signed net movement.

Transport enters through the density-ratio geometry and two contrast restrictions.  In the \leanref{S-3}{transport and overlap geometry world}, the weight \leanref{sym:w(dP_T/dP_S^X; carrier 𝒳→[0,∞))}{$w(X)$} transports source covariate averages to target covariate averages whenever the target law is dominated by the source covariate marginal.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:outcome-transport}[Outcome-contrast transport]
For \(P_T\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=0\right]
=
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=1\right].
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport} is the standard conditional transport of the assignment-outcome contrast \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  It moves the reduced-form outcome contrast across populations at fixed covariates.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:receipt-transport}[Receipt-contrast transport]
The target-population average of the conditional treatment-receipt contrast agrees across the two population states:
\[
\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}\!\left[
  \mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[D(1)-D(0)\mid X,S=0\right]
\right]
=
\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}\!\left[
  \mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[D(1)-D(0)\mid X,S=1\right]
\right].
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:receipt-transport} is the standard integrated transport of the first stage \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  The condition targets the average receipt contrast needed for the transported Wald denominator.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:target-complier-positivity}[Target complier positivity]
The target population has positive complier mass:
\[
P_n^F\!\left(D(1)>D(0)\mid S=0\right)>0.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:target-complier-positivity} is the standard positive target complier share condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  It ensures that the target complier-conditional contrast is a well-defined causal estimand.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:transport-domination}[Transport domination]
The target covariate law is absolutely continuous with respect to the source covariate marginal on \(\mathcal X\):
\[
P_T\ll P_S^X.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:transport-domination} is the standard transport covariate-support inclusion condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  It permits the density-ratio representation of target covariate averages through source covariate averages.

The next definition collects the identification functionals built from this geometry: the source conditional contrasts, their transported means, and the effective-strength index.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_6}[Transported contrasts and effective strength]
Let \(w(X)=dP_T/dP_S^X(X)\) be a nonnegative version of the transport density ratio.  The source conditional assignment-outcome and receipt contrasts are
\[
\Delta_Y(x)=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,\ S=1\right],
\qquad
\Delta_D(x)=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[D(1)-D(0)\mid X=x,\ S=1\right].
\]
Their transported means and the transport-weight dispersion are
\[
\mu_{Y,n}=\mathbb E_{P_S^X}\!\left[w(X)\,\Delta_Y(X)\right],
\qquad
\mu_n=\mathbb E_{P_S^X}\!\left[w(X)\,\Delta_D(X)\right],
\qquad
\kappa_n=\mathbb E_{P_S^X}\!\left[w(X)^2\right],
\]
and the effective identification strength at index \(n\) is \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\).
\end{definitionv}

The rate block records how transported overlap and weak first stages are allowed to evolve.  The same sequence \leanref{sym:k_n}{$k_n$} controls the weight envelope and, in the finite-cell submodel, the number of cells.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:weight-envelope}[Weight envelope]
The transport weight satisfies
\[
0 \le w(X) \le 2k_n
\]
\(P_S\)-almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope} is specific to this analysis.  It gives a row-wise envelope for transported importance weights while still allowing the covariate shift to degrade with \(n\).

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:weight-second-moment}[Weight second moment]
The second moment of the transport weight satisfies
\[
\kappa_n \le k_n .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:weight-second-moment} is specific to this analysis.  The quantity \leanref{sym:\kappa_n}{$\kappa_n$} is the weight-dispersion scale that enters the effective sample size for transported first-stage information.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:degrading-array}[Degrading array rates]
The array satisfies
\[
k_n \to \infty,
\qquad
k_n=o(n^{1/2}),
\qquad
\mu_n\to 0 .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:degrading-array} is specific to this analysis.  It places the paper in the weak-first-stage regime through \leanref{sym:\mu_n}{$\mu_n$} while keeping the transport envelope below the square-root sample scale.

The finite-cell specialization adds a uniform source-cell structure to the preceding model.  It is useful because the target weights can then be learned from the target covariate sample without oracle knowledge of the full density ratio.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:finite-cell-source}[Uniform finite cells]
The source covariate marginal \(P_S^X\) is supported on exactly \(k_n\) measurable cells, assigns mass \(1/k_n\) to each cell, and satisfies
\[
e(X)=1/2
\]
\(P_S\)-almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source} is specific to this analysis.  It defines the uniform finite-cell source design used for the feasible cell-weight construction.

We now collect these restrictions into the model classes used by the minimax analysis.  The full class is \leanref{sym:\mathcal P_n}{$\mathcal P_n$}; the finite-cell class is \leanref{sym:\mathcal N_n}{$\mathcal N_n$}.

\mathcal P_n

\bigl\{(P_n^F,e): (P_n^F,e)\text{ satisfies the following conditions at index }n\bigr\}.
\]
The required conditions are:
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{(Instrument randomization.)} \cref{obj:ass:iv-randomization}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument exclusion.)} \cref{obj:ass:iv-exclusion}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument monotonicity.)} \cref{obj:ass:iv-monotonicity}.
\item \textbf{(Outcome transport.)} \cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport}.
\item \textbf{(Receipt transport.)} \cref{obj:ass:receipt-transport}.
\item \textbf{(Target complier positivity.)} \cref{obj:ass:target-complier-positivity}.

\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

Thus \(\mathcal P_n\) is the transported LATE model at row \(n\), with bounded outcomes, source-IV validity, target transport, domination, and rate geometry imposed simultaneously.

The finite-cell submodel at index \(n\) is

\bigl\{P\in\mathcal P_n: P\text{ satisfies the following conditions at index }n\bigr\}.

The required conditions are:

\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

The finite-cell class therefore preserves the causal and transport restrictions of \(\mathcal P_n\) and adds the uniform cell design in \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}.

For later score statistics, define the inverse-propensity instrument score.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_1}[Inverse-propensity instrument score $H(O)$]
For a source observation $O=(X,Z,D,Y)$ and source propensity $e$, the inverse-propensity instrument score is the function $H$ given by \[H(O)=\frac{Z}{e(X)}-\frac{1-Z}{1-e(X)}.\]
\end{definitionv}

Under \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}, \(H(O)\) is uniformly controlled by the overlap constant and converts observed source outcomes and receipts into covariate-specific contrasts.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_2}[Score-inversion critical radius $L_\alpha$]
We define the score-inversion critical radius by \(L_\alpha=\{8/(\alpha\varepsilon^2)\}^{1/2}\), where \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) is the noncoverage level and \(\varepsilon\) is the source instrument-overlap constant.
\end{definitionv}

The radius \(L_\alpha\) is calibrated to the bounded inverse-propensity score and will be used in the oracle score-inversion rule.

The causal parameter and the effective-strength index are collected in the \leanref{S-4}{identification and effective-strength scalar world}: the source conditional contrasts \leanref{sym:\Delta_Y}{$\Delta_Y(x)$} and \leanref{sym:\Delta_D}{$\Delta_D(x)$}, their transported means \leanref{sym:\mu_{Y,n}}{$\mu_{Y,n}$} and \(\mu_n\), and the effective strength \leanref{sym:t_n}{$t_n$} are as defined above.  The target complier estimand \leanref{sym:\theta_T}{$\theta_T$} is defined next, and the parameter space is \leanref{sym:\Theta}{$\Theta$}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_7}[Target complier effect $\theta_T$]
The target complier effect is the ratio
\[
\theta_T
=
\frac{\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\,\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}\mid S=0\right]}
     {P_n^F\!\left\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\right\}},
\]
the mean effect of treatment receipt among target-population compliers.  Under target complier positivity the denominator is positive and \(\theta_T=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0,\ S=0\right]\); when the denominator is zero, the ratio is taken to be zero.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{theoremv}{prop:compact-causal-range}[Compact causal range]
Fix \(n\), and let \(P\) be a transported array with sample-size and envelope schedules \(N_n\) and \(k_n\), constants \(c\) and \(\varepsilon\), and model class \(\mathcal P_n\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-class}. Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Model membership.)} \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) at the index \(n\).
\item \textbf{(Source contrasts.)} The assignment outcome contrast and the receipt contrast for the source population satisfy the usual measurability and integrability conditions under the source covariate marginal \(P_S^X\).
\end{itemize}
Then \(\Delta_Y\) and \(\Delta_D\) represent the observed source outcome and receipt contrasts: for every measurable covariate set \(A\),
\[
\int_{\{O:X\in A\}} H(O)Y\,dP_S(O)
=
\int_A \Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x),
\qquad
\int_{\{O:X\in A\}} H(O)D\,dP_S(O)
=
\int_A \Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
Moreover, the transported reduced-form outcome mean satisfies
\[
\mu_{Y,n}
=
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[
\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}\mid S=0
\right],
\]
the transported first-stage mean satisfies
\[
\mu_n
=
P_n^F\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\},
\]
and the Wald ratio equals the target complier-conditional contrast,
\[
\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}=\theta_T.
\]
Finally, \(\theta_T\in\Theta=[-1,1]\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} gives the identification statement used throughout the paper: the transported Wald ratio equals the target complier-conditional contrast, and bounded outcomes place that contrast in the compact interval \([-1,1]\).  This compact range is the length scale for the confidence sets studied below.

A confidence-set sequence lives in the \leanref{S-5}{procedure and minimax-risk world}.  The oracle class allows the procedure to evaluate the data together with the row-specific density ratio and propensity, while the finite-cell feasible class uses only the observed source and target samples.

\begin{definitionv}{def:oracle-honesty}[Oracle honest class $\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}$]
The oracle honest class is the class of oracle procedures \((C_n)_{n\ge1}\) satisfying \(0<\alpha<1\) and the following conditions.  For every \(n\), \(C_n\) maps each input
\[
\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right),
\qquad
w:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R,\quad w(x)\ge0,\quad e:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R,
\]
to a subset of \(\Theta=[-1,1]\).  For every \(n\) and every measurable such pair \((w,e)\), the sample-by-parameter graph
\[
\left\{
\left(((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}),\vartheta\right):
\vartheta\in
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right)
\right\}
\]
is measurable.  For every \(n\), every \(P\in\mathcal P_n\), and every pair \(w,w'\) of measurable nonnegative versions of \(dP_T/dP_S^X\), the evaluations using the row-\(n\) propensity score agree for the two-sample law almost surely:
\[
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right)
=
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w',e\right).
\]
Finally,
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
P\!\left(\theta_T\in C_n\right), & \mathcal P_n\neq\varnothing,\\[0.8em]
1, & \mathcal P_n=\varnothing
\end{cases}
\ge 1-\alpha,
\]
where in the probability term \(C_n\) is evaluated at the canonical row-\(n\) density ratio \(dP_T/dP_S^X\) and the row-\(n\) propensity score.
\end{definitionv}

The class \leanref{sym:\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}{$\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}$} encodes asymptotic honesty uniformly over \(\mathcal P_n\).  The measurability and version-invariance clauses make the random set \leanref{sym:C_n}{$C_n$} a well-defined function of the observed samples and oracle inputs.

\begin{definitionv}{def:frontier-risk}[Frontier risk $R((C_n),t_0)$]
For a confidence-set sequence \((C_n)_{n\ge1}\) and threshold \(t_0\), the limiting worst-case expected length above effective strength \(t_0\) is
\[
R((C_n),t_0)
=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
\end{definitionv}

The risk \leanref{sym:R}{$R$} evaluates expected \leanref{sym:\lambda}{$\lambda$}-length on the compact space \(\Theta\), uniformly over laws whose effective strength exceeds the fixed threshold \leanref{sym:t_0}{$t_0$}.  The noncoverage level \leanref{sym:\alpha}{$\alpha$} remains fixed throughout the comparison.

\begin{definitionv}{def:oracle-value}[Oracle value $V^\star(t_0)$]
For any fixed effective-strength threshold \(t_0\) with \(0<t_0\), the oracle minimax expected-length value is
\[
V^\star(t_0)
=
\inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}
R((C_n),t_0).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The value \leanref{sym:V^\star}{$V^\star$} is the oracle benchmark for honest transported ratio inference at strength threshold \(t_0\).

\begin{definitionv}{def:finite-cell-oracle-value}[Finite-cell oracle value $V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)$]
The finite-cell oracle minimax expected-length value at threshold \(t_0\) is
\[
V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)
=
\inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
\end{definitionv}

The finite-cell oracle value \leanref{sym:V_{\mathcal N}^\star}{$V_{\mathcal N}^\star$} applies the same oracle procedures to the submodel \(\mathcal N_n\), isolating the effect of the finite-cell geometry before weight learning is imposed.

\begin{definitionv}{def:finite-cell-feasible-honesty}[Feasible finite-cell class $\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}$]
The feasible finite-cell honest class is
\[
\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}
=
\left\{
(C_n)_{n\ge1}:
C_n
=
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\right)
\subseteq\Theta,\ 
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n}
P(\theta_T\in C_n)
\ge 1-\alpha
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The class \leanref{sym:\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}{$\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}$} is the feasible counterpart for the uniform finite-cell model.  It requires honest coverage using only the two samples, so the density ratio must be learned from target cell frequencies rather than supplied as an oracle input.

Finally, we record notation for the fixed-geometry least-favorable calculations used in the lower-bound analysis.  These definitions give names to the centered law and to the two distance measures used later.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_5}[Centered fixed-geometry law $Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}$]
We write $Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}$ for the $h=0$ member of the fixed-geometry least-favorable family: for the fixed admissible geometry $\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}$, conditional on $X=x$ the compliance types $(D(0),D(1))=(0,1),(1,1),(0,0)$ have probabilities $p_n(x),(1-p_n(x))/2,(1-p_n(x))/2$, $Y(0)=0$, $Y(1)$ is Bernoulli$(1/2)$ for every compliance type, the same conditional full-data law is used in both populations, source encouragement is drawn with propensity $e(X)$, and the notation denotes the resulting joint observed-data law together with the fixed oracle inputs.
\end{definitionv}

The fixed admissible geometry \leanref{sym:\mathfrak g}{$\mathfrak g$} supplies the carrier for this family; \cref{obj:synth_5} identifies its centered member.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_3}[Chi-square separation of the fixed-geometry submodel]
For the fixed-geometry laws \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) and \(Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\), we write \(\chi^2(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\Vert Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g})\) for the chi-square divergence \[\chi^2(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\Vert Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g})=\int \left(\frac{dQ_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}}{dQ_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}}-1\right)^2\,dQ_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g},\] whenever \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\ll Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\), with value \(+\infty\) otherwise.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_4}[Total variation separation of least-favorable laws]
We write \(\|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) for the total variation distance between the index-\(n\) joint observed-data laws with fixed oracle inputs generated by the geometry \(\mathfrak g\) at local complier-outcome tilt \(h\) and at the center \(h=0\), namely \(\sup_A |Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}(A)-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}(A)|\) over measurable events \(A\) in the corresponding source-sample, target-covariate-sample, and oracle-input space.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:synth_3,obj:synth_4} provide the separation metrics for the fixed-geometry submodel.  They support the change-of-measure comparison between nearby transported complier laws in the oracle lower-bound argument.
