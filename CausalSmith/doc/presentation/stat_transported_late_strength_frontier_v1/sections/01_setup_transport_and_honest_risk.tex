\section{Setup, Transport, and Honest Risk}

At row \leanref{sym:n}{$n$}, the \leanref{S-1}{two-population full-data potential-outcome world} is governed by \leanref{sym:P_n^F}{$P_n^F$} on \leanref{sym:S}{$S$}, \leanref{sym:X}{$X$}, \(D(0),D(1)\), and \(Y(0),Y(1)\).  The source population, \(S=1\), supplies an encouragement experiment with observed variables \leanref{sym:Z}{$Z$}, \leanref{sym:D}{$D$}, and \leanref{sym:Y}{$Y$}; the target population, \(S=0\), supplies the covariate law to which the complier effect is transported.  The measurable covariate space is \leanref{sym:\mathcal X}{$\mathcal X$}.  Potential outcomes are bounded because the expected-length problem is posed over a compact causal range, and treatment receipt is binary because compliance type is the margin through which the encouragement identifies the complier contrast.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_1}[Inverse-propensity instrument score $H(O)$]
For a source observation $O=(X,Z,D,Y)$ and source propensity $e$, the inverse-propensity instrument score is the function $H$ given by \[H(O)=\frac{Z}{e(X)}-\frac{1-Z}{1-e(X)}.\]
\end{definitionv}

The score in \cref{obj:synth_1} is the source inverse-propensity contrast used to recover conditional reduced-form and first-stage contrasts under the source experiment.  Its normalization keeps the assignment contrast centered conditionally on covariates when the source propensity \leanref{sym:e}{$e(X)$} is bounded away from zero and one.

The full-data and sampling primitives are fixed by the following population and array conditions.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:full-data-support}[Full-data support]
The full-data law satisfies
\[
P_n^F\{S\in\{0,1\},\ D(0),D(1)\in\{0,1\},\ Y(0),Y(1)\in[0,1]\}=1.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

This support restriction is specific to this analysis: it places the ratio parameter on a bounded outcome scale and makes the induced complier contrast live in a fixed compact interval.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:population-presence}[Population presence]
For each \(s\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
P_n^F(S=s)>0.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

Population presence is the standard two-population support condition, ensuring that the source and target conditional laws are well defined \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:two-sample-array}[Two-sample array]
For a transported triangular array on the measurable covariate space \(\mathcal X\), let \(P_S\) denote the index-\(n\) observed source law induced on \(O_i^S=(X_i,Z_i,D_i,Y_i)\), and let \(P_T\) denote the index-\(n\) target covariate law on \(X_j^T\). The two sample factors satisfy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Source factor.)} For every \(n\), \(P_S\) is a probability measure.
\item \textbf{(Target factor.)} For every \(n\), \(P_T\) is a probability measure.
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} \(0<c\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\end{itemize}
\end{assumptionv}

The observed \leanref{S-2}{two-sample triangular-array sampling world} treats the source observations \leanref{sym:O_i^S}{$O_i^S$} and target covariates \leanref{sym:X_j^T}{$X_j^T$} as the two empirical inputs, with source law \leanref{sym:P_S}{$P_S$}, target covariate law \leanref{sym:P_T}{$P_T$}, target sample size \leanref{sym:N_n}{$N_n$}, and positive sample-size limit \leanref{sym:c}{$c$}.  This is the standard independent two-sample triangular-array sampling condition used in transported noncompliance designs \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

The next block gives the source instrumental-variables structure.  These restrictions follow the usual LATE logic for an encouragement design, adapted to the transported two-population notation of the present problem \citep{ImbensAngrist1994,AngristImbensRubin1996}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:instrument-overlap}[Instrument overlap]
The instrument-overlap constant satisfies \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), and the source propensity score satisfies
\[
\varepsilon\le e(X)\le 1-\varepsilon
\]
\(P_S^X\)-almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

Instrument overlap is the standard instrument positivity condition, here indexed by the constant \leanref{sym:\varepsilon}{$\varepsilon$}, and it keeps inverse-propensity contrasts uniformly controlled \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:source-observation}[Source observation law]
Conditional on \(S=1\), the observed source law satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Assignment propensity.)} \(P(Z=1\mid X,S=1)=e(X)\).
\item \textbf{(Treatment receipt.)} \(D=D(Z)\).
\item \textbf{(Observed outcome.)} \(Y=Y(D)\).
\end{itemize}
\end{assumptionv}

The source observation law is the standard assignment and consistency condition linking the observed source experiment to receipt and outcome potential outcomes \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iv-randomization}[Instrument randomization]
The instrument satisfies
\[
Z\perp (Y(0),Y(1),D(0),D(1))\mid (X,S=1).
\]
\end{assumptionv}

Instrument randomization is the standard conditional instrument exchangeability condition; within source covariate strata, the encouragement is as good as randomized for the relevant potential outcomes \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iv-exclusion}[Instrument exclusion]
For every \(z,s\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
Y^Z(z)=Y(D(z))
\]
\(P_n^F\)-almost surely conditional on \(S=s\).
\end{assumptionv}

The exclusion restriction is the standard condition that the assignment potential outcome \leanref{sym:Y^Z(z)}{$Y^Z(z)$} operates through treatment receipt \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iv-monotonicity}[Instrument monotonicity]
For every \(s\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
D(1)\ge D(0)
\]
\(P_n^F\)-almost surely conditional on \(S=s\).
\end{assumptionv}

Monotonicity is the standard no-defiers condition, allowing the receipt contrast to be interpreted as a complier share in each population state \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

Transport enters through an outcome-contrast restriction, an integrated first-stage restriction, and covariate-support inclusion.  The outcome condition is conditional in \(X\), whereas the receipt condition is imposed at the target average; this distinction is the transport structure used by the identification result below.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:outcome-transport}[Outcome-contrast transport]
For \(P_T\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=0\right]
=
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=1\right].
\]
\end{assumptionv}

Outcome-contrast transport is the standard conditional transport of the assignment-outcome contrast across the two population states \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:receipt-transport}[Receipt-contrast transport]
The target-population average of the conditional treatment-receipt contrast agrees across the two population states:
\[
\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}\!\left[
  \mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[D(1)-D(0)\mid X,S=0\right]
\right]
=
\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}\!\left[
  \mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[D(1)-D(0)\mid X,S=1\right]
\right].
\]
\end{assumptionv}

Receipt-contrast transport equates the target-law average compliance margin across populations while allowing covariate-specific receipt contrasts to differ \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  This condition is the denominator link that represents the target complier share by the source conditional first stage averaged over \(P_T\).

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:target-complier-positivity}[Target complier positivity]
The target population has positive complier mass:
\[
P_n^F\!\left(D(1)>D(0)\mid S=0\right)>0.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

Target complier positivity is the standard positive target complier share condition, so the transported complier ratio is well defined \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:transport-domination}[Transport domination]
The target covariate law is absolutely continuous with respect to the source covariate marginal on \(\mathcal X\):
\[
P_T\ll P_S^X.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

Transport domination is the standard covariate-support inclusion condition for generalizing source contrasts to the target covariate distribution \citep{ChenHuang2025}.  It yields the \leanref{S-3}{transport and overlap geometry} through the source marginal \leanref{sym:P_S^X}{$P_S^X$}, the density-ratio weight \(w(X)=dP_T/dP_S^X(X)\), and the source propensity.

The next definition collects the identification functionals built from this geometry: the source conditional contrasts, their transported means, and the effective-strength index.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_6}[Transported contrasts and effective strength]
Let \(w(X)=dP_T/dP_S^X(X)\) be a nonnegative version of the transport density ratio.  The source conditional assignment-outcome and receipt contrasts are
\[
\Delta_Y(x)=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,\ S=1\right],
\qquad
\Delta_D(x)=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[D(1)-D(0)\mid X=x,\ S=1\right].
\]
Their transported means and the transport-weight dispersion are
\[
\mu_{Y,n}=\mathbb E_{P_S^X}\!\left[w(X)\,\Delta_Y(X)\right],
\qquad
\mu_n=\mathbb E_{P_S^X}\!\left[w(X)\,\Delta_D(X)\right],
\qquad
\kappa_n=\mathbb E_{P_S^X}\!\left[w(X)^2\right],
\]
and the effective identification strength at index \(n\) is \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\).
\end{definitionv}

The remaining restrictions define the weak-first-stage geometry.  They separate the size of the transport weights from the transported first stage, producing a single effective-strength index for honest expected length.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:weight-envelope}[Weight envelope]
The transport weight satisfies
\[
0 \le w(X) \le 2k_n
\]
\(P_S\)-almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

The weight envelope is specific to this analysis: the sequence \leanref{sym:k_n}{$k_n$} controls the largest admissible density-ratio values while permitting the amount of covariate shift to grow with \(n\).

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:weight-second-moment}[Weight second moment]
The second moment of the transport weight satisfies
\[
\kappa_n \le k_n .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The second-moment bound is specific to this analysis and records the Kish-type dispersion scale \(\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]\) that governs the variance of transported inverse-propensity averages.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:degrading-array}[Degrading array rates]
The array satisfies
\[
k_n \to \infty,
\qquad
k_n=o(n^{1/2}),
\qquad
\mu_n\to 0 .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

These rate restrictions are specific to this analysis: the envelope scale diverges at a sub-root-\(n\) rate, and the transported first-stage mean \leanref{sym:\mu_n}{$\mu_n$} enters the weak-identification regime.  Together with the dispersion scale, they define the effective-strength scalar \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\) used in the risk comparison.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:finite-cell-source}[Uniform finite cells]
The source covariate marginal \(P_S^X\) is supported on exactly \(k_n\) measurable cells, assigns mass \(1/k_n\) to each cell, and satisfies
\[
e(X)=1/2
\]
\(P_S\)-almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

The uniform finite-cell condition is specific to this analysis.  It creates the sample-only cell-learning submodel used for feasible procedures, with equal source cell masses and a randomized source encouragement probability.

The full model and the finite-cell submodel collect these assumptions at each row of the triangular array.

\begin{definitionv}{def:model-class}[Transported LATE model class \(\mathcal P_n\)]
The transported LATE model class at index \(n\) is
\[
\mathcal P_n
=
\bigl\{(P_n^F,e): (P_n^F,e)\text{ satisfies the following conditions at index }n\bigr\}.
\]
The required conditions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Full-data support.)} \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support}.
\item \textbf{(Population presence.)} \cref{obj:ass:population-presence}.
\item \textbf{(Two-sample array.)} \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument overlap.)} \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}.
\item \textbf{(Source observation.)} \cref{obj:ass:source-observation}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument randomization.)} \cref{obj:ass:iv-randomization}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument exclusion.)} \cref{obj:ass:iv-exclusion}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument monotonicity.)} \cref{obj:ass:iv-monotonicity}.
\item \textbf{(Outcome transport.)} \cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport}.
\item \textbf{(Receipt transport.)} \cref{obj:ass:receipt-transport}.
\item \textbf{(Target complier positivity.)} \cref{obj:ass:target-complier-positivity}.
\item \textbf{(Transport domination.)} \cref{obj:ass:transport-domination}.
\item \textbf{(Weight envelope.)} \cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope}.
\item \textbf{(Weight second moment.)} \cref{obj:ass:weight-second-moment}.
\item \textbf{(Degrading array.)} \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

The transported LATE model class \leanref{sym:\mathcal P_n}{$\mathcal P_n$} is the main domain for the oracle minimax statements.  It combines the source IV restrictions with the transport and dispersion conditions that make the ratio target comparable across rows.

The finite-cell class is the transported model with one added row restriction:
\[
\mathcal N_n=\{P\in\mathcal P_n:P\text{ satisfies }\cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}\}.
\]
Thus the IV, transport, overlap, envelope, second-moment, and degrading-array conditions are inherited from \(\mathcal P_n\).  The added content is the uniform source-cell geometry in \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}; the anchored checklist form of \leanref{sym:\mathcal N_n}{$\mathcal N_n$} is deferred to \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.

Before defining risk, we record the oracle inputs and the two score radii used later.  Oracle procedures receive the source sample, the target covariate sample, the transport weight, and the source propensity.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_2}[Score-inversion critical radius $L_\alpha$]
We define the score-inversion critical radius by \(L_\alpha=\{8/(\alpha\varepsilon^2)\}^{1/2}\), where \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) is the noncoverage level and \(\varepsilon\) is the source instrument-overlap constant.
\end{definitionv}

The constant in \cref{obj:synth_2} combines the noncoverage level \leanref{sym:\alpha}{$\alpha$} with instrument overlap.  It will determine the radius of the score-inversion set in the oracle construction.

The coverage requirements below are stated for the following causal estimand.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_7}[Target complier effect $\theta_T$]
The target complier effect is the ratio
\[
\theta_T
=
\frac{\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\,\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}\mid S=0\right]}
     {P_n^F\!\left\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\right\}},
\]
the mean effect of treatment receipt among target-population compliers.  Under target complier positivity the denominator is positive and \(\theta_T=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0,\ S=0\right]\); when the denominator is zero, the ratio is taken to be zero.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:oracle-honesty}[Oracle honest class $\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}$]
The oracle honest class is the class of oracle procedures \((C_n)_{n\ge1}\) satisfying \(0<\alpha<1\) and the following conditions.  For every \(n\), \(C_n\) maps each input
\[
\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right),
\qquad
w:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R,\quad w(x)\ge0,\quad e:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R,
\]
to a subset of \(\Theta=[-1,1]\).  For every \(n\) and every measurable such pair \((w,e)\), the sample-by-parameter graph
\[
\left\{
\left(((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}),\vartheta\right):
\vartheta\in
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right)
\right\}
\]
is measurable.  For every \(n\), every \(P\in\mathcal P_n\), and every pair \(w,w'\) of measurable nonnegative versions of \(dP_T/dP_S^X\), the evaluations using the row-\(n\) propensity score agree for the two-sample law almost surely:
\[
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right)
=
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w',e\right).
\]
Finally,
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
P\!\left(\theta_T\in C_n\right), & \mathcal P_n\neq\varnothing,\\[0.8em]
1, & \mathcal P_n=\varnothing
\end{cases}
\ge 1-\alpha,
\]
where in the probability term \(C_n\) is evaluated at the canonical row-\(n\) density ratio \(dP_T/dP_S^X\) and the row-\(n\) propensity score.
\end{definitionv}

The \leanref{S-5}{procedure and minimax-risk world} treats each confidence set \leanref{sym:C_n}{$C_n$} as a random subset of the compact parameter space \(\Theta=[-1,1]\).  Oracle honesty, written \leanref{sym:\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}{$\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}$}, requires uniform asymptotic coverage over \(\mathcal P_n\) while allowing the procedure to use the canonical nonnegative weight input \(w\) and the propensity.

\begin{definitionv}{def:frontier-risk}[Frontier risk $R((C_n),t_0)$]
For a confidence-set sequence \((C_n)_{n\ge1}\) and threshold \(t_0\), the limiting worst-case expected length above effective strength \(t_0\) is
\[
R((C_n),t_0)
=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
\end{definitionv}

The risk \leanref{sym:R}{$R((C_n),t_0)$} measures limiting worst-case Lebesgue length \leanref{sym:\lambda}{$\lambda(C_n)$} over laws whose effective strength exceeds the positive threshold \leanref{sym:t_0}{$t_0$}.  This conditioning by \leanref{sym:t_n}{$t_n$} is the paper's way of comparing honest procedures at a fixed level of transported identification strength.

\begin{definitionv}{def:oracle-value}[Oracle value $V^\star(t_0)$]
For any fixed effective-strength threshold \(t_0\) with \(0<t_0\), the oracle minimax expected-length value is
\[
V^\star(t_0)
=
\inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}
R((C_n),t_0).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The value \leanref{sym:V^\star}{$V^\star(t_0)$} is the oracle benchmark over honest procedures with known transport weights and propensities.

\begin{definitionv}{def:finite-cell-oracle-value}[Finite-cell oracle value $V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)$]
The finite-cell oracle minimax expected-length value at threshold \(t_0\) is
\[
V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)
=
\inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
\end{definitionv}

The finite-cell oracle value \leanref{sym:V_{\mathcal N}^\star}{$V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)$} applies the same oracle procedure class to the uniform finite-cell submodel.  It gives the comparison point for feasible cell-based weight learning.

\begin{definitionv}{def:finite-cell-feasible-honesty}[Feasible finite-cell class $\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}$]
The feasible finite-cell honest class is
\[
\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}
=
\left\{
(C_n)_{n\ge1}:
C_n
=
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\right)
\subseteq\Theta,\ 
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n}
P(\theta_T\in C_n)
\ge 1-\alpha
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The feasible class \leanref{sym:\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}{$\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}$} uses the source observations and target covariates as the procedure inputs in the uniform finite-cell design.  Its coverage requirement remains uniform over \(\mathcal N_n\).

The identification result connects the transported functionals of \cref{obj:synth_6} to the target complier effect of \cref{obj:synth_7}: the inverse-propensity score recovers the source conditional contrasts \leanref{sym:\Delta_Y}{$\Delta_Y$} and \leanref{sym:\Delta_D}{$\Delta_D$} from observed source moments, transport moves \leanref{sym:\mu_{Y,n}}{$\mu_{Y,n}$} and \(\mu_n\) to target complier functionals, and the Wald ratio equals \leanref{sym:\theta_T}{$\theta_T$}.

\begin{theoremv}{prop:compact-causal-range}[Compact causal range]
Fix \(n\), and let \(P\) be a transported array with sample-size and envelope schedules \(N_n\) and \(k_n\), constants \(c\) and \(\varepsilon\), and model class \(\mathcal P_n\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-class}. Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Model membership.)} \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) at the index \(n\).
\item \textbf{(Source contrasts.)} The assignment outcome contrast and the receipt contrast for the source population satisfy the usual measurability and integrability conditions under the source covariate marginal \(P_S^X\).
\end{itemize}
Then \(\Delta_Y\) and \(\Delta_D\) represent the observed source outcome and receipt contrasts: for every measurable covariate set \(A\),
\[
\int_{\{O:X\in A\}} H(O)Y\,dP_S(O)
=
\int_A \Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x),
\qquad
\int_{\{O:X\in A\}} H(O)D\,dP_S(O)
=
\int_A \Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
Moreover, the transported reduced-form outcome mean satisfies
\[
\mu_{Y,n}
=
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[
\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}\mid S=0
\right],
\]
the transported first-stage mean satisfies
\[
\mu_n
=
P_n^F\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\},
\]
and the Wald ratio equals the target complier-conditional contrast,
\[
\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}=\theta_T.
\]
Finally, \(\theta_T\in\Theta=[-1,1]\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} establishes the identification link used throughout the paper: the weighted source reduced form equals the target complier-weighted outcome contrast, the weighted source first stage equals the target complier share, and their ratio is the target complier-conditional contrast.  The bounded outcome support then places the estimand in \leanref{sym:\Theta}{$\Theta$}, making expected length on a fixed compact parameter space the relevant risk criterion.

For later fixed-geometry arguments, we also record the notation for the centered least-favorable law and the two distance measures used to compare local alternatives.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_5}[Centered fixed-geometry law $Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}$]
We write $Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}$ for the $h=0$ member of the fixed-geometry least-favorable family: for the fixed admissible geometry $\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}$, conditional on $X=x$ the compliance types $(D(0),D(1))=(0,1),(1,1),(0,0)$ have probabilities $p_n(x),(1-p_n(x))/2,(1-p_n(x))/2$, $Y(0)=0$, $Y(1)$ is Bernoulli$(1/2)$ for every compliance type, the same conditional full-data law is used in both populations, source encouragement is drawn with propensity $e(X)$, and the notation denotes the resulting joint observed-data law together with the fixed oracle inputs.
\end{definitionv}

The centered law in \cref{obj:synth_5} is the baseline member of the fixed-geometry least-favorable family used in the lower-bound construction.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_3}[Chi-square separation of the fixed-geometry submodel]
For the fixed-geometry laws \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) and \(Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\), we write \(\chi^2(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\Vert Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g})\) for the chi-square divergence \[\chi^2(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\Vert Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g})=\int \left(\frac{dQ_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}}{dQ_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}}-1\right)^2\,dQ_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g},\] whenever \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\ll Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\), with value \(+\infty\) otherwise.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_4}[Total variation separation of least-favorable laws]
We write \(\|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) for the total variation distance between the index-\(n\) joint observed-data laws with fixed oracle inputs generated by the geometry \(\mathfrak g\) at local complier-outcome tilt \(h\) and at the center \(h=0\), namely \(\sup_A |Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}(A)-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}(A)|\) over measurable events \(A\) in the corresponding source-sample, target-covariate-sample, and oracle-input space.
\end{definitionv}

The separations in \cref{obj:synth_3,obj:synth_4} calibrate the statistical distance between fixed-geometry local alternatives.  They provide the notation needed to express how lower-bound perturbations move the transported complier contrast while remaining close in observed-data law.
