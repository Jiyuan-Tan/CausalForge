The paper sits at the intersection of noncompliance, weak identification, and transportability. The causal estimand lineage begins with instrumental-variable and complier-effect work: Bloom’s treatment of no-shows, the LATE identification results of Imbens and Angrist and Angrist, Imbens and Rubin, Balke and Pearl’s sharp IV bounds, Imbens and Rubin’s complier outcome distributions, and the principal-stratification framework of Frangakis and Rubin. Heckman and Vytlacil’s marginal treatment effect framework supplies a neighboring extrapolation language for IV-defined causal effects, while the deep-canvassing and Moving to Opportunity applications motivate transported complier-effect targets in settings with noncompliance and external-validity goals.

A second strand studies robust inference when denominators or first stages are weak. Anderson and Rubin introduced the test-inversion logic, Fieller’s interval problem is the classical ratio analogue, and the weak-IV literature developed finite-sample and asymptotic tools through Staiger and Stock, Stock, Wright and Yogo, Moreira, Andrews, Moreira and Stock, Mikusheva, and the survey of Andrews, Stock and Sun. Gleser and Hwang, Dufour, and Bertanha and Moreira establish impossibility phenomena for nearly unidentified models with unbounded parameter geometry. The present paper uses the same ratio-inference discipline in a causal parameter space whose finite diameter is forced by bounded outcomes and binary treatment receipt, yielding a finite expected-length frontier indexed by the effective strength \(n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\).

The transportability and generalizability lineage includes Hotz, Imbens and Mortimer on predicting program effects across locations; Cole and Stuart, Stuart et al., Tipton, Dahabreh et al., and Nguyen et al. on trial-to-target generalization, weighting, doubly robust estimation, and sensitivity to moderators; and Pearl, Bareinboim and coauthors on graphical transportability and data fusion. Semiparametric efficiency and modern nuisance estimation enter through Robins, Rotnitzky and Zhao, Hirano, Imbens and Ridder, van der Laan and Rubin, and Chernozhukov et al. Zeng, Kennedy, Bodnar and Naimi and Rudolph et al. give recent efficiency and minimax results for transported average treatment effects; those results form the regular transported-ATE benchmark against which the transported-CACE weak-first-stage frontier is positioned.

The directly related frontier is concentrated around transported noncompliance and weak-IV-robust LATE procedures. Rudolph and van der Laan construct robust transported encouragement-design estimators, and Chen and Huang identify and estimate the transported CACE in two-sample generalizability settings with noncompliance. Choi, Gu and Shen and Choi and Shen analyze weak-instrument robust inference for linear two-sample IV, while Ma and Smucler, Lanni and Masip study weak-identification-robust LATE confidence sets with modern nuisance estimation and score-test inversion. Aronow, Chang and Lopatto provide finite-population randomization-based LATE confidence sets, Ross et al. develop transport methods for trial settings where participation affects adherence, and Ren studies extrapolating LATE with weak IVs in an MTE framework. The present contribution characterizes the matched minimax expected-length frontier for the transported complier ratio under fixed overlap, bounded outcomes, compact causal range, and Kish weight dispersion, with a feasible finite-cell target-weight-learning result aligned to that oracle frontier.
