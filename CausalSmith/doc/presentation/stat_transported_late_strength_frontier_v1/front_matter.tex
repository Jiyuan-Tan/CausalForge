\begin{abstract}
This paper studies honest confidence sets for a transported complier effect in a two-sample encouragement design.  Source data contain randomized encouragement, treatment receipt, outcomes, and covariates; target data contain covariates.  Under bounded outcomes, binary receipt, source instrumental-validity conditions, transport restrictions for the reduced form and first stage, domination of the target covariate law by the source covariate law, fixed instrument overlap, and controlled transport-weight dispersion, the transported Wald ratio identifies a target complier-conditional contrast in \([-1,1]\).  The paper characterizes the minimax expected length of honest oracle confidence sets over laws with effective strength at least \(t_0\), where \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\) combines the transported first-stage mean \(\mu_n\) and the transport-weight second moment \(\kappa_n\).  The oracle frontier has order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\): every honest oracle procedure has worst-case limiting expected length at least this order, and an Anderson--Rubin-style transported score inversion attains it up to constants.  The same order holds in fixed-geometry slices uniformly over admissible transport geometries.  For finite-cell target-weight learning, sample-only score inversions using target cell frequencies attain the oracle order in uniform and regular finite-cell designs under sub-root cell growth.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Encouragement designs often identify causal effects for compliers in a source population, while policy questions ask for the corresponding complier contrast in a target population.  This paper studies the precision cost of making that transport step when the transported first stage may be weak and the target covariate distribution may differ from the source distribution.  The object is a transported local average treatment effect: a target complier-conditional contrast formed by transporting source reduced-form and first-stage contrasts to the target covariate law.

The setup has two samples.  The source sample observes covariates, encouragement, treatment receipt, and outcomes.  The target sample observes covariates.  The full-data model imposes bounded outcomes, binary treatment receipt, source consistency, conditional random assignment of encouragement, exclusion, monotonicity, target complier positivity, and transport conditions for the assignment-outcome and assignment-receipt contrasts.  The target covariate law is dominated by the source covariate marginal, with density ratio \(w\), and the source encouragement propensity is bounded away from zero and one.  The asymptotic array allows the transport geometry and the first stage to degrade through a weight envelope \(k_n\), a second-moment scale \(\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]\), and a transported first-stage mean \(\mu_n\).

The identification result in \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} establishes the causal ratio used throughout the paper.  Under the transported LATE model class \(\mathcal P_n\) in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, inverse-propensity source scores identify the source conditional reduced-form and first-stage contrasts, target weighting transports their averages, and the Wald ratio \(\mu_{Y,n}/\mu_n\) equals the target complier-conditional contrast \(\theta_T\).  The bounded-outcome and binary-receipt scale places \(\theta_T\) in the compact interval \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), which supplies the length scale for all confidence-set comparisons.

The main quantity governing honest length is the effective-strength index
\[
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}.
\]
This scalar combines the two sources of difficulty in the transported ratio problem.  A small transported first stage lowers the denominator, while a dispersed transport weight increases the variance of transported source contrasts.  The ratio \(n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\) therefore plays the role that the first-stage concentration parameter plays in weak-instrument inference, with the Kish weight dispersion translating covariate shift into the same scale.

The first main result is an oracle minimax frontier.  Oracle procedures observe the source and target samples together with the row-specific density ratio \(w\) and propensity \(e\), and they satisfy uniform asymptotic honesty over \(\mathcal P_n\) as in \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}.  For every fixed threshold \(t_0>0\), \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse} gives the lower bound
\[
V^\star(t_0)\gtrsim \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
under the stated sampling-ratio, overlap, carrier, envelope, second-moment, and degrading-array conditions.  The same converse applies to the finite-cell oracle value \(V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)\), and for sufficiently small positive \(t_0\) the expected length remains bounded away from zero.

The matching oracle construction is a transported Anderson--Rubin inversion.  For each candidate \(\vartheta\in[-1,1]\), \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle} forms the weighted source score
\[
w(X)\left\{\frac{Z}{e(X)}-\frac{1-Z}{1-e(X)}\right\}(Y-\vartheta D)
\]
and accepts \(\vartheta\) when its empirical mean lies within a radius proportional to \((\widehat\kappa_n/n)^{1/2}\).  \Cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} shows that this score-inversion sequence belongs to the oracle honest class and satisfies
\[
R((C_n),t_0)\lesssim \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\]
for every fixed \(t_0>0\).  Together with \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse}, this characterizes the oracle minimax expected-length order over the transported model.

The second set of results fixes the transport geometry.  An admissible geometry \(\mathfrak g\) in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} specifies the source covariate law, target density ratio, induced target law, and propensity sequence.  The fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} holds these objects fixed while varying the causal full-data law.  \Cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} proves the same two-sided frontier,
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\asymp \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
with constants uniform over admissible geometries.  In the unit-weight specialization, \cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction} gives \(\kappa_n=1\), \(P_T=P_S^X\), and \(t_n=n\mu_n^2\), connecting the transported formulation to the familiar single-population weak-first-stage scale.

The third set of results replaces oracle transport weights with target-sample estimates in finite-cell designs.  In the uniform finite-cell class \(\mathcal N_n\) of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}, source covariates are supported on \(k_n\) equally likely cells and \(e(X)=1/2\).  The feasible procedure in \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} estimates target cell masses from the target covariate sample, estimates source reduced-form and first-stage contrasts cell by cell, and uses a target-cell collision statistic to estimate the weight-dispersion scale.  Under \(N_n/n\to c>0\), fixed overlap and coverage levels, and \(k_n=o(\sqrt n)\), the resulting sample-only confidence sets are honest over \(\mathcal N_n\) and attain the same \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) expected-length order, with a matching lower order over the feasible finite-cell class.

The regular-cell extension covers unequal source cell probabilities known from design or a sampling frame.  \Cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class} allows cell masses \(q_{x,n}\) between fixed multiples of \(1/k_n\), and \cref{obj:def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty} gives procedures access to the known \(q_{x,n}\) and known cell propensities.  \Cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} normalizes source cell contrasts by \(q_{x,n}\), weights target-cell collisions by \(q_{x,n}^{-1}\), and attains the same frontier order under the stated regularity, overlap, sampling-ratio, and cell-growth conditions.

The paper connects the LATE and principal-stratification traditions of \citet{Bloom1984}, \citet{ImbensAngrist1994}, \citet{AngristImbensRubin1996}, \citet{ImbensRubin1997}, \citet{FrangakisRubin2002}, and \citet{BalkePearl1997} with weak-identification inference based on score or ratio inversion \citep{AndersonRubin1949,Fieller1954,StaigerStock1997,StockWrightYogo2002,Moreira2003,AndrewsMoreiraStock2006,Mikusheva2010,AndrewsStockSun2019}.  It also builds on transportability and generalizability work emphasizing overlap, weighting, and target-population interpretation \citep{HotzImbensMortimer2005,ColeStuart2010,StuartColeBradshawLeaf2011,Tipton2013,Dahabreh2020,DahabrehPetitoRobertson2020,Nguyen2017,PearlBareinboim2011,BareinboimPearl2013,BareinboimPearl2016}.  Closest in estimand and design are transported noncompliance and weak-IV-robust LATE analyses \citep{RudolphVanderLaan2017,ChenHuang2025,ChoiGuShen2018,ChoiShen2019,Ma2023,SmuclerLanniMasip2025,Aronow2026,Ren2025,Ross2026}.  The contribution here is the matched expected-length characterization for transported complier-ratio inference, with the transport weight second moment entering directly into the weak-denominator frontier.

The formal statements and proof arguments are accompanied by Lean 4 verification, with precise scope recorded in \cref{sec:deferred-proofs}.
