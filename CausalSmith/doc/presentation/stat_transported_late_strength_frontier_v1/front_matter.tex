\begin{abstract}
This paper studies honest confidence sets for a transported complier average causal effect in a two-sample encouragement design.  A source population supplies an instrumental-variables experiment with noncompliance, while a target population supplies the covariate law.  Under the transported-IV model class, with source contrasts satisfying the stated measurability and integrability conditions, the transported Wald ratio equals the target complier contrast and lies in the compact range \([-1,1]\).  The paper characterizes the oracle minimax expected-length frontier for honest confidence sets over transported triangular arrays satisfying the stated overlap, sample-size, envelope, second-moment, and degrading-array conditions, with weight second moment \(\kappa_n\) and transported first-stage mean \(\mu_n\).  The frontier is indexed by the effective strength
\[
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}.
\]
For every fixed threshold \(t_0>0\), oracle procedures that know the transport weight and source propensity have minimax expected length of order
\[
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
A transported Anderson--Rubin score-inversion set attains this order. The unit-transport-weight least-favorable slice is embedded in the transported class, so its weak-ratio lower bound applies to the larger transported model. Up to constants depending on the sampling ratio, overlap, and noncoverage level, the same order holds conditionally on any admissible fixed covariate-shift geometry. In finite-cell designs, target cell frequencies and a collision-scale U-statistic yield feasible score-inversion procedures that learn the target weights from the target covariate sample and attain the same order under the stated cell-growth and overlap conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Encouragement experiments with imperfect take-up identify complier effects in the source population under the usual instrumental-variables restrictions \citep{Bloom1984,ImbensAngrist1994,AngristImbensRubin1996}.  Many applications also require a target-population interpretation: the source experiment is observed in one population, while the policy-relevant covariate distribution belongs to another.  Transportability and generalizability methods address this covariate-shift problem through support and weighting conditions \citep{HotzImbensMortimer2005,ColeStuart2010,StuartColeBradshawLeaf2011,Tipton2013,Dahabreh2020,DahabrehPetitoRobertson2020,Nguyen2017,PearlBareinboim2011,BareinboimPearl2013,BareinboimPearl2016}.  For transported noncompliance, the estimand is a ratio: a transported reduced form divided by a transported first stage.  This ratio structure makes weak identification central when the target complier share is small or when transport weights are dispersed.

This paper characterizes honest expected length for that transported complier ratio.  The source sample contains \((X,Z,D,Y)\) from an encouragement design, and the target sample contains covariates \(X\).  The target-to-source density ratio \(w=dP_T/dP_S^X\) transports source conditional contrasts to the target covariate law.  Under the transported-IV model class \(\mathcal P_n\) in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, with source contrasts satisfying the stated measurability and integrability conditions, \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} shows that the transported reduced form equals the target complier-weighted outcome contrast, the transported first stage equals the target complier share, and their ratio equals the target complier effect \(\theta_T\).  Bounded outcomes and binary treatment receipt place \(\theta_T\) in \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), so expected length is measured on a fixed compact parameter space.

The main statistical object is the effective strength
\[
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n},
\]
where \(\mu_n\) is the transported first-stage mean and \(\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]\) is the Kish-type dispersion scale of the transport weights.  This scalar combines two sources of difficulty.  A small \(\mu_n\) is the familiar weak-first-stage problem from IV ratio inference \citep{AndersonRubin1949,Fieller1954,StaigerStock1997,StockWrightYogo2002,Moreira2003,AndrewsMoreiraStock2006,Mikusheva2010,AndrewsStockSun2019}.  A large \(\kappa_n\) records the variance inflation from transporting the source experiment to a different target covariate law.  The frontier conditions allow \(k_n\to\infty\), \(k_n=o(\sqrt n)\), and \(\mu_n\to0\), so the analysis covers triangular arrays in which covariate shift grows while the transported first stage weakens.

The oracle benchmark allows procedures to use the transport weight \(w\) and the source propensity \(e\).  \Cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse} proves that every oracle honest confidence-set sequence over \(\mathcal P_n\) has limiting worst-case expected length at least a constant times
\[
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\]
over laws satisfying \(t_n\ge t_0\).  The same lower bound already holds on the uniform finite-cell submodel \(\mathcal N_n\).  \Cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} gives the matching construction: invert the transported residual score
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
w(X_i)\left\{\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)}\right\}(Y_i-\vartheta D_i)
\]
with a radius proportional to \(\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\).  This Anderson--Rubin style inversion is honest over \(\mathcal P_n\) and attains the same expected-length order for every fixed \(t_0>0\).

The fixed-geometry results isolate the role of covariate shift.  For an admissible deterministic geometry \(\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}\), \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} holds the source covariate law, target weight, and propensity fixed while allowing the causal response law to vary.  \Cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} establishes the same two-sided order
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)\asymp \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\]
with constants uniform over admissible geometries.  The lower bound uses the least-favorable family in \cref{obj:def:least-favorable-witness}: the complier probability is proportional to the transport weight, so the transported first stage is calibrated to \(t_0\), and a local complier-outcome tilt moves the target ratio while the observed-data laws remain close in total variation as recorded in \cref{obj:def:geometry-handle}.  \Cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction} specializes this comparison to \(w=1\), where \(\kappa_n=1\), \(P_T=P_S^X\), and the effective strength reduces to \(n\mu_n^2\).

The feasible results replace oracle-known weights by target-sample learning in finite-cell designs.  In the uniform finite-cell class \(\mathcal N_n\), the source covariate law assigns mass \(1/k_n\) to each cell and the source propensity equals \(1/2\).  \Cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} constructs a sample-only score-inversion set using target empirical cell frequencies, source cellwise reduced-form and first-stage contrasts, and a target-sample U-statistic for the collision scale.  Under \(N_n/n\to c>0\) and \(k_n=o(\sqrt n)\), this feasible procedure is honest over \(\mathcal N_n\) and attains the oracle frontier order.  \Cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} extends the construction to regular finite-cell designs with known source cell probabilities bounded between fixed multiples of \(1/k_n\) and known cell-varying propensities satisfying overlap.

The contribution is therefore a matched minimax expected-length characterization for transported complier ratios under the stated transported-IV, overlap, positivity, support, envelope, dispersion, sample-size, and cell-growth conditions.  The analysis connects transported noncompliance methods such as \citet{RudolphVanderLaan2017} and \citet{ChenHuang2025} with weak-identification-robust LATE and two-sample IV inference \citep{ChoiGuShen2018,ChoiShen2019,Ma2023,SmuclerLanniMasip2025}.  For the compact transported complier ratio under the stated transported-IV restrictions and growth conditions, the frontier is indexed by \(n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\), so first-stage weakness and transport-weight dispersion enter through a common scale.
