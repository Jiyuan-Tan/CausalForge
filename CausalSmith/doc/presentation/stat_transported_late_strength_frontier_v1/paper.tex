\begin{abstract}
This paper studies honest confidence sets for a transported complier effect in a two-sample encouragement design. Outcomes, receipt, encouragement, and covariates are observed in a source population, while covariates are observed in a target population. Under the transported complier-effect model class with binary receipt, bounded outcomes, instrument overlap, monotonicity, and target-to-source covariate domination, the target complier effect \(\theta_T\), the target complier-conditional outcome contrast, is identified as a transported reduced-form ratio and lies in the compact causal range \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). The expected-length frontier is governed by the effective strength \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\), where \(\mu_n\) is the transported first-stage mean and \(\kappa_n\) is the Kish second moment of the transport weights. For every fixed threshold \(t_0>0\), oracle honest confidence sets have minimax expected length of order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\), both globally and conditional on each admissible deterministic source-covariate, transport-weight, and propensity geometry. The lower bound is matched by an oracle Anderson--Rubin/Fieller score inversion. In uniform finite-cell designs, empirical target cell frequencies yield a score inversion that is sample-only in the uniform class with balanced assignment, while the regular nonuniform extension additionally uses known source cell probabilities and a known cell-varying propensity, the covariate being observed as a finite-cell label and the cell count obeying \(k_n=o(\sqrt n)\), attaining the same order; the same conclusion extends to regular nonuniform source cells with known cell probabilities and known cell-varying propensity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Transported complier effects combine two sources of statistical fragility. The first is the ratio structure of the local average treatment effect: the causal contrast for compliers is recovered by dividing an outcome reduced form by a first-stage receipt contrast \citep{ImbensAngrist1994,AngristImbensRubin1996}. When the first stage is weak, conventional ratio inference can behave poorly, motivating Anderson--Rubin and Fieller inversions and the weak-IV literature on procedures with reliable coverage under small denominators \citep{AndersonRubin1949,Fieller1954,StaigerStock1997,StockWrightYogo2002,Moreira2003,AndrewsMoreiraStock2006,Mikusheva2010,AndrewsStockSun2019}. The second is transport: the source encouragement design is reweighted to represent a target covariate distribution, so the effective information in the source sample depends on the dispersion of the target-to-source density ratio \citep{ColeStuart2010,Tipton2013,Dahabreh2020,ChenHuang2025,ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2024}.

This paper characterizes the minimax expected-length rate order, up to constants, of honest confidence sets for the transported complier ratio, at each fixed effective-strength threshold and within the oracle, fixed-geometry, uniform finite-cell, and known regular-cell classes studied here. The source sample contains encouragement, treatment receipt, outcomes, and covariates; the target sample contains covariates. The maintained model class in \cref{obj:def:model-class} imposes the standard binary encouragement-design conditions, conditional transport of the outcome reduced form, integrated transport of the first stage, target complier positivity, target-to-source covariate domination, and deterministic controls on transport-weight dispersion. The target parameter is the target complier-conditional outcome contrast. Under these conditions, \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} identifies the parameter as
\[
\theta_T=\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}
\]
and places it in the compact range \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), where \(\mu_{Y,n}\) is the transported reduced-form outcome mean and \(\mu_n\) is the transported first-stage mean.

The main rate index is
\[
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n},
\]
where \(\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]\) is the Kish dispersion of the transport weights. This scalar combines the source sample size, the transported first-stage magnitude, and the unevenness of target reweighting. A design with the same first-stage mean has less effective strength when the target population is represented by more dispersed source weights. In the no-shift case \(w(X)=1\), \cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction} gives \(\kappa_n=1\) and reduces the scale to \(n\mu_n^2\).

The oracle results give matching lower and upper expected-length rates. The oracle class in \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty} allows the procedure to use the target-to-source density ratio and the source assignment propensity. This construction serves as an oracle benchmark for the general model; feasible target-weight learning is established in the finite-cell classes of \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}. For every fixed effective-strength threshold \(t_0>0\), \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse} gives a lower bound of order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) for oracle honest procedures. \Cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} matches this order with a score inversion for the transported moment of \(Y-\vartheta D\). The resulting confidence set keeps a candidate value \(\vartheta\in\Theta\) when the transported empirical score is within a radius proportional to \(\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\). This is the transported complier analogue of weak-ratio inversion in the Anderson--Rubin and Fieller tradition.

The same frontier holds after conditioning on deterministic geometry. The admissible geometry class in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} fixes the source covariate marginal, the transport weight, the target covariate law induced by that weight, and the source propensity. Within a fixed-geometry slice, the causal contrasts and the first-stage strength vary subject to the transported model restrictions. \Cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} establishes the same two-sided order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) uniformly over admissible deterministic geometries. This conditional statement isolates the weak-first-stage and outcome-tilt components of the problem from the deterministic transport geometry.

The feasible part of the paper studies finite-cell target-weight learning. In the uniform finite-cell submodel of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}, the source covariate law assigns equal mass to \(k_n\) cells and the source propensity is balanced. The target covariate sample estimates the target cell probabilities, and the source sample estimates cellwise outcome and receipt contrasts. \Cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} shows that the resulting score inversion using sample-supplied target weights is honest over the uniform finite-cell class and attains the oracle order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\). \Cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} extends the construction to regular nonuniform source cells when the source cell probabilities and cell-varying propensity are known.

The analysis connects transported CACE identification and estimation \citep{RudolphVanderLaan2017,ChenHuang2025} with weak-identification-robust ratio inference \citep{GleserHwang1987,Dufour1997,ChoiGuShen2018,ChoiShen2019,Ma2023,SmuclerLanniMasip2025}. It also complements transport and generalizability theory for average treatment effects \citep{HotzImbensMortimer2005,PearlBareinboim2011,BareinboimPearl2013,BareinboimPearl2016,DahabrehPetitoRobertson2020,Nguyen2017,ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2024} by treating the transported denominator as part of the identification problem. The finite-cell constructions estimate target-cell weights from the target covariate sample while retaining the compact-range and weak-ratio features of the transported complier estimand.

The formal derivations state their assumptions and conclusions explicitly.

The rest of the paper proceeds as follows. The related-work section places the results in the literatures on complier effects, weak ratio inference, and transportability. The setup section defines the two-sample transported encouragement design, the model classes, the target estimand, and the expected-length criterion. The oracle minimax section proves the global and fixed-geometry frontiers and gives the oracle score inversion. The cell-weight-learning section gives the uniform and regular finite-cell feasible procedures. The discussion section interprets the effective-strength scale and records directions for future work.


\section{Related work}

\subsection{Comparison with the closest results}

The nearest results are best placed by naming, for each, the estimand, the sampling design, the
first-stage regime, the status of the transport weights, the geometry of the parameter space, and the
guarantee delivered.

\citet{ChenHuang2025} study the transported complier average causal effect in a two-sample
generalizability design, with a first stage bounded away from zero, transport weights estimated as
nuisances, an unrestricted parameter space, and asymptotic normality with a consistent variance
estimator as the guarantee. The present paper adopts that estimand and design and extends the
guarantee to honest coverage with a two-sided expected-length rate that remains meaningful as the
transported first stage degenerates.

\citet{ChoiGuShen2018} and \citet{ChoiShen2019} study structural coefficients in two-sample
instrumental variables within a single population, under weak identification, on unbounded parameter
spaces, delivering weak-identification-robust tests and the confidence sets obtained by inverting them.
The transported problem here adds a covariate shift, and the dispersion of the transport weight enters
the rate as a Kish effective-sample-size deflation, which is the specific mechanism this paper isolates.

\citet{Ma2023} and \citet{SmuclerLanniMasip2025} construct weak-identification-robust confidence sets
for the local average treatment effect in a single population, the former with machine-learning
nuisances, and deliver coverage guarantees. This paper contributes the matching expected-length
frontier, in the transported setting, alongside coverage.

\citet{ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2024} develop minimax theory for transported average treatment effects,
where the functional is point identified under strong overlap and the rates concern estimation error.
Here the estimand is a ratio whose denominator is permitted to vanish, and the compact parameter space
forced by bounded outcomes and binary receipt is what keeps the minimax expected length finite.

This paper belongs to the literature on causal effects for compliers in encouragement designs. The local average treatment effect framework of \citet{ImbensAngrist1994} and \citet{AngristImbensRubin1996} formalizes the ratio of an intention-to-treat outcome contrast to a first-stage receipt contrast under random assignment, exclusion, and monotonicity, building on randomized encouragement designs such as \citet{Bloom1984}. Principal stratification gives the same object a broader potential-outcome interpretation, including bounds and strata-specific estimands in settings with post-assignment behavior \citep{BalkePearl1997,ImbensRubin1997,FrangakisRubin2002}. Selection and marginal-treatment-effect approaches provide related ways to organize heterogeneity in treatment take-up and response \citep{HeckmanVytlacil2005}. The analysis here keeps the complier-ratio target and studies its transported version when outcomes, receipt, and encouragement are observed in a source population and covariates are observed in a target population.

A second line of work develops inference for ratios under weak first stages. Anderson--Rubin and Fieller methods provide classical confidence sets for ratio parameters \citep{AndersonRubin1949,Fieller1954}; \citet{GleserHwang1987} and \citet{Dufour1997} characterize fundamental constraints on bounded expected length and uniform confidence coverage for ratio-type problems. Weak-instrument asymptotics and robust IV procedures were developed by \citet{BoundJaegerBaker1995}, \citet{StaigerStock1997}, \citet{StockWrightYogo2002}, \citet{Moreira2003}, \citet{AndrewsMoreiraStock2006}, \citet{Mikusheva2010}, and \citet{AndrewsStockSun2019}, with modern refinements including \citet{BertanhaMoreira2020}. Recent work on LATE confidence sets under weak identification includes \citet{Ma2023} and \citet{SmuclerLanniMasip2025}. The present paper uses the same ratio-inference logic to study honest expected length for a bounded transported complier effect, with the first-stage strength summarized together with transport-weight dispersion.

The transportability and generalizability literature studies how causal conclusions move from an observed population to a target population. Early econometric work on external validity includes \citet{HotzImbensMortimer2005}; graphical and causal-transport formulations are developed by \citet{PearlBareinboim2011}, \citet{BareinboimPearl2013}, and \citet{BareinboimPearl2016}. Weighting and sampling-score approaches for trial generalization and transport appear in \citet{ColeStuart2010}, \citet{StuartColeBradshawLeaf2011}, \citet{Tipton2013}, \citet{Dahabreh2020}, \citet{DahabrehPetitoRobertson2020}, and \citet{Nguyen2017}. Efficiency and minimax theory for transported average treatment effects is studied by \citet{ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2024}. This paper focuses on the transported complier ratio, where the denominator is itself a transported first-stage contrast and the target population enters through a covariate-density ratio.

The closest transported-noncompliance benchmarks are \citet{RudolphVanderLaan2017} and \citet{ChenHuang2025}. \citet{RudolphVanderLaan2017} analyze transported stochastic direct and indirect effects in encouragement-design settings, while \citet{ChenHuang2025} study transported CACE identification and regular estimation. Related two-sample weak-IV work by \citet{ChoiGuShen2018} and \citet{ChoiShen2019} develops weak-instrument-robust inference when information is split across samples. Recent work by \citet{Ross2026} and \citet{Ren2025} studies transported adherence and extrapolated LATE-type targets, while \citet{Rudolph2025} contributes recent efficiency theory for transported average treatment effects. The contribution here is the fixed-geometry honest expected-length characterization for bounded transported complier-ratio confidence sets, together with oracle and finite-cell target-weight-learning procedures attaining the same rate order.

\subsection{Full-data, sampling, and instrument structure}

At each index, the \leanref{S-1}{two-population full-data potential-outcome world} is carried by the full-data law \leanref{sym:P_n^F}{\ensuremath{P_n^F}}. The population indicator \leanref{sym:S}{\ensuremath{S}} distinguishes the source and target populations; \leanref{sym:X}{\ensuremath{X}} denotes covariates; \leanref{sym:D(z)}{\ensuremath{D(z)}} and \leanref{sym:Y(d)}{\ensuremath{Y(d)}} denote treatment-receipt and outcome potential variables. The first condition fixes the bounded binary structure used by the ratio problem.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:full-data-support}[Full-data support]
The full-data vector is \((S,X,D(0),D(1),Y(0),Y(1))\), where \(X\) is the covariate taking values in \(\mathcal X\). The full-data law \(P_n^F\) satisfies
\[
P_n^F\{S\in\{0,1\},\ D(0),D(1)\in\{0,1\},\ Y(0),Y(1)\in[0,1]\}=1.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:full-data-support} is specific to this analysis: it encodes binary receipt, bounded outcomes, and the two-population indexing in a single support condition. The bounded outcome scale is the source of the compact causal range established below.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:population-presence}[Population presence]
For each \(s\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
P_n^F(S=s)>0.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:population-presence} is the standard two-population support condition, ensuring that both source and target conditional laws are well defined \citep{ChenHuang2025}. Sampling then proceeds from the observed source law \leanref{sym:P_S}{\ensuremath{P_S}} and target covariate law \leanref{sym:P_T}{\ensuremath{P_T}}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:two-sample-array}[Two-sample array]
The source observed law \(P_S\), target covariate law \(P_T\), target sample size \(N_n\), and asymptotic target-to-source sample-size ratio \(c\) satisfy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Source law.)} For every \(n\), \(P_S\) at array index \(n\) is a probability measure on the source observation \(O_i^S\).
\item \textbf{(Target law.)} For every \(n\), \(P_T\) at array index \(n\) is a probability measure on the target covariate \(X_j^T\).
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} \(0<c\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\end{itemize}
\end{assumptionv}

The \leanref{S-2}{observed two-sample triangular-array sampling world} comprises source observations \leanref{sym:O_i^S}{\ensuremath{O_i^S}}, target covariate draws \leanref{sym:X_j^T}{\ensuremath{X_j^T}}, target sample size \leanref{sym:N_n}{\ensuremath{N_n}}, and sample-size limit \leanref{sym:c}{\ensuremath{c}}. \Cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array} is the standard independent two-sample triangular-array sampling condition for transported CACE settings \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

The next group imposes the source encouragement-design restrictions. Let \leanref{sym:e}{\ensuremath{e(X)}} be the source assignment propensity and \leanref{sym:\varepsilon}{\ensuremath{\varepsilon}} the overlap constant.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:instrument-overlap}[Instrument overlap]
For the source assignment propensity \(e(X)\) and instrument-overlap constant \(\varepsilon\), \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), and
\[
\varepsilon\le e(X)\le 1-\varepsilon
\]
\(P_S^X\)-almost surely, where \(P_S^X\) denotes the covariate marginal of the source law \(P_S\).
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap} is the standard instrument positivity condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}. It keeps the inverse-propensity source contrasts controlled over the source covariate marginal \leanref{sym:P_S^X}{\ensuremath{P_S^X}}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:source-observation}[Source observation]
Conditional on \(S=1\), the observed source law satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Assignment propensity.)} \(P(Z=1\mid X,S=1)=e(X)\).
\item \textbf{(Treatment receipt.)} \(D=D(Z)\).
\item \textbf{(Observed outcome.)} \(Y=Y(D)\).
\end{itemize}
\end{assumptionv}

With source encouragement \leanref{sym:Z}{\ensuremath{Z}}, observed receipt \leanref{sym:D}{\ensuremath{D}}, and observed outcome \leanref{sym:Y}{\ensuremath{Y}}, \cref{obj:ass:source-observation} is the standard source assignment and consistency condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}. It connects the observed source variables to the potential-outcome objects in \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iv-randomization}[Instrument randomization]
Conditional on \((X,S=1)\),
\[
Z\perp (Y(0),Y(1),D(0),D(1)).
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:iv-randomization} is the standard conditional instrument exchangeability condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}. It makes the source encouragement contrast interpretable within covariate strata.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iv-exclusion}[Instrument exclusion]
For each \(z,s\in\{0,1\}\), under the full-data law \(P_n^F\),
\[
Y^Z(z)=Y(D(z))
\]
almost surely conditional on \(S=s\).
\end{assumptionv}

The assignment-generated outcome \leanref{sym:Y^Z(z)}{\ensuremath{Y^Z(z)}} satisfies the standard instrument exclusion restriction in \cref{obj:ass:iv-exclusion} \citep{ChenHuang2025}. The restriction ties assignment effects on outcomes to assignment effects on treatment receipt.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:iv-monotonicity}[Instrument monotonicity]
For each \(s\in\{0,1\}\), under the full-data law \(P_n^F\),
\[
D(1)\ge D(0)
\]
almost surely conditional on \(S=s\).
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:iv-monotonicity} is the standard monotonicity condition for the encouragement design \citep{ChenHuang2025}. Together, \cref{obj:ass:source-observation,obj:ass:iv-randomization,obj:ass:iv-exclusion,obj:ass:iv-monotonicity} supply the IV structure used to read source reduced-form and receipt contrasts as complier-weighted quantities.

\subsection{Transport and dispersion}

Transport is organized through the covariate space \leanref{sym:\mathcal X}{\ensuremath{\mathcal X}} and the density ratio \leanref{sym:w}{\ensuremath{w(X)}} from the target covariate law to the source covariate marginal. The \leanref{S-3}{transport and overlap geometry world} collects the source marginal, density ratio, and propensity array that govern extrapolation from the source sample to the target population.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:outcome-transport}[Outcome transport]
The source conditional outcome contrast is
\[
\Delta_Y(x)
=
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=1\right].
\]
For \(P_T\)-almost every \(x\), where \(P_T\) is the target covariate law,
\[
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=0\right]
=
\Delta_Y(x).
\]
The transported reduced-form outcome mean is \(\mu_{Y,n}:=\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}[\Delta_Y(X)]\).
\end{assumptionv}

The source conditional outcome contrast \leanref{sym:\Delta_Y}{\ensuremath{\Delta_Y(x)}} is transported conditionally in \cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport}. This is the standard conditional transport of the assignment-outcome contrast condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:receipt-transport}[Receipt transport]
The source conditional receipt contrast is
\[
\Delta_D(x)
=
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[D(1)-D(0)\mid X=x,S=1\right].
\]
Under the full-data law \(P_n^F\),
\[
\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}\!\left[
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[D(1)-D(0)\mid X,S=0\right]
\right]
=
\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}\!\left[\Delta_D(X)\right].
\]
This common value is the transported first-stage mean \(\mu_n:=\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}[\Delta_D(X)]\).
\end{assumptionv}

The source conditional receipt contrast \leanref{sym:\Delta_D}{\ensuremath{\Delta_D(x)}} enters through the integrated first-stage transport condition in \cref{obj:ass:receipt-transport}. This is the standard integrated transport of the first stage \citep{ChenHuang2025}, matching the target average receipt contrast to the transported source contrast.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:target-complier-positivity}[Target complier positivity]
Under the full-data law \(P_n^F\),
\[
P_n^F\!\left(D(1)>D(0)\mid S=0\right)>0.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:target-complier-positivity} is the standard positive target complier share condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}. It gives a positive denominator for the transported complier ratio.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:transport-domination}[Transport domination]
The target covariate law \(P_T\) and the source covariate marginal \(P_S^X\) are laws on the covariate space \(\mathcal X\), and \(P_T\) is absolutely continuous with respect to \(P_S^X\):
\[
P_T\ll P_S^X.
\]
We write
\[
w:=\frac{dP_T}{dP_S^X}
\]
for the resulting target-to-source covariate density ratio.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:transport-domination} is the standard transport covariate-support inclusion condition \citep{ChenHuang2025}. It provides the density-ratio representation used by the transported moments.

The remaining conditions are specific to this analysis. They encode the weight-dispersion and weak-first-stage array along which the expected-length results are evaluated.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:weight-envelope}[Weight envelope]
The target-to-source covariate density ratio satisfies
\[
0 \le w(X) \le 2k_n
\]
\(P_S\)-almost surely, where \(k_n\) is the transport-weight envelope scale.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope} is specific to this analysis. It bounds the pointwise transport weight by the envelope and cell-count scale \leanref{sym:k_n}{\ensuremath{k_n}}, allowing target reweighting while keeping a finite deterministic envelope.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:weight-second-moment}[Weight second moment]
The second moment of the transport weight,
\[
\kappa_n:=\mathbb E_S\!\left[w(X)^2\right],
\]
satisfies
\[
\kappa_n \le k_n .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The second moment \leanref{sym:\kappa_n}{\ensuremath{\kappa_n}} is the Kish dispersion scale of the transport weights. \Cref{obj:ass:weight-second-moment} is specific to this analysis and links dispersion to the same scale that controls the weight envelope.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:degrading-array}[Degrading array rates]
The array satisfies the following rate conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Envelope scale.)} \(k_n \to \infty\).
\item \textbf{(Sampling scale.)} \(k_n=o(n^{1/2})\).
\item \textbf{(First-stage strength.)} \(\mu_n\to0\).
\end{itemize}
\end{assumptionv}

The transported first-stage mean \leanref{sym:\mu_n}{\ensuremath{\mu_n}} is allowed to drift toward zero under \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array}. The condition is specific to this analysis and defines the weak-first-stage asymptotic regime while keeping the weight scale sub-root in the source sample size.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:finite-cell-source}[Uniform finite-cell source]
The source covariate marginal \(P_S^X\) is supported on exactly \(k_n\) measurable cells, assigns mass \(1/k_n\) to each cell, and satisfies
\[
e(X)=\frac12
\]
\(P_S\)-almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source} is specific to this analysis. It defines the uniform finite-cell design used for the feasible target-weight-learning construction, with equal source cell masses and a known balanced assignment propensity.

\subsection{Model classes, estimand, and risk}

The main model class collects the support, sampling, IV, transport, and dispersion restrictions above.

\begin{definitionv}{def:model-class}[Transported complier-effect model class \(\mathcal P_n\)]
For each \(n\), the transported complier-effect model class is
\[
\mathcal P_n
=
\left\{
P=(P_n^F,e):
P \text{ satisfies the conditions below at index } n
\right\}.
\]
The conditions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Full-data and population structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support,obj:ass:population-presence}.
\item \textbf{(Sampling structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument and observation structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:source-observation,obj:ass:iv-randomization,obj:ass:iv-exclusion,obj:ass:iv-monotonicity}.
\item \textbf{(Transport and target-complier structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport,obj:ass:receipt-transport,obj:ass:target-complier-positivity,obj:ass:transport-domination}.
\item \textbf{(Envelope and degradation structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment,obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

The transported complier-effect model class \leanref{sym:\mathcal P_n}{\ensuremath{\mathcal P_n}} is the domain for the oracle confidence-set analysis. Its elements bundle a full-data law and source propensity satisfying the restrictions at a fixed array index.

\begin{definitionv}{def:finite-cell-class}[Finite-cell submodel \(\mathcal N_n\)]
For each \(n\), the finite-cell submodel is
\[
\mathcal N_n
=
\left\{
P=(P_n^F,e):
P \text{ satisfies the conditions below at index } n
\right\}.
\]
The conditions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Full-data and population structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support,obj:ass:population-presence}.
\item \textbf{(Sampling structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument and observation structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:source-observation,obj:ass:iv-randomization,obj:ass:iv-exclusion,obj:ass:iv-monotonicity}.
\item \textbf{(Transport and target-complier structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport,obj:ass:receipt-transport,obj:ass:target-complier-positivity,obj:ass:transport-domination}.
\item \textbf{(Envelope and degradation structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment,obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\item \textbf{(Finite-cell source structure.)} \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}.
\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

The finite-cell submodel \leanref{sym:\mathcal N_n}{\ensuremath{\mathcal N_n}} adds the uniform source-cell structure in \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}. It is the benchmark for procedures that learn target weights from the covariate-only target sample.

Before defining the expected-length criterion, we record the identification and range statement supplied by the assumptions. The \leanref{S-4}{identification and effective-strength scalar world} uses the transported reduced-form outcome mean \leanref{sym:\mu_{Y,n}}{\ensuremath{\mu_{Y,n}}}, first-stage mean \(\mu_n\), and target complier contrast \leanref{sym:\theta_T}{\ensuremath{\theta_T}}.

\begin{theoremv}{prop:compact-causal-range}[Compact causal range]
Fix \(n\), \(P\), \(N_n\), \(k_n\), \(c\), and the instrument-overlap constant \(\varepsilon\). Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Model class.)} \(P\) belongs to the transported complier-effect model class \(\mathcal P_n\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class}.
\item \textbf{(Source contrasts.)} The source conditional outcome contrast \(\Delta_Y(x)\) and the source conditional receipt contrast \(\Delta_D(x)\) are measurable and integrable with respect to the source covariate marginal \(P_S^X\).
\end{itemize}
Then \(\Delta_Y\) and \(\Delta_D\) represent the observed source contrasts: for every measurable covariate set \(A\),
\[
\int_{\{O^S:X\in A\}} H(O^S)Y\,dP_S
=
\int_A \Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x),
\qquad
\int_{\{O^S:X\in A\}} H(O^S)D\,dP_S
=
\int_A \Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
Moreover, with
\[
\mu_{Y,n}
=
\int \bigl(Y(1)-Y(0)\bigr)\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}\,dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=0)
\]
and
\[
\mu_n
=
P_n^F\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\},
\]
the transported reduced-form and first-stage means satisfy
\[
\int w(x)\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=\mu_{Y,n},
\qquad
\int w(x)\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=\mu_n.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}
=
\theta_T
=
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0,\ S=0\right]
\in \Theta=[-1,1].
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} establishes that the transported source reduced-form and first-stage contrasts identify the target complier-conditional contrast, and that the contrast lies in the compact parameter space \([-1,1]\). The intuition is direct: bounded potential outcomes place every complier outcome contrast in the same bounded interval, while monotone binary receipt turns the transported first stage into the target complier share.

The confidence-set definitions use the compact parameter space \leanref{sym:\Theta}{\ensuremath{\Theta}}. The \leanref{S-5}{procedure and minimax-risk world} treats a confidence-set sequence as a random subset of this space, with the oracle version allowed to use the transport weights and assignment propensity.

\begin{definitionv}{def:oracle-honesty}[Oracle honesty class $\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}$, estimand $\theta_T$, and space $\Theta$]
Let the target complier estimand and compact parameter space be
\[
\theta_T=\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n},
\qquad
\Theta=[-1,1].
\]
For a noncoverage level \(\alpha\), the oracle honest confidence-set class is
\[
\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}
=
\left\{
(C_n)_{n\ge 1} :
C_n
=
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right)
\subseteq \Theta,\ 
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
P\{\theta_T\in C_n\}
\ge 1-\alpha
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The oracle class \leanref{sym:\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}{\ensuremath{\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}} formalizes honest coverage uniformly over \(\mathcal P_n\) for random sets \leanref{sym:C_n}{\ensuremath{C_n}}. The noncoverage level is \leanref{sym:\alpha}{\ensuremath{\alpha}}.

\begin{definitionv}{def:frontier-risk}[Frontier risk $R((C_n),t_0)$]
For a confidence set \(C_n\subseteq\Theta\), let \(\lambda(C_n)\) denote its Lebesgue length restricted to \(\Theta\). The effective identification strength is
\[
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}.
\]
Fix a threshold \(t_0>0\) and let \((C_n)_{n\ge 1}\) be an oracle procedure: each
\[
C_n=C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right)\subseteq\Theta
\]
is formed from the observed samples together with the transport weight \(w\) and the propensity \(e\). For each fixed measurable pair \((w,e)\), the sample-by-parameter graph \(\{(\text{sample},\vartheta):\vartheta\in C_n\}\) is measurable; and under each law in \(\mathcal P_n\), replacing \(w\) by another version of that law's transport weight, evaluated at the true propensity, leaves \(C_n\) almost surely unchanged. The frontier risk of \((C_n)_{n\ge1}\) at \(t_0\) is
\[
R((C_n),t_0)
=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
Here the supremum over an empty set of laws is zero, so a sample size at which no law meets the strength restriction contributes nothing to the frontier risk.
\end{definitionv}

The effective identification strength \leanref{sym:t_n}{\ensuremath{t_n}} combines source sample size, transported first-stage magnitude, and weight dispersion. For a fixed threshold \leanref{sym:t_0}{\ensuremath{t_0}}, the risk \leanref{sym:R}{\ensuremath{R((C_n),t_0)}} measures limiting worst-case expected length over laws whose effective strength exceeds that threshold.

\begin{definitionv}{def:oracle-value}[Oracle value $V^\star(t_0)$]
\[
V^\star(t_0)
=
\inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}
R((C_n),t_0).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The oracle value \leanref{sym:V^\star}{\ensuremath{V^\star(t_0)}} is the minimax expected length over oracle-honest procedures. It is the population-weight-known benchmark for the rate results in the next section.

\begin{definitionv}{def:finite-cell-oracle-value}[Finite-cell oracle value $V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)$]
\[
V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)
=
\inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
\end{definitionv}

The finite-cell oracle value \leanref{sym:V_{\mathcal N}^\star}{\ensuremath{V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)}} evaluates the same oracle procedures on the finite-cell submodel. It separates the statistical effect of the finite-cell geometry from the additional task of learning target weights.

\begin{definitionv}{def:finite-cell-feasible-honesty}[Finite-cell feasible class $\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}$]
\[
\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}
=
\left\{
(C_n)_{n\ge 1} :
C_n
=
C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\right)
\subseteq \Theta,\ 
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n}
P\{\theta_T\in C_n\}
\ge 1-\alpha
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The feasible finite-cell class \leanref{sym:\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}{\ensuremath{\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}} restricts procedures to the observed source sample and target covariate sample. This class is the setting for the finite-cell target-weight-learning construction in \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}.

\subsection{Reading the design restrictions}

The feasible classes carry restrictions worth separating into design features, approximations, and
technical regimes. Uniform source cells of mass \(1/k_n\) and balanced assignment describe a stratified
experiment in which the sampling frame fixes equal-sized strata and randomizes within them; this is a
design the experimenter can implement, not an assumption about nature. In the regular-cell class the
cell probabilities \(q_{x,n}\) and the cell-varying propensity are supplied by that same frame — they
are known because the design records them — so the result should be read as covering registry- or
survey-based settings where stratum sizes and assignment rates are administrative facts rather than
quantities to be estimated. The growth condition \(k_n=o(\sqrt n)\) is the technical regime: it keeps
the number of cells small enough that per-cell frequencies concentrate fast enough for the collision
estimator to learn the target weights, and it is the condition under which learning the weights costs
nothing in rate. The theorems accordingly cover designs with known strata, supplied assignment probabilities, and cell counts obeying \(k_n=o(\sqrt n)\), the regime in which the cell-frequency and collision terms calibrate at the oracle rate.


\section{Oracle minimax rates}

The expected-length criterion in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk} reduces the transported weak-first-stage problem to the scalar
\[
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}.
\]
The numerator is the squared transported first stage scaled by the source sample size, and the denominator is the Kish dispersion of the target-to-source weights. Thus a design with the same transported first stage can be statistically weaker when the target population is represented by a more uneven reweighting of the source covariate distribution.

The oracle benchmark treats the target-to-source density ratio and source assignment propensity as known. This leads to an Anderson--Rubin and Fieller style inversion of a transported reduced-form score \citep{AndersonRubin1949,Fieller1954}. The candidate value is retained when the weighted source score is small relative to the oracle weight-dispersion radius.

\begin{algorithmv}{def:inversion-handle}[Score-inversion set $C_n$]
With oracle-known \(w\) and \(e\), define \(C_n\) by the following source-sample inversion procedure.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each \(\vartheta\in\Theta\), define the inverse-propensity score
\[
\psi_i(\vartheta)
=
w(X_i)\left\{\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)}\right\}(Y_i-\vartheta D_i).
\]

\item Define the empirical score mean
\[
\widehat m_n(\vartheta)
=
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \psi_i(\vartheta).
\]

\item Define the oracle critical radius
\[
r_n
=
L_\alpha
\left\{
\frac{1}{n}\cdot \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n w(X_i)^2
\right\}^{1/2},
\]
where \(L_\alpha\) is the critical constant supplied to the procedure at noncoverage level \(\alpha\).

\item Output the score-inversion confidence set
\[
C_n
=
\left\{
\vartheta\in\Theta:
\left|\widehat m_n(\vartheta)\right|\le r_n
\right\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithmv}

\Cref{obj:def:inversion-handle} is the transported analogue of weak-IV score inversion. For a fixed candidate \(\vartheta\), the score tests whether the transported reduced-form contrast is compatible with \(\vartheta\) times the transported first-stage contrast. The critical radius has order \(\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\), so it expands with the empirical second moment of the oracle weights.

The lower bound applies to the oracle value over the full transported model and to the finite-cell oracle value. It shows that the same effective-strength index governing the score radius also governs the minimax expected length.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:oracle-converse}[Oracle lower frontier]
Fix \(c>0\), an instrument-overlap constant \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\), and a noncoverage level \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). There exist constants \(c_0>0\) and \(t_c>0\), depending only on \((\alpha,\varepsilon,c)\), such that the following holds for every measurable covariate space \(\mathcal X\).

Let \(N_n\) and \(k_n\) be integer sequences satisfying:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Target-source ratio.)} \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Positive cells.)} \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\).
\item \textbf{(Diverging cells.)} \(k_n\to\infty\).
\item \textbf{(Sub-root growth.)} \(k_n/\sqrt n\to 0\).
\item \textbf{(Measurable carrier.)} For each \(n\), \(\mathcal X\) contains a measurable \(k_n\)-cell carrier.
\item \textbf{(Transported-IV model.)} Every array in the transported complier-effect class \(\mathcal P_n\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class} satisfies \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support,obj:ass:population-presence,obj:ass:two-sample-array,obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:source-observation,obj:ass:iv-randomization,obj:ass:iv-exclusion,obj:ass:iv-monotonicity,obj:ass:outcome-transport,obj:ass:receipt-transport,obj:ass:target-complier-positivity,obj:ass:transport-domination,obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment,obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\item \textbf{(Finite-cell source.)} Every array in the finite-cell class \(\mathcal N_n\) of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class} satisfies \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}.
\end{itemize}

Then, for every effective-strength threshold \(t_0>0\),
\[
  c_0 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
  \le V^\star(t_0)
\]
and
\[
  c_0 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
  \le V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0),
\]
where \(V^\star(t_0)\) and \(V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)\) are the oracle values in \cref{obj:def:oracle-value,obj:def:finite-cell-oracle-value}. Moreover, whenever \(0<t_0\le t_c\),
\[
  c_0 \le V^\star(t_0).
\]
\end{theoremv}

The converse in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse} gives the information-theoretic side of the rate. Above a fixed threshold \(t_0\), honest expected length is bounded below by the smaller of a constant and \(t_0^{-1/2}\). At very small effective strength, the final display gives the order-one elbow: honest oracle procedures retain nonvanishing worst-case expected length.

The matching upper bound is attained by the oracle-known-weight score-inversion rule, which takes the true transport-weight version and the source propensity as oracle inputs. Writing
\[
H_i=\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)},
\]
the procedure can be expressed as an inversion of the weighted reduced-form mean against the weighted first-stage mean.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment}[Oracle score inversion]
Let \(N_n\) and \(k_n\) be integer sequences, and let \(c\), \(\varepsilon\), and \(\alpha\) be constants.  Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sampling ratio.)} \(c>0\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap and coverage constants.)} \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).
\item \textbf{(Envelope scale.)} \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\), \(k_n\to\infty\), and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\).
\item \textbf{(Admissible geometry.)} The measurable covariate space admits a deterministic geometry \(\mathfrak g\) satisfying \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} with envelope scale \(k_n\) and overlap constant \(\varepsilon\).
\item \textbf{(Model-class implications.)} For every \(n\) and every transported array \(P\), membership of \(P\) in the transported complier-effect model class \(\mathcal P_n\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class} implies \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array,obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment,obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\end{itemize}
Define
\[
L_\alpha=\left(\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
H_i=\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)},
\]
and
\[
\widehat A_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n w(X_i)H_iY_i,
\qquad
\widehat B_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n w(X_i)H_iD_i,
\qquad
\widehat\kappa_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n w(X_i)^2 .
\]
Let the score-inversion confidence set be
\[
C_n
=
\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
\left|\widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n\right|
\le
L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}
\right\}.
\]
Then the sequence \((C_n)\) is oracle honest, \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}, and for every fixed effective-strength threshold \(t_0>0\),
\[
R((C_n),t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\qquad
C_0=\max\left\{2,4L_\alpha+\frac8{\varepsilon^2}\right\}.
\]
Consequently, for the oracle minimax value \(V^\star(t_0)\) of \cref{obj:def:oracle-value},
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V^\star(t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
and for every admissible geometry \(\mathfrak g'\in\mathcal G\) the fixed-geometry value of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} obeys the same two-sided bounds,
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g'}^\star(t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} supplies the constructive half of the oracle frontier. The set is always restricted to \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), and its random width is controlled by the same dispersion-normalized standard-error scale that appears in \(t_n\). Combining \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} yields the matched oracle rate \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) for \(V^\star(t_0)\), up to constants depending on the stated coverage, overlap, and sampling-ratio inputs.

It is useful to separate the causal array from the deterministic geometry that carries the source covariate law, transport weights, and source propensity. The next definitions fix this geometry and evaluate honest expected length conditionally on it.

\begin{definitionv}{def:admissible-geometry-class}[Admissible geometry class \(\mathcal G\)]
\(\mathcal G\) is the class of deterministic arrays
\[
\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}
\]
such that, for each \(n\),
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Source marginal.)} \(P_S^X\) is a probability law on \(\mathcal X\).
\item \textbf{(Instrument overlap.)} The source assignment propensity satisfies
\[
e:\mathcal X\to[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon].
\]
\item \textbf{(Transport weights.)} The target-to-source covariate density ratio satisfies
\[
w:\mathcal X\to[0,2k_n],
\qquad
\mathbb E_S[w(X)]=1.
\]
\item \textbf{(Dispersion.)} The second moment of the transport weight is
\[
\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]\le k_n.
\]
\item \textbf{(Target law.)} For every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
P_T(A)=\mathbb E_S[w(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in A\}].
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} records the deterministic ingredients shared by the lower and upper bounds: an overlapped assignment propensity, a normalized target-to-source density ratio, a second-moment bound, and the induced target covariate law. Holding these objects fixed isolates the weak-first-stage and outcome-tilt variation inside the transported model class.

\begin{definitionv}{def:fixed-geometry-slice}[Fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\)]
For \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\),
\[
\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)
=
\{P\in\mathcal P_n:(P_S^X,w,e)\text{ equals the index-}n\text{ component of }\mathfrak g\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} restricts attention to laws whose source covariate marginal, transport weight, and propensity match the chosen geometry. The causal contrasts and first-stage strength vary within that slice subject to the model restrictions.

\begin{definitionv}{def:fixed-geometry-honesty}[Fixed-geometry oracle class \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\)]
For \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) as in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class},
\[
\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)
=
\left\{
(C_n)_{n\ge1}:
C_n=C_n\!\left((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\right)\subseteq\Theta,\ 
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}
P(\theta_T\in C_n)\ge1-\alpha
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The fixed-geometry honesty class in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-honesty} requires uniform asymptotic coverage only over the slice in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}. The procedure remains oracle in the same sense as \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}: it may use the fixed weight and propensity inputs.

\begin{definitionv}{def:fixed-geometry-value}[Fixed-geometry value \(V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\)]
For \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) as in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class},
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
=
\inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
\end{definitionv}

The value in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} is the conditional minimax expected length at effective-strength threshold \(t_0\). It keeps the weighting and overlap geometry fixed while retaining the same length and strength criteria used in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk}.

A transparent special case is the absence of covariate shift. When the density ratio is identically one, the target and source covariate laws coincide and the effective strength becomes the familiar sample-size-scaled squared first stage.

\begin{theoremv}{prop:no-shift-reduction}[No-shift reduction]
Fix a measurable covariate space and deterministic arrays \(N_n\), \(k_n\), and \(\mathfrak g\). Suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} \(c>0\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Coverage and overlap constants.)} \(0<\alpha<1\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\).
\item \textbf{(Cell-scale regime.)} For every \(n\), \(k_n>0\), with \(k_n\to\infty\) and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\).
\item \textbf{(Admissible geometry.)} The geometry \(\mathfrak g\) belongs to the admissible class \(\mathcal G\) in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class}.
\item \textbf{(No covariate shift.)} For every \(n\), \(w_n(X)=1\) \(P_{S,n}^X\)-almost surely.
\end{itemize}
Then, for every \(n\), the geometry has
\[
\kappa_n=\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)=1
\]
and \(P_{T,n}=P_{S,n}^X\). Moreover, for every transported array \(P_n^F\) in the fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice},
\[
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}=n\mu_n^2 .
\]
Finally, for every \(t_0>0\), define
\[
L_\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}},
\qquad
c_\alpha=\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16},
\qquad
C_\alpha=\max\left\{2,\,4L_\alpha+\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2}\right\}.
\]
The fixed-geometry minimax value \(V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\) in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} satisfies
\[
c_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
\le
C_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction} anchors the transported rate to the single-population weak-ratio scale. With \(w=1\), there is no Kish deflation and the denominator of \(t_n\) is one. The displayed bounds then express the same order-one elbow and \(t_0^{-1/2}\) decay in terms of \(n\mu_n^2\).

The fixed-geometry theorem gives the conditional version of the full oracle characterization. It shows that the lower and upper bounds hold uniformly over admissible deterministic geometry arrays.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}[Fixed-geometry length frontier]
Let \(c>0\), let \(N_n\) be target sample sizes with \(N_n/n\to c\), let \(k_n\) be positive integers with \(k_n\to\infty\) and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\), let \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\), and let \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  Let \(\mathfrak g\) be a deterministic geometry array satisfying \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} at scale \(k_n\) and overlap parameter \(\varepsilon\).  Assume that, for every \(n\), every law in the fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} satisfies
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Two samples.)} \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array} with target-to-source ratio \(c\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap.)} \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap} with overlap parameter \(\varepsilon\).
\item \textbf{(Envelope.)} \cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope} at scale \(k_n\).
\item \textbf{(Second moment.)} \cref{obj:ass:weight-second-moment} at scale \(k_n\).
\item \textbf{(Degradation.)} \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array} at scale \(k_n\).
\end{itemize}
Define
\[
L_\alpha=\left(\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
c_\alpha=\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16},
\qquad
C_\alpha=\max\{2,4L_\alpha+8\varepsilon^{-2}\}.
\]
Then, for every fixed effective-strength threshold \(t_0>0\), the fixed-geometry minimax expected-length value \(V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} satisfies
\[
c_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
\le
C_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
The constants \(c_\alpha\) and \(C_\alpha\) depend only on \(\alpha\) and \(\varepsilon\), uniformly over admissible deterministic geometry arrays \(\mathfrak g\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} establishes the same rate after conditioning on the source-covariate, transport-weight, and propensity array. The lower bound treats Kish dispersion as part of the information scale, and the upper bound is attained by the same score radius \(L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\) used in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment}. Together, the oracle, no-shift, and fixed-geometry results pin the minimax expected-length rate order, up to constants, as a function of the effective-strength threshold, within the procedure classes stated.


\section{Cell weight learning}

The oracle procedure in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} uses the target-to-source weights as inputs. This section turns that benchmark into a feasible finite-cell construction. In the uniform finite-cell experiment of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}, the target covariate sample estimates the cell probabilities directly, and the source sample estimates the cellwise reduced-form and first-stage contrasts. The resulting score inversion has the same expected-length order as the oracle benchmark, with the dispersion scale estimated from target-cell coincidences. This connects transported noncompliance inference to the weighting and two-sample transport ideas in \citet{RudolphVanderLaan2017}, \citet{ChenHuang2025}, and \citet{ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2024}, while retaining the weak-ratio inversion logic used in robust IV procedures \citep{ChoiGuShen2018,ChoiShen2019}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}[Finite-cell sample-only attainment]
Let \(N_n\) and \(k_n\) be positive integer sequences, let \(c,\varepsilon,\alpha\in\mathbb R\), and suppose the following conditions hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sampling ratio.)} \(c>0\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap and coverage levels.)} \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).
\item \textbf{(Cell growth.)} \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\), \(k_n\to\infty\), and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\).
\item \textbf{(Ambient carrier.)} For every \(n\), the measurable covariate space contains an injected copy of \(\{1,\ldots,k_n\}\) whose singleton images are measurable.
\item \textbf{(Finite-cell rows.)} For every row \(n\) and every transported array \(P\in\mathcal N_n\), with \(\mathcal N_n\) as in \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}, the row satisfies the two-sample condition in \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}, the uniform finite-cell source condition in \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}, and the degrading-array condition in \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\end{itemize}
Put
\[
B_c=32(1+c^{-1}),\qquad
L_{\alpha,c}=\left(\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2},\qquad
C_0=\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B_c\}.
\]
Then \(C_0>0\), and there exists a sample-only finite-cell confidence-set sequence \(C=(C_n)\) in the feasible class \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-feasible-honesty} such that, for every \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal N_n\), \(C_n\) agrees \(P\)-almost surely under the two-sample law with the finite-cell score-inversion rule using critical constant \(L_{\alpha,c}\). Equivalently, on the finite-cell experiment, this rule is given by
\[
H_i=2(2Z_i-1),\qquad
\widehat p_{x,n}=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{j=1}^{N_n}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=x\},
\]
and, for \(G\in\{Y,D\}\),
\[
\widehat m_{G,n}(x)=\frac{k_n}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i,\qquad
\widehat M_{G,n}=\sum_{x=1}^{k_n}\widehat p_{x,n}\widehat m_{G,n}(x).
\]
For \(N_n\ge2\), define
\[
\widehat\kappa_{U,n}
=\frac{k_n}{N_n(N_n-1)}
\sum_{j\ne\ell}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\},\qquad
\widehat K_n=1+\widehat\kappa_{U,n},
\]
and set
\[
C_n=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|
\le L_{\alpha,c}\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\right\},
\]
with \(C_n=\Theta\) at indices for which \(N_n<2\). For every \(t_0>0\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Moreover, for every \(t_0>0\), the feasible finite-cell honest minimax risk satisfies
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
\inf_{\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)].
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} establishes that empirical target cell frequencies can be substituted for oracle transport weights in the uniform finite-cell design while preserving the order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\). The statistic \(\widehat p_{x,n}\) learns the target mass of each cell; \(\widehat m_{G,n}(x)\) estimates the source encouragement contrast in that cell; and \(\widehat M_{G,n}\) transports those source contrasts to the target population. The U-statistic \(\widehat\kappa_{U,n}\) estimates the collision component of the weight dispersion, so the feasible radius uses the same strength scale that governs the oracle expected length.

The lower bound in \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} matches the oracle order from \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse} on the same finite-cell submodel. Thus the target-weight-learning step contributes a fully sample-based construction whose honest expected length remains governed by the effective-strength threshold. The condition \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\) is the cell-growth regime under which the theorem calibrates the source-cell contrast error and the target-frequency error at the displayed rate.

The regular extension allows the source cells to have known nonuniform probabilities. This formulation keeps the source cell masses and the assignment propensity available to the procedure, matching settings in which the sampling frame or design supplies these quantities, as in standard inverse-probability and semiparametric weighting constructions \citep{HiranoImbensRidder2003,RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,VanDerLaanRubin2006,Chernozhukov2018}.

\begin{definitionv}{def:regular-finite-cell-class}[Regular finite-cell class \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\)]
\[
\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}
=
\left\{
P\in\mathcal P_n:
\begin{array}{l}
P_S^X \text{ is supported on exactly } k_n \text{ measurable cells},\\
q_{x,n}=P_S^X(X \text{ lies in cell } x),\\
c_-/k_n\le q_{x,n}\le c_+/k_n \text{ for every } x
\end{array}
\right\},
\]
where \(0<c_-\le 1\le c_+<\infty\) are fixed.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class} replaces equal source-cell masses with regular cell masses bounded above and below by constants times \(1/k_n\). The known probabilities \(q_{x,n}\) let the source contrast estimator normalize each cell by its actual source mass, while the constants \(c_-\) and \(c_+\) keep the finite-cell geometry comparable across cells.

\begin{definitionv}{def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty}[Regular-cell feasible honesty class \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\)]
\[
\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}
=
\left\{
(C_n)_{n\ge 1}:
C_n
=
C_n\left(
(O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,
(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},
(q_{x,n})_{x=1}^{k_n},e
\right)
\subseteq \Theta,\ 
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}}
P(\theta_T\in C_n)\ge 1-\alpha
\right\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty} records the information available to the regular-cell procedure: the two samples, the known source-cell probabilities, and the propensity. Honesty is uniform over the regular finite-cell class, using the same compact parameter space and target complier contrast introduced in \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty,obj:prop:compact-causal-range}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}[Regular-cell score attainment]
Fix \(c,\varepsilon,\alpha,c_-,c_+\in\mathbb R\), target sample sizes \(N_n\), and cell counts \(k_n\).  Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} \(0<c\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap and coverage levels.)} \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).
\item \textbf{(Regular cell constants.)} \(0<c_-\le 1\) and \(1\le c_+\).
\item \textbf{(Cell growth.)} \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\), \(k_n\to\infty\), and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\).
\item \textbf{(Measurable carrier.)} For every \(n\), the covariate carrier contains \(k_n\) injected measurable singleton cells.
\item \textbf{(Model restrictions.)} Every array \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) from \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class} with constants \(c,\varepsilon,c_-,c_+\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}, \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}, and \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\end{itemize}
On \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), let \(q_{x,n}=P_S^X(X=x)\) and define
\[
H_i=\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)}.
\]
For \(G\in\{Y,D\}\), set
\[
\widehat p_{x,n}=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{j=1}^{N_n}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=x\},
\qquad
\widehat m_{G,n}(x)=\frac1{nq_{x,n}}\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i,
\]
and
\[
\widehat M_{G,n}=\sum_{x=1}^{k_n}\widehat p_{x,n}\widehat m_{G,n}(x).
\]
For \(N_n\ge2\), set
\[
\widehat\kappa_{U,n}
=
\frac1{N_n(N_n-1)}\sum_{j\ne\ell}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}}{q_{X_j^T,n}},
\qquad
\widehat K_n=1+\widehat\kappa_{U,n},
\]
and put \(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}=\Theta\) when \(N_n<2\).  Let
\[
B_{\varepsilon,c}=8(\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1}).
\]
For every \(L\ge\{2B_{\varepsilon,c}/\alpha\}^{1/2}\), define
\[
C_{0}=\max\{2,4\sqrt2L+8B_{\varepsilon,c}\}.
\]
Then \(0<C_0\), and there exists a regular-cell procedure \(C=(C_n)_{n\ge1}\) such that, for every \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), \(C_n\) agrees almost surely under the two-sample law with
\[
C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}
=
\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|
\le L\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\right\}
\]
for \(N_n\ge2\), together with the preceding convention for \(N_n<2\).  This procedure belongs to \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) from \cref{obj:def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty}.  Moreover, for every fixed \(t_0>0\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
For every fixed \(t_0>0\), the minimax risk over \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) satisfies
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
\inf_{C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
Thus, with known regular source-cell probabilities and a known cell-varying propensity satisfying the stated overlap, the feasible regular-cell construction attains the oracle frontier order on \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} extends the sample-only construction to regular nonuniform source cells. The inverse-propensity score \(H_i\) now uses the known cell-varying assignment propensity, and the source contrast estimator rescales observations by \(q_{x,n}\). The dispersion estimator similarly weights target-cell coincidences by the known source mass of the matched cell. Under the displayed regularity and growth conditions, the resulting score-inversion confidence set is honest over \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) and has worst-case expected length bounded by a constant times \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\).

In the uniform finite-cell class and in the known regular nonuniform-cell class with known source cell probabilities and known cell-varying propensity, \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} show that empirical target-cell frequencies and collision dispersion estimates attain the oracle expected-length order. The finite-cell procedures use the target sample only through empirical cell frequencies and dispersion estimates, while the source sample supplies the outcome and receipt contrasts. In both finite-cell classes, the effective-strength scalar \(t_n\) remains the quantity that determines the transition between the compact-range regime and the square-root contraction regime.


\section{Discussion and limitations}

The results identify the effective-strength scalar \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\) as the organizing quantity for honest expected length in the transported complier-ratio problem. The lower and upper bounds in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} give the order
\[
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\]
for oracle honest procedures above a fixed threshold \(t_0\). Thus the expected-length curve has an order-one elbow: near the weak-identification region, the compact parameter range governs the achievable length, while stronger effective first stages support square-root contraction in the threshold scale.

The compact range is a substantive part of the conclusion rather than a normalization artifact. \Cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} identifies the target complier-conditional contrast as the ratio of the transported reduced-form outcome mean to the transported first-stage mean and places that contrast in \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). This bounded target space gives honest procedures a finite fallback length when the transported first stage is weak. It also makes the expected-length criterion in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk} interpretable on the causal scale of outcome contrasts, with \(\lambda(C_n)\) measuring the part of the confidence set that lies in the feasible causal range.

The role of transport enters through the Kish dispersion \(\kappa_n\). Fixed instrument overlap, encoded in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}, controls the source inverse-propensity scores, while \cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment} control the size and second moment of the target-to-source weights. The resulting scalar \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\) captures the way transported covariate reweighting reduces effective source information. \Cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction} makes this interpretation explicit: when \(w(X)=1\), the target covariate law equals the source covariate marginal, \(\kappa_n=1\), and the effective-strength scale reduces to \(n\mu_n^2\).

The oracle and feasible constructions also have the same statistical shape. The oracle score inversion in \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle,obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment} tests each candidate ratio by centering the transported score for \(Y-\vartheta D\), following the Anderson--Rubin and Fieller logic for weak ratios. The finite-cell procedures in \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment,obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} replace oracle weights with empirical target cell frequencies and use target-sample collision statistics to estimate the dispersion scale. Their matched lower and upper bounds show that, in the stated finite-cell designs, in the uniform finite-cell class with balanced assignment, and in the regular nonuniform-cell class with known cell probabilities and known cell-varying propensity, learning the target weights attains the same honest minimax expected-length order as the oracle benchmark.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

The feasible weight-learning results are stated for the uniform finite-cell class in \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class} and the known regular nonuniform-cell class in \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class}. Extending the same honest expected-length characterization to richer feasible weighting schemes, including continuous-covariate approximations and data-adaptive partitions, remains an open direction.

The finite-cell procedures use the assignment propensity information specified by their model classes. Future work can study joint learning of the source assignment propensity and target transport weights while preserving weak-ratio honesty. Such an extension would connect the present score-inversion approach with semiparametric nuisance-estimation tools, while retaining the finite-sample caution emphasized in weak-identification analysis \citep{ArmstrongKolesar2021}.

The analysis fixes the effective-strength threshold \(t_0\) when evaluating limiting expected length. Moving-threshold regimes, sharp constants, and refinements of the order-one elbow are natural next targets. These questions would sharpen the comparison between compact-range behavior and square-root contraction without changing the core scale \(n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\).

\section{Proofs, auxiliary arguments, and verification note}

This appendix collects the auxiliary statements used by the identification, oracle, fixed-geometry, and finite-cell arguments in the order in which the main text relies on them. The first group supplies the fixed-geometry least-favorable family and the inhabitation facts that make the lower-bound comparisons well posed on the stated rows.

The lower-bound argument works with a fixed deterministic geometry and varies the complier probability and outcome tilt inside that geometry. The construction below pins down the law used for that comparison.

\begin{definitionv}{def:least-favorable-witness}[Least-favorable law \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\)]
For \(\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}\in\mathcal G\), \(t_0>0\), and \(|h|\le H(t_0)\), \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) is the joint observed-data law generated by the following construction. Set
\[
\mu_n=\left(\frac{t_0\kappa_n}{n}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
p_n(x)=\frac{\mu_n w(x)}{\kappa_n},
\qquad
\rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{8},
\qquad
H(t_0)=\min\{1/4,\rho t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
where \(\kappa_n\) is the second moment of the transport weight and \(\alpha\) is the noncoverage level. For a source observation \(O^S=(X,Z,D,Y)\), define the inverse-propensity instrument score
\[
H(O^S)=\frac{Z}{e(X)}-\frac{1-Z}{1-e(X)}.
\]
Conditional on \(X=x\), assign compliance types by
\[
\Pr\{(D(0),D(1))=(0,1)\mid X=x\}=p_n(x),
\]
\[
\Pr\{(D(0),D(1))=(1,1)\mid X=x\}
=
\Pr\{(D(0),D(1))=(0,0)\mid X=x\}
=
\frac{1-p_n(x)}{2}.
\]
Set \(Y(0)=0\). For compliers,
\[
Y(1)\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2+h),
\]
and for always-takers and never-takers,
\[
Y(1)\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2).
\]
The same conditional full-data law is used in both populations, source encouragement is drawn with propensity \(e(X)\), and the resulting joint observed-data law together with the fixed oracle inputs is denoted by \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\).
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:least-favorable-witness} fixes the local experiment behind the oracle converse. The complier probability is proportional to the transport weight, so the transported first stage is calibrated to the effective-strength threshold, while the scalar tilt \(h\) moves the target complier contrast within the compact causal range.

\begin{definitionv}{def:geometry-handle}[Geometry handle at \((n,t_0,\alpha)\)]
Fix \(n\), a threshold \(t_0>0\), a noncoverage level \(0<\alpha<1\), and an admissible geometry array \(\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)\in\mathcal G\), and set
\[
\mu_n=\left(\frac{t_0\kappa_n}{n}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
p_n(x)=\frac{\mu_n w(x)}{\kappa_n},
\qquad
\rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{8}.
\]
Say that \(\mathfrak g\) admits a geometry handle at \((n,t_0,\alpha)\) when all of the following hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Valid compliance probabilities.)} \(0\le p_n(x)\le 1\) for every \(x\).
\item \textbf{(Transported first stage.)} \(\int w(x)p_n(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=\mu_n\).
\item \textbf{(Exact strength.)} \(n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n=t_0\).
\item \textbf{(Least-favourable family.)} There is a family \(h\mapsto Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) such that for every \(h\in\mathbb R\) with
\[
|h|\le \min\{1/4,\rho t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
the law \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) is a least-favourable fixed-geometry witness in the sense of \cref{obj:def:least-favorable-witness} and satisfies, relative to the member \(Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\) of that family at \(h=0\),
\[
1+\chi^2(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\Vert Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g})\le\exp(8t_0h^2),
\qquad
\|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le2\rho.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

The handle in \cref{obj:def:geometry-handle} packages the validity and distance calculations needed for the Le Cam comparison. Its chi-square and total-variation bounds are the probability-distance inputs used to translate honest coverage into expected-length lower bounds, following the weak-ratio logic associated with Anderson--Rubin and Fieller comparisons \citep{AndersonRubin1949,Fieller1954,GleserHwang1987,Dufour1997}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:geometry-handle-eventually-inhabited}[Eventual geometry-handle existence]
Assume the following conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} The asymptotic target-to-source sample-size ratio \(c\) satisfies \(0<c\), and \(N_n/n\to c\) as in \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}.
\item \textbf{(Overlap level.)} The instrument-overlap constant satisfies \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), as in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}.
\item \textbf{(Coverage level and calibration constant.)} The coverage level \(\alpha\) of \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty} satisfies \(0<\alpha<1\), and the Le Cam calibration constant is \(\rho=(1-\alpha)/8\).
\item \textbf{(Strength threshold.)} The fixed effective-strength threshold satisfies \(t_0>0\), for the effective identification strength \(t_n\) of \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk}.
\item \textbf{(Envelope growth.)} For every \(n\), \(k_n>0\), and \(k_n\to\infty\) with \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\), as in \cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:degrading-array}.
\item \textbf{(Admissible geometry.)} The deterministic geometry array \(\mathfrak g=(P_S^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}\) belongs to the admissible class \(\mathcal G\) of \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} at envelope scale \(k_n\) and overlap constant \(\varepsilon\).
\end{itemize}
Then, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the geometry handle of \cref{obj:def:geometry-handle} exists at \(\mathfrak g\). In particular, writing
\[
\mu_n=\left(\frac{t_0\kappa_n}{n}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
p_n(x)=\frac{\mu_n w_n(x)}{\kappa_n},
\]
one has
\[
0\le p_n(x)\le1,
\qquad
\int w_n(x)p_n(x)\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)=\mu_n,
\qquad
\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}=t_0,
\]
and the least-favorable family \(h\mapsto Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) of \cref{obj:def:least-favorable-witness}, defined for every \(|h|\le H(t_0)=\min\{1/4,\rho\,t_0^{-1/2}\}\), takes values in the fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} and satisfies, writing \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}\) for the joint law of the source sample of size \(n\) and the target covariate sample of size \(N_n\) under \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\),
\[
1+\chi^2\!\left(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}\,\middle\|\,Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right)\le\exp(8t_0h^2),
\qquad
\left\|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le2\rho .
\]
The conclusion is eventual in \(n\): admissibility, \(N_n/n\to c\), and the growth conditions on \(k_n\) make the compliance function valid and the calibration hold from some index onwards, not at every \(n\).
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:geometry-handle-eventually-inhabited}]
Write
\[
\kappa_n=\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x),
\qquad
\mu_n=\left(\frac{t_0\kappa_n}{n}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
p_n(x)=\frac{\mu_n w_n(x)}{\kappa_n}.
\]
Admissibility gives \(0\le w_n(x)\le 2k_n\), \(\int w_n\,dP_{S,n}^X=1\), and \(\kappa_n\le k_n\). Since \(w_n\) is bounded and \(P_{S,n}^X\) is a probability law, expanding the nonnegative square gives
\[
0
\le
\int (w_n(x)-1)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
-2\int w_n(x)\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)+1
=
\kappa_n-1 .
\] % lean: one_le_geometryKish
Thus \(1\le \kappa_n\). Since \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\) and \(t_0>0\), for all sufficiently large \(n\) with \(n\ge1\),
\[
\frac{k_n}{\sqrt n}<\frac1{2\sqrt{t_0}} .
\] % lean: geometryCompliance_eventually_valid
For such \(n\), using \(\kappa_n\ge1\), \(w_n(x)\le2k_n\), and
\[
p_n(x)
=
\frac{\sqrt{t_0}\,w_n(x)}
{\sqrt n\,\sqrt{\kappa_n}},
\]
we obtain
\[
0\le p_n(x)
\le
\frac{\sqrt{t_0}\,2k_n}{\sqrt n}
\le 1
\qquad\text{for every }x .
\] % lean: geometryCompliance_eventually_valid

The transported first stage is exact:
\[
\int w_n(x)p_n(x)\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\frac{\mu_n}{\kappa_n}
\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\mu_n .
\] % lean: geometry_firstStage
Also, since \(n\ge1\), \(t_0>0\), and \(\kappa_n>0\),
\[
\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}
=
\frac{n}{\kappa_n}\cdot \frac{t_0\kappa_n}{n}
=
t_0 .
\] % lean: geometry_strength

The construction used for the least-favorable family is first defined, at every row \(m\), with the clamped compliance function
\[
p_m^\ast(x)=\min\{1,p_m(x)\}.
\]
On the same eventual set of indices, the preceding bound gives \(p_n^\ast=p_n\) pointwise. The transported array whose row \(n\) is denoted \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) is generated from the source and target covariate laws of \(\mathfrak g\), the propensity \(e_m\), and the clamped compliance probabilities \(p_m^\ast\), so that at the fixed row \(n\) the compliance probabilities are \(p_n\). For every
\[
|h|\le H(t_0)=\min\{1/4,\rho t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
the bound \(|h|\le1/4\) makes the Bernoulli means \(1/2+h\) valid, while the positivity of
\[
\int p_n\,dP_{T,n}
=
\int w_n(x)p_n(x)\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\mu_n
>
0
\]
supplies the target-complier positivity clause.

It remains in the class-membership step to account for the sampling, envelope, and degrading-array clauses in \cref{obj:def:model-class,obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}. The hypotheses give \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), \(c>0\), \(N_m/m\to c\), \(k_m\to\infty\), and \(k_m/\sqrt m\to0\). Admissibility of \(\mathfrak g\) supplies the source and target covariate probability laws, the propensity bounds \(e_m\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), transport domination by \(w_m\), the envelope bound \(0\le w_m\le2k_m\), and the second-moment bound \(\int w_m^2\,dP_{S,m}^X\le k_m\). The witness construction supplies the full-data support, population presence, source-observation, randomization, exclusion, monotonicity, and transport clauses. For the first-stage part of the degrading array, the clamp is eventually inactive and
\[
\int w_m(x)p_m^\ast(x)\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)
=
\int w_m(x)p_m(x)\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)
=
\mu_m
\]
on that tail, while \(\mu_m\to0\) by the following chain. Since \(\kappa_m\le k_m\), the definition of \(\mu_m\) gives
\[
\mu_m=\left(\frac{t_0\kappa_m}{m}\right)^{1/2}\le\left(\frac{t_0k_m}{m}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The envelope growth condition supplies \(k_m/\sqrt m\to0\), and therefore
\[
\frac{k_m}{m}=\frac{k_m}{\sqrt m}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt m}\longrightarrow 0,
\]
being a product of a null sequence and a bounded one. Hence \(t_0k_m/m\to0\) and the displayed bound forces \(\mu_m\to0\). Thus the transported first-stage sequence of the constructed array tends to zero. Consequently \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) satisfies the transported-IV class clauses, has
\[
P_{S,Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}}^X=P_{S,n}^X,
\qquad
P_{T,Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}}=P_{T,n},
\qquad
w_{Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}}=w_n\quad P_{S,n}^X\text{-a.e.},
\qquad
e_{Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}}=e_n,
\]
and therefore belongs to \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\). Its transported first stage is \(\mu_n\), its weight second moment is \(\kappa_n\), and its effective strength is \(t_0\). % lean: geometryWitnessFamily_eventually_leastFavorable

It remains to record the calibration. Let \(Q_h^S\) denote the one-source-observation marginal under \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) and \(Q_0^S\) the corresponding marginal at \(h=0\). For \(|h|\le1/4\), the single-observation chi-square divergence is
\[
\chi^2(Q_h^S\Vert Q_0^S)
=
\int
\frac{8e_n(x)p_n(x)^2h^2}{1+p_n(x)}
\,dP_{S,n}^X(x).
\] % lean: geometryWitnessFamily_source_chiSq_bound
Because \(0\le e_n(x)\le1\) and \(p_n(x)\ge0\),
\[
\frac{8e_n(x)p_n(x)^2h^2}{1+p_n(x)}
\le
8p_n(x)^2h^2 .
\]
Hence
\[
\chi^2(Q_h^S\Vert Q_0^S)
\le
8h^2\int p_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\frac{8\mu_n^2h^2}{\kappa_n}.
\] % lean: geometryWitnessFamily_source_chiSq_bound
The target covariate sample has the same law under \(h\) and under \(0\), so it is ancillary for this divergence, and independence of the \(n\) source observations gives
\[
1+\chi^2\!\left(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}\middle\|Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right)
=
\left(1+\chi^2(Q_h^S\Vert Q_0^S)\right)^n .
\] % lean: geometryWitnessFamily_twoSample_chiSq_bound
Using \(1+u\le e^u\), the preceding one-observation bound, and \(n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n=t_0\),
\[
1+\chi^2\!\left(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}\middle\|Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right)
\le
\exp\!\left(n\,\chi^2(Q_h^S\Vert Q_0^S)\right)
\le
\exp(8t_0h^2).
\] % lean: geometryWitnessFamily_twoSample_chiSq_bound

For the total-variation bound, the radius condition also gives
\[
t_0h^2\le \rho^2 .
\] % lean: geometryWitnessFamily_twoSample_tv_bound
Set \(u=8\rho^2\). Since \(\rho=(1-\alpha)/8\) and \(0<\alpha<1\), one has \(0<\rho\le1/8\), hence \(0\le u\le1\). The elementary exponential remainder bound on \([0,1]\) yields
\[
\exp(u)-1\le u+u^2\le 9\rho^2 .
\] % lean: geometryWitnessFamily_twoSample_tv_bound
Combining this with the chi-square bound gives
\[
\chi^2\!\left(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}\middle\|Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right)
\le 9\rho^2 .
\] % lean: geometryWitnessFamily_twoSample_tv_bound
Finally, the standard comparison between total variation and chi-square distance gives
\[
\left\|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\frac12
\sqrt{
\chi^2\!\left(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}\middle\|Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right)}
\le
\frac32\rho
\le
2\rho .
\] % lean: geometryWitnessFamily_twoSample_tv_bound
All displayed properties therefore hold for every sufficiently large \(n\), which is exactly the asserted eventual geometry handle. % lean: geometryHandle_eventually_inhabited
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:geometry-handle-eventually-inhabited} connects the abstract handle to the admissible geometries used in \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}. The sub-root envelope condition makes the compliance probabilities valid eventually, and the calibrated family then belongs to the fixed-geometry slice with exactly the displayed effective strength.

The next three inhabitation lemmas record that the finite-cell and fixed-geometry classes used by the minimax criteria contain rows satisfying the stated structural restrictions.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:finite-cell-class-inhabited}[Finite-cell class nonemptiness]
Suppose the following conditions hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Measurable finite cells.)} For every \(n\), the covariate space \(\mathcal X\) of \cref{obj:ass:transport-domination} contains \(k_n\) distinct injected points whose singletons are measurable.
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} The constant \(c\) satisfies \(0<c\), and \(N_n/n\to c\) as in \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument overlap.)} The constant \(\varepsilon\) satisfies \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), as in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}.
\item \textbf{(Cell-count sequence.)} For every \(n\), \(k_n>0\), with \(k_n\to\infty\) as in \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array} and
\[
\frac{k_n}{\sqrt n}\to 0
\]
as in \cref{obj:ass:weight-envelope}.
\end{itemize}
Then, for every \(n\), there exists a transported array law \(P\), consisting of full-data and observed source laws together with the associated propensity, assignment-outcome, assignment-contrast, and receipt-contrast functions, such that \(P\) belongs to the finite-cell submodel \(\mathcal N_n\) of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}. In particular, \(P\) satisfies the finite source-cell condition in \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source} and the clauses of the transported model class \(\mathcal P_n\) in \cref{obj:def:model-class}.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:finite-cell-class-inhabited}]
Choose the injected measurable cells simultaneously for all rows: for each row
\(m\), let
\[
i\longmapsto x_{i,m},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,k_m\},
\]
be the injection supplied by the carrier assumption.  Define whole covariate-law,
weight, propensity, and complier-probability arrays by
\[
P_{S,m}^X=P_{T,m}
=
\frac1{k_m}\sum_{i=1}^{k_m}\delta_{x_{i,m}},
\qquad
w_m(x)=1,
\qquad
e_m(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
p_m(x)=\frac1{m+1}.
\]
The hypotheses \(k_m>0\) and singleton measurability make these displayed
finite laws probability measures on the covariate space.  For every measurable
\(A\),
\[
P_{T,m}(A)=P_{S,m}^X(A)=\int_A 1\,dP_{S,m}^X,
\]
so the target-to-source covariate density ratio is represented by
\(w_m\equiv1\).  The overlap and weight bounds hold rowwise:
\[
\varepsilon\le \frac12\le 1-\varepsilon,
\qquad
0\le w_m(x)=1\le 2k_m,
\qquad
\int w_m(x)^2\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)=1\le k_m .
\]
Here the first inequality uses \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), and the last two use
\(k_m>0\). % lean: uniformFiniteCellClass_inhabited

Construct the full-data law in each row as the half-source, half-target mixture
with
\[
P_m^F(S=1)=P_m^F(S=0)=\frac12,
\qquad
X\mid S=1\sim P_{S,m}^X,
\qquad
X\mid S=0\sim P_{T,m}.
\]
Conditional on \((S,X)=(s,x)\), draw a complier indicator with probability
\(p_m(x)\), draw an independent common noncomplier receipt indicator with
probability \(1/2\), and draw an independent treated-outcome indicator with
probability \(1/2\).  If the complier indicator is one, set
\((D(0),D(1))=(0,1)\); otherwise set \(D(0)=D(1)\) equal to the common receipt
indicator.  Set \(Y(0)=0\), set \(Y(1)\) equal to the treated-outcome indicator
read as a number in \(\{0,1\}\), and take the assignment potential outcome to be
\(Y^Z(z)=Y(D(z))\).  Since \(0\le p_m(x)\le1\), this specifies probability
kernels and hence the full-data laws.  The construction has full-data support,
positive mass for both populations, exclusion, and monotonicity by inspection,
and it gives, for every measurable \(A\) and \(s\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}\!\bigl(Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\bigr)\,dP_m^F(\cdot\mid S=s)
=
\int_A \frac{p_m(x)}2\,dP_{s,m}^X(x),
\]
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}\!\bigl(D(1)-D(0)\bigr)\,dP_m^F(\cdot\mid S=s)
=
\int_A p_m(x)\,dP_{s,m}^X(x),
\]
where \(P_{1,m}^X=P_{S,m}^X\) and \(P_{0,m}^X=P_{T,m}\).  Thus the population
outcome and receipt contrasts are \(p_m/2\) and \(p_m\), respectively, in both
populations. % lean: witnessArray_mem_transportedIVClass

The observed source law is induced from the \(S=1\) component by drawing
\(Z\) conditionally on the full data with probability \(e_m(X)=1/2\), then
observing \(D=D(Z)\) and \(Y=Y(D(Z))\).  This assigned-source law has the
\(S=1\) full-data marginal, and for every measurable \(A\),
\[
P_{S,m}\{X\in A, Z=1\}
=
\int_A e_m(x)\,dP_{S,m}^X(x).
\]
The same construction pins the observed source score contrasts: for every
measurable \(A\), with the Boolean variables read as \(0\)-\(1\) variables,
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}
\left(\frac{Z}{e_m(X)}-\frac{1-Z}{1-e_m(X)}\right)Y\,dP_{S,m}
=
\int_A \frac{p_m(x)}2\,dP_{S,m}^X(x),
\]
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}
\left(\frac{Z}{e_m(X)}-\frac{1-Z}{1-e_m(X)}\right)D\,dP_{S,m}
=
\int_A p_m(x)\,dP_{S,m}^X(x).
\]
Therefore the source observation, randomization, outcome-transport, and
receipt-transport clauses hold for the transported array. % lean: witnessArray_mem_transportedIVClass

Now fix the row \(n\).  The target complier share under the target population is
\[
P_n^F\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\}
=
\int p_n(x)\,dP_{T,n}(x)
=
\frac1{n+1}
>
0,
\]
and the conditioning event has mass \(1/2\) by the mixture construction.  The
transport domination, envelope, and second-moment clauses are the row-\(n\)
statements already verified above.  For the degrading-array clause, the whole
array construction gives
\[
\mu_m
=
\int w_m(x)p_m(x)\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)
=
\frac1{m+1}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
while the hypotheses give \(k_m\to\infty\), \(k_m/\sqrt m\to0\), and
\(N_m/m\to c\).  Hence the constructed array satisfies all clauses of
\(\mathcal P_n\) in \cref{obj:def:model-class}. % lean: witnessArray_mem_transportedIVClass

Finally, the source covariate law in row \(n\) is exactly \(P_{S,n}^X\).  It is
supported on the injected cells, and each injected singleton has mass
\[
P_{S,n}^X(\{x_{i,n}\})=\frac1{k_n}.
\]
Moreover \(e_n(X)=1/2\) almost surely under the source covariate law.  Thus the
finite source-cell condition in \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source} holds, and by
\cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class} the constructed transported array belongs to
\(\mathcal N_n\).  Since \(n\) was arbitrary, the finite-cell class is inhabited
for every row. % lean: finiteCellClass_inhabited
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:finite-cell-class-inhabited} supplies the uniform finite-cell rows used in the finite-cell oracle and feasible comparisons. The measurable injected carrier gives a concrete cell support, while the overlap and growth conditions align those rows with the model class.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited}[Fixed slice inhabitation]
Let \(\mathfrak g\), the deterministic geometry array, be fixed, and let \(N_n\) and \(k_n\) be integer-valued sequences. Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Admissible geometry.)} The geometry \(\mathfrak g\) is admissible for the envelope sequence \(k_n\) and the instrument-overlap constant \(\varepsilon\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class}.
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} The constant \(c\), the asymptotic target-to-source sample-size ratio, satisfies \(c>0\), and \(N_n/n\to c\) as in \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}.
\item \textbf{(Overlap level.)} The instrument-overlap constant satisfies \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), as in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}.
\item \textbf{(Strength threshold.)} The fixed effective-strength threshold satisfies \(t_0>0\).
\item \textbf{(Envelope growth.)} The envelope sequence satisfies \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\), \(k_n\to\infty\), and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\), as in \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array,obj:ass:weight-envelope}.
\end{itemize}
Then, for all sufficiently large \(n\), there exists a transported-array law \(P\) such that \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), the fixed-geometry slice of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}, and its effective identification strength \(t_n(P)=n\mu_n(P)^2/\kappa_n(P)\) from \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk} satisfies
\[
t_n(P)\ge t_0 .
\]
Equivalently, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the strength-restricted fixed-geometry slice
\[
\{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g): t_n(P)\ge t_0\}
\]
is nonempty.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited}]
Write the fixed geometry at row \(m\) as
\[
(P_{S,m}^X,P_{T,m},w_m,e_m),
\]
and put
\[
\kappa_m=\int w_m(x)^2\,dP_{S,m}^X(x),
\qquad
\mu_m=\left(\frac{t_0\kappa_m}{m}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
p_m(x)=\min\left\{1,\frac{\mu_m w_m(x)}{\kappa_m}\right\}.
\]
The geometry supplies the measurability of \(w_m\) and \(e_m\). Admissibility in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} supplies probability laws \(P_{S,m}^X,P_{T,m}\), the overlap bounds
\[
\varepsilon\le e_m(x)\le1-\varepsilon,
\]
the weight bounds
\[
0\le w_m(x)\le2k_m,
\qquad
\kappa_m\le k_m,
\]
the normalization
\[
\int w_m(x)\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)=1,
\]
and the transport identity
\[
P_{T,m}(A)=\int_A w_m(x)\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)
\]
for every measurable \(A\). % lean: fixedGeometrySlice_eventually_inhabited

The construction uses the untruncated value
\[
\bar p_m(x)=\frac{\mu_m w_m(x)}{\kappa_m}.
\]
The envelope bound and measurability make \(w_m\) and \(w_m^2\) integrable. Also \(\kappa_m>0\): if \(\kappa_m=0\), then the nonnegative integral of \(w_m^2\) is zero, hence \(w_m=0\) \(P_{S,m}^X\)-almost surely, contradicting \(\int w_m\,dP_{S,m}^X=1\). Moreover,
\[
0\le \int (w_m(x)-1)^2\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)
=\int w_m(x)^2\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)-2\int w_m(x)\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)+1
=\kappa_m-1,
\]
so \(1\le\kappa_m\). For all sufficiently large \(m\), with \(m\ge1\), the assumption \(k_m/\sqrt m\to0\) gives
\[
\frac{k_m}{\sqrt m}<\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t_0}}.
\]
For such \(m\) and every \(x\),
\[
0\le \bar p_m(x)
=\frac{\sqrt{t_0}\,w_m(x)}{\sqrt m\,\sqrt{\kappa_m}}
\le
\frac{2\sqrt{t_0}\,k_m}{\sqrt m}
<1.
\]
Thus, eventually,
\[
p_m(x)=\bar p_m(x)=\frac{\mu_m w_m(x)}{\kappa_m}
\qquad\text{for every }x.
\]
For every row, \(p_m\) is measurable and takes values in \([0,1]\). % lean: geometryCompliance_eventually_valid

Define a transported array row by row as follows. In the source population draw \(X\sim P_{S,m}^X\), and in the target population draw \(X\sim P_{T,m}\). Conditional on \(X=x\), draw a complier indicator with probability \(p_m(x)\), a common-receipt indicator with probability \(1/2\), and a treated-outcome indicator with probability \(1/2\). Set \((D(0),D(1))=(0,1)\) for compliers and \((D(0),D(1))=(U,U)\) otherwise, where \(U\) is the common-receipt draw; set \(Y(0)=0\) and \(Y(1)=V\), where \(V\) is the treated-outcome draw. The full-data law mixes the source and target population laws with weights \(1/2\) and \(1/2\). The assigned source law then draws \(Z\) with propensity \(e_m(X)\) and observes \(D=D(Z)\) and \(Y=Y(D(Z))\). The associated assignment and receipt contrasts are \(p_m/2\) and \(p_m\), respectively.

Fix a sufficiently large \(n\) in the eventual set above. In the target population component of this witness, the complier event \(\{D(1)=1,D(0)=0\}\) is exactly the success event of the complier draw. Hence the target complier share is
\[
\int p_n(x)\,dP_{T,n}(x).
\]
Using the transport identity to integrate against \(P_{T,n}\) and using the eventual identity \(p_n=\bar p_n\),
\[
\int p_n(x)\,dP_{T,n}(x)
=
\int w_n(x)p_n(x)\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\int w_n(x)\frac{\mu_n w_n(x)}{\kappa_n}\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\mu_n.
\]
This value is strictly positive because \(t_0>0\), \(n>0\), and \(\kappa_n>0\). % lean: fixedGeometrySlice_eventually_inhabited

The displayed construction supplies the clauses of the transported model class in \cref{obj:def:model-class}. The full-data support and population-presence clauses follow from the probability laws and the binary support of the three Bernoulli draws. The two-sample clause uses \(c>0\), \(N_n/n\to c\), and the source and target probability laws. The instrument-overlap clause is the inherited bound on \(e_n\). The source-observation, randomization, exclusion, and monotonicity clauses follow from drawing \(Z\) conditionally on \(X\), observing \(D(Z)\) and \(Y(D(Z))\), and setting \(D(1)\ge D(0)\) by construction. The outcome- and receipt-transport clauses hold because the conditional contrasts are the same functions, \(p_m/2\) and \(p_m\), in the source and target population components. The target-complier-positivity clause is the strict positivity just displayed. The transport identity identifies \(P_{T,n}\) with the weighted measure \(w_n\,P_{S,n}^X\), so the Radon--Nikodym transport weight of the witness equals \(w_n\) \(P_{S,n}^X\)-almost surely; this gives transport domination and the weight-envelope clause, while the second-moment clause is \(\int w_n^2\,dP_{S,n}^X\le k_n\).

For the degrading-array clause, the witness first stage satisfies, eventually in \(m\),
\[
\mu_m(P)
=
\int w_m(x)p_m(x)\,dP_{S,m}^X(x)
=
\mu_m.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
0\le \mu_m
=\left(\frac{t_0\kappa_m}{m}\right)^{1/2}
\le
\left(\frac{t_0 k_m}{m}\right)^{1/2}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
since \(k_m/m=(k_m/\sqrt m)(1/\sqrt m)\to0\). Together with \(k_m\to\infty\) and \(k_m/\sqrt m\to0\), this verifies the degrading-array requirement. Therefore the witness belongs to \(\mathcal P_n\). % lean: witnessArray_mem_transportedIVClass

The source covariate law, target covariate law, propensity, and transport weight of the witness agree with the row-\(n\) geometry:
\[
P_{S,n}^{X,P}=P_{S,n}^X,
\qquad
P_{T,n}^{P}=P_{T,n},
\qquad
e_n^P=e_n,
\]
and the transport weight is \(w_n\) \(P_{S,n}^X\)-almost surely by the Radon--Nikodym identification above. Hence the witness belongs to the fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}. % lean: fixedGeometrySlice_eventually_inhabited

For this witness,
\[
\mu_n(P)=\int w_n(x)p_n(x)\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)=\mu_n,
\qquad
\kappa_n(P)=\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)=\kappa_n.
\]
Using the effective-strength definition in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk},
\[
t_n(P)
=
\frac{n\mu_n(P)^2}{\kappa_n(P)}
=
\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}
=
t_0,
\]
where the last equality uses \(\mu_n^2=t_0\kappa_n/n\), \(n>0\), and \(\kappa_n>0\). Thus \(t_n(P)\ge t_0\) for every sufficiently large \(n\), proving eventual nonemptiness of the strength-restricted fixed-geometry slice. % lean: fixedGeometrySlice_eventually_inhabited
\end{proof}

The fixed-geometry value in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} takes a supremum over strength-restricted slices. \Cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited} ensures that those slices are populated eventually under the same admissible-geometry and growth conditions used for the fixed-geometry characterization.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:regular-finite-cell-class-inhabited}[Regular class nonemptiness]
Assume the following conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Finite measurable cells.)} For every \(n\), the covariate space \(\mathcal X\) of \cref{obj:ass:transport-domination} contains \(k_n\) distinct points, indexed by \(\{1,\ldots,k_n\}\), whose singletons are measurable.
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} The asymptotic target-to-source sample-size ratio \(c\) satisfies \(0<c\), and \(N_n/n\to c\) as in \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}.
\item \textbf{(Instrument overlap.)} The instrument-overlap constant \(\varepsilon\) satisfies \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), as in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}.
\item \textbf{(Regular-cell bounds.)} The fixed regular-cell mass constants \(c_-\) and \(c_+\) satisfy \(0<c_-\le 1\le c_+\).
\item \textbf{(Cell-count growth.)} For every \(n\), \(k_n>0\), and \(k_n\to\infty\) with \(k_n/\sqrt n\to 0\), as in \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array,obj:ass:weight-envelope}.
\end{itemize}
Then, for every \(n\), there exists a transported array \(P\), consisting of full-data laws, assigned-source laws, propensities, assignment-outcome maps, assignment contrasts, and receipt contrasts with the usual measurability structure, such that \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), the regular finite-cell model class of \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class}. In particular, the source cell probabilities \(q_{x,n}\) on the witnessing measurable cells satisfy
\[
c_-/k_n\le q_{x,n}\le c_+/k_n .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:regular-finite-cell-class-inhabited}]
Use the same uniform finite-cell witness constructed in the proof of \cref{obj:lem:finite-cell-class-inhabited}. Thus, for each fixed \(n\), choose injected measurable cells \(x_{i,n}\), set
\[
P_{S,n}^X=P_{T,n}
=
\frac1{k_n}\sum_{i=1}^{k_n}\delta_{x_{i,n}},
\qquad
w_n\equiv1,\qquad
e_n\equiv\frac12,\qquad
p_n\equiv\frac1{n+1},
\]
and generate the corresponding transported array. The preceding verification gives membership in \(\mathcal P_n\) and the finite source-cell structure. % lean: uniformFiniteCellClass_inhabited

For the witnessing cells, the source cell probabilities are
\[
q_{i,n}=P_{S,n}^X(\{x_{i,n}\})=\frac1{k_n}.
\]
Since \(k_n>0\), multiplication by \(1/k_n\) preserves the inequalities \(c_-\le1\le c_+\). Hence, for every cell \(i\),
\[
\frac{c_-}{k_n}\le\frac1{k_n}=q_{i,n}\le\frac{c_+}{k_n}.
\]
The constructed array therefore satisfies the regular finite-cell bounds in \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class}, and so belongs to \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\). Since \(n\) was arbitrary, the regular finite-cell class is inhabited for every \(n\). % lean: regularFiniteCellClass_inhabited
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:regular-finite-cell-class-inhabited} provides the regular nonuniform-cell analogue. The bounded source-cell probabilities make the regular class compatible with the finite-cell sampling structure used by \cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}.

We next record the measure and source-observation facts that underwrite the identification and score calculations. These statements translate setwise contrast identities into almost-sure bounds and integrability conclusions.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:setwise-domination-unit-bound}[Setwise domination gives an almost-sure unit bound]
Let \(\mu\) be a finite measure on a measurable space \(\Omega\), and let \(f:\Omega\to\mathbb R\) be a measurable function. Suppose that
\[
\left|\int_A f\,d\mu\right|\le\mu(A)
\]
for every measurable set \(A\subseteq\Omega\). Then
\[
|f(x)|\le 1\qquad\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }x .
\]
No integrability hypothesis on \(f\) is imposed. The truncated measurable slices
\[
\{q<f\le m\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{-m\le f<-q\},\qquad q>1,\ m\in\mathbb N,
\]
carry a bounded restriction of \(f\) and therefore a genuine integral, so the displayed domination may be applied on them; exhausting the regions \(\{f>q\}\) and \(\{f<-q\}\) as \(m\to\infty\) and then letting \(q\downarrow 1\) gives the stated almost-everywhere bound. The intended application takes \(\mu=P_S^X\), the source covariate marginal of \cref{obj:ass:transport-domination}.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:setwise-domination-unit-bound}]
Fix \(q>1\).  For \(m\in\mathbb N\), put
\[
A_m(q)=\{x:q<f(x)\le m\}.
\]
This set is measurable, and \(f\mathbf 1_{A_m(q)}\) is integrable because \(|f|\le m\) on \(A_m(q)\) and \(\mu\) is finite.  On this slice,
\[
q\,\mu(A_m(q))
=
\int_{A_m(q)}q\,d\mu
\le
\int_{A_m(q)}f\,d\mu
\le
\left|\int_{A_m(q)}f\,d\mu\right|
\le
\mu(A_m(q)).
\]
Since \(q>1\), this gives \(\mu(A_m(q))=0\) for every \(m\). % lean: abs_le_one_ae_of_setIntegral_le
If \(f(x)>q\), choose \(m\in\mathbb N\) with \(f(x)\le m\); then \(x\in A_m(q)\).  Hence
\[
\mu\{x:f(x)>q\}=0,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
f\le q\quad \mu\text{-a.e.}
\]
% lean: abs_le_one_ae_of_setIntegral_le

The lower tail is identical after reversing signs.  For \(m\in\mathbb N\), set
\[
B_m(q)=\{x:-m\le f(x)<-q\}.
\]
Again \(f\mathbf 1_{B_m(q)}\) is integrable, and
\[
\int_{B_m(q)}f\,d\mu
\le
\int_{B_m(q)}(-q)\,d\mu
=
-q\,\mu(B_m(q)).
\]
The assumed domination also gives
\[
-\mu(B_m(q))
\le
\int_{B_m(q)}f\,d\mu.
\]
Therefore \((q-1)\mu(B_m(q))\le0\), and \(\mu(B_m(q))=0\).  Taking \(m\) with \(-f(x)\le m\) shows
\[
\mu\{x:f(x)<-q\}=0,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
-q\le f\quad \mu\text{-a.e.}
\]
% lean: abs_le_one_ae_of_setIntegral_le

Apply the preceding two conclusions to
\[
q_j=1+\frac{1}{j+1},\qquad j=0,1,2,\ldots .
\]
Outside a null set, \(-q_j\le f(x)\le q_j\) for every \(j\).  Letting \(j\to\infty\) gives
\[
-1\le f(x)\le 1
\]
outside that null set, equivalently \(|f(x)|\le1\) \(\mu\)-almost surely. % lean: abs_le_one_ae_of_setIntegral_le
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:setwise-domination-unit-bound} is the elementary setwise domination step used to obtain almost-sure unit bounds for conditional contrasts. Those bounds are what make the compact range in \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} available directly from the model restrictions.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:fixed-geometry-value-le-frontier-risk}[Fixed-geometry value below global frontier risk]
Assume the following conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample-size ratio.)} The asymptotic target-to-source sample-size ratio \(c\) satisfies \(0<c\), and \(N_n/n\to c\) as in \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}.
\item \textbf{(Overlap level.)} The instrument-overlap constant satisfies \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), as in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}.
\item \textbf{(Envelope growth.)} For every \(n\), \(k_n>0\), and \(k_n\to\infty\) with \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\), as in \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array,obj:ass:weight-envelope}.
\item \textbf{(Admissible geometry.)} The deterministic geometry array \(\mathfrak g\) belongs to the admissible class \(\mathcal G\) of \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} at envelope scale \(k_n\) and overlap constant \(\varepsilon\).
\item \textbf{(Strength threshold.)} The fixed effective-strength threshold satisfies \(t_0>0\).
\end{itemize}
Then, for every oracle-honest sequence \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\) of \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}, the fixed-geometry minimax value \(V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} and the frontier risk \(R((C_n),t_0)\) of \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk} satisfy
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\le R((C_n),t_0).
\]
The globally honest sequence is first restricted to a fixed-geometry honest sequence in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-honesty}, whose strength-restricted rows over \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} are eventually nonempty; no pointwise identification of almost-everywhere equal transport-weight versions is needed. Taking the infimum over \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\) gives
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\le V^\star(t_0)
\]
for the oracle minimax value of \cref{obj:def:oracle-value}.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-value-le-frontier-risk}]
Fix an oracle-honest sequence \(C=(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\).  Let \(\widetilde C\) be the same oracle procedure evaluated with the fixed geometry \(\mathfrak g\):
\[
\widetilde C_n\bigl((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\bigr)
=
C_n\bigl((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n,(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n},w,e\bigr),
\]
where \(w,e\) are the \(n\)th weight and propensity components of \(\mathfrak g\).  If \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), then \(P\in\mathcal P_n\), the propensity input equals the \(n\)th geometry propensity, and the \(n\)th geometry weight is source-almost surely a valid transport-weight version for \(P\), by \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}.  The oracle procedure is evaluated on admissible transport-weight versions modulo source-null changes, so under the two-sample law generated by such a \(P\),
\[
\mathbf 1\{\theta_T\in \widetilde C_n\}=\mathbf 1\{\theta_T\in C_n\},
\qquad
\lambda(\widetilde C_n)=\lambda(C_n)
\quad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
Consequently, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\),
\[
P\{\theta_T\in \widetilde C_n\}=P\{\theta_T\in C_n\},
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)]=\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
% lean: OracleHonest.fixedGeometry

By \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited}, for all sufficiently large \(n\) there exists \(P_0\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) with \(t_n(P_0)\ge t_0\).  Hence the fixed-geometry slice and the global class are inhabited on those rows.  On such rows, the preceding coverage identity and the inclusion \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\subseteq\mathcal P_n\) give
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}P\{\theta_T\in C_n\}
\le
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}P\{\theta_T\in \widetilde C_n\}.
\]
The same nonempty-row reduction gives the bounds used to pass this comparison to liminf:
\[
0\le \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}P\{\theta_T\in C_n\},
\qquad
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}P\{\theta_T\in \widetilde C_n\}\le 1
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), since coverage probabilities lie in \([0,1]\).  Therefore the eventual row comparison yields
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}
P\{\theta_T\in \widetilde C_n\}
\ge
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
P\{\theta_T\in C_n\}
\ge 1-\alpha,
\]
and the \(0<\alpha<1\) clauses are inherited from \(C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\).  Thus \(\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\) by \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-honesty}. % lean: OracleHonest.fixedGeometry

The eventual strength-restricted fixed-geometry rows are also nonempty by \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited}.  On those rows, the expected-length identity and the inclusion of the fixed slice into the global class give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)]
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n:\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
For every set \(A\subseteq\Theta=[-1,1]\), the restricted length satisfies \(0\le\lambda(A)\le2\).  For the displayed real suprema over strength-restricted rows, an empty indexing set gives the real supremum of the empty set, which is \(0\); on nonempty rows, the same length bound makes each fixed-geometry row nonnegative and each global row bounded above by \(2\).  These bounds are the hypotheses needed to pass the eventual row comparison through limsup, so
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)]
\le
R((C_n),t_0).
\]
% lean: OracleHonest.fixedGeometry

It remains to take the infimum over fixed-geometry honest procedures.  For any oracle procedure \(E\), the same real-supremum convention and the bound \(0\le\lambda(A)\le2\) imply that each strength-restricted fixed-geometry risk row for \(E\) lies in \([0,2]\), and hence
\[
0\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(E_n)].
\]
Thus the collection of fixed-geometry risks over \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\) is bounded below by \(0\).  Since the constructed \(\widetilde C\) belongs to \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\), \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} gives
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)].
\]
Combining this inequality with the preceding risk comparison gives
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\le R((C_n),t_0).
\]
% lean: fixedGeometryValue_le_frontierRisk
\end{proof}

The comparison in \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-value-le-frontier-risk} places each fixed-geometry lower bound underneath the global oracle criterion. This is the bridge from the fixed-geometry least-favorable experiment to the oracle lower bound stated in \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:source-observation-facts}[Source observation facts]
Let \(P\) belong to the transported complier-effect model class \(\mathcal P_n\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class}, for the sample-size array \(N_n\), envelope scale \(k_n\), target-to-source ratio \(c\), and instrument-overlap constant \(\varepsilon\). In particular, the source-observation quantities below are governed by \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support,obj:ass:two-sample-array,obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:source-observation,obj:ass:iv-exclusion,obj:ass:iv-monotonicity,obj:ass:outcome-transport,obj:ass:receipt-transport,obj:ass:transport-domination}. Let \(P_{\mathrm{assign},S}\) denote the assigned-source overlay on \((F,Z)\), where \(F=(S,X,D(0),D(1),Y(0),Y(1))\), and let \(P_S\) be its observed-source image under \((F,Z)\mapsto (X,Z,D(Z),Y(D(Z)))\). Write \(P_S^X\) for the source covariate marginal and
\[
H(O^S)
=
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=1\}}{e(X)}
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=0\}}{1-e(X)} .
\]
Then \(P_{\mathrm{assign},S}\) and \(P_S\) are probability measures, the full-data marginal of \(P_{\mathrm{assign},S}\) is \(P_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1)\), and the observed outcome satisfies \(Y\in[0,1]\) \(P_S\)-almost surely. Moreover, for every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
P_S\{X\in A,\ Z=1\}
=
\int_A e(x)\,dP_S^X(x),
\]
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}} H(O^S)Y\,dP_S
=
\int_A \Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x),
\]
and
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}} H(O^S)D\,dP_S
=
\int_A \Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
Finally, \(\Delta_Y(X)\in[-1,1]\) and \(\Delta_D(X)\in[0,1]\) hold \(P_S^X\)-almost surely.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts}]
Unpacking the assigned-source part of \cref{obj:ass:source-observation}, the overlay
\(P_{\mathrm{assign},S}\) is a probability law on \((F,Z)\) whose full-data marginal is
\[
(P_{\mathrm{assign},S})\circ F^{-1}
=
P_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=1).
\]
The observed source law is the image of this overlay under
\((F,Z)\mapsto (X,Z,D(Z),Y(D(Z)))\), and \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array} gives that this image
\(P_S\) is a probability measure.  Therefore the source covariate marginal is the image of the
source-population full-data law under \(F\mapsto X(F)\), so
\[
P_S^X=P_n^F(X\in\cdot\mid S=1).
\]

The support condition in \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support}, restricted to the source population,
gives \(Y(0),Y(1)\in[0,1]\) under \(P_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1)\).  Pulling this statement back to
\(P_{\mathrm{assign},S}\) and then applying the observation map gives
\[
P_S\{0\le Y\le1\}=1.
\]

Let \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\) be measurable.  The assignment-propensity component of
\cref{obj:ass:source-observation} gives the observed assignment slice
\[
P_S\{X\in A,\ Z=1\}
=
\int_A e(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]

For the inverse-propensity identities, the assignment-propensity component of
\cref{obj:ass:source-observation} together with the conditional randomization in
\cref{obj:ass:iv-randomization} gives the two assigned-source slices: for every measurable
full-data set \(B\),
\[
P_{\mathrm{assign},S}\{F\in B,\ Z=1\}
=
\int_B e(X(F))\,dP_n^F(F\mid S=1),
\]
and
\[
P_{\mathrm{assign},S}\{F\in B,\ Z=0\}
=
\int_B \{1-e(X(F))\}\,dP_n^F(F\mid S=1).
\]
The overlap bounds in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap} make these weights nonnegative and make
\(e(X)\) and \(1-e(X)\) positive on the almost-sure sets where the cancellations below are made.

Define \(F_z(F)=Y(D(z))\) for \(z\in\{0,1\}\).  On the assigned-source space,
\[
H(O^S)Y
=
\begin{cases}
e(X)^{-1}F_1(F), & Z=1,\\
-\{1-e(X)\}^{-1}F_0(F), & Z=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Splitting the integral over \(Z=1\) and \(Z=0\), applying the two slice identities above, and
cancelling the propensity factors yields
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}} H(O^S)Y\,dP_S
=
\int_{\{X(F)\in A\}}\{Y(D(1))-Y(D(0))\}\,dP_n^F(F\mid S=1).
\]
By \cref{obj:ass:iv-exclusion}, the integrand agrees with \(Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\) under the source
population law, and the source part of \cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport} gives
\[
\int_{\{X(F)\in A\}}\{Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\}\,dP_n^F(F\mid S=1)
=
\int_A\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
Thus
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}} H(O^S)Y\,dP_S
=
\int_A\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]

The receipt identity uses the same two-slice calculation with \(G_z(F)=D(z)\), reading binary
receipts as their \(0\)-\(1\) values.  On the assigned-source space,
\[
H(O^S)D
=
\begin{cases}
e(X)^{-1}G_1(F), & Z=1,\\
-\{1-e(X)\}^{-1}G_0(F), & Z=0,
\end{cases}
\]
and therefore
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}} H(O^S)D\,dP_S
=
\int_{\{X(F)\in A\}}\{D(1)-D(0)\}\,dP_n^F(F\mid S=1).
\]
The source-population identity in \cref{obj:ass:receipt-transport} then gives
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}} H(O^S)D\,dP_S
=
\int_A\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]

It remains to record the pointwise ranges of the two covariate contrasts.  From
\cref{obj:ass:full-data-support,obj:ass:iv-exclusion},
\[
\left|Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\right|\le1
\]
under \(P_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1)\).  Combining this bound with the source part of
\cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport} gives, for every measurable \(A\),
\[
\left|\int_A\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x)\right|
\le
P_S^X(A).
\]
Since \(\Delta_Y\) is measurable and \(P_S^X\) is finite, \cref{obj:lem:setwise-domination-unit-bound}
gives
\[
\Delta_Y(X)\in[-1,1]
\qquad P_S^X\text{-a.s.}
\]

Similarly, binary receipt gives \(|D(1)-D(0)|\le1\), and the source part of
\cref{obj:ass:receipt-transport} gives
\[
\left|\int_A\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)\right|
\le
P_S^X(A)
\]
for every measurable \(A\).  Since \(\Delta_D\) is measurable, \cref{obj:lem:setwise-domination-unit-bound}
gives \(|\Delta_D(X)|\le1\) \(P_S^X\)-almost surely.  Monotonicity in
\cref{obj:ass:iv-monotonicity} gives \(D(1)-D(0)\ge0\) under the source population law, so
\[
\int_A\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)\ge0
\]
for every measurable \(A\).  The almost-sure unit bound makes \(\Delta_D\) integrable with respect
to \(P_S^X\), and the set-integral characterization of nonnegativity yields
\(\Delta_D(X)\ge0\) \(P_S^X\)-almost surely.  Combining the two bounds gives
\[
\Delta_D(X)\in[0,1]
\qquad P_S^X\text{-a.s.}
\]
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts} gathers the source-law identities needed by both identification and score inversion. It supplies the inverse-propensity representations of the source conditional outcome and receipt contrasts, together with the boundedness inherited from bounded outcomes and monotone binary receipt.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:transported-contrast-integrable}[Transported contrast integrability]
Fix an index \(n\), a transported array \(P\), target sample-size and envelope schedules \(N_n\) and \(k_n\), a target-to-source ratio \(c\), and the instrument-overlap constant \(\varepsilon\). Suppose that \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-class} for these schedules and constants. In particular, \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support,obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:iv-exclusion,obj:ass:iv-monotonicity,obj:ass:outcome-transport,obj:ass:receipt-transport,obj:ass:transport-domination} hold at index \(n\). Then the source conditional outcome contrast \(\Delta_Y\) and the source conditional receipt contrast \(\Delta_D\) are integrable with respect to the source covariate marginal \(P_S^X\):
\[
\Delta_Y\in L^1(P_S^X)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Delta_D\in L^1(P_S^X).
\]
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:transported-contrast-integrable}]
By \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts}, the source covariate marginal agrees with the source-population covariate marginal:
\[
P_S^X
=
P_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=1)\circ X^{-1}.
\]
The same result gives the assigned-source probability structure needed to identify the source-population conditional law. % lean: transportedIVClass_contrast_integrable

From \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support}, under \(P_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1)\),
\[
Y(0),Y(1)\in[0,1].
\]
Using \cref{obj:ass:iv-exclusion}, the assignment-outcome contrast equals
\[
Y(D(1))-Y(D(0))
\]
almost surely, and hence
\[
\left|Y(D(1))-Y(D(0))\right|\le1
\qquad P_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1)\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Therefore, by the conditional contrast representation in \cref{obj:ass:outcome-transport}, for every measurable \(A\),
\[
\left|\int_A \Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x)\right|
\le
P_S^X(A).
\]
% lean: transportedIVClass_contrast_integrable

A standard level-set argument turns this setwise domination into an almost-sure pointwise bound. If a measurable function \(f\) satisfies
\[
\left|\int_A f\,d\mu\right|\le\mu(A)
\]
for every measurable \(A\), then \(|f|\le1\) \(\mu\)-almost surely: apply the inequality to the sets \(\{q<f\le m\}\) and \(\{-m\le f<-q\}\), with \(q>1\) and \(m\in\mathbb N\), to show those sets have measure zero, then let \(q\downarrow1\). Applying this to \(f=\Delta_Y\) and \(\mu=P_S^X\) yields
\[
|\Delta_Y(x)|\le1
\qquad P_S^X\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Since \(\Delta_Y\) is measurable and \(P_S^X\) is a probability measure, this bound implies
\[
\Delta_Y\in L^1(P_S^X).
\]
% lean: transportedIVClass_contrast_integrable

For the receipt contrast, the binary nature of \(D(0)\) and \(D(1)\) gives
\[
\left|D(1)-D(0)\right|\le1
\qquad P_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1)\text{-almost surely}.
\]
By \cref{obj:ass:receipt-transport}, for every measurable \(A\),
\[
\left|\int_A \Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)\right|
\le
P_S^X(A).
\]
The same level-set domination argument gives
\[
|\Delta_D(x)|\le1
\qquad P_S^X\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Because \(\Delta_D\) is measurable and \(P_S^X\) is a probability measure, it follows that
\[
\Delta_D\in L^1(P_S^X).
\]
% lean: transportedIVClass_contrast_integrable
\end{proof}

The integrability statement in \cref{obj:lem:transported-contrast-integrable} discharges the side conditions attached to the identification result. Once a law belongs to the transported model class, the conditional contrasts can be integrated against the source covariate marginal.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:class-compact-causal-range}[Compact causal range on a model class]
Fix an index \(n\), target sample-size and envelope schedules \(N_n\) and \(k_n\), a target-to-source ratio \(c\), and the instrument-overlap constant \(\varepsilon\). Let \(\mathcal C\) be a family of transported arrays with the property that every \(P\in\mathcal C\) satisfies each clause of the transported complier-effect model class \(\mathcal P_n\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class} at index \(n\), that is, \cref{obj:ass:full-data-support,obj:ass:population-presence,obj:ass:two-sample-array,obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:source-observation,obj:ass:iv-randomization,obj:ass:iv-exclusion,obj:ass:iv-monotonicity,obj:ass:outcome-transport,obj:ass:receipt-transport,obj:ass:target-complier-positivity,obj:ass:transport-domination,obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment,obj:ass:degrading-array} hold at index \(n\). The class \(\mathcal P_n\) itself and, for \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) as in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class}, the fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} are such families.
Then, for every \(P\in\mathcal C\), the conclusions of \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} hold with no further hypothesis. In particular \(\Delta_Y\) and \(\Delta_D\) agree \(P_S^X\)-almost everywhere with the source conditional outcome and receipt contrasts,
\[
\mu_{Y,n}
=
\int\bigl(Y(1)-Y(0)\bigr)\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}\,dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=0),
\qquad
\mu_n=P_n^F\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\},
\]
and
\[
\theta_T=\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}\in\Theta=[-1,1].
\]
The measurability and integrability side conditions on \(\Delta_Y\) and \(\Delta_D\) that \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} carries as hypotheses are supplied from class membership alone by \cref{obj:lem:transported-contrast-integrable}, so a consumer working at \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) or at \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) need not discharge them separately.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range}]
Fix \(P\in\mathcal C\).  By the defining property of \(\mathcal C\), \(P\) satisfies all clauses of the transported model class \(\mathcal P_n\) at the index \(n\).  In particular, it is an admissible input for the source-observation facts of \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts}. % lean: ScoreRiskClassAtoms.toTransportedIVClass

First supply the side conditions required by \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range}.  The source-observation facts give, for every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}H(O^S)Y\,dP_S
=
\int_A\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x),
\qquad
\int_{\{X\in A\}}H(O^S)D\,dP_S
=
\int_A\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x),
\]
and also
\[
\Delta_Y(X)\in[-1,1],
\qquad
\Delta_D(X)\in[0,1],
\qquad P_S^X\text{-a.s.}
\]
Consequently
\[
\Delta_Y\in L^1(P_S^X),
\qquad
\Delta_D\in L^1(P_S^X).
\]
% lean: transportedIVClass_contrast_integrable

Now apply \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} to this \(P\) with those two integrability facts.  It gives the observed source contrast representations
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}H(O^S)Y\,dP_S
=
\int_A\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x),
\qquad
\int_{\{X\in A\}}H(O^S)D\,dP_S
=
\int_A\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)
\]
for every measurable \(A\), and the transported target identities
\[
\int w(x)\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=\mu_{Y,n},
\qquad
\int w(x)\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=\mu_n.
\]
% lean: compact_causal_range

The same application identifies the Wald ratio with the target complier contrast:
\[
\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}
=
\theta_T
=
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[
Y(1)-Y(0)\mid D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0,\ S=0
\right],
\]
where
\[
\mu_{Y,n}
=
\int\bigl(Y(1)-Y(0)\bigr)\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}\,
dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=0),
\qquad
\mu_n=P_n^F\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\}.
\]
Since \(Y(0),Y(1)\in[0,1]\) on the full-data support and the target complier event has positive probability by target-complier positivity, this conditional contrast lies in
\[
\Theta=[-1,1].
\]
% lean: compact_causal_range

Thus all conclusions of \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} hold for the fixed \(P\in\mathcal C\).  Since \(P\) was arbitrary, they hold for every member of \(\mathcal C\). % lean: scoreRiskClass_compact_causal_range
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range} makes \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} available uniformly on the model classes used by the minimax statements. In particular, it records that the fixed-geometry slice inherits the same identified target contrast and compact parameter space.

The final two auxiliary results support the expected-length bounds for score inversion. The first is a generic moment inequality for the first-stage denominator, and the second is the conditional mean and variance calculation used by the regular-cell feasible rule.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:score-receipt-bad-slope-probability}[Bad-slope probability for the first-stage mean]
Assume the following conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sample size and overlap.)} \(n\ge1\) and the instrument-overlap constant satisfies \(\varepsilon>0\), as in \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap}.
\item \textbf{(Strength inputs.)} \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class} has transported first-stage mean \(\mu_n(P)>0\) as in \cref{obj:ass:degrading-array} and transport-weight second moment \(\kappa_n(P)>0\) as in \cref{obj:ass:weight-second-moment}, and \(t_n(P)=n\mu_n(P)^2/\kappa_n(P)\) is the effective identification strength of \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk}.
\item \textbf{(Moment facts.)} \(\widehat B\) is a square-integrable statistic of the source sample with
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat B]=\mu_n(P),
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat B)\le\frac{\kappa_n(P)}{\varepsilon^2n}.
\]
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
P\!\left(\frac{\mu_n(P)}2<\left|\widehat B-\mu_n(P)\right|\right)\le\frac{4}{\varepsilon^2t_n(P)} .
\]
The bound is law-agnostic: only the two displayed moment facts are used. It therefore applies verbatim to the weighted first-stage sample mean
\[
\widehat B_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw(X_i)H(O_i^S)D_i
\]
of \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts,obj:ass:weight-envelope}, once that estimator has separately been shown to satisfy \(\mathbb E_P[\widehat B_n]=\mu_n(P)\) and \(\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat B_n)\le\kappa_n(P)/(\varepsilon^2n)\), and to the fixed-geometry version of the same construction.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:score-receipt-bad-slope-probability}]
Let \(\mu=\mu_n(P)\), \(\kappa=\kappa_n(P)\), and \(t=t_n(P)\). By assumption, \(\mu>0\), \(\kappa>0\), and
\[
t=\frac{n\mu^2}{\kappa}.
\]
Apply Chebyshev's inequality to the square-integrable statistic \(\widehat B\), with center \(\mu\), variance bound \(\kappa/(\varepsilon^2n)\), and threshold \(a=\mu/2>0\). This gives
\[
P\!\left(\frac{\mu}{2}<|\widehat B-\mu|\right)
\le
\frac{\kappa/(\varepsilon^2n)}{(\mu/2)^2}.
\] % lean: probability_abs_sub_mean_gt_le
The right-hand side is
\[
\frac{\kappa/(\varepsilon^2n)}{(\mu/2)^2}
=
\frac{4\kappa}{\varepsilon^2 n\mu^2}
=
\frac4{\varepsilon^2\,t},
\] % lean: scoreReceiptMean_bad_probability
and substituting back \(\mu=\mu_n(P)\) and \(t=t_n(P)\) proves the claim. % lean: scoreReceiptMean_bad_probability
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:score-receipt-bad-slope-probability} controls the event on which the estimated first-stage slope is too small relative to its mean. The bound depends on the effective strength \(t_n\), matching the scale that appears in the oracle and finite-cell expected-length rates.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:regular-cell-conditional-moment-bound}[Regular-cell conditional mean and variance]
Assume the following conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Regular-cell law.)} \(n\ge1\) and \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), the regular finite-cell class of \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class}, with known source cell probabilities \(q_{x,n}\) on the witnessing measurable cells and propensity \(e(\cdot)\); in particular the instrument overlap \(\varepsilon\le e(X)\le1-\varepsilon\) of \cref{obj:ass:instrument-overlap} holds, and the source observation \((X,Z,D,Y)\) of \cref{obj:ass:source-observation} has \(Y\in[0,1]\) and \(D\in\{0,1\}\).
\item \textbf{(Candidate value.)} \(\vartheta\in\Theta=[-1,1]\), as in \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle}.
\item \textbf{(Target sample.)} A realized target covariate sample \((X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\) of \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array}, with empirical cell frequencies \(\widehat p_{x,n}\), is held fixed, and the source observations are i.i.d. from the source law.
\end{itemize}
Let \(\widehat M_{Y,n}\) and \(\widehat M_{D,n}\) be the transported plug-in contrast estimators built from the inverse-propensity source scores \(H(O^S)\) of \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts} with cell weights \(\widehat p_{x,n}/q_{x,n}\). Then, conditionally on the target sample,
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}\mid(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\right]
=\sum_x\widehat p_{x,n}\left(\Delta_Y(x)-\vartheta\Delta_D(x)\right)
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}\mid(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\right)
\le\frac{4}{\varepsilon^2n}\sum_x\frac{\widehat p_{x,n}^2}{q_{x,n}},
\]
where \(\Delta_Y\) and \(\Delta_D\) are the source conditional contrasts of \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range}. The variance bound follows from the pointwise envelope
\[
\left|H(O^S)(Y-\vartheta D)\right|\le\frac2\varepsilon,
\]
which uses the bounded source outcome \(Y\in[0,1]\) and the Boolean receipt \(D\in\{0,1\}\), giving \(|Y-\vartheta D|\le2\) for \(\vartheta\in\Theta\), in addition to overlap; overlap and \(\Theta=[-1,1]\) alone do not entail the bound.
\end{lemmav}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:regular-cell-conditional-moment-bound}]
Fix the realized target sample. The regular-cell class supplies measurable cells \(x_i\), \(i\in\{1,\ldots,k_n\}\), carrying the source covariate law, with
\[
q_i=q_{x_i,n}=P_S^X\{X=x_i\}>0 .
\] % lean: regularCell_source_conditional_mean_variance
Write
\[
\widehat p_i=\widehat p_{x_i,n},
\qquad
H_e(O^S)=\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=1\}}{e(X)}
-\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=0\}}{1-e(X)},
\qquad
G_\vartheta(O^S)=H_e(O^S)(Y-\vartheta D).
\]
The contrast estimator, conditionally on the target sample, is the i.i.d. source average
\[
\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}
=
\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^n
\frac{\widehat p_{X_r,n}}{q_{X_r,n}}\,
G_\vartheta(O_r^S),
\]
where \(\widehat p_{X_r,n}/q_{X_r,n}\) means \(\widehat p_i/q_i\) on the cell \(X_r=x_i\). % lean: regularCell_crossAverage_identity

For each cell, the conditional cell mean equals the affine source contrast:
\[
\frac1{q_i}
\int_{\{X=x_i\}}G_\vartheta(O^S)\,dP_S
=
\frac1{q_i}
\left[
\int_{\{X=x_i\}}H_e(O^S)Y\,dP_S
-
\vartheta
\int_{\{X=x_i\}}H_e(O^S)D\,dP_S
\right].
\] % lean: regularCell_source_conditional_mean_variance_for_witness
By \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts},
\[
\int_{\{X=x_i\}}H_e(O^S)Y\,dP_S
=
q_i\Delta_Y(x_i),
\qquad
\int_{\{X=x_i\}}H_e(O^S)D\,dP_S
=
q_i\Delta_D(x_i).
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac1{q_i}
\int_{\{X=x_i\}}G_\vartheta(O^S)\,dP_S
=
\Delta_Y(x_i)-\vartheta\Delta_D(x_i).
\] % lean: regularCell_source_conditional_mean_variance_for_witness
Taking the mean of the displayed i.i.d. average gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}
\mid (X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}
\right]
=
\sum_i
\widehat p_i\bigl(\Delta_Y(x_i)-\vartheta\Delta_D(x_i)\bigr).
\] % lean: weighted_iid_average_mean_variance

For the variance, overlap gives \(|H_e(O^S)|\le1/\varepsilon\). Since \(Y\in[0,1]\), \(D\in\{0,1\}\), and \(\vartheta\in[-1,1]\),
\[
|Y-\vartheta D|
\le |Y|+|\vartheta|\,|D|
\le 2 .
\]
Thus
\[
|G_\vartheta(O^S)|
=
|H_e(O^S)|\,|Y-\vartheta D|
\le
\frac2\varepsilon .
\] % lean: abs_regularCellScore_le
Let
\[
F(O^S)=\frac{\widehat p_{X,n}}{q_{X,n}}\,G_\vartheta(O^S).
\]
Because the source observations are conditionally i.i.d.,
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(
\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^n F(O_r^S)
\mid (X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}
\right)
=
\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_P(F(O^S))
\le
\frac1n\mathbb E_P[F(O^S)^2].
\] % lean: weighted_iid_average_mean_variance
Using the cell support and the envelope just proved,
\[
\mathbb E_P[F(O^S)^2]
=
\sum_i
\left(\frac{\widehat p_i}{q_i}\right)^2
\int_{\{X=x_i\}}G_\vartheta(O^S)^2\,dP_S
\le
\sum_i
\left(\frac{\widehat p_i}{q_i}\right)^2
\frac4{\varepsilon^2}q_i
=
\frac4{\varepsilon^2}
\sum_i\frac{\widehat p_i^2}{q_i}.
\] % lean: weighted_iid_average_mean_variance
Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(
\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}
\mid (X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}
\right)
\le
\frac4{\varepsilon^2 n}
\sum_i\frac{\widehat p_i^2}{q_i}.
\] % lean: regularCell_source_conditional_mean_variance_for_witness
Replacing the finite index \(i\) by the cell notation \(x\) gives the two displayed formulas in the statement. % lean: regularCell_source_conditional_mean_variance
\end{proof}

\Cref{obj:lem:regular-cell-conditional-moment-bound} supplies the conditional score centering and variance control for the regular-cell procedure. The known cell probabilities enter only through the weights \(\widehat p_{x,n}/q_{x,n}\) and the resulting dispersion term, which is the regular-cell analogue of the collision-based scale used in \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction}]
Fix the admissible geometry array \(\mathfrak g=(P_{S,n}^X,w_n,e_n)_{n\ge1}\). We prove the three asserted conclusions in the order in which they appear.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Fix \(n\).  By \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} the geometry second moment at index \(n\) is
\[
\kappa_n
=
\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x),
\]
and \(P_{S,n}^X\) is a probability law on \(\mathcal X\).
% lean: geometryKish, AdmissibleGeometry
The unit-weight hypothesis states \(w_n(x)=1\) for \(P_{S,n}^X\)-almost every \(x\), hence also
\[
w_n(x)^2=1
\qquad
P_{S,n}^X\text{-a.s.}
\]
Two functions that agree almost everywhere have the same integral, so
\[
\kappa_n
=
\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\int 1\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
1 .
\]
% lean: no_shift_reduction (hGeom, first component), geometryKish

For the equality of covariate laws, let \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\) be measurable.  The change-of-measure clause of \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} states
\[
P_{T,n}(A)
=
\int_A w_n(x)\,dP_{S,n}^X(x).
\]
A \(P_{S,n}^X\)-almost-sure identity remains valid after restricting \(P_{S,n}^X\) to \(A\).  Therefore the identity \(w_n=1\) gives
\[
P_{T,n}(A)
=
\int_A 1\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
P_{S,n}^X(A).
\]
Since the two measures agree on every measurable set,
\[
P_{T,n}=P_{S,n}^X .
\]
% lean: no_shift_reduction (hGeom, second component), AdmissibleGeometry (change-of-measure clause)

\item Fix \(n\) and let \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\).  Write
\[
w_n^P=\frac{dP_{T,n}}{dP_{S,n}^X}
\]
for the transport-weight version induced by the laws of \(P\), and write
\[
\kappa_n^P
=
\int \bigl\{w_n^P(x)\bigr\}^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x),
\qquad
\mu_n
=
\int w_n^P(x)\,\Delta_D(x)\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
\]
for the associated second moment and transported first-stage mean.  In the notation of \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk}, the effective strength of \(P\) at index \(n\) is
\[
t_n
=
\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n^P}.
\]
% lean: transportWeight, kishDispersion, transportedFirstStage, effectiveStrength

By \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}, membership \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) means that \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) and that the index-\(n\) source covariate law, transport weight, and propensity of \(P\) are those of \(\mathfrak g\).  In particular the source covariate marginal of \(P\) is \(P_{S,n}^X\) and
\[
w_n^P(x)=w_n(x)
\qquad
P_{S,n}^X\text{-a.s.}
\]
(the almost-sure form records that a density-ratio version is determined up to \(P_{S,n}^X\)-null sets).  Integrating the squares of two functions that agree \(P_{S,n}^X\)-almost everywhere against the common law \(P_{S,n}^X\) gives
\[
\kappa_n^P
=
\int \bigl\{w_n^P(x)\bigr\}^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\kappa_n .
\]
% lean: no_shift_reduction (hkish), fixedGeometrySlice, kishDispersion, geometryKish

By the first step \(\kappa_n=1\), so \(\kappa_n^P=1\), and substituting this into the display for \(t_n\) yields
\[
t_n
=
\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n^P}
=
\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}
=
\frac{n\mu_n^2}{1}
=
n\mu_n^2 .
\]
% lean: no_shift_reduction (second conjunct), effectiveStrength

\item Fix \(t_0>0\).  We verify the hypotheses of \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} for the same \(\mathfrak g\), \(c\), \(\varepsilon\), \(\alpha\), \(k_n\), and \(N_n\).  Its target-sample-ratio hypothesis is \(c>0\) with \(N_n/n\to c\); its envelope-scale hypothesis is \(k_n>0\) for every \(n\), \(k_n\to\infty\), and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\); its overlap and noncoverage hypotheses are \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) and \(0<\alpha<1\); and its geometry hypothesis is admissibility of \(\mathfrak g\) at scale \(k_n\) and overlap constant \(\varepsilon\).  These are exactly the standing hypotheses of the present statement.

Its remaining hypothesis is the fixed-geometry slice condition: for every \(n\) and every transported array whose index-\(n\) law lies in \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), the conditions of \cref{obj:ass:two-sample-array,obj:ass:instrument-overlap,obj:ass:weight-envelope,obj:ass:weight-second-moment,obj:ass:degrading-array} hold.  This holds because \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} defines \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) as a subset of \(\mathcal P_n\), and each of those five conditions is one of the requirements listed in \cref{obj:def:model-class}; membership in \(\mathcal P_n\) therefore supplies all five directly.  Thus all hypotheses of \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} hold for \(\mathfrak g\).
% lean: no_shift_reduction (third conjunct), fixedGeometrySlice, TransportedIVClass

Applying \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} at this \(t_0\) therefore gives, with
\[
L_\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}},
\qquad
c_\alpha=\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16},
\qquad
C_\alpha=\max\left\{2,\;4L_\alpha+\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2}\right\},
\]
the two-sided bound on the fixed-geometry minimax value of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value},
\[
c_\alpha \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
\le
C_\alpha \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
which is the asserted conclusion since \(t_0>0\) was arbitrary.
% lean: fixed_geometry_frontier, fixedGeometryValue

\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item
By \cref{obj:lem:finite-cell-class-inhabited}, the finite-cell class is inhabited at every row under the carrier, ratio, overlap, and growth hypotheses.  Define
\[
B_c=32(1+c^{-1}),\qquad
L_{\alpha,c}=\left(\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2},\qquad
C_0=\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B_c\}.
\]
Since \(c>0\), \(B_c>0\); since \(\alpha>0\), \(L_{\alpha,c}\ge0\); hence
\[
C_0\ge2>0.
\]
Set
\[
\xi_c=4+\frac3c,\qquad
\eta_c=\xi_c^{-1/2},\qquad
\bar\varepsilon=\frac14+\frac12\eta_c .
\]
Then \(\xi_c>4\), so \(\sqrt{\xi_c}>2\), \(0<\eta_c<1/2\), and therefore
\[
0<\bar\varepsilon<\frac12 .
\]
Moreover,
\[
\bar\varepsilon\sqrt{\xi_c}
=
\frac14\sqrt{\xi_c}+\frac12
\ge 1,
\]
and consequently
\[
\bar\varepsilon^{-1}\le \sqrt{\xi_c},
\qquad
\bar\varepsilon^{-2}\le \xi_c=4+\frac3c .
\]
For the regular-cell variance constant at this interior overlap level,
\[
B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}
=
8(\bar\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1}),
\]
we obtain
\[
B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}
\le
8\left(4+\frac3c+\frac1c\right)
=
32(1+c^{-1})
=
B_c .
\]
Thus
\[
\left(\frac{2B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2}
\le
\left(\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2}
=
L_{\alpha,c}.
\]
Applying \cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} with
\[
c_-=c_+=1,\qquad \varepsilon=\bar\varepsilon,\qquad L=L_{\alpha,c},
\]
gives a regular-cell procedure \(C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) that is honest over the unit-regular class and satisfies, for every \(t_0>0\),
\[
R_{\mathrm{reg}}(C^{\mathrm{reg}},t_0)
\le
\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Since \(B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\le B_c\),
\[
R_{\mathrm{reg}}(C^{\mathrm{reg}},t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.regular_cell_unknown_weight_attainment

\item
Define the finite-cell procedure \(C^\circ\) by
\[
C^\circ_n(s^S,s^T)
=
\operatorname{Inv}_{k_n,n,N_n,L_{\alpha,c}}(s^S,s^T),
\]
where the inversion rule uses source mass \(q_x=1/k_n\) and propensity \(e(x)=1/2\), and where the totalized convention is
\[
C^\circ_n(s^S,s^T)=\Theta
\qquad\text{when }N_n<2.
\]
For \(N_n\ge2\), this is exactly
\[
H_i=2(2Z_i-1),
\]
\[
\widehat p_{x,n}=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{j=1}^{N_n}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=x\},
\]
\[
\widehat m_{G,n}(x)
=
\frac{k_n}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i,
\qquad
\widehat M_{G,n}
=
\sum_{x=1}^{k_n}\widehat p_{x,n}\widehat m_{G,n}(x),
\]
and
\[
\widehat K_n
=
1+
\frac{k_n}{N_n(N_n-1)}
\sum_{j\ne\ell}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}.
\]
Hence
\[
C^\circ_n
=
\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|
\le
L_{\alpha,c}\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}
\right\}.
\]
Fix a finite-cell row \(P\in\mathcal N_n\), and take the witnessing injected cells from \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source}.  On those cells the source masses are \(1/k_n\), and the propensity is \(1/2\) source-almost surely.  The same finite-cell source condition gives source covariate support on the injected cells; by the transport-domination clause in \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}, the target covariate law is also supported on the injected cells.  Therefore, under the product two-sample law, all source and target sample covariates lie in the injected support almost surely, and the source propensities equal \(1/2\) at all sampled source covariates almost surely.  On this event the regular-cell ambient extensions of the law's cell masses and propensity evaluate as the constants \(1/k_n\) and \(1/2\) on every sampled argument, so the regular-cell set \(C^{\mathrm{reg}}\), evaluated with the row's regular-cell design, agrees almost surely with \(C^\circ_n\).

The finite-to-regular bridge then gives
\[
C^\circ\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}
\]
and, for every \(t_0>0\),
\[
R_{\mathrm{cell}}(C^\circ,t_0)
\le
R_{\mathrm{reg}}(C^{\mathrm{reg}},t_0).
\]
Combining this inequality with the bound in the previous step yields
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C^\circ_n)]
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.finiteCellProcedure_upper_bridge

\item
It remains to prove the lower bound.  Choose the injected cells from the carrier hypothesis and denote them by
\[
\iota_{n,1},\ldots,\iota_{n,k_n}.
\]
Let \(P_{\circ,n}^X\) be the discrete probability law
\[
P_{\circ,n}^X
=
\frac1{k_n}\sum_{r=1}^{k_n}\delta_{\iota_{n,r}}.
\]
Because \(k_n>0\),
\[
P_{\circ,n}^X(\mathcal X)=1,
\qquad
P_{\circ,n}^X\{\iota_{n,r}\}=\frac1{k_n},
\qquad
P_{\circ,n}^X\bigl(\{\iota_{n,1},\ldots,\iota_{n,k_n}\}\bigr)=1 .
\]
Define a fixed geometry \(\mathfrak g^\circ\) by
\[
P_{S,n}^{X,\circ}=P_{T,n}^{\circ}=P_{\circ,n}^X,
\qquad
w_n(x)=1,
\qquad
e_n(x)=\frac12 .
\]
This geometry is admissible in the sense of \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class}.  Indeed, the source and target covariate laws are probabilities; since \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\),
\[
\varepsilon\le \frac12\le 1-\varepsilon;
\]
and, using \(k_n>0\),
\[
0\le 1\le 2k_n,
\qquad
\int 1\,dP_{\circ,n}^X=1,
\qquad
\int 1^2\,dP_{\circ,n}^X=1\le k_n.
\]
For every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\), the target-law identity is
\[
P_{T,n}^{\circ}(A)
=
P_{\circ,n}^X(A)
=
\int_A 1\,dP_{\circ,n}^X .
\]
A row in the fixed slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g^\circ)\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} has uniform source mass \(1/k_n\) on the injected support and propensity \(1/2\), so
\[
\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g^\circ)\subseteq \mathcal N_n
\qquad\text{for every }n.
\]
For each \(t_0>0\), \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited} supplies rows in this fixed slice with \(t_n\ge t_0\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.fixedGeometrySlice_eventually_inhabited

\item
Fix any \(\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\).  For a two-sample realization \(s=(s^S,s^T)\), define the support event
\[
\mathcal S_n(s)
=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
X_i^S\in\{\iota_{n,1},\ldots,\iota_{n,k_n}\}\ \text{for every source index }i,\\[2mm]
X_j^T\in\{\iota_{n,1},\ldots,\iota_{n,k_n}\}\ \text{for every target index }j .
\end{array}
\right.
\]
Construct an oracle procedure \(D^{\widetilde C}\) on the fixed geometry by
\[
D^{\widetilde C}_n(s,w,e)
=
\begin{cases}
\widetilde C_n(s), & \mathcal S_n(s),\\
\Theta, & \mathcal S_n(s)^c .
\end{cases}
\]
The event \(\mathcal S_n\) is measurable because it is built from the finite union of the measurable singleton cells, and the displayed rule is a measurable oracle rule that always returns a subset of \(\Theta\).

For every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g^\circ)\), both the source and target covariate marginals assign probability one to the injected support.  Under the product two-sample law,
\[
\mathbb P_P\{\mathcal S_n\}=1.
\]
Consequently, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g^\circ)\),
\[
D^{\widetilde C}_n=\widetilde C_n
\qquad
P\text{-almost surely under the two-sample law},
\]
and the coverage and expected-length quantities agree in the self-contained form
\[
\mathbb P_P\{\theta_T(P)\in D^{\widetilde C}_n\}
=
\mathbb P_P\{\theta_T(P)\in \widetilde C_n\},
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(D^{\widetilde C}_n)]
=
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)].
\]
Since \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g^\circ)\subseteq\mathcal N_n\) and the fixed slice is eventually nonempty at strength \(t_0\), the rowwise coverage infimum over \(\mathcal N_n\) is bounded above by the rowwise coverage infimum over \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g^\circ)\) eventually.  The honesty of \(\widetilde C\) in \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-feasible-honesty} therefore implies that \(D^{\widetilde C}\) is honest on the fixed geometry in the sense of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-honesty}.  Similarly,
\[
R_{\mathfrak g^\circ}(D^{\widetilde C},t_0)
\le
R_{\mathrm{cell}}(\widetilde C,t_0),
\]
with the empty-row convention harmless because the fixed slice is eventually inhabited; the length bound used for the rowwise suprema is
\[
\lambda(A)\le\lambda(\Theta)=2
\]
for every confidence set \(A\subseteq\Theta\).
Applying the lower half of \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} to \(\mathfrak g^\circ\) gives
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
\inf_{D\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g^\circ)}
R_{\mathfrak g^\circ}(D,t_0)
\le
R_{\mathfrak g^\circ}(D^{\widetilde C},t_0)
\le
R_{\mathrm{cell}}(\widetilde C,t_0).
\]
Taking the infimum over all
\(\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) yields
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
\inf_{\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)].
\]
Together with the construction of \(C^\circ\) and the positivity of \(C_0\), this proves the claim.
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let \(P_{\mathrm{assign},S}\) be the assigned-source law on full data and assignment, let \(P_S\) be its observed-source image, and let \(P_S^X\) be the source covariate marginal.  By \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts}, these are probability laws, the full-data marginal of \(P_{\mathrm{assign},S}\) is \(P_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=1)\), and for every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
\int_{\{O^S:X\in A\}}H(O^S)Y\,dP_S
=
\int_A\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x),
\qquad
\int_{\{O^S:X\in A\}}H(O^S)D\,dP_S
=
\int_A\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
This gives the two observed-source representation conclusions in the statement. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.sourceObservationFacts_of_class

\item For \(s\in\{0,1\}\), let \(P_s^X\) be the \(X\)-marginal of \(P_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=s)\), so \(P_1^X=P_S^X\) and \(P_0^X=P_T\).  Let \(\Gamma_{Y,s},\Gamma_{D,s}:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\) be the measurable contrast versions carried by the model class, with their \(P_s^X\)-a.e. values fixed by
\[
\int_A \Gamma_{Y,s}(x)\,dP_s^X(x)
=
\int_{\{X\in A\}}\{Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\}\,dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=s)
\]
and
\[
\int_A \Gamma_{D,s}(x)\,dP_s^X(x)
=
\int_{\{X\in A\}}\{\mathbf 1[D(1)=1]-\mathbf 1[D(0)=1]\}\,dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=s)
\]
for every measurable \(A\).  The source comparison uses the following inverse-propensity identity in the integrable cases used below: for measurable full-data functions \(F_0,F_1\),
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}
\left[
\frac{\mathbf 1[Z=1]}{e(X)}F_1
-
\frac{\mathbf 1[Z=0]}{1-e(X)}F_0
\right] dP_{\mathrm{assign},S}
=
\int_{\{X\in A\}}(F_1-F_0)\,dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=1).
\]
Indeed, splitting the left side over \(Z=1\) and \(Z=0\), conditional randomization gives the slice densities \(e(X)\) and \(1-e(X)\) with respect to \(P_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=1)\), and overlap makes the divisions legitimate; the two density factors cancel.

For the outcome contrast, apply this identity with \(F_z=Y(D(z))\), the treatment-potential outcome evaluated at the receipt induced by assignment \(z\).  Under the observation map, an assigned source record with \(Z=z\) has observed coordinates \(D=D(z)\) and \(Y=Y(D(z))\), so the left side is the observed-source score integral from \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts}.  The right side is
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}\{Y(D(1))-Y(D(0))\}\,dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=1),
\]
and exclusion rewrites \(Y(D(z))\) as \(Y^Z(z)\) \(P_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=1)\)-almost surely.  Applying the same inverse-propensity identity to the receipt functions \(F_z=\mathbf 1[D(z)=1]\), and using the observed coordinate \(D=D(Z)\), gives
\[
\int_A\Delta_Y\,dP_S^X=\int_A\Gamma_{Y,1}\,dP_S^X,
\qquad
\int_A\Delta_D\,dP_S^X=\int_A\Gamma_{D,1}\,dP_S^X
\]
for every measurable \(A\).  The source-contrast integrability hypotheses and the boundedness supplied by \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts} give the integrability needed to recover equality from equality of all measurable-set integrals, hence
\[
\Delta_Y=\Gamma_{Y,1}\quad P_S^X\text{-a.e.},
\qquad
\Delta_D=\Gamma_{D,1}\quad P_S^X\text{-a.e.}
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.ivRandomization_ipw_contrast

\item Let \(w=dP_T/dP_S^X\).  Transport domination gives the Radon--Nikodym change of measure
\[
\int w(x)g(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=\int g(x)\,dP_T(x)
\]
for the integrable functions used below.  Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int w(x)\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x)
&=
\int \Gamma_{Y,1}(x)\,dP_T(x)\\
&=
\int \Gamma_{Y,0}(x)\,dP_T(x)\\
&=
\int \{Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\}\,dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=0).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality is the outcome-contrast transport clause in \(\mathcal P_n\), and the last equality is the defining integral identity for \(\Gamma_{Y,0}\) with \(A=\mathcal X\).  On \(P_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=0)\)-almost every full-data record, exclusion gives \(Y^Z(z)=Y(D(z))\), and binary monotonicity gives
\[
Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)
=
\bigl(Y(1)-Y(0)\bigr)
\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}.
\]
Thus
\[
\int w(x)\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x)
=
\int \bigl(Y(1)-Y(0)\bigr)
\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}\,dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=0)
=
\mu_{Y,n}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.compact_causal_range

\item The same change of measure and the a.e. equality \(\Delta_D=\Gamma_{D,1}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int w(x)\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)
&=
\int \Gamma_{D,1}(x)\,dP_T(x)\\
&=
\int \Gamma_{D,0}(x)\,dP_T(x)\\
&=
\int \{\mathbf 1[D(1)=1]-\mathbf 1[D(0)=1]\}\,dP_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=0).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality is the receipt-contrast transport clause, and the last equality is the defining integral identity for \(\Gamma_{D,0}\) with \(A=\mathcal X\).  Binary monotonicity converts the final integrand into the complier indicator:
\[
\mathbf 1[D(1)=1]-\mathbf 1[D(0)=1]
=
\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}
\quad
P_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=0)\text{-a.s.}
\]
Hence
\[
\int w(x)\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)
=
P_n^F\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\}
=
\mu_n.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.compact_causal_range

\item Write
\[
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{co}}
=
\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\},
\qquad
\mu=P_n^F(\,\cdot\mid S=0).
\]
Target complier positivity in the model class gives
\[
0<\mu(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{co}})=\mu_n.
\]
Combining the two identities just proved,
\[
\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}
=
\frac{\int (Y(1)-Y(0))\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_{\mathrm{co}}}\,d\mu}
{\mu(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{co}})}
=
\mathbb E_{P_n^F}\!\left[
Y(1)-Y(0)\mid D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0,\ S=0
\right]
=
\theta_T .
\]
Full-data support gives \(0\le Y(0),Y(1)\le1\) \(\mu\)-almost surely, so
\[
-1\le Y(1)-Y(0)\le1
\quad \mu\text{-a.s.}
\]
Multiplying by \(\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_{\mathrm{co}}}\) and integrating yields
\[
-\mu(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{co}})
\le
\int (Y(1)-Y(0))\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_{\mathrm{co}}}\,d\mu
\le
\mu(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{co}}).
\]
Dividing by the positive denominator \(\mu(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{co}})\) gives
\[
-1\le \theta_T\le1,
\]
that is, \(\theta_T\in\Theta=[-1,1]\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.compact_causal_range
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse}]
Set
\[
  c_0=\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16},
  \qquad
  t_c=1 .
\]
Since \(0<\alpha<1\), \(1-\alpha>0\), and hence \(c_0>0\); also \(t_c>0\).
Fix \(\mathcal X\), \(N_n\), \(k_n\), and the hypotheses in the statement, and fix
\(t_0>0\). We prove the three asserted inequalities for this \(t_0\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item By \cref{obj:lem:finite-cell-class-inhabited}, for every \(n\) the finite-cell class
\(\mathcal N_n\) is inhabited. Since membership in \(\mathcal N_n\) includes membership in
\(\mathcal P_n\), the transported-IV class \(\mathcal P_n\) is also inhabited for every \(n\).
This supplies the nonempty rows used below in the oracle coverage infima. % lean: finiteCellClass_inhabited

\item Choose, for each \(n\), an injected measurable carrier
\[
  \xi_{n,\iota}\in\mathcal X,
  \qquad
  \iota\in I_n:=\{1,\ldots,k_n\},
\]
as supplied by the measurable-carrier hypothesis. Define the probability measure
\[
  \nu_n
  :=
  \frac1{k_n}\sum_{\iota\in I_n}\delta_{\xi_{n,\iota}} .
\]
The identity \(k_n\cdot k_n^{-1}=1\), using \(k_n>0\), gives
\(\nu_n(\mathcal X)=1\). Now define the deterministic geometry \(\mathfrak g\) by
\[
  P^{X,\mathfrak g}_{S,n}=\nu_n,
  \qquad
  P^{X,\mathfrak g}_{T,n}=\nu_n,
  \qquad
  w^{\mathfrak g}_n(x)=1,
  \qquad
  e^{\mathfrak g}_n(x)=\frac12 .
\]
This geometry is admissible: the source and target covariate laws are probabilities; the
propensity satisfies
\[
  \varepsilon\le \frac12\le 1-\varepsilon
\]
because \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\); the weight satisfies \(0\le 1\le 2k_n\) because
\(k_n\ge1\); and
\[
  \int w^{\mathfrak g}_n\,dP^{X,\mathfrak g}_{S,n}=1,
  \qquad
  \int (w^{\mathfrak g}_n)^2\,dP^{X,\mathfrak g}_{S,n}=1\le k_n .
\]
Finally, for every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
  P^{X,\mathfrak g}_{T,n}(A)
  =
  \nu_n(A)
  =
  \int_A 1\,d\nu_n
  =
  \int_A w^{\mathfrak g}_n\,dP^{X,\mathfrak g}_{S,n},
\]
so the change-of-measure requirement holds. % lean: oracle_converse

\item The fixed-geometry slice determined by \(\mathfrak g\) lies inside the finite-cell
submodel. Indeed, if
\(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), then \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) and its source covariate marginal
is \(\nu_n\), so
\[
  P_S^X\{\xi_{n,\iota}\}=\frac1{k_n}
  \quad\text{for every }\iota\in I_n,
\]
and
\[
  P_S^X\!\left(\{\xi_{n,\iota}:\iota\in I_n\}\right)=1 .
\]
The same source law is exactly the uniform pushforward on the injected carrier, and the slice
also gives \(e=1/2\) source-almost surely. These are the finite-cell source clauses in
\cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}, together with the transported-IV clauses already present in
\cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}. Hence
\[
  P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  P\in\mathcal N_n .
\] % lean: oracle_converse

\item Let \(\mathcal A^\Theta\) be the oracle procedure that always returns
\[
  \Theta=[-1,1].
\]
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_n\), the source contrasts \(\Delta_Y\) and \(\Delta_D\) are
measurable by construction and are integrable by \cref{obj:lem:transported-contrast-integrable}.
Applying \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} gives \(\theta_T(P,n)\in\Theta\). Therefore
\[
  \Pr_P\{\theta_T(P,n)\in\mathcal A^\Theta_n\}=1 .
\]
Since each row \(\mathcal P_n\) is inhabited, the rowwise coverage infimum is \(1\) for every
\(n\), and hence
\[
  \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
  \Pr_P\{\theta_T(P,n)\in\mathcal A^\Theta_n\}
  =
  1
  \ge 1-\alpha .
\]
Thus the oracle-honest class over which \(V^\star(t_0)\) and
\(V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)\) take their infima is inhabited. % lean: oracle_converse

\item The unit-weight reduction in \cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction}, applied to the
geometry \(\mathfrak g\), gives
\[
  \kappa_n(\mathfrak g)=1,
  \qquad
  P^{X,\mathfrak g}_{T,n}=P^{X,\mathfrak g}_{S,n},
\]
and, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\),
\[
  t_n(P)
  =
  n\,\mu_n(P)^2 .
\]
The same result gives the fixed-geometry lower frontier
\[
  c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
  \le
  V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0).
\] % lean: no_shift_reduction

\item We next compare the fixed-geometry value with the global oracle value. Let
\(\mathcal A\) be any globally oracle-honest procedure. Evaluating \(\mathcal A\) on the
declared weight and propensity of \(\mathfrak g\) gives a fixed-geometry procedure
\(\mathcal A^{\mathfrak g}\). For every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) and every
two-sample realization \(s\),
\[
  \mathcal A^{\mathfrak g}_n(s)
  =
  \mathcal A_n(s,w^{\mathfrak g}_n,e^{\mathfrak g}_n),
\]
and the fixed-geometry slice identifies \(w^{\mathfrak g}_n\) with an allowed
source-almost-sure version of the law-induced oracle weight and identifies
\(e^{\mathfrak g}_n\) with the law-induced propensity. Hence the coverage and expected-length
evaluations agree on the slice:
\[
  \Pr_P\{\theta_T(P,n)\in \mathcal A^{\mathfrak g}_n\}
  =
  \Pr_P\{\theta_T(P,n)\in \mathcal A_n\},
\]
and
\[
  \mathbb E_P\lambda(\mathcal A^{\mathfrak g}_n)
  =
  \mathbb E_P\lambda(\mathcal A_n).
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited}, the strength-restricted fixed-geometry
rows are eventually inhabited. With the empty-row convention that a coverage infimum is
\(1\), the global coverage infimum is bounded above by the fixed-slice coverage infimum
eventually; both quantities lie in \([0,1]\), so the same inequality passes to the liminf.
Thus \(\mathcal A^{\mathfrak g}\) is fixed-geometry honest. For risk, the rowwise supremum
over the fixed slice is bounded by the rowwise supremum over \(\mathcal P_n\):
\[
  \sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\\ t_n(P)\ge t_0}}
  \mathbb E_P\lambda(\mathcal A^{\mathfrak g}_n)
  \le
  \sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P_n\\ t_n(P)\ge t_0}}
  \mathbb E_P\lambda(\mathcal A_n).
\]
When the left row is empty its supremum is \(0\); otherwise it is nonnegative. In all cases
\(\lambda(\mathcal A_n)\le 2\), since \(\mathcal A_n\subseteq[-1,1]\), so the row suprema
are bounded by \(2\) and the inequality passes to the limsup. Therefore
\[
  V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
  \le
  R((\mathcal A_n),t_0).
\]
Taking the infimum over all globally oracle-honest \(\mathcal A\) and using the fixed-geometry
lower frontier above yields
\[
  c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
  \le
  V^\star(t_0).
\] % lean: fixedGeometryValue_le_frontierRisk

\item The same constructed geometry gives the finite-cell lower bound. Let \(\mathcal A\) be
any globally oracle-honest procedure. Define a fixed-geometry procedure by feeding
\(\mathcal A\) the canonical density ratio
\[
  x\mapsto
  \left(\frac{dP^{X,\mathfrak g}_{T,n}}{dP^{X,\mathfrak g}_{S,n}}(x)\right)
\]
and the propensity \(e^{\mathfrak g}_n\). On the fixed slice, the source and target covariate
laws equal the geometry laws and the propensity equals \(e^{\mathfrak g}_n\), so these inputs
match the oracle inputs used to evaluate \(\mathcal A\). Thus, for every
\(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\),
\[
  \mathbb E_P\lambda(\mathcal A^{\mathfrak g}_n)
  =
  \mathbb E_P\lambda(\mathcal A_n).
\]
The same coverage comparison as above, using the eventual inhabitation in
\cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited}, shows that \(\mathcal A^{\mathfrak g}\) is
fixed-geometry honest. By the slice inclusion already proved,
\[
  \{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):t_n(P)\ge t_0\}
  \subseteq
  \{P\in\mathcal N_n:t_n(P)\ge t_0\}.
\]
Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the single-row finite-cell risk bound gives
\[
  \sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\\ t_n(P)\ge t_0}}
  \mathbb E_P\lambda(\mathcal A^{\mathfrak g}_n)
  \le
  \sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal N_n\\ t_n(P)\ge t_0}}
  \mathbb E_P\lambda(\mathcal A_n).
\]
The left rows are nonnegative, and the finite-cell rows are bounded above by \(2\) because
confidence sets are evaluated inside \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). Passing to limsups gives
\[
  V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
  \le
  R_{\mathcal N}((\mathcal A_n),t_0),
\]
where the right-hand side is the finite-cell restricted oracle risk. Taking the infimum over
globally oracle-honest \(\mathcal A\) and using the fixed-geometry lower frontier above gives
\[
  c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
  \le
  V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0).
\] % lean: oracle_converse

\item Finally suppose \(0<t_0\le t_c=1\). Since the exponent \(-1/2\) is nonpositive,
\[
  1\le t_0^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}=1 .
\]
Substituting this into the global lower bound proved above gives
\[
  c_0\le V^\star(t_0).
\] % lean: oracle_converse
\end{enumerate}
The constants \(c_0\) and \(t_c\) were chosen as functions only of
\((\alpha,\varepsilon,c)\), completing the proof. % lean: oracle_converse
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment}]
\textbf{1. Construction.}
Choose an admissible deterministic geometry \(\mathfrak g\) supplied by the admissible-geometry hypothesis; this choice is used below in two places: it gives eventual row nonemptiness, and in the final step it carries the fixed-geometry frontier bounds across to the oracle value. Put
\[
L=L_\alpha=\left(\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
C_0=\max\left\{2,4L+\frac8{\varepsilon^2}\right\}.
\]
Since \(\alpha>0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), \(L>0\), and hence \(C_0\ge2>0\). Define the oracle procedure from the oracle input \((w,e)\) by
\[
C_n
=
\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
\left|\widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n\right|
\le
L\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}
\right\},
\]
with \(\widehat A_n\), \(\widehat B_n\), and \(\widehat\kappa_n\) formed from that input. The set is contained in \(\Theta\) by construction. Its sample-by-parameter graph is measurable for every measurable oracle pair \((w,e)\), because the three displayed statistics are measurable functions of the source sample and the defining inequality is Borel. If two admissible versions of \(w\) agree \(P_S^X\)-almost surely, then their source-sample evaluations agree with probability one, and the same equality holds after adjoining the independent target covariate sample. Thus the random set is a valid oracle procedure and is invariant to the density-ratio representative under the product two-sample law. % lean: inversionHandle_weight_version_ae_eq

\textbf{2. Pointwise receipt and scale facts.}
Fix \(n\ge1\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_n\). Write
\[
\mu_n=\mu_n(P),\qquad
\kappa_n=\kappa_n(P),\qquad
t_n=t_n(P)=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}.
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range}, \(\theta_T\in\Theta\) and \(\mu_n\) equals the target complier share; its strict positivity \(\mu_n>0\) comes separately from target complier positivity in \cref{obj:ass:target-complier-positivity}. The change-of-measure identity for the canonical density ratio gives
\[
\int w(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=1.
\]
The weight envelope gives square-integrability of \(w\), and Cauchy--Schwarz then yields
\[
1\le \int w(x)^2\,dP_S^X(x)=\kappa_n,
\]
so \(\kappa_n>0\).

We shall use the following pullout consequence of \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts}. Let \(q\) be an integrable source-observation function and let \(d\) be an integrable covariate function such that, for every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}q(O^S)\,dP_S
=
\int_A d(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
If \(w\) is the measurable transport weight and both weighted functions are integrable, then
\[
\int w(X)q(O^S)\,dP_S
=
\int w(x)d(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
Indeed, the setwise identity says that \(d(X)\) is a version of the conditional expectation of \(q(O^S)\) given \(X\); multiplying by the \(X\)-measurable factor \(w(X)\) and integrating gives the display.

Let
\[
G_{D,n}(O^S)=w(X)H(O^S)D .
\]
Applying the preceding pullout identity to the receipt-score identity in \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts}, with \(q(O^S)=H(O^S)D\) and \(d(x)=\Delta_D(x)\), and then using \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range}, gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[G_{D,n}]
=
\int w(x)\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)
=
\mu_n .
\]
Overlap gives \(|H(O^S)|\le\varepsilon^{-1}\) almost surely and \(D\in\{0,1\}\), hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P(G_{D,n})
\le
\mathbb E_P[G_{D,n}^2]
\le
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^2}
\int w(x)^2\,dP_S^X(x)
=
\frac{\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2}.
\]
Therefore the source-sample average \(\widehat B_n=n^{-1}\sum_iG_{D,n}(O_i^S)\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat B_n]=\mu_n,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat B_n)\le\frac{\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2 n}.
\]
Applying \cref{obj:lem:score-receipt-bad-slope-probability} gives
\[
P\!\left(\frac{\mu_n}{2}<|\widehat B_n-\mu_n|\right)
\le
\frac{4}{\varepsilon^2t_n}.
\]
The empirical scale \(\widehat\kappa_n=n^{-1}\sum_i w(X_i)^2\) is nonnegative and has mean
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_n]=\kappa_n .
\] % lean: scoreReceiptMean_bad_probability, empiricalKish_mean

\textbf{3. Expected length at a fixed law.}
For a realized source sample set
\[
r_n=L\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}.
\]
The acceptance set is the intersection of \(\Theta=[-1,1]\) with the inverse image of \([-r_n,r_n]\) under the affine map
\[
\vartheta\mapsto \widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n .
\]
Consequently, always \(\lambda(C_n)\le2\), and on
\[
\left\{|\widehat B_n-\mu_n|\le\frac{\mu_n}{2}\right\}
\]
one has \(|\widehat B_n|\ge\mu_n/2\) and therefore
\[
\lambda(C_n)\le\frac{2r_n}{|\widehat B_n|}
\le\frac{4r_n}{\mu_n}.
\]
Taking expectations and using Jensen's inequality,
\[
\mathbb E_P[r_n]
\le
L\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_n]}{n}}
=
L\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{n}}.
\]
Together with the bad-slope bound,
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
\frac{4L}{\sqrt{t_n}}
+
\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2t_n}.
\]
If \(t_n\ge1\), then \(t_n^{-1}\le t_n^{-1/2}\), so
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
\left(4L+\frac8{\varepsilon^2}\right)t_n^{-1/2}.
\]
If \(0<t_n<1\), the bound \(\lambda(C_n)\le2\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]\le2.
\]
Both cases are summarized, for \(n\ge1\), as
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_n^{-1/2}\}.
\] % lean: scoreInversion_expectedLength_frontier_le

\textbf{4. Coverage at a fixed law.}
The row definitions in \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty,obj:def:frontier-risk} start at \(n\ge1\). For the rowwise limiting argument, extend the auxiliary error sequence to the single index \(n=0\) by setting \(\delta_0=1\); this added row is immaterial for the limiting statements. At that auxiliary index the coverage probability is nonnegative, and
\[
1-\alpha-\delta_0=-\alpha\le0.
\]
Now let \(n\ge1\) and set
\[
\delta_n=\frac{16k_n^2}{n}.
\]
Define the centered affine score
\[
\xi_{n,P}(O^S)
=
w(X)H(O^S)\{Y-\theta_TD\},
\qquad
\overline\xi_{n,P}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_{n,P}(O_i^S).
\]
Apply the preceding pullout identity to the two score identities in \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts}. With \(q(O^S)=H(O^S)Y\), \(d=\Delta_Y\), and with \(q(O^S)=H(O^S)D\), \(d=\Delta_D\), it gives
\[
\int w(X)H(O^S)Y\,dP_S
=
\int w(x)\Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x)
=
\mu_{Y,n},
\]
and
\[
\int w(X)H(O^S)D\,dP_S
=
\int w(x)\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x)
=
\mu_n,
\]
where the last equalities use \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range}. Combining these identities with
\[
\frac{\mu_{Y,n}(P)}{\mu_n(P)}=\theta_T
\]
from \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range} gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[\xi_{n,P}]=0,
\qquad
\overline\xi_{n,P}=\widehat A_n-\theta_T\widehat B_n .
\]
Since \(Y\in[0,1]\), \(D\in\{0,1\}\), and \(\theta_T\in[-1,1]\),
\[
|Y-\theta_TD|\le2 .
\]
Together with overlap,
\[
\xi_{n,P}(O^S)^2
\le
\frac{4w(X)^2}{\varepsilon^2}
\quad\text{almost surely},
\]
and hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P(\overline\xi_{n,P})
\le
\frac{4\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2 n}.
\]
The envelope \(0\le w\le2k_n\) gives
\[
w(X)^4\le4k_n^2w(X)^2
\quad\text{almost surely},
\]
so the empirical scale satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\kappa_n)
\le
\frac{4k_n^2\kappa_n}{n}.
\]
Chebyshev's inequality and \(\kappa_n\ge1\) yield
\[
P\!\left(\widehat\kappa_n<\frac{\kappa_n}{2}\right)
\le
\frac{16k_n^2}{n\kappa_n}
\le
\delta_n .
\]
Let
\[
a_{n,P}=L\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{2n}}.
\]
Using \(L^2=8/(\alpha\varepsilon^2)\), another application of Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
P\!\left(a_{n,P}<|\overline\xi_{n,P}|\right)
\le
\frac{4\kappa_n/(\varepsilon^2 n)}{a_{n,P}^2}
=
\alpha .
\]
On the complement of these two bad source-sample events,
\[
|\widehat A_n-\theta_T\widehat B_n|
=
|\overline\xi_{n,P}|
\le
L\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{2n}}
\le
L\sqrt{\frac{\widehat\kappa_n}{n}},
\]
and \(\theta_T\in\Theta\), so \(\theta_T\in C_n\). The events depend only on the source sample, and the product two-sample law has the source marginal as its first factor. The union bound therefore gives, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n\),
\[
P(\theta_T\in C_n)\ge1-\alpha-\delta_n .
\] % lean: empiricalKish_lower_tail_le, probability_abs_sub_mean_gt_le

\textbf{5. Oracle honesty.}
The sequence \(\delta_n\) tends to zero because \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\). By \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited}, applied to the chosen admissible \(\mathfrak g\) and the fixed threshold \(1\), the model-class rows are eventually nonempty. The coverage probabilities lie in \([0,1]\), and the preceding display holds uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_n\). Applying the rowwise limiting bound to these ordinary nonempty row infima gives
\[
1-\alpha
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}P(\theta_T(P)\in C_n).
\]
The explicit score-inversion formula for \(C_n\) is the procedure being evaluated, and the representative-invariance established in the construction makes the oracle evaluation well-defined. Hence \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\) by \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}. % lean: abstractClass_coverage_liminf, scoreProcedure_oracleCoverageClassInf_eq

\textbf{6. Frontier risk and oracle values.}
Using the same harmless extension at the single auxiliary row, define
\[
\ell_n(P)=
\begin{cases}
0, & n=0,\\
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)], & n\ge1 .
\end{cases}
\]
The row loss \(\ell_n(P)\) is nonnegative. At the auxiliary index \(n=0\) the displayed value is \(0\); on the rows \(n\ge1\), the fixed-law expected-length calculation above gives
\[
\ell_n(P)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_n(P)^{-1/2}\}.
\]
The score-inversion row formula identifies the strength-restricted risk row with the supremum of \(\ell_n(P)\) for every \(n\ge1\). On the strength-restricted row \(t_n(P)\ge t_0>0\), the capped inverse-root map is decreasing, so
\[
\min\{1,t_n(P)^{-1/2}\}
\le
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Taking the row supremum and then the limiting upper supremum gives
\[
R((C_n),t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\] % lean: abstractClassFrontierRisk_le, scoreProcedure_frontierRiskRow_eq

We next record the fixed-geometry comparison that is part of the same conclusion. Let \(\mathfrak g'\) be any admissible deterministic geometry. A law in the fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g')\) of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} is, in particular, a law in \(\mathcal P_n\), so the model-class implication hypotheses supply the two-sample, overlap, envelope, second-moment, and degradation clauses required by \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}. Therefore, for every \(t_0>0\),
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g'}^\star(t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\] % lean: fixed_geometry_frontier

It remains to pass from fixed-geometry values to the global oracle value. For every oracle-honest sequence \((E_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\), \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-value-le-frontier-risk} applied to the chosen admissible geometry \(\mathfrak g\) gives
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
\le
R((E_n),t_0).
\]
Combining this inequality with the fixed-geometry lower bound just displayed yields
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
R((E_n),t_0)
\]
for every \((E_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\). The class over which \cref{obj:def:oracle-value} takes the infimum is nonempty because the constructed score-inversion sequence is oracle honest. Taking that infimum gives
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V^\star(t_0).
\]
For the upper oracle-value bound, the risk of any oracle procedure is nonnegative: each row is a supremum of nonnegative expected lengths, with value \(0\) under the empty-row convention in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk}, and \(\lambda(C_n)\le2\). Thus the infimum in \cref{obj:def:oracle-value} is bounded below, and the constructed honest sequence is an admissible candidate. Consequently,
\[
V^\star(t_0)
\le
R((C_n),t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\] % lean: fixedGeometryValue_le_frontierRisk

This proves oracle honesty of the constructed score-inversion sequence, its frontier-risk upper bound, the two-sided oracle-value bounds, and the two-sided fixed-geometry value bounds at every admissible geometry.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}]
Fix \(t_0>0\).  By \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited}, the strength-restricted fixed-geometry rows
\[
\bigl\{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):t_n(P)\ge t_0\bigr\}
\]
are nonempty for all sufficiently large \(n\).

\begin{enumerate}
\item Define
\[
\rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{8},
\qquad
H(t_0)=\min\left\{\frac14,\rho\,t_0^{-1/2}\right\}.
\]
Then \(\rho>0\) and \(H(t_0)>0\).  By \cref{obj:lem:geometry-handle-eventually-inhabited}, for all sufficiently large \(n\) there is a least-favorable family
\[
\bigl(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}: |h|\le H(t_0)\bigr)
\]
inside \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), with \(t_n(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g})=t_0\) and
\[
\left\|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le 2\rho .
\]
By \cref{obj:def:least-favorable-witness}, the target parameter in this family is
\[
\theta_T(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g})=\frac12+h .
\]
% lean: geometryHandle_eventually_inhabited

\item Let \(\mathscr C\) be any fixed-geometry oracle-honest procedure.  For each \(n\), set
\[
\Gamma_n(\mathscr C)
=
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}
Q_P^N\{\theta_T(P)\in \mathscr C_n\}
\]
and
\[
\Lambda_n(\mathscr C;t_0)
=
\sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\\ t_n(P)\ge t_0}}
\mathbb E_P\bigl[\lambda(\mathscr C_n)\bigr],
\]
using value \(0\) for the empty infimum or supremum rows.  Here, for a law \(P\in\mathcal P_n\), \(Q_P^{N}\) denotes the joint law of the source sample of size \(n\) and the target covariate sample of size \(N_n\) generated by \(P\); for the least-favourable laws this is the notation \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g,N}\) already in use.  Since every confidence set is contained in \(\Theta=[-1,1]\),
\[
0\le \Gamma_n(\mathscr C)\le 1,
\qquad
0\le \Lambda_n(\mathscr C;t_0)\le 2 .
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), put
\[
\mathcal J=[1/2-H(t_0),\,1/2+H(t_0)].
\]
Since \(H(t_0)\le1/4\), \(\mathcal J\subseteq\Theta\).  For \(u\in\mathcal J\), define
\[
Q_{n,u}=Q_{n,u-1/2}^{\mathfrak g}.
\]
Then \(Q_{n,u}\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), \(t_n(Q_{n,u})=t_0\), \(\theta_T(Q_{n,u})=u\), and
\[
\left\|Q_{n,u}^{N}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le 2\rho .
\]
Tonelli's theorem, applied to the measurable graph of \(\mathscr C_n\), gives
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}}\lambda(\mathscr C_n)
=
\int_{\Theta}
Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\{u\in\mathscr C_n\}\,du .
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}}\lambda(\mathscr C_n)
&\ge
\int_{\mathcal J}
Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\{u\in\mathscr C_n\}\,du\\
&\ge
\int_{\mathcal J}
\left[
Q_{n,u}^{N}\{u\in\mathscr C_n\}
-
\left\|Q_{n,u}^{N}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g,N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\right]du\\
&\ge
2H(t_0)\bigl(\Gamma_n(\mathscr C)-2\rho\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\) belongs to the strength-restricted row, this yields
\[
2H(t_0)\bigl(\Gamma_n(\mathscr C)-2\rho\bigr)
\le
\Lambda_n(\mathscr C;t_0)
\]
eventually in \(n\).
% lean: coverage_tv_expectedLength_lower

\item Fixed-geometry honesty gives
\[
1-\alpha
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Gamma_n(\mathscr C),
\]
because the rows are eventually nonempty.  The map \(x\mapsto 2H(t_0)(x-2\rho)\) is increasing, so the preceding display implies
\[
2H(t_0)(1-\alpha-2\rho)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Lambda_n(\mathscr C;t_0)
=
R_{\mathfrak g}(\mathscr C,t_0).
\]
Now
\[
1-\alpha-2\rho=\frac{3(1-\alpha)}4 .
\]
If \(\rho t_0^{-1/2}\le1/4\), then \(H(t_0)=\rho t_0^{-1/2}\), and
\[
2H(t_0)(1-\alpha-2\rho)
=
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\,t_0^{-1/2}
\ge
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
If \(1/4\le\rho t_0^{-1/2}\), then \(H(t_0)=1/4\), and
\[
2H(t_0)(1-\alpha-2\rho)
=
\frac{3(1-\alpha)}8
\ge
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}
\ge
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Thus every fixed-geometry honest procedure satisfies
\[
R_{\mathfrak g}(\mathscr C,t_0)
\ge
c_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\qquad
c_\alpha=\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}.
\]
The full rule \(\mathscr C_n^{\mathrm{all}}=\Theta\) is fixed-geometry honest, since \(\theta_T(P)\in\Theta\) for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) by \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range}.  Hence the honest class over which \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} takes its infimum is nonempty, and
\[
c_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0).
\]
% lean: fixed_geometry_frontier

\item For the upper bound, define
\[
L_\alpha=\left(\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
For \(n\ge1\), let the oracle score-inversion rule of \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle}, specialized to the fixed geometry, be
\[
\mathscr C_n^{\mathrm{sc}}
=
\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
\left|\widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n\right|
\le
L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\widehat\kappa_n}{n}}
\right\},
\]
where, using the fixed geometry density-ratio and propensity inputs,
\[
\widehat A_n
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
w_n(X_i)
\left\{\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z_i=1\}}{e_n(X_i)}
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z_i=0\}}{1-e_n(X_i)}
\right\}Y_i,
\]
\[
\widehat B_n
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
w_n(X_i)
\left\{\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z_i=1\}}{e_n(X_i)}
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z_i=0\}}{1-e_n(X_i)}
\right\}D_i,
\]
and
\[
\widehat\kappa_n
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n w_n(X_i)^2 .
\]
At \(n=0\), set \(\mathscr C_0^{\mathrm{sc}}=\varnothing\).  The sample-by-parameter graph required in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk} is measurable by the measurability of the fixed geometry weight and propensity and by closure of measurable functions under finite sums, products, absolute values, intervals, and square roots.  The fixed-geometry invariance clause is immediate because the rule is written from the deterministic geometry inputs supplied to the fixed slice.
% lean: fixed_geometry_frontier

\item Fix \(n\ge1\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\).  Write
\[
\mu_n=\mu_n(P),\qquad
\kappa_n=\kappa_n(P),\qquad
t_n=t_n(P)=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}.
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range}, \(\theta_T(P)\in\Theta\) and \(\mu_n\) agrees with the target complier share.  The target-complier-positivity clause in \cref{obj:def:model-class} gives \(\mu_n>0\).  The transport weight has mean one under \(P_S^X\), so Cauchy's inequality gives
\[
1\le \kappa_n,
\qquad
\kappa_n>0.
\]
We shall use the following weighted form of the source-observation identities.  Suppose that \(F(O^S)\) and \(d(X)\) are integrable, \(w_n\) is the fixed geometry weight, the products below are integrable, and
\[
\int_{\{X\in \mathcal A\}}F(O^S)\,dP_S
=
\int_{\mathcal A}d(x)\,dP_S^X(x)
\]
for every measurable \(\mathcal A\).  Then the defining property of conditional expectation with respect to the sigma-field generated by \(X\), followed by multiplication by the \(X\)-measurable function \(w_n(X)\), gives
\[
\int w_n(X)F(O^S)\,dP_S
=
\int w_n(x)d(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
The needed integrability conditions follow here from the overlap bound, the bounded outcome and contrast statements in \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts} together with the binary receipt indicator carried by the model class of \cref{obj:def:model-class}, and the fixed-geometry envelope.  Taking
\[
F(O^S)=
\left\{\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=1\}}{e(X)}
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=0\}}{1-e(X)}
\right\}D
\]
and \(d=\Delta_D\), \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts} supplies the unweighted set-integral identity, while \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range} identifies the weighted covariate integral with \(\mu_n\).  Hence
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat B_n]=\mu_n .
\]
The overlap bound gives
\[
\left|
\left\{\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=1\}}{e(X)}
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=0\}}{1-e(X)}
\right\}D
\right|
\le
\varepsilon^{-1},
\]
and hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat B_n)
\le
\frac{\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2 n}.
\]
Therefore \cref{obj:lem:score-receipt-bad-slope-probability} gives
\[
P\left\{\frac{\mu_n}{2}<|\widehat B_n-\mu_n|\right\}
\le
\frac{4}{\varepsilon^2 t_n}.
\]
% lean: scoreReceiptMean_bad_probability

\item The affine inversion set has length at most \(2\), and on the event
\[
|\widehat B_n-\mu_n|\le \frac{\mu_n}{2}
\]
it has length at most
\[
\frac{2L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}}{|\widehat B_n|}
\le
\frac{4L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}}{\mu_n}.
\]
Since \(\widehat\kappa_n\ge0\) and
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_n]=\kappa_n,
\]
Jensen's inequality gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\sqrt{\frac{\widehat\kappa_n}{n}}
\le
\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{n}}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\mathscr C_n^{\mathrm{sc}})]
\le
\frac{4L_\alpha}{\sqrt{t_n}}
+
\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2t_n}.
\]
Together with \(\lambda(\mathscr C_n^{\mathrm{sc}})\le2\), this implies
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\mathscr C_n^{\mathrm{sc}})]
\le
\max\{2,4L_\alpha+8\varepsilon^{-2}\}
\min\{1,t_n^{-1/2}\}.
\]
The same inequality holds at \(n=0\) by the definition \(\mathscr C_0^{\mathrm{sc}}=\varnothing\).
% lean: scoreInversion_expectedLength_frontier_le

\item It remains to verify honesty.  Define
\[
\delta_n=
\begin{cases}
1, & n=0,\\
16k_n^2/n, & n\ge1 .
\end{cases}
\]
Since \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\), one has \(\delta_n\to0\).  Fix \(n\ge1\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), and put
\[
\widehat S_n
=
\widehat A_n-\theta_T(P)\widehat B_n .
\]
Applying the weighted pullout identity from the preceding step first with
\[
F(O^S)=
\left\{\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=1\}}{e(X)}
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=0\}}{1-e(X)}
\right\}Y
\]
and \(d=\Delta_Y\), and then with
\[
F(O^S)=
\left\{\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=1\}}{e(X)}
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=0\}}{1-e(X)}
\right\}D
\]
and \(d=\Delta_D\), the set-integral identities in \cref{obj:lem:source-observation-facts} and the weighted integral identities in \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range} give
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat S_n]
=
\mu_{Y,n}-\theta_T(P)\mu_n
=
0.
\]
Moreover, since \(Y\in[0,1]\), \(D\in\{0,1\}\), \(\theta_T(P)\in[-1,1]\), and the overlap bound gives \(\varepsilon\le e(X)\le1-\varepsilon\),
\[
\left|
\left\{\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=1\}}{e(X)}
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=0\}}{1-e(X)}
\right\}
\bigl(Y-\theta_T(P)D\bigr)
\right|
\le
\frac{2}{\varepsilon}.
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat S_n)
\le
\frac{4\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2 n}.
\]
Also,
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_n]=\kappa_n,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\kappa_n)
\le
\frac{4k_n^2\kappa_n}{n},
\]
because \(0\le w_n\le2k_n\) implies
\[
w_n^4\le 4k_n^2w_n^2 .
\]
Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
P\{\widehat\kappa_n<\kappa_n/2\}
\le
\frac{16k_n^2}{n\kappa_n}
\le
\frac{16k_n^2}{n}.
\]
With
\[
a_n=L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{2n}},
\]
another application of Chebyshev gives
\[
P\{|\widehat S_n|>a_n\}
\le
\frac{4\kappa_n/(\varepsilon^2 n)}
{L_\alpha^2\kappa_n/(2n)}
=
\alpha .
\]
Let
\[
G_n=
\left\{\widehat\kappa_n\ge\frac{\kappa_n}{2}\right\}
\cap
\left\{|\widehat S_n|\le a_n\right\}.
\]
The statistics defining the score-inversion rule and the two bad events defining \(G_n^c\) depend only on the source sample.  Under the joint two-sample law \(Q_P^N\), the source and target samples have product law, so \(Q_P^N(G_n)=P(G_n)\) for these source-sample events.  On \(G_n\), for every target covariate sample,
\[
\widehat\kappa_n\ge \frac{\kappa_n}{2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
|\widehat S_n|
\le
L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{2n}}
\le
L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\widehat\kappa_n}{n}},
\]
so \(\theta_T(P)\in\mathscr C_n^{\mathrm{sc}}\).  Hence
\[
Q_P^N\{\theta_T(P)\in\mathscr C_n^{\mathrm{sc}}\}
\ge
1-\alpha-\delta_n .
\]
At \(n=0\) the same lower bound is immediate from \(\delta_0=1\).  Since \(\delta_n\to0\) and the fixed-geometry slice is eventually nonempty, \(\mathscr C^{\mathrm{sc}}\) is fixed-geometry oracle honest.
% lean: abstractClass_coverage_liminf

\item Let
\[
C_\alpha=\max\{2,4L_\alpha+8\varepsilon^{-2}\}.
\]
The pointwise bound from the preceding steps implies, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\mathscr C_n^{\mathrm{sc}})]
\le
C_\alpha\min\{1,t_n(P)^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Taking the supremum over the row \(t_n(P)\ge t_0\), then the limsup over \(n\), gives
\[
R_{\mathfrak g}(\mathscr C^{\mathrm{sc}},t_0)
\le
C_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Since \(\mathscr C^{\mathrm{sc}}\) is fixed-geometry oracle honest,
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)
\le
C_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Combining this with the lower bound proves the two displayed inequalities.  The constants \(c_\alpha\) and \(C_\alpha\) involve only \(\alpha\) and \(\varepsilon\), so the bounds are uniform over admissible deterministic geometry arrays \(\mathfrak g\).
% lean: abstractClassFrontierRisk_le
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Define
\[
B_{\varepsilon,c}=8(\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1}),\qquad
C_0=\max\{2,4\sqrt2L+8B_{\varepsilon,c}\}.
\]
The hypotheses give \(B_{\varepsilon,c}\ge0\), \(L\ge0\), and hence \(C_0\ge2>0\). By \cref{obj:lem:regular-finite-cell-class-inhabited}, the regular class is inhabited at every row \(n\). Let \(C^\star\) be the regular-cell procedure which, on a regular-cell input consisting of a two-sample draw, the selected cell design, the displayed source cell masses, and the displayed propensity values, returns the score-inversion set
\[
\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|
\le L\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\}
\]
when \(N_n\ge2\), and returns \(\Theta\) when \(N_n<2\). The measurable graph and \(\Theta\)-valuedness are part of this construction. For every \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), the input obtained from the two-sample law uses the same source cell masses \(q_{x,n}\) and propensity \(e\), so \(C^\star_n\) agrees almost surely with the displayed \(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), with the stated convention for \(N_n<2\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.regular_cell_unknown_weight_attainment

\item We verify regular-cell honesty for \(C^\star\). Since the regular class is inhabited at every \(n\), the row coverage convention in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) is the ordinary infimum over \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\). Put
\[
\delta_n=\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}}
P\!\left(\widehat K_n<\frac{\kappa_n(P)}2\right),
\]
where the probability is over the target covariate sample. The sample-size ratio gives \(n>0\) and \(N_n\ge2\) eventually. For those rows, write \(\widehat K_n=1+\widehat\kappa_{U,n}\). The collision calculation gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_{U,n}]=\kappa_n(P),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\kappa_{U,n})
\le \frac{32k_n^2}{c_-^2N_n}.
\]
Also \(\kappa_n(P)\ge1\). If \(\kappa_n(P)\le2\), then \(\widehat K_n\ge1\ge\kappa_n(P)/2\) pointwise. If \(\kappa_n(P)>2\), Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
P\!\left(\widehat K_n<\frac{\kappa_n(P)}2\right)
\le
\frac{128k_n^2}{c_-^2N_n\kappa_n(P)^2}
\le
\frac{128k_n^2}{c_-^2N_n}.
\]
Because \(N_n/n\to c>0\) and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\), the last bound tends to zero uniformly on the regular class; hence \(\delta_n\to0\).

Fix such an \(n\) and \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), and set \(\theta=\theta_T(P)\). Let
\[
\mathsf{score}_{n,P}(s)
=
\widehat M_{Y,n}(s)-\theta\,\widehat M_{D,n}(s),
\qquad
\mathsf{smallK}_{n,P}
=
\left\{s:\widehat K_n(s)<\frac{\kappa_n(P)}2\right\},
\]
and
\[
\mathsf{largeScore}_{n,P}
=
\left\{s:
L\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n(P)}{2n}}
<
|\mathsf{score}_{n,P}(s)|
\right\}.
\]
The source sample lies in the witnessing finite cell support with probability one, and target domination transfers that support statement to the target sample. On the event where this support condition holds and neither \(\mathsf{smallK}_{n,P}\) nor \(\mathsf{largeScore}_{n,P}\) occurs, the inequality
\[
|\mathsf{score}_{n,P}(s)|
\le L\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n(P)}{2n}}
\le L\sqrt{\frac{\widehat K_n(s)}{n}}
\]
places \(\theta_T(P)\) in \(C^\star_n(s)\).

It remains to bound the large-score event. The finite witnessing support gives the needed integrability of the assignment and receipt contrasts. The transported causal-range conclusions in \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range} put \(\theta\in\Theta\) and identify \(\theta\) with the transported ratio \(\mu_{Y,n}(P)/\mu_n(P)\). On the witnessing cells \(\xi_i\), the regular-cell score-mean identity gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\{H(O^S)(Y-\theta D)\mid X=\xi_i\}
=
\Delta_Y(\xi_i)-\theta\Delta_D(\xi_i),
\]
and the transported ratio identity yields the centered finite-cell score equation
\[
\sum_i P_T\{\xi_i\}
\{\Delta_Y(\xi_i)-\theta_T(P)\Delta_D(\xi_i)\}=0.
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:regular-cell-conditional-moment-bound}, conditionally on the target sample the score has mean
\(\sum_x\widehat p_{x,n}\{\Delta_Y(x)-\theta\Delta_D(x)\}\) and conditional variance at most
\[
\frac{4}{\varepsilon^2n}\sum_x\frac{\widehat p_{x,n}^2}{q_{x,n}}.
\]
The target-sampling and multinomial calculations give
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(
\sum_x\widehat p_{x,n}\{\Delta_Y(x)-\theta\Delta_D(x)\}
\right)
\le \frac4{N_n},
\qquad
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\sum_x\frac{\widehat p_{x,n}^2}{q_{x,n}}\right]
\le \kappa_n(P)+\frac{k_n}{c_-N_n}.
\]
Combining these bounds gives, uniformly for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[\mathsf{score}_{n,P}]=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_P(\mathsf{score}_{n,P})
\le
B_{\varepsilon,c}\frac{\kappa_n(P)}{n}.
\]
Here the last inequality uses \(k_n/(c_-N_n)\le\kappa_n(P)\), \(4/N_n\le8/(cn)\), and \(\kappa_n(P)\ge1\) eventually. Chebyshev's inequality and \(L^2\ge 2B_{\varepsilon,c}/\alpha\) yield
\[
P(\mathsf{largeScore}_{n,P})
\le
\frac{B_{\varepsilon,c}\kappa_n(P)/n}
{L^2\kappa_n(P)/(2n)}
=
\frac{2B_{\varepsilon,c}}{L^2}
\le\alpha .
\]
Thus
\[
P\{\theta_T(P)\in C^\star_n\}
\ge 1-\alpha-\delta_n .
\]
Taking the infimum over \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), then the limit inferior, gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}}
P\{\theta_T(P)\in C^\star_n\}\ge1-\alpha,
\]
so \(C^\star\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.regularCellProcedure_coverage_liminf

\item Fix \(t_0>0\). The sample-size ratio again gives \(N_n\ge2\) eventually. For \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), write
\[
\mu_n(P)=\text{the transported first-stage mean},\qquad
\kappa_n(P)=\text{the transport-weight second moment},\qquad
t_n(P)=\frac{n\mu_n(P)^2}{\kappa_n(P)} .
\]
By \cref{obj:lem:class-compact-causal-range}, \(\mu_n(P)>0\), and the regular-cell mass calculation gives \(\kappa_n(P)\ge1\). The receipt score has mean \(\mu_n(P)\), and the uniform moment calculation used above gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat M_{D,n})
\le B_{\varepsilon,c}\frac{\kappa_n(P)}{n}.
\]
Therefore
\[
P\!\left(
\frac{\mu_n(P)}2<|\widehat M_{D,n}-\mu_n(P)|
\right)
\le
\frac{4B_{\varepsilon,c}}{t_n(P)}.
\]
Moreover the feasible dispersion proxy satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat K_n]=1+\kappa_n(P)\le2\kappa_n(P).
\]
For a realized sample let \(r_n=L\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\). On
\(\{|\widehat M_{D,n}-\mu_n(P)|\le\mu_n(P)/2\}\), affine inversion of
\(|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|\le r_n\) over \(\Theta\) has length at most \(4r_n/\mu_n(P)\); on the complementary event its length is at most \(2\). Jensen's inequality gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C^\star_n)]
\le
\frac{4L}{\mu_n(P)}
\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb E_P[\widehat K_n]}{n}}
+
2\,P\!\left(
\frac{\mu_n(P)}2<|\widehat M_{D,n}-\mu_n(P)|
\right),
\]
and hence
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C^\star_n)]
\le
\frac{4\sqrt2L}{\sqrt{t_n(P)}}
+
\frac{8B_{\varepsilon,c}}{t_n(P)}.
\]
This last display supplies the inverse-root bound when \(t_n(P)\ge1\): since \(t_n(P)^{-1}\le t_n(P)^{-1/2}\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C^\star_n)]
\le
(4\sqrt2L+8B_{\varepsilon,c})t_n(P)^{-1/2}
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_n(P)^{-1/2}\}.
\]
When \(0<t_n(P)<1\), the diameter bound \(\lambda(C^\star_n)\le2\) gives instead
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C^\star_n)]\le2
\le C_0
= C_0\min\{1,t_n(P)^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Thus for all sufficiently large rows and every regular-cell law in the row,
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C^\star_n)]
\le C_0\min\{1,t_n(P)^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) with \(t_n(P)\ge t_0\), then the limit superior, yields
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C^\star_n)]
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
The finitely many rows before \(N_n\ge2\) are absorbed by the limit superior. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.expectedLength_affineInversion_frontier_le_inflated

\item It remains to prove the lower bound. Choose the injected cells \(\xi_{n,i}\), \(i\in\{1,\ldots,k_n\}\), from the carrier hypothesis and define the deterministic geometry
\[
\nu_n=\frac1{k_n}\sum_{i=1}^{k_n}\delta_{\xi_{n,i}},
\qquad
P_{S,n}^X=\nu_n,
\qquad
P_{T,n}=\nu_n,
\qquad
w_n\equiv1,
\qquad
e_n\equiv\frac12 .
\]
This geometry is admissible: \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) gives overlap for \(e_n\), the transport identity is \(\int_A1\,d\nu_n=\nu_n(A)\), the envelope bound is \(0\le1\le2k_n\), and the weight-second-moment bound is
\[
\int w_n^2\,dP_{S,n}^X=1\le k_n.
\]
Its source cell masses are
\[
\nu_n\{\xi_{n,i}\}=\frac1{k_n},
\]
so
\[
\frac{c_-}{k_n}\le \frac1{k_n}\le \frac{c_+}{k_n}
\]
by \(c_-\le1\le c_+\). Hence every law in this fixed-geometry slice belongs to \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\).

Let \(C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\). Build a fixed-geometry oracle procedure \(\mathcal O\) by feeding \(C_n\) the regular-cell design \((\xi_{n,i})_{i=1}^{k_n}\), the masses \(1/k_n\), and the propensity values \(1/2\) whenever the realized source and target samples lie on the selected cells; set \(\mathcal O_n=\Theta\) outside that support event. Under every law in the fixed-geometry slice, the support event has probability one, so
\[
\mathcal O_n(s)=C_n(s)
\quad\text{for two-sample almost every }s
\]
after identifying the fixed-geometry input with the corresponding regular-cell input. Consequently, for every such \(P\),
\[
P\{\theta_T(P)\in\mathcal O_n\}
=
P\{\theta_T(P)\in C_n\},
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\mathcal O_n)]
=
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
Since the fixed-geometry slice is contained in the regular class, regular-cell honesty of \(C\), together with the preceding coverage identity and \cref{obj:lem:fixed-geometry-slice-inhabited}, makes \(\mathcal O\) a fixed-geometry honest oracle procedure. The rowwise expected lengths are bounded by \(2\) because all sets lie in \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). Therefore the fixed-geometry risk of \(\mathcal O\), written as the limiting supremum from \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value}, is bounded by the corresponding regular-cell risk:
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\mathcal O_n)]
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)] .
\]
Because \(V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\) is the infimum of this fixed-geometry risk over fixed-geometry honest oracle procedures in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value},
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
Taking the infimum over regular-cell honest \(C\) and applying the fixed-geometry lower bound from \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} gives
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
\inf_{C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n(P)\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)] .
\]
Combining this lower bound with the construction and upper bound above proves the asserted regular-cell attainment statement. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.TransportedLateStrengthFrontier.regular_cell_unknown_weight_attainment
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
