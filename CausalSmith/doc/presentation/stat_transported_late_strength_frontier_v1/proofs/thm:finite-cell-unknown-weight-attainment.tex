\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}]
Throughout, \(\lambda(A)\) is the Lebesgue measure of \(A\cap\Theta\) as in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk}, and rows over an empty family are read with the standard conventions: a rowwise coverage infimum is evaluated as \(1\), and a rowwise strength-restricted expected-length supremum as \(0\).  The coverage convention is the one \cref{obj:def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty} records for \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) and \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty} records for \(\mathcal P_n\); the same reading is used below for the coverage rows over \(\mathcal N_n\) and over \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), and the length convention for every strength-restricted row.

\begin{enumerate}
\item
Fix the constants of the statement,
\[
B_c=32(1+c^{-1}),\qquad
L_{\alpha,c}=\left(\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2},\qquad
C_0=\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B_c\},
\]
so that \(C_0\ge2>0\).  This is the first assertion.
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hC0pos)

Next, every finite-cell row class is inhabited:
\[
\mathcal N_n\neq\varnothing
\qquad\text{for every }n .
\]
An explicit witness is available: put the uniform law on \(k_n\) injected measurable singletons of the ambient carrier, take the target covariate law equal to it, transport weight \(w\equiv1\), source propensity \(e\equiv1/2\), and conditional complier probability \(1/(n+1)\) at row \(n\); under \(c>0\) with \(N_n/n\to c\), \(k_n>0\) with \(k_n\to\infty\) and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), this array satisfies every clause of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}.
% lean: finiteCellClass_inhabited

Finally we calibrate an auxiliary overlap level at which the regular-cell result will be invoked.  Set
\[
\gamma=4+\frac3c,\qquad
a=\gamma^{-1/2},\qquad
\bar\varepsilon=\frac14+\frac a2 .
\]
Since \(c>0\) we have \(\gamma>4\) and hence \(\sqrt\gamma>2\), so
\[
0<a=\frac1{\sqrt\gamma}<\frac12,
\qquad
0<\frac14<\bar\varepsilon<\frac12 .
\]
Moreover \(a\sqrt\gamma=1\), so
\[
\bar\varepsilon\sqrt\gamma
=\frac{\sqrt\gamma}4+\frac{a\sqrt\gamma}2
=\frac{\sqrt\gamma}4+\frac12
>\frac12+\frac12
=1 .
\]
Dividing by \(\bar\varepsilon>0\) gives \(\bar\varepsilon^{-1}\le\sqrt\gamma\), and squaring gives
\[
\bar\varepsilon^{-2}\le \gamma=4+\frac3c .
\]
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hx, hsqrtTwo, haPos, haHalf, hBar, haMul, hepsSqrt, hinvLeSqrt, hinvSq)

Write \(B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\) for the regular-cell variance constant of \cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} evaluated at overlap level \(\bar\varepsilon\),
\[
B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}=8\left(\bar\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1}\right).
\]
Then \(B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\ge0\), and the previous display gives the calibration that fixes \(\gamma\):
\[
B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}
\le
8\left(4+\frac3c+\frac1c\right)
=32+\frac{32}c
=B_c .
\]
Since \(\alpha>0\) and \(t\mapsto t^{1/2}\) is increasing,
\[
\left(\frac{2B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2}
\le
\left(\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2}
=L_{\alpha,c}.
\]
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hBnonneg, hBLe, hBcPos, hLreg)

\item
Apply \cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} with sampling ratio \(c\), noncoverage level \(\alpha\), overlap constant \(\bar\varepsilon\), regular-cell mass constants \(c_-=c_+=1\), the same sequences \(N_n\) and \(k_n\), the same measurable carrier, and critical constant \(L_{\alpha,c}\).  Write \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) for the regular finite-cell class of \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class} formed with these constants, and \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) for the corresponding feasible class of \cref{obj:def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty}.  Its hypotheses hold: \(c>0\) and \(N_n/n\to c\); \(0<\bar\varepsilon<1/2\) by Step 1 and \(0<\alpha<1\); \(0<c_-=1\le1\le c_+=1\); \(k_n>0\), \(k_n\to\infty\) and \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\); the ambient-carrier condition; the two-sample, instrument-overlap and degrading-array restrictions, each of which is one of the defining conditions of \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), since \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class} places \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) inside \(\mathcal P_n\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class}; and the critical-value requirement \((2B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}/\alpha)^{1/2}\le L_{\alpha,c}\) established in Step 1.

The conclusion supplies a regular-cell procedure \(C^{\mathrm{reg}}=(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})\) with three properties.  First, for every \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), \(P\)-almost surely
\[
C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}
=
\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
\left|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}\right|
\le L_{\alpha,c}\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\right\}
\quad(N_n\ge2),
\qquad
C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}=\Theta
\quad(N_n<2),
\]
where \(\widehat M_{Y,n}\), \(\widehat M_{D,n}\) and \(\widehat K_n\) are built from the law's own source-cell masses \(q_{x,n}=P_S^X(X=x)\) and its own propensity \(e\) exactly as displayed in \cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}; call this set \(C_n^{\dagger}\).  Second, \(C^{\mathrm{reg}}\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\).  Third, for every \(t_0>0\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})]
\le
\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
% lean: regular_cell_unknown_weight_attainment

\item
Transport this regular-cell rule to the finite-cell experiment.  First, every uniform finite-cell law is a regular finite-cell law with unit mass constants:
\[
\mathcal N_n\subseteq\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}
\qquad\text{for every }n .
\]
Indeed, let \(P\in\mathcal N_n\).  By \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source} its source covariate marginal is supported on exactly \(k_n\) measurable cells with
\[
q_{x,n}=\frac1{k_n},
\]
which meets the two-sided mass bound \(c_-/k_n\le q_{x,n}\le c_+/k_n\) of \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class} with \(c_-=c_+=1\) as an equality; and \(e(X)=1/2\) \(P_S\)-almost surely, so that \(0<\bar\varepsilon<1/2\) gives the overlap requirement at level \(\bar\varepsilon\),
\[
\bar\varepsilon\le e(X)=\frac12\le1-\bar\varepsilon
\qquad P_S\text{-a.s.}
\]
The remaining conditions of \cref{obj:def:regular-finite-cell-class} are those of \cref{obj:def:model-class}, which \(\mathcal N_n\) inherits by \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class}.
% lean: FiniteCellClass.toRegularFiniteCellClass

Let \(C=(C_n)\) be the sample-only rule displayed in the statement: for \(N_n\ge2\),
\[
C_n=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
\left|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}\right|
\le L_{\alpha,c}\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\right\},
\qquad
C_n=\Theta\ \ (N_n<2),
\]
with \(H_i=2(2Z_i-1)\), \(\widehat p_{x,n}\), \(\widehat m_{G,n}\), \(\widehat M_{G,n}\), \(\widehat\kappa_{U,n}\) and \(\widehat K_n=1+\widehat\kappa_{U,n}\) as in the statement.  This is exactly the regular-cell inversion rule of Step 2 run with the constant inputs
\[
q_{x,n}\equiv\frac1{k_n},
\qquad
e\equiv\frac12 ,
\]
so it uses only \((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n\) and \((X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\), takes values in \(\Theta\), and has a measurable sample-by-\(\vartheta\) graph on the realized cell experiment.  In particular the almost-sure agreement with the finite-cell score-inversion rule asserted in the statement holds by construction.
% lean: finiteCellProcedure, finiteCellInversion

Now fix \(n\) and \(P\in\mathcal N_n\).  Three almost-sure identities combine.  (i) \(P\)-almost surely every sampled source covariate \(X_i\) and every sampled target covariate \(X_j^T\) lies in the \(k_n\) cells of \(P_S^X\), and on those points the constant inputs \(1/k_n\) and \(1/2\) coincide with the law's own \(q_{x,n}\) and \(e\); hence \(P\)-almost surely \(C_n\) equals the regular-cell inversion rule fed \(P\)'s own cell design, masses and propensity.
% lean: finiteCellProcedure_eq_regularCellSet_ae
(ii) That rule, evaluated on the law's own finite cell vectors, agrees \(P\)-almost surely with \(C_n^{\dagger}\), because the sampled covariates almost surely lie in the realized support where the finite vectors and their ambient extensions coincide.
% lean: regularCellProcedure_set_eq_ambient_ae
(iii) \(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}=C_n^{\dagger}\) \(P\)-almost surely by Step 2.  Chaining (i)--(iii),
\[
C_n=C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}
\qquad P\text{-almost surely, for every }n\text{ and every }P\in\mathcal N_n,
\]
and therefore
\[
P(\theta_T\in C_n)=P(\theta_T\in C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}),
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]=\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})].
\]
% lean: finiteCellProcedure_upper_bridge (hcoverageEq, hlengthEq)

Two consequences follow.  For coverage: by the inclusion above, both row classes are nonempty for every \(n\) by Step 1, and an infimum over the larger class is no larger, so
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}}P(\theta_T\in C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})
\le
\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n}P(\theta_T\in C_n)
\qquad\text{for every }n .
\]
Coverage probabilities lie in \([0,1]\), so both row sequences are bounded and the inequality passes through \(\liminf\); with \(C^{\mathrm{reg}}\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) from Step 2 this gives
\[
1-\alpha
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}}P(\theta_T\in C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n}P(\theta_T\in C_n),
\]
that is, \(C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-feasible-honesty}.
% lean: finiteCellProcedure_upper_bridge (hcovRows, hfiniteHonest)

For risk: fix \(t_0>0\).  The inclusion also preserves the strength restriction \(t_n\ge t_0\), so for every \(n\)
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})].
\]
Every expected length is nonnegative and, by \(\lambda(A)\le\lambda(\Theta)=2\), at most \(2\); hence both row sequences lie in \([0,2]\) and the inequality passes through \(\limsup\):
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})].
\]
% lean: finiteCellProcedure_upper_bridge (hrowRisk, hriskDC)

\item
Fix \(t_0>0\).  Chaining the risk comparison of Step 3, the regular-cell bound of Step 2, and the constant comparison \(B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\le B_c\) of Step 1 (legitimate because \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\ge0\)),
\begin{align*}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
&\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})]\\
&\le
\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\\
&\le
\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B_c\}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\\
&=
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\end{align*}
This is the asserted attainment bound, for every \(t_0>0\).
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hrisk, hconst, hminNonneg)

\item
For the converse, fix \(t_0>0\).  For each \(n\) choose an injection \(i\mapsto x_{i,n}\) of \(\{1,\dots,k_n\}\) into the measurable carrier whose singletons \(\{x_{i,n}\}\) are measurable, and write
\[
\mathcal X_n=\{x_{1,n},\dots,x_{k_n,n}\}.
\]
Define the deterministic geometry \(\mathfrak g=(P_{S,n}^X,w,e)_{n\ge1}\) by
\[
P_{S,n}^X=P_{T,n}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_n}k_n^{-1}\delta_{x_{i,n}},
\qquad
w\equiv1,
\qquad
e\equiv\frac12 .
\]
Then \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class}.  Both covariate laws are probability measures because \(k_n\cdot k_n^{-1}=1\).  The overlap clause holds because \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) gives
\[
\varepsilon\le e(x)=\frac12\le1-\varepsilon .
\]
The envelope clause holds because \(k_n\ge1\) gives
\[
0\le w(x)=1\le 2k_n .
\]
The normalization and second-moment clauses are
\[
\mathbb E_S[w(X)]=1,
\qquad
\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]=1\le k_n ,
\]
and the target-law clause holds because, for every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
\mathbb E_S\!\left[w(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in A\}\right]
=
P_{S,n}^X(A)
=
P_{T,n}(A).
\]
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hkinv, hmu, hg), AdmissibleGeometry

\item
The selected range is measurable, being the finite union \(\mathcal X_n=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k_n}\{x_{i,n}\}\) of measurable singletons, and
\[
P_{S,n}^X(\mathcal X_n)=P_{T,n}(\mathcal X_n)=1,
\qquad
P_{S,n}^X(\{x_{i,n}\})=k_n^{-1}
\quad(1\le i\le k_n).
\]
Hence every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:finite-cell-source} with these cells: by \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} its source covariate marginal is exactly \(P_{S,n}^X\), which is supported on the \(k_n\) cells and assigns each mass \(1/k_n\), and its propensity is \(e\equiv1/2\).  All remaining conditions of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class} are the conditions of \cref{obj:def:model-class}, which hold because \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\subseteq\mathcal P_n\).  Therefore
\[
\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\subseteq\mathcal N_n
\qquad\text{for every }n .
\]
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hrange, hatom, hsliceFinite), FiniteCellSource

The same measurable range also makes
\[
E_n
=
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\{X_i\in\mathcal X_n\}
\ \cap\
\bigcap_{j=1}^{N_n}\{X_j^T\in\mathcal X_n\}
\]
a measurable event of the two-sample space, being a finite intersection of preimages of \(\mathcal X_n\) under coordinate projections.
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hrangeMeas, hsampleSupportMeas)

\item
\Cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} applies to \(\mathfrak g\): its target-sample-ratio, envelope-scale, overlap and noncoverage hypotheses are the standing hypotheses of the present statement, its geometry hypothesis is Step 5, and its fixed-geometry-slice hypothesis holds because \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\subseteq\mathcal P_n\) and the five conditions it lists are among those of \cref{obj:def:model-class}.  Its lower half gives, with \(c_\alpha=3(1-\alpha)^2/16\),
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0).
\]
% lean: fixed_geometry_frontier

It therefore suffices to show that for every feasible finite-cell honest \(\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\),
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)];
\]
taking the infimum over \(\widetilde C\) then yields the asserted converse.  That infimum is over a nonempty class, since \(C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) by Step 3.

\item
Fix \(\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) and define a fixed-geometry oracle procedure \(D=(D_n)\) by
\[
D_n=
\begin{cases}
\widetilde C_n, & \text{on }E_n,\\
\Theta, & \text{on }E_n^{\mathrm c}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then \(D_n\subseteq\Theta\).  Its sample-by-\(\vartheta\) graph is
\[
\left\{(s,\vartheta):s\in E_n,\ \vartheta\in\widetilde C_n(s)\right\}
\ \cup\
\left(E_n^{\mathrm c}\times\Theta\right),
\]
which is measurable: the first set is the restriction to the measurable event \(E_n\) of the graph of \(\widetilde C_n\), which is measurable on the realized cell experiment with cells \(\mathcal X_n\), and the second is a product of measurable sets.  Finally \(D_n\) is a function of the samples alone, so it does not change when the oracle density-ratio input is replaced by another version, which is the weight-invariance requirement imposed on oracle procedures in \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}.
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (D, subset, measurableGraph, weightAEInvariant)

\item
Let \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\).  Its source and target covariate laws are both \(P_{S,n}^X=P_{T,n}\), which gives \(\mathcal X_n\) probability one by Step 6, and the two samples have independent coordinates, so
\[
P(E_n)=1 .
\]
Hence \(D_n=\widetilde C_n\) \(P\)-almost surely, and therefore
\[
P(\theta_T\in D_n)=P(\theta_T\in\widetilde C_n),
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(D_n)]=\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)].
\]
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hsupportAE, hsetEq, hcoverageEq, hlengthEq)

For all sufficiently large \(n\) the fixed-geometry threshold slice \(\{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):t_n\ge t_0\}\) is inhabited, hence so is \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\); and \(\mathcal N_n\neq\varnothing\) for every \(n\) by Step 1.
% lean: fixedGeometrySlice_eventually_inhabited
For such \(n\), the inclusion of Step 6 and the coverage equality just displayed give
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n}P(\theta_T\in\widetilde C_n)
\le
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}P(\theta_T\in D_n).
\]
The left-hand row sequence is bounded below by \(0\) and the right-hand one is eventually bounded above by \(1\), since coverage probabilities lie in \([0,1]\); so \(\liminf\) preserves this eventual inequality, and honesty of \(\widetilde C\) over \(\mathcal N_n\) gives
\[
1-\alpha
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n}P(\theta_T\in\widetilde C_n)
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}P(\theta_T\in D_n),
\]
that is, \(D\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-honesty}.
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hcovRows, hcovCnonneg, hcovFone, hDhonest)

\item
Every confidence set is measured inside the compact parameter space, so
\[
\lambda(A)\le\lambda(\Theta)=2
\qquad\text{for every }A\subseteq\mathbb R .
\]
Consequently \(\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)]\le2\) for every \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal N_n\), and \(\mathbb E_P[\lambda(E_n')]\le2\) for every fixed-geometry oracle procedure \((E_n')\) and every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\); all these expectations are nonnegative.
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hsetLengthTwo, hfiniteExpectedTwo, hfixedExpectedTwo)

For all sufficiently large \(n\) the fixed-geometry threshold slice is inhabited; on those \(n\), the inclusion of Step 6 (which also preserves the restriction \(t_n\ge t_0\)) and the equality of expected lengths from Step 9 give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(D_n)]
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)].
\]
The left-hand row sequence is eventually nonnegative, because the slice is eventually inhabited and expected lengths are nonnegative, and the right-hand row sequence is bounded above by \(2\) for every \(n\) by the length bound (with the empty-row supremum evaluated as \(0\)).  These two bounds let the eventual inequality pass through \(\limsup\):
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(D_n)]
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)].
\]
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hrowRisk, hrowFnonneg, hrowCtwo, hriskDC)

\item
It remains to place the fixed-geometry value below the risk of this particular \(D\).  By the length bound of Step 10, for every fixed-geometry oracle procedure \((E_n')\) each row supremum lies in \([0,2]\), so
\[
0
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(E_n')].
\]
The set of fixed-geometry risks over \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\) is therefore bounded below by \(0\), and it contains the risk of \(D\) because \(D\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\) by Step 9.  Hence the infimum defining \(V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)\) in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} satisfies
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(D_n)].
\]
% lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (hfixedRiskNonneg, hvaluesBdd)

Combining with Step 10,
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)].
\]
Since \(\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) was arbitrary, taking the infimum over that nonempty class and combining with Step 7 yields
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)
\le
\inf_{\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)] ,
\]
which is the asserted converse for every \(t_0>0\).  This completes the proof.
% lean: fixed_geometry_frontier, finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment (converse branch)
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
