\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range}]
Write
\[
H(O)=
\begin{cases}
1/e(X), & Z=1,\\
-1/\{1-e(X)\}, & Z=0.
\end{cases}
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Model membership in \(\mathcal P_n\), as specified in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, supplies the full-data support, population presence, two-sample laws, overlap, source observation, randomization, exclusion, monotonicity, transport, target-complier positivity, domination, and envelope facts used below.  The source observation map \((S,X,D(0),D(1),Y(0),Y(1),Z)\mapsto (X,Z,D(Z),Y(D(Z)))\) is measurable, and its \(X\)-marginal agrees with the source full-data \(X\)-marginal.  Therefore overlap transfers to the observed source law:
\[
\varepsilon\le e(X)\le 1-\varepsilon
\qquad P_S\text{-a.s.}
\]
Consequently \(H\) is measurable and \(|H|\le 1/\varepsilon\) \(P_S\)-almost surely.  Since the observed outcome lies in \([0,1]\) and the receipt indicator is bounded by one, both \(H(O)Y\) and \(H(O)D\) are integrable under \(P_S\).

\item The inverse-propensity calculation used for the full-data contrasts is as follows.  Let \(F_0,F_1\) be measurable and integrable under \(P_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1)\), and assume that the assigned-source inverse-propensity integrand displayed below is integrable.  For each measurable \(A\), conditional randomization and overlap identify the \(Z=z\) slice of the assigned source law, mapped back to full data, as the source full-data law weighted by \(e(X)\) when \(z=1\) and by \(1-e(X)\) when \(z=0\).  Splitting the assigned law into the two slices gives
\[
\int_{\{X\in A\}}
\left[
\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=1\}}{e(X)}F_1
-\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z=0\}}{1-e(X)}F_0
\right] dP_{\mathrm{assign},S}
=
\int_{\{X\in A\}}(F_1-F_0)\,dP_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1).
\]
On the \(Z=1\) slice the density \(e(X)\) cancels \(1/e(X)\), and on the \(Z=0\) slice the density \(1-e(X)\) cancels \(-1/\{1-e(X)\}\); the two slice integrals then add to the displayed contrast.

\item The chosen version \(\Delta_Y\) is measurable, and the source-observation facts give, for every measurable covariate set \(A\),
\[
\int_{\{O:X\in A\}}H(O)Y\,dP_S(O)
=
\int_A \Delta_Y(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
For the source full-data comparison used in the transport step, apply the preceding identity with \(F_z=Y(D(z))\).  The required integrability of the assigned-source inverse-propensity integrand follows by pulling back the already established integrability of \(H(O)Y\) through the source observation map.  Pulling the observed integral back through that map and splitting over \(Z,D(0),D(1)\) gives
\[
\int_{\{O:X\in A\}}H(O)Y\,dP_S(O)
=
\int_{\{X\in A\}}\{Y(D(1))-Y(D(0))\}\,dP_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1).
\]
Exclusion rewrites \(Y(D(z))\) as \(Y^Z(z)\), and the source outcome-transport identity rewrites the last display as the integral over \(A\) of the source conditional assignment-outcome contrast.  Hence \(\Delta_Y\) agrees \(P_S^X\)-almost surely with that source contrast, while the displayed source-observation identity is the required observed source representation.

\item The chosen version \(\Delta_D\) is measurable, and the source-observation facts give, for every measurable covariate set \(A\),
\[
\int_{\{O:X\in A\}}H(O)D\,dP_S(O)
=
\int_A \Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
For the source full-data comparison, apply the inverse-propensity identity with \(F_z=D(z)\), written as the real indicator of receipt.  The required integrability of the assigned-source inverse-propensity integrand follows by pulling back the already established integrability of \(H(O)D\) through the source observation map.  Pulling the observed integral back and splitting over \(Z,D(0),D(1)\) gives
\[
\int_{\{O:X\in A\}}H(O)D\,dP_S(O)
=
\int_{\{X\in A\}}\{D(1)-D(0)\}\,dP_n^F(\cdot\mid S=1).
\]
The source receipt-transport identity rewrites the last display as the integral over \(A\) of the source conditional receipt contrast.  Hence \(\Delta_D\) agrees \(P_S^X\)-almost surely with that source contrast, while the displayed source-observation identity is the required observed source representation.

\item For the transported reduced-form outcome mean, the already proved source almost-sure equality between \(\Delta_Y\) and the source conditional assignment-outcome contrast first rewrites the integrand in \(\mu_{Y,n}\).  The Radon--Nikodym transport identity and target domination then change measure from \(P_S^X\) to \(P_T\).  The target outcome-transport equality replaces the transported source contrast by the target contrast, and the target full-data integral identity gives
\[
\mu_{Y,n}
=
\int \{Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\}\,dP_n^F(\cdot\mid S=0).
\]
Exclusion changes \(Y^Z(z)\) to \(Y(D(z))\).  Finally, monotonicity permits a pointwise case split over \(D(0),D(1)\), yielding
\[
\mu_{Y,n}
=
\int
\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\}
\,dP_n^F(\cdot\mid S=0).
\]

\item For the transported first-stage mean, the weighted source representation first unfolds \(\mu_n\) as
\[
\mu_n=\int w(x)\Delta_D(x)\,dP_S^X(x).
\]
The envelope \(0\le w\le 2k_n\), the bound \(|H|\le 1/\varepsilon\), and the receipt-indicator bound give integrability of the weighted observed receipt-score integrand.  Separately, the envelope and \(0\le\Delta_D\le1\) give integrability of \(w\Delta_D\).  Using the source almost-sure equality between \(\Delta_D\) and the source receipt contrast, Radon--Nikodym transport, the target receipt-transport equality, and then the target full-data receipt identity,
\[
\mu_n
=
\int \{D(1)-D(0)\}\,dP_n^F(\cdot\mid S=0).
\]
Monotonicity turns \(D(1)-D(0)\) into \(\mathbf 1\{D(1)=1,D(0)=0\}\), and integration of this indicator gives
\[
\mu_n
=
P_n^F\{D(1)=1,\ D(0)=0\mid S=0\}.
\]

\item By the definition of \(\theta_T\), the numerator is the target complier-weighted outcome contrast and the denominator is the target complier share.  Substituting the two identities just proved gives
\[
\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}=\theta_T.
\]

\item Let \(\mu=P_n^F(\cdot\mid S=0)\) and \(C=\{D(1)=1,D(0)=0\}\).  Full-data support makes \(P_n^F\) a probability law, and together with population presence for \(S=0\) it makes \(\mu\) a probability law.  Target-complier positivity gives \(0<\mu(C)\).  The conditional law \(\mu\) is absolutely continuous with respect to \(P_n^F\), so bounded full-data support gives
\[
-1\le Y(1)-Y(0)\le 1
\qquad \mu\text{-a.s.}
\]
The set \(C\) is measurable, so integral monotonicity gives
\[
-\mu(C)
\le
\int (Y(1)-Y(0))\mathbf 1_C\,d\mu
\le
\mu(C).
\]
Dividing by the positive number \(\mu(C)\) yields \(\theta_T\in[-1,1]=\Theta\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:oracle-converse}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Set
\[
c_0=\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16},
\qquad
t_c=1 .
\]
Since \(0<\alpha<1\), \(1-\alpha>0\), and hence \(c_0>0\); also \(t_c>0\).  Fix an arbitrary measurable covariate space, arbitrary arrays \(N_n,k_n\) satisfying the displayed size and cell-scale assumptions, and a threshold \(t_0>0\).

\item Choose the measurable singleton carriers \(x_{n,i}\), \(i\in\{1,\ldots,k_n\}\), supplied by the carrier assumption.  Let
\[
\mu_n=\sum_{i=1}^{k_n} k_n^{-1}\delta_{x_{n,i}} .
\]
The positivity of \(k_n\) gives \(k_n k_n^{-1}=1\), so each \(\mu_n\) is a probability measure.  Define the deterministic geometry
\[
P_{S,n}^X=\mu_n,\qquad P_{T,n}=\mu_n,\qquad w_n\equiv 1,\qquad e_n\equiv \frac12 .
\]
The probability, overlap, envelope, second-moment, and domination requirements in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} follow directly from these definitions and from \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), with the change-of-measure identity reducing to integration of the constant weight \(1\).

\item The fixed-geometry slice associated with this \(\mathfrak g\) is contained in the finite-cell class \(\mathcal N_n\) of \cref{obj:def:finite-cell-class} at each \(n\).  Indeed, the source covariate law is the uniform measure on the displayed \(k_n\) carriers, the carrier cells are measurable singletons, each cell has source mass \(1/k_n\), the target law agrees with the source law, and the transport weight is \(1\) almost surely.

\item Consider the oracle procedure \(D^{\mathrm{full}}\) that always returns the whole compact parameter space \(\Theta=[-1,1]\).  Its graph is measurable, its values are subsets of \(\Theta\), and it is invariant to almost-sure changes of the supplied weight.  For every transported-IV row \(P\), \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} places \(\theta_T(P,n)\) in \(\Theta\), so the coverage of \(D^{\mathrm{full}}\) is exactly one.  Taking the infimum over the nonempty transported class preserves the value one, and therefore \(D^{\mathrm{full}}\) is oracle-honest in the sense of \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}.  Thus the class over which \(V^\star(t_0)\) and \(V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)\) take their infima is nonempty.

\item On the unit-weight geometry just constructed, the weight is \(1\) almost surely for every \(n\).  Applying \cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction} yields, for every \(t_0>0\),
\[
c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0),
\]
where \(V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\) is the fixed-geometry value from \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value}.

\item Let \(C\) be any oracle-honest procedure.  Restricting \(C\) to the fixed geometry gives a fixed-geometry honest procedure, and the global frontier risk from \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk} dominates the corresponding fixed-geometry risk.  Hence
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)\le R((C_n),t_0).
\]
Taking the infimum over all oracle-honest \(C\) in \cref{obj:def:oracle-value} gives
\[
c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\le V^\star(t_0).
\]

\item For the finite-cell value, first note the two boundedness facts used in the limsup comparison.  The length criterion is always between \(0\) and \(2\), since it is Lebesgue length after intersecting with \(\Theta=[-1,1]\).  Therefore every fixed-geometry risk term is nonnegative: if the strength-indexed slice is empty the supremum is zero, and otherwise the expected length of any selected law is nonnegative.  The same length bound gives an eventual upper bound by \(2\) for the relevant fixed and finite-cell risk terms.

\item Fix an arbitrary oracle-honest procedure \(C\).  Build a fixed-geometry oracle procedure \(D\) by feeding \(C\) the observed sample together with the canonical Radon--Nikodym weight \(dP_{T,n}/dP_{S,n}^X\) of \(\mathfrak g\) and the fixed propensity \(e_n\).  On every row \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), the source law, target law, weight, and propensity agree with those inputs, so \(D\)'s confidence set, coverage probability, and expected length agree with those of \(C\) on that row.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), the fixed-geometry strength slice is nonempty, which supplies the required coverage comparison; the coverage probabilities lie between \(0\) and \(1\), so the liminf comparison transfers oracle honesty of \(C\) to fixed-geometry honesty of \(D\).

\item For all sufficiently large \(n\), the fixed-geometry strength slice has admissible laws.  On such rows,
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g):\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P\lambda(D_n)
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P\lambda(C_n),
\]
because \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\subseteq\mathcal N_n\) and the expected lengths of \(D\) and \(C\) agree on the slice.  The nonnegativity and boundedness from the previous step allow the limsup comparison, hence the fixed-geometry risk of \(D\) is bounded by the finite-cell oracle risk of \(C\).  Taking the infimum over oracle-honest \(C\), and using the fixed-geometry lower bound, gives
\[
c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0).
\]

\item Combining the preceding inequalities proves the two displayed lower bounds for every \(t_0>0\).  If \(0<t_0\le t_c=1\), then \(t_0^{-1/2}\ge1\), so
\[
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}=1,
\]
and the oracle lower bound yields \(c_0\le V^\star(t_0)\).  The constants \(c_0\) and \(t_c\) were chosen before the covariate space, arrays, transported laws, and effective-strength decomposition; the argument uses a row only through the threshold condition \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\ge t_0\).  Thus the same constants apply uniformly over all decompositions of \(t_0\) into \(\mu_n\) and \(\kappa_n\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:prop:no-shift-reduction}]
Fix the admissible geometry array \(\mathfrak g\). We prove the three asserted conclusions in the order in which they appear.

\begin{enumerate}
\item For each \(n\), the unit-weight hypothesis gives
\[
w_n(x)^2=1
\qquad
P_{S,n}^X\text{-a.s.}
\]
Hence, by the definition of the geometry second moment and because \(P_{S,n}^X\) is a probability law under \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class},
\[
\kappa_n
=
\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\int 1\,dP_{S,n}^X
=
1 .
\]
For the equality of covariate laws, let \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\) be measurable. The change-of-measure clause in \cref{obj:def:admissible-geometry-class} gives
\[
P_{T,n}(A)
=
\int_A w_n(x)\,dP_{S,n}^X(x).
\]
The same almost-sure equality \(w_n=1\) remains valid after restricting \(P_{S,n}^X\) to \(A\), so
\[
P_{T,n}(A)
=
\int_A 1\,dP_{S,n}^X
=
P_{S,n}^X(A).
\]
Since this holds for every measurable \(A\), the measures agree:
\[
P_{T,n}=P_{S,n}^X .
\]


\item Let \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\). By \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}, its source covariate marginal is \(P_{S,n}^X\) from \(\mathfrak g\), and its Radon--Nikodym transport weight agrees \(P_{S,n}^X\)-almost surely with the geometry weight \(w_n\). Therefore the law-induced Kish dispersion of \(P\) equals the geometry second moment:
\[
\int \{w_n^P(x)\}^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\int w_n(x)^2\,dP_{S,n}^X(x)
=
\kappa_n .
\]
By the first step, this common value is \(1\). Substituting this into the definition of effective strength in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk} yields
\[
t_n
=
\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}
=
\frac{n\mu_n^2}{1}
=
n\mu_n^2 .
\]


\item Fix \(t_0>0\). The hypotheses on \(c\), \(N_n/n\), \(\varepsilon\), \(\alpha\), \(k_n\), and the admissibility of \(\mathfrak g\) are exactly the structural hypotheses required by \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}. For every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), the defining membership condition in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice} contains the transported-IV model assumptions needed there: the two-sample array condition, instrument overlap, the weight envelope, the weight second-moment condition, and the degrading-array condition. Applying \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} therefore gives, with
\[
L_\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}},
\qquad
c_\alpha=\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16},
\qquad
C_\alpha=\max\left\{2,\;4L_\alpha+\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2}\right\},
\]
the bounds
\[
c_\alpha \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
\le
C_\alpha \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]

\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Choose an admissible deterministic geometry \(\mathfrak g\) supplied by the carrier assumption.  The fixed-geometry inhabitation argument gives, for every \(t_0>0\), eventual existence of some \(P\in\mathcal P_n\); this is the nonemptiness input needed later for classwise infima and liminf coverage.  Since
\[
0<2\le \max\left\{2,\ 4L_\alpha+\frac8{\varepsilon^2}\right\},
\]
the stated constant \(C_0\) is positive. % lean: fixedGeometrySlice_eventually_inhabited

\item Set \(L=L_\alpha\) and define the oracle procedure by
\[
C_n=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
  |\widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n|
  \le L\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\right\}.
\]
The set is contained in \(\Theta\) by construction.  Its graph is measurable because the score \(H_i\), the empirical means \(\widehat A_n,\widehat B_n\), and \(\widehat\kappa_n\) are measurable functions of the source sample.  If two versions of the oracle weight agree almost everywhere under the source covariate law, the induced inversion sets agree almost surely under the two-sample law, so the procedure is a valid oracle procedure with the displayed handle. % lean: inversionHandle_weight_version_ae_eq

\item Fix \(n>0\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_n\).  Write \(\mu_n\) for the transported first-stage mean and \(\kappa_n\) for the second moment of \(w(X)\).  The model restrictions give \(\theta_P\in\Theta\), \(\mu_n>0\), measurable weights, \(0\le w\le 2k_n\), \(\int w\,dP_S^X=1\), and hence \(\kappa_n\ge1\).  They also give the source score identities: conditional integrals of the inverse-propensity instrument score recover the reduced-form and first-stage covariate contrasts.  The conditional-expectation pullout identity therefore yields
\[
\int w(X)H D\,dP_S=\mu_n.
\]
The overlap bound gives \(|H|\le 1/\varepsilon\), and since \(D\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{P_S}(w(X)HD)\le \frac{\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2}.
\]
For the empirical first-stage score \(\widehat B_n\), independence of the source observations gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat B_n]=\mu_n,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat B_n)\le
\frac{\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2 n}.
\]
Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
P\left(\frac{\mu_n}{2}<|\widehat B_n-\mu_n|\right)
\le \frac{4}{\varepsilon^2\,t_n},
\qquad
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}.
\]
Together with \(\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_n]=\kappa_n\) and \(\widehat\kappa_n\ge0\), the score-inversion length bound gives the pointwise risk inequality
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
\max\left\{2,\ 4L+\frac8{\varepsilon^2}\right\}
\min\{1,t_n^{-1/2}\}.
\]
% lean: scoreInversion_expectedLength_frontier_le

\item Define
\[
\delta_n=
\begin{cases}
1, & n=0,\\
16k_n^2/n, & n>0.
\end{cases}
\]
The condition \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\) gives \(\delta_n\to0\).  For \(n=0\), the coverage lower bound \(1-\alpha-\delta_n\le P(\theta_P\in C_n)\) is immediate.  For \(n>0\), let
\[
S_n=\widehat A_n-\theta_P\widehat B_n .
\]
The reduced-form and first-stage pullout identities imply that the single-observation affine score has mean zero; the compact range and overlap bounds give its variance bound
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P(S_n)\le \frac{4\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2 n}.
\]
The empirical dispersion satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_n]=\kappa_n,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\kappa_n)\le \frac{4k_n^2\kappa_n}{n},
\]
using \(w^4\le 4k_n^2w^2\).  Hence
\[
P(\widehat\kappa_n<\kappa_n/2)\le 16k_n^2/n=\delta_n.
\]
With \(a=L\sqrt{\kappa_n/(2n)}\) and \(L^2=8/(\alpha\varepsilon^2)\), Chebyshev's inequality also gives
\[
P(a<|S_n|)\le \alpha.
\]
On the complement of these two bad events,
\[
|S_n|
\le L\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n},
\]
so \(\theta_P\in C_n\).  The source-sample event lifts unchanged to the two-sample law, and therefore
\[
1-\alpha-\delta_n\le P(\theta_P\in C_n).
\]
Taking the classwise liminf and using the eventual nonemptiness from the first step proves \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\) as in \cref{obj:def:oracle-honesty}. % lean: abstractClass_coverage_liminf

\item Convert the pointwise length bound into the frontier bound.  Let the row loss be \(0\) at \(n=0\) and \(\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]\) for \(n>0\).  It is nonnegative and bounded by
\[
\max\left\{2,\ 4L+\frac8{\varepsilon^2}\right\}
\min\{1,t_n^{-1/2}\}
\]
for every admissible \(P\).  The abstract frontier-risk monotonicity over rows with \(t_n\ge t_0\) gives, for every \(t_0>0\),
\[
R((C_n),t_0)
\le
\max\left\{2,\ 4L+\frac8{\varepsilon^2}\right\}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Substituting \(L=L_\alpha\) gives the asserted bound with \(C_0\). % lean: abstractClassFrontierRisk_le

\item It remains to identify the value comparison.  For every admissible geometry, \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier} gives
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
For the chosen \(\mathfrak g\), the fixed-geometry value is bounded above by the frontier risk of every oracle-honest procedure, so the lower display transfers to \(V^\star(t_0)\) in \cref{obj:def:oracle-value}.  Conversely, \(V^\star(t_0)\) is at most the risk of the specific oracle-honest procedure constructed above.  The uniform bound \(\lambda(A\cap\Theta)\le2\), since \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), supplies the bounded-below condition for this infimum.  Hence
\[
V^\star(t_0)\le C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
and the oracle value has the stated attainable frontier order at each fixed \(t_0>0\). % lean: fixedGeometryValue_le_frontierRisk
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}]
Work with the constants in the statement, and put
\[
x=4+\frac{3}{c},\qquad
a=x^{-1/2},\qquad
\bar\varepsilon=\frac14+\frac12 a .
\]
The sampling and cell assumptions give at least one finite-cell row in every row. Since \(c>0\), one has \(x>4\), hence \(a\in(0,1/2)\) and
\[
0<\bar\varepsilon<\frac12,\qquad
\bar\varepsilon^{-1}\le \sqrt x,\qquad
\bar\varepsilon^{-2}\le x .
\]
Thus, for
\[
B=8(\bar\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1}),
\]
we have \(0\le B\le B_c\). Consequently
\[
\sqrt{\frac{2B}{\alpha}}\le
\sqrt{\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}}=L_{\alpha,c}.
\]
Apply \cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment} with overlap level \(\bar\varepsilon\) and regular-cell constants \(c_-=c_+=1\), using the critical value \(L_{\alpha,c}\). This gives a regular-cell procedure \(C^{\mathrm{reg}}\), honest at level \(1-\alpha\), whose regular-cell risk is bounded by
\[
\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B\}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
On a uniform finite-cell law, the sample-only finite-cell rule agrees almost surely with the regular-cell rule evaluated at the law's cell masses and propensity; the regular-cell procedure also agrees almost surely with its ambient inversion representation. Therefore the finite-cell/regular-cell bridge transfers honesty and risk from \(C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) to the sample-only finite-cell inversion rule with critical value \(L_{\alpha,c}\). Since
\[
0<2\le C_0
\]
and
\[
\max\{2,4\sqrt2\,L_{\alpha,c}+8B\}\le C_0,
\]
the asserted finite-cell procedure exists, agrees almost surely with the displayed score-inversion rule, is in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\), and satisfies the stated upper bound for every \(t_0>0\). % lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment

It remains to prove the lower bound. Fix \(t_0>0\). Choose, for each \(n\), the measurable injection of \(k_n\) cells supplied by the carrier condition, and let
\[
\mu_n=\sum_{i=1}^{k_n} k_n^{-1}\,\delta_{x_{i,n}} .
\]
Each \(\mu_n\) is a probability measure, assigns total mass one to the selected cell range, and gives each selected singleton mass \(k_n^{-1}\). Define a deterministic geometry by taking both source and target covariate laws equal to \(\mu_n\), the transport weight equal to \(1\), and the propensity equal to \(1/2\). The overlap condition \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), the positivity of \(k_n\), and the preceding mass identities verify admissibility of this geometry. Every fixed-geometry row for this geometry is therefore a member of the finite-cell class: the source law is uniform on the selected cells, the target law has the same support, and the propensity is \(1/2\). % lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment

By the lower-bound part of \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}, this fixed geometry has oracle value at least
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
We compare that oracle value with the feasible finite-cell value. Take an arbitrary honest finite-cell procedure \(C\). Construct a fixed-geometry oracle procedure \(D\) as follows: on samples whose source and target covariates all lie in the selected cell range, \(D\) returns \(C\)'s set; off that support it returns \(\Theta\). The support event is measurable, and the graph of \(D\) is measurable because the graph of \(C\) is measurable on the selected cells. The weight-invariance condition is immediate, since \(D\)'s definition on this geometry does not depend on an alternative representative of the weight. % lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment

For every row in the fixed-geometry slice, both the source and target covariate laws put probability one on the selected cell range. Hence the product two-sample law puts probability one on the event where \(D\) and \(C\) coincide. On that event their confidence sets are equal, so their coverages and expected lengths are equal row by row. Since the fixed-geometry slice is a subclass of the finite-cell class, the rowwise coverage infimum for \(C\) over the finite-cell class is eventually no larger than the corresponding fixed-geometry coverage infimum for \(D\). Coverages lie in \([0,1]\), so the liminf comparison preserves honesty; therefore \(D\) is fixed-geometry oracle honest. % lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment

The same almost-sure equality gives the expected-length comparison. The length of any reported set, restricted to \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), is at most \(2\), so all relevant rowwise expected lengths are bounded above by \(2\) and nonnegative. The eventual inclusion of the fixed-geometry rows in the finite-cell rows gives
\[
R_{\mathrm{fixed}}(D,t_0)\le R_{\mathrm{cell}}(C,t_0).
\]
The fixed-geometry risks are bounded below by \(0\), so taking the infimum over fixed-geometry honest procedures yields
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)\le R_{\mathrm{cell}}(C,t_0).
\]
Since \(C\) was an arbitrary member of \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\), taking the infimum over feasible finite-cell honest procedures gives
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
\inf_{\widetilde C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(\widetilde C_n)] .
\]
This proves the asserted lower bound for the fixed \(t_0\), and \(t_0>0\) was arbitrary. % lean: finite_cell_unknown_weight_attainment
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}]
Fix \(t_0>0\).  The assumptions on \(N_n\), \(k_n\), \(\varepsilon\), and the admissible geometry imply that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the fixed-geometry slice contains a law with effective strength at least \(t_0\).  On those indices, the liminf coverage term is the ordinary slice infimum, and the strength-restricted risk supremum is over a nonempty set.

1. For the lower bound, set
\[
\rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{8},
\qquad
H=\min\{1/4,\rho t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Then \(\rho>0\) and \(H>0\).  The fixed-geometry least-favorable construction supplies, for all sufficiently large \(n\), a family \(\{Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}: |h|\le H\}\) from \cref{obj:def:least-favorable-witness,obj:def:geometry-handle}.  Each member lies in the fixed-geometry slice of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-slice}; the centered member has effective strength \(t_0\); the target contrast equals \(1/2+h\); and
\[
\|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le 2\rho .
\]


2. Let \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\) be any fixed-geometry honest oracle procedure.  For each row define
\[
a_n=\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}
P\{\theta_T(P)\in C_n\},
\qquad
r_n=\sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\\ t_n(P)\ge t_0}}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)] .
\]
The coverage values lie in \([0,1]\).  Since every confidence set is contained in \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), every expected length is at most \(2\), and the strength-restricted risk terms are bounded between \(0\) and \(2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

3. Fix a sufficiently large \(n\) and put \(I=[1/2-H,1/2+H]\).  For \(u\in I\), compare the law \(Q_{n,u-1/2}^{\mathfrak g}\) to the centered law \(Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\).  Because the target contrast under \(Q_{n,u-1/2}^{\mathfrak g}\) is \(u\), fixed-geometry coverage gives
\[
a_n\le
Q_{n,u-1/2}^{\mathfrak g}\{u\in C_n\}.
\]
The total-variation bound gives the comparison with \(Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\).  Applying the coverage-to-expected-length reduction over the interval \(I\), whose Lebesgue length is \(2H\), yields
\[
2H(a_n-2\rho)
\le
\mathbb E_{Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}}[\lambda(C_n)]
\le r_n .
\]


4. Pass the preceding inequality to limits.  The honesty condition in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-honesty} gives
\[
1-\alpha\le \liminf_{n\to\infty} a_n .
\]
The affine map \(x\mapsto 2H(x-2\rho)\) is monotone, and the boundedness from Step 2 justifies the liminf--limsup comparison.  Hence every honest \((C_n)\) satisfies
\[
2H(1-\alpha-2\rho)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty} r_n
=
R_{\mathfrak g}((C_n),t_0).
\]


5. The constants reduce by elementary arithmetic.  If \(\rho t_0^{-1/2}\le 1/4\), then \(H=\rho t_0^{-1/2}\) and
\[
2H(1-\alpha-2\rho)
=
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\,t_0^{-1/2}.
\]
If \(1/4\le \rho t_0^{-1/2}\), then \(H=1/4\), and the same expression dominates
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Thus
\[
c_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le R_{\mathfrak g}((C_n),t_0)
\]
for every \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\).  The procedure \(C_n=\Theta\) has coverage one on every row, so the honest class is nonempty; taking the infimum in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value} gives the lower bound for \(V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)\).

6. For the upper bound, put
\[
L=L_\alpha=\left(\frac{8}{\alpha\varepsilon^2}\right)^{1/2}
\]
and define an oracle score-inversion rule using the fixed geometry:
for every sufficiently large index, take the set in \cref{obj:def:inversion-handle} with \(w\) equal to the Radon--Nikodym weight of the target covariate law relative to the source covariate law in \(\mathfrak g\), and with propensity \(e\) equal to the fixed propensity in \(\mathfrak g\). Choose any measurable value at the finitely many earlier indices; changing finitely many values leaves the limiting statements unchanged. Measurability and containment in \(\Theta\) follow from the measurability of the fixed weight and propensity and from the construction of the inversion set.

7. Fix \(n>0\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\).  The slice condition identifies the oracle weight and propensity with those of \(P\), up to the admissible almost-sure weight version.  The compact range result in \cref{obj:prop:compact-causal-range} gives \(\theta_T\in\Theta\), identifies the transported first stage with the target complier share, and gives
\[
\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}=\theta_T,
\qquad
\mu_n>0 .
\]
The Radon--Nikodym change-of-measure identity gives \(\mathbb E_S[w(X)]=1\), so the Kish dispersion satisfies \(\kappa_n\ge1\).  A conditional-expectation pullout identity converts weighted source-score integrals into the corresponding covariate integrals.  Applying it to the receipt score gives mean \(\mu_n\), while instrument overlap bounds its variance by \(\kappa_n/\varepsilon^2\).  Chebyshev's inequality therefore yields
\[
P\!\left(\mu_n/2<
\left|\widehat B_n-\mu_n\right|\right)
\le
\frac{4}{\varepsilon^2 t_n}.
\]
The generic affine-inversion length bound then gives the pointwise inequality
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\le
\max\left\{2,\,4L+\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2}\right\}
\min\{1,t_n^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Changing finitely many initial indices leaves the limiting expected-length bound unchanged.

8. Define
\[
\delta_n=
\begin{cases}
1, & n=0,\\
16k_n^2/n, & n>0 .
\end{cases}
\]
Since \(k_n/\sqrt n\to0\), we have \(\delta_n\to0\).

9. The same fixed row \(n>0\) also gives the coverage bound.  For the true \(\theta_T\), the transported residual score has mean zero and variance at most \(4\kappa_n/\varepsilon^2\).  The empirical Kish statistic has mean \(\kappa_n\), and the envelope \(0\le w\le2k_n\) gives
\[
P(\widehat\kappa_n<\kappa_n/2)\le \frac{16k_n^2}{n\kappa_n}
\le \delta_n .
\]
With
\[
a=L\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{2n}},
\]
the identity \(L^2=8/(\alpha\varepsilon^2)\) and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
P\!\left(
\left|\widehat A_n-\theta_T\widehat B_n\right|>a
\right)\le \alpha .
\]
On the complement of these two bad events,
\[
\left|\widehat A_n-\theta_T\widehat B_n\right|
\le
L\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n},
\]
so \(\theta_T\in C_n\).  Hence, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\),
\[
1-\alpha-\delta_n
\le
P\{\theta_T(P)\in C_n\}.
\]
Changing the value assigned at \(n=0\) leaves the limiting coverage statement unchanged.

10. The coverage values lie in \([0,1]\), the slice is nonempty for all sufficiently large \(n\), and \(\delta_n\to0\).  The preceding coverage inequality therefore implies
\[
(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)
\]
in the sense of \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-honesty}.

11. Let
\[
C_0=\max\left\{2,\,4L+\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2}\right\}.
\]
The pointwise length inequality in Step 7 and the strength restriction \(t_n\ge t_0\) imply, row by row and then after taking the limsup frontier risk in \cref{obj:def:frontier-risk},
\[
R_{\mathfrak g}((C_n),t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Since \((C_n)\) is admissible for the infimum in \cref{obj:def:fixed-geometry-value}, and the risk functional is nonnegative by compactness of \(\Theta\), this gives
\[
V_{\mathfrak g}^{\star}(t_0)
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Substituting \(L=L_\alpha\) makes \(C_0=C_\alpha\).

Combining Steps 5 and 11 gives the asserted two-sided bound.  The constants used in both arguments depend only on \(\alpha\) and \(\varepsilon\), so they are uniform over all admissible deterministic geometries \(\mathfrak g\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}]
\begin{enumerate}
\item The measurable-carrier hypothesis and the regular-cell mass bounds give a member of \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) in every row \(n\), so the coverage term in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) is evaluated by an ordinary infimum.  Put
\[
B_{\varepsilon,c}=8(\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1}),
\qquad
C_0=\max\{2,4\sqrt2L+8B_{\varepsilon,c}\}.
\]
Then \(B_{\varepsilon,c}\ge0\), \(L\ge0\), and \(C_0>0\).  Define \(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) by the regular-cell inversion rule, with the stated \(\Theta\)-valued convention when \(N_n<2\).  The rule is the measurable regular-cell procedure obtained by applying the known source masses \(q_{x,n}\), the known propensity values, and the source and target samples to the score inversion map.

\item For every \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), the constructed random set agrees almost surely with the regular-cell inversion rule using the ambient source-cell mass function and propensity score.  This is the representation used in the honesty argument.  On rows with \(2\le N_n\), that same inversion rule is the affine set
\[
\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
|\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|
\le L\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\right\},
\]
whereas on rows with \(N_n<2\) it is \(\Theta\) by definition.  Since the class is nonempty in every row, the coverage term in \cref{obj:def:regular-cell-feasible-honesty} is
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}}
P(\theta_T\in C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}).
\]
The uniform coverage bound for the regular-cell inversion rule applies at the critical inequality
\(L\ge\{2B_{\varepsilon,c}/\alpha\}^{1/2}\), and yields
\[
1-\alpha\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\inf_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}}
P(\theta_T\in C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}).
\]
Thus \((C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\).

\item Fix \(t_0>0\).  From \(N_n/n\to c>0\), eventually \(n>0\) and \(2\le N_n\).  On this tail, for every \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), the compact causal-range identities give a positive transported first stage \(\mu_n\).  The regular-cell moment calculations give integrability of the outcome and receipt score moments, nonnegativity of \(\widehat K_n\), and
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widehat K_n]=1+\kappa_n\le2\kappa_n .
\]
The first-stage deviation event satisfies
\[
P\{|\widehat M_{D,n}-\mu_n|>\mu_n/2\}
\le
\frac{4B_{\varepsilon,c}}{t_n},
\qquad
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}.
\]
Using the almost-sure representation from the preceding step together with the nondegenerate identity for the regular-cell inversion rule, the expected length of \(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) is the expected length of this affine inversion set for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Applying the inflated affine-inversion length bound with \(Y=8B_{\varepsilon,c}\) gives, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})]
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_n^{-1/2}\}
\]
on that tail.  The abstract frontier-risk monotonicity then converts the pointwise bound into
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})]
\le
C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]

\item For the lower bound, choose the injected singleton carrier in each row and put the same uniform law on the source and target covariates:
\[
\nu_n=\sum_{i=1}^{k_n} k_n^{-1}\delta_{x_{i,n}},
\qquad
w_n\equiv1,
\qquad
e_n\equiv\frac12 .
\]
This deterministic geometry is admissible: \(\nu_n\) is a probability measure supported on the chosen cells, the propensity obeys the overlap interval, the weight envelope and second-moment bounds hold, and the equal cell masses satisfy
\[
\frac{c_-}{k_n}\le \frac1{k_n}\le \frac{c_+}{k_n}.
\]
Consequently, every fixed-geometry slice for this geometry is contained in \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\).  By \cref{obj:thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}, its fixed-geometry honest minimax risk is bounded below by
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]

\item Let \(C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) be arbitrary.  Construct a fixed-geometry oracle procedure \(D\) by feeding \(C\) the observed two-sample draw, the selected regular-cell design, the mass vector \(q_{x,n}=1/k_n\), and the propensity vector \(e_{x,n}=1/2\) whenever the sample lies on the selected support; on the complementary event set \(D_n=\Theta\).  Under every law in the fixed-geometry slice, the support event has probability one, so \(D_n\) and \(C_n\) agree almost surely after the slice is viewed as a regular-cell law.  Hence their coverages agree on the slice, and on the tail where the fixed-geometry slice has admissible laws, the fixed-geometry coverage infimum is at least the corresponding regular-cell infimum.  Thus \(D\) is fixed-geometry honest.  The same almost-sure equality gives equality of expected lengths on the slice, while every set length is bounded by \(\lambda(\Theta)=2\); therefore the fixed-geometry risk of \(D\) is at most the regular-cell risk of \(C\).  Combining this domination with the previous fixed-geometry lower bound and then taking the infimum over all \(C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) proves
\[
\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
\le
\inf_{C\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)].
\]
Together with the constructed upper bound, this gives the stated regular-cell attainment conclusion.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
