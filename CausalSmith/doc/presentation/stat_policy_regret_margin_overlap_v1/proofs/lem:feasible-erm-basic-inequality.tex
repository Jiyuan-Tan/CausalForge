\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:feasible-erm-basic-inequality}]
Work throughout in the positive-sample-size case \(n\ge1\).  Fix a realized sample, the pointwise-dense skeleton \(\Pi_0=\{\pi_j:j\ge1\}\) used in \cref{obj:def:feasible-erm}, and measurable foldwise nuisance components \(\widehat\mu_0^{(-k)}\), \(\widehat\mu_1^{(-k)}\), and \(\widehat e^{(-k)}\) for each fold \(k\).  Abbreviate the observed foldwise scores of \cref{obj:def:clipped-aipw-score} and the empirical criterion of \cref{obj:def:feasible-erm} by
\[
\Gamma_i=\Gamma_q\!\left(O_i;\widehat\eta^{(-k(i))}\right),
\qquad
\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{\pi(X_i)=1\}\,\Gamma_i .
\]
The criterion \(\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi)\) depends on \(\pi\) only through the finitely many values \(\pi(X_1),\ldots,\pi(X_n)\).

\emph{Step 1: a \(1/n\)-near maximizer over the skeleton exists.}  Since each indicator is \(0\) or \(1\),
\[
\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_j)
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{\pi_j(X_i)=1\}\,\Gamma_i
\le\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|\Gamma_i|
\qquad\text{for every }j\ge1.
\]
Thus the set of skeleton scores \(\{\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_j):j\ge1\}\) is nonempty and bounded above.  Write \(S=\sup_{j\ge1}\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_j)\), which is finite.  Because \(n\ge1\), \(1/n>0\), so \(S-1/n<S\).  By the defining property of the supremum, there is an index \(j\) with \(\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_j)>S-1/n\), and hence
\[
\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_{j'})\le \widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_j)+\frac1n
\qquad\text{for every }j'\ge1 .
\]
The set of such indices is a nonempty set of positive integers and has a least element.  This least element is the selector index \(j_n\) in \cref{obj:def:feasible-erm}; consequently
\[
\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_{j'})\le \widehat V_{n,q}(\widehat\pi_n)+\frac1n
\qquad\text{for every }j'\ge1 .
\]
% lean: feasibleERM_nearSet_nonempty

\emph{Step 2: the selector is measurable and the selected policy lies in \(\Pi\).}  Every enumerated policy \(\pi_j\) belongs to \(\Pi\) by the skeleton condition in \cref{obj:ass:policy-class} and \cref{obj:def:feasible-erm}, so \(\widehat\pi_n=\pi_{j_n}\in\Pi\).  For measurability, the event \(\{j_n=j\}\) is exactly the event that \(j\) is a \(1/n\)-near maximizer and every smaller index is outside the near-maximizer set:
\[
\{j_n=j\}
=
N_j\cap\left(\text{complement of }N_1\right)\cap\ldots\cap\left(\text{complement of }N_{j-1}\right),
\qquad
N_k=\left\{\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_{j'})\le \widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_k)+\tfrac1n\ \text{ for every }j'\ge1\right\}.
\]
Each score \(\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_j)\) is a measurable function of the sample: \(\pi_j\) is measurable by \cref{obj:ass:policy-class}, and the foldwise components \(\widehat\mu_0^{(-k)}\), \(\widehat\mu_1^{(-k)}\), and \(\widehat e^{(-k)}\) are measurable by the regularity condition fixed above.  Therefore each \(N_k\) is a countable intersection of measurable score comparisons, and \(\{j_n=j\}\) is a finite intersection of such sets and their complements.  Hence every fiber of \(j_n\) is measurable, so \(j_n\) is a measurable integer-valued selector.  Since \(j\mapsto R_P(\pi_j)\) is a function on a countable set, the regret map \(s\mapsto R_P(\pi_{j_n(s)})=R_P(\widehat\pi_n(s))\) is measurable. % lean: feasible_erm_basic_inequality

\emph{Step 3: the skeleton inequality reaches every comparator in \(\Pi\).}  Let \(\pi^b\in\Pi\).  By \cref{obj:ass:policy-class} and the pointwise-dense skeleton fixed in \cref{obj:def:feasible-erm}, there is a sequence \((\pi_{j_t})_{t\ge1}\) from \(\Pi_0\) such that \(\pi_{j_t}(x)\) is eventually equal to \(\pi^b(x)\) at each covariate value \(x\).  Applying this eventual equality to the finite set \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\), choose one index \(t\) such that
\[
\pi_{j_t}(X_i)=\pi^b(X_i)\qquad\text{for all }i=1,\ldots,n .
\]
Since \(\widehat V_{n,q}\) depends on the policy only through those \(n\) values,
\[
\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_{j_t})=\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi^b).
\]
Applying the near-maximality from Step 1 to the skeleton index \(j_t\) gives
\[
\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi^b)
=\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi_{j_t})
\le
\widehat V_{n,q}(\widehat\pi_n)+\frac1n .
\]
Thus every comparator \(\pi^b\in\Pi\) has empirical score at most \(\widehat V_{n,q}(\widehat\pi_n)+1/n\), which is the \(\Pi\)-wide \(1/n\)-ERM inequality in pointwise-comparator form. % lean: feasible_erm_basic_inequality

\emph{Step 4: the empirical-process form.}  For any comparator \(\pi^b\in\Pi\), the definition of \(\widehat V_{n,q}\) gives
\[
\widehat V_{n,q}(\widehat\pi_n)-\widehat V_{n,q}(\pi^b)
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\left\{\mathbf 1\{\widehat\pi_n(X_i)=1\}-\mathbf 1\{\pi^b(X_i)=1\}\right\}\Gamma_i
=
P_n\!\left[(\widehat\pi_n-\pi^b)\Gamma_q\right].
\]
The inequality from Step 3 is therefore
\[
P_n\!\left[(\widehat\pi_n-\pi^b)\Gamma_q\right]\ge -\frac1n .
\]
% lean: feasible_erm_basic_inequality
Finally, under \cref{obj:ass:optimal-in-class} the oracle policy satisfies \(\pi^\star_P\in\Pi\), so the comparator inequality applies with \(\pi^b=\pi^\star_P\). % lean: feasible_erm_basic_inequality
\end{proof}
