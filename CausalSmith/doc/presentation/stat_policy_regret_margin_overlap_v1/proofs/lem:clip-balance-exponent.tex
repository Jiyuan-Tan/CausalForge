\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:clip-balance-exponent}]
Throughout, we use the elementary monotonicity rule for powers of \(n\): if \(n\ge 1\) and \(r\le e\), then
\[
n^{-e}\le n^{-r}.
\]
% lean: nat_rpow_neg_le_of_le

\emph{Choice of the constants.}  The pair \((C,p)\) is chosen once, before the case distinction on \(\gamma\), and the same pair serves both branches.  Set
\[
K_{\mathrm{off}}=(q_0^2)^{-A_\alpha},
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{prod}}=\frac{C_{\mathrm{prod}}}{q_0},
\qquad
K_\mu=C_\mu\,|\bar u|^{\alpha/2}q_0^{1/(2\gamma)},
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{sq}}=\frac{C_\mu^2}{|\bar u|},
\]
and
\[
C=2+K_{\mathrm{off}}+K_{\mathrm{prod}}+K_\mu+K_{\mathrm{sq}}+C_{\mathrm{prod}},
\qquad
p=0 .
\]
The four auxiliary constants are formed from \(|\bar u|\) rather than \(\bar u\), and are read under the convention that a quotient with vanishing denominator is \(0\) and that the exponent \(1/(2\gamma)\) is \(0\) when \(\gamma=0\); with this convention all four are defined for every value of the parameters, including the fixed-overlap regime, where neither \(\bar u>0\) nor \(\gamma>0\) is available.  They are nonnegative there: \(K_{\mathrm{off}}\) is a real power of \(q_0^2\ge0\); \(K_{\mathrm{prod}}\) and \(K_{\mathrm{sq}}\) are quotients of the nonnegative numbers \(C_{\mathrm{prod}}\) and \(C_\mu^2\) by the nonnegative numbers \(q_0\) and \(|\bar u|\); and \(K_\mu\) is a product of the nonnegative factors \(C_\mu\), \(|\bar u|^{\alpha/2}\) and \(q_0^{1/(2\gamma)}\).  Hence \(C\ge2>0\) and \(p\ge0\).  Neither branch below alters these constants: the fixed-overlap branch never uses the values of \(K_{\mathrm{off}},K_{\mathrm{prod}},K_\mu,K_{\mathrm{sq}}\), which enter its estimate only as nonnegative slack inside \(C\). % lean: clip_balance_exponent

\emph{Case \(\gamma>0\).}  Here \(\bar u>0\), so \(|\bar u|=\bar u\) and the two \(\bar u\)-dependent constants take their stated values \(K_\mu=C_\mu\bar u^{\alpha/2}q_0^{1/(2\gamma)}\) and \(K_{\mathrm{sq}}=C_\mu^2/\bar u\).  Write \(s=s_{\mathrm{feas}}\), \(t=t_{\mathrm{feas}}\), so that the deterministic schedules of \cref{obj:def:feasible-rate} are
\[
q_n=q_0n^{-s},
\qquad
u_n=\bar u\,n^{-t}.
\]
By \cref{obj:def:feasible-rate},
\[
r_{\mathrm{feas}}=\min\{r_\star(\alpha,\gamma),g_{\mathrm{joint}}\},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le r_\star(\alpha,\gamma)
\quad\text{and}\quad
r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le g_{\mathrm{joint}} .
\]

The pair \((s,t)\) is selected in \cref{obj:def:feasible-rate} as a maximizer of \(\phi\) over the feasible box; in particular it is a point of that box,
\[
0\le s\le\tfrac12,
\qquad
0\le t\le\frac{s}{\gamma},
\]
so the two schedules above are the ones the definition prescribes and \(\phi\) is evaluated at an admissible pair. % lean: feasibleMaximizer_mem
The box is nonempty and compact and \(\phi\) is continuous on it, so \(\phi\) attains its supremum there, and the selected pair realizes that value:
\[
g_{\mathrm{joint}}=\phi(s,t)
=
\min\left\{
A_\alpha(1-2s),\,
c-s,\,
a+\frac{s}{2\gamma}+\frac{\alpha t}{2},\,
2a-t
\right\}.
\]
% lean: feasibleMaximizer_value
Since a minimum is at most each of its entries, this yields the four exponent inequalities
\[
g_{\mathrm{joint}}\le A_\alpha(1-2s),\quad
g_{\mathrm{joint}}\le c-s,\quad
g_{\mathrm{joint}}\le a+\frac{s}{2\gamma}+\frac{\alpha t}{2},\quad
g_{\mathrm{joint}}\le 2a-t,
\]
and therefore, combining with \(r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le g_{\mathrm{joint}}\),
\[
r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le A_\alpha(1-2s),\quad
r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le c-s,\quad
r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le a+\frac{s}{2\gamma}+\frac{\alpha t}{2},\quad
r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le 2a-t .
\]
% lean: feasibleMaximizer_value

Work on the eventual index set on which \(n\ge1\), the polynomial nuisance bounds of \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-nuisance-exponents},
\[
r_{\mu,n}\le C_\mu n^{-a},
\qquad
r_{\mu,n}r_{e,n}\le C_{\mathrm{prod}}n^{-c},
\]
hold, and \(r_{\mu,n}\ge0\).  Write \(b_n=n^{-r_{\mathrm{feas}}}\ge0\) for the target rate, and bound the five master-bound terms one at a time.

For the converse term, \(r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le r_\star(\alpha,\gamma)\) gives directly
\[
n^{-r_\star(\alpha,\gamma)}\le 1\cdot b_n .
\]

For the empirical-process term, \(nq_n^2=q_0^2n^{1-2s}\), so raising to the power \(-A_\alpha\) is an exact identity, and the exponent inequality then applies:
\[
(nq_n^2)^{-A_\alpha}
=
(q_0^2)^{-A_\alpha}\,n^{-A_\alpha(1-2s)}
\le
K_{\mathrm{off}}\,b_n .
\]

For the clipped product-nuisance remainder, dividing the product bound by \(q_n=q_0n^{-s}>0\),
\[
\frac{r_{\mu,n}r_{e,n}}{q_n}
\le
\frac{C_{\mathrm{prod}}n^{-c}}{q_0n^{-s}}
=
\frac{C_{\mathrm{prod}}}{q_0}\,n^{-(c-s)}
\le
K_{\mathrm{prod}}\,b_n .
\]

For the localized weak-overlap drift term, the factor \(u_n^{\alpha/2}q_n^{1/(2\gamma)}\) is nonnegative, so it may be multiplied into the bound on \(r_{\mu,n}\):
\[
r_{\mu,n}u_n^{\alpha/2}q_n^{1/(2\gamma)}
\le
C_\mu n^{-a}\,(\bar u n^{-t})^{\alpha/2}(q_0n^{-s})^{1/(2\gamma)}
=
C_\mu\bar u^{\alpha/2}q_0^{1/(2\gamma)}\,
n^{-\{a+s/(2\gamma)+\alpha t/2\}}
\le
K_\mu\,b_n .
\]

For the localization-complement outcome term, the squaring step uses both \(0\le r_{\mu,n}\) and \(C_\mu\ge0\), so that \(r_{\mu,n}\le C_\mu n^{-a}\) implies \(r_{\mu,n}^2\le C_\mu^2n^{-2a}\); dividing by \(u_n=\bar un^{-t}>0\),
\[
\frac{r_{\mu,n}^2}{u_n}
\le
\frac{C_\mu^2n^{-2a}}{\bar un^{-t}}
=
\frac{C_\mu^2}{\bar u}\,n^{-(2a-t)}
\le
K_{\mathrm{sq}}\,b_n .
\]

Adding the five displays gives the sum bound with coefficient \(1+K_{\mathrm{off}}+K_{\mathrm{prod}}+K_\mu+K_{\mathrm{sq}}\); since \(C_{\mathrm{prod}}\ge0\), that coefficient is at most \(C\), and \(b_n\ge0\), so
\[
n^{-r_\star(\alpha,\gamma)}+(nq_n^2)^{-A_\alpha}
+\frac{r_{\mu,n}r_{e,n}}{q_n}
+r_{\mu,n}u_n^{\alpha/2}q_n^{1/(2\gamma)}
+\frac{r_{\mu,n}^2}{u_n}
\le
\bigl(1+K_{\mathrm{off}}+K_{\mathrm{prod}}+K_\mu+K_{\mathrm{sq}}\bigr)b_n
\le
C\,n^{-r_{\mathrm{feas}}}(\log n)^0,
\]
which is the asserted positive-\(\gamma\) bound with \(r_{\mathrm{feas}}=\min\{r_\star(\alpha,\gamma),g_{\mathrm{joint}}\}\) and \(p=0\). % lean: clip_balance_exponent

\emph{Case \(\gamma=0\).}  The constants are those fixed above; nothing is rechosen.  Here \cref{obj:def:feasible-rate} gives the fixed clipping schedule \(q_n=q_0\) and
\[
r_{\mathrm{feas}}=\min\{A_\alpha,c\},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le A_\alpha
\quad\text{and}\quad
r_{\mathrm{feas}}\le c .
\]
On the eventual index set where \(n\ge1\) and the polynomial product bound holds, the same monotonicity rule gives the two termwise bounds, again with \(b_n=n^{-r_{\mathrm{feas}}}\ge0\),
\[
n^{-A_\alpha}\le 1\cdot b_n,
\qquad
r_{\mu,n}r_{e,n}\le C_{\mathrm{prod}}n^{-c}\le C_{\mathrm{prod}}\,b_n .
\]
Adding them gives the sum bound with coefficient \(1+C_{\mathrm{prod}}\); since \(K_{\mathrm{off}},K_{\mathrm{prod}},K_\mu,K_{\mathrm{sq}}\ge0\), that coefficient is at most \(C\), whence
\[
n^{-A_\alpha}+r_{\mu,n}r_{e,n}
\le
\bigl(1+C_{\mathrm{prod}}\bigr)b_n
\le
C\,n^{-r_{\mathrm{feas}}}(\log n)^0
=
C\,n^{-\min\{A_\alpha,c\}}(\log n)^0 .
\]
% lean: clip_balance_exponent
\end{proof}
