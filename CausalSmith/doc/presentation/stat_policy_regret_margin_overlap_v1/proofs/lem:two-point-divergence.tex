\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:two-point-divergence}]
Take
\[
C=\max\{8c_B,\,5\}>0,
\]
and fix \(n\ge1\) in the eventual range where \(0<h_n\le1/2\) and \(0<q_n\le1/2\).  Write \(h=h_n\), \(q=q_n\), \(P_+=P_{n,+}\), and \(P_-=P_{n,-}\).

\emph{Step 1: the one-observation divergence.}  By \cref{obj:def:two-point-witness}, the two laws have the same covariate law, the same propensity, and the same outcome law except on the treated active cell.  Let
\[
s_1=\{X\in B_n,\ A=1,\ Y=1\},
\qquad
s_2=\{X\in B_n,\ A=1,\ Y=-1\},
\qquad
s_0=(s_1\cup s_2)^c .
\]
These three measurable cells partition the full observation space.  On \(s_0\), the restrictions of \(P_+\) and \(P_-\) agree: this includes the common off-cell part of the law and the residual part of the active treated cell outside the two charged outcome atoms, which has zero mass under both laws.  On the two charged cells the outcome probabilities in \cref{obj:def:two-point-witness} give constant restriction ratios
\[
c_0=1,
\qquad
c_1=\frac{1+h}{1-h},
\qquad
c_2=\frac{1-h}{1+h},
\]
so \(P_+\ll P_-\), and the squared likelihood-ratio deviation is \(P_-\)-integrable.

Set
\[
A_n=P_X(B_n)\,q_n .
\]
Under \(P_-\), the two charged cells have masses
\[
P_-(s_1)=A_n\frac{1-h}{2},
\qquad
P_-(s_2)=A_n\frac{1+h}{2}.
\]
The finite-partition chi-square formula for these restriction ratios yields
\[
\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)
=
\sum_{i=0}^2(c_i-1)^2P_-(s_i)
=
\left(\frac{2h}{1-h}\right)^2\frac{A_n(1-h)}2
+
\left(\frac{2h}{1+h}\right)^2\frac{A_n(1+h)}2
=
\frac{4A_nh^2}{1-h^2}.
\]
Since \(0\le h\le1/2\), we have \(1-h^2\ge1/2\), and hence
\[
\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)
\le 8A_nh^2 .
\]
% lean: chiSqDiv_three_cell_bound_of_restrict
Because \(P_X\) is Lebesgue measure restricted to \([0,1]\) and \(B_n=\{x:0\le x\le c_Bh^\alpha\}\), its block mass obeys \(P_X(B_n)\le c_Bh^\alpha\).  Thus
\[
\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)
 \le 8\,c_Bh^\alpha\,q_n\,h^2 .
\]
The weak-arm level satisfies \(q_n\le h^{\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}}\): if \(\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}=0\), then \(q_n=1/4\le1=h^0\), while otherwise \(q_n=h^{\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}}\), as specified in \cref{obj:def:two-point-witness}.  Therefore
\[
\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)
 \le 8c_B\,h_n^{2+\alpha+\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}}
 \le C\,h_n^{2+\alpha+\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}},
\]
which is the asserted one-observation bound. % lean: twoPointWitness_one_draw_chiSq_bound

\emph{Step 2: the product experiment.}  The one-observation absolute continuity and square-integrability just obtained tensorize, so \(P_+^{\otimes n}\ll P_-^{\otimes n}\) and
\[
\int \left(\frac{dP_+^{\otimes n}}{dP_-^{\otimes n}}-1\right)^2 dP_-^{\otimes n}<\infty .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.pi_iid_absolutelyContinuous % lean: Causalean.Stat.pi_iid_integrable_sq_dev
By \cref{obj:def:exponents}, \(D_{\alpha,\gamma}=2+\alpha+\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}>0\), and \cref{obj:def:two-point-witness} gives \(h_n=n^{-1/D_{\alpha,\gamma}}\).  Hence
\[
h_n^{D_{\alpha,\gamma}}=n^{-1},
\qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad
n\,\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)
\le 8c_B .
\]
The chi-square divergence tensorizes as
\[
1+\chi^2(P_+^{\otimes n}\,\|\,P_-^{\otimes n})
 =
 \left(1+\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)\right)^n .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.one_add_chiSqDiv_pi_iid_general
Using \(1+x\le e^x\) for \(x=\chi^2(P_+\|P_-)\ge0\) and the assumption \(8c_B<\log5\),
\[
\left(1+\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)\right)^n
 \le \exp\left\{n\,\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)\right\}
 \le \exp\{8c_B\}
 < 5 .
\]
Therefore \(1+\chi^2(P_+^{\otimes n}\|P_-^{\otimes n})\le5\), and so
\[
\chi^2(P_+^{\otimes n}\,\|\,P_-^{\otimes n})\le4\le 5\le C .
\]
% lean: two_point_divergence
\end{proof}
