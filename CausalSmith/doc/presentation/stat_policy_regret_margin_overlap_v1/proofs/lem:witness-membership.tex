\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:witness-membership}]
Fix a witness sign \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\).  Write \(h=h_n\), \(q=q_n\), \(\tau_0=(u_0+2)/2\), and \(B_n=[0,c_Bh^\alpha]\).  Since \(0<u_0<2\) by \cref{obj:ass:margin-window}, we have \(\tau_0\in(u_0,2)\).  After discarding finitely many \(n\), the construction of \cref{obj:def:two-point-witness} has \(n\ge1\), \(0<h\le1/2\), and \(0<q\le1/2\). % lean: eventually_hLower_le_half % lean: eventually_qLower_pos_le_half
By construction the contrast, propensity, and overlap score of \(P_{n,\sigma}\) are
\[
\tau_{P_{n,\sigma}}(x)=
\begin{cases}\sigma h,&x\in B_n,\\ \tau_0,&x\notin B_n,\end{cases}
\qquad
e_{P_{n,\sigma}}(x)=
\begin{cases}q,&x\in B_n,\\ 1/2,&x\notin B_n,\end{cases}
\qquad
p_{P_{n,\sigma}}(x)=
\begin{cases}q,&x\in B_n,\\ 1/2,&x\notin B_n,\end{cases}
\]
the last because \(q\le1/2\) makes \(\min\{q,1-q\}=q\).  The arm regressions are \(\mu_0=0\) and \(\mu_1=\sigma h\) on \(B_n\), and \(\mu_0=-\tau_0/2\), \(\mu_1=\tau_0/2\) off \(B_n\).  We check the defining conditions of \cref{obj:def:law-class} in turn.

\emph{Well-formedness and bounded outcomes.}  The construction specifies the covariate law, propensity, outcome kernels, and nuisance functions explicitly; these fields are measurable, the contrast is the difference of the two regressions, and the tested propensity and regression identities agree with the kernels, so \(P_{n,\sigma}\) is a well-formed observed law. % lean: twoPointWitness_wellFormed
Its outcome is supported on \(\{-1,1\}\) or on \(\{0\}\), so \(Y\in[-1,1]\) almost surely; and the arm regressions take only the values \(0\), \(\sigma h\), and \(\pm\tau_0/2\), which lie in \([-1,1]\) because \(h\le1/2\) and \(\tau_0<2\). % lean: twoPointWitness_boundedOutcome

\emph{Positivity.}  From the display above, \(e_{P_{n,\sigma}}(x)\in\{q,1/2\}\), and \(0<q\le1/2<1\), so \(0<e_{P_{n,\sigma}}(x)<1\) for every \(x\). % lean: twoPointWitness_positivity

\emph{Zero-effect.}  The contrast takes only the values \(\sigma h\ne0\) and \(\tau_0>0\), so \(\{\tau_{P_{n,\sigma}}=0\}=\varnothing\) and in particular \(P_X\{\tau_{P_{n,\sigma}}(X)=0\}=0\). % lean: twoPointWitness_zeroEffect

\emph{Margin.}  Fix \(0<u\le u_0\) and put \(E_m=\{x:0<|\tau_{P_{n,\sigma}}(x)|\le u\}\).  Off \(B_n\) the contrast has magnitude \(\tau_0>u_0\ge u\), so \(E_m\subseteq B_n\), and since \(P_X\) is Lebesgue measure restricted to \([0,1]\),
\[
P_X(E_m)\le P_X(B_n)\le c_Bh^\alpha .
\]
If \(h>u\), then on \(B_n\) the contrast has magnitude \(h>u\), so \(E_m=\varnothing\) and the bound is trivial.  If \(h\le u\), then \(h^\alpha\le u^\alpha\) because \(\alpha\ge0\), and, using \(c_B\le C_m\),
\[
P_X(E_m)\le c_Bh^\alpha\le c_Bu^\alpha\le C_mu^\alpha .
\]
% lean: twoPointWitness_marginTail

\emph{Strict-overlap endpoint.}  Suppose \(\gamma=0\).  Then \(\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}=0\), so \cref{obj:def:two-point-witness} gives \(q=1/4\), and the overlap score takes only the values \(1/4\) and \(1/2\).  Since \(0<\underline p\le1/4\), we get \(p_{P_{n,\sigma}}(x)\ge\underline p\) for every \(x\). % lean: twoPointWitness_strictOverlapEndpoint

\emph{Overlap decay.}  Fix \(u,v\) with \(0<u\le u_0\), \(v>0\), and \(v\le c_ou^\gamma\), and let
\[
E=\{x:\ p_{P_{n,\sigma}}(x)\le v,\ 0<|\tau_{P_{n,\sigma}}(x)|\le u\}.
\]
As in the margin check, off-block points have contrast magnitude \(\tau_0>u_0\ge u\), so
\[
E\subseteq B_n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
P_X(E)\le P_X(B_n)\le c_Bh^\alpha .
\]
Moreover, on \(B_n\) the contrast magnitude is \(h\).  Hence if \(h>u\), then \(E=\varnothing\) and the required bound holds because its right-hand side is nonnegative.  It remains to consider the case \(h\le u\), and we split first on \(\gamma\).

If \(\gamma=0\), the required bound is \(P_X(E)\le C_ou^\alpha\) by the convention factor in \cref{obj:def:law-class}.  From \(h\le u\), \(\alpha\ge0\), and \(c_B\le C_o\),
\[
P_X(E)\le c_Bh^\alpha\le c_Bu^\alpha\le C_ou^\alpha .
\]

Now suppose \(\gamma>0\).  On \(B_n\) the overlap score is \(q\), so if \(q>v\), then \(E=\varnothing\) and the required bound again follows from nonnegativity of the right-hand side.  Assume \(q\le v\).

If \(\alpha=0\), then \(\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}=0\), so \(q=1/4\), and \(q\le v\) reads \(1/4\le v\).  Raising to the nonnegative power \(1/\gamma\) gives \(4^{-1/\gamma}\le v^{1/\gamma}\), so with the assumed \(c_B\le C_o4^{-1/\gamma}\) and \(u^\alpha=u^0=1\),
\[
P_X(E)\le c_Bh^0=c_B\le C_o4^{-1/\gamma}\le C_o v^{1/\gamma}=C_ou^\alpha v^{1/\gamma}.
\]
% lean: activeBlock_overlap_bound_gzero

If \(\alpha>0\), then \(q=h^{\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}}\) with \(\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}=\alpha\gamma/(\alpha+1)>0\), and the calibration identity is
\[
\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}\cdot\frac{\alpha+1}{\gamma}=\alpha,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
h^\alpha=\left(h^{\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}}\right)^{(\alpha+1)/\gamma}
=\left(h^{\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}}\right)^{1/\gamma}\left(h^{\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}}\right)^{\alpha/\gamma}.
\]
Using \(h^{\beta_{\alpha,\gamma}}=q\le v\) in both factors, and then \(v\le c_ou^\gamma\) in the second factor,
\[
h^\alpha
\le
v^{1/\gamma}\,v^{\alpha/\gamma}
\le
v^{1/\gamma}\left(c_ou^\gamma\right)^{\alpha/\gamma}
=
v^{1/\gamma}c_o^{\alpha/\gamma}u^\alpha .
\]
Multiplying by \(c_B\) and using the assumed \(c_B\le C_oc_o^{-\alpha/\gamma}\),
\[
P_X(E)\le c_Bh^\alpha
\le
C_oc_o^{-\alpha/\gamma}\,c_o^{\alpha/\gamma}\,u^\alpha v^{1/\gamma}
=
C_ou^\alpha v^{1/\gamma}.
\]
% lean: activeBlock_overlap_bound_gpos
Thus the overlap-decay condition holds in every case.

\emph{Conclusion.}  All the defining conditions of \cref{obj:def:law-class} hold for the fixed sign \(\sigma\).  Applying the preceding argument for \(\sigma=1\) and \(\sigma=-1\), and intersecting the two eventual ranges of \(n\), gives \(P_{n,+},P_{n,-}\in\mathcal P_{\alpha,\gamma}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). % lean: witness_membership
For the oracle rules: under \(P_{n,+}\) the contrast equals \(h\ge0\) on \(B_n\) and \(\tau_0>0\) off \(B_n\), so \(\pi^\star_{P_{n,+}}(x)=\mathbf 1\{\tau_{P_{n,+}}(x)\ge0\}=1\) for every \(x\). % lean: twoPointWitness_optimal_plus
Under \(P_{n,-}\) the contrast equals \(-h<0\) on \(B_n\) and \(\tau_0>0\) off \(B_n\), so \(\pi^\star_{P_{n,-}}(x)=1\) exactly when \(x\notin B_n\), that is \(\pi^\star_{P_{n,-}}(x)=\mathbf 1\{x\notin B_n\}\). % lean: twoPointWitness_optimal_minus
If these two maps belong to \(\Pi\), the displayed oracle identities identify the two policy actions used in the associated two-point reduction.  Finally, the normalization \(0<u_0<2\) is exactly what permits the displayed choice \(\tau_0=(u_0+2)/2\in(u_0,2)\), which is both strictly above the margin window and compatible with the outcome bound \(|Y|\le1\). % lean: witness_membership
\end{proof}
