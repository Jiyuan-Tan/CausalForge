\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:le-cam-two-point-chisq}]
Write \(P=P_+^{\otimes n}\) and \(Q=P_-^{\otimes n}\), and let
\[
A=\{\psi=+\}
\]
be the (measurable) region on which the test chooses \(+\), so that
\[
P_+^{\otimes n}(\psi\ne +)=P(A^c),
\qquad
P_-^{\otimes n}(\psi\ne -)=Q(A).
\]
Throughout we work under the two standing regularity conditions that make the chi-square divergence a genuine density functional: \(P\ll Q\), and \((dP/dQ-1)^2\) is \(Q\)-integrable.  Both are displayed hypotheses of the lemma.  Under them the chi-square divergence has the second-moment form
\[
\int \left(\frac{dP}{dQ}\right)^2\,dQ
=1+\chi^2(P\,\|\,Q)\le C_\chi+1,
\]
obtained by expanding \((dP/dQ-1)^2=(dP/dQ)^2-2\,dP/dQ+1\) and using \(\int (dP/dQ)\,dQ=1\).  Note \(C_\chi+1\ge1>0\) since \(\chi^2\ge0\). % lean: Causalean.Stat.chiSqDiv_eq

\emph{Step 1: Cauchy--Schwarz mass transfer.}  Writing the \(P\)-mass of \(A\) as a \(Q\)-integral against the density and applying Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
P(A)
=\int \mathbf 1_A\,\frac{dP}{dQ}\,dQ
\le
\left(\int \mathbf 1_A^2\,dQ\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int \left(\frac{dP}{dQ}\right)^2dQ\right)^{1/2}
\le
\sqrt{Q(A)}\,\sqrt{C_\chi+1}
=
\sqrt{(C_\chi+1)\,Q(A)} .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.rnDeriv_setIntegral_le_sqrt_chi

\emph{Step 2: the testing floor.}  Let \(e=P(A^c)+Q(A)\) be the combined error.  Since \(P(A^c)=1-P(A)\) and \(Q(A)\ge0\), Step 1 gives
\[
1-e=P(A)-Q(A)\le P(A)\le \sqrt{(C_\chi+1)\,Q(A)} .
\]
Suppose, to get a contradiction, that
\[
e<\frac{1}{4(C_\chi+1)} .
\]
Since \(P(A^c)\ge0\), we have \(Q(A)\le e\), hence \(Q(A)<1/(4(C_\chi+1))\) and therefore
\[
(C_\chi+1)Q(A)<\tfrac14,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\sqrt{(C_\chi+1)Q(A)}<\tfrac12 .
\]
Also \(C_\chi+1\ge1\) gives \(1/(4(C_\chi+1))\le1/4\), so \(e<1/4\) and hence \(1-e>1/2\).  This contradicts the previous display.  Therefore
\[
P_+^{\otimes n}(\psi\ne +)+P_-^{\otimes n}(\psi\ne -)
 =P(A^c)+Q(A)
 \ge \frac{1}{4(C_\chi+1)}
 =c(C_\chi)>0,
\]
a floor that depends only on \(C_\chi\) and not on the particular pair of laws or on the test. % lean: Causalean.Stat.testing_error_lower_of_chi % lean: Causalean.Stat.le_cam_two_point_chisq

\emph{Step 3: the one-observation sufficient condition.}  Suppose now \(c_0\ge0\), \(n>0\), and the single-observation divergence satisfies \(\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)\le c_0/n\), the two standing regularity conditions above now being imposed on the single-observation pair \((P_+,P_-)\).  The product experiment obeys the tensorization identity
\[
1+\chi^2(P_+^{\otimes n}\,\|\,P_-^{\otimes n})
=
\left(1+\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)\right)^n .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.one_add_chiSqDiv_pi_iid_general
Using \(1+x\le e^x\) with \(x=\chi^2(P_+\|P_-)\ge0\) and then \(n\chi^2(P_+\|P_-)\le c_0\),
\[
\left(1+\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)\right)^n
\le
\exp\left\{n\,\chi^2(P_+\,\|\,P_-)\right\}
\le e^{c_0},
\]
so the product divergence is bounded, \(\chi^2(P_+^{\otimes n}\|P_-^{\otimes n})\le e^{c_0}-1\).  Applying Steps 1--2 to the product experiment with the budget \(C_\chi=e^{c_0}-1\ge0\) yields the uniform testing floor
\[
P_+^{\otimes n}(\psi\ne+)+P_-^{\otimes n}(\psi\ne-)
\ge
\frac{1}{4\{(e^{c_0}-1)+1\}}
=
\frac{1}{4e^{c_0}}
>0 .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Stat.le_cam_two_point_chisq
\end{proof}
