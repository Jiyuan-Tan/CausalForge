# Euclidean radial-polynomial VC-subgraph classes

## Goal

Build reusable Causalean covering certificates for bounded, compactly
supported radial-polynomial classes with a moving Euclidean center.  The
immediate dimension is two, but a finite-dimensional Euclidean statement is
preferred when it does not materially enlarge the proof.

## Provides

- A pseudo-dimension or polynomial L²-cover certificate for moving-center
  compactly supported Euclidean radial monomials.
- Finite Boolean/signed-arm variants and the finite linear-combination closure
  needed by bounded polynomial score classes.

## Statement / milestones

For fixed positive bandwidth bounds and fixed natural degree `p`, cover the
class generated by functions of the form

    z ↦ 1{a ≤ dist(z,x) ∧ dist(z,x) ≤ b}
          * (dist(z,x) / q)^k

where the center `x` varies, `0 < q`, `0 ≤ k ≤ p`, and the interval endpoints
are fixed multiples of `q`.  Signed-arm variants may be represented by a
finite Boolean index.  The useful public conclusion is either:

- an explicit `HasPseudoDimAtMost` bound depending only on Euclidean
  dimension and `p`; or
- directly a `HasPolynomialL2Cover` certificate with a positive constant
  envelope.

Also provide the finite-vector/linear-combination closure needed for

    indicator * radial_monomial_j
      * (bounded_response - Σₖ bₖ radial_monomial_k),

with coefficients in a fixed box.  Existing general algebra lemmas should be
used for this assembly rather than duplicated.

The certificate must be uniform over every probability measure `Q`, not only
Lebesgue or atomless measures.  Consequently a continuity/Lipschitz net in
the center is not sufficient: atomic `Q` can charge moving kernel boundaries.
The proof should work at the VC-subgraph/finite-trace level, using the
semialgebraic description of squared Euclidean distance and Boolean
combinations of ball/annulus predicates.  It must cover odd radial powers as
well as even ones.

## Standard reference

Standard VC-subgraph and semialgebraic growth bounds for finite-dimensional
families, combined with Sauer--Shelah and the pseudo-dimension-to-L²-cover
conversion. The result must be trace-level and uniform over atomic measures.
