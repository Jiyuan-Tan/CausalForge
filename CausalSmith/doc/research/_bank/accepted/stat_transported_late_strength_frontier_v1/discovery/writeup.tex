\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}transported\_\allowbreak{}late\_\allowbreak{}strength\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The transported complier effect is forced into \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), and its honest expected-length frontier is governed by the effective strength \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\).  Conditional on each admissible deterministic source-covariate, transport-weight, and propensity geometry, a converse and one oracle source-score inversion sequence match at order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) for every fixed threshold \(t_0\), even when \(\mu_n\to0\) and \(\kappa_n\to\infty\).  In the separate uniform finite-cell experiment, target cell probabilities are learned from the covariate-only target sample with no additional minimax-order loss; the feasible conclusion also extends to known regular nonuniform source cells and known cell-varying propensities.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Chen and Huang (2025, \cite{ChenHuang2025}) give the transported-CACE identifying ratio and regular weighted or multiply robust asymptotics; Zeng et al. (2024, \cite{ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2024}) give regular transport/generalization efficiency and minimax theory for target causal effects. Choi and Shen (2019, \cite{ChoiShen2019}) address weak-IV confidence sets in a linear two-sample IV model, while Mikusheva (2010, \cite{Mikusheva2010}) analyzes a different invariant linear-IV experiment. The accepted-bank discrete-ATE minimax result studies MSE under unconfoundedness with dimension and overlap, not an IV-ratio confidence-length problem. These comparators do not give a fixed-geometry honest expected-length frontier for a bounded transported causal ratio with \(\mu_n\to0\) and \(\kappa_n\to\infty\), nor a no-order-loss theorem for learning target cell weights from a covariate-only sample.

\paragraph{Niche.} The note fixes the deterministic geometry \((P_S^X,w,e)\) before taking the conditional minimax risk, isolates the index \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\), and then separately studies target-weight learning where uniform or regular source cell probabilities and the assignment propensity are known.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves matching lower and upper oracle frontiers, including a converse conditional on every admissible fixed geometry, and proves that estimating target cell probabilities causes no order loss on the uniform finite-cell class or its known regular nonuniform extension. This is not a general learned-nuisance result: unknown assignment propensities and feasibility beyond the stated finite-cell designs remain outside the established scope.

\section{Related work}

Chen and Huang (2025, \cite{ChenHuang2025}) provide the transported-CACE Wald identifying ratio and regular weighted and multiply robust asymptotics. Their guarantee is not a conditional minimax bound for honest confidence-set length, does not fix an arbitrary deterministic \((P_S^X,w,e)\) geometry, and does not cover the joint boundary \(\mu_n\to0\), \(\kappa_n\to\infty\). Zeng, Kennedy, Bodnar, and Naimi (2024, \cite{ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2024}) provide regular efficiency and minimax theory for transported target causal effects; that is a different estimand and regularity regime from a bounded transported complier ratio with a vanishing first stage. Rudolph and van der Laan (2017, \cite{RudolphVanderLaan2017}) likewise develop regular semiparametric transported-encouragement inference under a different observed-data design. Mikusheva (2010, \cite{Mikusheva2010}), Ma (2023, \cite{Ma2023}), and Smucler, Lanni, and Masip (2025, \cite{SmuclerLanniMasip2025}) concern weak-IV-robust inference in one-population or invariant linear-IV settings rather than a bounded transported CACE with diverging weight dispersion. Choi and Shen (2019, \cite{ChoiShen2019}) give weak-instrument-robust confidence sets for linear two-sample IV, but their experiment does not have a covariate-only target sample, transport density ratio, or conditional minimax expected-length frontier; Choi, Gu, and Shen (2018, \cite{ChoiGuShen2018}) has the same relevant linear-two-sample contrast. Within the accepted CausalSmith bank, stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap is also distinct: it proves a discrete unconfounded-ATE MSE frontier driven by dimension and overlap, rather than IV-ratio identification, transport/Kish geometry, or honest confidence-set length. The present results instead establish matched honest expected-length orders, first conditionally on each fixed transport geometry and then for the stated structured target-weight-learning experiments.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:full-data-support]\label{ass:full-data-support}
\(P_n^F\{S\in\{0,1\},D(0),D(1)\in\{0,1\},Y(0),Y(1)\in[0,1]\}=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Bounded potential outcomes are routine for minimax expected-length analysis of binary or rescaled outcomes. This load-bearing restriction forces the causal ratio to have a finite range rather than importing an arbitrary truncation.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:population-presence]\label{ass:population-presence}
\(P_n^F(S=s)>0\) for \(s\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (two-population support, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:two-sample-array]\label{ass:two-sample-array}
\((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n\sim P_S^{\otimes n}\), \((X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\sim P_T^{\otimes N_n}\), \((O_i^S)_{i=1}^n\perp(X_j^T)_{j=1}^{N_n}\), and \(N_n/n\to c\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent two-sample triangular-array sampling, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:instrument-overlap]\label{ass:instrument-overlap}
\(\varepsilon\le e(X)\le1-\varepsilon\) \(P_S\)-almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (instrument positivity, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:source-observation]\label{ass:source-observation}
\(P(Z=1\mid X,S=1)=e(X)\), \(D=D(Z)\), and \(Y=Y(D)\) conditional on \(S=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (source assignment and consistency, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iv-randomization]\label{ass:iv-randomization}
\(Z\perp(Y(0),Y(1),D(0),D(1))\mid(X,S=1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (conditional instrument exchangeability, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iv-exclusion]\label{ass:iv-exclusion}
\(Y^Z(z)=Y(D(z))\) \(P_n^F\)-almost surely conditional on \(S=s\), for \(z,s\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (instrument exclusion restriction, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iv-monotonicity]\label{ass:iv-monotonicity}
\(D(1)\ge D(0)\) \(P_n^F\)-almost surely conditional on \(S=s\), for \(s\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (no-defiers monotonicity, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-transport]\label{ass:outcome-transport}
\(\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=0]=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=1]\) for \(P_T\)-almost every \(x\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (conditional transport of the assignment-outcome contrast, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:receipt-transport]\label{ass:receipt-transport}
\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}[\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[D(1)-D(0)\mid X,S=0]]=\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}[\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[D(1)-D(0)\mid X,S=1]]\)}}\par\noindent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (integrated transport of the first stage, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:target-complier-positivity]\label{ass:target-complier-positivity}
\(P_n^F(D(1)>D(0)\mid S=0)>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (positive target complier share, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:transport-domination]\label{ass:transport-domination}
\(P_T\ll P_S^X\) on \(\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (transport covariate-support inclusion, \cite{ChenHuang2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:weight-envelope]\label{ass:weight-envelope}
\(0\le w(X)\le2k_n\) \(P_S\)-almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Calibrated or coarsened transport designs can impose an externally auditable density-ratio cap while allowing the cap to grow with increasingly distinct target populations.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:weight-second-moment]\label{ass:weight-second-moment}
\(\kappa_n\le k_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition permits growing Kish design effects realized by finite calibration tables while retaining a transparent overlap scale.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:degrading-array]\label{ass:degrading-array}
\(k_n\to\infty\), \(k_n=o(n^{1/2})\), and \(\mu_n\to0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A target recruitment shift can simultaneously concentrate covariates in poorly represented strata and reduce the target complier share.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-cell-source]\label{ass:finite-cell-source}
\(P_S^X\) is supported on exactly \(k_n\) measurable cells, assigns mass \(1/k_n\) to each cell, and \(e(X)=1/2\) \(P_S\)-almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Published calibration tables can fix source-cell shares and assignment probabilities while a registry leaves only the target cell probabilities unknown.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:compact-causal-range]\label{prop:compact-causal-range}
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_n\), source randomization identifies \(\Delta_Y(x)=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=1]\) and \(\Delta_D(x)=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[D(1)-D(0)\mid X=x,S=1]\). Outcome-contrast transport and integrated first-stage transport imply \(\mu_{Y,n}=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[(Y(1)-Y(0))\mathbf 1\{D(1)>D(0)\}\mid S=0]\) and \(\mu_n=P_n^F(D(1)>D(0)\mid S=0)\). Hence \(\mu_{Y,n}/\mu_n=\theta_T=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid D(1)>D(0),S=0]\in\Theta=[-1,1]\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_n\). All conditional equalities below are understood for the relevant almost-everywhere versions. By source consistency and exclusion, for \(z\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
D=D(z)\quad\text{and}\quad Y=Y(D(z))=Y^Z(z)
\]
on the event \(\{Z=z,S=1\}\). Conditional instrument exchangeability implies that \(Z\) is conditionally independent, given \((X,S=1)\), of both \(D(z)\) and \(Y^Z(z)=Y(D(z))\), because the latter is a measurable function of \((Y(0),Y(1),D(z))\). Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E_S[Y\mid Z=z,X=x]
 =\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y^Z(z)\mid X=x,S=1]
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_S[D\mid Z=z,X=x]
 =\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[D(z)\mid X=x,S=1].
\]
Subtracting the identities for \(z=0\) from those for \(z=1\), and using the definitions of the observed source contrasts, gives
\[
\Delta_Y(x)=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X=x,S=1]
\]
and
\[
\Delta_D(x)=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[D(1)-D(0)\mid X=x,S=1].
\]
These equalities establish source identification of the two conditional contrasts.

Let \(A=\mathbf 1\{D(1)>D(0)\}\). Since \(D(0),D(1)\) are binary and monotonicity holds in both populations,
\[
D(1)-D(0)=A.
\]
Exclusion and the elementary binary-treatment identity \(Y(d)=Y(0)+d\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\) further yield
\[
Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)
 =Y(D(1))-Y(D(0))
 =\{D(1)-D(0)\}\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}
 =A\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}.
\]
Transport domination and the definition \(w=dP_T/dP_S^X\) imply the change-of-measure identity \(\mathbb E_S[w(X)h(X)]=\mathbb E_T[h(X)]\) for every bounded measurable \(h\). Applying it to \(h=\Delta_Y\), then applying conditional outcome-contrast transport and iterated expectation, gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned}
\mu_{Y,n}
&=\mathbb E_T[\Delta_Y(X)]\\
&=\mathbb E_T\!\left[\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X,S=1]\right]\\
&=\mathbb E_T\!\left[\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid X,S=0]\right]\\
&=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y^Z(1)-Y^Z(0)\mid S=0]\\
&=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}A\mid S=0].
\end{aligned}\)}}
\]
Similarly, change of measure, the identified receipt contrast, integrated first-stage transport, and iterated expectation imply
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned}
\mu_n
&=\mathbb E_T[\Delta_D(X)]\\
&=\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}\!\left[\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[D(1)-D(0)\mid X,S=1]\right]\\
&=\mathbb E_{X\mid S=0}\!\left[\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[D(1)-D(0)\mid X,S=0]\right]\\
&=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[D(1)-D(0)\mid S=0]\\
&=P_n^F(D(1)>D(0)\mid S=0).
\end{aligned}\)}}
\]
Target complier positivity gives \(\mu_n>0\), so division is valid. Therefore
\[
\frac{\mu_{Y,n}}{\mu_n}
=\mathbb E_{P_n^F}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid D(1)>D(0),S=0]
=\theta_T.
\]
Finally, full-data support gives \(-1\le Y(1)-Y(0)\le1\) almost surely. A conditional expectation of this contrast also lies in \([-1,1]\), and hence \(\theta_T\in[-1,1]=\Theta\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition makes the target a genuine causal estimand and establishes that compactness follows from the full-data outcome bound. \textit{Gap.} Dufour (1997) addresses an unbounded locally unidentified coefficient, not an intrinsically bounded target complier effect. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the finite-diameter premise for the frontier.

\begin{theorem}[thm:oracle-converse]\label{thm:oracle-converse}
There exist constants \(c_0>0\) and \(t_c>0\), depending only on \(\alpha\), \(\varepsilon\), and the fixed class constants, such that for every \(t_0>0\), \(V^\star(t_0)\ge c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\) and \(V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)\ge c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\). In particular, \(V^\star(t_0)\ge c_0\) whenever \(0<t_0\le t_c\). The constants do not depend on the decomposition of \(t_0\) into \(\mu_n\) and \(\kappa_n\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set \(c_0=3(1-\alpha)^2/16\) and \(t_c=1\). Fix \(t_0>0\). For any admissible deterministic geometry \(\mathfrak g\), every globally oracle-honest sequence is honest on the fixed-geometry slice \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), and its global risk dominates its risk on that slice. Therefore the fixed-geometry frontier theorem gives
\[
 V^\star(t_0)\ge V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
 \ge c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
For the finite-cell value, choose the admissible geometry for which \(P_S^X\) is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,k_n\}\), \(w=1\), and \(e=1/2\). Its fixed-geometry slice is contained in \(\mathcal N_n\). By the no-shift reduction, \(\kappa_n=1\) and the restriction \(t_n\ge t_0\) on this slice is exactly \(n\mu_n^2\ge t_0\). Every globally oracle-honest sequence is conditionally honest on the slice, and its finite-cell risk dominates the slice risk. Hence
\[
 V_{\mathcal N}^\star(t_0)
 \ge V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
 \ge c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
These conclusions hold for every \(t_0>0\). If \(0<t_0\le t_c=1\), then the minimum equals one and thus \(V^\star(t_0)\ge c_0\). Finally, the fixed-geometry least-favourable construction holds \(P_S^X\), \(w\), \(e\), and \(\kappa_n\) fixed and sets \(\mu_n=(t_0\kappa_n/n)^{1/2}\). Its total-variation calibration and therefore \(c_0\) depend on the pair \((\mu_n,\kappa_n)\) only through \(t_0=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\). This proves the asserted decomposition invariance.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The converse tests whether Kish deflation is an information bound rather than a variance diagnostic. \textit{Gap.} Mikusheva (2010) treats invariant linear-IV arc length, while Armstrong and Kolesar (2021) treat regular overlap-sensitive ATEs; neither supplies this transported causal-ratio converse. \textit{Consumer.} It identifies regimes where even an oracle cannot give a short honest set in deep-canvassing and transported-encouragement studies.

\begin{proposition}[prop:no-shift-reduction]\label{prop:no-shift-reduction}
If \(w(X)=1\) \(P_S\)-almost surely, then \(\kappa_n=1\) and \(t_n=n\mu_n^2\); hence the frontier specializes to compact single-population weak-ratio inference.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(w(X)=1\) \(P_S\)-almost surely. Since \(w\) is a function of \(X\), this equality also holds \(P_S^X\)-almost surely. The definitions give
\[
 \kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]=1,
 \qquad
 t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}=n\mu_n^2.
\]
Moreover, because \(w=dP_T/dP_S^X\), every measurable \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\) satisfies
\[
 P_T(A)=\int_Aw(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=P_S^X(A),
\]
so there is no covariate-distribution shift. The compact-range proposition gives the forced parameter space \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). Now fix any admissible no-shift geometry \(\mathfrak g\) with \(w\equiv1\). For every fixed \(t_0>0\), the fixed-geometry frontier theorem gives the lower bound
\[
 V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
 \ge \frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
whereas the oracle score-inversion rule applies on this slice and gives the matching upper order. Since \(t_n=n\mu_n^2\), the strength restriction is precisely \(n\mu_n^2\ge t_0\). Thus the no-shift conditional frontier is the compact single-population weak-ratio frontier, up to the universal constants in the two theorems, and it is indexed by \(n\mu_n^2\). The dependency order is acyclic: the fixed-geometry theorem supplies the lower bound, the score-inversion theorem supplies the upper bound, and neither conclusion is obtained from the global converse.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} When \(w=1\), the effective-strength index reduces to the familiar single-population weak-ratio index \(n\mu_n^2\). \textit{Gap.} The proposition is an index reduction; the fixed-geometry theorem supplies the separate two-sided minimax statement on the no-shift slice. \textit{Consumer.} It connects the transported frontier to compact single-population weak-ratio inference without conflating the two experiments.

\begin{theorem}[thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment]\label{thm:oracle-score-inversion-attainment}
Let
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle H_i=\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)},\quad
 \widehat A_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw(X_i)H_iY_i,\quad
 \widehat B_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw(X_i)H_iD_i,\)}}
\]
and \(\widehat\kappa_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nw(X_i)^2\).  With \(L_\alpha=\{8/(\alpha\varepsilon^2)\}^{1/2}\), define the single score-inversion sequence
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle C_n=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
 |\widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n|
 \le L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\right\}.\)}}
\]
Then \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\), and, simultaneously for every \(t_0>0\),
\[
 R((C_n),t_0)\le C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
 \qquad
 C_0=\max\left\{2,4L_\alpha+\frac8{\varepsilon^2}\right\}.
\]
Consequently \(V^\star(t_0)\) has order \(\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\).  The displayed upper bound holds for every admissible weight geometry and depends on a member law through \(t_n=n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\) only, apart from the fixed constants.  Combined with the fixed-geometry frontier theorem, the achievability bound and the conditional converse match in order uniformly over admissible geometries, so any two admissible geometries with the same effective strength are equally difficult up to universal constants.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(\vartheta\in\Theta\), define
\[
 \psi_{i,\vartheta}=w(X_i)H_i(Y_i-\vartheta D_i).
\]
Conditional randomization, consistency, and the definition of \(H_i\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\psi_{i,\vartheta}]
 =\mu_{Y,n}-\vartheta\mu_n.
\]
By the compact-range proposition this mean is zero at \(\vartheta=\theta_T\). Instrument overlap and boundedness give \(|H_i|\le\varepsilon^{-1}\) and \(|Y_i-\theta_TD_i|\le2\), whence
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\psi_{i,\theta_T})
 \le \frac{4\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2}.
\]
The weight envelope gives \(w^4\le4k_n^2w^2\), so
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\kappa_n)
 \le\frac{\mathbb E_P[w(X)^4]}n
 \le\frac{4k_n^2\kappa_n}{n}.
\]
Since \(\mathbb E_P[w(X)]=1\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\kappa_n\ge1\). Because \(k_n=o(n^{1/2})\) and \(\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_n]=\kappa_n\), Chebyshev's inequality yields, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_n\),
\[
 P(\widehat\kappa_n<\kappa_n/2)
 \le\frac{16k_n^2}{n\kappa_n}=o(1).
\]
On the complementary event, failure to include \(\theta_T\) implies
\[
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{i,\theta_T}\right|
 >L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{2n}}.
\]
A second application of Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle P\left(
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{i,\theta_T}\right|
 >L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{2n}}
 \right)
 \le\frac{8}{L_\alpha^2\varepsilon^2}=\alpha.\)}}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
 P(\theta_T\in C_n)\ge1-\alpha,
\]
so \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\).

For the length bound, put \(r_n=L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\). The acceptance set is the intersection of \(\Theta\) with the inverse image of \([-r_n,r_n]\) under the affine map \(\vartheta\mapsto\widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n\). Therefore
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \lambda(C_n)\le
 \begin{cases}
 \min\{2,2r_n/|\widehat B_n|\},&\widehat B_n\ne0,\\
 2,&\widehat B_n=0.
 \end{cases}\)}}
\]
The summand \(w(X_i)H_iD_i\) has mean \(\mu_n\) and variance at most \(\kappa_n/\varepsilon^2\). Thus
\[
 P(|\widehat B_n-\mu_n|>\mu_n/2)
 \le\frac{4\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2n\mu_n^2}
 =\frac4{\varepsilon^2t_n}.
\]
On the complementary event, \(|\widehat B_n|\ge\mu_n/2\), while Jensen's inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[r_n]
 \le L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_n]}n}
 =L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{n}}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
 \le\frac{4L_\alpha}{\sqrt{t_n}}
 +\frac8{\varepsilon^2t_n}.
\]
For \(t_0\ge1\), taking the supremum over \(t_n\ge t_0\) bounds the right-hand side by \((4L_\alpha+8\varepsilon^{-2})t_0^{-1/2}\). For \(0<t_0<1\), use \(\lambda(C_n)\le2\). Taking the required limit superior proves
\[
 R((C_n),t_0)
 \le C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
For the matching lower order, fix any \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\). Every globally oracle-honest sequence is honest on \(\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)\), and its global risk dominates its fixed-slice risk. Hence the lower half of the fixed-geometry frontier theorem implies
\[
 V^\star(t_0)\ge V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
 \ge\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Together with the displayed achievable upper bound, this proves the global minimax order. The upper-bound calculation uses a geometry only through \(\kappa_n\), while the fixed-geometry theorem supplies a matching conditional converse with constants uniform in \(\mathfrak g\). Thus the two sides match uniformly over admissible geometries, and geometries with the same effective strength are equally difficult up to universal constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment]\label{thm:finite-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}
On \(\mathcal N_n\), write
\[
 H_i=2(2Z_i-1),\qquad
 \widehat p_{x,n}=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{j=1}^{N_n}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=x\},
\]
and, for \(G\in\{Y,D\}\), define
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \widehat m_{G,n}(x)=\frac{k_n}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i,\qquad
 \widehat M_{G,n}=\sum_{x=1}^{k_n}\widehat p_{x,n}\widehat m_{G,n}(x).\)}}
\]
For \(N_n\ge2\), let
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \widehat\kappa_{U,n}=
 \frac{k_n}{N_n(N_n-1)}
 \sum_{j\ne\ell}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\},\qquad
 \widehat K_n=1+\widehat\kappa_{U,n}.\)}}
\]
Put \(B_c=32(1+c^{-1})\), \(L_{\alpha,c}=\{2B_c/\alpha\}^{1/2}\), and
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle C_n=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
 |\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|
 \le L_{\alpha,c}\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\right\},\)}}
\]
with \(C_n=\Theta\) at the finitely many indices for which \(N_n<2\).  This sequence belongs to \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\), does not use \(w\), and satisfies, simultaneously for all \(t_0>0\),
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
 \le C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},\)}}
\]
where
\[
 C_0=\max\{2,4\sqrt2L_{\alpha,c}+8B_c\}.
\]
Moreover, the finite-cell change-of-measure family used for the oracle converse applies to every sequence in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\); hence the corresponding infimum of the displayed worst-case risk is at least \(c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\).  Thus estimating the target cell probabilities creates no additional minimax-order loss on \(\mathcal N_n\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Specialize the regular-cell theorem to
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle q_{x,n}=\frac1{k_n},
 \qquad e(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 L=L_{\alpha,c}=\left\{\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right\}^{1/2},
 \qquad B_c=32(1+c^{-1}).\)}}
\]
Then its score is \(H_i=2(2Z_i-1)\), and its source-cell estimator becomes
\[
 \frac1{nq_{x,n}}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i
 =\frac{k_n}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i.
\]
Its collision estimator likewise becomes
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \frac1{N_n(N_n-1)}\sum_{j\ne\ell}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}}{q_{X_j^T,n}}
 =\frac{k_n}{N_n(N_n-1)}\sum_{j\ne\ell}
 \mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}.\)}}
\]
Because \(c>0\), one has \((4+3/c)^{-1/2}<1/2\). Choose
\[
 \bar\varepsilon=\frac14+\frac12(4+3/c)^{-1/2}.
\]
Then \(\bar\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\) and \(\bar\varepsilon^{-2}\le 4+3/c\). Since \(e(x)=1/2\) on \(\mathcal N_n\),
\[
 \bar\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\bar\varepsilon,
\]
so the regular-cell theorem applies with the admissible interior overlap constant \(\bar\varepsilon\). Its variance constant satisfies
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned}
 B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}
 &=8(\bar\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1})\\
 &\le8(4+3c^{-1}+c^{-1})
 =32(1+c^{-1})=B_c.
 \end{aligned}\)}}
\]
Therefore \(L_{\alpha,c}\ge\{2B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}/\alpha\}^{1/2}\), so the exact confidence set in the statement is honest. Because the specialized construction uses only the two observed samples, it lies in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) and does not use \(w\). The regular-cell length bound gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned}
 &\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]\\
 &\qquad\le
 \max\{2,4\sqrt2L_{\alpha,c}+8B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\\
 &\qquad\le C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
 \end{aligned}\)}}
\]
For the converse, use the fixed geometry \(q_{x,n}=1/k_n\), \(w=1\), and \(e=1/2\). It is a submodel of \(\mathcal N_n\), and the target-sample law and all known design inputs are fixed throughout the continuum of outcome alternatives in the fixed-geometry construction. Every sequence in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) is therefore conditionally honest on this slice, while its finite-cell risk dominates its slice risk. The lower half of the fixed-geometry frontier theorem, equivalently the finite-cell specialization used in the oracle converse, yields
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
 \ge c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},\)}}
\]
with \(c_0=3(1-\alpha)^2/16\). The upper and lower orders coincide, proving that estimating the target cell probabilities causes no additional minimax-order loss.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result separates learning the transport weights from weak-ratio identification: in the stated uniform-cell experiment, the target covariate sample can learn the cell probabilities without changing the honest expected-length order. \textit{Gap.} The closest transported-CACE reference, Chen and Huang (2025, \cite{ChenHuang2025}), supplies the transported identifying ratio and regular weighted or multiply robust asymptotics; it does not analyze a covariate-only target sample that learns unknown target cell probabilities, nor worst-case honest expected set length as the target first stage vanishes. Choi, Gu, and Shen (2018, \cite{ChoiGuShen2018}) give weak-IV-robust procedures for linear two-sample IV, not a transport-weight learning problem with a bounded causal-ratio target, known uniform source cells, and a collision scale estimate. Thus neither comparator establishes the no-additional-order-loss conclusion for \(\mathcal N_n\). \textit{Consumer.} It identifies a genuinely feasible benchmark against the oracle frontier and provides the uniform-cell specialization consumed by the regular-cell extension.

\begin{theorem}[thm:fixed-geometry-frontier]\label{thm:fixed-geometry-frontier}
Let \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) be any admissible deterministic geometry array.  Put \(L_\alpha=\{8/(\alpha\varepsilon^2)\}^{1/2}\), \(c_\alpha=3(1-\alpha)^2/16\), and \(C_\alpha=\max\{2,4L_\alpha+8\varepsilon^{-2}\}\).  Then, for every fixed \(t_0>0\),
\[
 c_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
 \le V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
 \le C_\alpha\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Both constants are uniform in \(\mathfrak g\).  In particular, after conditioning on any admissible source-covariate law, transport-weight array, and propensity array, geometries having the same effective strength are equally difficult up to the displayed universal constants.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) and \(t_0>0\). We prove the lower and upper bounds separately.

For the lower bound, abbreviate the fixed geometry by \((P_S^X,w_n,e_n)\), let \(\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w_n(X)^2]\), and set
\[
 \mu_n=\sqrt{\frac{t_0\kappa_n}{n}},
 \qquad
 p_n(x)=\frac{\mu_nw_n(x)}{\kappa_n}.
\]
The normalization \(\mathbb E_S[w_n(X)]=1\) implies \(\kappa_n\ge1\). The envelope and the degrading-array condition then give
\[
 0\le\sup_xp_n(x)
 \le\frac{2\sqrt{t_0}\,k_n}{\sqrt{n\kappa_n}}
 \le\frac{2\sqrt{t_0}\,k_n}{\sqrt n}=o(1),
\]
so \(p_n(x)\) is a valid compliance probability for all sufficiently large \(n\). Also
\[
 \mu_n^2\le\frac{t_0k_n}{n}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so the constructed triangular array satisfies \(\mu_n\to0\).

Use the fixed-geometry continuum witness: conditional on \(X=x\), assign complier, always-taker, and never-taker probabilities \(p_n(x)\), \((1-p_n(x))/2\), and \((1-p_n(x))/2\). Set \(Y(0)=0\). For compliers let \(Y(1)\) be Bernoulli with success probability \(1/2+h\); for noncompliers use success probability \(1/2\) for \(Y(1)\). Use the same conditional full-data law in both populations and draw source encouragement with propensity \(e_n(X)\). This construction has no defiers, satisfies exclusion and conditional randomization, and, because the conditional full-data laws agree between populations, satisfies both transport restrictions. Its target complier share is
\[
 \mathbb E_T[p_n(X)]
 =\mathbb E_S[w_n(X)p_n(X)]
 =\frac{\mu_n}{\kappa_n}\mathbb E_S[w_n(X)^2]
 =\mu_n>0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}=t_0,
 \qquad
 \theta_T=\frac12+h.
\]
All laws in the continuum have exactly the same source covariate law, transport weight, target covariate law, propensity, Kish dispersion, and effective strength.

Take
\[
 \rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{8},
 \qquad
 H(t_0)=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{t_0}}\right\},
\]
and restrict \(|h|\le H(t_0)\). Conditional on \(X=x\), the parameter \(h\) changes the observed source law only on \(\{Z=1,D=1\}\). Under the central law \(h=0\), this event has probability \(e_n(x)(1+p_n(x))/2\); within it, the complier fraction is \(2p_n(x)/(1+p_n(x))\), and hence \(Y\) is Bernoulli with success probability
\[
 \frac12+\frac{2p_n(x)h}{1+p_n(x)}.
\]
The chi-square divergence of Bernoulli \((1/2+u)\) from Bernoulli \((1/2)\) is \(4u^2\). Therefore the one-observation conditional contribution is
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \frac{e_n(x)(1+p_n(x))}{2}
 4\left\{\frac{2p_n(x)h}{1+p_n(x)}\right\}^2
 =\frac{8e_n(x)p_n(x)^2h^2}{1+p_n(x)}
 \le8p_n(x)^2h^2.\)}}
\]
Averaging over \(P_S^X\), using \(\mathbb E_S[p_n(X)^2]=\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\), and taking the product of the \(n\) independent source laws gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle 1+\chi^2(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\Vert Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g})
 \le\left(1+\frac{8\mu_n^2h^2}{\kappa_n}\right)^n
 \le\exp(8t_0h^2)
 \le\exp(8\rho^2).\)}}
\]
The target sample and oracle inputs have the same distribution for every \(h\). For a likelihood ratio \(L\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\|Q-Q_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\tfrac12\{\mathbb E_{Q_0}(L-1)^2\}^{1/2}\). Since \(8\rho^2\le1\) and \(e^u-1\le2u\) for \(0\le u\le1\),
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sup_{|h|\le H(t_0)}
 \|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le2\rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{4}.\)}}
\]

Let \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\). Conditional honesty supplies a deterministic \(\gamma_n\downarrow0\) such that coverage throughout the slice is at least \(1-\alpha-\gamma_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Put \(I=[1/2-H(t_0),1/2+H(t_0)]\). Tonelli's theorem and the total-variation inequality imply
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}}[\lambda(C_n)]
 &=\int_\Theta Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}(u\in C_n)\,du\\
 &\ge\int_I\left\{
 Q_{n,u-1/2}^{\mathfrak g}(u\in C_n)
 -\|Q_{n,u-1/2}^{\mathfrak g}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \right\}\,du\\
 &\ge2H(t_0)\left\{\frac{3(1-\alpha)}4-\gamma_n\right\}.
 \end{aligned}\)}}
\]
Because \(\rho\le1/4\),
\[
 H(t_0)\ge\rho\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Taking limits and then the infimum over conditionally honest sequences gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
 \ge\frac{3(1-\alpha)\rho}{2}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
 =\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.\)}}
\]

For the upper bound, use the source-score inversion rule
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle C_n^{\mathrm{sc}}=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw_n(X_i)
 \left\{\frac{Z_i}{e_n(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e_n(X_i)}\right\}
 (Y_i-\vartheta D_i)\right|
 \le L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\widehat\kappa_n}{n}}
 \right\},\)}}
\]
where \(\widehat\kappa_n=n^{-1}\sum_iw_n(X_i)^2\). At \(\vartheta=\theta_T\), the score has mean zero by the compact-range proposition and variance at most \(4\kappa_n/\varepsilon^2\). The envelope gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\kappa_n)
 \le\frac{4k_n^2\kappa_n}{n},
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}
 P(\widehat\kappa_n<\kappa_n/2)
 \le\frac{16k_n^2}{n\kappa_n}=o(1).\)}}
\]
Chebyshev's inequality therefore bounds noncoverage by
\[
 \frac{8}{L_\alpha^2\varepsilon^2}+o(1)=\alpha+o(1),
\]
uniformly on the slice, so the rule is conditionally honest.

Writing its radius as \(r_n=L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\), affine inversion gives length at most \(2r_n/|\widehat B_n|\), truncated at two, where
\[
 \widehat B_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw_n(X_i)
 \left\{\frac{Z_i}{e_n(X_i)}-
 \frac{1-Z_i}{1-e_n(X_i)}\right\}D_i.
\]
This denominator has mean \(\mu_n\) and variance at most \(\kappa_n/(n\varepsilon^2)\). Hence
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle P(|\widehat B_n-\mu_n|>\mu_n/2)
 \le\frac4{\varepsilon^2t_n},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[r_n]\le L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{n}},\)}}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{sc}})]
 \le\frac{4L_\alpha}{\sqrt{t_n}}
 +\frac8{\varepsilon^2t_n}.
\]
For \(t_0\ge1\), take the supremum over \(t_n\ge t_0\); for \(t_0<1\), use \(\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{sc}})\le2\). This gives
\[
 V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
 \le\max\{2,4L_\alpha+8\varepsilon^{-2}\}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
All constants are independent of \(\mathfrak g\), proving both the two-sided bound and the asserted fixed-geometry invariance.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the theorem-level boundary: it turns the transported CACE ratio into a conditional minimax expected-length experiment, holding the entire source-covariate, transport-weight, and propensity geometry fixed while allowing the first stage to vanish and the Kish dispersion to grow. Its novelty claim is only this joint scope, not a claim to subsume regular transport, linear two-sample weak-IV, or discrete-ATE minimax theory. \textit{Gap.} Chen and Huang (2025, \cite{ChenHuang2025}) identify the transported CACE Wald ratio and give regular weighted and multiply robust asymptotics, but do not give a fixed-geometry honest expected-length minimax frontier under \(\mu_n\to0\) and \(\kappa_n\to\infty\). Zeng, Kennedy, Bodnar, and Naimi (2024, \cite{ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2024}) give regular transport/generalization efficiency and minimax results for target causal effects, rather than a noncompliance ratio with a vanishing first stage or confidence-set length conditional on a fixed geometry. Choi and Shen (2019, \cite{ChoiShen2019}) give weak-instrument-robust confidence sets for a linear two-sample IV model, but not a covariate-only target transport experiment, density-ratio learning, or this conditional minimax class. The accepted-bank result stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap instead treats MSE for a discrete unconfounded ATE with a dimension--overlap frontier; it has neither an IV ratio nor transport/Kish geometry nor an honest expected-length criterion. Mikusheva (2010, \cite{Mikusheva2010}) studies near-shortest expected arc length in an invariant linear-IV experiment, not a bounded transported CACE conditional on an arbitrary deterministic weight geometry. The new boundary is therefore limited to uniform, geometry-by-geometry order equivalence indexed only by \(n\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\). \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the conditional lower bound used by the global oracle converse and certifies that the oracle score bound is not hiding a favorable transport geometry.

\begin{theorem}[thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment]\label{thm:regular-cell-unknown-weight-attainment}
On \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), let \(q_{x,n}=P_S^X(X=x)\), let
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle H_i=\frac{Z_i}{e(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e(X_i)},
 \qquad \widehat p_{x,n}=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{j=1}^{N_n}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=x\},\)}}
\]
and, for \(G\in\{Y,D\}\), define
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \widehat m_{G,n}(x)=\frac1{nq_{x,n}}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i,
 \qquad
 \widehat M_{G,n}=\sum_{x=1}^{k_n}\widehat p_{x,n}\widehat m_{G,n}(x).\)}}
\]
For \(N_n\ge2\), set
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \widehat\kappa_{U,n}=
 \frac1{N_n(N_n-1)}\sum_{j\ne\ell}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}}{q_{X_j^T,n}},
 \qquad \widehat K_n=1+\widehat\kappa_{U,n}.\)}}
\]
Put \(B_{\varepsilon,c}=8(\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1})\).  For every fixed \(L\ge\{2B_{\varepsilon,c}/\alpha\}^{1/2}\), the sequence
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
 |\widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}|
 \le L\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\right\},\)}}
\]
with \(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}}=\Theta\) when \(N_n<2\), belongs to \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\), does not use \(w\), and satisfies, for every fixed \(t_0>0\),
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})]
 \le \max\{2,4\sqrt2L+8B_{\varepsilon,c}\}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.\)}}
\]
The infimum of this risk over \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\) is at least \(3(1-\alpha)^2\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}/16\).  Thus unknown target cell probabilities cause no minimax-order loss for known regular source-cell probabilities and a known cell-varying propensity bounded by \(\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), write \(p_x=P_T(X=x)\), and observe that
\[
 w(x)=\frac{p_x}{q_{x,n}},
 \qquad
 \kappa_n=\sum_{x=1}^{k_n}\frac{p_x^2}{q_{x,n}}.
\]
For \(\vartheta\in\Theta\), set
\[
 g_{i,\vartheta}=H_i(Y_i-\vartheta D_i),
 \qquad
 a_\vartheta(x)=\mathbb E_S[g_{i,\vartheta}\mid X_i=x].
\]
Conditional randomization gives
\[
 a_\vartheta(x)=\Delta_Y(x)-\vartheta\Delta_D(x),
 \qquad
 \sum_xp_xa_{\theta_T}(x)=\mu_{Y,n}-\theta_T\mu_n=0.
\]
Moreover, \(|g_{i,\vartheta}|\le2/\varepsilon\) and \(|a_\vartheta(x)|\le2\). The estimated transported moment is the cross-average
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \frac{\widehat p_{X_i,n}}{q_{X_i,n}}g_{i,\vartheta}.\)}}
\]
Conditional on the target sample, its source-sample mean is \(\sum_x\widehat p_{x,n}a_\vartheta(x)\), and its conditional variance is at most
\[
 \frac4{\varepsilon^2n}
 \sum_{x=1}^{k_n}\frac{\widehat p_{x,n}^2}{q_{x,n}}.
\]
At \(\vartheta=\theta_T\), independence of the samples and the zero target mean yield
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P\left(\sum_x\widehat p_{x,n}a_{\theta_T}(x)\right)
 \le\frac4{N_n}.
\]
The multinomial second-moment identity gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\left[\sum_x\frac{\widehat p_{x,n}^2}{q_{x,n}}\right]
 &=\kappa_n+\frac1{N_n}\sum_x\frac{p_x-p_x^2}{q_{x,n}}\\
 &\le\kappa_n+\frac{k_n}{c_-N_n}.
 \end{aligned}\)}}
\]
Since \(\kappa_n\ge1\), \(N_n/n\to c\), and \(k_n/N_n\to0\), the law of total variance gives, uniformly over the class for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat M_{Y,n}-\theta_T\widehat M_{D,n})
 \le B_{\varepsilon,c}\frac{\kappa_n}{n},
 \qquad
 B_{\varepsilon,c}=8(\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1}).\)}}
\]
The same bound applies to \(\widehat M_{D,n}\), whose expectation is \(\mu_n\).

The collision component is unbiased:
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_{U,n}]
 =\sum_x\frac{p_x^2}{q_{x,n}}=\kappa_n.
\]
Its symmetric kernel is bounded by \(k_n/c_-\). In the variance expansion, disjoint index pairs are independent; bounding the order \(N_n^2\) individual variance terms and order \(N_n^3\) overlapping-pair covariance terms by the squared kernel envelope gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\kappa_{U,n})
 \le\frac{32k_n^2}{c_-^2N_n}.
\]
If \(\kappa_n\le2\), then \(\widehat K_n\ge1\ge\kappa_n/2\) deterministically. If \(\kappa_n>2\), Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 P(\widehat K_n<\kappa_n/2)
 \le\frac{128k_n^2}{c_-^2N_n\kappa_n^2}
 \le\frac{128k_n^2}{c_-^2N_n}=o(1),
\]
uniformly, because \(k_n=o(n^{1/2})\) and \(N_n/n\to c\).

On \(\{\widehat K_n\ge\kappa_n/2\}\), exclusion of \(\theta_T\) implies that its centered moment exceeds \(L\sqrt{\kappa_n/(2n)}\). Thus
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}}
 P(\theta_T\notin C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})
 \le o(1)+\frac{2B_{\varepsilon,c}}{L^2}
 \le\alpha+o(1).\)}}
\]
This proves \((C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}\).

For length, let \(r_n=L\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\). Affine inversion gives length at most \(4r_n/\mu_n\) on \(\{|\widehat M_{D,n}-\mu_n|\le\mu_n/2\}\), and at most two otherwise. Since
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle P(|\widehat M_{D,n}-\mu_n|>\mu_n/2)
 \le\frac{4B_{\varepsilon,c}}{t_n},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P[\widehat K_n]=1+\kappa_n\le2\kappa_n,\)}}
\]
Jensen's inequality yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})]
 \le\frac{4\sqrt2L}{\sqrt{t_n}}
 +\frac{8B_{\varepsilon,c}}{t_n}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(t_n\ge t_0\) for \(t_0\ge1\), and using \(\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{reg}})\le2\) for \(t_0<1\), proves the stated upper bound.

For the converse, take the regular geometry \(q_{x,n}=1/k_n\), \(w=1\), and \(e=1/2\). It lies in \(\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) because \(c_-\le1\le c_+\). Every regular-cell-honest sequence is conditionally honest on this fixed geometry, while its regular-class risk dominates its slice risk. The fixed-geometry frontier theorem therefore gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{reg}}}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n^{\mathrm{reg}}:\,t_n\ge t_0}
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
 \ge\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.\)}}
\]
This completes the matched-order result.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

The order-one elbow may be normalized at \(t_c=1\), but this is not a sharp critical constant.  Below an order-one effective-strength level the theorem proves a nonvanishing lower bound on worst-case expected length; it does not claim that every honest set converges to the full diameter two.  The same score-inversion sequence obeys the bound at each fixed \(t_0>0\); this is not a joint moderate-deviation assertion for moving thresholds \(t_{0,n}\).

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The order result deliberately uses crude Chebyshev and collision-U-statistic constants and does not claim practical or sharp calibration.  The fixed-geometry converse is complete.  The remaining technical limitation is feasibility outside known regular finite-cell source designs: unknown propensities require control of random cell denominators and their scale estimate, while continuous-covariate sieves require approximation assumptions, weak-denominator bias control, and a partition-complexity rule.

\section{Honest scope}

Proved results are the transported identifying ratio, its forced compact range, the matched global and fixed-geometry oracle frontiers, one geometry-uniform oracle score-inversion sequence, and matched feasible target-cell weight learning on the uniform class \(\mathcal N_n\) and a known regular nonuniform-cell extension with known cell-varying propensity.  The oracle result is not presented as a feasible general-model procedure.  No claim is made for unknown assignment propensities, continuous-covariate weight learning, sieve approximation, moving strength thresholds, disconnected-set taxonomy, or sharp constants.
