For \(\vartheta\in\Theta\), write
\[
 \psi_{i,\vartheta}=w(X_i)H_i(Y_i-\vartheta D_i).
\]
Conditional randomization and consistency give
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\psi_{i,\vartheta}]
 =\mu_{Y,n}-\vartheta\mu_n.
\]
In particular, the mean is zero at \(\vartheta=\theta_T\).  Since \(|H_i|\le\varepsilon^{-1}\), \(|Y_i-\vartheta D_i|\le2\), and \(\mathbb E_S[w^2]=\kappa_n\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\psi_{i,\theta_T})
 \le\frac{4\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2}.
\]

It remains to justify replacing \(\kappa_n\) by its empirical version uniformly over the triangular array.  The envelope gives \(w^4\le4k_n^2w^2\), hence
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\kappa_n)
 \le\frac{\mathbb E_P[w^4]}n
 \le\frac{4k_n^2\kappa_n}{n}.
\]
Because \(\kappa_n\ge1\) and \(k_n=o(n^{1/2})\), Markov's inequality applied to \((\widehat\kappa_n-\kappa_n)^2\) yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
 P(\widehat\kappa_n<\kappa_n/2)
 \le\frac{16k_n^2}{n\kappa_n}=o(1).
\]
On the complementary event, failure to include \(\theta_T\) implies
\[
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{i,\theta_T}\right|
 >L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{2n}}.
\]
Another application of Markov's inequality to the squared centered average gives, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 P\left(
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{i,\theta_T}\right|
 >L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{2n}}
 \right)
 \le\frac{8}{L_\alpha^2\varepsilon^2}=\alpha.
\]
Thus
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
 P(\theta_T\in C_n)\ge1-\alpha,
\]
so the displayed construction is one member of \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}\).

For the length bound, put \(r_n=L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\).  The acceptance region is the intersection of \(\Theta\) with the inverse image of \([-r_n,r_n]\) under the affine map \(\vartheta\mapsto\widehat A_n-\vartheta\widehat B_n\).  Consequently,
\[
 \lambda(C_n)\le
 \begin{cases}
 \min\{2,2r_n/|\widehat B_n|\},&\widehat B_n\ne0,\\
 2,&\widehat B_n=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The denominator summand \(w(X_i)H_iD_i\) has mean \(\mu_n\) and variance at most \(\kappa_n/\varepsilon^2\).  Therefore
\[
 P\left(|\widehat B_n-\mu_n|>\mu_n/2\right)
 \le\frac{4\kappa_n}{\varepsilon^2n\mu_n^2}
 =\frac{4}{\varepsilon^2t_n}.
\]
On the complementary event, \(|\widehat B_n|\ge\mu_n/2\), while Jensen's inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[r_n]
 \le L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_n]}n}
 =L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{n}}.
\]
It follows that, for every member law,
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
 \le\frac{4L_\alpha}{\sqrt{t_n}}+\frac{8}{\varepsilon^2t_n}.
\]
If \(t_0\ge1\), taking the supremum over \(t_n\ge t_0\) bounds this expression by \((4L_\alpha+8\varepsilon^{-2})t_0^{-1/2}\).  If \(t_0<1\), the deterministic bound \(\lambda(C_n)\le2\) applies.  Taking the required limsup proves the asserted simultaneous risk bound.  Combining it with the oracle converse proves the stated minimax order.  No step used the shape of \(w\) beyond its second moment and the envelope needed for uniform estimation of that moment, which proves the claimed achievability-side geometry invariance.
