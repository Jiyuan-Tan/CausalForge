Specialize the regular-cell theorem to
\[
 q_{x,n}=\frac1{k_n},
 \qquad e(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 L=L_{\alpha,c}=\left\{\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right\}^{1/2},
 \qquad B_c=32(1+c^{-1}).
\]
Because \(c>0\), one has \((4+3/c)^{-1/2}<1/2\).  Hence, for example,
\[
 \bar\varepsilon
 =\frac12\left\{\frac12+(4+3/c)^{-1/2}\right\}
 \in(0,1/2)
\]
and \(\bar\varepsilon^{-2}\le 4+3/c\).  Since the actual propensity on \(\mathcal N_n\) is identically \(1/2\), it obeys
\[
 \bar\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\bar\varepsilon
\]
for every cell.  Thus the regular-cell theorem is applicable with the admissible interior overlap constant \(\bar\varepsilon\).

Under this specialization its score is \(H_i=2(2Z_i-1)\), and its source-cell estimator becomes
\[
 \frac1{nq_{x,n}}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i
 =\frac{k_n}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i.
\]
Its collision estimator likewise becomes
\[
 \frac1{N_n(N_n-1)}\sum_{j\ne\ell}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}}{q_{X_j^T,n}}
 =\frac{k_n}{N_n(N_n-1)}\sum_{j\ne\ell}
 \mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}.
\]
The regular-cell variance constant at this admissible calibration satisfies
\[
 B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}
 =8(\bar\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1})
 \le8(4+3c^{-1}+c^{-1})
 =32(1+c^{-1})
 =B_c.
\]
Consequently
\[
 L_{\alpha,c}
 =\{2B_c/\alpha\}^{1/2}
 \ge\{2B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}/\alpha\}^{1/2},
\]
so the exact confidence set displayed in the statement is honest.  Because the specialized construction uses only the two observed samples, it lies in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) and does not use \(w\).  The regular-cell length bound gives, for every \(t_0>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]\\
 &\qquad\le
 \max\{2,4\sqrt2L_{\alpha,c}+8B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\\
 &\qquad\le
 \max\{2,4\sqrt2L_{\alpha,c}+8B_c\}
 \min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\\
 &\qquad=C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the interior calibration closes with exactly the constant \(C_0\) in the theorem statement.

For the converse, use the fixed geometry \(q_{x,n}=1/k_n\), \(w=1\), and \(e=1/2\).  It is a submodel of \(\mathcal N_n\), and the target-sample law and all known design inputs are fixed throughout the continuum of outcome alternatives in the fixed-geometry construction.  Every sequence in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) is therefore conditionally honest on this slice, while its finite-cell risk dominates its slice risk.  The finite-cell change-of-measure specialization used in the oracle converse yields
\[
 \inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
 \ge c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
with \(c_0=3(1-\alpha)^2/16\).  The upper and lower orders coincide, proving that estimating the target cell probabilities causes no additional minimax-order loss.  In this proof, and in the other supplied proofs, occurrences of \(e=1/2\) specify an actual propensity; no theorem is instantiated with an overlap constant at the excluded endpoint \(1/2\).
