Specialize the regular-cell theorem to
\[
q_{x,n}=\frac{1}{k_n},
\qquad e(x)=\frac12,
\qquad L=L_{\alpha,c}=\left\{\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right\}^{1/2},
\qquad B_c=32(1+c^{-1}).
\]
Because \(c>0\), one has \(4+3/c>4\).  Since \(u^{-2}\downarrow4\) as \(u\uparrow1/2\), choose an interior constant \(\bar\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
\bar\varepsilon^{-2}\le 4+\frac3c.
\]
On \(\mathcal N_n\), the propensity is identically \(e(x)=1/2\), and therefore
\[
\bar\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\bar\varepsilon.
\]
Thus the regular-cell theorem is applicable with the admissible positivity constant \(\bar\varepsilon\).  Its variance constant then obeys
\[
B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}
=8\bigl(\bar\varepsilon^{-2}+c^{-1}\bigr)
\le8\left(4+\frac3c+\frac1c\right)
=32(1+c^{-1})
=B_c.
\]
Consequently,
\[
L_{\alpha,c}=\left\{\frac{2B_c}{\alpha}\right\}^{1/2}
\ge\left\{\frac{2B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}}{\alpha}\right\}^{1/2},
\]
which is precisely the calibration condition in the regular-cell theorem.

Under \(q_{x,n}=1/k_n\), the regular-cell score is
\[
H_i=\frac{Z_i}{1/2}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1/2}=2(2Z_i-1),
\]
and its source-cell estimator becomes
\[
\frac1{nq_{x,n}}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i
=\frac{k_n}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}H_iG_i.
\]
Its collision estimator likewise becomes
\[
\frac1{N_n(N_n-1)}\sum_{j\ne\ell}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}}{q_{X_j^T,n}}
=\frac{k_n}{N_n(N_n-1)}\sum_{j\ne\ell}
\mathbf 1\{X_j^T=X_\ell^T\}.
\]
Hence the specialized regular-cell confidence set is exactly the set \(C_n\) displayed in the theorem.  It uses only the source observations and the target covariate sample, so it does not use \(w\) and belongs to \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\).  The regular-cell length bound gives, for every fixed \(t_0>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]\\
&\qquad\le
\max\{2,4\sqrt2L_{\alpha,c}+8B_{\bar\varepsilon,c}\}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\\
&\qquad\le
\max\{2,4\sqrt2L_{\alpha,c}+8B_c\}
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}\\
&\qquad=C_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the asserted honest upper bound with the unchanged displayed constant \(C_0\).

For the converse, use the fixed geometry \(q_{x,n}=1/k_n\), \(w=1\), and \(e=1/2\).  It is a submodel of \(\mathcal N_n\), and the target-sample law and all known design inputs are fixed throughout the continuum of outcome alternatives in the fixed-geometry construction.  Every sequence in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) is therefore conditionally honest on this slice, while its finite-cell risk dominates its slice risk.  The finite-cell specialization of the change-of-measure argument used in the oracle converse yields
\[
\inf_{(C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
\mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
\ge c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\},
\]
where \(c_0=3(1-\alpha)^2/16\).  The upper and lower orders coincide, proving that estimating the target cell probabilities creates no additional minimax-order loss.  Notice that \(1/2\) is used only as the admissible propensity value \(e(x)\), whereas the overlap constant used to invoke the regular-cell theorem is the strict interior value \(\bar\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\); no excluded endpoint is invoked.
