Assume that \(w(X)=1\) \(P_S\)-almost surely. By the definition of Kish dispersion and invariance of expectation under almost-sure equality,
\[
\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]=\mathbb E_S[1]=1.
\]
Substituting this identity into the definition of effective identification strength gives
\[
t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}=n\mu_n^2.
\]
Moreover, because \(w=dP_T/dP_S^X\), the equality \(w=1\) \(P_S^X\)-almost surely implies \(P_T=P_S^X\): for every measurable set \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
P_T(A)=\int_A w(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=P_S^X(A).
\]
Thus there is no covariate-distribution shift, and the oracle observes only the known constant weight. In the risk entering \(V^\star(t_0)\), the restriction \(t_n\ge t_0\) therefore becomes exactly \(n\mu_n^2\ge t_0\). Since the parameter space remains the forced compact interval \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), the resulting specialization is precisely the compact single-population weak-ratio frontier indexed by \(n\mu_n^2\).
