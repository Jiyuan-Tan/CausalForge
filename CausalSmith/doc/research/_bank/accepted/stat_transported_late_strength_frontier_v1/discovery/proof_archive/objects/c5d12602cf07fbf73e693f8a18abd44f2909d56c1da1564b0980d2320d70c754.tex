Assume that \(w(X)=1\) \(P_S\)-almost surely. Since \(w\) is a function of \(X\), the same equality holds \(P_S^X\)-almost surely. By the definitions of \(\kappa_n\) and \(t_n\),
\[
\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w(X)^2]=\mathbb E_S[1]=1
\quad\text{and}\quad
 t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}=n\mu_n^2.
\]
Moreover, because \(w=dP_T/dP_S^X\), for every measurable set \(A\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
P_T(A)=\int_A w(x)\,dP_S^X(x)=P_S^X(A).
\]
Thus this geometry has no covariate-distribution shift. Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}compact\text{-}causal\text{-}range}\) gives the forced compact parameter space \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). For every fixed \(t_0>0\), the restriction \(t_n\ge t_0\) is now exactly \(n\mu_n^2\ge t_0\). Applying the fixed-geometry lower bound in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}oracle\text{-}converse}\) to the admissible geometry \(w\equiv1\), and the geometry-uniform upper bound in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}oracle\text{-}score\text{-}inversion\text{-}attainment}\), shows that the oracle expected-length frontier restricted to this no-shift geometry is bounded above and below by constant multiples of
\[
\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Hence the frontier specializes to compact single-population weak-ratio inference, with strength index \(n\mu_n^2\), as claimed.
