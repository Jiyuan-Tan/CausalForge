Fix \(\mathfrak g\in\mathcal G\) and \(t_0>0\).  We prove the lower and upper bounds separately.

For the lower bound, abbreviate the fixed geometry by \((P_S^X,w_n,e_n)\), let \(\kappa_n=\mathbb E_S[w_n(X)^2]\), and set
\[
 \mu_n=\sqrt{\frac{t_0\kappa_n}{n}},
 \qquad p_n(x)=\frac{\mu_nw_n(x)}{\kappa_n}.
\]
The envelope and \(\kappa_n\ge1\) imply
\[
 0\le\sup_xp_n(x)\le
 \frac{2\sqrt{t_0}\,k_n}{\sqrt{n\kappa_n}}
 \le\frac{2\sqrt{t_0}\,k_n}{\sqrt n}=o(1).
\]
Thus \(p_n(x)\) is a valid compliance probability for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Also
\[
 \mu_n^2\le\frac{t_0k_n}{n}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so the degrading-array condition is respected.

Conditional on \(X=x\), give compliers probability \(p_n(x)\), and split the remaining probability equally between always-takers and never-takers.  Use the same conditional law of the potential variables in the source and target populations.  Set \(Y(0)=0\).  For compliers let \(Y(1)\) be Bernoulli with success probability \(1/2+h\); for always-takers let \(Y(1)\) be Bernoulli with success probability \(1/2\); the unused potential outcome of never-takers may be given the same Bernoulli law.  Draw \(Z\) in the source with propensity \(e_n(X)\).  Let \(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\) denote the joint law of both observed samples and the fixed oracle inputs.  The construction has no defiers, satisfies exclusion and conditional randomization, and uses identical conditional full-data laws in the two populations, so both transport restrictions hold.  Its target complier share is
\[
 \mathbb E_T[p_n(X)]
 =\mathbb E_S[w_n(X)p_n(X)]
 =\frac{\mu_n}{\kappa_n}\mathbb E_S[w_n(X)^2]
 =\mu_n>0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 t_n=\frac{n\mu_n^2}{\kappa_n}=t_0,
 \qquad \theta_T=\frac12+h.
\]
All members of the continuum have the same geometry and the same effective strength.

Take
\[
 \rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{8},
 \qquad H(t_0)=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{t_0}}\right\},
\]
and restrict \(|h|\le H(t_0)\).  We now calculate the experiment distance.  Conditional on \(X=x\), the parameter \(h\) changes the observed source law only when \(Z=1\) and \(D=1\).  Under \(Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\), that event has conditional probability \(e_n(x)(1+p_n(x))/2\).  Within the event, the complier fraction is \(2p_n(x)/(1+p_n(x))\), so \(Y\) is Bernoulli with success probability
\[
 \frac12+\frac{2p_n(x)h}{1+p_n(x)}.
\]
The chi-square divergence of Bernoulli \((1/2+u)\) from Bernoulli \((1/2)\) is \(4u^2\).  Hence the one-observation conditional contribution is
\[
 \frac{e_n(x)(1+p_n(x))}{2}\,
 4\left\{\frac{2p_n(x)h}{1+p_n(x)}\right\}^2
 =\frac{8e_n(x)p_n(x)^2h^2}{1+p_n(x)}
 \le8p_n(x)^2h^2.
\]
Averaging over \(P_S^X\) and using \(\mathbb E_S[p_n(X)^2]=\mu_n^2/\kappa_n\) gives
\[
 \chi^2(Q_{n,h}^{S,\mathfrak g}\Vert Q_{n,0}^{S,\mathfrak g})
 \le\frac{8\mu_n^2h^2}{\kappa_n}.
\]
The target sample and oracle inputs have the same distribution for every \(h\).  Independence of the \(n\) source observations therefore yields
\[
 1+\chi^2(Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}\Vert Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g})
 \le\left(1+\frac{8\mu_n^2h^2}{\kappa_n}\right)^n
 \le\exp(8t_0h^2)
 \le\exp(8\rho^2).
\]
For a likelihood ratio \(L\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\|Q-Q_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\tfrac12\{\mathbb E_{Q_0}(L-1)^2\}^{1/2}\).  Since \(8\rho^2\le1\) and \(e^u-1\le2u\) for \(0\le u\le1\),
\[
 \sup_{|h|\le H(t_0)}
 \|Q_{n,h}^{\mathfrak g}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le2\rho=\frac{1-\alpha}{4}.
\]

Let \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathfrak g)\).  Conditional honesty provides a deterministic \(\gamma_n\downarrow0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), coverage throughout the slice is at least \(1-\alpha-\gamma_n\).  For \(u\in I=[1/2-H(t_0),1/2+H(t_0)]\), use the member with \(h=u-1/2\).  Tonelli's theorem and the total-variation inequality imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}}[\lambda(C_n)]
 &=\int_\Theta Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}(u\in C_n)\,du\\
 &\ge\int_I\left\{Q_{n,u-1/2}^{\mathfrak g}(u\in C_n)
 -\|Q_{n,u-1/2}^{\mathfrak g}-Q_{n,0}^{\mathfrak g}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\right\}\,du\\
 &\ge2H(t_0)\left\{\frac{3(1-\alpha)}4-\gamma_n\right\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because \(\rho\le1/4\),
\[
 H(t_0)\ge\rho\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Taking the limit inferior, then the supremum over the slice and the infimum over conditionally honest sequences, gives
\[
 V_{\mathfrak g}^\star(t_0)
 \ge\frac{3(1-\alpha)\rho}{2}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}
 =\frac{3(1-\alpha)^2}{16}\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]

For the upper bound, use the single source-score inversion rule
\[
 C_n^{\mathrm{sc}}=\left\{\vartheta\in\Theta:
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw_n(X_i)
 \left\{\frac{Z_i}{e_n(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e_n(X_i)}\right\}
 (Y_i-\vartheta D_i)\right|
 \le L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\widehat\kappa_n}{n}}\right\},
\]
where \(\widehat\kappa_n=n^{-1}\sum_iw_n(X_i)^2\).  At \(\vartheta=\theta_T\), the score has mean zero by prop:compact-causal-range and variance at most \(4\kappa_n/\varepsilon^2\).  The envelope gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\kappa_n)
 \le\frac{4k_n^2\kappa_n}{n},
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\mathfrak g)}
 P(\widehat\kappa_n<\kappa_n/2)
 \le\frac{16k_n^2}{n\kappa_n}=o(1).
\]
Chebyshev's inequality then bounds noncoverage by \(8/(L_\alpha^2\varepsilon^2)+o(1)=\alpha+o(1)\), uniformly on the slice.  Hence this sequence is conditionally honest.

Writing its radius as \(r_n=L_\alpha\sqrt{\widehat\kappa_n/n}\), affine inversion gives length at most \(2r_n/|\widehat B_n|\), truncated at two, where
\[
 \widehat B_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw_n(X_i)
 \left\{\frac{Z_i}{e_n(X_i)}-\frac{1-Z_i}{1-e_n(X_i)}\right\}D_i.
\]
This denominator has mean \(\mu_n\) and variance at most \(\kappa_n/(n\varepsilon^2)\).  Thus
\[
 P(|\widehat B_n-\mu_n|>\mu_n/2)
 \le\frac{4}{\varepsilon^2t_n},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[r_n]\le L_\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_n}{n}},
\]
and consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{sc}})]
 \le\frac{4L_\alpha}{\sqrt{t_n}}+
 \frac{8}{\varepsilon^2t_n}.
\]
For \(t_0\ge1\), take the supremum over \(t_n\ge t_0\); for \(t_0<1\), use \(\lambda(C_n^{\mathrm{sc}})\le2\).  This proves the claimed upper bound.  Every constant is independent of \(\mathfrak g\), completing the proof.
