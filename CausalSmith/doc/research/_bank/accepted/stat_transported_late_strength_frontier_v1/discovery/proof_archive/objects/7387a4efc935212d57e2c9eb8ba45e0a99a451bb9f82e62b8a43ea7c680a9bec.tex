Fix a member law in \(\mathcal N_n\), and abbreviate its target cell probabilities by \(p_x=P_T(X=x)\).  For \(\vartheta\in\Theta\), let
\[
 g_{i,\vartheta}=H_i(Y_i-\vartheta D_i),\qquad
 a_\vartheta(x)=\mathbb E_S[g_{i,\vartheta}\mid X_i=x].
\]
Because the source propensity is \(1/2\), conditional randomization gives
\[
 a_\vartheta(x)=\Delta_Y(x)-\vartheta\Delta_D(x),\qquad
 \sum_xp_xa_{\theta_T}(x)=\mu_{Y,n}-\theta_T\mu_n=0.
\]
Also \(|g_{i,\vartheta}|\le4\).  The proposed moment statistic can be written as the two-sample cross-average
\[
 \widehat M_{Y,n}-\vartheta\widehat M_{D,n}
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nk_n\widehat p_{X_i,n}g_{i,\vartheta}.
\]
Conditional on the target sample, its source-sample mean is
\[
 \overline a_{\vartheta,T}=\sum_x\widehat p_{x,n}a_\vartheta(x),
\]
and its conditional variance is bounded by
\[
 \frac{16}{n}k_n\sum_x\widehat p_{x,n}^2.
\]
At \(\vartheta=\theta_T\), independence of the two samples and the zero-mean identity above also give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\overline a_{\theta_T,T})\le\frac{16}{N_n}.
\]
Since
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[k_n\sum_x\widehat p_{x,n}^2\right]
 =\kappa_n+\frac{k_n-\kappa_n}{N_n}
 \le\kappa_n+\frac{k_n}{N_n},
\]
the relations \(N_n/n\to c\), \(k_n/N_n\to0\), and \(\kappa_n\ge1\) imply, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat M_{Y,n}-\theta_T\widehat M_{D,n})
 \le B_c\frac{\kappa_n}{n},\qquad B_c=32(1+c^{-1}).
\]
The same bound holds for \(\widehat M_{D,n}\), whose expectation is \(\mu_n\).

We next verify that \(\widehat K_n\) is a uniformly valid scale.  The collision statistic is unbiased:
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\widehat\kappa_{U,n}]
 =k_n\sum_xp_x^2=\kappa_n.
\]
To bound its variance, write the ordered-pair statistic as twice the sum over unordered pairs.  Indicators attached to disjoint pairs are independent.  There are fewer than \(N_n^2/2\) individual variance terms and fewer than \(N_n^3/2\) overlapping unordered pair-of-pair covariance terms, each bounded in absolute value by one.  Including the prefactor therefore gives the uniform elementary bound
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\kappa_{U,n})
 \le\frac{32k_n^2}{N_n}.
\]
If \(\kappa_n\le2\), then \(\widehat K_n\ge1\ge\kappa_n/2\) deterministically.  If \(\kappa_n>2\), Markov's inequality applied to the squared centered collision statistic yields
\[
 P(\widehat K_n<\kappa_n/2)
 \le\frac{128k_n^2}{N_n\kappa_n^2}
 \le\frac{128k_n^2}{N_n}=o(1),
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal N_n\).

On \(\{\widehat K_n\ge\kappa_n/2\}\), exclusion of \(\theta_T\) from \(C_n\) implies that the centered true-parameter moment exceeds \(L_{\alpha,c}\sqrt{\kappa_n/(2n)}\) in absolute value.  The variance bound consequently gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n}P(\theta_T\notin C_n)
 \le o(1)+\frac{2B_c}{L_{\alpha,c}^2}
 =\alpha+o(1).
\]
Thus \((C_n)\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\).

For expected length, set \(r_n=L_{\alpha,c}\sqrt{\widehat K_n/n}\).  The same affine-acceptance-set geometry as in the oracle proof gives length at most \(4r_n/\mu_n\) on
\(\{|\widehat M_{D,n}-\mu_n|\le\mu_n/2\}\), and length at most two otherwise.  Since
\[
 P(|\widehat M_{D,n}-\mu_n|>\mu_n/2)
 \le\frac{4B_c\kappa_n}{n\mu_n^2}
 =\frac{4B_c}{t_n}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\widehat K_n]=1+\kappa_n\le2\kappa_n,
\]
Jensen's inequality yields the pointwise bound
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
 \le\frac{4\sqrt2L_{\alpha,c}}{\sqrt{t_n}}+
 \frac{8B_c}{t_n}.
\]
For \(t_0\ge1\), take the supremum over \(t_n\ge t_0\); for \(t_0<1\), use \(\lambda(C_n)\le2\).  This proves the asserted simultaneous upper bound.

Finally, the continuum \(\{Q_{n,h}:|h|\le H(t_0)\}\) constructed in the proof of the oracle converse lies wholly in \(\mathcal N_n\), has a target covariate law that is common across \(h\), and has joint total-variation distance from \(Q_{n,0}\) at most \((1-\alpha)/4\).  For an arbitrary finite-cell-honest sequence, its defining honesty condition supplies the same uniform coverage inequality on this family.  Repeating the Tonelli change-of-measure calculation therefore gives
\[
 \limsup_n\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n:\,t_n\ge t_0}
 \mathbb E_P[\lambda(C_n)]
 \ge c_0\min\{1,t_0^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{cell}}\) proves the final matching assertion.
