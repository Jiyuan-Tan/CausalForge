\title{A Matched Uniform Minimax Lower Bound under CTY Assumptions 1--2\\for Distance-Based Boundary Discontinuity Estimation}
\author{}
\date{Draft note, August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu (2026, CTY) obtain the uniform rate $(\log n/n)^{1/4}$ for distance-based local polynomial estimation by combining their Assumptions 1--2 with the uniform approximation-bias class of Theorem 2, and conjecture that the logarithmic factor is unavoidable. Their stated minimax lower bound is instead proved on a different class in which the relevant curve is the support boundary. This note proves the matching logarithmic lower bound directly over the regular CTY class. For every fixed regression smoothness $q=p+1\ge2$, if $\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)$ denotes an $L$-uniformized version of the data-generating processes satisfying CTY Assumptions 1--2 and the envelope restrictions of Theorem 2, then
\[
\inf_{\widehat\tau\in\mathcal T_{\rm db}^{\pm}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}|\widehat\tau(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
\gtrsim \left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
Here $\mathcal T_{\rm db}^{\pm}$ is an enlarged distance-based class allowed to know the full assignment geometry and to observe signed, rather than merely unsigned, distances. The proof proceeds by exhibiting a hard subclass having a \emph{fixed support, fixed known assignment rule, and fixed known interior boundary}. It combines $q$-smooth regression bumps of width $\delta^{1/q}$ with angular perturbations of the score density that exactly mask each bump from its signed-distance experiment outside a radius $O(\delta)$. A coupling argument over polynomially many disjoint boundary sites produces the logarithmic amplification. The construction exploits a literal feature of CTY's class: $f_X$ is continuous and uniformly bounded, but no uniform derivative/H\"older bound is imposed on it.
\end{abstract}

\section{The CTY class and main result}

Fix $p\ge1$, $\nu\ge2$, and write $q=p+1$.  Let $\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)$ be the class of boundary-discontinuity data-generating processes satisfying CTY Assumptions 1 and 2, with all constants uniformized by a fixed $L\ge1$, together with the envelope restrictions used in CTY Theorem 2.  Concretely, in addition to Assumptions 1--2, we require the score support to lie in $[-L,L]^2$,
\[
L^{-1}\le \inf_{x\in\mathcal X}f_X(x)
\le \sup_{x\in\mathcal X}f_X(x)\le L,
\]
for each $t\in\{0,1\}$,
\[
\max_{0\le|\alpha|\le p}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X}|\partial^\alpha\mu_t(x)|
+
\max_{0\le|\alpha|\le p}\sup_{x\ne y}
\frac{|\partial^\alpha\mu_t(x)-\partial^\alpha\mu_t(y)|}{\|x-y\|}
\le L,
\]
and the kernel-mass lower bound in CTY Theorem 2(iii).  We also take the lower variance bound, the $(2+\nu)$th conditional-moment bound, the metric-equivalence constants in Assumption 2(ii), and the Gram-matrix lower bound in Assumption 2(iv) to be bounded uniformly by $L$.  This is only a uniformization of the constants appearing in CTY Assumptions 1--2 and Theorem 2; no derivative or H\"older envelope is imposed on $f_X$.

The assignment geometry $G=(\mathcal X,\mathcal A_0,\mathcal A_1,\mathcal B,d,K)$ is a known design object.  Let $\mathcal T_{\rm db}^{\pm}$ denote the class of all collections of estimators that may depend on $G$ and, at each $x\in\mathcal B$, may use the signed-distance sample
\[
U_n^{\pm}(x)=\bigl((Y_i,D_i(x)):1\le i\le n\bigr),
\qquad
D_i(x)=\{\1(X_i\in\mathcal A_1)-\1(X_i\in\mathcal A_0)\}d(X_i,x).
\]
Thus $\mathcal T_{\rm db}^{\pm}$ is at least as informative as the unsigned-distance class used in CTY's minimax formulation and contains their signed-distance local-polynomial estimator.

For $P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)$, write $\mathcal B_P$ for its known assignment boundary and
\[
\mathcal R_n(\widehat\tau,P)
=
\E_P\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}|\widehat\tau(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\right].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Uniform minimax lower bound under CTY Assumptions 1--2]
\label{thm:main}
For every fixed $p\ge1$ and $\nu\ge2$, there exist finite constants $L$ and $c=c(p,\nu,L)>0$ such that
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/4}
\inf_{\widehat\tau\in\mathcal T_{\rm db}^{\pm}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\mathcal R_n(\widehat\tau,P)
\ge c.
}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\inf_{\widehat\tau\in\mathcal T_{\rm db}^{\pm}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\E_P\!\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}|\widehat\tau(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\right]
\gtrsim
\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
\end{theorem}

This is formulated over the \emph{whole} regular A1/A2 class, rather than over the hard family used in the proof.  The proof is by subclass reduction: it suffices to construct, for each $n$, a finite family
\[
\mathcal P_n^\star\subset\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)
\]
on which the same lower bound holds.  We choose $\mathcal P_n^\star$ to have one fixed support, one fixed known assignment rule, and one fixed known interior boundary.  Smoothness $q=p+1$ determines how many hard sites can be packed along that boundary, but it does not alter the exponent $1/4$ because the score-density nuisance masks each regression bump outside an $O(\delta)$ core.

\section{A fixed-geometry hard subclass}

For the remainder of the proof set
\[
\cX=[-3,3]^2,
\qquad
\cA_1=[-1,1]\times[0,2],
\qquad
\cA_0=\cX\setminus\cA_1,
\qquad
\cB=\partial\cA_1,
\]
use Euclidean distance and the uniform kernel.  This geometry is fixed and known under every law in the hard family.  The boundary is a positive-length rectifiable curve contained in $\operatorname{int}(\cX)$, and treatment is deterministic, $A=\1\{X\in\cA_1\}$.

Choose constants $a>0$ small and $C>0$ large.  Let $\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]$ be a $C^\infty$ cutoff equal to one near zero and zero on $[1,\infty)$, flat at the endpoints of its transition region.  Let $\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]$ be $C^\infty$ with
\[
\eta(u)=0\quad(u\le1),
\qquad
\eta(u)=1\quad(u\ge2).
\]
For a signal size $\delta\downarrow0$, set
\[
w=A\delta^{1/q},
\]
where $A>0$ is a sufficiently large fixed constant.  Place points
\[
x_j=(s_j,0),\qquad j=1,\ldots,M,
\]
on $[-1/2,1/2]\times\{0\}$ with pairwise spacing at least $4w$.  Then
\[
M\asymp w^{-1}\asymp\delta^{-1/q},
\]
and the disks $S_j=B(x_j,w)$ are disjoint.

For $r>0$ define
\[
b_{\delta,w}(r)
=
\frac{2\delta}{a r}\,
\phi(r/w)\,
\eta\!\left(\frac{r}{C\delta}\right),
\qquad b_{\delta,w}(0)=0.
\]
For $x\in S_j$, write $x-x_j=r_j(\cos\alpha_j,\sin\alpha_j)$.  For
$\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_M)\in\{0,1\}^M$, define
\begin{align}
 f_\theta(x)
 &=\frac1{36}\left[
 1+\sum_{j=1}^M \theta_j b_{\delta,w}(r_j)\cos\alpha_j
 \right],                                                     \label{eq:density}\\
 \mu_{0,\theta}(x)&=\frac12,                                  \label{eq:mu0}\\
 \mu_{1,\theta}(x)
 &=\frac12+a x_1
 -\delta\sum_{j=1}^M\theta_j
 \phi\!\left(\frac{\|x-x_j\|}{w}\right).                   \label{eq:mu1}
\end{align}
The summands are interpreted as zero outside $S_j$.  Conditional on $X=x$, take $Y(t)$ to be Bernoulli with success probability $\mu_{t,\theta}(x)$; the two potential outcomes may be taken conditionally independent.

\begin{lemma}[Class membership]
\label{lem:class}
For suitable fixed $a,C,A$ and all sufficiently small $\delta$, every law in \eqref{eq:density}--\eqref{eq:mu1} belongs to one fixed class $\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)$, uniformly over $M$ and $\theta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $b_{\delta,w}(r)$ vanishes for $r\le C\delta$ and for $r\ge w$, each perturbation in \eqref{eq:density} is continuous (indeed smooth for each fixed $\delta$).  Whenever it is nonzero,
\[
|b_{\delta,w}(r)|\le \frac{2}{aC}.
\]
Choosing $C$ large gives fixed bounds, for example
\[
\frac{3}{4}\frac1{36}\le f_\theta(x)\le\frac{5}{4}\frac1{36}.
\]
Each density perturbation integrates to zero because
$\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\alpha\,d\alpha=0$, so $f_\theta$ integrates to one.

For every multi-index $\nu$ with $|\nu|\le q$, a regression bump satisfies
\[
\left\|\partial^\nu\left[
\delta\phi\!\left(\frac{\|\cdot-x_j\|}{w}\right)
\right]\right\|_\infty
\lesssim \delta w^{-|\nu|}
=A^{-|\nu|}\delta^{1-|\nu|/q}.
\]
The disks are disjoint, so the same bound holds for the sum.  At order $q$ it is $O(A^{-q})$; hence the derivatives through order $p=q-1$ and the Lipschitz constants of the $p$th derivatives are uniformly bounded.  Taking $a$ and $\delta$ small keeps all Bernoulli probabilities uniformly inside $(0,1)$, which gives the variance and moment conditions in Assumption 1.

Finally, the fixed rectangular boundary satisfies Assumption 2.  For all sufficiently small radii, each treatment side contains at least a quarter-disk at every $x\in\cB$.  Together with the uniform lower bound on $f_\theta$, this gives the uniform population-Gram lower bound in Assumption 2(iv); the corresponding circular arcs have positive finite length, giving Assumption 2(v).  The same quarter-disk argument verifies the additional kernel-mass condition in CTY's Theorem 2 for the uniform kernel.  All constants are independent of $n,M,$ and $\theta$.
\end{proof}

At a hard point $x_j$,
\begin{equation}
\tau_\theta(x_j)
=\mu_{1,\theta}(x_j)-\mu_{0,\theta}(x_j)
=a s_j-\delta\theta_j.                                       \label{eq:targetbits}
\end{equation}
Thus the $j$th bit changes the target by exactly $\delta$.

\section{Angular masking of the signed-distance experiment}

Fix a site $j$ and condition on $X\in S_j$.  Let $D_j=D(x_j)$.  Since $x_j$ lies on a flat portion of the boundary, $D_j=r>0$ corresponds to the upper semicircle $0\le\alpha\le\pi$ and $D_j=-r<0$ to the lower semicircle.

\begin{lemma}[Exact radial masking]
\label{lem:mask}
Conditional on $X\in S_j$, the marginal distribution of $D_j$ is the same under $\theta_j=0$ and $\theta_j=1$.  Its density is $r/w^2$ on each of $(0,w)$ and $(-w,0)$.  Moreover, if
\[
q_{b,j}(r)=\Pp(Y=1\mid D_j=r,X\in S_j,\theta_j=b),
\]
then for $r>0$,
\begin{align}
q_{0,j}(r)&=\frac12+a s_j,                                    \label{eq:q0}\\
q_{1,j}(r)-q_{0,j}(r)
&=-\delta\phi(r/w)
\left[1-\eta\!\left(\frac{r}{C\delta}\right)\right],        \label{eq:qdiff}
\end{align}
and for $r<0$ the two conditional laws of $Y$ coincide.  Hence the reduced laws differ only for $0<D_j<2C\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identities
\[
\int_0^\pi\cos\alpha\,d\alpha=0,
\qquad
\int_0^\pi\cos^2\alpha\,d\alpha=\frac\pi2
\]
show first that the density perturbation has zero semicircular average, so the signed-radius marginal is unchanged.  Under $\theta_j=0$, the angular average of $a r\cos\alpha$ is zero, giving \eqref{eq:q0}.  Under $\theta_j=1$, averaging the product of \eqref{eq:density} and \eqref{eq:mu1} over the treatment semicircle gives
\[
q_{1,j}(r)
=\frac12+a s_j-\delta\phi(r/w)+\frac{a r}{2}b_{\delta,w}(r),
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:qdiff}.  On the control side the observed outcome has mean $1/2$ under both hypotheses.  Because a mixture of Bernoulli laws is Bernoulli with success probability equal to the mixed mean, these calculations identify the entire conditional law of $Y\mid D_j$, not only its first moment.
\end{proof}

Let $Q_{j,b}$ denote the one-observation law of $(Y,D_j)$ conditional on $X\in S_j$ and $\theta_j=b$.  Since the Bernoulli probabilities are uniformly bounded away from zero and one, Lemma \ref{lem:mask} implies
\begin{equation}
\KL(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
\lesssim
\int_0^{2C\delta}\delta^2\frac{r}{w^2}\,dr
\lesssim \frac{\delta^4}{w^2}.                              \label{eq:localkl}
\end{equation}
If $m$ observations fall in $S_j$, then
\begin{equation}
\KL(Q_{j,0}^{\otimes m},Q_{j,1}^{\otimes m})
\lesssim m\frac{\delta^4}{w^2}.                             \label{eq:mkl}
\end{equation}

\section{Coupling amplification across boundary sites}

The logarithm comes from packing many sites, but one must respect the coordinatewise information restriction: the estimator at $x_j$ sees only distances to $x_j$, not the collection of distances to all centers.  The following coupling avoids treating those coordinatewise experiments as a single unrestricted experiment.

Let $N_j$ be the number of observations in $S_j$.  Since each perturbation in \eqref{eq:density} integrates to zero over its disk,
\[
\rho:=\Pp_\theta(X\in S_j)=\frac{\pi w^2}{36}
\]
is independent of $\theta$ and $j$.  Hence the vector of cell labels (and therefore $(N_1,\ldots,N_M)$) has the same law under all hypotheses.

Condition on the cell labels.  For each $j$, maximally couple the two reduced samples
\[
Z_j^0\sim Q_{j,0}^{\otimes N_j},
\qquad
Z_j^1\sim Q_{j,1}^{\otimes N_j}.
\]
After coupling the reduced samples, use regular conditional distributions to complete each one to a raw block of observations in $S_j$.  Do this independently across $j$, and use one common draw for observations outside $\cup_jS_j$.  For every $\theta$, selecting the $\theta_j$ version of block $j$ yields exactly the law $P_\theta$ conditional on the cell labels.

Write
\[
E_j=\{Z_j^0=Z_j^1\}.
\]
By maximal coupling and the Bretagnolle--Huber bound,
\begin{align}
\Pp(E_j\mid N_j)
&=1-\TV(Q_{j,0}^{\otimes N_j},Q_{j,1}^{\otimes N_j})\\
&\ge \frac12
\exp\left\{-\KL(Q_{j,0}^{\otimes N_j},Q_{j,1}^{\otimes N_j})\right\}.       \label{eq:eqprob}
\end{align}
Let
\[
G_n=\left\{\max_{1\le j\le M}N_j\le2n\rho\right\}.
\]
A Chernoff bound gives $\Pp(G_n)\to1$ because $nw^2\gg\log M$.  On $G_n$, \eqref{eq:mkl} and \eqref{eq:eqprob} yield
\begin{equation}
\Pp(E_j\mid N_1,\ldots,N_M)
\ge \frac12 e^{-C_0n\delta^4}                                \label{eq:eqprob2}
\end{equation}
for a fixed constant $C_0$.  The block couplings can be taken independent, so
\begin{equation}
\Pp\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^M E_j\,\middle|\,G_n\right)
\ge
1-\exp\left\{-\frac{M}{2}e^{-C_0n\delta^4}\right\}.          \label{eq:anymatch}
\end{equation}

The importance of $E_j$ is deterministic.  If $E_j$ occurs, then for every pair $\theta$ and $\theta\oplus e_j$, the \emph{entire signed-distance input at $x_j$} is identical under the coupled datasets: all blocks other than $j$ are literally the same, while block $j$ has the same $(Y,D_j)$ sample.  Consequently any distance-based estimator must output the same value at $x_j$ for these paired hypotheses.

\section{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}}

Given any estimator, decode the $j$th bit by
\[
\widehat\theta_j
=\1\left\{\widehat\tau(x_j)<a s_j-\frac\delta2\right\}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:targetbits}, if $\widehat\theta_j\ne\theta_j$, then
\[
|\widehat\tau(x_j)-\tau_\theta(x_j)|\ge\frac\delta2.
\]
Thus exact recovery of all bits is necessary for uniform error below $\delta/2$.

Put the uniform prior on $\theta\in\{0,1\}^M$.  Fix a realization of the grand coupling.  If some $E_j$ occurs, pair the $2^M$ parameter vectors along coordinate $j$.  The estimator receives identical input at $x_j$ at the two endpoints of every pair, but the true bit differs.  Hence at least one endpoint of every pair has a decoding error.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\Pp_{\theta\sim\mathrm{Unif}}
\left(
\sup_{x\in\cB}|\widehat\tau(x)-\tau_\theta(x)|\ge\frac\delta2
\,\middle|\,\text{coupling}
\right)
\ge\frac12\,\1\left\{\bigcup_jE_j\right\}.                 \label{eq:pairing}
\end{equation}

Now choose
\[
\delta_n=\gamma\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4},
\qquad
w_n=A\delta_n^{1/q},
\qquad
M_n\asymp\delta_n^{-1/q}.
\]
Then
\[
M_n e^{-C_0n\delta_n^4}
\asymp
\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/(4q)}n^{-C_0\gamma^4}.
\]
Choose the fixed constant $\gamma>0$ so small that
\[
C_0\gamma^4<\frac1{4q}.
\]
It follows from \eqref{eq:anymatch} that $\Pp(\cup_jE_j)\to1$.  Averaging \eqref{eq:pairing} over the coupling and using that every selected dataset has the correct $P_\theta$ marginal gives
\[
2^{-M_n}\sum_\theta
\Pp_\theta\left(
\sup_{x\in\cB}|\widehat\tau(x)-\tau_\theta(x)|\ge\frac{\delta_n}{2}
\right)
\ge \frac12-o(1).
\]
Hence
\begin{align*}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
\E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\cB}|\widehat\tau(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
&\ge
\frac{\delta_n}{2}\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)\\
&\gtrsim
\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}.
\end{align*}
Taking the infimum over all enlarged signed-distance estimators proves the theorem. \qed

\section{Interpretation}

The regression smoothness $q$ enters the construction through the width required for a height-$\delta$ bump:
\[
w\asymp\delta^{1/q}.
\]
This leaves $M\asymp\delta^{-1/q}$ disjoint boundary sites, so $\log M\asymp\log(1/\delta)$ for every fixed finite $q$.  The score-density perturbation then masks the regression signal outside a radius $O(\delta)$, making the local information cost
\[
n\times \delta^2\times \delta^2=n\delta^4,
\]
rather than the ordinary smooth-regression cost $n\delta^{2+2/q}$.  Balancing $n\delta^4$ against $\log M\asymp\log n$ yields $(\log n/n)^{1/4}$.

The mechanism is therefore not regression nonsmoothness: the regression functions lie in one fixed $q$-smooth class for any prescribed $q=p+1\ge2$.  The least-favorable nuisance is angular variation in $f_X$, which distance reduction discards.

\begin{remark}[Scope]
The proof deliberately uses the literal CTY assumptions.  It does \emph{not} establish the same rate if $f_X$ is known, fixed across the parameter class, or constrained by a uniform derivative/H\"older bound.  The masking density changes on the $O(\delta)$ radial scale and therefore has derivatives that are not uniformly bounded as $\delta\downarrow0$.  A lower bound under a uniformly smooth or known design density is a distinct, stronger problem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to CTY]
CTY's Assumption 2 places the assignment boundary in the interior of the score support, whereas their Theorem 6 lower-bound class sets the relevant curve equal to the support boundary.  The construction above removes that mismatch: both $\cX$ and $\cB\subset\operatorname{int}(\cX)$ are fixed under every hypothesis.  It also gives the estimator signed distance and the known geometry.  The theorem itself is stated over the full $\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)$ class used to express CTY's regular A1/A2 upper theory; the fixed rectangle appears only as a hard subclass in the proof.  Hence the lower bound is in the correct minimax direction and matches the $(\log n/n)^{1/4}$ uniform rate obtained by combining CTY Theorems 2 and 4(ii).
\end{remark}
