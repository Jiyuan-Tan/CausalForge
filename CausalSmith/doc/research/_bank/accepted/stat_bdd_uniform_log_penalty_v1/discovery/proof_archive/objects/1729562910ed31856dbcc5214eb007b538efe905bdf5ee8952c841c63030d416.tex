For every integer \(m\geq1\), if the normalized arm-specific angular integrals entering \(N_{t,P,x}/Q_{t,P,x}\) admit a class-uniform order-\(m\) Taylor remainder at zero, uniformly over the trimmed boundary, then a degree-\((m-1)\) radial local polynomial has bias \(O(h^m)\), stochastic error \(O(\sqrt{\log(1/h)/(nh^2)})\), and optimized rate \((\log n/n)^{m/(2m+2)}\).  Conversely, bounded continuous densities with no common angular modulus admit the angular-masking family and force the first-order rate \((\log n/n)^{1/4}\).  What is not determined is a unique weakest primitive condition between these endpoints.
