Put
\[
 g_{t,P,x}(s)=\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)},
 \qquad 0<s\leq r_*.
\]
The assumed lower bound on \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\) makes this division valid at every displayed radius.  The radial-coarea identification lemma then shows that \(g_{t,P,x}\) is an observed exact-distance regression almost everywhere and that its limit identifies the separately defined causal arm value \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\).  Subtracting the arms identifies \(\tau_P(x)\); identification is used here and is not made part of the target's definition.

We first prove the uniform upper bound.  Fix \(0<h\leq r_*\), put \(v(u)=v_m(u)\), and define the population Gram matrix and moment vector
\[
 G_{t,P,x}(h)=\frac1{h^2}\int_0^hK_\triangle(s/h)v(s/h)v(s/h)^\top Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds,
\]
\[
 r_{t,P,x}(h)=\frac1{h^2}\int_0^hK_\triangle(s/h)v(s/h)g_{t,P,x}(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
Coarea, deterministic assignment, and consistency show that these are exactly the expectations of \(\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\) and \(\widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\).  With
\[
 G_m=\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)v(u)v(u)^\top\,du,
\]
the radial mass bounds imply the Loewner inequalities
\[
 \underline cG_m\preceq G_{t,P,x}(h)\preceq\overline cG_m.
\]
The matrix \(G_m\) is positive definite: for nonzero \(a\in\mathbb R^m\), the polynomial \(a^\top v(u)\) cannot vanish on the open interval \((0,1)\), and hence
\[
 a^\top G_ma=\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)\{a^\top v(u)\}^2,du>0.
\]
Thus every population inverse used below exists and
\[
 \lVert G_{t,P,x}(h)^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\{\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)\}^{-1}.
\]

Let \(B\) be a common bound for the displayed coefficient functions and set
\[
 \beta_{t,P,x}(h)=
 \bigl(\mu_{t,P}(x),b_{t,1,P}(x)h,\ldots,
 b_{t,m-1,P}(x)h^{m-1}\bigr)^\top.
\]
The H\"older-ball envelope bounds \(|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\), so \(\|\beta_{t,P,x}(h)\|\leq C\) for \(h\leq1\).  Polynomial reproduction and the assumed remainder give
\[
 r_{t,P,x}(h)=G_{t,P,x}(h)\beta_{t,P,x}(h)+\zeta_{t,P,x}(h),
\]
where, using \(Q(s)\leq\overline cs\) and \(\|v(u)\|\leq\sqrt m\),
\[
 \|\zeta_{t,P,x}(h)\|
 \leq\frac{A}{h^2}\int_0^hK_\triangle(s/h)\|v(s/h)\|s^mQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds
 \leq C h^m.
\]
Consequently the population local-polynomial intercept differs from \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\) by at most \(Ch^m\).

It remains to control the empirical matrices and moments uniformly.  The upper radial bound gives
\[
 P\{T=t,\lVert X-x\rVert_2\leq h\}
 =\int_0^hQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds\leq\frac{\overline c}{2}h^2.
\]
Every coordinate of a normalized Gram summand is bounded by \(h^{-2}\) and has second moment at most \(Ch^{-2}\).  Conditional sub-Gaussianity, consistency, and the bounded regression likewise imply, for every integer \(k\geq2\), the coordinatewise moment inequality
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left|h^{-2}\mathbf1\{T=t,R_x\leq h\}
 K_\triangle(R_x/h)v_\ell(R_x/h)Y\right|^k
 \leq k!C^kh^{2-2k}.
\]
Expanding the exponential series and applying exponential Markov inequality therefore gives, for every coordinate of either centered empirical object,
\[
 P\{|(\mathbb P_n-P)Z_{t,x,h}|>u\}
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{cnu^2}{h^{-2}+h^{-2}u}\right\}.
\]

For each law, cover \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\subseteq[-L,L]^2\) by an \(n^{-4}\)-net of cardinality at most \(C_Ln^8\).  A \(C^1\) embedded curve has planar Lebesgue measure zero, so almost surely no sample score lies on \(\mathcal B_P\); hence the strict arm indicator in the estimator equals \(\mathbf1\{T=t\}\) simultaneously for every boundary point.  On \([0,1]\), the functions \(K_\triangle(u)v_k(u)v_\ell(u)\) and \(K_\triangle(u)v_k(u)\) are Lipschitz, with constants depending only on \(m\).  The reverse triangle inequality therefore bounds empirical oscillations between neighboring centers by
\[
 C_mn^{-4}h^{-3},
 \qquad
 C_mn^{-4}h^{-3}\mathbb P_n|Y|,
\]
and bounds the corresponding population oscillations analogously.  Conditional sub-Gaussianity gives uniformly bounded second moments of \(\mathbb P_n|Y|\).  A union bound over the two arms, the finitely many matrix and vector coordinates, and the planar net now yields, for arbitrary fixed \(A_0>0\), an event \(\mathcal E_n\) with
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal C}P(\mathcal E_n)\geq1-Cn^{-A_0}
\]
on which
\[
 \sup_{t,x}\left|\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-G_{t,P,x}(h)\right|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\sup_{t,x}\left|\widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-r_{t,P,x}(h)\right|
 \leq C\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+\frac{\log n}{nh^2}\right\}.
\]
Here and below \(x\) ranges over \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\).  This argument uses only ambient planar covering, so no common length, speed, reach, or derivative bound for the charts is needed.

For large \(n\), the last display is at most \(\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)/2\).  Weyl's eigenvalue inequality then places every empirical matrix on the active-cutoff branch and gives
\[
 \left\|\{\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\frac{2}{\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)}.
\]
Using the population decomposition above,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\beta_{t,P,x}(h)
 &=\{\widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-r_{t,P,x}(h)\}
 +\zeta_{t,P,x}(h)\\
 &\quad+\{G_{t,P,x}(h)-\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\}
 \beta_{t,P,x}(h).
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplication by the uniformly bounded inverse and by \(e_1^\top\), followed by subtraction of the two arms, proves on \(\mathcal E_n\) that
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+
 \frac{\log n}{nh^2}\right\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n^c\), the admissibility lemma bounds each arm intercept by \(Ch^{-2}\mathbb P_n|Y|\), while the target is uniformly bounded.  Cauchy--Schwarz, the uniform second moment of \(\mathbb P_n|Y|\), and a sufficiently large \(A_0\) make the expected exceptional contribution negligible.  Choose
\[
 h_n\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(2m+2)}.
\]
Then \(h_n^m=\sqrt{\log n/(nh_n^2)}\), and \(\log n/(nh_n^2)=h_n^{2m}\leq h_n^m\).  The admissibility lemma places this single data-driven estimator in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\), so
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq Ch_n^m,
 \qquad
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal C)\leq Ch_n^m.
\]

We next prove the conditional converse.  Work on \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\) with uniform density, straight assignment interface, and trimmed centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) separated by \(3h_n\), where \(M\asymp h_n^{-1}\).  Let \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\), \(\phi(0)=1\), and set \(\Delta=\eta h_n^m\).  Keep the control regression fixed and, in alternative \(j\), add \(\Delta\phi((z-x_j)/h_n)\) to a fixed treated baseline.  Use the Gaussian-noised Bernoulli outcomes from the packing construction.  If \(m\geq q\), then for every \(k\leq q\),
\[
 \left\|D^k\{\Delta\phi((\cdot-x_j)/h_n)\}\right\|_\infty
 \leq C\eta h_n^{m-k}\leq C\eta,
\]
so, after choosing \(\eta\) small, all alternatives obey the common \(q\)-H\"older envelope.  In fact their derivatives through order \(m\) are uniformly bounded.  Taylor's formula through degree \(m-1\), integrated over each straight half-circle, therefore verifies the displayed order-\(m\) normalized-coarea remainder with constants independent of \(n,j,t,x\); moreover \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\pi s\) for all sufficiently small radii at trimmed centers.  Thus these are valid contained bump laws of the asserted kind.  When \(m<q\), the derivative of order \(q\) has size \(h_n^{m-q}\), which is why this construction supplies no converse under the frozen \(q\)-H\"older envelope.

For each alternative versus the unperturbed law, the Bernoulli inequality and data processing through the common Gaussian channel give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn\Delta^2h_n^2
 =C\eta^2nh_n^{2m+2}=C\eta^2\log n.
\]
Since \(\log M=(1/(2m+2)+o(1))\log n\), choose \(\eta\) so that this is at most \(\alpha\log M\) for a fixed \(\alpha<1/4\).  If \(J\) is uniform on the \(M\) alternatives, the log-sum inequality gives \(I(J;O_{1:n})\leq\alpha\log M\).  For any decoder with error event \(E\), partitioning the conditional entropy according to \(E\) gives
\[
 H(J\mid O_{1:n})\leq\log2+P(E)\log M.
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log M\), it follows that \(P(E)\geq1-\alpha-o(1)\).  Supremum error below \(\Delta/3\) identifies the unique bumped center, and therefore every coordinatewise-distance estimator, indeed every full-sample estimator, has risk at least \(c\Delta=ch_n^m\) on this contained family.  If \(\mathcal C\) contains it, the upper and lower bounds match.

The angular-imbalance assertion is separate.  If a law class contains the family from the angular-density packing, Poissonization into its disjoint bump cells gives the conditional product experiment required by the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma.  The local compressed divergences are at most \(2\alpha\log M_n\) with \(2\alpha<1\), and midpoint decoding succeeds whenever the uniform loss is less than \(c_1a_n/2\).  The lemma and marked de-Poissonization therefore give the lower bound \(ca_n\).  For \(m=1\), \(h_n^m=a_n\); for every \(m\geq2\),
\[
 \frac{m}{2m+2}>\frac14,
\]
so the proved upper rate is strictly faster.  The contained-bump argument applies exactly in the stated regime \(m\geq q\), and no converse has been inferred outside it.

Finally we construct the announced obstruction.  On \([-1,1]^2\) with interface \(z_2=0\), choose \(0<\alpha<1\), a continuous radial cutoff \(\chi\) equal to one near zero and zero for \(r\geq r_c<1\), and define, in polar coordinates with the value at zero set by continuity,
\[
 f(z)=\frac14\left\{1+\chi(\lVert z\rVert_2)
 \lVert z\rVert_2^\alpha\frac{z_1}{\lVert z\rVert_2}\right\}.
\]
Choose the support radius of \(\chi\) strictly below one.  Then the bracket is bounded above and bounded away from zero.  Its perturbation is odd in \(z_1\), so its integral over the square is zero; hence \(f\) is a bounded positive continuous density.  At the boundary point zero and every sufficiently small treated-half-circle radius \(s\),
\[
 Q(s)=\frac{s}{4}\int_0^\pi(1+s^\alpha\cos\theta)\,d\theta
 =\frac{\pi s}{4}.
\]
For a constant regression \(\mu\equiv c\), one has \(N(s)/Q(s)=c\) exactly.  For the affine regression \(\mu(z)=z_1\),
\[
 \frac{N(s)}{Q(s)}
 =\frac{(s^2/4)\int_0^\pi\cos\theta(1+s^\alpha\cos\theta)\,d\theta}
 {\pi s/4}
 =\frac{s^{1+\alpha}}2.
\]
If an order-two polynomial remainder existed, there would be a constant \(b\) with \(s^{1+\alpha}/2-bs=O(s^2)\).  Division by \(s\) and passage to zero force \(b=0\), after which division by \(s^2\) gives \(s^{\alpha-1}/2\to\infty\), a contradiction.  The same density therefore yields an exact constant normalized-coarea regression for one outcome surface and failure of the order-two remainder for another.  This proves the stated jointness conclusion and no stronger weakest-threshold impossibility.
