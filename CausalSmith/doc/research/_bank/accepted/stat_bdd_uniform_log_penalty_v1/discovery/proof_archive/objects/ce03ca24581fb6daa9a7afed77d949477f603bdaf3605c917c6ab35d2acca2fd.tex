Fix an integer \(m\geq1\), ambient indices \(q\geq1\) and \(\iota\geq1\), and \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\).  This is a conditional risk-transfer theorem.  Let \(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{coarea}}(q,\iota,L)\) be a subclass for which there are constants \(A<\infty\), \(r_*>0\), and \(0<\underline c\leq\overline c<\infty\) such that, for every \(P\in\mathcal C\), \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), for Lebesgue-almost every \(s\in(0,r_*]\),
\[
 \underline c s\leq Q_{t,P,x}(s)\leq\overline c s,
\]
\[
 \left|g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 -\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k\right|
 \leq As^m,
\]
where the coefficient functions are uniformly bounded over \(P,t,x,k\).  Under exactly these radial hypotheses, zero lies in the essential support of each observed radial measure, \(\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\) equals \((0,r_*]\) modulo a Lebesgue-null set there, and the separate observed arm limits identify \(\tau_P(x)\).  With
\[
 h_n\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(2m+2)},
\]
the stabilized coordinatewise degree-\((m-1)\) estimator \(\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h_n}\) belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\) and satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 \left|\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)\right|
 \right]
 \leq C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]
Consequently, under those same radial hypotheses,
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal C)
 \leq C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]
If, in addition, \(m\geq q\) and \(\mathcal C\) contains for every sufficiently large \(n\) the uniform-design straight-interface \(q\)-H\"older bump submodel of width \(h_n\) and height proportional to \(h_n^m\) constructed in the proof, then the reverse inequality holds and the displayed rate is exact.  No matching converse is asserted without that containment, and none is asserted for \(m<q\).  Independently, a law class that contains the shrinking angular-imbalance family generated by \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{ang}}\) has coordinatewise-distance lower frontier \(ca_n\).  Thus the order-one upper rate is the first-order saturation rate only when the order-one radial hypotheses and that hard-family containment both hold; for every \(m\geq2\), the faster displayed upper rate is asserted only under the order-\(m\) radial hypotheses, and its matching converse additionally requires contained bumps and \(m\geq q\).

Finally, there exists a bounded positive continuous density on a square with a straight interface such that, for Lebesgue-almost every sufficiently small treated-half-circle radius, a locally constant regression gives a constant normalized-coarea ratio, whereas the affine regression \(\mu(z)=z_1\) gives the ratio \(s^{1+\alpha}/2\) for some \(0<\alpha<1\) and therefore fails an order-two polynomial remainder even in the almost-everywhere sense.  Hence density alone does not determine these examples' joint normalized-coarea remainder; no universal weakest-threshold impossibility is asserted.
