Fix \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\).  For every sufficiently large \(n\), take the family supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}support\text{-}boundary\text{-}angular\text{-}packing}\).  Write \(M=M_n\), put \(\Delta=c_1a_n\), and let
\[
 C_j=B_2(x_j,w_n)\cap S,
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq M,
\]
be its pairwise disjoint half-disc cells.  The packing lemma gives numbers \(u_{j,0},u_{j,1}\) satisfying
\[
 \mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)=u_{j,\omega_j},
 \qquad |u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|\geq\Delta.
\]
It also gives \(M_n\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rates}\), \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\to0\), and therefore
\[
 M_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Let \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) be arbitrary.  By \(\mathrm{def:point\text{-}indexed\text{-}distance\text{-}decision\text{-}class}\), it is generated by a law-independent family
\[
 \{T_{n,x}:x\in\mathbb R^2\},
 \qquad
 T_{n,x}:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\]
such that each fixed-\(x\) section is Borel measurable and
\[
 \widehat\mu_{n,P}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)
 =T_{n,x}(V_{n,P}(x))
\]
for every admissible \(P\) and every \(x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\).  No map jointly measurable in \((x,v)\) is assumed or used below.

Embed this fixed-sample rule in an experiment whose restrictions to the cells are independent.  Under \(P_\omega\), generate a Poisson random measure on
\[
 \mathbb R\times S\times(0,1)
\]
with intensity \(2n\,P_\omega(dy,dx)\,du\), where \(u\) is an independent uniform mark.  Its total count \(N\) has the \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\) law.  If \(N\geq n\), retain the \(n\) observations having the smallest marks, order them by their marks, form their compressed distance sample at each queried point, and apply the corresponding section \(T_{n,x}\).  If \(N<n\), return zero at every queried point.  Ties have probability zero and may be resolved by any fixed Borel rule.  Denote the resulting pointwise rule by \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\).  Conditional on \(N=k\geq n\), the retained observations are independent with common law \(P_\omega\), because the marks are independent of the observations and their ordering gives a random permutation independent of the observation values.  The construction uses the fixed family \(\{T_{n,x}\}\), so it is independent of \(\omega\).

Put independent uniform bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) on the packing vertices.  Let \(Z_j\) be the complete marked restriction of the Poisson random measure to \(C_j\), and let \(Z_0\) be its restriction to
\[
 S\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^M C_j.
\]
The marked observation space is standard Borel, as is its space of finite counting measures.  Conditional on the bits, \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) are independent, because restrictions of a Poisson random measure to disjoint Borel sets are independent.  The cellwise locality asserted by \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}support\text{-}boundary\text{-}angular\text{-}packing}\) implies that the law of \(Z_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), whereas the law of \(Z_0\) is independent of all bits.

For each \(j\), let \(S_j\) retain from \(Z_j\) only every point's mark, outcome, and distance from \(x_j\).  A decoder constructed from \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\) is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\).  Indeed, the full restrictions outside \(C_j\) reveal the marks, outcomes, and locations of all off-cell points and hence their distances from \(x_j\), while \(S_j\) supplies exactly the marks, outcomes, and distances of the in-cell points.  Sorting the finitely many revealed marks reconstructs the retained ordered compressed sample at \(x_j\).  The fixed section \(T_{n,x_j}\) is Borel measurable by definition.  Thus \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\), and every Borel threshold of it, is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\).  This argument is separate for each \(j\) and uses no regularity of \(x\mapsto T_{n,x}\).

We next verify the relative-entropy hypothesis of \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\).  Adjacent packing laws are identical off \(C_j\), their common cell mass is \(m_n\), and every support point at distance less than \(w_n\) from \(x_j\) belongs to \(C_j\).  Let \(\nu_{j,b}\) be the subprobability law of one outcome--distance pair restricted to \(C_j\), under bit value \(b\in\{0,1\}\).  Both subprobabilities have mass \(m_n\).  The corresponding complete one-observation compressed laws have the disjoint-support form
\[
 \nu_{j,b}+\lambda_j,
\]
where \(\lambda_j\) is common to the two bits and is supported on radii at least \(w_n\), while \(\nu_{j,b}\) is supported on radii below \(w_n\), up to null boundaries.  Orient the bits and define
\[
 D_j=int\log\!\left(\frac{d\nu_{j,0}}{d\nu_{j,1}}\right)d\nu_{j,0}.
\]
The disjoint-support decomposition and factorization of likelihood ratios give
\[
 nD_j
 =\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
 \mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
 \right)
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
The finiteness of this divergence supplies the required absolute continuity.  Interchanging the adjacent vertices gives the same assertion in the reverse orientation.

Let \(Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)\).  Under either bit, the cell count has the common law
\[
 K_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2nm_n),
\]
and the independent marks contribute no relative entropy.  Conditional on \(K_j=k\), the compressed cell points are independent with law \(\nu_{j,b}/m_n\).  Since the two subprobabilities have the same mass,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\nu_{j,0}/m_n,\nu_{j,1}/m_n)
 =\frac{D_j}{m_n}.
\]
Conditioning on the common count and taking expectations yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
 =\mathbb E[K_j]\frac{D_j}{m_n}
 =2nD_j
 \leq2\alpha\log M.
\]
The packing lemma fixes \(0<\alpha<1/8\); hence \(\kappa=2\alpha<1\).

At \(x_j\), decode \(\Omega_j\) by thresholding \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\) at the midpoint of \(u_{j,0}\) and \(u_{j,1}\), with the inequality reversed when \(u_{j,1}<u_{j,0}\).  The exact finite-\(M\) conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\), applied with \(\kappa=2\alpha\), gives
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one coordinate is decoded incorrectly}\}
 \geq\frac12\left[1-\exp\{-M_n^{1-2\alpha}/2\}\right].
\]
Because \(M_n\to\infty\) and \(2\alpha<1\), the right-hand side is \(1/2-o(1)\), with the displayed finite-\(M_n\) expression retained for the next step.

Define the measurable finite-coordinate loss
\[
 L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin,Pois}}
 =\max_{1\leq j\leq M_n}
 \left|
 \widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)
 \right|.
\]
It is measurable because it is the maximum of finitely many random variables obtained from Borel fixed-point sections.  If any midpoint decoder errs, then the separation inequality implies
\[
 L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin,Pois}}\geq\frac{\Delta}{2}.
\]
Averaging over the uniform bit prior and using the preceding error bound therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 2^{-M_n}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{M_n}}
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}
 \left[L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin,Pois}}\right]
 &\geq\frac{\Delta}{2}
 \Pr\{\text{at least one decoding error}\}\\
 &\geq\frac{\Delta}{4}
 \left[1-\exp\{-M_n^{1-2\alpha}/2\}\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}
 \left[L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin,Pois}}\right]
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{4}
 \left[1-\exp\{-M_n^{1-2\alpha}/2\}\right].
\]

We now de-Poissonize this finite maximum, without invoking the measurability of a supremum over the whole boundary.  Define
\[
 L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin}}
 =\max_{1\leq j\leq M_n}
 \left|
 \widehat\mu_{n,P_\omega}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x_j)
 -\mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)
 \right|.
\]
This is again measurable.  The packing construction has \(1/4\leq\mu_{P_\omega}\leq3/4\), so on \(\{N<n\}\) the zero default has finite-coordinate loss at most one.  On \(\{N\geq n\}\), the retained observations have law \(P_\omega^{\otimes n}\) and are processed at every \(x_j\) by the original fixed section \(T_{n,x_j}\).  Hence, for every \(\omega\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}
 \left[L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin,Pois}}\right]
 \leq
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}
 \left[L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin}}\right]
 +\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}.
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-tN}\), with \(t=\log2\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr\{N<n\}
 &\leq e^{tn}\mathbb E[e^{-tN}]\\
 &=\exp\{n\log2+2n(2^{-1}-1)\}\\
 &=\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}
 =o(a_n).
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining the last three displays, using \(\Delta=c_1a_n\), and then decreasing the positive constant if necessary yields a constant \(c=c(q,L)>0\) such that
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}
 \left[L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin}}\right]
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

For each \(\omega\), the pointwise inequality
\[
 L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin}}
 \leq
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 \left|
 \widehat\mu_{n,P_\omega}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)
 -\mu_{P_\omega}(x)
 \right|
\]
holds even if the supremum on the right is not measurable.  By the definition of outer expectation in \(\mathrm{def:point\text{-}indexed\text{-}distance\text{-}risk}\), every measurable majorant of the right-hand side also majorizes the measurable variable on the left.  Therefore monotonicity of outer expectation and equality of ordinary and outer expectation for the measurable finite maximum imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}
 \left[L_{n,\omega}^{\mathrm{fin}}\right]
 \leq
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^*\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 \left|
 \widehat\mu_{n,P_\omega}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)
 -\mu_{P_\omega}(x)
 \right|
 \right].
\]
This is the only passage from the finite packing points to the whole boundary, and it requires no measurability of the latter supremum.

The packing lemma explicitly verifies independent sampling, compact support, density positivity, rectifiable boundary, H\"older regression, and continuous conditional variance for every \(P_\omega\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\).  The supremum over the full class is therefore no smaller than the supremum over this finite family.  Since the point-indexed rule was arbitrary, the last two inequalities imply
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}
 =\inf_{\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)}
 \mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
 \left|\widehat\mu_{n,P}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)-\mu_P(x)\right|
 \right]
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Dividing by \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) and taking lower limits proves the displayed conclusion with \(c_{\mathrm{PI}}=c\).  Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:common\text{-}map\text{-}strict\text{-}in\text{-}point\text{-}indexed}\) shows that this sectionwise-Borel point-indexed class strictly contains the inherited common-map class.  No Assumption~2 upper theorem and no full-class achievability statement enter the argument.
