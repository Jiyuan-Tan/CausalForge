All constants in this proof depend only on \(q,L,c_-,r_0\).  The proof separates achievability from the compressed-experiment converse.

\emph{Achievability.}
For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and \(h>0\), write
\[
W_{i,t,x}(h)=\mathbf1\{T_i=t\}K_\triangle(D_{P,x,i}/h),
\qquad
G_{t,x}(h)=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^nW_{i,t,x}(h).
\]
The arm estimate in \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}\) is the weighted outcome average when \(G_{t,x}(h)>0\), and is zero otherwise.  Every summand is a Borel function of \((Y_i,D_{P,x,i})\), because away from the probability-zero event \(D_{P,x,i}=0\), the sign of \(D_{P,x,i}\) equals \(2T_i-1\).  Consequently the arm difference is one data-driven element of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).

For \(0<h\leq2r_0\), \(\mathrm{lem:fixed\text{-}local\text{-}constant\text{-}stability}\) gives
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}\inf_{t,x}\mathbb E_PG_{t,x}(h)\geq\kappa_0>0.
\]
The support of a kernel summand lies in \(\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,h)\}\), whose probability is at most \(\pi Lh^2\) by \(\mathrm{lem:density\text{-}implies\text{-}upper\text{-}mass}\).  Conditional sub-Gaussianity, consistency, \(0\leq K_\triangle\leq1\), and the support-probability bound imply, uniformly in \(P,t,x\),
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[W_{i,t,x}(h)^2\{Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\}^2\right]\leq Ch^2
\]
and the exponential-moment bound for the centered summand.  Applying exponential Markov optimization to the product mgf gives, for \(0<u\leq1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&P\!\left(\left|G_{t,x}(h)-\mathbb E_PG_{t,x}(h)\right|>u\right)\\
&\quad+P\!\left(\left|\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^nW_{i,t,x}(h)
\{Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\}\right|>u\right)
\leq C\exp(-cnh^2u^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
This calculation uses only iid sampling, the density upper envelope, and the conditional tail bound; neither density continuity, a variance lower bound, nor boundary rectifiability appears.

The common interface is compact as a closed subset of the compact support.  Choose a maximal \(n^{-3}\)-separated subset of it.  Disjoint ambient disks inside a fixed enlargement of \([-L,L]^2\) show that the net has at most \(Cn^6\) points, irrespective of any curve length.  A union bound over this net and the two arms, with \(u=C\sqrt{\log n/(nh^2)}\), gives an event \(E_{n,h}\) with \(\sup_PP(E_{n,h}^c)\leq Cn^{-12}\) on which the displayed numerator deviation is uniformly bounded by \(u\) and every net denominator is at least \(3\kappa_0/4\).

The reverse triangle inequality and the one-Lipschitz property of \(K_\triangle\) show that moving the center by \(d\) changes \(G_{t,x}(h)\) by at most \(dh^{-3}\), and changes the scaled outcome numerator by at most
\[
dh^{-3}\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|\right).
\]
The H\"older envelope bounds \(|\mu_{t,P}|\) by \(L\), and conditional sub-Gaussianity gives \(\sup_P\mathbb E_PY^2<\infty\).  At \(h\asymp a_n\), the mesh oscillations \(n^{-3}h^{-3}\{1+n^{-1}\sum_i|Y_i|\}\) are \(o(a_n)\) in mean.  Thus the net bounds extend to every \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), with all denominators at least \(\kappa_0/2\).

On the kernel support, \(q\geq1\) gives \(|\mu_{t,P}(X_i)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq Lh\).  Decomposing the weighted outcome about \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\) and dividing by the positive denominator on \(E_{n,h}\) yields
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}|\widehat\mu_{t,h}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|
\leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}+o(a_n).
\]
On \(E_{n,h}^c\), a nonzero local-constant estimate is a convex combination of observed outcomes, the zero-denominator branch is zero, and \(\sup_x|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L\).  The conditional tail envelope gives \(\mathbb E_P\max_{i\leq n}|Y_i|^2\leq C\log n\).  Cauchy--Schwarz makes the exceptional contribution \(O(n^{-6}\sqrt{\log n})\).  Taking the arm difference and balancing the two leading terms with \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/4}=a_n\) proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|\leq Ca_n.
\]
The canonical coarea lemma separately verifies that the boundary contrast is the limit of the identifying radial regressions; the preceding display estimates the causal target itself.

\emph{Converse.}
Use the \(M=M_n\) centers and hypercube laws of \(\mathrm{lem:angular\text{-}density\text{-}packing}\).  Those laws satisfy the enlarged primitive class because the packing lemma verifies all of its remaining member properties.  Poissonize at mean \(2n\) and attach independent continuous ordering marks.  Conditional on independent uniform hypercube bits, split the marked process into the disjoint bump cells \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) and their complement \(Z_0\).  Cell \(j\) depends only on bit \(j\), the cells are conditionally independent, and \(Z_0\) is bit-independent.  Let \(S_j\) retain from cell \(j\) only the outcomes, signed radii about \(x_j\), and ordering marks.  Even after revealing \((Z_{-j},Z_0)\), a coordinatewise decision at \(x_j\) is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\).

Poisson relative entropy is intensity times one-observation relative entropy.  The packing lemma's fixed-\(n\) adjacent bound and its equality outside the local cell therefore give
\[
\operatorname{KL}\{\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=0),\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=1)\}
\leq2\alpha\log M,
\qquad 2\alpha<1.
\]
The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma implies probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\) of at least one bit error.  Midpoint thresholding of the estimated target at the centers would recover every bit whenever the sup-norm error were smaller than \(c_1a_n/2\).  Hence every coordinatewise rule in the Poisson experiment has Bayes expected sup-loss at least \(ca_n\).

To de-Poissonize, take any fixed-\(n\) rule, apply it to the \(n\) observations with smallest marks when the Poisson count is at least \(n\), and output zero otherwise.  Conditional on the first event the selected observations are iid from \(P\); exponential Markov optimization gives \(P\{\operatorname{Poi}(2n)<n\}\leq e^{-cn}\).  Since \(\sup_x|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L\),
\[
ca_n\leq\sup_P\mathbb E_P^{\operatorname{Poi}(2n)}[\text{sup-loss}]
\leq\sup_P\mathbb E_P^n[\text{sup-loss}]+2Le^{-cn}.
\]
Taking the infimum over fixed-sample rules proves \(R_n^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}})\geq c'a_n\).  Combining both directions gives the asserted positive finite liminf and limsup.  The hard family is internal to this project's primitive class; no inclusion in the class of Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu, Theorem 6, is claimed.
