Fix \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), take the family supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}support\text{-}boundary\text{-}angular\text{-}packing}\).  Write \(M=M_n\), put \(\Delta=c_1a_n\), and let
\[
 C_j=B_2(x_j,w_n)\cap S,\qquad 1\leq j\leq M,
\]
be its pairwise disjoint half-disc cells.  By that lemma there are numbers \(u_{j,0},u_{j,1}\) such that
\[
 \mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)=u_{j,\omega_j},
 \qquad |u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|\geq\Delta.
\]

Let \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) be arbitrary.  By \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}distance\text{-}decision\text{-}class}\), there is one measurable map
\[
 T_n:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\]
selected independently of the law, such that
\[
 \widehat\mu_n(x)=T_n(V_{n,P}(x))
\]
at every boundary point.  In particular, the center enters this common rule only through the compressed sample.

We first turn this fixed-\(n\) rule into a rule in an experiment whose restrictions to the cells are independent.  Under \(P_\omega\), generate a Poisson point process of mean \(2n\) on the observation space and attach independent marks with the uniform distribution on \((0,1)\).  If its point count \(N\) is at least \(n\), retain the \(n\) observations with the smallest marks, order them by their marks, form the corresponding distance sample at every queried boundary point, and apply the same map \(T_n\).  If \(N<n\), return the zero boundary function.  Denote the resulting rule by \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\).  Conditional on every value \(N=k\geq n\), the retained observations are an independent sample from \(P_\omega\): the marks are independent of the observations, and their ordering induces a random permutation independent of the observation values.  This construction is independent of \(\omega\) and of \(P_\omega\).

Place independent uniform bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) on the packing vertices.  Let \(Z_j\) be the complete marked restriction of the Poisson process to \(C_j\), and let \(Z_0\) be its restriction to the complement of \(\bigcup_{j=1}^M C_j\).  The marked observation space is standard Borel, and the spaces of its finite counting measures, hence all spaces carrying these restrictions, are standard Borel.  Conditional on the bits, \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) are independent, because restrictions of a Poisson point process to disjoint Borel sets are independent.  The locality conclusions of the packing lemma state that the one-observation law on \(C_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), whereas its law on the complement of the union of the cells is bit-independent.  Therefore the law of \(Z_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), and the law of \(Z_0\) is independent of all bits.

Let \(S_j\) retain from \(Z_j\) only each point's mark, outcome, and distance from \(x_j\).  At \(x_j\), the rule \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\) is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\): the full restrictions outside \(C_j\) reveal their marks, outcomes, and distances from \(x_j\), while \(S_j\) supplies exactly the information about points inside \(C_j\) permitted by \(V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\).  Sorting all revealed marks reconstructs the thinned ordered sample to which \(T_n\) is applied.

We next verify the relative-entropy hypothesis for this marked-cell experiment.  By the packing lemma, adjacent laws are identical off \(C_j\), their common cell mass is \(m_n\), and every support point at distance less than \(w_n\) from \(x_j\) belongs to \(C_j\).  Let \(\nu_{j,b}\) be the subprobability law of one outcome--distance pair, restricted to \(C_j\), under bit value \(b\in\{0,1\}\).  Its mass is \(m_n\) for both bits.  The corresponding full one-observation compressed laws are
\[
 \nu_{j,b}+\lambda_j,
\]
where \(\lambda_j\) is common to the two bits and is supported on radii at least \(w_n\), whereas \(\nu_{j,b}\) is supported on radii below \(w_n\), up to null boundaries.  Thus, writing
\[
 D_j=\int\log\!\left(\frac{d\nu_{j,0}}{d\nu_{j,1}}\right)d\nu_{j,0},
\]
the disjoint-support decomposition and additivity of relative entropy for independent products give
\[
 nD_j
 =\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
 \mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
 \right)
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
The displayed equality is unaffected by the common component \(\lambda_j\), and finiteness of the right-hand side supplies the required absolute continuity.  The packing lemma gives the same bound after reversing the adjacent orientation.

Let \(Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)\).  Under either bit, the cell count is \(K_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2nm_n)\), so its law contributes zero relative entropy.  Conditional on \(K_j=k\), the compressed cell points are independent with law \(\nu_{j,b}/m_n\), and the independent marks again contribute zero.  Since the two subprobabilities have the same mass,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\nu_{j,0}/m_n,\nu_{j,1}/m_n)=\frac{D_j}{m_n}.
\]
Conditioning on the common count and taking its expectation therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
 =\mathbb E[K_j]\frac{D_j}{m_n}
 =2nD_j
 \leq2\alpha\log M.
\]
Because \(2\alpha<1\), \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\), with \(\kappa=2\alpha\), applies.

At each \(x_j\), decode \(\Omega_j\) by thresholding \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\) at the midpoint of \(u_{j,0}\) and \(u_{j,1}\), reversing the inequality if \(u_{j,1}<u_{j,0}\).  The direct-product lemma and \(M_n\to\infty\) imply
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one coordinate is decoded incorrectly}\}
 \geq\frac12-o(1).
\]
If the uniform boundary loss is less than \(\Delta/2\), every midpoint decoder is correct.  Averaging over the uniform bit prior and then using that a maximum dominates an average gives
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{2}\left\{\frac12-o(1)\right\}
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), where \(c>0\) depends only on \(q\) and \(L\).

It remains to return to the fixed-\(n\) experiment.  Every packing regression takes values in \([1/4,3/4]\), so the loss of the zero default on \(\{N<n\}\) is at most one.  On \(\{N\geq n\}\), the selected data have law \(P_\omega^{\otimes n}\) and are processed by the original common map \(T_n\).  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 +\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-tN}\), optimized at \(t=\log2\), gives
\[
 \Pr\{N<n\}
 \leq\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}
 =o(a_n).
\]
Combining the preceding displays and decreasing \(c\) if necessary proves, uniformly over the arbitrary rule \(\widehat\mu_n\),
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

The packing lemma verifies explicitly the independent-sampling, compact-support, density-positivity, rectifiable-boundary, H\"older-regression, and continuous-variance conditions required for \(P_\omega\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\).  Hence the supremum over the full class is no smaller than the supremum over this finite family.  Taking the infimum over the inherited law-independent common-map class \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) gives
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Finally, \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), while \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}distance\text{-}risk}\) identifies the expanded infimum--supremum display with \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).  Dividing by \(a_n\) and taking lower limits proves the claimed result with \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}=c\).  The whole finite family lies in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), so this is a same-class converse and uses no treatment-effect or two-sided-thickness subclass.
