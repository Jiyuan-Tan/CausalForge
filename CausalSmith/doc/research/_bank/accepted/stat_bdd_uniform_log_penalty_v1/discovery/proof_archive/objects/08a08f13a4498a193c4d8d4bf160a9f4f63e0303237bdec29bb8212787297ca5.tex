For \(L>4\), the canonical-square version of the angular packing lies in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\), so the direct-product argument gives the distance lower bound \(ca_n\); the local-constant calculation gives the matching upper bound.  For full-location data, a degree-\((q-1)\) bivariate local-polynomial estimator with bandwidth \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\) has uniform bias \(O(h^q)\) and stochastic term \(O(\sqrt{\log n/(nh^2)})\), hence risk \(O(s_{n,q})\).  Conversely, put disjoint two-dimensional \(q\)-H\"older bumps of width \(h\) and height \(h^q\) along the trimmed interface.  There are order \(h^{-1}\) sites, adjacent product divergence is \(O(nh^{2q+2})=O(\log n)\), and the ordinary Fano argument gives a sup-norm lower bound of order \(h^q=s_{n,q}\).

If \(L=4\), the fixed support has area four and the density envelope gives \(1=\int f_P\geq4/4=1\).  Equality forces \(f_P=1/4\) almost everywhere and continuity makes this pointwise.  For the straight geometry, angular averaging against the constant density turns the exact-distance arm regression into the semicircular average of the bivariate regression.  The preceding local-polynomial upper construction and bump lower construction then apply in the one-to-one radialized experiment with the same bias \(h^q\) and effective local mass \(h^2\), yielding \(R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}\asymp s_{n,q}\).  Comparing \(q/(2q+2)\) with \(1/4\) gives the stated phase comparison.
