We prove the upper, lower, and nonexistence assertions separately.

For the upper bound, use the basis \(v_m(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^{m-1})^\top\) and the fixed triangular kernel on each signed arm.  After scaling by \(h^{-2}\), the population Gram matrix is
\[
 G_{t,P,x}(h)=\int_0^1K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top\frac{Q_{t,P,x}(hu)}{hu}\,u\,du.
\]
For every nonzero vector \(a\), the lower radial-density inequality gives
\[
 a^\top G_{t,P,x}(h)a
 \geq\underline c\int_0^1K_\triangle(u)u\{a^\top v_m(u)\}^2\,du>0.
\]
The last integral is positive because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an interval.  Compactness of the unit sphere in the fixed dimension \(m\) therefore supplies a uniform positive eigenvalue; the upper inequality supplies a uniform upper eigenvalue.  Thus every inverse used by the local-polynomial intercept exists for all sufficiently small \(h\), uniformly over the class.

Insert the displayed expansion of \(N/Q\) into the population normal equations.  Polynomial reproduction cancels the terms of degrees zero through \(m-1\), leaving intercept bias at most \(C h^m\).  The conditional sub-Gaussian moment-generating bound and a polynomial mesh of the compact score square give, exactly as in the rectifiable proof but for the fixed vector basis,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau_{n,h}^{(m-1)}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
The estimator is coordinatewise because its value at \(x\) uses only \((Y_i,D_{P,x,i})_{i=1}^n\).  Balancing the two terms gives
\(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2m+2)}\) and the asserted exponent.

For the converse on a rich class, take uniform density on a straight interface and \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) disjoint bumps along the boundary, of spatial width \(h\) and height \(h^m\).  The regressions satisfy the order-\(m\) H\"older envelope, the target curves are separated by \(ch^m\), and the product relative entropy from the center is
\[
 Cn(h^m)^2h^2=Cnh^{2m+2}\leq\alpha\log M
\]
at the balanced bandwidth after reducing the bump constant.  The entropy calculation displayed in the proof of the straight-information theorem gives a testing error bounded away from zero, so the minimax risk is at least \(ch^m\).  This matches the upper bound.

For the first-order alternative, the narrowed angular packing and its exact-recovery reduction give \(ca_n\).  Its densities are continuous but possess no common shrinking-scale angular modulus, and its normalized integral has an uncontrolled first-order coefficient.  This is precisely the failure of every uniform remainder with \(m\geq2\).

Finally, density regularity alone cannot be the weakest threshold.  Fix any continuous density with nonregular normalized first angular moment at a boundary point.  If \(\mu_{t,P}\) is constant, then \(N/Q=\mu_{t,P}(x)\) identically and it has remainders of every order, irrespective of that density.  With the same density and an affine regression having nonzero tangential component, polar coordinates give
\[
 \frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 =s\,\frac{\int \langle\beta,e_\theta\rangle f_P(x+se_\theta)\,d\theta}{\int f_P(x+se_\theta)\,d\theta},
\]
whose coefficient inherits the absence of a uniform angular modulus.  Hence the same density can be harmless for one regression class and rate-limiting for another.  The joint normalized coarea remainder is therefore the minimal operational condition, and the theorem determines the rate on each order-\(m\) remainder class without assuming the desired exponent.
