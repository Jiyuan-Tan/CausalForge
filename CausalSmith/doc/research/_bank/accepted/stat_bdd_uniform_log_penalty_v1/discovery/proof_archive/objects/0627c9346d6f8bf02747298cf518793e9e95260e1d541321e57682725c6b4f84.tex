Put \(q=p+1\geq1\).  Fix smooth functions \(\phi,\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\) such that \(\phi=1\) near zero, \(\phi=0\) on \([1,\infty)\), all transition endpoints are flat, \(\eta(u)=0\) for \(u\leq1\), and \(\eta(u)=1\) for \(u\geq2\).  Choose a small slope \(a>0\), then a cutoff constant \(C\) so large that \(2/(aC)\leq1/4\), and finally a smoothness constant \(A\) so large that all derivative bounds below hold and \(A>2C\).  Set
\[
 w=A\Delta^{1/q}.
\]
Place \(x_j=(s_j,0)\) on \([-1/2,1/2]\times\{0\}\) with separation at least \(4w\).  Then
\[
 M\asymp w^{-1}\geq c\Delta^{-1/q},
\]
and the disks \(S_j=B_2(x_j,w)\) are disjoint, lie strictly inside \(\mathcal X=[-3,3]^2\), and avoid the corners of \(\mathcal B=\partial([-1,1]\times[0,2])\).

For \(r>0\), define
\[
 b_{\Delta,w}(r)={2\Delta\over ar}\phi(r/w)\eta(r/(C\Delta)),
 \qquad b_{\Delta,w}(0)=0.
\]
For \(x\in S_j\), write \(x-x_j=r_j(\cos\alpha_j,\sin\alpha_j)\), interpreting each summand as zero outside its disk, and put
\[
 f_\omega(x)={1\over36}\left[1+\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_jb_{\Delta,w}(r_j)\cos\alpha_j\right],
\]
\[
 \mu_{0,\omega}(x)={1\over2},
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)={1\over2}+ax_1-\Delta\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j\phi(r_j/w).
\]
Conditional on \(X=x\), take \(Y(0)\) and \(Y(1)\) conditionally independent Bernoulli variables with these means, and impose the fixed deterministic assignment and consistency equations.

The density perturbation vanishes for \(r\leq C\Delta\) and smoothly before \(r=w\).  The choice \(A>2C\) ensures \(2C\Delta<w\) also when \(q=1\), because then \(w=A\Delta\).  Whenever the perturbation is nonzero,
\[
 |b_{\Delta,w}(r)|\leq {2\over aC}\leq{1\over4}.
\]
Disjointness gives \(1/48\leq f_\omega\leq5/144\).  On every disk,
\[
 \int_0^w b_{\Delta,w}(r)r\,dr\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\alpha\,d\alpha=0,
\]
so \(\int_{\mathcal X}f_\omega=1\).  Thus the density is continuous, normalized, positive on the entire square, and satisfies the envelope for \(L\geq48\).

For every derivative order \(0\leq k\leq q\), scaling and disjointness yield
\[
 \left\|D^k\left[\Delta\sum_j\omega_j
 \phi(\|\,\cdot-x_j\|_2/w)\right]\right\|_\infty
 \leq C_k\Delta w^{-k}=C_kA^{-k}\Delta^{1-k/q}.
\]
At \(k=q\) this is \(C_qA^{-q}\), and at smaller orders it vanishes with \(\Delta\).  This proves the derivative and Lipschitz bounds through order \(p\).  In the endpoint \(p=0,q=1\), the gradient is bounded by \(C_1/A\), so the regression itself has the required uniform Lipschitz constant; no higher-order condition is being used.  Taking \(a\) and then \(\Delta\) small places both Bernoulli probabilities in \([1/4,3/4]\).  Their variances lie in \([3/16,1/4]\), and their raw conditional \((2+\nu)\)-moments are at most one.  Smooth extension holds because the disks are strictly interior and the affine part is global.

The assignment rectangle has perimeter eight and distance at least one from the support boundary.  At an edge or corner, for sufficiently small \(h\), each arm contains a sector with opening angle at least \(\pi/2\); hence
\[
 {1\over h^2}\int_{\mathcal A_t}K_\square(\{(2t-1)\|z-x\|_2\}/h)\,dz\geq{\pi\over8}.
\]
The radius-\(s\) arc length lies between \((\pi/2)s\) and \(2\pi s\), proving finite positive radial Hausdorff integrals.  For \(v\in\mathbb R^{p+1}\), polar coordinates in such a sector and the density lower bound give
\[
 v^\top\Psi_{t,P_\omega,x}(h)v
 \geq c\int_0^1\{v^\top r_p((2t-1)u)\}^2u\,du.
\]
The integral is positive for \(v\ne0\), including the one-dimensional constant-polynomial case \(p=0\).  Compactness of the unit sphere gives a positive uniform minimum.  Enlarging \(L_0(p)\) verifies the Gram bound.  The Euclidean-ball VC clause follows from \(\mathrm{lem:euclidean\text{-}balls\text{-}vc}\).  Thus every displayed A1/A2 and Theorem-2-envelope clause holds.

Angular integration gives
\[
 P_\omega(X\in S_j)={|S_j|\over36}={\pi w^2\over36}=\rho.
\]
Disjoint support gives bit locality.  At \(x_j\),
\[
 \tau_{P_\omega}(x_j)=as_j-\Delta\omega_j,
\]
so adjacent targets differ by \(\Delta\).

Inside \(S_j\), positive signed radius is the upper semicircle.  Using
\[
 \int_0^\pi\cos\alpha\,d\alpha=0,
 \qquad \int_0^\pi\cos^2\alpha\,d\alpha={\pi\over2},
\]
the signed-radius marginal is bit-independent.  On the treatment half, angular averaging gives
\[
 q_{1,j}(r)-q_{0,j}(r)
 =-\Delta\phi(r/w)\{1-\eta(r/(C\Delta))\}.
\]
Thus the conditional laws agree outside \(0<r<2C\Delta\), an interval contained in \((0,w)\) by the choice of \(A\).  Since all Bernoulli probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\), in either orientation
\[
 \operatorname{kl}(u,v)\leq{(u-v)^2\over v(1-v)}\leq C_1\Delta^2.
\]
The conditional signed-radius density is \(r/w^2\), so disintegration yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
 \leq C_1\Delta^2\int_0^{2C\Delta}{r\over w^2}\,dr
 \leq C_0{\Delta^4\over w^2}.
\]
All constants are uniform in the asserted indices.  This proves the hypercube lemma for every \(p\geq0\).
