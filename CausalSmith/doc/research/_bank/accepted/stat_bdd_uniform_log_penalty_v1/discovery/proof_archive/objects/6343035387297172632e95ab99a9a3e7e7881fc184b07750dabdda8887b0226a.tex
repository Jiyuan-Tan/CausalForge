Define the feature map
\[
 \Phi(z)=(1,z_1,z_2,\lVert z\rVert_2^2)\in\mathbb R^4.
\]
For a center \(x\in\mathbb R^2\) and radius \(r\geq0\),
\[
 \lVert z-x\rVert_2\leq r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (\lVert x\rVert_2^2-r^2,-2x_1,-2x_2,1)^\top\Phi(z)\leq0.
\]
Thus every Euclidean ball is the inverse image under \(\Phi\) of a homogeneous halfspace in \(\mathbb R^4\).

We give the elementary finite-dimensional VC argument.  Take five feature vectors \(v_i=\Phi(z_i)\).  They are linearly dependent, so there are coefficients \(c_i\), not all zero, with \(\sum_i c_i v_i=0\).  Since the first coordinate of every \(v_i\) is one, \(\sum_i c_i=0\); after discarding zero coefficients, both signs occur.  Label the indices with \(c_i>0\) inside and those with \(c_i<0\) outside.  If a homogeneous halfspace realized this labeling, its defining linear functional \(\ell\) would satisfy \(\ell(v_i)\leq0\) for \(c_i>0\) and \(\ell(v_i)>0\) for \(c_i<0\).  Hence \(\sum_i c_i\ell(v_i)<0\), contradicting
\[
 \sum_i c_i\ell(v_i)=\ell\!\left(\sum_i c_i v_i\right)=0.
\]
No five points can therefore be shattered.  Restricting balls to any support \(\mathcal X_P\) cannot increase VC dimension.  This proves the claimed uniform VC index and exactly verifies the uniform-kernel clause used in the class definition.
