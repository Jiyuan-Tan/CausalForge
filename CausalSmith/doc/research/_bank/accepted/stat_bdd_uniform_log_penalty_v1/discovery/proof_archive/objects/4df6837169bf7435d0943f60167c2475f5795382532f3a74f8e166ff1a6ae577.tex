We construct the family and verify all class, observed-arm, separation, and
information properties.  Begin with
\(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\), the lower and upper half-square assignment regions, and
the interface
\[
 \mathcal B=\{(u-1/2,0):0\leq u\leq1\},
 \qquad \gamma(u)=(u-1/2,0).
\]
The base density is one.  The affine chart is injective and smooth of every
order.  At an endpoint, either observed arm contains a quarter disk.  All
densities constructed below are at least \(3/4\), so, for
\(0<r\leq r_0\leq1/4\),
\[
 P\{T=t,\ X\in B_2(x,r)\}\geq\frac{3\pi}{16}r^2
 \geq c_-r^2.
\]
Thus the repaired thickness property holds for the actual treatment arms.

Choose a radial function
\(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\) with
\(0\leq\phi\leq1\) and \(\phi(0)=1\), and put
\[
 \Delta=d a_n,\qquad w=(d_1\Delta)^{1/q}.
\]
Pack the trimmed interface with centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) separated by at
least \(3w\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), their radius-\(w\) disks are
disjoint and remain away from the endpoints, and elementary interval packing
gives
\[
 M\geq c w^{-1}\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}.
\]
Write \(\phi_j(z)=\phi((z-x_j)/w)\).  For \(0\leq k\leq q\),
\[
 \lVert D^k(\Delta\phi_j)\rVert_\infty
 \leq C_\phi\Delta w^{-k}\leq C_\phi d_1^{-1}.
\]
Disjoint supports give the same bound for every signed sum.  Choosing
\(d_1\) large and the affine slope \(b>0\) small puts all regressions below in
the fixed \(q\)-H\"older ball.

For \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\), let
\[
 p_{0,\omega}(z)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 p_{1,\omega}(z)=\frac12+bz_1+Delta\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j\phi_j(z).
\]
After decreasing \(b\) and \(d\), these functions lie in
\([1/4,3/4]\).  Conditional on \(X=z\), take independent
\[
 Y(t)=B_t+\varepsilon_t,qquad
 B_t\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_{t,\omega}(z)),qquad
 \varepsilon_t\sim N(0,1).
\]
Then \(\mu_{t,P_\omega}=p_{t,\omega}\), while
\(1\leq\sigma^2_{t,P_\omega}\leq5/4\).  Hoeffding's lemma and the Gaussian
moment-generating identity yield
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 e^{\lambda\{Y(t)-p_{t,\omega}(X)\}}\mid X\right]
 \leq e^{\lambda^2/8}e^{\lambda^2/2}
 \leq e^{L^2\lambda^2/2},
\]
so the common variance and tail envelopes hold.

To hide the bump at its center, choose a continuous cutoff \(\chi\) satisfying
\(\chi(v)=0\) for \(v\leq1\) and \(\chi(v)=1\) for \(v\geq2\), and set
\[
 A_n(r)=-\frac{2\Delta\phi(r/w)}{br}
 \chi\!\left(\frac{br}{8\Delta}\right).
\]
There is no division at zero because the cutoff vanishes on a neighborhood of
zero.  On its active region \(br\geq8\Delta\),
\(|A_n(r)|\leq1/4\), after increasing the fixed cutoff constant if necessary.
Smooth compact support makes the correction continuous at its outer edge.  Put
\[
 f_\omega(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j
 A_n(\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2)
 \frac{z_1-(x_j)_1}{\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2}
 \mathbf1\{z\in B_2(x_j,w)\},
\]
with each summand set to zero at its center.  Disjointness makes the expression
unambiguous.  Each angular correction integrates to zero on every full circle,
so \(\int_Sf_\omega=1\).  Moreover \(f_\omega\) is continuous and lies in
\([3/4,5/4]\subseteq[L^{-1},L]\).  Compact support, the assignment partition,
absolute continuity, and the observed-arm local-mass conditions now hold
uniformly over the hypercube.  Set
\(T=\mathbf1\{X\in\mathcal E_{1,P_\omega}\}\), set
\(Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\), and sample the resulting observed units independently;
this verifies deterministic assignment, consistency, and iid sampling for every
vertex.

Compare adjacent vertices \(\omega\) and \(\omega^{(j)}\), oriented so that
\(\omega_j=1\).  On either observed half-circle centered at \(x_j\),
\(\int\cos\theta\,d\theta=0\); hence the two observed-arm radial mass
densities agree at every radius.  On the treated upper half-circle, whenever
\(br\geq16\Delta\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\int_0^\pi p_{1,\omega}(x_j+re_\theta)
 \{1+A_n(r)\cos\theta\}\,d\theta
 -\int_0^\pi p_{1,\omega^{(j)}}(x_j+re_\theta)\,d\theta\\
 &\qquad=\pi\Delta\phi(r/w)+\frac\pi2brA_n(r)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The control equality follows from its constant regression.  A Bernoulli
variable followed by the common Gaussian channel is determined, after angular
mixing at a fixed radius, by the averaged Bernoulli probability.  Therefore the
entire observed radial laws agree outside \(r\leq16\Delta/b\).

Inside that radius the common radial density is at most \(Cr\), and the
radially averaged Bernoulli probabilities differ by at most \(C\Delta\) while
remaining in \([1/4,3/4]\).  The elementary bounds
\[
 \operatorname{kl}(p,p')\leq
 \frac{(p-p')^2}{p'(1-p')},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{KL}(B+\varepsilon,B'+\varepsilon)
 \leq\operatorname{kl}(p,p')
\]
give, by data processing through the Gaussian channel,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P^{\mathrm{dist}}_{\omega,x_j},
 P^{\mathrm{dist}}_{\omega^{(j)},x_j})
 \leq C\int_0^{16\Delta/b}\Delta^2r\,dr
 \leq C'\Delta^4.
\]
Tensorization yields \(C'n\Delta^4=C'd^4\log n\), whereas
\[
 \log M=\left(\frac1{4q}+o(1)\right)\log n.
\]
Choosing \(d\) small gives the claimed bound with a fixed
\(\alpha<1/8\), retaining enough slack for Poissonization at mean \(2n\).
At \(x_j\), adjacent target curves differ by
\(\Delta\phi_j(x_j)=da_n\), proving the separation with \(c_1=d\).

For the canonical square \([-1,1]^2\), use
\(\gamma(u)=(2u-1,0)\), base density \(1/4\), and the same construction in
relative-density form.  When \(L>4\), set
\(\varepsilon_L=1-4/L>0\), enlarge the cutoff constant to depend on \(L\),
and choose \(d_1\) large and then \(d/b\) small.  The relative perturbation is
then at most \(\varepsilon_L/2\), so
\[
 L^{-1}\leq\frac14(1-\varepsilon_L/2)
 \leq f_\omega\leq\frac14(1+\varepsilon_L/2)\leq L.
\]
All cancellation and divergence calculations change only by fixed factors.
Thus the family lies in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\).  At \(L=4\),
normalization and the lower envelope force the density to be \(1/4\), so the
nonzero angular perturbation is unavailable; the statement makes no such claim
in that case.
