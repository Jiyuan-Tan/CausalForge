All constants below may depend on \(m,q,\iota,L,\delta,A,r_*,\underline c,\overline c\), and the common coefficient bound, but not on \(n\), \(P\), \(t\), or \(x\).  We take the cutoff input suppressed in \(\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}\) to be \(c=\underline c\), and take \(h\leq r_*\).

First, the lower radial inequality gives
\[
 \int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds\geq\frac{\underline c}{2}r^2>0,
 \qquad 0<r\leq r_*.
\]
It also gives \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(s\in(0,r_*]\), so \(\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\) equals that interval modulo a null set.  If \(B\) is the common coefficient bound, then the remainder assumption implies
\[
 |g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|
 \leq B\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}s^k+As^m\longrightarrow0
\]
for almost every such \(s\downarrow0\).  The radial-coarea identification lemma therefore proves the separate essential limits and
\[
 \ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P}(x)-\ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
\]

We next prove the uniform upper bound.  Put \(v(u)=v_m(u)\), \(G=G_m\), and \(S_x=\lVert X-x\rVert_2\).  The arm-specific population matrices and vectors corresponding to the estimator are
\[
 \overline G_{t,P,x}(h)
 =\frac1{h^2}\int_0^hK_\triangle(s/h)v(s/h)v(s/h)^\top
 Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds
\]
and
\[
 \overline r_{t,P,x}(h)
 =\frac1{h^2}\int_{\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\cap(0,h)}
 K_\triangle(s/h)v(s/h)
 g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
The assumed radial lower bound gives \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(s\in(0,h)\), so the complement of \(\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\cap(0,h)\) in \((0,h)\) is Lebesgue-null.  By the Radon--Nikodym identities defining \(N_{t,P,x}\) and \(Q_{t,P,x}\), the restricted integral is therefore exactly the population outcome vector corresponding to the estimator.  In particular, no value of the partial regression is used outside \(\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\).  Changing variables \(s=hu\) and using the almost-everywhere radial bounds gives, in Loewner order,
\[
 \underline cG\preceq\overline G_{t,P,x}(h)
 \preceq\overline cG.
\]
The matrix \(G\) is positive definite: if \(a\neq0\), the polynomial \(a^\top v(u)\) is not zero almost everywhere on \((0,1)\), and hence
\[
 a^\top Ga=\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)\{a^\top v(u)\}^2\,du>0.
\]
Thus \(\lambda_{\min}(\overline G_{t,P,x}(h))\geq\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G)\), exactly the population margin used by the declared cutoff.

Define
\[
 \beta_{t,P,x}(h)
 =\bigl(\mu_{t,P}(x),b_{t,1,P}(x)h,\ldots,
 b_{t,m-1,P}(x)h^{m-1}\bigr)^\top.
\]
The polynomial part of the radial expansion equals \(v(s/h)^\top\beta_{t,P,x}(h)\).  Consequently,
\[
 \overline r_{t,P,x}(h)
 =\overline G_{t,P,x}(h)\beta_{t,P,x}(h)+e_{t,P,x}(h),
\]
where, since \(\lVert v(u)\rVert_2\leq\sqrt m\) on \([0,1]\),
\[
 \lVert e_{t,P,x}(h)\rVert_2
 \leq\frac{A\overline c\sqrt m}{h^2}
 \int_0^h s^{m+1}\,ds
 \leq C h^m.
\]
It follows that the population fitted intercept obeys
\[
 \left|e_1^\top\overline G_{t,P,x}(h)^{-1}
 \overline r_{t,P,x}(h)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \leq Ch^m.
\]
This calculation uses only almost-everywhere radial identities; changing a Radon--Nikodym representative on a null set changes none of the integrals.

For completeness, we derive the stochastic order.  For a fixed \(t,x\), each entry of the empirical Gram summand is bounded by \(Ch^{-2}\), and the upper radial inequality gives
\[
 P\{T=t,\ S_x\leq h\}=\int_0^hQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds
 \leq\frac{\overline c}{2}h^2.
\]
Hence its variance is at most \(Ch^{-2}\).  Conditional sub-Gaussianity, consistency, and the H\"older-ball supremum bound similarly imply, for every integer \(k\geq2\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left|
 h^{-2}\mathbf1\{T=t\}K_\triangle(S_x/h)v_j(S_x/h)Y
 \right|^k
 \leq k!C^kh^{2-2k}.
\]
Centering changes only the constant.  Expanding the exponential series and applying exponential Markov inequality therefore gives, for every scalar Gram or outcome coordinate \(Z_{t,x,h}\),
\[
 P\!\left(\left|(\mathbb P_n-P)Z_{t,x,h}\right|>u\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{cnu^2}{h^{-2}+h^{-2}u}\right\}.
\]

The smooth embedded boundary is contained in \([-L,L]^2\).  For \(\epsilon_n=n^{-4}\), it has an \(\epsilon_n\)-net of cardinality at most \(C_Ln^8\), obtained from the ambient square.  A \(C^1\) image of a compact interval is planar-null, so score absolute continuity implies that, with probability one, no sample score lies on \(\mathcal B_P\).  Therefore the strict sign in the estimator equals \(\mathbf1\{T_i=t\}\) simultaneously for every boundary point and sample index.  On \([0,1]\), the finitely many functions
\[
 u\longmapsto K_\triangle(u)v_j(u),
 \qquad
 u\longmapsto K_\triangle(u)v_j(u)v_k(u)
\]
are Lipschitz.  Reverse triangle inequality consequently bounds empirical oscillations between a boundary point and its net point by
\[
 C_m\epsilon_nh^{-3}
 \quad\text{for Gram entries},
 \qquad
 C_m\epsilon_nh^{-3}\mathbb P_n|Y|
 \quad\text{for outcome coordinates},
\]
with the same population bounds.  The conditional tail envelope gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P(\mathbb P_n|Y|)^2\leq C,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\max_{i\leq n}|Y_i|^2\leq C\log n.
\]
A union bound over the fixed matrix dimension, the two arms, and the polynomial net shows that, for every fixed \(A_0>0\), an event \(\mathcal G_n\) of probability at least \(1-Cn^{-A_0}\) exists on which
\[
 \sup_{P,t,x}\left\{
 \lVert\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-\overline G_{t,P,x}(h)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\lVert\widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-\overline r_{t,P,x}(h)\rVert_2
 \right\}
 \leq C\eta_n(h),
\]
where
\[
 \eta_n(h)=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+\frac{\log n}{nh^2}.
\]
Here and below the supremum notation means that the same constants and probability bound hold uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal C\), not that samples under different laws share one probability space.

For \(h=h_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2m+2)}\), \(\eta_n(h_n)\to0\).  Weyl's eigenvalue inequality and the population margin imply on \(\mathcal G_n\), for all large \(n\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h_n))
 \geq\frac12\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G).
\]
Thus every cutoff is active and every empirical inverse is uniformly bounded.  The coefficient vector \(\beta_{t,P,x}(h_n)\) is uniformly bounded, so the inverse identity
\[
 \widehat G^{-1}-\overline G^{-1}
 =\widehat G^{-1}(\overline G-\widehat G)\overline G^{-1}
\]
and the preceding population bias yield
\[
 \sup_{t,x}
 |\widehat\mu^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{t,h_n,\underline c}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|
 \leq C\{h_n^m+\eta_n(h_n)\}
\]
on \(\mathcal G_n\).  On its complement, the admissibility lemma gives
\[
 |\widehat\mu^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{t,h_n,\underline c}(x)|
 \leq Ch_n^{-2}\mathbb P_n|Y|,
\]
with zero on the failed-cutoff branch.  Cauchy--Schwarz and the arbitrarily large polynomial exponent \(A_0\) make the exceptional expected contribution \(o(h_n^m)\).  The admissibility lemma also proves that the arm difference belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).  Since
\[
 h_n^m\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)},
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh_n^2}}\asymp h_n^m,
 \qquad
 \frac{\log n}{nh_n^2}\asymp h_n^{2m},
\]
we obtain
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right]
 \leq C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]
Taking the infimum over the distance decision class proves the asserted risk upper bound.

We now verify the conditional converse.  Work on the uniform square \([-1/2,1/2]^2\) with the horizontal straight interface, and choose \(M\asymp h_n^{-1}\) trimmed centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) whose radius-\(h_n\) disks are disjoint.  Let \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\) be radial with \(\phi(0)=1\), put \(\Delta_n=\eta h_n^m\), and keep the control regression zero.  Under the baseline the treated regression is zero, while under alternative \(j\) it is
\[
 \mu_{1,j}(z)=\Delta_n\phi((z-x_j)/h_n).
\]
Take independent standard Gaussian errors in both potential outcomes.  For every derivative order \(k\leq q\),
\[
 \lVert D^k\mu_{1,j}\rVert_\infty
 \leq C\eta h_n^{m-k}\leq C\eta
\]
because \(m\geq q\) and \(h_n\leq1\).  Thus sufficiently small fixed \(\eta\) puts every law in the ambient \(q\)-H\"older and conditional sub-Gaussian class.  In fact the scaled bumps have all derivatives through order \(m\) uniformly bounded, so Taylor's formula and half-circle averaging verify the order-\(m\) normalized-coarea remainder uniformly at every trimmed boundary point.  Uniform design gives \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\pi s\) for all sufficiently small relevant radii.  Hence these are precisely valid contained bump laws of the kind stipulated by the theorem; the extra containment hypothesis places them in \(\mathcal C\).

For the observed full sample, Gaussian relative entropy and the area of a bump support give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =\frac n2\mathbb E\!\left[
 \mathbf1\{T=1\}\mu_{1,j}(X)^2\right]
 \leq Cn\Delta_n^2h_n^2
 =C\eta^2nh_n^{2m+2}.
\]
Now \(nh_n^{2m+2}\asymp\log n\) and \(\log M\sim(2m+2)^{-1}\log n\).  Decreasing \(\eta\) makes the last divergence at most \(\alpha\log M\) for a fixed \(\alpha<1/8\).  If \(J\) is uniform on \(\{0,1,\ldots,M\}\), the log-sum inequality gives
\[
 I(J;O_{1:n})
 \leq\frac1{M+1}\sum_{j=1}^M
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
For any decoder \(\widehat J\), writing \(E=\mathbf1\{\widehat J\neq J\}\) and expanding conditional entropy gives
\[
 H(J\mid O_{1:n})\leq H(E)+P(E)\log M
 \leq\log2+P(E)\log M.
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log(M+1)\), the preceding two displays bound \(P(E)\) away from zero.  Uniform estimation error below \(\Delta_n/3\) identifies whether there is no bump or which unique center carries the bump.  Therefore even the full-location minimax risk, and hence the distance-only risk, is at least
\[
 c\Delta_n
 =c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]
This proves exactness under the stated containment and \(m\geq q\).  When either condition is absent, this construction supplies no same-class converse, exactly as the theorem records.

Independently, suppose a law class contains the shrinking angular-imbalance hypercube from \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{ang}}\).  Its separated centers, adjacent compressed divergences, and target gap are those of the angular-density packing lemma.  Given an arbitrary fixed-\(n\) coordinatewise rule, first induce a marked-Poisson rule by retaining the \(n\) smallest-mark observations on \(\{N\geq n\}\), applying the fixed-\(n\) rule there, and using the zero default otherwise.  The retained observations are independent with the original law.  The disjoint bump-cell restrictions of the marked Poisson process are conditionally independent, and midpoint thresholding the induced rule gives the decentralized decoders of the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma.  The packing slack keeps their Poisson divergences below \(2\alpha\log M_n\) with \(2\alpha<1\), so the probability of at least one error is at least \(1/2-o(1)\).  Error below half the target separation would decode every bit.  Since \(P\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}\leq e^{-cn}\) and the zero-default target loss is uniformly bounded on the hard family, the induced Poisson risk is at most the fixed-\(n\) risk plus \(O(e^{-cn})\).  Thus that class has distance lower frontier \(ca_n\).  For \(m=1\), the upper exponent is \(1/4\); for \(m\geq2\), the displayed conditional upper exponent is strictly larger.  The converse statements remain limited to the two explicitly contained hard families.

It remains to prove the density example.  Fix \(0<\alpha<1\), take the square \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\) with horizontal interface through the origin, and define
\[
 f(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 1+\lVert z\rVert_2^{\alpha-1}z_1,&z\neq0,\\
 1,&z=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Because \(|\lVert z\rVert_2^{\alpha-1}z_1|\leq\lVert z\rVert_2^\alpha<1\) on \(S\), this density is bounded, positive, and continuous.  Its second term is odd in \(z_1\), so its integral over \(S\) is zero; since \(S\) has area one, \(f\) integrates to one.  On a treated half-circle of radius \(0<s<1/2\), write \(z=s(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\), \(0\leq\theta\leq\pi\).  Then
\[
 f(z)=1+s^\alpha\cos\theta,
 \qquad
 Q_{1,P,0}(s)=s\int_0^\pi(1+s^\alpha\cos\theta)\,d\theta=\pi s.
\]
For a constant regression \(\mu(z)=c\), the radial ratio is identically \(c\).  For the affine regression \(\mu(z)=z_1\), it is instead
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P,0}(s)
 &=\frac{s\int_0^\pi s\cos\theta
 (1+s^\alpha\cos\theta)\,d\theta}{\pi s}\\
 &=\frac{s^{1+\alpha}}2.
\end{aligned}
\]
If an order-two expansion existed, some constant \(b_1\) and finite \(A\) would satisfy
\[
 \left|\frac{s^{1+\alpha}}2-b_1s\right|\leq As^2
\]
for almost every sufficiently small \(s\).  Dividing first by \(s\) and sending \(s\downarrow0\) forces \(b_1=0\); dividing then by \(s^2\) would require \(s^{\alpha-1}/2\leq A\), which is impossible because \(\alpha<1\).  Thus the same bounded positive continuous density supports a constant-regression example with an exact constant ratio and an affine-regression example that fails the order-two remainder.  This proves the final claim without asserting any universal weakest density threshold.
