Choose dominating measures and write \(q_{j,0},q_{j,1}\) for the two densities.  Their common submeasure has density \(q_{j,0}\wedge q_{j,1}\) and mass
\[
\rho_j=\int(q_{j,0}\wedge q_{j,1})=1-\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Thus the binary experiment can be generated by first drawing an ambiguity indicator \(A_j\), independent of \(\Omega_j\), with \(\Pr(A_j=1)=\rho_j\).  On \(A_j=1\), draw \(S_j\) from the normalized common submeasure, independently of \(\Omega_j\); on \(A_j=0\), draw it from the appropriate normalized residual measure.  Use independent indicators and random seeds across \(j\).  Conditional laws for the uncompressed parts of \(Z_j\) may then be generated by regular conditional kernels.  This representation preserves the original experiment, and it allows those uncompressed parts to reveal arbitrary information to decoders other than \(j\).

Fix all ambiguity indicators, all non-bit random seeds, and the bits outside the ambiguous coordinate set \(A=\{j:A_j=1\}\).  If \(A\neq\varnothing\), consider the set of configurations of \((\Omega_j:j\in A)\) on which every decoder is correct.  Two configurations differing only in coordinate \(j\in A\) cannot both belong to this set: decoder \(j\) sees the same \(S_j\), the same \(Z_{-j}\), and the same ancillary randomization at the two endpoints, so its output is unchanged while the correct bit is reversed.  The successful configurations therefore form an independent set in the \(|A|\)-dimensional hypercube.  Pairing vertices by any one coordinate, or equivalently using the bipartition by parity, shows that such a set has at most \(2^{|A|-1}\) elements.  Since the ambiguous bits remain uniform, conditional exact-success probability is at most \(1/2\).  If \(A=\varnothing\), it is at most one.  Averaging gives
\[
\Pr\{\text{exact success}\}
\leq\Pr(A=\varnothing)+\frac12\Pr(A\neq\varnothing)
=\frac12\left\{1+\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)\right\}.
\]

For the consequence from relative entropy, let \(H_j=\int\sqrt{q_{j,0}q_{j,1}}\).  Jensen's inequality under \(Q_{j,0}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\geq-2\log H_j.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
\leq\sqrt{1-H_j^2}
\leq\sqrt{1-e^{-\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})}}.
\]
Since \(1-\sqrt{1-u}\geq u/2\) for \(0\leq u\leq1\),
\[
\rho_j\geq\frac12e^{-\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})}
\geq\frac12M^{-\kappa}.
\]
Hence \(\prod_j(1-\rho_j)\leq\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\}=o(1)\), proving the final assertion.  The argument explicitly permits cross-coordinate observations and common randomization; it uses only the prohibition on decoder \(j\) accessing the uncompressed part of its own local channel.
