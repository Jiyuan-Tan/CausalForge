Fix a displayed law \(P\), boundary point \(x\), and arm \(t\), and put \(A_t=\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P\).  The map \(z\mapsto\lVert z-x\rVert_2\) is Lipschitz and has gradient of norm one away from the Lebesgue-null singleton \(\{x\}\).  Score absolute continuity and the polar coarea formula give, for every \(r>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\{X\in A_t\cap B_2(x,r)\}
 &=\int_0^r\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap A_t}
 f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)\,ds\\
 &=\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(0<r\leq r_0\), two-sided thickness bounds the last expression below by \(c_-r^2\).  If \(Q_{t,P,x}=0\) almost everywhere on some \((0,r)\), its nonnegative integral there would be zero, a contradiction.  Thus positive-denominator radii accumulate at zero, although positivity at every radius need not hold.

Absolute continuity also gives \(P(X=x)=0\).  Hence \(D_{P,x}\neq0\) almost surely, and deterministic assignment makes its sign equal to \(2T-1\).  For every bounded Borel \(a:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\), consistency and the definition of \(\mu_{t,P}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[Y\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(\lVert X-x\rVert_2)\right]
 &=\int_{A_t}a(\lVert z-x\rVert_2)\mu_{t,P}(z)f_P(z)\,dz\\
 &=\int_0^\infty a(s)N_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds,
\end{aligned}
\]
whereas the same calculation with \(Y\) replaced by one gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(\lVert X-x\rVert_2)\right]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
The Radon--Nikodym characterization of conditional expectation therefore permits the version
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
 =\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(s>0\) for which \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\).  Division occurs only on that set.

Because \(q\geq1\), the H\"older condition supplies
\[
 |\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\lVert z-x\rVert_2.
\]
When \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\), the ratio \(N/Q\) is an \(f_P\,d\mathcal H^1\)-weighted average over points at distance \(s\), and hence
\[
 \left|\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \leq\frac{\int Ls f_P\,d\mathcal H^1}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}=Ls.
\]
At \(s=0\), and at every positive zero-denominator radius, the canonical definition sets \(g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)=\mu_{t,P}(x)\), so the same inequality holds for all \(s\geq0\).  Therefore
\[
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)=\mu_{t,P}(x).
\]
Subtracting the arm limits and using the separately defined causal target gives
\[
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}\{g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P,x}(s)-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P,x}(s)\}
 =\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
\]
Thus the observed radial functional identifies, rather than defines, the causal contrast.  Neither a density floor nor density continuity is used.
