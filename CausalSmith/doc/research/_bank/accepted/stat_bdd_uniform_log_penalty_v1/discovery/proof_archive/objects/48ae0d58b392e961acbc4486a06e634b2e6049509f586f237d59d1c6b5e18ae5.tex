Fix \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), take the family supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}support\text{-}boundary\text{-}angular\text{-}packing}\).  Write \(M=M_n\), \(\Delta=c_1a_n\), and let \(C_j=B_2(x_j,w_n)\cap S\) be its pairwise disjoint half-disc cells.  By that lemma there are numbers \(u_{j,0},u_{j,1}\) such that
\[
 \mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)=u_{j,\omega_j},
 \qquad |u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|\geq\Delta.
\]

Let \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) be arbitrary.  By \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}distance\text{-}decision\text{-}class}\), there is one measurable map
\[
 T_n:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\]
selected independently of the law, such that
\[
 \widehat\mu_n(x)=T_n(V_{n,P}(x))
\]
at every boundary point.  This common-map architecture is the inherited decision class itself: the center enters only through the compressed sample.

We first turn this fixed-\(n\) rule into a rule in an experiment having independent cell restrictions.  Under \(P_\omega\), generate a Poisson point process of mean \(2n\) on the observation space and attach independent marks having a continuous distribution.  If its point count \(N\) is at least \(n\), retain the \(n\) observations with the smallest marks, in increasing-mark order, form the corresponding distance sample at each queried boundary point, and apply the same map \(T_n\).  If \(N<n\), return the zero boundary function.  Denote the resulting rule by \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\).  Conditional on every value \(N=k\geq n\), the retained observations are an independent sample from \(P_\omega\): the marks are independent of the observations, and their ordering induces a random permutation independent of the observation values.  This construction is independent of \(\omega\) and of \(P_\omega\).

Place independent uniform bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) on the packing vertices.  Let \(Z_j\) be the complete marked restriction of the Poisson process to \(C_j\), and let \(Z_0\) be its restriction to the complement of the union of the cells.  Conditional on the bits, \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) are independent, because restrictions of a Poisson process to disjoint measurable sets are independent.  The locality conclusions of the packing lemma state that the one-observation law on \(C_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), whereas its law on the complement is bit-independent.  Therefore the law of \(Z_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), and the law of \(Z_0\) is independent of all bits.  Let \(S_j\) retain from \(Z_j\) only each point's mark, outcome, and distance from \(x_j\).  At \(x_j\), the rule \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\) is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\): the full restrictions outside \(C_j\) reveal their marks, outcomes, and distances from \(x_j\), while \(S_j\) supplies exactly the information about points inside \(C_j\) permitted by \(V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\).  Sorting all revealed marks reconstructs the thinned ordered sample to which \(T_n\) is applied.

We next verify the divergence hypothesis for this marked cell experiment.  By the packing lemma, adjacent laws are identical off \(C_j\), their common cell mass is \(m_n\), and every support point at distance less than \(w_n\) from \(x_j\) belongs to \(C_j\).  Let \(\nu_{j,b}\) be the subprobability law of one pair consisting of outcome and distance, restricted to \(C_j\), under bit value \(b\).  Its mass is \(m_n\) for both bits.  The corresponding full one-observation compressed laws are \(\nu_{j,b}+\lambda_j\), where \(\lambda_j\) is common to the two bits and is supported on radii at least \(w_n\), whereas \(\nu_{j,b}\) is supported on radii below \(w_n\), up to null boundaries.  Thus, writing
\[
 D_j=\int\log\!\left(\frac{d\nu_{j,0}}{d\nu_{j,1}}\right)d\nu_{j,0},
\]
the disjoint supports and factorization of the independent \(n\)-sample likelihood give
\[
 nD_j
 =\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
 \mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
 \right)
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]

Let \(Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)\).  Under either bit, the cell count is \(K_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2nm_n)\), so its law contributes zero relative entropy.  Conditional on \(K_j=k\), the compressed cell points are independent with law \(\nu_{j,b}/m_n\), and the independent marks again contribute zero.  Conditioning on the common count and taking its expectation therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
 =\mathbb E[K_j]\operatorname{KL}(\nu_{j,0}/m_n,\nu_{j,1}/m_n)
 =2nD_j
 \leq2\alpha\log M.
\]
The packing bound also holds after reversing the adjacent orientation.  Since \(2\alpha<1\), \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\), with \(\kappa=2\alpha\), applies.

At each \(x_j\), decode \(\Omega_j\) by thresholding \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\) at the midpoint of \(u_{j,0}\) and \(u_{j,1}\), reversing the inequality if \(u_{j,1}<u_{j,0}\).  The direct-product lemma and \(M_n\to\infty\) imply
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one coordinate is decoded incorrectly}\}
 \geq\frac12-o(1).
\]
If the uniform boundary loss were less than \(\Delta/2\), every midpoint decoder would be correct.  Averaging first over the uniform bit prior and then using that a maximum dominates an average gives
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{2}\left\{\frac12-o(1)\right\}
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), where \(c>0\) depends only on \(q\) and \(L\).

It remains to return to the fixed-\(n\) experiment.  Every packing regression takes values in \([1/4,3/4]\), so the loss of the zero default on \(\{N<n\}\) is at most one.  On \(\{N\geq n\}\), the selected data have law \(P_\omega^{\otimes n}\) and are processed by the original common map \(T_n\).  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 +\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-tN}\), optimized at \(t=\log2\), gives
\[
 \Pr\{N<n\}
 \leq\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}=o(a_n).
\]
Combining the last three displays and decreasing \(c\) if necessary proves, uniformly over the arbitrary rule \(\widehat\mu_n\),
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq c a_n.
\]

The packing lemma verifies explicitly all independent-sampling, compact-support, density-positivity, rectifiable-boundary, H\"older-regression, and continuous-variance conditions required for \(P_\omega\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\).  Hence the supremum over the full class is no smaller than the supremum over this finite family.  Taking the infimum over the inherited law-independent common-map class \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) gives
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Finally, \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), while \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}distance\text{-}risk}\) identifies the expanded infimum--supremum display with \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).  Dividing by \(a_n\) and taking lower limits proves the claimed result with \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}=c\).  The entire finite family lies in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), so this is a same-class converse and uses no treatment-effect or two-sided-thickness subclass.
