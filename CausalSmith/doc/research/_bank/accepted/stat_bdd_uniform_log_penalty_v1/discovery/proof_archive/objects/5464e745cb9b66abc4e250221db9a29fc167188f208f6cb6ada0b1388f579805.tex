Let \(h=h_n\asymp a_n\).  We first prove the upper bound with the single local-constant estimator in the statement.  Because the kernel is symmetric,
\[
W_{i,t,x}=\mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_\triangle(\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2/h)
\]
is measurable from \((Y_i,D_{P,x,i})\); the zero-distance event has probability zero under the density assumption.  Hence the estimator belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).

Let \(A_{t,x}=\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\) and, on arm \(t\), let \(\varepsilon_{i,t}=Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\).  When \(A_{t,x}>0\),
\[
\widehat\mu_{t,h}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
=\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}}{A_{t,x}}
+\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\{\mu_{t,P}(X_i)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\}}{A_{t,x}}.
\]
The H\"older assumption with \(q\geq1\) bounds the second term by \(Lh\).  The stability and upper-mass lemmas give, uniformly in \(P,t,x\),
\[
\kappa_0nh^2\leq\mathbb E_PA_{t,x}\leq\pi Lnh^2.
\]

For completeness, choose for each fixed boundary a net of mesh \(n^{-3}\).  Intersecting an ambient square grid with the curve produces at most a polynomial number of net points; alternatively the length envelope gives the sharper bound \(O(n^3)\).  Bernstein's inequality for the bounded weights, followed by a union bound, gives with probability at least \(1-n^{-4}\),
\[
\inf_{t,x}A_{t,x}\geq\frac{\kappa_0}{2}nh^2,
\qquad
\sup_{t,x}A_{t,x}\leq Cnh^2.
\]
Conditionally on the scores, sub-Gaussianity and \(\sum_iW_{i,t,x}^2\leq A_{t,x}\) give
\[
\Pr\!\left(\left.\left|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|>u\,\right|X_{1:n}\right)
\leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2L^2A_{t,x}}\right\}.
\]
A second union bound and integration of its tail yield
\[
\mathbb E_P\sup_{t,x}
\frac{\left|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|}{A_{t,x}}
\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+o(a_n).
\]
The extension from the net to the curve is valid because \(K_\triangle\) is one-Lipschitz: moving a center by \(n^{-3}\) changes a denominator by at most \(n^{-2}/h\) and a centered numerator by at most \(n^{-3}h^{-1}\sum_i|\varepsilon_{i,t}|\), whose expectation is \(O(n^{-2}/h)\).  These terms are negligible.  On the exceptional event, every nonempty-denominator local average is bounded by \(\max_i|Y_i|\); the conditional sub-Gaussian assumption gives \(\mathbb E\max_i|Y_i|^2=O(\log n)\), so Cauchy--Schwarz makes its contribution \(o(a_n)\).  Zero denominators are already covered by the exceptional event and by the definition of the estimator.  Therefore
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}
\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Balancing gives \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/4}=a_n\) and proves the upper limsup.

For the lower bound, use the unit-square hypercube from the angular-density packing lemma, with target separation \(\Delta=c_1a_n\) and \(M=M_n\).  Poissonize at mean \(2n\).  The disjoint bump disks generate independent Poisson point processes \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\), while the process outside them is ancillary.  For decoder \(j\), the observations from its own disk that appear in \(U_{n,P}(x_j)\) form the compressed local channel \(S_j\); everything from other disks is arbitrary cross-coordinate side information.  Restriction to the local disk cannot increase relative entropy.  The construction was chosen with slack so that the mean-\(2n\) channel satisfies
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\leq\kappa\log M
\]
for a fixed \(\kappa<1\).  The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma therefore gives, under the product prior,
\[
\Pr\{\widehat\omega_j\neq\omega_j\text{ for some }j\}\geq\frac12-o(1).
\]
This conclusion remains valid although observations from other centers can be informative about their own bits and can be used to coordinate the decisions; precisely that ancillary coordination is allowed in the direct-product lemma.

At each center the baseline target value is known and the two bit values differ by \(\Delta\).  Thresholding \(\widehat\tau_n(x_j)\) recovers \(\omega_j\) whenever the sup-norm error is less than \(\Delta/2\).  Thus the Poissonized Bayes expected sup-norm error is at least \((\Delta/2)(1/2-o(1))\).  If the Poisson count \(N\) is at least \(n\), uniformly subsample \(n\) observations and apply any fixed-\(n\) coordinatewise estimator.  Conditional on \(N\geq n\), the subsample has law \(P^{\otimes n}\), while \(\Pr\{N<n\}\leq e^{-cn}\).  Hence the fixed-\(n\) Bayes risk is no smaller than the Poissonized lower bound minus \(o(\Delta)\).  A Bayes lower bound is bounded above by the worst-law risk, so
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}})}{a_n}>0.
\]
Together with the upper bound this proves the claimed chain and the asserted attainment.  The causal curve remains the potential-outcome contrast; its observed radial identifying representation is supplied by the canonical-regression lemma and is not being substituted for the estimand by definition.
