All constants may depend on \(q,\iota,L,c_-,r_0,\rho_0,\delta\), but not on \(n\) or \(P\).  Apply the local-constant construction on \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\).  The stability lemma supplies a denominator lower bound \(\kappa_0\), the density lemma supplies probability at most \(\pi Lh^2\) for every kernel window, and \(q\geq1\) supplies the bias bound \(Lh\).  A maximal \(n^{-3}\)-separated subset of the trimmed curve has at most \(Cn^6\) points because the entire curve lies in \([-L,L]^2\).  Bernstein's inequality for the bounded kernel terms and the conditionally sub-Gaussian outcome terms, a union bound over this ambient net, and the Lipschitz oscillation of the kernel give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}}
\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
For completeness, on the concentration complement a nonzero local-constant estimate is a convex combination of outcomes, while a zero denominator produces zero; uniform sub-Gaussianity and Cauchy--Schwarz make this contribution negligible.  Taking \(h\asymp a_n\) proves the upper bound.  No common modulus for \(f_P\) is used.

For the lower bound, use the unit-square version of the angular-density packing.  Its straight interface is a \(C^\infty\) injective chart, its speed and derivatives satisfy the common envelopes, its line segment has infinite reach, and all centers lie on \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\).  Thus every vertex is in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\) for every \(\iota\geq1\).  Poissonize at mean \(2n\), split the process into its disjoint bump cells and complement, and compress cell \(j\) to the signed-radius observations seen at \(x_j\).  The packing gives adjacent separation \(c_1a_n\) and local relative entropy at most \(2\alpha\log M_n\), with \(2\alpha<1\).  The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma therefore bounds the probability of simultaneous bit recovery away from one.  Midpoint thresholding converts sup-norm loss below \(c_1a_n/2\) into simultaneous recovery.  Subsampling the first \(n\) marked observations when the Poisson count exceeds \(n\), and using its exponentially small lower tail, gives
\[
R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}})\geq ca_n.
\]
Together with the upper bound this proves the two-sided limit-rate statement.  The lower family uses disjoint \(q\)-H\"older bump perturbations of affine baselines and bounded continuous score densities with no common modulus, exactly as asserted.
