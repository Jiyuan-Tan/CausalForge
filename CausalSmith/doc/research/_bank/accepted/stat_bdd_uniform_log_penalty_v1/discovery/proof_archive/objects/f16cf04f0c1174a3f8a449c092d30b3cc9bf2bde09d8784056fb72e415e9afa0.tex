Write \(h=h_n\asymp a_n\).  We first prove the upper bound with the single estimator displayed in \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}estimator}\).  Symmetry of \(K_\triangle\) implies that its weight at \(x\) is
\[
 W_{i,t,x}=\mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_\triangle(\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2/h),
\]
which is a measurable function of \((Y_i,D_{P,x,i})\); the event \(D_{P,x,i}=0\) has probability zero by the density assumption.  Hence this estimator belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).

Let \(A_{t,x}=\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\), and put \(\varepsilon_{i,t}=Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\) on \(\{T_i=t\}\).  Whenever \(A_{t,x}>0\), the exact decomposition is
\[
 \widehat\mu_{t,h}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 =\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}}{A_{t,x}}
 +\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\{\mu_{t,P}(X_i)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\}}{A_{t,x}}.
\]
The second term has absolute value at most \(Lh\), because a nonzero weight implies \(\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2\leq h\).  By the stability lemma,
\(\mathbb EA_{t,x}\geq\kappa_0nh^2\), while the upper-mass lemma gives \(\mathbb EA_{t,x}\leq\pi Lnh^2\).

For completeness, the required uniform concentration can be obtained without an unmentioned empirical-process premise.  Cover \([-L,L]^2\) by a deterministic mesh of spacing \(n^{-3}\); its cardinality is polynomial in \(n\).  At a mesh point, the Chernoff argument based on
\(\mathbb E e^{\lambda(W-\mathbb EW)}\leq\exp\{\lambda^2\mathbb EW/2(1-\lambda/3)\}\) for \(0\leq W\leq1\) gives, after a union bound,
\[
 \inf_{t,x}A_{t,x}\geq\frac{\kappa_0}{2}nh^2,qquad
 \sup_{t,x}A_{t,x}\leq Cnh^2
\]
with probability at least \(1-n^{-4}\), because \(nh^2/\log n\to\infty\).  Conditional sub-Gaussianity gives
\[
 \mathbb P\!\left(\left.\left|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|>u\,\right|X_{1:n}\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2L^2\sum_iW_{i,t,x}^2}\right\},
\]
and \(\sum_iW_{i,t,x}^2\leq A_{t,x}\).  A second union bound therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{t,x}\left|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|
 \leq C\sqrt{nh^2\log n}
\]
on the same event.  The kernel is \(1\)-Lipschitz.  Moving a mesh point by \(n^{-3}\) changes a denominator by at most \(n^{-2}/h\) and a centered numerator by at most \(n^{-3}h^{-1}\sum_i|\varepsilon_{i,t}|\).  Conditional sub-Gaussianity gives \(\sum_i|\varepsilon_{i,t}|=O_P(n)\) uniformly in the two arms, so these changes are negligible relative to the displayed bounds.  This extends the inequalities from the mesh to every \(x\), hence to \(\mathcal B_P\), uniformly over \(P\).

It follows that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
On the exceptional event, truncate each arm estimate to \([-L,L]\); this cannot increase error because the H\"older ball bounds \(|\mu_{t,P}|\leq L\), and its contribution is \(O(n^{-4})\).  Balancing the two terms gives \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/4}=a_n\), proving the upper limsup.

For the lower bound use the explicit hypercube constructed in the narrowed angular-packing lemma, on its unit-square support version.  Put \(\Delta=c_1a_n\) and let \(M=M_n\).  If a curve estimator has sup-norm error less than \(\Delta/2\), thresholding its values at the centers \(x_j\) recovers every bit \(\omega_j\).  Poissonize the sample size with mean \(2n\).  The score balls are disjoint, so their Poisson point processes are independent.  For bit \(j\), observations outside its inner ball have exactly the same compressed law under \(\omega_j=0\) and \(\omega_j=1\); they are ancillary for that bit.  The relative-entropy bound and the binary testing inequality obtained from \(1-\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq\tfrac12e^{-\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)}\) imply a Bayes bit-error probability at least
\[
 p_j\geq\frac14M^{-2\alpha}.
\]
Under the product prior, the local likelihoods factor.  Thus even the optimal simultaneous decoder has success probability at most
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^M(1-p_j)\leq\exp\{-\tfrac14M^{1-2\alpha}\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The probability that a Poisson variable of mean \(2n\) is smaller than \(n\) is exponentially small.  On its complement the Poisson experiment can simulate the fixed-\(n\) experiment by uniform subsampling, so de-Poissonization changes the bounded, truncated risk by only \(o(\Delta)\).  Hence every distance-only estimator has worst-law expected sup error at least \(c\Delta\).  This gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}})}{a_n}>0.
\]
Combining the two bounds proves the chain of inequalities and the asserted attainment.  Identification of the causal curve used in the bias decomposition is supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\text{-}exact\text{-}distance\text{-}regression}\); it is not being redefined as a statistical regression target.
