Fix \(P,x,t\) as in the statement.  Apply polar coordinates centered at \(x\) to the nonnegative measurable function
\(z\mapsto f_P(z)\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P\cap B_2(x,r)\}\).  The polar-coordinate identity, equivalently the coarea identity for \(z\mapsto\lVert z-x\rVert_2\), yields
\[
 P\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,r)\}
 =\int_0^r\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P}f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)\,ds
 =\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
The thickness assumption supplies the lower bound \(c_-r^2\) for \(0<r\leq r_0\).  If \(\{s:Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\}\) did not accumulate at zero, then \(Q_{t,P,x}=0\) on some interval \((0,\varepsilon)\), contradicting the preceding lower bound at \(r=\varepsilon\).

For bounded measurable \(\varphi\), consistency and deterministic assignment give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[Y\mathbf 1\{T=t\}\varphi(|D_{P,x}|)\right]
 &=\int_0^\infty \varphi(s)N_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds,\\
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbf 1\{T=t\}\varphi(|D_{P,x}|)\right]
 &=\int_0^\infty \varphi(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
The defining property of conditional expectation therefore shows that, for Lebesgue-almost every \(s\) with \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\), its conditional mean is \(N_{t,P,x}(s)/Q_{t,P,x}(s)\).

Membership in the stated H\"older ball with \(q\geq1\) implies the uniform Lipschitz inequality
\[
 |\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\lVert z-x\rVert_2.
\]
If \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\), division by that positive denominator and the definitions of \(N\) and \(Q\) give
\[
 \left|\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \leq\frac{\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P}Ls f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
 =Ls.
\]
If \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)=0\), or if \(s=0\), the canonical definition sets the left member equal to zero.  Consequently the bound holds for every \(s\geq0\).  Letting \(s\downarrow0\) arm by arm and using the definition \(\tau_P(x)=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)\) proves the final limit.  No division is made at a zero-denominator radius.
