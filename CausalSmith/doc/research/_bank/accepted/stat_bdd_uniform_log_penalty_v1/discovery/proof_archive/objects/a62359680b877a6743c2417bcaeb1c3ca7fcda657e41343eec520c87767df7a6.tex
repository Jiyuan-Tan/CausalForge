Use the hypercube in \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}support\text{-}boundary\text{-}angular\text{-}packing}\), with target separation \(\Delta=c_1a_n\) and \(M=M_n\).  Poissonize the sample size at mean \(2n\), attach independent continuous marks, and partition the resulting point process into the disjoint half-disc cells supporting the bumps and their complement.  Conditional on the independent uniform bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\), Poisson restrictions to these sets are independent; cell \(j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), and the complement is bit independent.

Fix any coordinatewise rule \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).  At center \(x_j\), threshold \(\widehat\mu_n(x_j)\) at the midpoint of the two adjacent regression values to obtain a decoder of \(\Omega_j\).  Within cell \(j\), this decoder observes only the compressed pairs \((Y_i,\lVert X_i-x_j\rVert_2)\).  Giving it the uncompressed restrictions of every other cell and of the complement can only make decoding easier.  The resulting enlarged experiment has exactly the conditional product form in \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\).

Poisson tensorization multiplies the one-observation relative entropy by \(2n\).  The slack chosen in the packing lemma therefore gives, for every \(j\),
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
 \leq2\alpha\log M,
 \qquad 2\alpha<1.
\]
The direct-product lemma implies
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one coordinate is decoded incorrectly}\}
 \geq\frac12-o(1).
\]
If the uniform estimation loss on \(\operatorname{bd}(S)\) were less than \(\Delta/2\), every midpoint decoder would be correct.  Therefore the Poissonized Bayes risk, and hence its maximal risk over the finite family, satisfies
\[
 \sup_\omega\mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{2}\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)
 \geq c a_n.
\]

It remains to transfer this bound to exactly \(n\) observations.  On the event that a \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\) sample contains at least \(n\) points, retain the \(n\) observations with the smallest independent marks and apply any putative fixed-\(n\) rule; on the complement, return zero.  The retained observations are independently distributed from the same law, while
\[
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}\leq e^{-c'n}.
\]
Every regression in the packing is bounded by one, so the zero-branch loss is uniformly bounded.  An \(o(a_n)\) fixed-sample risk would consequently produce an \(o(a_n)\) Poissonized risk, contradicting the preceding display.  Thus
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Since the constructed family is contained in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) itself, taking the infimum over the inherited decision class, dividing by \(a_n\), and passing to the lower limit proves the theorem.
