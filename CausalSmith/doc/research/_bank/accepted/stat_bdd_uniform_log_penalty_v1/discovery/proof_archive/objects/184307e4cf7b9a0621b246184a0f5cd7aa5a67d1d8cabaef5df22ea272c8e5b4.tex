Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu, Theorem~6, gives an \(n^{-1/4}\) lower rate on \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), while its discussion conjectures that the logarithmic uniform penalty is unavoidable.  The missing step is a same-law-class construction producing many hard boundary alternatives.  The strengthened question asks whether the logarithm remains necessary even after their one-common-map restriction is relaxed to arbitrary law-independent point-indexed families with only fixed-section Borel measurability.  Their uniform upper theorem imposes the additional geometric condition in Assumption~2 and hence does not furnish a full-class upper bound under rectifiability alone.
