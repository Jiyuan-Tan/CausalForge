The decision restriction is coordinatewise: \(\widehat\tau_n(x)\) may use \(U_{n,P}(x)\) but may not triangulate score locations released at other boundary points.  Exact-radius conditionals are determined only almost everywhere, and the canonical representative fills zero-denominator radii with \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\).  Two-sided thickness makes positive-denominator radii accumulate at zero but does not make every circle positive.  The local-polynomial proof controls the concentration complement on both cutoff branches: a failed cutoff gives zero, whereas an active cutoff is bounded by the cutoff-controlled inverse times \(h^{-2}n^{-1}\sum_i|Y_i|\); the conditional sub-Gaussian second moment and a polynomially small complement probability make this contribution negligible.  The width-\(h\), height-\(h^m\) converse is not used when \(m<q\), because its \(q\)-th derivatives would diverge.
