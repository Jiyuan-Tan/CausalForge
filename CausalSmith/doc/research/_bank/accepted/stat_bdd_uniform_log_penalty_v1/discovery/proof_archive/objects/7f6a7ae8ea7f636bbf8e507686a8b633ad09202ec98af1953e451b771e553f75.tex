We first verify that the canonical class is nonempty.  Uniform density \(1/4\) on \([-1,1]^2\), the prescribed two half-squares, \(\gamma(u)=(2u-1,0)\), constant regressions in \((1/4,3/4)\), and Gaussian-noised Bernoulli outcomes satisfy every class condition.  The chart speed is two, its higher derivatives vanish, the interface is convex with infinite reach, and the restriction \(c_{-}\leq1/(16L)\) is below the mass of every relevant half- or quarter-ball.

For the full-location upper bound, define the degree-\((q-1)\) bivariate local-polynomial estimator separately in each arm, using a fixed compactly supported positive kernel and taking the difference of its two intercepts.  At every endpoint-trimmed boundary point and all sufficiently small bandwidths, each arm contains a fixed half-ball.  Together with \(L^{-1}\leq f_P\leq L\), this makes the scaled population polynomial Gram matrix uniformly positive definite.  Taylor expansion of \(\mu_{t,P}\) through degree \(q-1\) gives bias at most \(Ch^q\).  Bounded-weight matrix concentration on a polynomial boundary net, conditional sub-Gaussian concentration, and Lipschitz extension give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}}
\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
|\widetilde\tau_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\leq C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
The estimator is one jointly measurable map of the full sample and the known geometry, so it belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\).  Taking \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\) gives the upper bound \(Cs_{n,q}\).

For its converse, retain the uniform score density and put one of \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) disjoint smooth bivariate regression bumps of width \(h\) and height \(c h^q\) along the trimmed interface.  Use the same bounded Gaussian-noised Bernoulli outcome construction as above.  The alternatives lie in the straight class.  Their target curves are separated by \(ch^q\), and relative entropy from the null is bounded by
\[
C n(h^q)^2h^2=Cnh^{2q+2}.
\]
With \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\), decreasing the fixed bump amplitude makes this at most \(\alphalog M\) for some \(\alpha<1\).  If the alternative index \(J\) is uniform, then
\[
I(J;O_{1:n})
\leq M^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^M\operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
\leq\alpha\log M.
\]
For every decoder with error probability \(p_e\), the entropy identity gives
\(I(J;O_{1:n})\geq(1-p_e)\log M-\log2\).  Hence \(p_e\) is bounded away from zero.  Sup-norm accuracy below half the bump height would decode \(J\), proving the lower bound \(cs_{n,q}\), even for the full-location class.

Suppose next that \(L>4\).  The degree-zero distance upper bound just proved for the smooth class restricts to \(Ca_n\).  The canonical-square angular family belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\).  Its disjoint Poisson local channels and the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma yield an any-bit-error probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\), despite arbitrary cross-center ancillary observations.  Thresholding and de-Poissonization therefore give the distance lower bound \(ca_n\).

Finally suppose \(L=4\).  Since the square has area four and \(f_P\geq1/4\),
\[
1=\int_{[-1,1]^2}f_P(z)\,dz\geq1.
\]
Equality implies \(f_P=1/4\) almost everywhere; density continuity makes the equality pointwise.  For an endpoint-trimmed \(x\) and \(s\) below its fixed distance from the vertical sides, each arm-specific half-circle is inside the square and
\[
Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\frac\pi4s.
\]
Taylor's formula for the \(q\)-H\"older regression, integrated over the relevant half-circle, yields uniformly bounded coefficients such that
\[
\left|g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
-\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k\right|\leq Cs^q.
\]
The scaled one-sided radial Gram matrix for degree \(q-1\) is bounded below because its limiting weight is proportional to \(uK_\triangle(u)\).  The explicit one-sided local-polynomial intercept consequently has uniform risk
\[
C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\},
\]
and the same balancing gives the distance upper bound \(Cs_{n,q}\).  Every coordinatewise distance rule is also a full-location rule, since the latter can compute all signed distances from the full scores.  The full-location lower bound thus transfers and gives \(R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}\geq cs_{n,q}\).  These arguments prove all cases, including equality of exponents when \(q=1\) and the strict gap only when \(q>1\) and \(L>4\).
