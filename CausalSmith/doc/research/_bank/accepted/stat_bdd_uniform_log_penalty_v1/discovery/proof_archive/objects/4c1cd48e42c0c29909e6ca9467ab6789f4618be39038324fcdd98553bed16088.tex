The logarithm is an information-loss phenomenon for simultaneous boundary recovery from coordinatewise outcome--distance samples, not an artifact of forcing the same estimator map at every point.  Many disjoint angular perturbations remain individually hard after radial compression, and the direct-product argument permits a different Borel decoder at every packed coordinate.  The proof requires no measurability of the arbitrary full boundary supremum: it derives the lower bound for a measurable finite maximum and passes to the full supremum by monotonicity of outer expectation.  The inherited CTY common-map result is a corollary.
