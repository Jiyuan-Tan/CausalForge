There are constants \(c_0,c_1>0\) and \(0<\alpha<1/8\), depending only on the law-class parameters and \(\delta\), such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), one can construct \(M_n\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}\) centers on a trimmed straight boundary, separated by a constant multiple of \(a_n^{1/q}\), and a finite hypercube family of laws in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\cap\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\).  The observed arms are the two half-squares; the regressions are disjoint \(q\)-H\"older bump perturbations of affine baselines; every \(f_P\) is continuous and stays within the fixed density envelope; and the variance, tail, and actual-arm local-mass constants are common.  If two vertices differ only in the coordinate attached to a center \(x_j\), their target curves differ by at least \(c_1a_n\) at \(x_j\), both observed arm-specific radial measures at \(x_j\) agree, their fixed Radon--Nikodym densities therefore agree Lebesgue-almost everywhere, their arm-specific radial outcome laws agree outside radii of order \(a_n\), and
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\!\left(\mathbb Q_{n,P_\omega,x_j},\mathbb Q_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}},x_j}\right)
 \leq\alpha\log M_n.
\]
The perturbations use \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{ang}}\), and the density envelope and actual-arm local-mass bounds hold uniformly over the whole hypercube.  If \(L>4\), the construction may be made on the canonical square, in which case the entire family also lies in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\).
