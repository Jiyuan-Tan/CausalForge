Fix the integer \(m\geq1\), the subclass \(\mathcal C\), and the constants in the proposed statement.  Constants below may depend on the fixed quantities \(m,q,\iota,L,c_{-},r_0,\rho_0,\delta,A,r_*,\underline c,\overline c\), but never on \(n\), \(P\), \(t\), or \(x\).  The proof has five parts.

\emph{Population rank, support, and causal centering.}
For \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), put \(S_{P,x}=|D_{P,x}|\).  By coarea, the definition of \(Q_{t,P,x}\), and the strict arm convention in the estimator, for \(0<h\leq r_*\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)
 =\int_0^1K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top
 \frac{Q_{t,P,x}(hu)}h\,du.
\]
The radial bounds in the statement therefore imply, for every \(a\in\mathbb R^m\),
\[
 \underline c\,a^\top G_ma
 \leq a^\top\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)a
 \leq\overline c\,a^\top G_ma.
\]
If \(a\neq0\), the polynomial \(a^\top v_m(u)\) cannot vanish on an interval, whereas \(uK_\triangle(u)>0\) for \(0<u<1\).  Thus \(G_m\) is positive definite, and
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\{\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\}
 \geq\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)=\lambda_m(\underline c)>0.
\]
This verifies all population rank and inverse conditions.  The restriction \(h\leq r_*\) keeps every positive radius on the kernel support inside the domain of the assumed bounds, and \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\geq\underline cs>0\) verifies every division by \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\) used in this proof.

By \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\text{-}exact\text{-}distance\text{-}regression}\), the conditional mean of \(Y\) at signed radius \((2t-1)s\) is the canonical ratio for almost every positive \(s\), and its boundary limit is \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\).  Hence the causal target remains
\[
 \tau_P(x)=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x);
\]
the radial ratio is the identifying regression and is not a definition of the causal effect.  Write
\[
 g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)
 =\mu_{t,P}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k+R_{t,P,x}(s),
 \qquad |R_{t,P,x}(s)|\leq As^m,
\]
and define
\[
 \beta_{t,P,x}(h)=
 \bigl(\mu_{t,P}(x),hb_{t,1,P}(x),\ldots,
 h^{m-1}b_{t,m-1,P}(x)\bigr)^\top.
\]
At every fixed \(x\), the empirical outcome vector has the exact polynomial-reproduction decomposition
\[
 \widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)
 =\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\beta_{t,P,x}(h)
 +B_{t,x}(h)+Z_{t,x}(h),
\]
where \(B_{t,x}(h)\) is obtained by replacing \(Y_i\) in \(\widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\) by \(R_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x,i})\), and \(Z_{t,x}(h)\) is obtained by replacing it by
\(Y_i-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x,i})\).  This is an algebraic identity and uses no empirical inverse.

The centered radial error has a uniform conditional sub-Gaussian envelope.  Indeed,
\[
 Y-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x})
 =\{Y-\mu_{t,P}(X)\}
 +\{\mu_{t,P}(X)-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x})\}.
\]
The first term obeys \(\mathrm{ass:conditional\text{-}subgaussian}\).  Conditional on the signed radius in arm \(t\), the second is centered by the radial conditional-mean identity and lies in \([-2L,2L]\), since the H\"older envelope contains \(\|\mu_{t,P}\|_\infty\leq L\).  Hoeffding's exponential bound for a centered variable in this interval, followed by conditioning first on \(X\) and then on the signed radius, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \exp\!\left(\lambda\{Y-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x})\}\right)
 \mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)S_{P,x}
 \right]
 \leq\exp(C_L\lambda^2/2),
 \qquad \lambda\in\mathbb R.
\]

\emph{A uniform good event.}
Let \(\varepsilon_n=n^{-3}\).  Compact support places every trimmed boundary in \([-L,L]^2\), so an ambient square grid supplies an \(\varepsilon_n\)-net of \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\) with at most \(C_L\varepsilon_n^{-2}\) points.  At a fixed net point, every Gram summand before division by \(n\) has operator norm at most \(C_mh^{-2}\), and the upper radial bound makes its second moment at most \(C_{m,\overline c}h^{-2}\).  Bernstein's inequality, written at first use as
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(W_i-\mathbb EW_i)\right|>z\right\}
 \leq2\exp\!\left{-\frac{nz^2}{2(v+Mz/3)}\right}
\]
when \(|W_i-\mathbb EW_i|\leq M\) and \(n^{-1}\sum_i\operatorname{Var}(W_i)\leq v\), gives entrywise Gram concentration.  The preceding conditional sub-Gaussian bound gives the analogous weighted concentration for \(Z_{t,x}(h)\).  A union bound over two arms, the fixed number of coordinates, and the polynomial-size net shows that, for every prescribed \(B>0\), the constants in the deviations may be chosen so that an event \(E_n(P)\) satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}P\{E_n(P)^c\}\leq n^{-B},
\]
and, on \(E_n(P)\), uniformly over arms and net points,
\[
 \left\|\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)
 -\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\frac14\lambda_m(\underline c),
\]
\[
 \left|e_1^\top\{\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\}^{-1}Z_{t,x}(h)\right|
 \leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}},
 \qquad
 \max_{1\leq i\leq n}|Y_i|\leq C\sqrt{\log n}.
\]
Here the inverse in the second display is legitimate by the first display and the population eigenvalue bound.  These probability bounds use independent sampling, which is a member property of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\).  They require \(nh^2/\log n\to\infty\), verified below at the selected bandwidth.

On the nonnegative domain, the maps
\[
 u\longmapsto K_\triangle(u)v_m(u),
 \qquad
 u\longmapsto K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top
\]
are Lipschitz when their displayed formulas are used on \([0,1]\) and they are extended by zero only for \(u>1\).  No extension through \(u=0\) is made or needed because \(u=S_{P,x}/h\geq0\).  Moreover,
\[
 \bigl|\|X_i-x\|_2-\|X_i-x'\|_2\bigr|\leq\|x-x'\|_2.
\]
The smooth embedded boundary has two-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero, so the density assumption implies that almost surely no sample score lies on it.  Therefore the strict arm indicator is constant as \(x\) moves along the boundary.  The Gram oscillation within one mesh cell is at most \(C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\), while the raw outcome-vector oscillation is at most
\[
 C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|\right).
\]
At the bandwidth selected below, both are negligible relative to the displayed stochastic order.  Weyl's inequality thus extends the cutoff activity from the net to every \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\).  The inverse identity \(A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}\), the raw outcome-vector oscillation, and the Lipschitz target bound
\[
 |\mu_{t,P}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x')|\leq L\|x-x'\|_2
\]
then extend the pointwise net error bound to the whole trimmed boundary.  This last inequality uses only \(q\geq1\); no relation between \(m\) and either \(q\) or \(\iota\) appears.

On \(E_n(P)\), the empirical inverse norm is at most \(2/\lambda_m(\underline c)\).  On the kernel support, \(|R_{t,P,x}(S)|\leq Ah^m\); the first diagonal Gram entry and its concentration bound the corresponding empirical kernel moment uniformly.  The polynomial-reproduction decomposition therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 \left|\widehat\mu^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{t,h,\underline c}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Subtracting the arm estimates gives the same bound for the causal curve.

\emph{The concentration complement and bandwidth optimization.}
Both estimator branches must be controlled off \(E_n(P)\).  On a failed-cutoff branch the arm intercept is zero, and \(|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\).  On an active branch, \(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\text{-}distance\text{-}local\text{-}polynomial\text{-}admissible}\) gives simultaneously for every \(x\)
\[
 \left|\widehat\mu^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{t,h,\underline c}(x)\right|
 \leq Ch^{-2}\overline Y_n,
 \qquad
 \overline Y_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|.
\]
The H\"older envelope and conditional sub-Gaussian assumption give \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_PY_i^2\leq C\).  Jensen's inequality yields \(\sup_P\mathbb E_P\overline Y_n^2\leq C\), and Cauchy--Schwarz consequently gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \mathbf1\{E_n(P)^c\}
 \right]
 \leq C\{n^{-B}+h^{-2}n^{-B/2}\}.
\]
Taking \(B=10\) makes this term negligible below.  Combining the two events gives, for \(h\leq r_*\) and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right]
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Balancing the two terms gives
\[
 h_n\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(2m+2)},
 \qquad
 h_n^m\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]
For each fixed \(m\geq1\), this bandwidth tends to zero, eventually obeys \(h_n\leq r_*\), and satisfies
\[
 \frac{nh_n^2}{\log n}
 \asymp\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{m/(m+1)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The admissibility lemma makes the stabilized rule one explicit data-driven member of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).  Substitution in the definition of \(R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal C)\) proves the upper bound for every fixed integer \(m\geq1\), without any restriction \(m\leq q\) or \(m\leq\iota\).

\emph{The contained-bump converse for every \(m\geq q\).}
Suppose now that \(m\geq q\) and that \(\mathcal C\) contains the submodel described in the proposed statement.  Use the square \([-1/2,1/2]^2\), uniform score density, horizontal assignment interface, and deterministic treatment assignment.  The trimmed boundary stays a fixed positive distance from the square's vertical sides.  Choose \(r_*>0\) smaller than this distance and smaller than \(1/2\); then, at every trimmed center and for both arms,
\[
 Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\pi s,
 \qquad 0<s\leq r_*.
\]
Thus the radial lower and upper constants are common and strictly positive.  The straight interface is an injective smooth chart with positive speed, bounded derivatives, and infinite reach; the square has compact support, and uniform density plus the half-plane geometry gives the required two-sided thickness, including at all relevant boundary points.

Let \(M\asymp h_n^{-1}\) centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) on the trimmed interface be separated by \(3h_n\).  Fix \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\) with \(0\leq\phi\leq1\) and \(\phi(0)=1\), put
\[
 \Delta_n=\eta h_n^m,
 \qquad
 \mu_{0,j}(z)=0,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,j}(z)=\Delta_n\phi((z-x_j)/h_n),
\]
and let \(Y(t)=\mu_{t,j}(X)+\varepsilon_t\), with independent standard Gaussian errors, deterministic assignment, and consistency.  For every derivative order \(0\leq k\leq q\),
\[
 \|D^k\mu_{1,j}\|_\infty
 \leq C_\phi\eta h_n^{m-k}
 \leq C_\phi\eta,
\]
because \(m\geq q\) and eventually \(h_n\leq1\).  Choosing the fixed \(\eta>0\) sufficiently small puts every regression in the frozen \(q\)-H\"older envelope.  The variance is one and the centered errors are Gaussian, so the variance and sub-Gaussian envelopes also hold.

The same derivative calculation through order \(m\) gives
\[
 \max_{0\leq k\leq m}\|D^k\mu_{1,j}\|_\infty\leq C_\phi\eta.
\]
Taylor's theorem in \(\mathbb R^2\), integrated over either half-circle, therefore gives coefficient functions \(b_{t,k,P}(x)\), uniformly bounded for \(1\leq k<m\), such that
\[
 \left|\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 -\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k\right|
 \leq C\eta s^m,
 \qquad 0<s\leq r_*.
\]
Indeed, under the uniform density, \(N/Q\) is precisely the normalized half-circle average of the regression; integrating the multivariate Taylor polynomial produces the displayed powers, and integrating its uniform remainder preserves the \(Cs^m\) bound.  Disjoint bump supports make all derivative and coefficient bounds uniform over the entire finite family.  This proves, rather than assumes through the construction's name, membership in the order-\(m\) coarea subclass.

Let \(P_0\) have both regressions zero and let \(P_j\) have the single treated bump at \(x_j\).  Conditional Gaussian relative entropy and the bump's score area \(O(h_n^2)\) imply
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =\frac n2\mathbb E\{\mu_{1,j}(X)^2\mathbf1(T=1)\}
 \leq Cn\Delta_n^2h_n^2
 =C\eta^2nh_n^{2m+2}
 \leq C'\eta^2\log n.
\]
Since \(M\asymp h_n^{-1}\),
\[
 \log M=\left(\frac1{2m+2}+o(1)\right)\log n.
\]
Choose \(\eta\) small enough that the relative entropy is at most \(\alpha\log M\) for a fixed \(\alpha<1/4\).  If \(J\) is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,M\}\), the log-sum inequality gives
\[
 I(J;O_{1:n})
 \leq\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M\operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
For any decoder with error event \(E\), the entropy identity and the bounds \(H(E)\leq\log2\), \(H(J\mid E=0,O_{1:n})=0\), and \(H(J\mid E=1,O_{1:n})\leq\log M\) yield
\[
 H(J\mid O_{1:n})\leq\log2+P(E)\log M,
\]
so \(P(E)\geq1-\alpha-o(1)\).  If a boundary-curve estimator had sup-norm error below \(\Delta_n/3\), maximizing its values at the centers would recover \(J\): the true curve is \(\Delta_n\) at \(x_J\) and zero at every other center because the supports are disjoint.  Hence even the full-location experiment has risk at least \(c\Delta_n\); the restricted coordinatewise distance class cannot have smaller risk.  Since
\[
 \Delta_n=\eta h_n^m
 \asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)},
\]
the upper and lower rates match whenever \(m\geq q\) and the stated submodel is contained.

For contrast, if \(m<q\), a width-\(h_n\), height-\(h_n^m\) bump has a derivative of order \(q\) of size
\[
 C\eta h_n^{m-q}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
It therefore leaves the frozen \(q\)-H\"older envelope.  This invalid construction supplies no same-class converse, and none is asserted for \(m<q\).

For the independent angular-imbalance clause, use \(\mathrm{lem:angular\text{-}density\text{-}packing}\).  After Poissonization at mean \(2n\), its disjoint local point processes are conditionally independent standard-Borel channels and the outside process is bit-independent.  Adjacent target separation is \(c_1a_n\), and the local relative entropy is at most \(2\alpha\log M\), with \(2\alpha<1\).  The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma therefore gives probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\) of some bit error even after all cross-center ancillary observations are revealed.  Midpoint thresholding converts sup-norm error below \(c_1a_n/2\) into simultaneous recovery.  Subsampling the first \(n\) observations when the Poisson count exceeds \(n\), whose failure probability is exponentially small by the exponential Markov bound, returns the fixed-sample lower frontier \(ca_n\).

\emph{Why density alone does not determine the order.}
If an arm regression is locally constant on every relevant circle, then
\[
 N_{t,P,x}(s)=\mu_{t,P}(x)Q_{t,P,x}(s)
\]
exactly, whatever the differentiability of \(f_P\); density smoothness is therefore not necessary for an arbitrarily high normalized-coarea remainder at that law.  Conversely, near an interior point of a straight interface, take the same bounded positive continuous density in polar coordinates
\[
 f(r,\theta)=c_0\{1+r^\alpha\cos\theta\},
 \qquad 0<\alpha<1,
\]
with a continuous compactly supported localization and a compensating normalization away from the point.  The angular perturbation integrates to zero on each complete circle and tends to zero at the center, so positivity, continuity, normalization, and radial mass bounds are preserved.  Paired with a constant regression, \(N/Q\) is constant.  Paired instead with the affine regression \(\mu(z)=z_1\), its value on an upper half-circle centered at the point is
\[
 \frac{N(s)}{Q(s)}
 =\frac{s\int_0^\pi\cos\theta\{1+s^\alpha\cos\theta\}\,d\theta}
 {\int_0^\pi\{1+s^\alpha\cos\theta\}\,d\theta}
 =\frac12s^{1+\alpha},
\]
which admits no order-two polynomial remainder.  The same density can thus induce different normalized-coarea orders when paired with different smooth regressions.  The joint ratio \(N/Q\), rather than \(f_P\) alone, is the operational object.  This completes the proof.
