Fix the displayed \(P\), \(x\), and \(t\), and put
\(R_x=\lVert X-x\rVert_2\).  By deterministic assignment and the observed-arm
definition,
\(\mathbf1\{T=t\}=\mathbf1\{X\in\mathcal E_{t,P}\}\) almost surely.  The
score law is absolutely continuous, so polar coarea and Tonelli's theorem give
for every \(r>0\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\{T=t,\ R_x\leq r\}
 &=\int_{\mathcal E_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,r)}f_P(z)\,dz\\
 &=\int_0^r\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap\mathcal E_{t,P}}
 f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)\,ds\\
 &=\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
No positivity or division is used.  Absolute continuity also gives
\(P(X=x)=0\), so \(D_{P,x}=(2T-1)R_x\) has sign \(2T-1\) almost surely.

For every bounded Borel \(a:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\), the observable
Radon--Nikodym definitions yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds,
\]
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)N_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
Integrability follows from conditional sub-Gaussianity and the bounded
regression envelope.  The Radon--Nikodym characterization of conditional
expectation thus gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
 =\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
 =g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(s\in\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\).  Nothing is defined or
claimed at a zero-denominator radius.

Under the final displayed hypotheses, each separate observed-arm essential
limit is well defined and equals its potential-outcome regression value.  The
definition of the causal target then gives
\[
 \ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P}(x)-\ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P}(x)
 =\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
\]
The conclusion does not require a radius sequence common to the two arms.
