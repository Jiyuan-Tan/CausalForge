Fix \(m\) and \(\mathcal C\) as in the statement.  For \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), put \(S_{P,x}=|D_{P,x}|\) and \(v_m(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^{m-1})^\top\).  For \(0<h\leq r_*\), define
\[
 \widehat G_{t,x}(h)=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{T_i=t\}
 K_\triangle(S_{P,x,i}/h)v_m(S_{P,x,i}/h)v_m(S_{P,x,i}/h)^\top
\]
and
\[
 \widehat r_{t,x}(h)=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{T_i=t\}
 K_\triangle(S_{P,x,i}/h)v_m(S_{P,x,i}/h)Y_i.
\]
Let
\[
 G_m=\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top\,du,
 \qquad \lambda_* =\underline c\,\lambda_{\min}(G_m)>0.
\]
Define the stabilized intercept by
\[
 \widehat\mu^{(m)}_{t,h}(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 e_1^\top\widehat G_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\widehat r_{t,x}(h),
 &\lambda_{\min}(\widehat G_{t,x}(h))\geq\lambda_*/2,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\]
and set \(\widehat\tau^{(m)}_{n,h}=\widehat\mu^{(m)}_{1,h}-\widehat\mu^{(m)}_{0,h}\).  The arm is recovered from the sign of \(D_{P,x}\) away from its probability-zero origin, so this is an explicit measurable function of \(U_{n,P}(x)\) and belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).  The eigenvalue cutoff states every inverse and boundary convention explicitly.

Coarea and the radial mass bounds give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)
 =\int_0^1K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top
 \frac{Q_{t,P,x}(hu)}h\,du.
\]
Thus, for every \(a\in\mathbb R^m\),
\[
 \underline c\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)\{a^\top v_m(u)\}^2du
 \leq a^\top\mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)a
 \leq\overline c\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)\{a^\top v_m(u)\}^2du.
\]
A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an interval of positive measure.  Hence \(G_m\) is positive definite; since \(m\) is fixed, the displayed inequalities give uniform population rank and inverse bounds.

Write
\[
 g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)
 =\mu_{t,P}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k+R_{t,P,x}(s),
 \qquad |R_{t,P,x}(s)|\leq As^m.
\]
On the event that the eigenvalue cutoff passes, the empirical normal equations reproduce the polynomial exactly.  The scaled coefficient vector is
\((\mu_{t,P}(x),h b_{t,1,P}(x),\ldots,h^{m-1}b_{t,m-1,P}(x))^\top\).  Since \(|R_{t,P,x}(S)|\leq Ah^m\) on the kernel support, the uniform inverse bound and the bounded empirical kernel moments give an intercept remainder at most \(Ch^m\).

For completeness, the stochastic scale follows directly from the primitive tails.  Conditional on \(T=t\) and \(S_{P,x}=s\),
\[
 Y-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)
 =\{Y-\mu_{t,P}(X)\}
 +\{\mu_{t,P}(X)-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)\}.
\]
The first term satisfies the stated conditional sub-Gaussian bound.  The second is conditionally centered and bounded by the H\"older envelope; applying the bounded centered moment-generating inequality after conditioning on \(S_{P,x}\) shows that their sum is conditionally sub-Gaussian with a constant depending only on \(L\).

Take a maximal \(n^{-3}\)-net of the trimmed boundary.  The smooth-boundary length lemma, or the ambient square bound, gives polynomial cardinality.  Each Gram entry, after the \(h^{-2}\) scaling, is bounded by \(Ch^{-2}\), while its second moment is bounded by \(Ch^{-2}\) because \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\leq\overline c s\).  Exponential Markov applied entrywise and a union bound therefore give
\[
 \sup_{P,t,x}\left\|\widehat G_{t,x}(h)-
 \mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)\right\|
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}
ight)
\]
with complement probability at most \(n^{-4}\).  The kernel-polynomial weight is Lipschitz after extension by zero outside \([0,1]\); its oscillation between a point and its net representative is \(O(n^{-3}h^{-3})\), which is negligible at the bandwidth below.  Weyl's inequality then makes the cutoff pass uniformly because \(nh^2/\log n\to\infty\).

At a net point, conditionally on the signed radii, the coefficient of each centered outcome in the stabilized intercept is at most \(C/(nh^2)\), and the sum of squared coefficients is at most \(C/(nh^2)\).  The preceding conditional sub-Gaussian bound therefore gives
\[
 P\!\left\{|\widehat\mu^{(m)}_{t,h}(x)
 -\mathbb E[\widehat\mu^{(m)}_{t,h}(x)\mid D_{P,x,1:n}]|>u\right\}
 \leq2e^{-cnh^2u^2}
\]
on the good Gram event.  A union bound over the net and tail integration produce \(C\sqrt{\log n/(nh^2)}\).  To extend off the net, work with the original outcome vector \(\widehat r_{t,x}\): its kernel weights are Lipschitz in \(x\), and conditional sub-Gaussianity gives \(\mathbb E\max_i|Y_i|^2=O(\log n)\).  The \(n^{-3}\)-mesh error is therefore negligible.  The H\"older condition similarly controls the target's mesh oscillation.  On the exceptional Gram event the stabilized estimator is zero and the target is uniformly bounded, so its expected contribution is negligible.  Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m)}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Balancing the terms gives
\[
 h\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(2m+2)},
 \qquad
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal C)
 \leq C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]

For the conditional converse, use the uniform-design straight-interface submodel whose containment in \(\mathcal C\) is the theorem's explicit premise.  Place \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) disjoint smooth bivariate regression bumps of width \(h\) and height \(c h^m\) at trimmed centers, and use Gaussian-noised Bernoulli outcomes.  The premise supplies submodel membership; directly, adjacent target values differ by \(c h^m\), while one bump has score mass \(O(h^2)\).  The Bernoulli relative-entropy inequality and data processing give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn h^{2m+2}.
\]
At the displayed bandwidth, choose the fixed amplitude small enough that this is at most \(\alpha\log M\), with \(\alpha<1\).  For a uniform bump index \(J\),
\[
 I(J;O_{1:n})\leq M^{-1}\sum_j
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n}),
 \qquad
 I(J;O_{1:n})\geq(1-p_e)\log M-\log2.
\]
The second inequality follows by decomposing \(H(J\mid O_{1:n})\) according to the decoding-error indicator.  Thus every decoder errs with probability bounded away from zero.  Sup-norm accuracy below half the bump height would decode the center, proving the matching lower bound \(ch^m\).

If a class contains the shrinking angular-imbalance family, its adjacent separation is \(c a_n\).  In the mean-\(2n\) Poisson experiment, disjoint local point processes are conditionally independent standard-Borel channels and the outside process is bit-independent.  The packing entropy slack and the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma give probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\) of some bit error, even after all cross-center observations are revealed.  Thresholding and fixed-sample subsampling yield the lower frontier \(ca_n\).  At \(m=1\), the upper rate equals \(a_n\), while for \(m\geq2\), \(m/(2m+2)>1/4\).

Finally, the normalized coarea product, rather than density smoothness in isolation, is the object used in every bias calculation.  Bounded continuity alone is not sufficient: it permits the shrinking angular-imbalance family and its \(a_n\) lower bound.  Nor is density differentiability necessary for the upper order: with a bounded continuous positive but nondifferentiable density and a regression locally constant on the relevant circles, one has \(N_{t,P,x}(s)/Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\mu_{t,P}(x)\) exactly.  More generally, the same continuous radial-angular density can be paired either with such a constant regression or, for \(m\geq2\), with an affine regression whose angular product moment has a nonpolynomial remainder of order below \(m\).  Thus no necessary-and-sufficient threshold stated solely in terms of \(f_P\) can characterize the faster order; the joint \(N/Q\) remainder is the operational condition.  Throughout, \(\tau_P\) is the causal curve, while \(N/Q\) is its identifying radial regression and is not a redefinition of the causal target.
