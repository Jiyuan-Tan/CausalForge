We verify an explicit family, including support, normalization, positivity, smoothness, moment, local-mass, separation, and divergence conditions.  Begin with \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\), the lower and upper half-square assignment regions, and
\[
 \mathcal B=\{(u-1/2,0):0\leq u\leq1\},
 \qquad \gamma(u)=(u-1/2,0).
\]
The base density is one.  The chart is injective and \(C^\infty\).  At either endpoint, the intersection of either arm with a ball of radius at most \(r_0\leq1/4\) contains a quarter disk.  Every density below is at least \(3/4\), so the arm mass is at least \(3\pi r^2/16>c_-r^2\), because \(c_-\leq1/(16L)\).  Away from the endpoints the same bound is immediate.  Compact support, the assignment partition, and two-sided thickness are therefore uniform.

Choose a nonnegative radial \(C^\infty\) function \(\phi\), compactly supported in the open unit disk, with \(\phi(0)=1\).  For fixed constants \(d,d_1>0\), put
\[
 \Delta=d a_n,
 \qquad w=(d_1\Delta)^{1/q}.
\]
Choose centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) on \(\gamma([\delta,1-\delta])\), separated by at least \(3w\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), their radius-\(w\) disks are disjoint and lie in the interior of \(S\), and interval packing gives
\[
 M\geq c w^{-1}\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}.
\]
Writing \(\phi_j(z)=\phi((z-x_j)/w)\), for \(0\leq k\leq q\) one has
\[
 \lVert D^k(\Delta\phi_j)\rVert_\infty
 \leq C_\phi\Delta w^{-k}.
\]
For \(k=q\) this equals \(C_\phi/d_1\), and for \(k<q\) it tends to zero.  Disjoint supports give the same bounds for every signed sum.  Taking \(d_1\) large and then a fixed affine slope \(b>0\) small places all regressions below in the prescribed \(q\)-H\"older ball.

For \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\), define
\[
 p_{0,\omega}(z)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 p_{1,\omega}(z)=\frac12+bz_1+\Delta\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j\phi_j(z).
\]
After decreasing \(b\) and \(d\), these functions lie in \([1/4,3/4]\).  Conditional on \(X=z\), take independent potential outcomes
\[
 Y(t)=B_t+\varepsilon_t,
 \qquad B_t\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_{t,\omega}(z)),
 \qquad \varepsilon_t\sim N(0,1),
\]
and impose deterministic assignment and consistency.  Then \(\mu_{t,P_\omega}=p_{t,\omega}\) and
\[
 1\leq\sigma^2_{t,P_\omega}(z)
 =1+p_{t,\omega}(z)\{1-p_{t,\omega}(z)\}
 \leq\frac54,
\]
which lies in \([L^{-1},L]\).  Hoeffding's exponential bound and the Gaussian moment-generating identity give
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[e^{\lambda\{Y(t)-p_{t,\omega}(X)\}}\mid X\right]
 \leq e^{\lambda^2/8}e^{\lambda^2/2}
 \leq e^{L^2\lambda^2/2}.
\]

To hide coordinate \(j\) in the radial experiment centered at \(x_j\), choose a continuous cutoff \(\chi\) with \(\chi(v)=0\) for \(v\leq1\) and \(\chi(v)=1\) for \(v\geq2\), and define
\[
 A_n(r)=-\frac{2\Delta\phi(r/w)}{br}
 \chi\!\left(\frac{br}{8\Delta}\right),
\]
with value zero on the neighborhood of zero where the cutoff vanishes.  Thus there is no division at zero.  On the active region \(br\geq8\Delta\), \(|A_n(r)|\leq1/4\).  Choose \(d_1\) also so that \(16\Delta/b<w\), including when \(q=1\).  Define
\[
 f_\omega(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_jA_n(\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2)
 \frac{z_1-(x_j)_1}{\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2}
 \mathbf1\{z\in B_2(x_j,w)\},
\]
with every summand set to zero at its center.  Disjointness makes the formula unambiguous.  Each angular correction integrates to zero on every full circle, so \(\int_Sf_\omega=1\).  The cutoff at the center and compact support of \(\phi\) at the outer edge make \(f_\omega\) continuous, and \(3/4\leq f_\omega\leq5/4\), which is inside \([L^{-1},L]\).

Compare adjacent vertices \(\omega\) and \(\omega^{(j)}\), oriented so that \(\omega_j=1\).  On either half-circle centered at \(x_j\), \(\int\cos\theta\,d\theta=0\); hence the two arm-specific radial mass densities agree at every radius.  On the treated upper half-circle, whenever \(br\geq16\Delta\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\int_0^\pi p_{1,\omega}(x_j+r e_\theta)
       \{1+A_n(r)\cos\theta\}\,d\theta
 -\int_0^\pi p_{1,\omega^{(j)}}(x_j+r e_\theta)\,d\theta\\
 &\qquad=\pi\Delta\phi(r/w)+\frac\pi2brA_n(r)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The control equality follows from its constant regression.  Angular mixing of a Bernoulli variable produces a Bernoulli variable with the averaged success probability, and addition of the common Gaussian noise is a common Markov kernel.  Thus the complete arm-specific radial outcome laws agree outside \(r\leq16\Delta/b\).

Inside that exceptional radius, the common radial density is at most \(Cr\), and the two averaged Bernoulli probabilities differ by at most \(C\Delta\) while remaining in \([1/4,3/4]\).  From \(\log u\leq u-1\),
\[
 \operatorname{kl}(p,p')\leq\frac{(p-p')^2}{p'(1-p')},
\]
and relative entropy cannot increase through the common Gaussian channel.  Hence one compressed observation obeys
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P^{\mathrm{dist}}_{\omega,x_j},
 P^{\mathrm{dist}}_{\omega^{(j)},x_j})
 \leq C\int_0^{16\Delta/b}\Delta^2r\,dr
 \leq C'\Delta^4.
\]
Independence tensorizes relative entropy, so the sample divergence is at most
\[
 C'n\Delta^4=C'd^4\log n.
\]
Moreover \(\log M=(1/(4q)+o(1))\log n\).  Choosing \(d\) sufficiently small makes this at most \(\alpha\log M\) for a fixed \(\alpha<1/8\).  At \(x_j\), the adjacent target values differ by \(\Delta\phi_j(x_j)=d a_n\), so take \(c_1=d\).  The fixed half-square geometry and the global density bounds give common local-mass constants.  This verifies membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\cap\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\).

For the canonical square \([-1,1]^2\), use \(\gamma(u)=(2u-1,0)\) and base density \(1/4\).  If \(L>4\), put \(\varepsilon_L=1-4/L>0\), replace the cutoff constant eight by a sufficiently large constant depending on \(L\), and multiply the relative angular correction by the base density.  Choose \(d_1\) large enough that cancellation begins inside every bump and then choose \(d/b\) small enough that all disks stay inside the trimmed square.  The relative perturbation can be bounded by \(\varepsilon_L/2\), whence
\[
 L^{-1}\leq\frac14(1-\varepsilon_L/2)
 \leq f_\omega(z)
 \leq\frac14(1+\varepsilon_L/2)\leq L.
\]
All preceding cancellation and entropy calculations change only by constants depending on \(L\).  Thus the family lies in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\).  At \(L=4\), normalization and \(f_P\geq1/4\) on a square of area four force \(f_P\equiv1/4\), explaining exactly why the canonical-square clause requires \(L>4\).
