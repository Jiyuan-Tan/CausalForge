\textbf{Proof.}
All constants below may depend on the frozen class parameters, on \(q\), and on \(\delta\), but not on \(n\) or on the member law \(P\).  We prove nonemptiness, the full-location bounds, and then the two distance regimes.

\emph{Nonemptiness and boundary conditions.}
Take \(X\) uniform on \([-1,1]^2\), use the prescribed two half-squares and the chart \(\gamma_P(u)=(2u-1,0)\), and take constant regression functions with values in \((1/4,3/4)\).  Given \(X\), let each potential outcome be the sum of a Bernoulli variable having the prescribed regression and an independent \(N(0,1)\) variable.  Impose the deterministic assignment and consistency conditions.  The density is \(1/4\), the chart has speed two and all derivatives of order at least two equal to zero, and the line segment has infinite reach.  Every boundary ball of radius at most \(r_0\) contains, in each arm, at least a quarter disk, and hence has arm probability at least \(\pi r^2/16\geq c_-r^2\).  Moreover,
\[
 1\leq \sigma^2_{t,P}(x)\leq \frac54,
\]
and the product of the centered-Bernoulli and Gaussian moment-generating functions is at most \(\exp(5\lambda^2/8)\), which is bounded by the required \(\exp(L^2\lambda^2/2)\).  Since \(L\geq4\), all support, density, H\"older, variance, tail, chart, reach, positivity, and two-sided-thickness conditions hold.  Thus \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\) is nonempty.

\emph{A uniform cutoff calculation.}
We record the calculation carefully because it also controls the exceptional event.  Let
\[
 \mathcal I_q=\{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^2:|\alpha|\leq q-1\},
 \qquad r_q(u)=(u^\alpha: \alpha\in\mathcal I_q),
 \qquad K_2(u)=(1-\lVert u\rVert_2)_+,
\]
and let \(e_0\) select the constant monomial.  For a boundary point \(x\), arm \(t\), and bandwidth \(h\), define
\[
 \widehat G_{t,x}(h)=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_2\!\left(\frac{X_i-x}{h}\right)
 r_q\!\left(\frac{X_i-x}{h}\right)r_q\!\left(\frac{X_i-x}{h}\right)^{\!\top},
\]
\[
 \widehat z_{t,x}(h)=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_2\!\left(\frac{X_i-x}{h}\right)
 r_q\!\left(\frac{X_i-x}{h}\right)Y_i.
\]
For \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\) and all sufficiently small \(h\), the rescaled support in arm \(t\) contains a fixed half-disk.  Therefore the density lower envelope gives, for every coefficient vector \(v\),
\[
 v^\top\mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)v
 \geq L^{-1}\int_{H_t\cap B_2(0,1/2)}
 K_2(u)\{v^\top r_q(u)\}^2\,du,
\]
where \(H_t\) is the appropriate open half-plane.  A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on this open half-disk.  Compactness of the Euclidean unit sphere in the fixed-dimensional coefficient space consequently supplies a constant \(\lambda_*>0\), uniform in \(P,t,x,h\), such that
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\{\mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)\}\geq\lambda_*.
\]
Fix \(\kappa=\lambda_*/2\) and set
\[
 \widehat\mu^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,h}(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 e_0^\top\widehat G_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\widehat z_{t,x}(h),
 &\lambda_{\min}\{\widehat G_{t,x}(h)\}\geq\kappa,\\
 0,&\lambda_{\min}\{\widehat G_{t,x}(h)\}<\kappa.
 \end{cases}
\]
The arm difference is a jointly measurable function of the full sample and the known geometry, and hence belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\).

Here is a self-contained uniform concentration bound used below.  The kernel and monomials are bounded on their support.  By the density upper envelope, the second moment of an unnormalised kernel summand is \(O(h^2)\).  Expanding the conditional sub-Gaussian moment-generating-function bound and then integrating over \(X\) gives, for the centered outcome summands and for \(0\leq u\leq1\),
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left(
 \left\lVert\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_2\!\left(\frac{X_i-x}{h}\right)
 r_q\!\left(\frac{X_i-x}{h}\right)
 \{Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\}\right\rVert_2>u
 \right)
 \leq C\exp(-cnh^2u^2).
\]
The same exponential bound holds entrywise for \(\widehat G_{t,x}(h)-\mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)\).  Indeed, for a scalar summand \(W\varepsilon\), the conditional bound gives \(\mathbb E[e^{\lambda W\varepsilon}\mid X]\leq e^{L^2\lambda^2W^2/2}\); boundedness of \(W\), together with \(\mathbb E W^2=O(h^2)\), yields \(\log\mathbb E e^{\lambda W\varepsilon}\leq C\lambda^2h^2\) for bounded \(|\lambda|\), and Chernoff optimization yields the display.  The bounded Gram case is identical with the usual centered exponential expansion.

Cover the trimmed line segment by a grid of mesh \(h n^{-B}\), where the fixed integer \(B\) is chosen large.  Its cardinality is polynomial in \(n\).  A union bound, the Lipschitz property of the kernel, and
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|Y_i|>C_B\sqrt{\log n}\right\}\leq Cn^{-20}
\]
extend the preceding fixed-point bounds to every trimmed boundary point.  This extension uses only the density upper envelope: translating a kernel by \(d\) changes its population integral by at most \(C d/h\), so no common modulus of continuity for \(f_P\) is used.  At the bandwidths selected below, \(nh^2/\log n\to\infty\).  Increasing the constants in the thresholds gives an event \(E_{n,h}\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}}P(E_{n,h}^{c})\leq Cn^{-12},
\]
and, on \(E_{n,h}\), all empirical Gram matrices have minimum eigenvalue at least \(\kappa\), while all centered outcome vectors are bounded by
\[
 C\varepsilon_{n,h},\qquad
 \varepsilon_{n,h}=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}.
\]

Taylor's formula through total degree \(q-1\), using \(\mathrm{ass:holder\text{-}regressions}\), writes \(\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\) inside a kernel window as the corresponding polynomial plus a remainder bounded by \(Ch^q\).  Multiplication by the empirical inverse on \(E_{n,h}\) therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,h}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|
 \leq C\{h^q+\varepsilon_{n,h}\}.
\]
It remains to justify expectation loss on \(E_{n,h}^{c}\).  The cutoff does not generally fail everywhere on that event.  Instead, whenever it is active it gives the deterministic inverse bound
\[
 |\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,h}(x)|
 \leq\kappa^{-1}\lVert\widehat z_{t,x}(h)\rVert_2,
\]
while on cutoff failure the estimate is zero.  Uniformly in \(P\), the tail and H\"older envelopes imply \(\mathbb E_PY^2\leq C\), and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_x\lVert\widehat z_{t,x}(h)\rVert_2^2\right]
 \leq \frac{C}{h^4}\mathbb E_P\!\left[\left\{\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|\right\}^2\right]
 \leq Ch^{-4}.
\]
Since \(|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L\), Cauchy--Schwarz now yields the explicit exceptional-event bound
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \mathbf 1_{E_{n,h}^{c}}
 \sup_x|\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{loc}}_{1,h}(x)
 -\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{loc}}_{0,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
 \leq C\{n^{-12}+h^{-2}n^{-6}\}.
\]
Thus the cutoff controls the inverse on cutoff-active exceptional outcomes, and boundedness of the target controls cutoff-fail outcomes; no assertion that the estimate vanishes throughout \(E_{n,h}^{c}\) is needed.

Combining the good- and exceptional-event calculations and choosing
\[
 h\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(2q+2)}
\]
proves
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \leq C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{q/(2q+2)}=Cs_{n,q}.
\]

\emph{Full-location converse.}
Let \(\phi\) be a fixed smooth nonnegative bump supported in the open unit disk with \(\phi(0)=1\).  Put
\[
 h\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(2q+2)}
\]
and choose \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) trimmed boundary centers whose radius-\(h\) disks are disjoint and contained in the square.  Keep the score density equal to \(1/4\), set the control regression to \(1/2\), and, for \(1\leq j\leq M\), set the treated regression to
\[
 \mu_{1,P_j}(z)=\frac12+c h^q\phi\!\left(\frac{z-x_j}{h}\right).
\]
Use Bernoulli potential outcomes plus independent standard Gaussian noise as above.  Because
\[
 \lVert D^k\{c h^q\phi((\,\cdot-x_j)/h)\}\rVert_\infty
 \leq C_\phi c h^{q-k},\qquad 0\leq k\leq q,
\]
a sufficiently small fixed \(c\) puts every alternative in the same \(q\)-H\"older ball.  The uniform density, straight geometry, variance, sub-Gaussian, positivity, support, reach, and thickness conditions are unchanged.  Thus this is a contained submodel of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\), and distinct target curves are separated in sup norm by at least \(c h^q\).

Let \(P_0\) denote the no-bump law.  The inequality
\[
 \operatorname{kl}(p,p')\leq\frac{(p-p')^2}{p'(1-p')}
\]
follows from \(\log u\leq u-1\).  Integrating it over the bump disk and using data processing under the common Gaussian channel gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cc^2nh^{2q+2}.
\]
Since \(\log M\asymp\log n\), reduce \(c\), if necessary, so the right-hand side is at most \(\alpha\log M\) for a fixed \(\alpha<1/2\).  If \(J\) is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,M\}\) and \(Z=O_{1:n}\), the log-sum inequality gives
\[
 I(J;Z)\leq\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
For any decoder with error probability \(p_e\), conditioning entropy on the error indicator gives
\[
 H(J\mid Z)\leq\log2+p_e\log M,
\]
so \(p_e\geq1-\alpha-o(1)\).  Nearest-curve decoding from any estimator shows that a decoding error entails sup-norm loss at least \(c h^q/2\).  Averaging over \(J\), and then bounding the average by the worst-case risk, proves
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq c' h^q=c's_{n,q}.
\]
This proves the first asserted equivalence.

\emph{Distance experiment when \(L>4\).}
For each arm define the distance-only local-constant quantities
\[
 \overline G_{t,x}(h)=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 K_\triangle(D_{P,x,i}/h)\mathbf 1\{T_i=t\},
 \qquad
 \overline z_{t,x}(h)=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 K_\triangle(D_{P,x,i}/h)\mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}Y_i.
\]
The sign of \(D_{P,x,i}\) recovers the arm outside a probability-zero boundary event, so these are measurable functions of \(U_{n,P}(x)\).  Use \(\overline z_{t,x}(h)/\overline G_{t,x}(h)\) when \(\overline G_{t,x}(h)\geq\kappa_d\), use zero otherwise, and subtract arms.  From \(K_\triangle\geq1/2\) on \([-1/2,1/2]\) and the two-sided-thickness condition,
\[
 \mathbb E_P\overline G_{t,x}(h)
 \geq\frac1{2h^2}P\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,h/2)\}
 \geq\frac{c_-}{8}
\]
for \(h\leq r_0\).  Take \(\kappa_d<c_-/16\).  The H\"older condition with \(q\geq1\) gives \(|\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq Ch\) inside the window.  The same grid and moment-generating-function calculation as above gives a uniform event of complement probability at most \(Cn^{-12}\), on which all denominators pass the cutoff and the centered numerator error is at most \(C\varepsilon_{n,h}\).  On the complement, cutoff-active ratios satisfy \(|\overline z/\overline G|\leq\kappa_d^{-1}|\overline z|\), cutoff-fail ratios are zero, and
\[
 \mathbb E_P\sup_x|\overline z_{t,x}(h)|^2\leq Ch^{-4}.
\]
The same Cauchy--Schwarz calculation therefore bounds the exceptional contribution by \(C\{n^{-12}+h^{-2}n^{-6}\}\).  Consequently this single distance-only rule satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}
\]
at the relevant bandwidths.  Taking \(h\asymp a_n\) proves the upper bound \(Ca_n\).

For the converse, use the canonical-square hypercube supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:angular\text{-}density\text{-}packing}\), which is contained in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\) when \(L>4\).  Poissonize the observations with mean \(2n\) and attach independent continuous ordering marks.  Decompose the marked Poisson process into the disjoint bump cells \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) and their complement \(Z_0\).  Conditional on the independent uniform hypercube bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\), these channels are independent; \(Z_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), and \(Z_0\) is bit-independent.  Let \(S_j\) retain from \(Z_j\) only outcomes, signed radii about \(x_j\), and ordering marks.  A coordinatewise distance decoder at \(x_j\), even after being given the full \(Z_{-j}\) and \(Z_0\), is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\).

Poisson relative entropy is intensity times one-observation relative entropy.  The adjacent radial laws in the packing agree outside the \(j\)-th local channel and have equal local masses.  Hence the fixed-sample entropy bound in that lemma implies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\{\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=0),
 \mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=1)\}
 \leq2\alpha\log M.
\]
Because \(2\alpha<1\), \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\) shows that the probability of at least one bit error is at least \(1/2-o(1)\).  Thresholding each estimated target value at the midpoint of its two possible values turns sup-norm error smaller than \(c_1a_n/2\) into simultaneous correct decoding.  Thus every coordinatewise distance rule in the mean-\(2n\) Poisson experiment has Bayes expected sup-norm loss at least \(c a_n\).

To transfer this bound to exactly \(n\) observations, start with an arbitrary fixed-\(n\) distance rule.  In the Poisson experiment, on the event that the count is at least \(n\), apply it to the \(n\) observations having the smallest independent ordering marks; otherwise output zero.  In the direct-product representation, the marked radial observations from cell \(j\) are precisely the compression \(S_j\), while the other cells and the common complement determine which marks are selected, so the preceding decentralized bound applies to this induced rule.  Conditional on a count of at least \(n\), the selected observations are i.i.d. from \(P\).  The Poisson lower-tail Chernoff bound gives \(\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(2n)<n\}\leq e^{-cn}\), and \(\sup_x|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L\).  Therefore
\[
 c a_n\leq
 \sup_P\mathbb E_P^{\operatorname{Poi}(2n)}[\text{sup-loss}]
 \leq\sup_P\mathbb E_P^{n}[\text{sup-loss}]+2Le^{-cn}.
\]
Taking the infimum over the original fixed-sample rules proves
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})\geq c'a_n.
\]
Together with the local-constant upper bound, this proves the \(L>4\) assertion.

\emph{Distance experiment when \(L=4\).}
The square has Lebesgue area four and the density lower envelope is \(1/4\).  Since
\[
 1=\int_{[-1,1]^2}f_P(z)\,dz\geq\int_{[-1,1]^2}\frac14\,dz=1,
\]
we have \(f_P=1/4\) almost everywhere; density continuity upgrades this to pointwise equality.  If \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\) and \(0<s<h_0(\delta)\), each arm-specific circle portion is a half-circle contained in the square, so polar coordinates give
\[
 Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\frac\pi4s.
\]
Consistency and deterministic assignment then show that the conditional arm mean at radius \(s\) is the uniform half-circle average of \(\mu_{t,P}\).  Taylor expansion of the bivariate regression through degree \(q-1\), followed by half-circle integration and grouping terms of equal total degree, yields coefficients \(b_{t,k,P}(x)\), uniformly bounded by the H\"older envelope, such that
\[
 \left|g_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 -\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k\right|\leq Cs^q.
\]
This conditioning is legitimate because \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\) for every displayed \(s\).

Let \(r_q^{\mathrm{rad}}(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^{q-1})^\top\).  Define the one-sided radial Gram matrix and outcome vector by
\[
 \widehat G^{\mathrm{rad}}_{t,x}(h)=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_\triangle(D_{P,x,i}/h)
 r_q^{\mathrm{rad}}(|D_{P,x,i}|/h)
 r_q^{\mathrm{rad}}(|D_{P,x,i}|/h)^\top,
\]
\[
 \widehat z^{\mathrm{rad}}_{t,x}(h)=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_\triangle(D_{P,x,i}/h)
 r_q^{\mathrm{rad}}(|D_{P,x,i}|/h)Y_i.
\]
Use the intercept when the smallest Gram eigenvalue exceeds a fixed cutoff, and zero otherwise.  This is a measurable function of \(U_{n,P}(x)\).  Its population Gram matrix is exactly
\[
 \frac\pi4\int_0^1K_\triangle(u)r_q^{\mathrm{rad}}(u)
 r_q^{\mathrm{rad}}(u)^\top u\,du.
\]
Its quadratic form is the integral of the square of a polynomial against a strictly positive weight on \((0,1)\), so it is positive definite.  This supplies the required uniform rank and cutoff constant.

Given the signed radius, \(Y-g_{t,P,x}(|D|)\) is uniformly sub-Gaussian: condition first on \(X\), use \(\mathrm{ass:conditional\text{-}subgaussian}\), and then apply the elementary bounded-variable moment-generating-function inequality to the centered variable \(\mu_{t,P}(X)-\mathbb E\{\mu_{t,P}(X)\mid D\}\).  The radial Taylor remainder, the same exponential grid argument, and the same cutoff-active moment calculation therefore give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{rad}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
More explicitly, on the concentration complement the active inverse is bounded by the cutoff, the raw radial outcome vector has squared supremum moment at most \(Ch^{-4}\), and cutoff-fail estimates are zero; hence that complement contributes at most \(C\{n^{-12}+h^{-2}n^{-6}\}\).  Balancing at \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\) proves the distance upper bound \(Cs_{n,q}\).

Finally, every distance-only rule can be implemented from the full scores and the known assignment geometry by computing each signed distance.  Thus \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\) is a restriction of the available full-location decision rules, and
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq c s_{n,q}.
\]
This proves the \(L=4\) equivalence.  Since
\[
 \frac{q}{2q+2}>\frac14\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q>1,
\]
the two exponents are strictly separated precisely in the asserted \(L>4,q>1\) regime, and they coincide when \(q=1\) or \(L=4\).  \(\square\)
