Fix \(P,x,t\).  Apply the polar-coordinate coarea formula to the nonnegative measurable function
\(z\mapsto f_P(z)\mathbf1\{z\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P\}\).  Since the distance map \(z\mapsto\|z-x\|_2\) has gradient of norm one away from \(x\),
\[
 P\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,r)\}=\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
The thickness assumption supplies the lower bound \(c_-r^2\).  If \(Q_{t,P,x}\) vanished almost everywhere on some interval \((0,\varepsilon)\), the integral would vanish for \(r\leq\varepsilon\), contradicting that lower bound.  Hence positive-denominator radii accumulate at zero.

Apply the same coarea identity after multiplying by \(\mu_{t,P}\).  Deterministic assignment and consistency give, for almost every positive radius with positive denominator,
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
 ={N_{t,P,x}(s)\over Q_{t,P,x}(s)}.
\]
Because \(q\geq1\), the stated H\"older ball gives \(|\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\|z-x\|_2\).  Therefore, when \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\),
\[
 \left|{N_{t,P,x}(s)\over Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \leq {\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P}Ls f_P\,d\mathcal H^1\over Q_{t,P,x}(s)}=Ls.
\]
On a zero-denominator radius and at zero, the canonical definition sets the value equal to \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\), so the same inequality holds everywhere.  Letting \(s\downarrow0\) in the two arms and subtracting gives
\[
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}\{g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P,x}(s)-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P,x}(s)\}
 =\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
\]
