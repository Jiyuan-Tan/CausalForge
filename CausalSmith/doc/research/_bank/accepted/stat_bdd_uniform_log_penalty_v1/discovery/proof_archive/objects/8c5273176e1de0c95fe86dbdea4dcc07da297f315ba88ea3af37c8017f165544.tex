The upper bound uses the same explicit local-constant estimator.  For \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), the stability lemma gives a population denominator at least \(\kappa_0h^2\), the upper-mass lemma gives an upper bound \(\pi Lh^2\), and \(q\geq1\) gives bias at most \(Lh\).  The smooth-boundary length lemma supplies a uniform finite net of the trimmed curve.  Applying the bounded-weight Bernstein and conditional sub-Gaussian calculations from first principles gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}}
\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Indeed, a boundary net of mesh \(n^{-3}\) has polynomial cardinality, the triangular weights are one-Lipschitz in the center, and the exceptional-event contribution is controlled by \(\mathbb E\max_i|Y_i|^2=O(\log n)\).  All constants are uniform by the displayed density, moment, chart, reach, and mass envelopes.  Taking \(h\asymp a_n\) proves the upper limsup.

For the lower bound, the unit-square construction in the narrowed angular-density packing lemma belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\) for every \(L\geq4\): it uses \(\gamma(u)=(u-1/2,0)\), whose length and speed are one, whose higher derivatives vanish, and whose image has infinite reach; all bump disks are eventually contained in the endpoint-trimmed part.  Its target separation is \(c_1a_n\).  Poissonizing its disjoint disks at mean \(2n\), restricting decoder \(j\)'s own-center data to the corresponding compressed local process, and treating every cross-center observation as arbitrary ancillary side information puts the experiment exactly under the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma.  The Kullback--Leibler slack in the packing gives an exponent \(\kappa<1\), so the probability of at least one bit error is at least \(1/2-o(1)\).  Thresholding the estimated curve converts sup-norm error below half the separation into exact bit recovery.  The mean-\(2n\) Poisson count is below \(n\) with exponentially small probability, so uniform subsampling de-Poissonizes the bound.  Consequently
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}})}{a_n}>0.
\]
Combining both inequalities proves saturation.  The lower family has disjoint \(q\)-H\"older bump perturbations of affine baselines; no common density modulus, and no affine-only regression claim, is used.
