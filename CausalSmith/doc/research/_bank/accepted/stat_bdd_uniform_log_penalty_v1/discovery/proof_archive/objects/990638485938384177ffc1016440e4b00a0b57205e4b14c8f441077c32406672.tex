Let \(h=h_n\asymp a_n\).  We first prove the upper bound with the single estimator specified in the statement.  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), put
\[
 W_{i,t,x}=\mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_\triangle(D_{P,x,i}/h),
 \qquad A_{t,x}=\sum_{i=1}^nW_{i,t,x}.
\]
Because \(K_\triangle\) is symmetric, the arm is recovered from the sign of \(D_{P,x,i}\) except on the probability-zero event \(D_{P,x,i}=0\).  Thus the estimator is a measurable function of \(U_{n,P}(x)\) at every \(x\), and \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).

On arm \(t\), let \(\varepsilon_{i,t}=Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\).  Whenever \(A_{t,x}>0\), algebra gives
\[
 \widehat\mu_{t,h}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 =\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}}{A_{t,x}}
 +\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\{\mu_{t,P}(X_i)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\}}{A_{t,x}}.
\]
Every nonzero weight has \(\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2\leq h\).  Since \(q\geq1\), the H\"older condition bounds the second term by \(Lh\).  The stability and upper-mass lemmas give, uniformly in \(P,t,x\),
\[
 \kappa_0nh^2\leq\mathbb E_PA_{t,x}\leq\pi Lnh^2.
\]
These are the positivity and scale bounds used below.

Take a maximal \(n^{-3}\)-net of \(\mathcal B_P\).  Since the boundary lies in \([-L,L]^2\), the net has at most \(C_Ln^6\) points.  For a fixed net point, the summands in \(A_{t,x}\) lie in \([0,1]\) and have total variance at most \(\mathbb E_PA_{t,x}\).  Exponential Markov applied to the elementary bounded-variable moment-generating bound therefore yields
\[
 P\{|A_{t,x}-\mathbb E_PA_{t,x}|>u\}
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2(\mathbb E_PA_{t,x}+u/3)}\right\}.
\]
A union bound, followed by the one-Lipschitz property of \(K_\triangle\), yields an event \(E_n\) such that \(\sup_PP(E_n^c)\leq n^{-4}\) and
\[
 \inf_{t,x}A_{t,x}\geq\frac{\kappa_0}{2}nh^2,
 \qquad \sup_{t,x}A_{t,x}\leq Cnh^2.
\]
Indeed, moving a center by \(n^{-3}\) changes \(A_{t,x}\) by at most \(n^{-2}/h=o(nh^2)\), and \(nh^2/\log n\to\infty\).

Conditionally on \(X_{1:n}\), independence and the conditional sub-Gaussian assumption give
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left(\left.\left|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|>u\,\right|X_{1:n}\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2L^2\sum_iW_{i,t,x}^2}\right\}
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2L^2A_{t,x}}\right\}.
\]
Union over the net, integration of this tail, and the denominator bounds on \(E_n\) give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{t,x}\frac{|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}|}{A_{t,x}}
 \mathbf1_{E_n}\right]
 \leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+o(a_n).
\]
For the extension off the net, the expected numerator oscillation is at most
\(n^{-3}h^{-1}\mathbb E_P\sum_i|\varepsilon_{i,t}|=O(n^{-2}/h)\), which is negligible after division by \(nh^2\).  The H\"older ball bounds the regressions uniformly, and conditional sub-Gaussianity implies
\(\sup_P\mathbb E_P\max_{i\leq n}|Y_i|^2=O(\log n)\).  On \(E_n^c\), every nonempty-denominator local average is bounded by \(\max_i|Y_i|\); Cauchy--Schwarz makes its expected contribution \(o(a_n)\).  A zero denominator lies in \(E_n^c\) and is covered by the estimator's zero convention.  Summing the two arms proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Balancing gives \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/4}=a_n\), proving the upper limsup.

For the converse, apply the angular-density packing with, say, the fixed trim \(\delta=1/4\).  It gives \(M=M_n\) disjoint centers and adjacent target separation \(\Delta=c_1a_n\).  Poissonize the sample at mean \(2n\).  Conditional on the hypercube bits, the marked observations in the disjoint bump disks are independent Poisson processes \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\); the outside process \(Z_0\) is bit-independent.  These point-process spaces are standard Borel.  Decoder \(j\)'s observations from its own disk form a measurable signed-distance compression \(S_j\) of \(Z_j\), while revealing \(Z_{-j}\) and \(Z_0\) only makes decoding easier.  Equality of adjacent radial mass laws and Poisson relative-entropy tensorization give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
 \leq2\alpha\log M=:\kappa\log M,
 \qquad \kappa<1.
\]
The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma therefore yields, under the uniform product prior,
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat\omega_j\neq\omega_j\text{ for at least one }j\}
 \geq\frac12-o(1).
\]
At \(x_j\), thresholding \(\widehat\tau_n(x_j)\) at the midpoint of the two possible target values recovers bit \(j\) whenever the sup-norm error is below \(\Delta/2\).  Thus the Poissonized Bayes expected sup-norm loss is at least
\((\Delta/2)(1/2-o(1))\).

To return to a fixed sample, on the event that a Poisson count \(N\) of mean \(2n\) is at least \(n\), select \(n\) observations uniformly and apply any fixed-\(n\) rule.  The selected observations have law \(P^{\otimes n}\), and \(P(N<n)\leq e^{-cn}\).  On the complement use the zero curve.  The target is uniformly bounded by the H\"older envelope, so the de-Poissonization remainder is \(o(\Delta)\).  Hence every fixed-\(n\) rule has worst-law risk at least \(ca_n\), and
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}})}{a_n}>0.
\]
Combining the two bounds proves the theorem.  The causal target remains \(\tau_P\); the canonical radial regression provides its observed-data limit and does not define the causal effect.
