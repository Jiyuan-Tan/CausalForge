We first prove the upper bound.  Fix an arm \(t\), a boundary point \(x\), and \(0<h\leq r_0\).  Write \(R_x=\lVert X-x\rVert_2\) and
\[
 W_{t,x,h}=h^{-2}\mathbf1\{T=t\}K_\triangle(R_x/h).
\]
For fixed \(x\), score absolute continuity gives \(P(X=x)=0\), so the strict-sign summand in the estimator has these population moments.  The stability lemma gives
\[
 d_{t,P,x}(h):=\mathbb E_PW_{t,x,h}\geq\kappa_0.
\]
The upper-mass lemma, \(0\leq K_\triangle\leq1\), the H\"older-ball sup-norm bound, consistency, and conditional sub-Gaussianity imply
\[
 \mathbb E_PW_{t,x,h}^2\leq \pi Lh^{-2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P\{W_{t,x,h}Y\}^2\leq Ch^{-2}.
\]
The same conditioning gives, for every integer \(k\geq2\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P|W_{t,x,h}\{Y-\mu_{t,P}(X)\}|^k
 \leq k!C^k h^{2-2k}.
\]
Expanding the exponential series and applying exponential Markov inequality yields constants \(c,C>0\), uniform in \(P,t,x\), such that for every centered denominator or numerator summand \(Z_{t,x,h}\),
\[
 P\!\left(\left|(\mathbb P_n-P)Z_{t,x,h}\right|>u\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{cnu^2}{h^{-2}+h^{-2}u}\right\}.
\]

Cover \(\mathcal B_P\subseteq[-L,L]^2\) by an ambient Euclidean \(\epsilon_n\)-net with \(\epsilon_n=n^{-4}\).  Its cardinality is at most \(C_Ln^8\), without any geometric regularity of \(\mathcal B_P\).  The map \(x\mapsto K_\triangle(\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2/h)\) is \(h^{-1}\)-Lipschitz, so normalized denominator and numerator oscillations are at most
\[
 C\epsilon_nh^{-3},
 \qquad C\epsilon_nh^{-3}\mathbb P_n|Y|,
\]
and the population oscillations obey the analogous bounds.  The strict-sign estimator differs from the arm-indicator expression only when \(x=X_i\).  Absolute continuity makes sample scores pairwise distinct almost surely, so this changes the normalized denominator by at most \((nh^2)^{-1}\) and the numerator by at most \((nh^2)^{-1}\max_i|Y_i|\), uniformly in \(x\).  Conditional sub-Gaussianity and the regression envelope give
\[
 \sup_P\mathbb E_P\max_{i\leq n}|Y_i|^2\leq C\log n,
 \qquad
 \sup_P\mathbb E_P(\mathbb P_n|Y|)^2\leq C.
\]
Consequently, a union bound over the net followed by the oscillation bounds shows that for every fixed \(A_0>0\), after enlarging \(C\), with probability at least \(1-Cn^{-A_0}\),
\[
 \sup_{t,x}|\mathbb P_nW_{t,x,h}-d_{t,P,x}(h)|
 +\sup_{t,x}|\mathbb P_n(W_{t,x,h}Y)-\mathbb E_P(W_{t,x,h}Y)|
 \leq C\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+\frac{\log n}{nh^2}\right\},
\]
for \(h\asymp a_n\), with \(x\in\mathcal B_P\).

For large \(n\), the right side is smaller than \(\kappa_0/2\), so every empirical denominator is positive and bounded below by \(\kappa_0/2\).  The population ratio is a kernel-weighted average of \(\mu_{t,P}(z)\) over \(R_x\leq h\).  Since \(q\geq1\),
\[
 \left|\frac{\mathbb E_P(W_{t,x,h}Y)}{d_{t,P,x}(h)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \leq Lh.
\]
The identity
\[
 \frac{\widehat r}{\widehat d}-\frac r d
 =\frac{\widehat r-r}{\widehat d}
 +\frac r d\frac{d-\widehat d}{\widehat d}
\]
then gives, on the good event,
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+\frac{\log n}{nh^2}\right\}.
\]
The causal target is not defined by this ratio: the canonical exact-distance lemma separately identifies its arm limits.

On the exceptional event each arm estimate is zero or a convex combination of observed outcomes, so the loss is at most \(2\max_i|Y_i|+2L\).  Cauchy--Schwarz and the maximal second-moment bound make its expected contribution negligible for large \(A_0\).  With \(h\asymp a_n\), all three displayed terms are bounded by \(Ca_n\).  The estimator is a measurable member of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\) by construction.  Hence
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}})\leq C_{\mathrm{rect}}a_n.
\]

For the converse, use the hypercube in the angular-density packing.  Poissonize at mean \(2n\), attach independent continuous marks, and partition the process into the disjoint bump cells and their complement.  Conditional on the hypercube bits, these restrictions are independent, the complement is bit-independent, and cell \(j\) depends only on bit \(j\).  Decoder \(j\), obtained by midpoint-thresholding \(\widehat\tau_n(x_j)\), observes its local cell only through the compressed statistic \(U_{n,P}(x_j)\), together with the other cells and the bit-independent complement.  Poisson tensorization multiplies the one-observation divergence by \(2n\); the packing slack gives \(2\alpha\log M_n\) with \(2\alpha<1\).  The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma therefore gives
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one bit is decoded incorrectly}\}\geq\frac12-o(1).
\]
If the boundary sup-norm loss were smaller than \(c_1a_n/2\), every midpoint decoder would be correct.  Hence the Poissonized expected loss is at least \(ca_n\).  Given a Poisson sample of mean \(2n\), marked subsampling produces exactly \(n\) independent observations when its size is at least \(n\), whose complement has probability at most \(e^{-cn}\).  Thus any estimator for exactly \(n\) observations would induce a Poissonized estimator with the same risk up to a negligible term.  Consequently
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}})\geq c_{\mathrm{rect}}a_n.
\]
Dividing by \(a_n\) and taking \(\liminf\) and \(\limsup\) proves the theorem.  The hard family remains in the enlarged project-defined class.  No inclusion in the class of Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu, Theorem 6, is used or asserted.
