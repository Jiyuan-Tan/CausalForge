Fix \(p\geq0\), \(\nu\geq2\), and \(L\geq L_0(p)\).  The established point-indexed converse provides \(c_0=c_0(p,\nu,L)>0\) such that
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)\over a_n}\geq c_0.
\]
It uses the same law class, outer-expectation risk, and full law-independent known-geometry point-indexed class with Borel fixed sections.

The explicit winsorized, Gram-stabilized rule is a member of \(\mathcal T_n^{12,\pm}\).  Evaluating the infimum in the risk definition at this one rule and applying \(\mathrm{prop:cty\text{-}a1\text{-}a2\text{-}winsorized\text{-}expected\text{-}outer\text{-}upper}\) gives a finite \(C_0=C_0(p,\nu,L)\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)
 \leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)}
 \mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
 |\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,a_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
 \leq C_0a_n.
\]
Division by \(a_n>0\) and passage to the upper limit give
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}{R_n^{12,\pm}(p,\nu,L)\over a_n}\leq C_0.
\]
Combining the two displays, and taking for example \(c=c_0/2\) and \(C=C_0\), proves the asserted chain.  Finally \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), \(q=p+1\geq1\), the upper witness is valid already for every \(L\geq4\), and the matched conclusion uses \(L\geq L_0(p)\) because that is the regime of the converse.
