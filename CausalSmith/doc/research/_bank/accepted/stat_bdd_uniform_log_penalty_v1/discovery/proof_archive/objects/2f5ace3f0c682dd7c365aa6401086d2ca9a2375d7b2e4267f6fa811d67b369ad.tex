Take a rule in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\), generated by one law-independent Borel map
\[
 T_n:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R.
\]
Set \(T_{n,x}=T_n\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^2\).  Each fixed section is Borel measurable, the entire family is selected independently of \(P\), and
\[
 T_{n,x}(V_{n,P}(x))=T_n(V_{n,P}(x))
\]
at every boundary point.  Thus \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\subseteq\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\).  This inclusion uses only sectionwise measurability and imposes no joint condition in \(x\).

For strictness, consider the law-independent point-indexed family
\[
 T_{n,x}(v)=x_1,
 \qquad x=(x_1,x_2)\in\mathbb R^2.
\]
For every fixed \(x\), this is a constant, hence Borel measurable, function of \(v\).  It therefore belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\).

Let \(P_0\) be the law under which \(X\) is uniform on \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\) and \(Y\sim N(0,1)\) is independent of \(X\).  Its density is one on its compact support, its boundary is rectifiable, its regression is zero, and its conditional variance is one.  Hence \(P_0\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) for every \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\).  At the boundary points
\[
 x_-=(-1/2,0),
 \qquad
 x_+=(1/2,0),
\]
reflection of the square in the vertical axis, together with independence of \(Y\), gives
\[
 \mathcal L_{P_0}\{V_{n,P_0}(x_-)\}
 =\mathcal L_{P_0}\{V_{n,P_0}(x_+)\}.
\]
If the displayed point-indexed family were generated by one common Borel map \(T_n\), applying that map to these identically distributed inputs would produce outputs with the same law.  The required outputs, however, are the distinct constants
\[
 T_{n,x_-}(V_{n,P_0}(x_-))=-\frac12,
 \qquad
 T_{n,x_+}(V_{n,P_0}(x_+))=\frac12.
\]
This contradiction proves that the inclusion is strict.
