All constants below may depend on the frozen class parameters, \(q\), and \(\delta\), but not on \(n\) or on the member law \(P\).  We prove nonemptiness, the full-location rate, and the two distance regimes in turn.

\emph{Nonemptiness and boundary conditions.}
Let \(X\) be uniform on \([-1,1]^2\), use the displayed half-plane assignment regions and chart \(\gamma(u)=(2u-1,0)\), take \(\mu_{0,P}=\mu_{1,P}=0\), and set \(Y(t)=\varepsilon_t\), where \(\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1\) are independent standard Gaussian variables independent of \(X\).  Then \(f_P=1/4\), \(\sigma^2_{t,P}=1\), and the conditional sub-Gaussian inequality holds with proxy one.  The support is compact, the regressions belong to every displayed H\"older ball, deterministic assignment and consistency hold by construction, and the chart is an injective smooth embedding.  For \(0<r\leq r_0\leq1/4\), the intersection of either arm with a boundary ball contains at least a quarter disk, including at the two endpoints.  Its probability is at least \(\pi r^2/16\), which is no smaller than \(c_-r^2\) because \(c_-\leq1/(16L)\).  Thus this law belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\), so the class is nonempty.  The same calculation records the support, positivity, overlap, and endpoint conditions used below.

\emph{Full-location upper bound.}
Write \(r_q(u)\) for the vector of all bivariate monomials of total degree at most \(q-1\), and put \(K_2(u)=(1-\lVert u\rVert_2)_+\).  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), and \(h>0\), define
\[
 \widehat G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)
 =\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_2((X_i-x)/h)
 r_q((X_i-x)/h)r_q((X_i-x)/h)^\top
\]
and define \(\widehat r^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)\) by replacing the last factor \(r_q((X_i-x)/h)^\top\) with \(r_q((X_i-x)/h)Y_i\).  Choose a fixed cutoff below the population eigenvalue bound derived next; use the intercept of \(\{\widehat G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)\}^{-1}\widehat r^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)\) on the active branch and zero on the other branch, and subtract the two arm intercepts.  This is a jointly Borel function of \(O_{1:n}\) and the known assignment geometry, hence is one rule in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\).

For every trimmed \(x\) and all sufficiently small \(h\), the rescaled score support in arm \(t\) contains the fixed open half-disk \(H_t\cap B_2(0,1/2)\).  Consequently, for every coefficient vector \(v\), the density lower bound gives
\[
 v^\top\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)v
 \geq L^{-1}\int_{H_t\cap B_2(0,1/2)}
 K_2(u)\{v^\top r_q(u)\}^2\,du.
\]
A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an open set.  Continuity on the unit sphere in the fixed-dimensional coefficient space therefore yields a constant \(\lambda_*>0\), uniform in \(P,t,x\), such that the population minimum eigenvalue is at least \(\lambda_*\).  This supplies every inverse and rank condition.

At a fixed point, bounded kernel-polynomial weights, the density upper envelope, independent sampling, and the displayed conditional sub-Gaussian inequality imply, by the scalar Bernstein inequality applied to the finitely many coordinates,
\[
 \left\|\widehat G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)
 -\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\left\|\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_2((X_i-x)/h)r_q((X_i-x)/h)
 \{Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\}\right\|_2
 \leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}
\]
outside an event of probability at most \(n^{-B}\), where \(B>0\) can be fixed arbitrarily large by increasing \(C\).  To make the assertion uniform, cover the trimmed segment by a mesh of width \(\varepsilon_n=n^{-3}\).  Its cardinality is polynomial in \(n\), so the same bound holds on the mesh after a union bound.  The maps \(u\mapsto K_2(u)r_q(u)\) and \(u\mapsto K_2(u)r_q(u)r_q(u)^\top\), extended by zero, are Lipschitz.  Hence moving \(x\) by at most \(\varepsilon_n\) changes the Gram matrix by at most \(C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\) and changes the outcome vector by at most
\[
 C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|.
\]
The sub-Gaussian assumption and \(\lVert\mu_{t,P}\rVert_\infty\leq L\) make these oscillations negligible at the bandwidth below.  Thus the concentration and the active cutoff hold uniformly over \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\).

Taylor's formula through total degree \(q-1\), with the H\"older remainder written explicitly as
\[
 \left|\mu_{t,P}(x+hu)-\sum_{|\alpha|<q}
 \frac{\partial^\alpha\mu_{t,P}(x)}{\alpha!}(hu)^\alpha\right|
 \leq C h^q\lVert u\rVert_2^q,
\]
gives intercept bias at most \(Ch^q\) after multiplication by the uniformly bounded empirical inverse.  It follows on the uniform good event that
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widetilde\tau_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
On the complement, an active intercept is bounded by \(Ch^{-2}n^{-1}\sum_i|Y_i|\), while a failed-cutoff intercept is zero.  The uniform second-moment bound implied by conditional sub-Gaussianity and the H\"older envelope, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, makes the complement contribution negligible when \(B\) is large.  Therefore
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \leq C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
With \(h=h_{n,q}:=(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\), both terms equal a constant multiple of \(s_{n,q}\), and \(nh_{n,q}^2/\log n\to\infty\), as required by the concentration step.

\emph{Full-location converse.}
Let \(h=h_{n,q}\), choose \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) points \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) on the trimmed segment with pairwise disjoint radius-\(h\) disks, and fix \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\) with \(0\leq\phi\leq1\) and \(\phi(0)=1\).  Retain the uniform score density, put \(\mu_{0,j}=0\), and let
\[
 \mu_{1,j}(z)=\Delta\phi((z-x_j)/h),
 \qquad \Delta=\eta h^q,
 \qquad Y(t)=\mu_{t,j}(X)+\varepsilon_t,
\]
with independent standard Gaussian errors.  For every derivative order \(k\leq q\),
\[
 \lVert D^k\mu_{1,j}\rVert_\infty
 \leq C_\phi\eta h^{q-k}\leq C_\phi\eta.
\]
Choosing the fixed \(\eta>0\) sufficiently small puts every alternative in the common H\"older envelope.  The density is continuous and equal to \(1/4\), the variance is one, the errors satisfy the tail condition, the fixed half-plane design satisfies the thickness and all endpoint conditions checked above, and the chart is unchanged.  Hence every alternative is in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\).

Let \(P_0\) denote the zero-regression law and \(P_j\) the alternative with its bump at \(x_j\).  Conditional Gaussian relative entropy and the \(O(h^2)\) area of a bump give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =\frac n2\mathbb E_{P_j}
 [\mu_{1,j}(X)^2\mathbf 1\{T=1\}]
 \leq Cn\Delta^2h^2=C\eta^2nh^{2q+2}.
\]
At the selected bandwidth this is \(C\eta^2\log n\), whereas
\(\log M=(1/(2q+2)+o(1))\log n\).  Choose \(\eta\) so that the last relative entropy is at most \(\alpha\log M\) for some \(\alpha<1/4\).  If \(J\) is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,M\}\), the log-sum inequality gives
\[
 I(J;O_{1:n})\leq\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
For any decoder, if \(E\) is its error indicator, then
\[
 H(J\mid O_{1:n})\leq H(E)+P(E)\log M
 \leq\log2+P(E)\log M.
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log M\), the preceding mutual-information bound implies \(P(E)\geq1-\alpha-o(1)\).  Supremum error below \(\Delta/3\) identifies \(J\) by maximizing the estimated curve over the centers, because the true value is \(\Delta\) at \(x_J\) and zero at every other center.  Thus
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq c\Delta=cs_{n,q}.
\]
Together with the upper bound, this proves the first displayed equivalence.

\emph{Distance experiment when \(L>4\).}
For the upper bound, use the explicit rule \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}\) from \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}estimator}\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:fixed\text{-}local\text{-}constant\text{-}stability}\), its population arm denominators, after the \(h^{-2}\) normalization, are uniformly bounded below.  By \(\mathrm{lem:density\text{-}implies\text{-}upper\text{-}mass}\), their second moments have the matching uniform upper order.  Bernstein concentration on a polynomial mesh of the boundary, followed by the Lipschitz extension for \(K_\triangle\), therefore gives positive empirical denominators and
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}
\]
outside an event of probability \(n^{-B}\).  Here the bias bound is the direct H\"older consequence
\(|\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\lVert z-x\rVert_2\leq Lh\) on the kernel support; the stochastic bound uses independent sampling and conditional sub-Gaussianity.  On the exceptional event, each nonzero local-constant arm estimate is a convex combination of the observed outcomes, so its magnitude is bounded by \(\max_i|Y_i|\); its expected contribution is negligible by the same sub-Gaussian maximal bound and Cauchy--Schwarz.  Taking \(h\asymp a_n\) proves
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \leq Ca_n.
\]

For the converse, invoke the canonical-square family in \(\mathrm{lem:angular\text{-}density\text{-}packing}\), which is contained in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\) precisely in the stated regime \(L>4\).  Its \(M=M_n\) disjoint cells have adjacent target separation at least \(c_1a_n\), and the fixed-\(n\) compressed relative entropy at coordinate \(j\) is at most \(\alpha\log M\), with \(\alpha<1/8\).  Poissonize at mean \(2n\).  Independent increments on the disjoint bump cells give the conditional product structure in \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\); the outside process and the other cells may be revealed as bit-independent or other-coordinate ancillary information.  Tensorization gives local Poisson relative entropy at most \(2\alpha\log M\), and \(2\alpha<1\).  The direct-product lemma consequently yields probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\) that at least one coordinate is decoded incorrectly.  Midpoint decoding at all centers would recover every bit whenever the boundary sup-norm error were below \(c_1a_n/2\).  Hence the Poissonized expected sup-norm loss is at least \(ca_n\).

To return to exactly \(n\) observations, attach independent continuous marks to the Poisson points and, on the event that their number is at least \(n\), run any proposed fixed-\(n\) rule on the first \(n\) marked observations.  Those observations have law \(P^{\otimes n}\), and the constructed decoder is still of the form allowed by the direct-product lemma after the marks and unused observations are revealed as ancillary information.  The event of fewer than \(n\) points under a Poisson variable of mean \(2n\) has probability at most \(e^{-c n}\), by the exponential Markov inequality.  Since \(|\tau_P|\leq2L\), this failure event is negligible relative to \(a_n\).  Thus every fixed-\(n\) distance rule has risk at least \(ca_n\), proving the \(L>4\) equivalence.

\emph{The no-density-slack case \(L=4\).}
For every member law,
\[
 1=\int_{[-1,1]^2}f_P(z)\,dz
 \geq\int_{[-1,1]^2}\frac14\,dz=1.
\]
Therefore \(f_P=1/4\) Lebesgue-almost everywhere, and density continuity upgrades this to pointwise equality.  If \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), then for a fixed \(r_*=r_*(\delta)>0\) and every \(0<s\leq r_*\), each arm contributes a complete half-circle contained in the square.  Thus the support and division conditions are explicit:
\[
 Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\frac\pi4s>0.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\text{-}exact\text{-}distance\text{-}regression}\), the ratio \(N_{t,P,x}(s)/Q_{t,P,x}(s)\) is the arm conditional mean at signed radius and is causally centered at \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\).  Integrating the order-\((q-1)\) Taylor polynomial of \(\mu_{t,P}\) over the half-circle and integrating its H\"older remainder give coefficients \(b_{t,k,P}(x)\), uniformly bounded over laws, arms, and trimmed centers, such that
\[
 \left|\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 -\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k\right|
 \leq Cs^q,
 \qquad 0<s\leq r_*.
\]

Instantiate \(\mathrm{def:stabilized\text{-}distance\text{-}local\text{-}polynomial}\) with \(m=q\), kernel \(K_\triangle\), and cutoff constant \(c=\pi/4\).  Coarea and the preceding exact radial density give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(q)}_{t,x}(h)
 =\frac\pi4G_q,
 \qquad
 G_q=\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)v_q(u)v_q(u)^\top\,du.
\]
For nonzero \(a\in\mathbb R^q\), the polynomial \(a^\top v_q(u)\) cannot vanish throughout \((0,1)\), on which \(uK_\triangle(u)>0\).  Hence \(G_q\) is positive definite and the population cutoff has a uniform positive margin.

The radial conditional error is uniformly sub-Gaussian.  Indeed, conditional on the signed radius,
\[
 Y-\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
 =\{Y-\mu_{t,P}(X)\}
 +\left\{\mu_{t,P}(X)-
 \mathbb E_P[\mu_{t,P}(X)\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]\right\}.
\]
The first summand obeys the frozen conditional sub-Gaussian inequality.  The second is conditionally centered and lies in an interval of length at most \(4L\); Hoeffding's exponential inequality followed by conditioning yields a sub-Gaussian proxy depending only on \(L\).  Bernstein concentration for the finite Gram coordinates and these weighted radial errors, first on an \(n^{-3}\)-mesh and then everywhere by the Lipschitz property of \(K_\triangle v_q\) and \(K_\triangle v_qv_q^\top\), gives a uniform good event of probability at least \(1-n^{-B}\).  Polynomial reproduction and the displayed order-\(q\) remainder then give on this event
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(q),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Every inverse is used only on the active-cutoff branch.  On the complementary branch, \(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\text{-}distance\text{-}local\text{-}polynomial\text{-}admissible}\) sets the intercept to zero; on the active branch the same lemma bounds it by \(Ch^{-2}n^{-1}\sum_i|Y_i|\).  Uniform second moments and Cauchy--Schwarz make the complement negligible for large \(B\).  The admissibility conclusion also verifies that this is one data-driven member of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).  Taking \(h=h_{n,q}\) proves
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \leq Cs_{n,q}.
\]

Conversely, the full sample and known geometry determine \(D_{P,x,i}\) at every \(x\).  Every coordinatewise distance rule can therefore be implemented as a full-location rule, so the decision-class inclusion gives
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq cs_{n,q}.
\]
This proves the \(L=4\) equivalence.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{q}{2q+2}>\frac14\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q>1.
\]
Thus the exponents separate exactly when \(q>1\) and \(L>4\), agree at \(q=1\), and also agree throughout the knife-edge no-density-slack case \(L=4\), as claimed.
