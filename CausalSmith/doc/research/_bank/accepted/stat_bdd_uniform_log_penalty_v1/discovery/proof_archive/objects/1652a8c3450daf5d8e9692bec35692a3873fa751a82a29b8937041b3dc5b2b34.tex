\textbf{Proof.}
Throughout, \(C,c\in(0,\infty)\) denote constants depending only on \(q,L,c_{-},r_0\); their values may change from line to line.  The rectifiable class used here is the enlarged class obtained by deleting the inert boundary-length-envelope member condition.  No step below uses a bound on \(\mathcal H^1(\mathcal B_P)\).

\emph{Upper bound.}
Fix \(h=h_n\asymp a_n\), with the implicit constants fixed.  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and \(1\leq i\leq n\), set
\[
 W_{i,t,x}=\mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_\triangle(D_{P,x,i}/h),
 \qquad
 A_{t,x}=\sum_{i=1}^n W_{i,t,x}.
\]
Because \(K_\triangle\) is symmetric, \(K_\triangle(D_{P,x,i}/h)\) is a function of \(|D_{P,x,i}|\), and the sign of \(D_{P,x,i}\) recovers \(T_i\) whenever \(D_{P,x,i}\ne0\).  The event \(D_{P,x,i}=0\) is the event \(X_i=x\), which has probability zero under the density envelope; define the arm label arbitrarily at zero.  Thus, at each \(x\), the displayed estimator is a measurable function of \(U_{n,P}(x)\), so
\[
 \widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}.
\]

The stability lemma and the identity
\[
 \mathbb E_P A_{t,x}
 =nh^2\Psi^{(0)}_{t,P,x}(h)
\]
give, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P A_{t,x}\geq \kappa_0nh^2.
\]
Since \(0\leq K_\triangle\leq1\) and it vanishes outside \([-1,1]\), the upper-mass lemma gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P A_{t,x}
 \leq nP\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,h)\}
 \leq \pi Lnh^2.
\]
These bounds are uniform over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\), \(t\), and \(x\), and state explicitly the positivity and denominator scale used below.

Let \(\eta_n=n^{-3}\), and choose a maximal \(\eta_n\)-separated subset \(\mathcal N_{n,P}\) of \(\mathcal B_P\).  Since \(\mathcal B_P\subseteq[-L,L]^2\), ambient planar packing gives
\[
 |\mathcal N_{n,P}|\leq C_L\eta_n^{-2}\leq C_Ln^6.
\]
This is the only covering argument; in particular, it does not use boundary length.  For a fixed net point, \(0\leq W_{i,t,x}\leq1\) and \(\sum_i\operatorname{Var}_P(W_{i,t,x})\leq\mathbb E_PA_{t,x}\).  The elementary bound
\[
 e^u-1-u\leq \frac{u^2}{2(1-|u|/3)},\qquad |u|<3,
\]
applied to the centered bounded summands and optimized in the exponential Markov inequality yields
\[
 P\{|A_{t,x}-\mathbb E_PA_{t,x}|>v\}
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{v^2}{2(\mathbb E_PA_{t,x}+v/3)}\right\}.
\]
Taking \(v\) to be a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(nh^2\), using the preceding expectation bounds, and taking a union bound over two arms and the net produces an event \(E_n\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}P(E_n^c)\leq n^{-4}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), because \(nh^2\asymp\sqrt{n\log n}\).  The kernel is one-Lipschitz and
\[
 \bigl||X_i-x|-|X_i-x'|\bigr|\leq\lVert x-x'\rVert_2,
\]
so \(|A_{t,x}-A_{t,x'}|\leq n\eta_n/h\) whenever \(\lVert x-x'\rVert_2\leq\eta_n\).  Since \(\eta_n/h^3=o(1)\), the net inequalities extend to every boundary point, and on \(E_n\),
\[
 \inf_{t,x}A_{t,x}\geq \frac{\kappa_0}{4}nh^2,
 \qquad
 \sup_{t,x}A_{t,x}\leq Cnh^2.
\]

On arm \(t\), define \(\varepsilon_{i,t}=Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\).  Whenever \(A_{t,x}>0\), exact algebra gives
\[
 \widehat\mu_{t,h}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 =\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}}{A_{t,x}}
 +\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\{\mu_{t,P}(X_i)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\}}{A_{t,x}}.
\]
Every nonzero weight has \(\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2\leq h\).  Since \(q\geq1\), the H\"older condition therefore bounds the second fraction in absolute value by \(Lh\).

For the first fraction, conditional independence and the conditional sub-Gaussian assumption imply, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 P\!\left(\left.\left|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|>u\,\right|X_{1:n}\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2L^2\sum_iW_{i,t,x}^2}\right\}
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2L^2A_{t,x}}\right\}.
\]
On \(E_n\), a union bound over the net and integration of this tail yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \max_{t\in\{0,1\}}\max_{x\in\mathcal N_{n,P}}
 \left|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|\mathbf1_{E_n}
 \right]
 \leq C\sqrt{nh^2\log n}.
\]
For \(x\) and a nearest net point \(x'\), the same Lipschitz inequality gives
\[
 \left|\sum_i(W_{i,t,x}-W_{i,t,x'})\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|
 \leq\frac{\eta_n}{h}\sum_i|\varepsilon_{i,t}|.
\]
Conditional sub-Gaussianity gives \(\sup_{P,t,i}\mathbb E_P|\varepsilon_{i,t}|\leq C\).  Dividing the last two displays by the lower denominator bound on \(E_n\) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{t,x}
 \frac{|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}|}{A_{t,x}}
 \mathbf1_{E_n}
 \right]
 \leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+C\frac{\eta_n}{h^3}.
\]
Here \(\eta_n/h^3=o(a_n)\).

It remains to control \(E_n^c\), including every zero-denominator case.  A nonempty-denominator local-constant fit is a convex combination of the observed outcomes, whereas the definition sets it to zero when its denominator vanishes.  The H\"older-ball envelope bounds \(\sup_{P,t,x}|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\) by a constant, and the conditional sub-Gaussian bound gives, by a union bound and tail integration,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}\mathbb E_P\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|Y_i|^2\leq C\log n.
\]
Consequently Cauchy--Schwarz and \(P(E_n^c)\leq n^{-4}\) give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|\mathbf1_{E_n^c}
 \right]
 \leq C(\log n)^{1/2}n^{-2}=o(a_n).
\]
Combining the bias, stochastic, and exceptional-event bounds for the two arms yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}+o(a_n).
\]
The balance \(h^4\asymp\log n/n\) gives \(h\asymp a_n\) and proves the asserted finite upper limsup with the estimator named in the theorem.

\emph{Lower bound.}
Apply the established angular-density packing with the fixed trim \(\delta=1/4\).  It supplies \(M=M_n\to\infty\) disjoint centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\), a hypercube \(\{P_\omega:\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\}\subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\), adjacent target separation
\[
 |\tau_{P_\omega}(x_j)-\tau_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)|\geq\Delta_n,
 \qquad \Delta_n=c_1a_n,
\]
and adjacent fixed-sample compressed relative entropy at most \(\alpha\log M\), where \(2\alpha<1\).  Every member of this family has a unit-length straight interface, so deleting the boundary-length condition from the ambient class preserves its membership.

Poissonize at mean \(2n\).  Conditional on the independent uniform hypercube bits, the observations in the disjoint bump disks are independent marked Poisson processes \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\), and the outside process \(Z_0\) is bit-independent.  For center \(x_j\), let \(S_j\) be the signed-distance and outcome compression of the observations in the \(j\)-th disk.  Revealing \((Z_{-j},Z_0)\) can only help decoder \(j\), so its information has the form required by the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma.

To verify that lemma's entropy premise, write \(K_j\) for the one-observation law after compression at \(x_j\).  Iid tensorization gives
\[
 n\operatorname{KL}(K_{j,0},K_{j,1})
 =\operatorname{KL}(\mathbb Q_{n,P_\omega,x_j},
                    \mathbb Q_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}},x_j})
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
Restriction to the \(j\)-th disk and measurable compression cannot increase relative entropy.  Moreover, direct calculation from the marked-Poisson likelihood gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\operatorname{PPP}(2nK),\operatorname{PPP}(2nK'))
 =2n\operatorname{KL}(K,K')
\]
when \(K,K'\) are probability laws; the same likelihood calculation for restrictions gives the corresponding inequality for the local subprobability intensities.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\leq2\alpha\log M=:\kappa\log M,
 \qquad \kappa<1.
\]
The direct-product lemma now gives, under the uniform product prior,
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat\omega_j\ne\omega_j\text{ for at least one }j\}
 \geq\frac12-o(1).
\]

At each center, threshold \(\widehat\tau_n(x_j)\) at the midpoint of its two adjacent target values.  If the sup-norm estimation error is smaller than \(\Delta_n/2\), every coordinate is decoded correctly.  Therefore every coordinatewise distance-only rule in the Poissonized experiment satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \geq\frac{\Delta_n}{2}\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)
 \geq ca_n.
\]
The Bayes lower bound implies the same lower bound for the maximum risk over the finite family.

Finally, let \(N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(2n)\).  From any fixed-\(n\) coordinatewise rule, construct a Poissonized rule by applying it to the first \(n\) observations when \(N\geq n\), and use the zero curve otherwise.  This remains coordinatewise measurable.  The selected observations have law \(P^{\otimes n}\), while a Chernoff calculation gives \(P(N<n)\leq e^{-cn}\).  The H\"older envelope bounds \(\sup_{P,x}|\tau_P(x)|\), so the loss contributed on \(\{N<n\}\) is \(O(e^{-cn})=o(a_n)\).  The Poisson lower bound therefore implies
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{R_n^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}})}{a_n}>0.
\]
Together with the upper bound this proves the displayed frontier.  The causal target is \(\tau_P\); the canonical exact-distance regression identifies it through its zero-radius limit, but it is not used to redefine the causal effect.  The construction and the proof concern only the project-defined two-sided-thickness class and make no inclusion or same-class claim about Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu, Theorem 6. \(\square\)
