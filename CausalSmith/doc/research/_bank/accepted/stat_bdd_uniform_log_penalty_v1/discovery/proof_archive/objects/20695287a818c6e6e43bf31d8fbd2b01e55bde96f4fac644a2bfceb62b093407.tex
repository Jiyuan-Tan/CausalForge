Put
\[
 g_{t,P,x}(s)=\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)},
 \qquad 0<s\leq r_*.
\]
The theorem's lower radial bound makes this division valid at every displayed radius.  The radial-coarea identification lemma, which needs only score absolute continuity, shows that \(g_{t,P,x}\) is an observed exact-distance regression almost everywhere and that its limit identifies the separately defined causal arm value \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\).  Subtracting the arms identifies \(\tau_P(x)\).

For the upper bound, fix \(0<h\leq r_*\), put \(v(u)=v_m(u)\), and define
\[
 G_{t,P,x}(h)=\frac1{h^2}\int_0^hK_\triangle(s/h)v(s/h)v(s/h)^\top Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds,
\]
\[
 r_{t,P,x}(h)=\frac1{h^2}\int_0^hK_\triangle(s/h)v(s/h)g_{t,P,x}(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
Coarea, deterministic assignment, and consistency identify these as the expectations of \(\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\) and \(\widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\).  With
\[
 G_m=\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)v(u)v(u)^\top\,du,
\]
the radial bounds imply
\[
 \underline cG_m\preceq G_{t,P,x}(h)\preceq\overline cG_m.
\]
For nonzero \(a\in\mathbb R^m\), the polynomial \(a^\top v(u)\) cannot vanish on \((0,1)\), and hence
\[
 a^\top G_ma=\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)\{a^\top v(u)\}^2\,du>0.
\]
Thus \(G_m\) is positive definite and
\[
 \lVert G_{t,P,x}(h)^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\{\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)\}^{-1}.
\]

Let \(B\) bound the coefficient functions and set
\[
 \beta_{t,P,x}(h)=
 \bigl(\mu_{t,P}(x),b_{t,1,P}(x)h,\ldots,
 b_{t,m-1,P}(x)h^{m-1}\bigr)^\top.
\]
The H\"older ball bounds \(|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\), so \(\|\beta_{t,P,x}(h)\|\leq C\) for \(h\leq1\).  Polynomial reproduction and the remainder give
\[
 r_{t,P,x}(h)=G_{t,P,x}(h)\beta_{t,P,x}(h)+\zeta_{t,P,x}(h),
\]
where
\[
 \|\zeta_{t,P,x}(h)\|
 \leq\frac{A}{h^2}\int_0^hK_\triangle(s/h)\|v(s/h)\|s^mQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds
 \leq Ch^m.
\]
Consequently the population intercept differs from \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\) by at most \(Ch^m\).

The upper radial bound gives
\[
 P\{T=t,\lVert X-x\rVert_2\leq h\}
 =\int_0^hQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds\leq\frac{\overline c}{2}h^2.
\]
Every normalized Gram coordinate is bounded by \(h^{-2}\) and has second moment at most \(Ch^{-2}\).  Conditional sub-Gaussianity, consistency, and bounded regression similarly imply, for every integer \(k\geq2\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left|h^{-2}\mathbf1\{T=t,R_x\leq h\}
 K_\triangle(R_x/h)v_\ell(R_x/h)Y\right|^k
 \leq k!C^kh^{2-2k}.
\]
Hence exponential Markov inequality gives, for each centered coordinate,
\[
 P\{|(\mathbb P_n-P)Z_{t,x,h}|>u\}
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{cnu^2}{h^{-2}+h^{-2}u}\right\}.
\]

Cover \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\subseteq[-L,L]^2\) by an \(n^{-4}\)-net of cardinality at most \(C_Ln^8\).  A \(C^1\) embedded curve has planar Lebesgue measure zero, so score absolute continuity implies that almost surely no sample score lies on \(\mathcal B_P\); the strict arm indicator then equals \(\mathbf1\{T=t\}\) simultaneously along the boundary.  On \([0,1]\), the kernel-polynomial products are Lipschitz, so empirical oscillations between neighboring centers are at most
\[
 C_mn^{-4}h^{-3},
 \qquad C_mn^{-4}h^{-3}\mathbb P_n|Y|,
\]
with analogous population bounds.  A union bound over arms, matrix and vector coordinates, and the net yields, for arbitrary fixed \(A_0>0\), an event \(\mathcal E_n\) with
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal C}P(\mathcal E_n)\geq1-Cn^{-A_0}
\]
on which
\[
 \sup_{t,x}\|\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-G_{t,P,x}(h)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\sup_{t,x}\|\widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-r_{t,P,x}(h)\|
 \leq C\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+\frac{\log n}{nh^2}\right\}.
\]
No score-density envelope is used here; the explicit radial upper bound supplies the window-mass control.

For large \(n\), the last display is at most \(\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)/2\).  Weyl's inequality places every empirical matrix on the active-cutoff branch and gives
\[
 \|\{\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\frac{2}{\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\beta_{t,P,x}(h)
 &=\{\widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-r_{t,P,x}(h)\}+\zeta_{t,P,x}(h)\\
 &\quad+\{G_{t,P,x}(h)-\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\}\beta_{t,P,x}(h).
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying by the bounded inverse and \(e_1^\top\), then subtracting arms, proves on \(\mathcal E_n\) that
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+
 \frac{\log n}{nh^2}\right\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n^c\), the admissibility lemma bounds each arm intercept by \(Ch^{-2}\mathbb P_n|Y|\), while the target is uniformly bounded.  Cauchy--Schwarz and sufficiently large \(A_0\) make the expected exceptional contribution negligible.  Choose
\[
 h_n\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(2m+2)}.
\]
Then \(h_n^m=\sqrt{\log n/(nh_n^2)}\) and \(\log n/(nh_n^2)=h_n^{2m}\leq h_n^m\).  Admissibility places this single data-driven estimator in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\), proving
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h_n}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq Ch_n^m
\]
and the asserted minimax upper bound.

For the conditional converse, work on \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\) with uniform density and a straight interface.  Choose \(M\asymp h_n^{-1}\) trimmed centers separated by \(3h_n\), let \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\) satisfy \(\phi(0)=1\), and put \(\Delta=\eta h_n^m\).  Keep the control regression fixed and in alternative \(j\) add \(\Delta\phi((z-x_j)/h_n)\) to the treated baseline.  If \(m\geq q\), then for every \(k\leq q\),
\[
 \|D^k\{\Delta\phi((\cdot-x_j)/h_n)\}\|_\infty
 \leq C\eta h_n^{m-k}\leq C\eta.
\]
Thus small \(\eta\) puts every alternative in the common \(q\)-H\"older ball.  Their derivatives through order \(m\) are uniformly bounded; Taylor's formula through degree \(m-1\), integrated over straight half-circles, verifies the stated order-\(m\) normalized-coarea remainder uniformly.  Also \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\pi s\) at sufficiently small trimmed radii.  Hence these are valid contained bump laws.  When \(m<q\), the derivative of order \(q\) has size \(h_n^{m-q}\), explaining why this construction gives no converse in that regime.

For each alternative against the null, the Bernoulli relative-entropy inequality and data processing through the common Gaussian channel give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn\Delta^2h_n^2
 =C\eta^2nh_n^{2m+2}=C\eta^2\log n.
\]
Since \(\log M=(1/(2m+2)+o(1))\log n\), choose \(\eta\) so this is at most \(\alpha\log M\) for fixed \(\alpha<1/4\).  If \(J\) is uniform on the alternatives, the log-sum inequality gives \(I(J;O_{1:n})\leq\alpha\log M\).  For any decoder with error event \(E\),
\[
 H(J\mid O_{1:n})\leq\log2+P(E)\log M.
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log M\), it follows that \(P(E)\geq1-\alpha-o(1)\).  Uniform error below \(\Delta/3\) identifies the unique bumped center, so every full-sample estimator, and therefore every coordinatewise-distance estimator, has risk at least \(c\Delta=ch_n^m\) on this family.  If \(\mathcal C\) contains it, the rate is exact.

The angular-imbalance assertion is separate.  If a law class contains the angular-density packing, Poissonization into disjoint cells gives the conditional product experiment of the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma.  The local compressed divergences are at most \(2\alpha\log M_n\) with \(2\alpha<1\), and midpoint decoding succeeds whenever the uniform loss is below \(c_1a_n/2\).  The lemma and marked de-Poissonization give the lower bound \(ca_n\).  For \(m=1\), \(h_n^m=a_n\); for \(m\geq2\),
\[
 \frac{m}{2m+2}>\frac14.
\]
The contained-bump converse applies in the stated regime \(m\geq q\), and no converse is inferred when \(m<q\).

Finally, on \([-1,1]^2\) with interface \(z_2=0\), choose \(0<\alpha<1\), a continuous radial cutoff \(\chi\) equal to one near zero and zero for \(r\geq r_c<1\), and define, with the value at zero set by continuity,
\[
 f(z)=\frac14\left\{1+\chi(\lVert z\rVert_2)
 \lVert z\rVert_2^\alpha\frac{z_1}{\lVert z\rVert_2}\right\}.
\]
The bracket is bounded above and away from zero.  Its perturbation is odd in \(z_1\), so it integrates to zero on the square and \(f\) is a bounded positive continuous density.  At the origin and every sufficiently small treated-half-circle radius \(s\),
\[
 Q(s)=\frac{s}{4}\int_0^\pi(1+s^\alpha\cos\theta)\,d\theta
 =\frac{\pi s}{4}.
\]
For \(\mu\equiv c\), \(N(s)/Q(s)=c\).  For \(\mu(z)=z_1\),
\[
 \frac{N(s)}{Q(s)}
 =\frac{(s^2/4)\int_0^\pi\cos\theta(1+s^\alpha\cos\theta)\,d\theta}
 {\pi s/4}
 =\frac{s^{1+\alpha}}2.
\]
If an order-two remainder existed, some \(b\) would satisfy \(s^{1+\alpha}/2-bs=O(s^2)\).  Division by \(s\) and passage to zero force \(b=0\), after which division by \(s^2\) gives \(s^{\alpha-1}/2\to\infty\), a contradiction.  Thus the normalized-coarea remainder is a joint density--regression property; no stronger weakest-threshold impossibility has been asserted.
