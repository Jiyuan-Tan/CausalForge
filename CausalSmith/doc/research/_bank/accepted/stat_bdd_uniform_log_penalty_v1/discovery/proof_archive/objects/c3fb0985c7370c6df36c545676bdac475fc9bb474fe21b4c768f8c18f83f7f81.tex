For each \(j\), dominate \(Q_{j,0}\) and \(Q_{j,1}\) by \(Q_{j,0}+Q_{j,1}\), and write the resulting densities as \(q_{j,0}\) and \(q_{j,1}\).  The common subprobability measure has density \(q_{j,0}\wedge q_{j,1}\) and mass
\[
 \rho_j=\int(q_{j,0}\wedge q_{j,1})
 =1-\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Construct the following equivalent version of the experiment.  Independently across coordinates, draw an ambiguity indicator \(A_j\), independent of \(\Omega_j\), with \(\Pr(A_j=1)=\rho_j\).  On \(\{A_j=1\}\), draw \(S_j\) from the normalized common submeasure, independently of \(\Omega_j\).  On \(\{A_j=0\}\), draw it from the normalized residual measure belonging to \(\Omega_j\).  A residual of mass zero may be assigned any probability kernel, because its associated event has probability zero.  This mixture has conditional law \(Q_{j,b}\) when \(\Omega_j=b\).

Because all spaces are standard Borel, a regular conditional distribution of \(Z_j\) given \((S_j,\Omega_j)\) exists.  Drawing from these conditional distributions reconstructs the prescribed conditional law of every \(Z_j\); drawing \(Z_0\) independently from its bit-independent law reconstructs the joint experiment by the assumed conditional product structure.

Condition on the ambiguity indicators and on all randomization other than the bits, and also condition on all bits outside
\[
 \mathcal A=\{j:A_j=1\}.
\]
If \(\mathcal A\neq\varnothing\), regard simultaneous success as a subset of the configurations of the remaining bits.  Two configurations differing only in coordinate \(j\in\mathcal A\) cannot both be successful: decoder \(j\) then receives the same \(S_j\), the same \(Z_{-j}\), and the same \(Z_0\), while the correct value of \(\Omega_j\) is reversed.  The successful configurations therefore form an independent set in the \(|\mathcal A|\)-dimensional binary hypercube.  Pairing vertices along any one coordinate shows that this set has at most \(2^{|\mathcal A|-1}\) elements.  Conditional on the fixed objects, the ambiguous bits remain independent and uniform.  Hence the conditional probability of simultaneous success is at most \(1/2\) when \(\mathcal A\neq\varnothing\), and is at most one when \(\mathcal A=\varnothing\).  Averaging gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr\{\widehat\Omega_j=\Omega_j\text{ for every }j\}
 &\leq \Pr(\mathcal A=\varnothing)+\frac12\Pr(\mathcal A\neq\varnothing)\\
 &=\frac12\left\{1+\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the relative-entropy consequence, let
\[
 H_j=\int\sqrt{q_{j,0}q_{j,1}}
\]
be the Hellinger affinity.  Jensen's inequality under \(Q_{j,0}\), followed by the standard affinity bound for total variation, gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\geq-2\log H_j,
 \qquad
 \lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\sqrt{1-H_j^2}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \rho_j
 \geq1-\sqrt{1-e^{-\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})}}
 \geq\frac12e^{-\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})}.
\]
If \(\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\leq\kappa\log M\), then
\[
 \rho_j\geq\frac12M^{-\kappa},
 \qquad
 \prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)
 \leq\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\}=o(1),
\]
because \(\kappa<1\).  Substitution in the first conclusion proves that the probability of at least one decoding error is at least \(1/2-o(1)\).
