Fix \(p\geq0\), \(\nu\geq2\), \(L\geq4\), and \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\).  Temporarily write \(h=h_n\) and \(B=B_n\).  Let \(\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\) solve the original unwinsorized population normal equations, and put
\[
 D_h=\sup_{t\in\{0,1\},\,x\in\mathcal B_P}
 \|\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\|,
 \qquad \mathcal G_h=\{D_h\leq(2L)^{-1}\}.
\]
All expectations involving a possibly nonmeasurable supremum are outer expectations.

On \(\mathcal G_h\), clause \(\mathrm{(viii)}\) and Weyl's inequality imply
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\{\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)\}\geq L^{-1}-(2L)^{-1}=(2L)^{-1}.
\]
The stabilization branch therefore does not activate, \(\|\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\|\leq2L\), and the empirical normal equations give
\[
 \widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)
 =\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h).
\]
Consequently the coefficient error is at most \(2L\sup_{t,x}\|\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)\|\) on \(\mathcal G_h\).

Subtracting the unwinsorized population equations from the definition of the score gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)
 ={1\over h^2}\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \mathbf1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)
 r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)\{\psi_B(Y)-Y\}
 \right].
\]
On the observed arm, clause \(\mathrm{(i)}\) gives \(Y=Y(t)\).  The basis is coordinatewise bounded by one on the kernel support.  The conditional bound in \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}winsorization\text{-}bias}\), the density bound \(f_P\leq L\), and the radius-\(h\) disk area imply
\[
 \sup_{t,x}\|\mathbb E_P\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)\|
 \leq {C B^{-3}\over h^2}P\{\|X-x\|_2\leq h\}
 \leq C B^{-3}.
\]
The corrected score lemma and the triangle inequality for outer expectation now yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P^*\sup_{t,x}\|\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)\|
 \leq C\left\{B^{-3}+\sqrt{{\log(B/h)\over nh^2}}
 +{B\log(B/h)\over nh^2}\right\}.
\]

The established first-order-bias lemma gives
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}\left|
 e_1^\top\{\beta^*_{1,P,x}(h)-\beta^*_{0,P,x}(h)\}-\tau_P(x)
 \right|\leq Ch.
\]
Clause \(\mathrm{(iii)}\) gives \(|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L\), so projection onto \([-2L,2L]\) is one-Lipschitz and fixes the target.  On \(\mathcal G_h^c\), both the projected estimator and target lie in that interval, so the loss is at most \(4L\).  Pointwise,
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq Ch+C\sup_{t,x}\|\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)\|
 +4L\mathbf1\{D_h>(2L)^{-1}\}.
\]
Since \(\mathbf1\{D_h>(2L)^{-1}\}\leq2LD_h\), the separate established Gram maximal bound gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P^*\mathbf1\{D_h>(2L)^{-1}\}
 \leq C\sqrt{{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}}
 \leq C\sqrt{{\log(B/h)\over nh^2}},
\]
where \(B\geq1\).  Therefore, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P^*\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
 |\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
 \leq C\left\{h+B^{-3}+\sqrt{{\log(B/h)\over nh^2}}
 +{B\log(B/h)\over nh^2}\right\}.
\]

Now set \(h=a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) and \(B=a_n^{-1/3}\).  The retained CTY bias and Gram regimes hold for all sufficiently large \(n\), since \(h\downarrow0\) and \(nh^2/\log(1/h)\to\infty\).  Directly,
\[
 \log(B/h)={4\over3}\log(1/h)
 ={1\over3}(\log n-\log\log n),
\]
and hence
\[
 h=a_n,\qquad B^{-3}=a_n,qquad
 \sqrt{{\log(B/h)\over nh^2}}=O(a_n),
\]
whereas
\[
 {B\log(B/h)\over nh^2}
 =O\!\left(n^{-5/12}(\log n)^{5/12}\right)=o(a_n).
\]
The constant is uniform in \(P\) and \(n\).  Taking the supremum over \(\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) proves the asserted \(Ca_n\) bound.  The construction definition supplies one explicit law-independent member of \(\mathcal T_n^{12,\pm}\).
