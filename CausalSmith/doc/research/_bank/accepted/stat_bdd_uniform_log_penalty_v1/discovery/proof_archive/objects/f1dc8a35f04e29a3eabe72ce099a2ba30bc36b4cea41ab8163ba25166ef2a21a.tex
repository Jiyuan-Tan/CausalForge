The statistical loss from a distance-only release is an angular-information loss, not a failure of causal identification.  Actual-arm thickness makes degree-zero distance smoothing stable, but without a shared angular modulus the radial conditional mean can be only first-order regular even along a perfectly straight interface.  When normalization at \(L=4\) removes all density slack, higher outcome smoothness becomes visible to the radial experiment again.  More generally, an order-\(m\) normalized-coarea expansion is sufficient for the corresponding faster distance rate, but it is an additional joint condition on density, regression, and geometry.
