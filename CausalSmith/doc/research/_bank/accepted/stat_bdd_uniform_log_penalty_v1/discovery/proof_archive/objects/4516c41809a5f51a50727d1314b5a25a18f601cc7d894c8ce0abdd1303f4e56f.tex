For each \(j\), dominate \(Q_{j,0}\) and \(Q_{j,1}\) by their sum and write their densities as \(q_{j,0}\) and \(q_{j,1}\).  The common subprobability measure has density \(q_{j,0}\wedge q_{j,1}\) and mass
\[
 \rho_j=\int(q_{j,0}\wedge q_{j,1})
 =1-\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Generate the binary compressed experiment by first drawing an ambiguity indicator \(A_j\), independent of \(\Omega_j\), with \(\Pr(A_j=1)=\rho_j\).  On \(A_j=1\), draw \(S_j\) from the normalized common submeasure, independently of \(\Omega_j\); on \(A_j=0\), draw it from the normalized residual measure corresponding to \(\Omega_j\).  Zero-mass cases may be assigned arbitrary kernels because their indicators then have probability zero.  Use independent indicators and seeds across \(j\).  Because all observation spaces are standard Borel, a regular conditional kernel for \(Z_j\) given \((S_j,\Omega_j)\) exists; applying it reconstructs the original local experiment.  Independently draw the bit-independent ancillary \(Z_0\) from its fixed law.  The conditional product premise shows that this coupling preserves the joint law of the stated experiment.

Fix the ambiguity indicators, all non-bit seeds, and the bits outside the ambiguous coordinate set \(A=\{j:A_j=1\}\).  If \(A\neq\varnothing\), consider the configurations of \((\Omega_j:j\in A)\) on which every decoder is correct.  Two configurations that differ only at coordinate \(j\in A\) cannot both succeed: decoder \(j\) sees the same \(S_j\), the same \(Z_{-j}\), and the same \(Z_0\) at the two endpoints, so its output is unchanged while the correct bit is reversed.  The successful configurations therefore form an independent set in the \(|A|\)-dimensional hypercube.  Pairing vertices along any coordinate shows that this set has at most \(2^{|A|-1}\) elements.  The ambiguous bits remain uniform, so conditional exact-success probability is at most \(1/2\).  If \(A=\varnothing\), it is at most one.  Averaging proves
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat\Omega_j=\Omega_j\text{ for every }j\}
 \leq\Pr(A=\varnothing)+\frac12\Pr(A\neq\varnothing)
 =\frac12\left\{1+\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)\right\}.
\]

For the relative-entropy consequence, define the Hellinger affinity \(H_j=\int\sqrt{q_{j,0}q_{j,1}}\).  Jensen's inequality under \(Q_{j,0}\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\geq-2\log H_j.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\sqrt{1-H_j^2}
 \leq\sqrt{1-e^{-\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})}}.
\]
Since \(1-\sqrt{1-u}\geq u/2\) for \(0\leq u\leq1\),
\[
 \rho_j\geq\frac12e^{-\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})}
 \geq\frac12M^{-\kappa}.
\]
Therefore \(\prod_j(1-\rho_j)\leq\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\}=o(1)\), and the preceding exact-success bound implies an any-error probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\).
