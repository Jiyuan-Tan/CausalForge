For the upper bound take \(h=c,a_n\) in the displayed local-constant estimator.  The canonical-distance lemma and the \(q\geq1\) H\"older envelope give uniform bias at most \(2Lh\).  The stability lemma bounds both population denominators below, while the density upper-mass lemma bounds the variance scale by \(C/(nh^2)\).  Bernstein's inequality for the bounded kernel combined with conditional sub-Gaussianity, symmetrization, and the polynomial covering bound for Euclidean balls in \(\mathbb R^2\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P,t\in\{0,1\}}
 |\widehat\mu_{t,h}(x)-\mathbb E_P\widehat\mu_{t,h}(x)|
 \leq C\sqrt{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}+o(a_n).
\]
The same concentration applied to the denominators makes the probability of a denominator smaller than half its population value exponentially small in \(nh^2\); the zero-denominator convention contributes \(o(a_n)\).  Therefore
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}})
 \leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}\right\}\leq C_{\mathrm{rect}}a_n.
\]
For the lower bound use the angular packing with \(\Delta=a_n\) and decode bit \(j\) from whether the estimate at \(x_j\) is closer to the two possible target values.  If every pointwise error is below \(c_1a_n/2\), all bits are decoded correctly.  The coordinatewise overlap direct-product lemma and the adjacent entropy bound imply that the probability of at least one error is bounded below by \(1/2-o(1)\).  The finite maximum over packing sites is measurable and is bounded by the boundary supremum, so integrating its tail gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \geq c_{\mathrm{rect}}a_n.
\]
This holds for every decision rule.  Taking the infimum and then the lower limit proves the lower inequality; combining it with the preceding witness proves the theorem.
