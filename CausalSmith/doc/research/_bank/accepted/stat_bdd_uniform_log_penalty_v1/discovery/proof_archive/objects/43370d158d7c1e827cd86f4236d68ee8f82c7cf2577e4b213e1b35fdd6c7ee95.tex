Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\) and a bandwidth \(h\leq L^{-1}\).  Let \(\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\) be the population local-polynomial coefficient and \(\widehat O_{t,x}(h)\) the centered empirical score at that coefficient.  Define the global good-Gram event
\[
 \mathcal G_h=\left\{
 \sup_{t\in\{0,1\},,x\in\mathcal B_P}
 \lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\rVert
 \leq(2L)^{-1}
 \right\}.
\]
Clause \(\mathrm{(viii)}\) of the class definition and Weyl's eigenvalue inequality give, on \(\mathcal G_h\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h))
 \geq\lambda_{\min}(\Psi_{t,P,x}(h))
 -\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\rVert
 \geq(2L)^{-1}.
\]
Thus stabilization does not activate on \(\mathcal G_h\), and the empirical and population normal equations imply
\[
 \lVert\widehat\beta_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\rVert
 \leq2L\lVert\widehat O_{t,x}(h)\rVert.
\]

The cited first-order bias lemma gives, uniformly in \(P\) and \(x\),
\[
 \left|e_1^\top\{\beta^*_{1,P,x}(h)-\beta^*_{0,P,x}(h)\}-\tau_P(x)\right|\leq Ch.
\]
Because the order-zero part of the regression envelope gives \(|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\), the true contrast belongs to \([-2L,2L]\).  Projection onto that interval is one-Lipschitz.  Consequently, on \(\mathcal G_h\),
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
 |\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq Ch+C\sup_{t,x}\lVert\widehat O_{t,x}(h)\rVert.
\]
On \(\mathcal G_h^c\), clipping bounds the loss by \(4L\).  Markov's inequality and the cited expected Gram bound give
\[
 P(\mathcal G_h^c)
 \leq2L\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
 \sup_{t,x}\lVert\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\rVert
 \right]
 \leq C\sqrt{{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}}.
\]
The class uses CTY's raw moment envelope; the centered moment required for \(\widehat O\) follows explicitly from \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}raw\text{-}to\text{-}centered\text{-}moment}\).  Combining the preceding bounds with \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}expected\text{-}local\text{-}polynomial\text{-}maximal\text{-}bounds}\) yields
\[
 \sup_P\mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
 \sup_x|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right]
 \leq C\left\{
 h+\sqrt{{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}}
 +{\log(1/h)\over n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h^2}
 \right\}.
\]

For completeness, the outer expectation is not hiding a measurability gap for this particular rule.  For a fixed known geometry, \((x,(Y_i,X_i)_{i=1}^n)\mapsto D^{\pm}_{P,x,i}\) is Borel, as are the finite sums, eigenvalue threshold, matrix inverse on the nonsingular set, and projection defining the stabilized estimator.  The target \(\tau_P\) is continuous.  Thus the loss is jointly Borel on the compact boundary times the raw sample space.  Its boundary supremum is upper semianalytic, hence universally measurable, and its completed expectation equals its outer expectation.  This argument is used only for the explicit upper-bound witness, not for arbitrary members of \(\mathcal T_n^{12,\pm}\).

Now take \(h=h_n=b_{n,\nu}\).  Then \(h_n\to0\) and
\[
 {n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h_n^2\over\log(1/h_n)}\to\infty,
\]
because \(h_n\) is at least the cube-root term in its definition.  Also \(h_n\geq a_n\), so the square-root stochastic term is \(O(a_n)\), and
\[
 h_n\geq\left\{{\log n\over n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}}\right\}^{1/3}
\]
makes the heavy-tail term no larger than the same cube-root rate.  Since \(\log(1/h_n)\asymp\log n\), the last risk display is \(O(b_{n,\nu})\), as claimed.
