Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\cup\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\), \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and put \(A=\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P\).  Because \(X\) has Lebesgue density \(f_P\), polar coordinates centered at \(x\) and Tonelli's theorem give, for every \(r>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P\{X\in A\cap B_2(x,r)\}
&=\int_{A\cap B_2(x,r)}f_P(z)\,dz\\
&=\int_0^r\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap A}f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)\,ds
=\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(0<r\leq r_0\), uniform two-sided thickness bounds the last display below by \(c_-r^2\).  If \(Q_{t,P,x}\) vanished almost everywhere on some \((0,r)\), the integral would be zero.  Thus positive-denominator radii occur in every such interval and accumulate at zero; no pointwise positivity claim is used.

The repair at the assignment interface is pointwise.  Absolute continuity alone gives \(P(X=x)=0\) for this fixed \(x\).  Hence, almost surely, \(D_{P,x}\ne0\), and its sign is \(2T-1\).  Arm recovery therefore needs neither rectifiability nor nullness of the whole interface.  For every bounded Borel function \(a\), deterministic assignment, consistency, and the same polar-coordinate identity yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[Y\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(\lVert X-x\rVert_2)\right]
&=\int_Aa(\lVert z-x\rVert_2)\mu_{t,P}(z)f_P(z)\,dz\\
&=\int_0^\infty a(s)N_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds,
\end{aligned}
\]
while the same calculation without \(Y\) gives \(\int_0^\infty a(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds\).  The Radon--Nikodym characterization consequently gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
=\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(s>0\) with \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\).

Membership in the \(q\)-H\"older ball with \(q\geq1\) gives
\[
|\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\lVert z-x\rVert_2.
\]
When \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\), the coarea ratio is an \(f_P\)-weighted average over points at distance \(s\), and therefore
\[
\left|\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
\leq\frac{\int Ls f_P\,d\mathcal H^1}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}=Ls.
\]
At \(s=0\) and at positive zero-denominator radii, the canonical definition sets the value to \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\), so the same bound holds there.  Thus \(g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)\to\mu_{t,P}(x)\) as \(s\downarrow0\).  Subtracting the two arm limits gives
\[
\lim_{s\downarrow0}\{g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P,x}(s)-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P,x}(s)\}
=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
\]
The coarea regression is therefore the identifying functional; the causal contrast remains the separately defined target.
