Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{coarea}}(q,\iota,L)\), \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and put
\[
A_t=\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P,
\qquad R_x=\lVert X-x\rVert_2.
\]
Membership in the radial-coarea ambient class and \(\mathrm{ass:density\text{-}envelope}\) give the Lebesgue density \(f_P\) of \(X\) on \(\mathcal X_P\).  Hence polar coordinates centered at \(x\), followed by Tonelli's theorem for the nonnegative integrand, give, for every \(r>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,r)\}
&=\int_{A_t\cap B_2(x,r)}f_P(z)\,dz\\
&=\int_0^r\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap A_t}
f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)\,ds\\
&=\int_0^r Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first equality also uses that \(X\in\mathcal X_P\) almost surely, and the last equality is exactly \(\mathrm{def:circle\text{-}density\text{-}integral}\).  This establishes the asserted coarea identity without any division or positivity condition on \(Q_{t,P,x}\).

Since a singleton has two-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero, absolute continuity yields
\[
P(X=x)=0.
\]
On the event \(\{X\ne x\}\), \(R_x>0\), and \(\mathrm{def:signed\text{-}distance}\) gives
\[
D_{P,x}=(2T-1)R_x.
\]
Consequently \(D_{P,x}>0\) if and only if \(T=1\), and \(D_{P,x}<0\) if and only if \(T=0\), almost surely.  Thus the sign recovers the arm; no claim that the entire interface has zero probability is required for this fixed-center conclusion.

It remains to identify a version of the conditional regression given the signed radius.  Let \(a:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\) be bounded and Borel.  The assignment-region convention in \(\mathrm{def:radial\text{-}coarea\text{-}ambient\text{-}class}\), together with \(\mathrm{ass:deterministic\text{-}assignment}\), identifies \(\{T=t\}\) with the corresponding arm up to a \(P\)-null set.  Moreover, \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) and the definition of \(\mu_{t,P}\) imply
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X,T=t]=\mu_{t,P}(X)
\quad\text{on }\{T=t\}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[Y\mathbf 1\{T=t\}a(R_x)\right]
&=\int_{A_t}a(\lVert z-x\rVert_2)\mu_{t,P}(z)f_P(z)\,dz\\
&=\int_0^\infty a(s)N_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the second equality follows by applying the same polar-coordinate identity to the positive and negative parts of the integrable outcome-weighted integrand, and the last integrand is the one in \(\mathrm{def:circle\text{-}outcome\text{-}integral}\).  Repeating the calculation with \(Y\) replaced by one yields
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbf 1\{T=t\}a(R_x)\right]
=\int_0^\infty a(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
Equivalently, for every bounded Borel function \(b:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\), the two identities with \(a(s)=b((2t-1)s)\) show on the half-line of sign \(2t-1\) that the outcome-weighted law of \(D_{P,x}\) has Lebesgue density \(N_{t,P,x}(s)\), whereas the law of \(D_{P,x}\) has density \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\).  The Radon--Nikodym characterization of conditional expectation thus permits the version
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
=\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(s>0\) such that \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\).  Division is made only on this positive-denominator set; the version can be assigned arbitrarily on zero-denominator radii.

Finally, suppose that for some \(r_*>0\) both denominators are positive on \((0,r_*]\) and that, for \(t=0,1\),
\[
\lim_{s\downarrow0}\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}=\mu_{t,P}(x).
\]
Subtraction of these two limits gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{s\downarrow0}
\left\{
\frac{N_{1,P,x}(s)}{Q_{1,P,x}(s)}
-\frac{N_{0,P,x}(s)}{Q_{0,P,x}(s)}
\right\}
&=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)\\
&=\tau_P(x),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the final equality is the definition of the causal boundary effect.  Thus the displayed radial limit identifies the causal target under the stated positivity and boundary-limit conditions; the causal target has not been defined to equal the observed-data functional.
