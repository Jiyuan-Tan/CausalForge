We first verify nonemptiness.  Take the uniform score density \(1/4\) on \([-1,1]^2\), the prescribed half-square assignment regions, \(\gamma_P(u)=(2u-1,0)\), constant regressions in \((1/4,3/4)\), and Bernoulli potential outcomes with independent standard Gaussian noise.  The chart has speed two and vanishing derivatives of order at least two, and its image, a closed convex segment, has infinite reach.  A boundary ball contains in each arm at least a quarter disk, so its arm mass is at least \(\pi r^2/16\geq c_-r^2\).  The support, density, assignment, H\"older, variance, and sub-Gaussian conditions all hold.

For the full-location upper bound, choose a fixed compactly supported Lipschitz bivariate kernel positive on the unit disk.  On each arm, fit a total-degree-\((q-1)\) local polynomial in \((X_i-x)/h\), use its intercept only when the smallest eigenvalue of the scaled empirical Gram matrix exceeds a fixed positive threshold, and use zero otherwise; subtract the two arm intercepts.  This is an explicit jointly measurable function of the full sample and known geometry, hence belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\).  Endpoint trimming leaves a fixed distance from the vertical sides.  For all sufficiently small \(h\), each population Gram quadratic form is the integral of the square of a polynomial against a density at least \(L^{-1}\) on a fixed half-disk.  A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on that open set, so compactness of the unit sphere in the fixed-dimensional coefficient space gives a uniform strictly positive minimum eigenvalue.  This is the required rank and inverse condition.

Taylor's formula through degree \(q-1\) gives bias at most \(Ch^q\).  Entrywise exponential bounds for the bounded Gram summands, and conditional sub-Gaussian exponential bounds for the outcome sums, on a polynomial boundary net imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widetilde\tau_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
The kernel's Lipschitz property extends the bounds off the net.  The eigenvalue cutoff makes the estimator zero on the exceptional event, whose probability is polynomially small, so no uncontrolled inverse occurs.  Taking \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\) proves the upper bound \(Cs_{n,q}\).

For the matching converse, retain the uniform score density and place \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) disjoint smooth bivariate regression bumps of width \(h\) and height \(c h^q\) at trimmed boundary centers.  Use the same Gaussian-noised Bernoulli outcomes.  For sufficiently small fixed \(c\), every alternative belongs to the straight class and adjacent target curves are separated by \(c h^q\).  Since a bump has area \(O(h^2)\), the Bernoulli relative-entropy inequality and data processing through the Gaussian channel give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn(h^q)^2h^2=Cnh^{2q+2}.
\]
At \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\), reduce \(c\) so this is at most \(\alpha\log M\) with \(\alpha<1\).  If \(J\) is uniform on the \(M\) alternatives and \(Z=O_{1:n}\), convexity of relative entropy gives
\[
 I(J;Z)\leq M^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^M
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
For any decoder with error probability \(p_e\), writing the conditional entropy according to the error indicator gives
\(H(J\mid Z)\leq\log2+p_e\log M\), and hence
\(I(J;Z)\geq(1-p_e)\log M-\log2\).  Thus \(p_e\) stays bounded away from zero.  Sup-norm accuracy below half the bump height would identify \(J\), so
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq c s_{n,q}.
\]
This proves the full-location equivalence.

Suppose next that \(L>4\).  The distance local-constant calculation, with denominator order \(nh^2\), first-order bias \(Ch\), and stochastic term \(C\sqrt{\log n/(nh^2)}\), gives the upper bound \(Ca_n\).  The canonical-square angular family is contained in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\) because the strict density-envelope slack permits its nonzero relative angular perturbations.  In the mean-\(2n\) Poisson experiment, its disjoint local channels satisfy the premises of the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma with relative entropy at most \(2\alpha\log M\), and \(2\alpha<1\).  Midpoint thresholding followed by the exponentially accurate fixed-sample subsampling argument therefore gives the matching lower bound \(ca_n\).

Finally suppose \(L=4\).  The square has area four and \(f_P\geq1/4\), so
\[
 1=\int_{[-1,1]^2}f_P(z)\,dz\geq1.
\]
Equality forces \(f_P=1/4\) almost everywhere, and continuity makes the equality pointwise.  For a trimmed boundary point and sufficiently small \(s\), each arm-specific half-circle lies inside the square, and therefore
\[
 Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\frac\pi4s.
\]
Taylor's formula for the bivariate \(q\)-H\"older regression, integrated over that half-circle, gives uniformly bounded coefficients with
\[
 \left|g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 -\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k\right|\leq Cs^q.
\]
Fit a degree-\((q-1)\) one-sided local polynomial in the signed radius and apply the same fixed eigenvalue cutoff.  Its limiting Gram quadratic form integrates the square of a nonzero polynomial against \(uK_\triangle(u)\,du\) on \((0,1)\), so it is uniformly positive definite.  The estimator consequently has risk
\[
 C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\},
\]
and balancing gives \(Cs_{n,q}\).  Every distance-only rule can be implemented from the full scores and known geometry, so \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\) is informationally no larger than \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\); the full-location lower bound thus gives the distance lower bound \(cs_{n,q}\).  Since \(q/(2q+2)>1/4\) exactly when \(q>1\), all asserted separation and coincidence cases follow.
