We first verify nonemptiness.  Take the uniform score density \(1/4\) on \([-1,1]^2\), the prescribed two half-squares, \(\gamma_P(u)=(2u-1,0)\), constant regressions in \((1/4,3/4)\), and Gaussian-noised Bernoulli potential outcomes.  The chart speed is two, its derivatives of order at least two vanish, and its image is a closed convex segment, hence has infinite reach.  Every sufficiently small boundary half- or quarter-ball has arm mass bounded below by a fixed multiple of \(r^2\), which exceeds \(c_-r^2\) under \(c_-\leq1/(16L)\).  All support, assignment, positivity, variance, and tail conditions therefore hold.

For the full-location upper bound, on each arm use a fixed compactly supported positive bivariate kernel and the degree-\((q-1)\) local-polynomial intercept, and subtract the two intercepts.  Endpoint trimming gives a fixed positive distance from the vertical sides.  Thus, for all sufficiently small bandwidths, the scaled population polynomial Gram matrix is uniformly positive definite: after rescaling, its quadratic form is the integral of a squared nonzero polynomial against a density bounded below on a fixed half-ball.  This supplies the rank and inverse condition.  Taylor's formula through degree \(q-1\) and the H\"older remainder give bias at most \(Ch^q\).  Matrix Bernstein and conditional sub-Gaussian concentration on a polynomial boundary net, followed by the kernel's Lipschitz extension, give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widetilde\tau_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
The estimator is a single jointly measurable map of the full sample and known geometry, so it belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\).  With \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\), this proves the upper bound \(Cs_{n,q}\).

For the matching full-location converse, retain the uniform score density and place \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) disjoint smooth bivariate regression bumps of width \(h\) and height \(c h^q\) at trimmed boundary centers.  Use bounded Gaussian-noised Bernoulli outcomes as in the packing lemma.  Choosing \(c\) small places every alternative in the same H\"older, variance, tail, support, and assignment class.  The target curves are separated by \(ch^q\), while tensorization and the area \(O(h^2)\) of one bump give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn(h^q)^2h^2=Cnh^{2q+2}.
\]
At \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\), decreasing \(c\) makes this at most \(\alpha\log M\) for some \(\alpha<1\).  If the alternative index \(J\) is uniform, then
\[
 I(J;O_{1:n})
 \leq M^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^M\operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
For every decoder with error probability \(p_e\), the entropy identity gives \(I(J;O_{1:n})\geq(1-p_e)\log M-\log2\).  Hence \(p_e\) stays bounded away from zero.  Sup-norm error below half the bump height would decode \(J\), proving \(R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}\geq cs_{n,q}\).

Suppose next that \(L>4\).  The smooth-class local-constant upper bound restricts to \(Ca_n\).  The canonical-square angular family belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\) precisely because the strict density-envelope slack allows its nonzero angular perturbations.  In the mean-\(2n\) Poisson experiment, the disjoint local channels are conditionally independent standard-Borel processes and the outside process is bit-independent.  The packing slack and the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma yield an any-bit-error probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\), even after all cross-center observations are revealed.  Midpoint thresholding and the same exponentially accurate subsampling argument as above give \(R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}\geq ca_n\).

Finally suppose \(L=4\).  The support square has area four and \(f_P\geq1/4\), so
\[
 1=\int_{[-1,1]^2}f_P(z)\,dz\geq1.
\]
Equality forces \(f_P=1/4\) almost everywhere, and density continuity upgrades this to pointwise equality.  For an endpoint-trimmed \(x\) and \(s\) below its fixed distance from the vertical sides, each arm-specific half-circle remains in the square, so
\[
 Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\frac\pi4s.
\]
Taylor's formula for the bivariate \(q\)-H\"older regression, integrated over that half-circle, supplies uniformly bounded coefficients satisfying
\[
 \left|g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 -\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k\right|\leq Cs^q.
\]
The scaled one-sided radial Gram matrix for degree \(q-1\) is positive definite because its limiting quadratic form integrates the square of a nonzero polynomial against \(uK_\triangle(u)\,du\) on \((0,1)\).  The explicit one-sided local-polynomial intercept therefore has risk
\[
 C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\},
\]
and balancing gives the distance upper bound \(Cs_{n,q}\).  A coordinatewise signed-distance rule can be computed from the full scores, so the full-location lower bound applies to the smaller distance decision class and gives \(R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}\geq cs_{n,q}\).  This proves every displayed rate.  Since \(q/(2q+2)>1/4\) exactly when \(q>1\), strict separation occurs only for \(q>1\) and \(L>4\); the exponents coincide when \(q=1\), and the entire \(L=4\) case has the common \(s_{n,q}\) rate.
