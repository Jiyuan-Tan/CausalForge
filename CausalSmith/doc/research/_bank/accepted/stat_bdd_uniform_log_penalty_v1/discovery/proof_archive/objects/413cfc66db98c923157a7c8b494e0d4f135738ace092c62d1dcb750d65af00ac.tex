Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{12}(p,\nu,L)\), put \(h=h_n=a_n\) and \(B=B_n=a_n^{-1/3}\), and let \(\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\) solve the original population normal equations formed with \(Y\).  Define
\[
 \mathcal G_h=\left\{
 \sup_{t,x}\|\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h)-\Psi_{t,P,x}(h)\|
 \leq(2L)^{-1}\right\}.
\]
Clause \(\mathrm{(viii)}\) and Weyl's inequality imply \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Psi_{t,x}(h))\geq(2L)^{-1}\) on \(\mathcal G_h\).  Stabilization therefore does not activate, and the empirical normal equations give
\[
 \|\widehat\beta^B_{t,x}(h)-\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\|
 \leq2L\|\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)\|.
\]

Because \(\beta^*\) solves the unwinsorized population equations,
\[
 \mathbb E_P\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)
 ={1\over h^2}\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \mathbf1\{D^{\pm}_{P,x}\in I_t\}K_\square(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)
 r_p(D^{\pm}_{P,x}/h)\{\psi_B(Y)-Y\}\right].
\]
On the observed arm, consistency makes the conditional law of \(Y\) that of \(Y(t)\).  The winsorization-bias lemma, boundedness of \(r_p\) on \([-1,1]\), the density upper bound, and the area of a radius-\(h\) disk yield
\[
 \sup_{t,x}\|\mathbb E_P\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)\|\leq CB^{-3}.
\]
The winsorized-score maximal lemma then gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P^*\sup_{t,x}\|\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)\|
 \leq C\left\{B^{-3}+\sqrt{{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}}
 +{B\log(1/h)\over nh^2}\right\}.
\]

The cited first-order bias lemma supplies
\[
 \sup_x\left|e_1^\top\{\beta^*_{1,P,x}(h)-\beta^*_{0,P,x}(h)\}-\tau_P(x)\right|
 \leq Ch.
\]
The order-zero regression envelope gives \(|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L\), and projection onto \([-2L,2L]\) is one-Lipschitz.  Thus, on \(\mathcal G_h\),
\[
 \sup_x|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+\sup_{t,x}\|\widehat O^B_{t,x}(h)\|\right\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal G_h^c\), clipping bounds the loss by \(4L\).  The cited expected Gram bound and Markov's inequality give
\[
 P(\mathcal G_h^c)
 \leq C\sqrt{{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sup_P\mathbb E_P^*\sup_x|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+B^{-3}+\sqrt{{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}}
 +{B\log(1/h)\over nh^2}\right\}.
\]

At \(h=a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) and \(B=a_n^{-1/3}\),
\[
 h=a_n,
 \qquad B^{-3}=a_n,
 \qquad \sqrt{{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}}=O(a_n),
\]
and
\[
 {B\log(1/h)\over nh^2}
 =O\!\left(n^{-5/12}(\log n)^{5/12}\right)=o(a_n).
\]
Also \(nh^2/\log(1/h)\to\infty\), so the maximal lemma applies.  This proves the uniform \(Ca_n\) bound.

For this explicit rule, all finite sums, eigenvalue thresholds, matrix inverses on the nonsingular set, winsorization, and clipping are jointly Borel in the boundary point and raw sample.  Since \(\tau_P\) is continuous and \(\mathcal B_P\) is compact, the boundary supremum is upper semianalytic and its completed expectation equals its outer expectation.  This measurability observation is used only for the explicit witness.
