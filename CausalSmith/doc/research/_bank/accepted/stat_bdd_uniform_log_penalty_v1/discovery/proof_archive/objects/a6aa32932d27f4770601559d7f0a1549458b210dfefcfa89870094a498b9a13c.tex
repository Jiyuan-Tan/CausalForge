We verify the construction, including support, normalization, positivity, smoothness, moment, and boundary conditions.

Fix first the square \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\), the two half-square assignment regions, and
\[
 \mathcal B=\{(u-1/2,0):0\leq u\leq1\},
 \qquad \gamma(u)=(u-1/2,0).
\]
The base score density is one.  The displayed affine chart is injective and \(C^\infty\), so it satisfies the smooth-embedding condition for every \(\iota\geq1\); no derivative, speed, reach, or length envelope is required.  At an endpoint, each arm intersected with a ball of radius at most \(r_0\leq1/4\) contains a quarter disk.  The densities below are at least \(3/4\), so each such arm has probability at least \(3\pi r^2/16\), which is greater than \(c_-r^2\) because \(c_-\leq1/(16L)\).  The same lower bound away from the endpoints is immediate.  Compact support, the assignment partition, and two-sided thickness are therefore uniform.

Choose a nonnegative radial \(C^\infty\) function \(\phi\), supported in the open unit disk, with \(\phi(0)=1\).  For fixed constants \(d,d_1>0\), put
\[
 \Delta=d a_n,
 \qquad w=(d_1\Delta)^{1/q}.
\]
Choose centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) on \(\gamma([\delta,1-\delta])\), separated by at least \(3w\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), the disks \(B_2(x_j,w)\) are disjoint and lie in the interior of \(S\), and one-dimensional packing gives
\[
 M\geq c w^{-1}\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}.
\]
Write \(\phi_j(z)=\phi((z-x_j)/w)\).  For \(0\leq k\leq q\),
\[
 \lVert D^k(\Delta\phi_j)\rVert_\infty
 \leq C_\phi\Delta w^{-k}.
\]
The right-hand side is at most \(C_\phi/d_1\) when \(k=q\), and, after \(n\) is sufficiently large, also when \(k<q\).  Since the supports are disjoint, the same bounds hold for every signed sum.  Taking \(d_1\) sufficiently large and then taking a fixed affine slope \(b>0\) sufficiently small places all regressions below in the prescribed isotropic \(q\)-H\"older ball.

For \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\), define
\[
 p_{0,\omega}(z)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 p_{1,\omega}(z)=\frac12+bz_1+\Delta\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j\phi_j(z).
\]
After decreasing \(b\) and \(d\), these functions take values in \([1/4,3/4]\).  Conditional on \(X=z\), take independent potential outcomes
\[
 Y(t)=B_t+\varepsilon_t,
 \qquad B_t\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_{t,\omega}(z)),
 \qquad \varepsilon_t\sim N(0,1).
\]
Use the deterministic assignment rule and consistency to define \(T\) and \(Y\).  Then \(\mu_{t,P_\omega}=p_{t,\omega}\) and
\[
 1\leq\sigma^2_{t,P_\omega}(z)
 =1+p_{t,\omega}(z)\{1-p_{t,\omega}(z)\}
 \leq\frac54,
\]
which lies in \([L^{-1},L]\).  Hoeffding's exponential bound for a centered Bernoulli variable and the Gaussian moment-generating identity give, for every real \(\lambda\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\exp\{\lambda(Y(t)-p_{t,\omega}(X))\}\mid X\right]
 \leq e^{\lambda^2/8}e^{\lambda^2/2}
 \leq e^{L^2\lambda^2/2}.
\]
Thus the variance and tail envelopes are common to the entire family.

It remains to hide the \(j\)-th regression bump in the radial experiment centered at \(x_j\).  Let \(\chi\) be continuous, with \(\chi(v)=0\) for \(v\leq1\) and \(\chi(v)=1\) for \(v\geq2\), and define
\[
 A_n(r)=-\frac{2\Delta\phi(r/w)}{br}
 \chi\!\left(\frac{br}{8\Delta}\right).
\]
There is no division at zero: the cutoff vanishes on a neighborhood of zero, and the displayed expression is defined to be zero there.  On its active region \(br\geq8\Delta\), \(|A_n(r)|\leq1/4\).  Smooth compact support of \(\phi\) makes the correction vanish at the outer edge.  Define
\[
 f_\omega(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j A_n(\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2)
 \frac{z_1-(x_j)_1}{\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2}
 \mathbf 1\{z\in B_2(x_j,w)\},
\]
with each summand set to zero at its center.  Disjointness makes the formula unambiguous.  The angular correction integrates to zero on every full circle and each bump disk lies inside \(S\), so \(\int_S f_\omega=1\).  Moreover, \(f_\omega\) is continuous and belongs to \([3/4,5/4]\subseteq[L^{-1},L]\).

Compare adjacent vertices \(\omega\) and \(\omega^{(j)}\), oriented so that \(\omega_j=1\).  On either half-circle centered at \(x_j\), \(\int\cos\theta\,d\theta=0\).  Hence the two arm-specific radial mass densities agree at every radius.  On the treated upper half-circle, whenever \(br\geq16\Delta\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\int_0^\pi p_{1,\omega}(x_j+r e_\theta)
       \{1+A_n(r)\cos\theta\}\,d\theta
 -\int_0^\pi p_{1,\omega^{(j)}}(x_j+r e_\theta)\,d\theta\\
 &\qquad=\pi\Delta\phi(r/w)+\frac\pi2brA_n(r)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the control arm the equality follows from the constant regression.  A mixture of Bernoulli variables is Bernoulli with success probability equal to the mixed success probability, and adding the common Gaussian noise is a common Markov kernel.  Therefore both complete arm-specific radial outcome laws agree outside \(r\leq16\Delta/b\), not merely their conditional means.

Within this exceptional radius, the common radial density is bounded by \(Cr\), all success probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\), and the radially averaged success probabilities differ by at most \(C\Delta\).  From \(\log u\leq u-1\),
\[
 \operatorname{kl}(p,p')\leq\frac{(p-p')^2}{p'(1-p')},
\]
and relative entropy cannot increase after adding the common Gaussian channel.  Hence one compressed observation obeys
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P^{\mathrm{dist}}_{\omega,x_j},
 P^{\mathrm{dist}}_{\omega^{(j)},x_j})
 \leq C\int_0^{16\Delta/b}\Delta^2r\,dr
 \leq C'\Delta^4.
\]
Independence tensorizes relative entropy, so
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb Q_{n,P_\omega,x_j},
 \mathbb Q_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}},x_j})
 \leq C' n\Delta^4=C'd^4\log n.
\]
Also \(\log M=(1/(4q)+o(1))\log n\).  Choosing \(d\) sufficiently small gives the displayed bound with a fixed \(\alpha<1/8\).  At \(x_j\), the adjacent target values differ by \(\Delta\phi_j(x_j)=d a_n\), so one may take \(c_1=d\).  The global density bounds and fixed half-square geometry give the common arm-specific local-mass bounds.  Taking independent observations now completes membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\cap\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\).

For the canonical square \([-1,1]^2\), use \(\gamma(u)=(2u-1,0)\) and base density \(1/4\).  If \(L>4\), set \(\varepsilon_L=1-4/L>0\), replace the cutoff constant eight by \(C_L\geq4/\varepsilon_L\), and multiply the preceding relative perturbation by the base density.  Choose \(d_1\) large enough that the cancellation region lies inside every bump, including when \(q=1\), and then choose \(d/b\) small enough that all disks remain away from the trimmed endpoints.  The relative density perturbation is at most \(\varepsilon_L/2\), whence
\[
 L^{-1}\leq\frac14(1-\varepsilon_L/2)
 \leq f_\omega(z)
 \leq\frac14(1+\varepsilon_L/2)\leq L.
\]
All cancellation and entropy calculations change only by constants depending on \(L\), proving the straight-class clause.  When \(L=4\), normalization and the lower envelope on a square of area four force \(f_P\equiv1/4\), so the strict-slack clause is correctly restricted to \(L>4\).
