If a rule in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) is generated by a common Borel map \(T_n\), then \(\mathsf T_n(x,v)=T_n(v)\) is jointly Borel and generates the same rule in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\).  This proves inclusion.

To see that the inclusion is strict, consider the point-indexed family
\[
 T_{n,x}(v)=x_1.
\]
It is generated by the jointly continuous map \(\mathsf T_n(x,v)=x_1\), so it belongs to \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\).  Let \(P_0\) have \(X\) uniform on \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\) and \(Y\sim N(0,1)\) independent of \(X\).  Its density is one on its compact support, its boundary is rectifiable, its regression is zero, and its conditional variance is one; hence \(P_0\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) for every \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\).  At the two boundary points \(x_-=(-1/2,0)\) and \(x_+=(1/2,0)\), reflection in the vertical axis shows
\[
 \mathcal L_{P_0}\{V_{n,P_0}(x_-)\}
 =\mathcal L_{P_0}\{V_{n,P_0}(x_+)\}.
\]
Were the displayed point-indexed family generated by one common map \(T_n\), the two random variables obtained by applying \(T_n\) to these identically distributed inputs would have the same law.  But the required outputs are the distinct constants \(-1/2\) and \(1/2\), respectively.  This contradiction proves strictness.
