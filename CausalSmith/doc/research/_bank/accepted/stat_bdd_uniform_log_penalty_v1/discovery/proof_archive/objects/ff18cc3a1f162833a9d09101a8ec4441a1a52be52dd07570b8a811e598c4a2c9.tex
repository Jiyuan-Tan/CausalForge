Take \(0<h\leq2r_0\).  The triangular kernel satisfies \(K_\triangle(u)\geq1/2\) whenever \(|u|\leq1/2\).  On \(\{T=t,\ \lVert X-x\rVert_2<h/2\}\), deterministic assignment and the definition of signed distance give \(|D_{P,x}|/h<1/2\).  Therefore, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\), \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Psi^{(0)}_{t,P,x}(h)
&=\frac1{h^2}\mathbb E_P\!\left[K_\triangle(D_{P,x}/h)\mathbf1\{T=t\}\right]\\
&\geq\frac1{2h^2}P\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,h/2)\}\\
&\geq\frac1{2h^2}c_-\left(\frac h2\right)^2
=\frac{c_-}{8}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality is the uniform two-sided-thickness assumption, whose radius condition holds because \(h/2\leq r_0\).  The same calculation applies to every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\) and every \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\subseteq\mathcal B_P\).  Thus one may take \(\kappa_0=c_-/8>0\).  This also records the required bandwidth boundary condition explicitly.
