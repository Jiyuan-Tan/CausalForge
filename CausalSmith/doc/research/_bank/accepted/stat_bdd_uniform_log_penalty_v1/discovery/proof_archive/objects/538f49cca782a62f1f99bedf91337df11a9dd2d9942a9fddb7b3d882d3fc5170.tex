For each \(j\), dominate \(Q_{j,0}\) and \(Q_{j,1}\) by \(Q_{j,0}+Q_{j,1}\), and denote the corresponding densities by \(q_{j,0}\) and \(q_{j,1}\).  Their common subprobability measure has density \(q_{j,0}\wedge q_{j,1}\) and mass
\[
 \rho_j=\int(q_{j,0}\wedge q_{j,1})
 =1-\lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Introduce an ambiguity indicator \(A_j\), independent of \(\Omega_j\), with \(\Pr(A_j=1)=\rho_j\).  On \(\{A_j=1\}\), draw \(S_j\) from the normalized common submeasure, independently of \(\Omega_j\).  On \(\{A_j=0\}\), draw \(S_j\) from the normalized residual measure belonging to \(\Omega_j\).  A zero-mass residual can be assigned an arbitrary probability kernel because the associated event has probability zero.  Use independent indicators and randomization seeds across coordinates.  Since the spaces are standard Borel, a regular conditional distribution of \(Z_j\) given \((S_j,\Omega_j)\) exists.  Drawing from these conditional distributions reconstructs the original conditional law of every \(Z_j\), and drawing \(Z_0\) independently from its bit-independent law reconstructs the joint experiment by the assumed conditional product structure.

Condition on the ambiguity indicators, all non-bit randomization seeds, and all bits outside
\[
 A=\{j:A_j=1\}.
\]
If \(A\neq\varnothing\), consider the configurations of the remaining bits for which every decoder is correct.  Two configurations differing only in coordinate \(j\in A\) cannot both be successful.  Indeed, decoder \(j\) then sees the same \(S_j\), the same \(Z_{-j}\), and the same \(Z_0\), while the correct value of \(\Omega_j\) is reversed.  Thus the successful configurations form an independent set in the \(|A|\)-dimensional binary hypercube.  Pairing vertices along any one coordinate shows that such a set has at most \(2^{|A|-1}\) elements.  Conditional on the fixed objects, the ambiguous bits remain independent and uniform, so the conditional probability of simultaneous success is at most \(1/2\).  When \(A=\varnothing\), that probability is at most one.  Averaging gives
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat\Omega_j=\Omega_j\text{ for every }j\}
 \leq \Pr(A=\varnothing)+\frac12\Pr(A\neq\varnothing)
 =\frac12\left\{1+\prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)\right\}.
\]

For the relative-entropy consequence, let
\[
 H_j=\int\sqrt{q_{j,0}q_{j,1}}
\]
be the Hellinger affinity.  Jensen's inequality under \(Q_{j,0}\) and then Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\geq-2\log H_j,
 \qquad
 \lVert Q_{j,0}-Q_{j,1}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\sqrt{1-H_j^2}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \rho_j
 \geq1-\sqrt{1-e^{-\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})}}
 \geq\frac12e^{-\operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})}.
\]
If the displayed divergence is at most \(\kappa\log M\), then
\[
 \rho_j\geq\frac12M^{-\kappa},
 \qquad
 \prod_{j=1}^M(1-\rho_j)
 \leq\exp\{-M^{1-\kappa}/2\}=o(1),
\]
because \(\kappa<1\).  Substitution in the first conclusion proves that the probability of at least one error is at least \(1/2-o(1)\).
