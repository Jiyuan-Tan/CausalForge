Fix a displayed law, boundary point, and arm.  Let \(A=\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P\).  The map \(z\mapsto\lVert z-x\rVert_2\) is Lipschitz and has gradient of norm one away from \(x\), a Lebesgue-null point.  The coarea formula and the density of \(X\) therefore give, for \(r>0\),
\[
P\{X\in A\cap B_2(x,r)\}
=\int_0^r\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap A}f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)\,ds
=\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
For \(0<r\leq r_0\), the thickness assumption bounds this expression below by \(c_-r^2\).  In particular, every interval \((0,r)\) with \(r\leq r_0\) contains a radius at which \(Q_{t,P,x}>0\); otherwise the nonnegative integral would vanish.  Hence the positive-denominator radii accumulate at zero.  Notice that this conclusion neither states nor requires positivity at every radius.

Rectifiability of \(\mathcal B_P\) implies two-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero, and the same is true for a smooth embedded curve.  Since \(X\) has a Lebesgue density, \(P(X\in\mathcal B_P)=0\).  Deterministic assignment therefore makes the sign of \(D_{P,x}\) identify the arm almost surely.  Applying coarea once to the joint measure of \((Y,D_{P,x})\), and using consistency, shows that the radial marginal density in arm \(t\) is \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\), while its outcome-weighted density is
\[
\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P}
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=z,T=t]f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)
=N_{t,P,x}(s)
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(s>0\).  Here consistency and deterministic assignment give \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=z,T=t]=\mu_{t,P}(z)\) on arm \(t\).  The Radon--Nikodym characterization of conditional expectation then yields
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
=\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every positive radius with positive denominator.

Because \(q\geq1\), membership in the stated H\"older ball gives the Lipschitz bound
\(|\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\lVert z-x\rVert_2\).  If \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\), the ratio \(N/Q\) is a weighted average over points at distance \(s\), whence
\[
\left|\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
\leq\frac{\int Ls f_P\,d\mathcal H^1}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}=Ls.
\]
If \(s=0\), or if \(s>0\) and \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)=0\), the canonical definition sets the regression equal to \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\), so the same inequality holds.  Consequently
\[
\lim_{s\downarrow0}g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)=\mu_{t,P}(x).
\]
Subtracting the two arm limits and using the definition of the causal target gives
\[
\lim_{s\downarrow0}\{g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P,x}(s)-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P,x}(s)\}
=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
\]
Thus the canonical radial regression identifies the causal contrast at the boundary; it does not redefine that contrast.
