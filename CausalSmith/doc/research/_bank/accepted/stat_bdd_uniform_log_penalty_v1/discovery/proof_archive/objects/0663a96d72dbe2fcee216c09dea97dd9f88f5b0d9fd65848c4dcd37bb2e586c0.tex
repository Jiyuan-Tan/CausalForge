Fix \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\).  For every sufficiently large \(n\), take the family supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}support\text{-}boundary\text{-}angular\text{-}packing}\).  Write \(M=M_n\), set \(\Delta=c_1a_n\), and let
\[
 C_j=B_2(x_j,w_n)\cap S,
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq M,
\]
be its pairwise disjoint half-disc cells.  The packing lemma gives numbers \(u_{j,0},u_{j,1}\) such that
\[
 \mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)=u_{j,\omega_j},
 \qquad |u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|\geq\Delta.
\]
It also gives \(M_n\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}\).  Because \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\to0\),
\[
 M_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Let \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) be arbitrary.  By \(\mathrm{def:point\text{-}indexed\text{-}distance\text{-}decision\text{-}class}\), there is a jointly Borel measurable map
\[
 \mathsf T_n:\mathbb R^2\times(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\]
selected independently of \(P\), and, on writing \(T_{n,x}(v)=\mathsf T_n(x,v)\),
\[
 \widehat\mu_{n,P}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)=T_{n,x}(V_{n,P}(x))
\]
for every admissible \(P\) and every boundary point \(x\).  If this rule has infinite maximal risk, the desired lower bound is immediate, so it remains only to treat the finite-risk case.

Embed the fixed-sample rule in an experiment whose restrictions to the cells are independent.  Under \(P_\omega\), generate a Poisson point process of mean \(2n\) on the observation space and attach independent marks uniformly distributed on \((0,1)\).  If the point count \(N\) is at least \(n\), retain the \(n\) observations having the smallest marks, order them by their marks, form their compressed distance sample at each queried point \(x\), and apply \(T_{n,x}\).  If \(N<n\), return zero at every boundary point.  Denote the resulting rule by \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\).  Conditional on \(N=k\geq n\), the retained observations are independent with common law \(P_\omega\): the marks are independent of the observations, and their ordering produces a random permutation independent of the observation values.  The construction uses the fixed family \(\{T_{n,x}:x\in\mathbb R^2\}\) and is therefore independent of \(\omega\) and of the law in the packing family.

Put independent uniform bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) on the packing vertices.  Let \(Z_j\) be the complete marked restriction of the Poisson process to \(C_j\), and let \(Z_0\) be its restriction to \(S\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^M C_j\).  The marked observation space and the spaces of its finite counting measures are standard Borel.  Conditional on the bits, \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) are independent because restrictions of a Poisson point process to disjoint Borel sets are independent.  By cellwise locality in \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}support\text{-}boundary\text{-}angular\text{-}packing}\), the one-observation subprobability law on \(C_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), whereas the law on the complement of the cells is independent of all bits.  Hence the law of \(Z_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), and the law of \(Z_0\) is bit-independent.

For each \(j\), let \(S_j\) retain from \(Z_j\) only every point's mark, outcome, and distance from \(x_j\).  The coordinate-specific decoder to be defined below is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\).  Indeed, the full restrictions outside \(C_j\) reveal the marks, outcomes, and locations of all their points and therefore their distances from \(x_j\), while \(S_j\) supplies precisely the allowed outcome--distance information for points inside \(C_j\).  Sorting all revealed marks reconstructs the thinned ordered sample.  The section \(T_{n,x_j}=\mathsf T_n(x_j,\cdot)\) is Borel measurable because \(\mathsf T_n\) is jointly Borel.  Consequently \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\), and hence its midpoint decoder, is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\), separately for every \(j\).  No equality among the sections \(T_{n,x_j}\) is used.

We next verify the relative-entropy hypothesis of \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\).  Adjacent packing laws are identical off \(C_j\), their common cell mass is \(m_n\), and every support point at distance less than \(w_n\) from \(x_j\) belongs to \(C_j\).  Let \(\nu_{j,b}\) be the subprobability law of one outcome--distance pair restricted to \(C_j\), under bit value \(b\in\{0,1\}\).  Both subprobabilities have mass \(m_n\).  The corresponding full one-observation compressed laws have the disjoint-support form
\[
 \nu_{j,b}+\lambda_j,
\]
where \(\lambda_j\) is common to the two bits and is supported on radii at least \(w_n\), whereas \(\nu_{j,b}\) is supported on radii below \(w_n\), up to null boundaries.  Orient the two bit values and define
\[
 D_j=\int\log\!\left(\frac{d\nu_{j,0}}{d\nu_{j,1}}\right)d\nu_{j,0}.
\]
The disjoint-support decomposition and product factorization of likelihood ratios yield
\[
 nD_j
 =\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
 \mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
 \right)
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
The finiteness of this divergence supplies the required absolute continuity, and the packing lemma gives the same inequality in the reverse orientation.

Let \(Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)\).  Under either bit, the cell count satisfies \(K_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2nm_n)\), so its common law and the independent marks contribute no relative entropy.  Conditional on \(K_j=k\), the compressed cell points are independent with law \(\nu_{j,b}/m_n\).  Because the two subprobabilities have the same mass,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\nu_{j,0}/m_n,\nu_{j,1}/m_n)=\frac{D_j}{m_n}.
\]
Conditioning on the common count and taking expectations therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
 =\mathbb E[K_j]\frac{D_j}{m_n}
 =2nD_j
 \leq2\alpha\log M.
\]
The packing fixes \(0<\alpha<1/8\), so \(\kappa=2\alpha<1\).

At \(x_j\), decode \(\Omega_j\) by thresholding \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\) at the midpoint of \(u_{j,0}\) and \(u_{j,1}\), reversing the inequality when \(u_{j,1}<u_{j,0}\).  Applying the exact finite-\(M\) conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\) with \(\kappa=2\alpha\) yields
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one coordinate is decoded incorrectly}\}
 \geq\frac12\left[1-\exp\{-M_n^{1-2\alpha}/2\}\right]
 =\frac12-o(1),
\]
because \(M_n\to\infty\) and \(2\alpha<1\).  If the uniform boundary loss is less than \(\Delta/2\), every midpoint decoder is correct.  Averaging over the uniform bit prior and then bounding that average by its largest summand gives
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 \left|\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)\right|
 \right]
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{4}
 \left[1-\exp\{-M_n^{1-2\alpha}/2\}\right].
\]
Since \(\Delta=c_1a_n\), the right-hand side is at least \(c a_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), for a constant \(c>0\) depending only on \(q\) and \(L\).

It remains to return to the fixed-\(n\) experiment.  The packing construction has \(1/4\leq\mu_{P_\omega}\leq3/4\), so the loss of the zero default on \(\{N<n\}\) is at most one.  On \(\{N\geq n\}\), the selected observations have law \(P_\omega^{\otimes n}\) and are processed pointwise by the original sections \(T_{n,x}\).  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 \left|\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)\right|
 \right]\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 \left|\widehat\mu_{n,P_\omega}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)\right|
 \right]
 +\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-tN}\), with \(t=\log2\), gives
\[
 \Pr\{N<n\}
 \leq e^{tn}\mathbb E[e^{-tN}]
 =\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}
 =o(a_n).
\]
Combining the preceding displays and decreasing \(c\) if necessary proves, uniformly over the arbitrary point-indexed rule,
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 \left|\widehat\mu_{n,P_\omega}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)\right|
 \right]
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

The packing lemma verifies independently and explicitly the independent-sampling, compact-support, density-positivity, rectifiable-boundary, H\"older-regression, and continuous-variance requirements for every \(P_\omega\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\).  Thus the supremum over the full class is no smaller than the supremum over this finite family.  Taking the infimum over \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\) gives
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Finally, \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rates}\) gives \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), and \(\mathrm{def:point\text{-}indexed\text{-}distance\text{-}risk}\) identifies the displayed minimax functional with \(R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\).  Dividing by \(a_n\) and taking lower limits proves the theorem with \(c_{\mathrm{PI}}=c\).  The argument began with arbitrary point-indexed sections and used only their law independence and joint measurability; it did not impose a common estimator map.  The strict enlargement relative to the inherited common-map class is established by \(\mathrm{lem:common\text{-}map\text{-}strict\text{-}in\text{-}point\text{-}indexed}\).
