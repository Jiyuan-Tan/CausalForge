All constants below may depend on the fixed class parameters, \(q\), and \(\delta\), but not on \(n\) or on \(P\).

\emph{Nonemptiness.}  Let \(X\) be uniform on \([-1,1]^2\), use the prescribed two half-planes and chart, take both regressions constant with values in \((1/4,3/4)\), and let each potential outcome be a Bernoulli variable with the corresponding conditional mean plus an independent standard Gaussian variable.  The density \(1/4\) obeys \(L^{-1}\leq 1/4\leq L\).  Each observed arm contains at least a quarter disk at every interface point, so its mass in a radius-\(r\) boundary ball is at least \(\pi r^2/16\), which is no smaller than \(c_-r^2\).  The regressions belong to every displayed H\"older ball after choosing their constant levels inside the envelope.  Finally, Hoeffding's inequality for the centered Bernoulli variable and the Gaussian moment-generating identity give
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\exp\{\lambda(Y(t)-\mu_{t,P}(X))\}\mid X\right]
 \leq \exp(\lambda^2/8)\exp(\lambda^2/2)
 \leq \exp(L^2\lambda^2/2).
\]
Thus this law is in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\).

\emph{Full-location upper bound.}  Write \(r_q\), \(K_2\), \(H_t\), and \(G^{\mathrm{hb}}_{t,q,L}\) as in \(\mathrm{def:stabilized\text{-}location\text{-}local\text{-}polynomial}\).  If \(v\neq0\), the polynomial \(u\mapsto v^\top r_q(u)\) cannot vanish on the open half-ball \(H_t\cap B_2(0,1/2)\).  Therefore
\[
 v^\top G^{\mathrm{hb}}_{t,q,L}v
 =L^{-1}\int_{H_t\cap B_2(0,1/2)}K_2(u)
 \{v^\top r_q(u)\}^2\,du>0.
\]
Compactness of the unit sphere in this fixed-dimensional coefficient space gives \(\lambda^{\mathrm{hb}}_{t,q,L}>0\).  The cutoff in the named estimator is consequently well defined.  Its finite sums are Borel, the minimum eigenvalue is continuous on symmetric matrices, and inversion is continuous on the positive-definite cone; hence the cutoff and zero branch make \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}\) an element of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\).

For \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\) and all sufficiently small \(h\), the rescaled half-ball \(x+h\{H_t\cap B_2(0,1/2)\}\) lies in the appropriate half-square.  The density floor therefore implies the Loewner inequality
\[
 G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,P,x}(h)
 :=\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)
 \succeq G^{\mathrm{hb}}_{t,q,L}.
\]
Put
\[
 b_n(h)=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+\frac{\log n}{nh^2}.
\]
The bounded kernel, bounded monomial vector, density upper bound, and conditional sub-Gaussian inequality imply, entry by entry and for \(0<u\leq1\),
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left(
 \left|\widehat G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)-G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,P,x}(h)\right|_{ab}>u
 \right)
 \leq2\exp(-cnh^2u^2),
\]
and
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left(
 \left|\widehat r^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)-r^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,P,x}(h)\right|_a>u
 \right)
 \leq2\exp\{-cnh^2\min(u^2,u)\}.
\]
These displays follow by applying the exponential Markov inequality to the centered summands; their conditional variances are at most \(Ch^2\), and the conditional sub-Gaussian premise bounds every higher exponential moment.  Cover the trimmed line segment by a grid of polynomial cardinality in \(n\), apply the union bound to the finitely many matrix and vector entries, and extend from the grid using the Lipschitz kernel and polynomial basis.  On the event \(n^{-1}\sum_i(1+|Y_i|)\leq C\), the extension error is made smaller than \(b_n(h)\) by a sufficiently fine polynomial grid; the complement has polynomially small probability by the same exponential-moment bound.  Thus, uniformly over the class,
\[
 \sup_{t,x}\left\{
 \left\|\widehat G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)-G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,P,x}(h)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\left\|\widehat r^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,x}(h)-r^{\mathrm{loc}}_{t,P,x}(h)\right\|_2
 \right\}=O_P\{b_n(h)\},
\]
with the same order after taking the expected supremum.

Taylor's formula through total degree \(q-1\), using \(\mu_{t,P}\in\mathcal H^q(\mathcal X_P,L)\), writes
\[
 \mu_{t,P}(x+hu)=r_q(u)^\top\beta_{t,P,x}(h)+R_{t,P,x}(u,h),
 \qquad |R_{t,P,x}(u,h)|\leq Ch^q
\]
on the kernel support.  Polynomial reproduction and the population Gram lower bound show that the population fitted intercept differs from \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\) by at most \(Ch^q\).  The Rayleigh-quotient perturbation inequality activates the cutoff whenever the preceding Gram deviation is below \(\lambda^{\mathrm{hb}}_{t,q,L}/2\) and bounds the inverse there.  On that event, the resolvent identity
\[
 \widehat G^{-1}-G^{-1}
 =\widehat G^{-1}(G-\widehat G)G^{-1}
\]
and the last two displays give stochastic error \(Cb_n(h)\).  On the active branch the cutoff gives \(|\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{LP}}_{t,h}(x)|\leq Ch^{-2}\mathbb P_n|Y|\), while the failed branch is zero.  Cauchy--Schwarz, the uniform second-moment bound implied by conditional sub-Gaussianity, and the polynomially small exceptional probability make the exceptional contribution negligible.  Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right]
 \leq C\{h^q+b_n(h)\}.
\]
For \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\), one has \(b_n(h)\leq Ch^q\), proving the upper bound \(Cs_{n,q}\) with the named data-driven estimator.

\emph{Full-location converse.}  Work inside the uniform-design submodel just constructed.  Choose \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) trimmed centers with disjoint radius-\(h\) disks.  Keep the control regression and a constant treated baseline fixed.  In alternative \(j\), add
\[
 \Delta\phi((z-x_j)/h),
 \qquad \Delta=\eta h^q,
\]
to the treated regression, where \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\), \(0\leq\phi\leq1\), and \(\phi(0)=1\).  For every \(k\leq q\),
\[
 \left\|D^k\{\Delta\phi((\cdot-x_j)/h)\}\right\|_\infty
 \leq C\eta h^{q-k}\leq C\eta.
\]
Thus sufficiently small fixed \(\eta\) places every alternative in the same H\"older ball and keeps its Bernoulli probability away from zero and one.  The common Gaussian-noised Bernoulli channel and the bound
\[
 \operatorname{kl}(p,p')\leq\frac{(p-p')^2}{p'(1-p')}
\]
give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn\Delta^2h^2=C\eta^2nh^{2q+2}.
\]
Take \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\), with its constant and then \(\eta\) small enough that the last display is at most \(\alpha\log M\) for some \(\alpha<1/4\).  If \(J\) is uniform on the \(M\) alternatives, the log-sum inequality yields
\[
 I(J;O_{1:n})\leq M^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^M
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
For every decoder, the entropy identity and the binary-error decomposition give
\[
 \Pr(\widehat J\neq J)
 \geq1-\frac{I(J;O_{1:n})+\log2}{\log M}
 \geq c>0
\]
for all large \(n\).  Uniform estimation error smaller than \(\Delta/3\) identifies the unique perturbed center, so Markov's inequality in reverse through this testing reduction gives
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq c\Delta=cs_{n,q}.
\]

\emph{Distance experiment when \(L>4\).}  The canonical exact-distance-regression lemma gives the armwise bias bound \(|g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq Ls\).  The fixed local-constant stability lemma gives a population denominator bounded below, and the upper-mass lemma bounds its variance scale by \(Ch^2\).  Repeating the preceding exponential-Markov, boundary-net, and ratio-stability calculation for the fixed triangular-kernel numerator and denominator yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right]
 \leq C\{h+b_n(h)\}.
\]
At \(h\asymp a_n\), \(b_n(h)\leq Ch\), so the distance upper bound is \(Ca_n\).  For \(L>4\), the canonical-square family in \(\mathrm{lem:angular\text{-}density\text{-}packing}\) lies inside \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\); its densities are continuous, although continuity is not required of every member of that class.  Applying \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\) to its compressed coordinate experiments shows that uniform error below a fixed multiple of \(a_n\) would decode all packed coordinates with probability tending to one, whereas the lemma bounds that success probability by \(1/2+o(1)\).  Hence
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq ca_n.
\]

\emph{Distance experiment when \(L=4\).}  The fixed square has Lebesgue area four, the score law has total mass one, and the density floor is \(1/4\).  Therefore
\[
 0=1-\int_{[-1,1]^2}\frac14\,dz
 =\int_{[-1,1]^2}\left\{f_P(z)-\frac14\right\}\,dz.
\]
The integrand is nonnegative Lebesgue-almost everywhere, so
\[
 f_P(z)=\frac14
 \quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }z\in[-1,1]^2.
\]
No continuity is used.  This almost-everywhere equality suffices for every expectation and risk because densities differing on a null set induce the same score law.  It also suffices for the fixed radial Radon--Nikodym versions: polar Fubini implies that, for each trimmed \(x\), the circle integral sees the null exceptional set at only a Lebesgue-null set of radii.  Consequently, for Lebesgue-almost every sufficiently small \(s\),
\[
 Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\frac{\pi}{4}s.
\]
Thus all expectations, Radon--Nikodym identities, circle identities, and risk bounds used below are unchanged by replacing \(f_P\) with its constant representative \(1/4\).

Taylor expansion of \(\mu_{t,P}\) through total degree \(q-1\), followed by integration over the appropriate half-circle and division by \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\), supplies uniformly bounded coefficients \(b_{t,k,P}(x)\) such that
\[
 \left|g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 -\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k\right|
 \leq Cs^q
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every sufficiently small \(s\).  This uses only the H\"older remainder \(Cs^q\); the half-circle moments of each Taylor monomial produce the displayed polynomial coefficients.

Instantiate \(\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}\) with \(m=q\) and cutoff constant \(c=\pi/4\).  Its population radial Gram matrix is
\[
 \frac1{h^2}\int_0^h K_\triangle(s/h)v_q(s/h)v_q(s/h)^\top
 Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds
 =\frac\pi4 G_q,
\]
where equality holds because changing an integrand on a null set does not change the integral.  The matrix \(G_q\) is positive definite: for \(v\neq0\), the nonzero polynomial \(v^\top v_q(u)\) has only finitely many zeros, while \(uK_\triangle(u)>0\) on \((0,1)\).  The established admissibility lemma places this estimator in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).  The same entrywise exponential inequalities, now for the radial polynomial basis, the same boundary net, polynomial reproduction, and the preceding remainder yield
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(q),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right]
 \leq C\{h^q+b_n(h)\}.
\]
At \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\), this is at most \(Cs_{n,q}\).

Every coordinatewise distance rule can be implemented from the full sample and known geometry by computing \(D_{P,x,i}\) from \((X_i,T_i)\).  Hence \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\) is a restriction of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\), and, for the common loss and law class,
\[
 R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
 \geq cs_{n,q}.
\]
This proves the \(L=4\) distance equivalence.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{q}{2q+2}>\frac14
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q>1.
\]
Thus the strict-slack distance exponent is slower exactly when \(q>1\), the exponents agree at \(q=1\), and the no-slack case agrees for every \(q\), as claimed.
