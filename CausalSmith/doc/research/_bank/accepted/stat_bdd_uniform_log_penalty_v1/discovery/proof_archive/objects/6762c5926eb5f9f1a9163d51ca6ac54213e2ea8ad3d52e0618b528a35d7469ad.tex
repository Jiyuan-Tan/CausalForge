Put \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\) and place all centers on a fixed middle subinterval of its lower edge.  For a center \(x_j\) and every sufficiently small radius, the portion of the circle in \(S\) is
\[
 x_j+r e_\theta,
 \qquad e_\theta=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),
 \qquad 0\leq\theta\leq\pi.
\]
The cancellation uses
\[
 \int_0^\pi\cos\theta\,d\theta=0,
 \qquad
 \int_0^\pi\cos^2\theta\,d\theta=\frac\pi2.
\]

Choose \(\psi\in C_c^\infty((-1,1))\) with \(0\leq\psi\leq1\) and \(\psi(0)=1\), and set
\[
 \phi(z)=\psi(\lVert z\rVert_2^2),
 \qquad
 \varphi(u)=\psi(u^2).
\]
For constants \(d,d_1>0\), to be fixed below, define
\[
 \Delta=d a_n,
 \qquad
 w=(d_1\Delta)^{1/q}.
\]
Choose centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) separated by \(3w\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), the half-discs \(B_2(x_j,w)\cap S\) are disjoint and avoid the corners.  Elementary interval packing therefore gives
\[
 M\geq c w^{-1}\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}.
\]
Write \(r_j(z)=\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2\) and \(\phi_j(z)=\phi((z-x_j)/w)\).

Fix a small slope \(b>0\).  For \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\), let
\[
 p_\omega(z)=\frac12+bz_1+\Delta\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j\phi_j(z).
\]
For every integer \(0\leq k\leq q\), scaling gives
\[
 \lVert D^k(\Delta\phi_j)\rVert_\infty
 \leq C_\phi\Delta w^{-k}.
\]
At \(k=q\), the right-hand side is \(C_\phi/d_1\); for \(k<q\), it converges to zero.  Disjoint supports preserve the same bounds for the sum.  Choose \(b\) small, then \(d_1\) large, and finally \(d\) small.  The affine part and the scaled bumps then place every \(p_\omega\) in \(\mathcal H^q(S,L)\), and
\[
 \frac14\leq p_\omega(z)\leq\frac34.
\]

Let \(\chi\in C^\infty(\mathbb R)\) satisfy \(0\leq\chi\leq1\), \(\chi(v)=0\) for \(v\leq1\), and \(\chi(v)=1\) for \(v\geq2\).  Fix \(c_A\geq8\) and define
\[
 A_n(r)=-\frac{2\Delta\varphi(r/w)}{br}
 \chi\!\left(\frac{br}{c_A\Delta}\right),
 \qquad A_n(0)=0.
\]
The cutoff makes \(A_n\) identically zero near zero, so there is no singularity.  On the active region,
\[
 |A_n(r)|\leq\frac2{c_A}\leq\frac14.
\]
The compact support of \(\varphi(r/w)\) makes this function vanish smoothly before \(r=w\).  Define on \(S\)
\[
 f_\omega(z)=1+sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j A_n(r_j(z))
 \frac{z_1-(x_j)_1}{r_j(z)}\mathbf1\{r_j(z)<w\},
\]
with each summand set to zero at its center.  Disjointness makes the definition unambiguous.  The cutoff at zero and smooth disappearance at the support edge show that \(f_\omega\) is continuous on \(S\), and
\[
 \frac34\leq f_\omega(z)\leq\frac54.
\]
Because \(L\geq4\), this interval is contained in \([L^{-1},L]\).  Polar integration in every affected half-disc yields
\[
 \int_0^w A_n(r)r\,dr\int_0^\pi\cos\theta\,d\theta=0.
\]
Hence \(\int_Sf_\omega=1\).  Positivity makes \(S\) the support, and \(\operatorname{bd}(S)\) is a rectifiable curve.

Conditional on \(X=z\), take
\[
 Y=B+\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 B\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_\omega(z)),
 \qquad
 \varepsilon\sim N(0,1),
\]
with conditional independence between \(B\) and \(\varepsilon\).  Then
\[
 \mu_{P_\omega}(z)=p_\omega(z),
 \qquad
 \sigma_{P_\omega}^2(z)=1+p_\omega(z)\{1-p_\omega(z)\}.
\]
The variance is continuous and lies in \([1,5/4]\subseteq[L^{-1},L]\).  Draw \((Y_i,X_i)_{i=1}^n\) independently from this common law.  Thus, for every vertex and every sufficiently large \(n\), condition \(\mathrm{(i)}\) in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\) holds by construction; condition \(\mathrm{(ii)}\) holds because \(S\subset[-L,L]^2\) is compact with rectifiable boundary and \(f_\omega\) is continuous, normalized, supported on \(S\), and lies in the required density envelope; condition \(\mathrm{(iii)}\) is the preceding scaled H\"older calculation; and condition \(\mathrm{(iv)}\) follows from the displayed continuous variance formula and envelope.  This verifies membership in the same class appearing in the theorem, without an additional support, overlap, or tail premise.

Now compare adjacent vertices, oriented so that \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j^{(j)}=0\).  All other perturbations vanish in the \(j\)-th half-disc.  At every \(0<r<w\), the angular density correction has zero integral, so the two radial densities agree and equal \(\pi r\).  For \(r\geq w\), the adjacent score densities are pointwise identical.  Hence the full laws of \(\lVert X-x_j\rVert_2\) agree.  On a half-circle within the \(j\)-th cell, the difference of the outcome-weighted angular integrals is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\int_0^\pi p_\omega(x_j+re_\theta)
 \{1+A_n(r)\cos\theta\}\,d\theta
 -\int_0^\pi p_{\omega^{(j)}}(x_j+re_\theta)\,d\theta\\
 &=\pi\Delta\varphi(r/w)+\frac\pi2brA_n(r).
\end{aligned}
\]
The constant part multiplied by \(A_n(r)\cos\theta\), and the radial bump multiplied by the same factor, integrate to zero.  If \(br\geq2c_A\Delta\), then \(\chi=1\) and the last display vanishes.  For \(r\geq w\), both perturbations vanish.  Conditional on a radius, an angular mixture of Bernoulli variables is Bernoulli with its averaged success probability; convolution with the common Gaussian preserves equality.  The conditional radial outcome laws therefore agree outside radii at most \(2c_A\Delta/b\), which are of order \(a_n\).

On that exceptional interval the common radial density is at most \(Cr\), the two averaged Bernoulli probabilities stay in \([1/4,3/4]\), and their difference is at most \(C\Delta\).  Thus
\[
 \operatorname{kl}(p,p')
 \leq\frac{(p-p')^2}{p'(1-p')}
 \leq C\Delta^2.
\]
Data processing through addition of the common Gaussian and integration with respect to the common radial marginal yield, for one compressed observation,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P^{\mathrm{rad}}_{\omega,j},P^{\mathrm{rad}}_{\omega^{(j)},j})
 \leq C\Delta^2\int_0^{2c_A\Delta/b}r\,dr
 \leq C'\Delta^4.
\]
Independent-product tensorization gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
 \mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
 \right)
 \leq C'n\Delta^4=C'd^4\log n.
\]
Meanwhile
\[
 \log M=\left(\frac1{4q}+o(1)\right)\log n.
\]
Choose \(d\) small enough that the divergence is at most \(\alpha\log M\) for a fixed \(\alpha<1/8\), retaining the strict inequality \(2\alpha<1\) needed after Poissonization.  Finally, \(\phi_j(x_j)=1\), while all other bumps vanish there, so adjacent boundary regression values differ by
\[
 |\mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)-\mu_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)|=\Delta=d a_n.
\]
Taking \(c_1=d\) completes the construction and proves every asserted property.
