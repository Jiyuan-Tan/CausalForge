Let \(h=h_n\asymp a_n\).  We first prove the upper bound with the single estimator named in the statement.  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), put
\[
 W_{i,t,x}=\mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}K_\triangle(D_{P,x,i}/h),
 \qquad A_{t,x}=\sum_{i=1}^n W_{i,t,x}.
\]
Because \(K_\triangle\) is symmetric, \(W_{i,t,x}\) is measurable from \((Y_i,D_{P,x,i})\); the event \(D_{P,x,i}=0\) has probability zero under the density envelope.  Hence \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).

On arm \(t\), define \(\varepsilon_{i,t}=Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\).  On \(A_{t,x}>0\), algebra gives
\[
 \widehat\mu_{t,h}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)
 =\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}}{A_{t,x}}
 +\frac{\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\{\mu_{t,P}(X_i)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\}}{A_{t,x}}.
\]
Every nonzero weight has \(\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2\leq h\).  Since \(q\geq1\), the H\"older-regression assumption bounds the second term by \(Lh\).  Moreover, the stability and upper-mass lemmas imply, uniformly in \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\), \(t\), and \(x\),
\[
 \kappa_0nh^2\leq \mathbb E_PA_{t,x}\leq \pi Lnh^2.
\]
These inequalities state the positivity and scale conditions used in every subsequent division.

For a completely uniform argument, take a maximal \(n^{-3}\)-net of \(\mathcal B_P\).  Compactness and \(\mathcal B_P\subseteq[-L,L]^2\) give at most \(C_Ln^6\) net points.  Bernstein's inequality for the independent weights \(0\leq W_{i,t,x}\leq1\), followed by a union bound, yields an event \(E_n\), with \(\sup_P P(E_n^c)\leq n^{-4}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), on which
\[
 \inf_{t,x}A_{t,x}\geq\frac{\kappa_0}{2}nh^2,
 \qquad
 \sup_{t,x}A_{t,x}\leq Cnh^2.
\]
Here and below the extension from the net is valid because \(K_\triangle\) is one-Lipschitz: moving the center by \(n^{-3}\) changes each weight by at most \(n^{-3}/h\).  Since \(nh^2/\log n\to\infty\) at the stated bandwidth, the Bernstein exponent dominates the logarithm of the net cardinality.

Conditionally on \(X_{1:n}\), deterministic assignment and the conditional sub-Gaussian assumption give
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left(\left.\left|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|>u\,\right|X_{1:n}\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2L^2\sum_iW_{i,t,x}^2}\right\}
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2L^2A_{t,x}}\right\}.
\]
A union bound over the net and integration of this tail, using the displayed denominator bounds on \(E_n\), prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{t,x}
 \frac{\left|\sum_iW_{i,t,x}\varepsilon_{i,t}\right|}{A_{t,x}}
 \mathbf 1_{E_n}
 \leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}+o(a_n).
\]
For the net extension of the centered numerator, the same Lipschitz inequality bounds its expected oscillation by \(n^{-3}h^{-1}\mathbb E_P\sum_i|\varepsilon_{i,t}|=O(n^{-2}/h)\), which is negligible after division by \(nh^2\).  The H\"older ball bounds \(\mu_{t,P}\), and conditional sub-Gaussianity therefore gives \(\sup_P\mathbb E_P\max_{i\leq n}|Y_i|^2=O(\log n)\).  On \(E_n^c\), every nonempty-denominator local average is bounded by \(\max_i|Y_i|\); Cauchy--Schwarz and \(P(E_n^c)\leq n^{-4}\) make this contribution \(o(a_n)\).  A zero denominator is contained in \(E_n^c\) and is covered by the estimator's stated boundary convention.  Summing the two arms yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_P}
 |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Balancing the two displayed terms gives \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/4}=a_n\), proving the upper limsup with the explicit, law-independent estimator.

For the converse, use the unit-square hypercube from \(\mathrm{lem:angular\text{-}density\text{-}packing}\), with \(M=M_n\) centers and adjacent target separation \(\Delta=c_1a_n\).  Poissonize the experiment at mean \(2n\).  Because the bump disks are disjoint, their marked observations form conditionally independent Poisson processes \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\); the outside process \(Z_0\) is bit-independent.  All these marked point-process spaces are standard Borel.  For decoder \(j\), the restriction of its signed-distance point process to its own disk is a measurable compression \(S_j\) of \(Z_j\); revealing all \(Z_{-j}\) and \(Z_0\) can only make decoding easier.  Data processing and the slack \(\alpha<1/8\) in the packing construction give, after doubling the Poisson intensity,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})\leq\kappa\log M
\]
for one fixed \(\kappa<1\).  The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma consequently yields
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat\omega_j\neq\omega_j\text{ for at least one }j\}\geq\frac12-o(1)
\]
under the uniform product prior, even with the displayed cross-center side information.

At \(x_j\), the two possible target values differ by \(\Delta\).  Thresholding \(\widehat\tau_n(x_j)\) at their midpoint recovers every bit whenever the sup-norm error is less than \(\Delta/2\).  Hence the Poissonized Bayes expected sup-norm loss is at least \((\Delta/2)(1/2-o(1))\).  If the Poisson count \(N\) is at least \(n\), uniformly select \(n\) observations and apply any fixed-\(n\) decision rule.  Conditional on \(N\geq n\), the selected observations have law \(P^{\otimes n}\), while \(\Pr\{N<n\}\leq e^{-cn}\).  The bounded target envelope makes the de-Poissonization remainder \(o(\Delta)\).  Thus the fixed-sample Bayes risk, and hence its worst-law risk, is bounded below by \(ca_n\).  Therefore
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}})}{a_n}>0.
\]
Combining the two inequalities proves the theorem.  The causal target remains \(\tau_P\); the canonical-regression lemma supplies its observed radial identifying limit and is not used to redefine the causal estimand.
