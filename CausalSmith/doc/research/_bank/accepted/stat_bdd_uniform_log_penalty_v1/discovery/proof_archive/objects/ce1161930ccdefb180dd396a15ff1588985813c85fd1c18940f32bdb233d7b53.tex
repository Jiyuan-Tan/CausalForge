Fix \(m\), \(\mathcal C\), and the constants in the statement.  The proof has four parts.

\emph{Population rank and causal centering.}
For \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), write \(S_{P,x}=|D_{P,x}|\).  Coarea and the definition of \(Q_{t,P,x}\) yield, for \(0<h\leq r_*\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)
 =\int_0^1 K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top
 \frac{Q_{t,P,x}(hu)}h\,du.
\]
Consequently, for every \(a\in\mathbb R^m\),
\[
 \underline c\,a^\top G_ma
 \leq a^\top\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)a
 \leq \overline c\,a^\top G_ma.
\]
If \(a\neq0\), the polynomial \(a^\top v_m(u)\) cannot vanish on an interval, while \(uK_\triangle(u)>0\) for \(0<u<1\).  Hence \(G_m\) is positive definite and
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\{\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\}
 \geq\lambda_m(\underline c)>0.
\]
This verifies every rank and inverse condition used below.  The bandwidth restriction makes every invocation of the radial bounds valid, and the lower inequality makes every displayed ratio well defined for \(0<s\leq r_*\).

By \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\text{-}exact\text{-}distance\text{-}regression}\), the conditional mean of \(Y\) at signed radius \((2t-1)s\) is the canonical coarea ratio for almost every positive radius, and its limit at zero is \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\).  Thus the target remains the causal contrast \(\tau_P(x)=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)\); the ratio is an identifying regression, not a redefinition of the target.  Write
\[
 g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)
 =\mu_{t,P}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k
 +R_{t,P,x}(s),
 \qquad |R_{t,P,x}(s)|\leq As^m.
\]
With
\[
 \beta_{t,P,x}(h)
 =\bigl(\mu_{t,P}(x),hb_{t,1,P}(x),\ldots,
 h^{m-1}b_{t,m-1,P}(x)\bigr)^\top,
\]
the empirical outcome vector decomposes exactly as
\[
 \widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)
 =\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\beta_{t,P,x}(h)
 +B_{t,x}(h)+Z_{t,x}(h),
\]
where \(B_{t,x}(h)\) is obtained by replacing \(Y_i\) with \(R_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x,i})\), and \(Z_{t,x}(h)\) by replacing it with
\(Y_i-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x,i})\).  This is the polynomial-reproduction identity; it does not assume a population inverse.

The centered radial error has a uniform conditional sub-Gaussian envelope.  Indeed,
\[
 Y-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x})
 =\{Y-\mu_{t,P}(X)\}
 +\{\mu_{t,P}(X)-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x})\}.
\]
The first summand satisfies the conditional exponential inequality in \(\mathrm{ass:conditional\text{-}subgaussian}\).  Conditional on the signed radius in arm \(t\), the second is centered by the definition of the radial conditional mean and is bounded by \(2L\), because the H\"older ball includes \(\|\mu_{t,P}\|_\infty\leq L\).  For a centered variable \(V\in[-2L,2L]\), convexity of the exponential gives
\[
 \mathbb E(e^{\lambda V})\leq e^{2L^2\lambda^2}.
\]
Conditioning first on \(X\) and then on the signed radius therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 e^{\lambda\{Y-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x})\}}
 \mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)S_{P,x}
 \right]
 \leq e^{C_L\lambda^2/2}.
\]

\emph{Uniform good-event bound.}
Let \(\varepsilon_n=n^{-3}\).  Since every trimmed boundary is contained in \([-L,L]^2\), it has an \(\varepsilon_n\)-net of cardinality at most \(C_L\varepsilon_n^{-2}\).  For a fixed net point, each Gram summand before division by \(n\) has norm at most \(C_mh^{-2}\), and the upper radial bound implies its second moment is at most \(C_{m,\overline c}h^{-2}\).  Exponential Markov's inequality applied to the bounded-summand log-moment bound
\[
 \log\mathbb E e^{\lambda(W-\mathbb EW)}
 \leq\frac{\lambda^2\mathbb E(W-\mathbb EW)^2}
 {2(1-|\lambda|\|W-\mathbb EW\|_\infty/3)}
\]
gives entrywise concentration.  The preceding conditional sub-Gaussian inequality gives the analogous bound for \(Z_{t,x}(h)\).  A union bound over the fixed number of matrix coordinates, two arms, and the polynomial-size net shows that, for every prescribed \(B>0\), constants in the deviations can be chosen so that an event \(E_n(P)\) satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}P\{E_n(P)^c\}\leq n^{-B}
\]
and, on \(E_n(P)\), uniformly in \(t\) and all net points,
\[
 \left\|\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)
 -\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\frac14\lambda_m(\underline c),
 \qquad
 \left|e_1^\top\{\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\}^{-1}Z_{t,x}(h)\right|
 \leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}},
\]
and \(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|Y_i|\leq C\sqrt{\log n}\).  Here \(nh^2/\log n\to\infty\), which will hold at the selected bandwidth.

On the nonnegative domain, the maps \(u\mapsto K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)\) and \(u\mapsto K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top\), defined by their displayed formulas for \(0\leq u\leq1\) and extended by zero only for \(u>1\), are Lipschitz on \([0,\infty)\).  No extension across \(u=0\) is made or used; this is exactly the relevant domain because \(u=S_{P,x}/h\geq0\).  Also \(|\|X_i-x\|_2-\|X_i-x'\|_2|\leq\|x-x'\|_2\).  Almost surely no sample score lies on the one-dimensional smooth boundary, so the strict arm indicator is unchanged as \(x\) varies along that boundary.  It follows that the Gram oscillation over a mesh cell is at most \(C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\), and the outcome-vector oscillation is at most
\[
 C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|\right).
\]
At \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2m+2)}\), both terms are smaller than every displayed stochastic order.  Hence the net inequalities extend to all \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), and Weyl's inequality makes the cutoff active everywhere on \(E_n(P)\).

On this event the inverse norm is at most \(2/\lambda_m(\underline c)\).  Since \(|R_{t,P,x}(S)|\leq Ah^m\) on the kernel support, the decomposition and the bounded empirical kernel moment give
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 \left|\widehat\mu^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{t,h,\underline c}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
The mesh extension for the target itself uses
\(|\mu_{t,P}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x')|\leq L\|x-x'\|_2\), which follows from \(q\geq1\).  Taking the difference of the two arms proves the same bound for the causal curve on \(E_n(P)\).

\emph{The concentration complement.}
Failure of \(E_n(P)\) does not imply failure of the eigenvalue cutoff, so both branches must be controlled.  If a cutoff fails, the corresponding intercept is zero and \(|\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\).  If it is active, \(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\text{-}distance\text{-}local\text{-}polynomial\text{-}admissible}\) gives, simultaneously for every \(x\),
\[
 \left|\widehat\mu^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{t,h,\underline c}(x)\right|
 \leq C h^{-2}\overline Y_n,
 \qquad \overline Y_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|.
\]
The H\"older envelope and the conditional sub-Gaussian inequality imply \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_PY_i^2\leq C\), and Jensen's inequality therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\overline Y_n^2
 \leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb E_PY_i^2
 \leq C.
\]
Thus Cauchy--Schwarz yields the explicit exceptional contribution
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \mathbf1_{E_n(P)^c}
 \right]
 \leq C\{n^{-B}+h^{-2}n^{-B/2}\}.
\]
Taking, for example, \(B=10\) makes this term negligible at the bandwidth below.  Combining the good and exceptional events gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right]
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}
\]
for \(h\leq r_*\) and all sufficiently large \(n\).  Balancing the two displayed terms gives
\[
 h_n\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(2m+2)},
 \qquad
 h_n^m\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]
The admissibility lemma makes this one explicit data-driven witness in \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\), so the definition of minimax risk proves the asserted upper bound.

\emph{The valid contained-bump converse.}
Now suppose \(m=q\) and that \(\mathcal C\) contains the stated submodel.  We spell out the witness to verify why the restriction \(m=q\) is necessary.  On the unit square with uniform score density and the horizontal straight interface, choose \(M\asymp h_n^{-1}\) trimmed centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) separated by \(3h_n\).  Let \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\) satisfy \(0\leq\phi\leq1\) and \(\phi(0)=1\), and put
\[
 \Delta_n=\eta h_n^q,
 \qquad
 \mu_{0,j}(z)=0,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,j}(z)=\Delta_n\phi((z-x_j)/h_n).
\]
Let \(Y(t)=\mu_{t,j}(X)+\varepsilon_t\), where the \(\varepsilon_t\) are independent standard Gaussian variables, and impose deterministic assignment and consistency.  For every derivative order \(0\leq k\leq q\),
\[
 \|D^k\mu_{1,j}\|_\infty
 \leq C_\phi\eta h_n^{q-k}\leq C_\phi\eta.
\]
After choosing the fixed amplitude \(\eta>0\) small enough, all laws satisfy the common \(q\)-H\"older, variance, sub-Gaussian, support, reach, and two-sided-thickness envelopes.  This derivative calculation is valid because the height is \(h_n^q\).  If instead one used height \(h_n^m\) with \(m<q\), its \(q\)-th derivative would be of order \(h_n^{m-q}\), which diverges; that invalid construction is not used.

Let \(P_0\) denote the same law with both regressions zero.  Conditional normal relative entropy and the fact that a bump occupies score area \(O(h_n^2)\) give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn\Delta_n^2h_n^2
 =C\eta^2nh_n^{2q+2}
 \leq C'\eta^2\log n.
\]
Because \(\log M=(1/(2q+2)+o(1))\log n\), \(\eta\) can be fixed so that the last display is at most \(\alpha\log M\) for some \(\alpha<1/4\).  If \(J\) is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,M\}\), the log-sum inequality gives
\[
 I(J;O_{1:n})
 \leq\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
For any decoder with error indicator \(E\), conditioning entropy on \(E\) yields
\[
 H(J\mid O_{1:n})\leq\log2+P(E=1)\log M,
\]
and hence \(P(E=1)\geq1-\alpha-o(1)\).  If a boundary-curve estimator had sup-norm error below \(\Delta_n/3\), maximizing its values at the centers would recover \(J\), because the true curve equals \(\Delta_n\) at \(x_J\) and zero at all other centers.  Therefore even the full-location experiment has risk at least \(c\Delta_n\), and the smaller coordinatewise distance decision class does as well.  Since \(\Delta_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{q/(2q+2)}\), this proves the matching lower bound only in the valid case \(m=q\).

For the angular-imbalance clause, use \(\mathrm{lem:angular\text{-}density\text{-}packing}\).  In the mean-\(2n\) Poisson experiment its disjoint local point processes are conditionally independent standard-Borel channels and the outside process is bit-independent.  The packing gives adjacent separation \(c_1a_n\) and relative entropy at most \(2\alpha\log M\), where \(2\alpha<1\).  The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma then gives probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\) of at least one bit error even after all cross-center ancillary observations are revealed.  Midpoint thresholding converts sup-norm error below \(c_1a_n/2\) into exact recovery.  Subsampling \(n\) observations whenever the Poisson count of mean \(2n\) exceeds \(n\), whose failure probability is exponentially small by exponential Markov's inequality, returns the fixed-sample lower bound \(ca_n\).

\emph{Why density alone does not determine the order.}
If an arm regression is locally constant on all relevant circles, then \(N_{t,P,x}(s)=\mu_{t,P}(x)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\) exactly, regardless of any differentiability of \(f_P\); density smoothness is therefore not necessary for an arbitrarily high normalized-coarea remainder at that law.  Conversely, fix a straight interface and, near an interior boundary point, use the same bounded positive continuous density in polar coordinates
\[
 f(r,\theta)=c_0\{1+r^\alpha\cos\theta\},
 \qquad 0<\alpha<1,
\]
with a continuous compactly supported localization and a compensating normalization away from the point.  The angular perturbation integrates to zero on every complete circle and tends to zero at the center, so positivity, continuity, normalization, and the radial mass bounds are preserved.  With a constant regression, \(N/Q\) is constant.  With the affine regression \(\mu(z)=z_1\), on an upper half-circle centered at the point,
\[
 \frac{N(s)}{Q(s)}
 =\frac{s\int_0^\pi\cos\theta\{1+s^\alpha\cos\theta\}\,d\theta}
 {\int_0^\pi\{1+s^\alpha\cos\theta\}\,d\theta}
 =\frac12s^{1+\alpha},
\]
which has no order-two polynomial remainder.  Thus the same density can yield different normalized product remainders when paired with different smooth regressions.  The joint ratio \(N/Q\), not \(f_P\) in isolation, is exactly the object controlled in the upper proof.  This completes the proof.
