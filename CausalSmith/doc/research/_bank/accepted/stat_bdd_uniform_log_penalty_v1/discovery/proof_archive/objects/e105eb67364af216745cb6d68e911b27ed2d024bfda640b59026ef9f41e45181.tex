Fix \(q\geq 1\) and \(L\geq 4\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), take the explicit family supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}support\text{-}boundary\text{-}angular\text{-}packing}\).  Write \(M=M_n\), \(\Delta=c_1a_n\), and let \(C_j=B_2(x_j,w)\cap S\) be the disjoint half-disc cells used in that construction.  At \(x_j\), the value \(\mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)\) depends only on \(\omega_j\), and its two possible values are separated by \(\Delta\).

Let \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) be arbitrary.  We first turn it into a rule in an experiment having independent cell restrictions.  Under \(P_\omega\), generate a Poisson point process of mean \(2n\) on the observation space and attach independent marks with a continuous distribution.  If its point count \(N\) is at least \(n\), retain the \(n\) observations with the smallest marks, in increasing-mark order, and apply \(\widehat\mu_n\); if \(N<n\), return the zero boundary function.  Denote the resulting rule by \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\).  Conditional on every value \(N=k\geq n\), the retained observations are an independent sample from \(P_\omega\): the marks are independent of the observations, and their ordering induces a random permutation independent of the observation values.  This construction is independent of \(\omega\) and of \(P_\omega\).

Place independent uniform bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) on the packing vertices.  Let \(Z_j\) be the complete marked restriction of the Poisson process to \(C_j\), and let \(Z_0\) be its restriction to the complement of the union of the cells.  Conditional on the bits, the variables \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) are independent by the independent-restrictions property of a Poisson process.  The explicit packing has the further properties that the law of \(Z_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), whereas the law of \(Z_0\) is bit-independent.  Let \(S_j\) retain from \(Z_j\) only each point's mark, outcome, and distance from \(x_j\).  At \(x_j\), the coordinatewise rule \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\) is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\): the full restrictions outside \(C_j\) reveal their distances from \(x_j\), and \(S_j\) supplies exactly the information about points inside \(C_j\) permitted by \(V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\).

We next verify the divergence hypothesis for this marked cell experiment.  In the packing construction, adjacent laws are identical off \(C_j\), the mass of \(C_j\) is the same under the two adjacent laws, and every point at distance less than \(w\) from \(x_j\) belongs to \(C_j\).  Thus the one-observation radial law is a common component outside \(C_j\) plus the local compressed component, with disjoint radial supports up to null boundaries.  If \(D_j\) denotes the relative entropy between the two one-observation local compressed components, tensorization and the packing lemma give
\[
 nD_j
 =\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
 \mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
 \right)
 \leq \alpha\log M.
\]
Independent marks add zero relative entropy.  The relative-entropy formula for Poisson point processes with equal finite component masses therefore yields, for \(Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)\),
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})=2nD_j\leq 2\alpha\log M.
\]
The same bound holds in the reverse orientation.  Since \(2\alpha<1\), \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\), with \(\kappa=2\alpha\), is applicable.

At each \(x_j\), decode \(\Omega_j\) by thresholding \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\) at the midpoint of the two possible regression values.  The direct-product lemma and \(M_n\to\infty\) imply
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one coordinate is decoded incorrectly}\}
 \geq \frac12-o(1).
\]
If the uniform boundary loss were less than \(\Delta/2\), every midpoint decoder would be correct.  Averaging first over the uniform bit prior and then using that a maximum dominates an average gives
\[
 \sup_{\omega}
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq \frac{\Delta}{2}\left\{\frac12-o(1)\right\}
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), where \(c>0\) depends only on \(q\) and \(L\).

It remains to de-Poissonize.  Every packing regression takes values in \([1/4,3/4]\), so the loss of the zero default on \(\{N<n\}\) is at most one.  On \(\{N\geq n\}\), the selected data have law \(P_\omega^{\otimes n}\).  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 +\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The exponential Markov inequality gives, for example,
\[
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}
 \leq \exp\{-n(1-\log 2)\}=o(a_n).
\]
Combining the last three displays and decreasing \(c\) if necessary proves, uniformly over the arbitrary rule \(\widehat\mu_n\),
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq c a_n.
\]

The packing lemma verifies all boundary, support, density-positivity, H\"older, variance, and independent-sampling conditions required for \(P_\omega\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\).  Hence the supremum over the full class is no smaller than the supremum over this finite family.  Taking the infimum over the law-independent class \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) gives
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Finally, \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), while \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}distance\text{-}risk}\) identifies the expanded infimum--supremum display with \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).  Dividing by \(a_n\) and taking lower limits proves the claimed result with \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}=c\).  The entire finite family lies in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), so this is a same-class converse and uses no treatment-effect or two-sided-thickness subclass.
