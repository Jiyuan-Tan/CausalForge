Fix \(P\), \(x\), and \(t\), and put \(R_x=\lVert X-x\rVert_2\).  The observed-arm definition and deterministic assignment give \(\mathbf1\{T=t\}=\mathbf1\{X\in\mathcal E_{t,P}\}\) almost surely.  Score absolute continuity gives \(P(R_x=0)=0\).  By the defining RN identity, for every \(r>0\),
\[
 P\{T=t,\ 0<R_x\leq r\}
 =\nu_{t,P,x}((0,r])
 =\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
Polar coarea also shows that, for Lebesgue-almost every \(s>0\), the fixed version equals the displayed observed-arm circle integral.  There is no pointwise assertion at exceptional radii.

For every bounded Borel \(a:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\), the two observable RN definitions give
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds,
\]
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)N_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
Integrability follows from the conditional sub-Gaussian and bounded-regression member properties of the ambient class.  Since the sign of \(D_{P,x}=(2T-1)R_x\) recovers \(T\) almost surely, the RN characterization of conditional expectation gives, for Lebesgue-almost every \(s\in\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
 =\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
 =g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s).
\]
Nothing is defined or divided by at a zero-denominator radius.

Under the final premises of the proposed statement, the two armwise essential limits exist separately and equal their potential-outcome regression values.  Hence
\[
 \ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P}(x)-\ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P}(x)
 =\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
\]
For the announced application, \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\geq\underline cs\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(s\in(0,r_*]\) implies positive radial mass in every neighborhood and makes \(\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\) equal to that interval modulo null sets.  The normalized-coarea remainder then forces the separate essential limit to be \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\).  No common positive interval pointwise and no common sequence are required.
