Fix \(m\geq1\), the subclass \(\mathcal C\), and the constants in the statement.  Constants below may depend on \(m,q,\iota,L,\delta,A,r_*,\underline c,\overline c\), but not on \(n,P,t,x\).

For \(x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), put \(S_{P,x}=|D_{P,x}|\).  Coarea and the strict arm convention give, for \(0<h\leq r_*\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)
 =\int_0^1K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top
 \frac{Q_{t,P,x}(hu)}h\,du.
\]
Thus, for every \(a\in\mathbb R^m\),
\[
 \underline c\,a^\top G_ma
 \leq a^\top\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)a
 \leq\overline c\,a^\top G_ma.
\]
Since \(uK_\triangle(u)>0\) on \((0,1)\) and a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an interval, \(G_m\) is positive definite.  Hence
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\{\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\}
 \geq\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)>0.
\]
This proves the population rank condition.  The restriction \(h\leq r_*\) keeps the kernel support inside the assumed radial range, and \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\geq\underline cs>0\) justifies every displayed division.

The radial-coarea lemma makes \(N/Q\) a version of the conditional mean given signed radius.  The stated remainder makes it converge to \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\), so that lemma identifies its two-arm boundary limit with the causal contrast \(\tau_P(x)\).  Write
\[
 \frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
 =\mu_{t,P}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k+R_{t,P,x}(s),
 \qquad |R_{t,P,x}(s)|\leq As^m,
\]
and
\[
 \beta_{t,P,x}(h)=
 (\mu_{t,P}(x),hb_{t,1,P}(x),\ldots,h^{m-1}b_{t,m-1,P}(x))^\top.
\]
At each fixed \(x\), polynomial reproduction gives the exact identity
\[
 \widehat r^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)
 =\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\beta_{t,P,x}(h)
 +B_{t,x}(h)+Z_{t,x}(h),
\]
where \(B\) replaces \(Y_i\) by \(R_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x,i})\), and \(Z\) replaces it by
\(Y_i-N_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x,i})/Q_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x,i})\) on the kernel support.

The latter radial error is conditionally sub-Gaussian uniformly.  Indeed,
\[
 Y-\frac{N(S_{P,x})}{Q(S_{P,x})}
 =\{Y-\mu_{t,P}(X)\}
 +\left\{\mu_{t,P}(X)-\frac{N(S_{P,x})}{Q(S_{P,x})}\right\}.
\]
The first term obeys the conditional sub-Gaussian assumption.  Given signed radius in arm \(t\), the second term is centered by coarea and lies in \([-2L,2L]\), because the H\"older envelope bounds \(|\mu_{t,P}|\) and the ratio is its weighted average.  Hoeffding's exponential inequality, after conditioning first on \(X\) and then on signed radius, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\exp\!\left\{\lambda\left(Y-\frac{N(S_{P,x})}{Q(S_{P,x})}\right)\right\}
 \mid D_{P,x}\right]\leq e^{C_L\lambda^2/2}.
\]

Let \(\varepsilon_n=n^{-3}\).  Compact support puts every trimmed boundary in \([-L,L]^2\), so it admits an ambient \(\varepsilon_n\)-net with at most \(C_Ln^6\) points.  At a fixed net point, bounded kernel-polynomial weights, the upper radial bound, iid sampling, and the preceding conditional sub-Gaussian inequality give Bernstein concentration.  Explicitly, if independent centered variables satisfy \(|W_i|\leq M\) and \(n^{-1}\sum_i\operatorname{Var}(W_i)\leq v\), then
\[
 P\!\left(\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nW_i\right|>z\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{nz^2}{2(v+Mz/3)}\right\}.
\]
Applying this entrywise and taking a union bound over the fixed matrix dimension, both arms, and the net shows that, for every fixed \(B>0\), an event \(E_n(P)\) may be chosen with
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}P\{E_n(P)^c\}\leq n^{-B},
\]
on which, at every net point,
\[
 \left\|\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)-\mathbb E_P\widehat G^{(m)}_{t,x}(h)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\frac14\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)
\]
and the inverse-weighted radial error is at most
\[
 C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}.
\]
The first bound makes the empirical cutoff active and bounds the inverse norm by \(2/\{\underline c\lambda_{\min}(G_m)\}\).

The image of the injective \(C^{\iota+2}\) chart has planar Lebesgue measure zero.  Hence no sample score lies on it almost surely, and the strict arm indicator is constant as \(x\) varies along the boundary.  On \([0,\infty)\), the functions \(K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)\) and \(K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top\), extended by zero past one, are Lipschitz.  Together with
\(|\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2-\lVert X_i-x'\rVert_2|\leq\lVert x-x'\rVert_2\), this gives within a mesh cell Gram oscillation at most \(C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\) and raw outcome-vector oscillation at most
\[
 C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|\right).
\]
Weyl's inequality extends cutoff activity to every boundary point.  The inverse identity \(A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}\), the raw-vector oscillation, and
\(|\mu_{t,P}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x')|\leq L\lVert x-x'\rVert_2\) extend the error bound from the net to the entire trimmed boundary.  No uniform chart derivative or length bound is used.

On the kernel support, \(|R_{t,P,x}(S)|\leq Ah^m\).  The empirical kernel moment is uniformly bounded on \(E_n(P)\), so the reproduction identity gives
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\mu^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{t,h,\underline c}(x)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Subtracting arms gives the same order for the causal curve.

Off \(E_n(P)\), the admissibility lemma gives on the active branch
\[
 |\widehat\mu^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{t,h,\underline c}(x)|
 \leq Ch^{-2}\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|\right),
\]
while the failed branch is zero.  The outcome second moments are uniformly bounded.  Cauchy--Schwarz, with \(B=10\), therefore makes the exceptional expected contribution \(o(h^m)\).  Thus
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
 |\widehat\tau^{(m),\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Balancing gives
\[
 h_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2m+2)},
 \qquad h_n^m\asymp(\log n/n)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]
For fixed \(m\), \(h_n\downarrow0\), eventually \(h_n\leq r_*\), and \(nh_n^2/\log n\to\infty\), validating the concentration regime.  The admissibility lemma supplies one explicit member of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\), proving both upper displays.

Now suppose \(m\geq q\) and \(\mathcal C\) contains the stated bump submodels.  On \([-1/2,1/2]^2\) use the uniform density, horizontal interface, and \(M\asymp h_n^{-1}\) trimmed centers separated by \(3h_n\).  Put
\[
 \Delta_n=\eta h_n^m,
 \qquad \mu_{0,j}=0,
 \qquad \mu_{1,j}(z)=\Delta_n\phi((z-x_j)/h_n),
\]
with \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\), \(\phi(0)=1\), and standard Gaussian errors.  For \(0\leq k\leq q\),
\[
 \lVert D^k\mu_{1,j}\rVert_\infty
 \leq C\eta h_n^{m-k}\leq C\eta,
\]
so small fixed \(\eta\) places every law in the \(q\)-H\"older envelope.  The same calculation through order \(m\) gives a uniform \(C\eta\) bound.  Since \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\pi s\) at sufficiently small trimmed radii, Taylor's theorem followed by half-circle integration proves the required order-\(m\) coarea remainder with uniformly bounded coefficients.  Thus all load-bearing submodel properties, rather than merely their name, have been verified.

Let \(P_0\) be the zero-regression law.  Conditional Gaussian relative entropy and the bump area give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn\Delta_n^2h_n^2
 =C\eta^2nh_n^{2m+2}\leq C'\eta^2\log n.
\]
Since \(\log M=(1/(2m+2)+o(1))\log n\), decrease \(\eta\) so that this is at most \(\alpha\log M\), with \(\alpha<1/4\).  The entropy argument used above gives a decoding-error probability bounded away from zero.  Sup-norm error below \(\Delta_n/3\) identifies the unique bumped center, so even the full-location risk is at least \(c\Delta_n\).  Consequently the distance risk is at least
\[
 c\Delta_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]
This matches the upper bound.  If \(m<q\), the same proposed height and width give a \(q\)-th derivative of order \(h_n^{m-q}\to\infty\), so that construction is not a valid member of the frozen H\"older class; no converse is claimed there.

For any class containing the angular-imbalance family, Poissonization at mean \(2n\), its disjoint-cell factorization, and the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma give probability \(1/2-o(1)\) of some bit error, because local relative entropy is at most \(2\alpha\log M\) with \(2\alpha<1\).  Target separation is \(c_1a_n\), and marked subsampling returns the fixed-sample lower bound \(ca_n\).  This proves the independent angular clause and the order-one saturation statement.

Finally, work at the origin of a horizontal interface inside a square.  Let \(\eta_0\) be a continuous radial cutoff equal to one near zero and supported in an interior disk.  For \(0<\alpha<1\), define in polar coordinates
\[
 f(r,\theta)=c_0\{1+\eta_0(r)r^\alpha\cos\theta\}.
\]
The perturbation integrates to zero on every complete circle, tends to zero at the origin, and, after shrinking its support if needed, is bounded, positive, continuous, and normalized by the uniform base constant \(c_0\).  With a constant regression \(\mu\equiv c\), one has \(N/Q=c\).  With \(\mu(z)=z_1\), on the treated upper half-circle and at radii where \(\eta_0=1\),
\[
 \frac{N(s)}{Q(s)}
 =\frac{s\int_0^\pi\cos\theta\{1+s^\alpha\cos\theta\}\,d\theta}
 {\int_0^\pi\{1+s^\alpha\cos\theta\}\,d\theta}
 =\frac12s^{1+\alpha}.
\]
If \(|s^{1+\alpha}/2-b_1s|\leq As^2\), division by \(s\) and passage to zero force \(b_1=0\), after which division by \(s^2\) would require \(s^{\alpha-1}/2\leq A\), a contradiction.  Thus the same density gives an exact constant normalized coarea regression for one regression and failure of an order-two polynomial remainder for another.  This proves the stated existential non-determination and nothing stronger.
