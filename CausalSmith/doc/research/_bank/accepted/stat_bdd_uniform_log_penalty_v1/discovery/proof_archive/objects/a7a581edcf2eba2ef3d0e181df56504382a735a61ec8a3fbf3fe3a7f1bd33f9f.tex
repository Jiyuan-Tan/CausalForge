All constants below depend only on \(q,\iota,L,c_-,r_0,\delta\).  The proof uses no derivative envelope, speed lower bound, reach bound, or uniform curve-length estimate.

For the upper bound, apply the local-constant estimator on \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\).  The stability lemma gives a uniform denominator lower bound \(\kappa_0\), the upper-mass lemma bounds every kernel-window probability by \(\pi Lh^2\), and \(q\geq1\) gives bias at most \(Lh\).  The trimmed boundary is contained in \([-L,L]^2\).  A maximal \(n^{-3}\)-separated subset therefore has at most \(Cn^6\) points by ambient two-dimensional packing, regardless of the chart's length or speed.  Exponential Markov optimization for the bounded kernel terms and conditionally sub-Gaussian outcome terms, followed by a union bound over the net and the two arms, gives an event of probability at least \(1-Cn^{-12}\) on which, uniformly over net points,
\[
\left|G_{t,x}(h)-\mathbb E_PG_{t,x}(h)\right|
+\left|\frac1{nh^2}\sum_iW_{i,t,x}(h)\{Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\}\right|
\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality and Lipschitz continuity of \(K_\triangle\) extend this event to the whole trimmed boundary, because the Gram oscillation is at most \(Cn^{-3}h^{-3}\) and the outcome-numerator oscillation is at most \(Cn^{-3}h^{-3}n^{-1}\sum_i|Y_i|\).  Conditional sub-Gaussianity makes these oscillations negligible in expectation at \(h\asymp a_n\).  Dividing the weighted regression decomposition by the denominator, which is then at least \(\kappa_0/2\), yields
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
On the concentration complement, the nonzero estimator is a convex combination of outcomes and the zero branch is zero; \(\mathbb E\max_i|Y_i|^2\leq C\log n\) and Cauchy--Schwarz make that contribution negligible.  With \(h\asymp a_n\), the two leading terms are both of order \(a_n\), proving the upper inequality with one member of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).  The canonical radial lemma supplies the separate causal-identification statement.

For the lower bound, use the unit-square straight-interface family in \(\mathrm{lem:angular\text{-}density\text{-}packing}\).  Its interface has an injective \(C^\infty\) chart, all centers lie in \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), and the packing proof verifies compact support, bounded continuous density, the \(q\)-H\"older regression envelope, the moment and tail conditions, and two-sided thickness.  Thus every vertex lies in the enlarged \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\); no quantitative chart geometry is needed.

Poissonize at mean \(2n\), split the process into the disjoint bump cells and complement, and retain at center \(x_j\) the signed-radius observations in its own cell.  The local relative entropy is at most \(2\alpha\log M_n\), where \(2\alpha<1\), while adjacent target values differ by \(c_1a_n\).  The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma bounds simultaneous bit recovery away from one.  Midpoint thresholding converts sup-loss below \(c_1a_n/2\) into simultaneous recovery, and the marked-subsampling argument with the exponentially small event \(\{\operatorname{Poi}(2n)<n\}\) returns the fixed-sample conclusion
\[
R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}})\geq ca_n.
\]
Combining the two bounds proves the proposed theorem.  In particular, smooth injective assignment geometry by itself does not improve the coordinatewise-distance frontier over this class of bounded continuous densities without a shared modulus.
