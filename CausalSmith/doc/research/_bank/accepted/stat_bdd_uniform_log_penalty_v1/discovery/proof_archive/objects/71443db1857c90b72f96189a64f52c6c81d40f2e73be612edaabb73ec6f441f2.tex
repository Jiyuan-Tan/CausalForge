Define the lifted feature vector
\[
 \Phi(z)=(1,z_1,z_2,\lVert z\rVert_2^2)\in\mathbb R^4.
\]
For a center \(c\in\mathbb R^2\) and radius \(r\geq0\), put
\[
 a_{c,r}=(\lVert c\rVert_2^2-r^2,-2c_1,-2c_2,1).
\]
Then the algebraic identity
\[
 \lVert z-c\rVert_2^2-r^2=a_{c,r}^{\top}\Phi(z)
\]
shows that
\[
 z\in B_2(c,r)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a_{c,r}^{\top}\Phi(z)\leq0.
\]

We prove directly that no four points can be shattered.  Take arbitrary \(z_1,\ldots,z_4\in\mathbb R^2\) and write \(v_i=\Phi(z_i)\).  First suppose that the four vectors are linearly dependent.  Choose real numbers \(\lambda_i\), not all zero, such that
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^4\lambda_i v_i=0.
\]
The first coordinate of every \(v_i\) is one, so \(\sum_i\lambda_i=0\); hence the nonzero coefficients include both signs.  Label \(z_i\) inside when \(\lambda_i>0\) and outside when \(\lambda_i<0\), assigning arbitrary labels to zero coefficients.  If a closed ball realized this labeling, then
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^4\lambda_i a_{c,r}^{\top}v_i<0:
\]
the positive-coefficient terms are nonpositive, while every negative coefficient multiplies a strictly positive outside-ball value.  This contradicts
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^4\lambda_i a_{c,r}^{\top}v_i
 =a_{c,r}^{\top}\sum_{i=1}^4\lambda_i v_i=0.
\]

It remains to consider the linearly independent case.  With \(e_4=(0,0,0,1)\), there are unique coefficients \(\lambda_i\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^4\lambda_i v_i=e_4.
\]
Again the first coordinate gives \(\sum_i\lambda_i=0\), so both signs occur.  Use the same sign labeling.  Any realizing ball would again make the weighted sum strictly negative, but the defining fourth coordinate of \(a_{c,r}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^4\lambda_i a_{c,r}^{\top}v_i
 =a_{c,r}^{\top}e_4=1,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus no set of four planar points is shattered by closed Euclidean balls.  Their VC dimension is at most three, and therefore their VC index is at most four.  Restriction of the balls to any support \(\mathcal X_P\) cannot increase the index.  Since the fixed Euclidean metric and \(K_\square(u)=\mathbf 1\{|u|\leq1\}\) generate precisely these restricted radius sublevel sets, the VC clause in \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}a1\text{-}a2\text{-}class}\) follows uniformly over every member law.
