Write \(S_{P,x}=|D_{P,x}|\).  We first construct the estimator appearing in the upper bound.  Let \(v_m(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^{m-1})^\top\).  For arm \(t\), boundary point \(x\), and bandwidth \(h\leq r_*\), define
\[
\widehat G_{t,x}(h)
=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}
K_\triangle(S_{P,x,i}/h)v_m(S_{P,x,i}/h)v_m(S_{P,x,i}/h)^\top
\]
and
\[
\widehat r_{t,x}(h)
=\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{T_i=t\}
K_\triangle(S_{P,x,i}/h)v_m(S_{P,x,i}/h)Y_i.
\]
Set \(\widehat\mu^{(m)}_{t,h}(x)=e_1^\top\widehat G_{t,x}(h)^{-1}\widehat r_{t,x}(h)\) when the Gram matrix is invertible and set it to zero otherwise; take the difference of the two arm intercepts.  This is an explicit measurable function of \(U_{n,P}(x)\), so it lies in the coordinatewise distance decision class.

The coarea identity and the assumed radial bounds give
\[
\mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)
=\int_0^1K_\triangle(u)v_m(u)v_m(u)^\top\frac{Q_{t,P,x}(hu)}h\,du.
\]
For every vector \(a\),
\[
\underline c\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)\{a^\top v_m(u)\}^2du
\leq a^\top\mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)a
\leq\overline c\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)\{a^\top v_m(u)\}^2du.
\]
The middle moment matrix is positive definite because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an interval of positive measure.  Since \(m\) is fixed, its smallest eigenvalue is a positive constant.  This states every rank and inverse condition used below.

The asserted coarea remainder says that the population radial regression is a polynomial of degree \(m-1\) at zero plus a remainder bounded by \(As^m\).  The population local-polynomial normal equations reproduce the polynomial exactly, and the uniformly bounded inverse above therefore makes the intercept bias at most \(Ch^m\).

It remains to control the sample inverse and stochastic term uniformly.  The smooth-boundary length lemma gives a polynomial-size net of \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\) at mesh \(n^{-3}\).  Each Gram summand is bounded and is nonzero with probability at most \(Ch^2\), by the upper radial-mass bound.  Matrix Bernstein on the net and the one-Lipschitz property of the kernel imply
\[
\sup_{P,t,x}\lVert\widehat G_{t,x}(h)-\mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)\rVert
=O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right).
\]
When \(nh^2/\log n\to\infty\), the displayed population eigenvalue bound and Weyl's inequality make every sample Gram matrix invertible with uniformly bounded inverse outside an event of polynomially small probability.  Conditional on signed radius, \(Y-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(S_{P,x})\) is uniformly sub-Gaussian: it is the sum of the assumed sub-Gaussian regression error and a centered angular regression fluctuation bounded by the H\"older envelope.  The same net, conditional exponential inequality, and Lipschitz extension yield
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
|\widehat\tau^{(m)}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\leq C\left\{h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Exceptional singular matrices contribute negligibly because the estimator is set to zero there, the target is bounded, and the same concentration probability may be made \(n^{-4}\).  Balancing the two terms gives
\[
h\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(2m+2)},
\qquad
R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal C)
\leq C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{m/(2m+2)}.
\]

For the stated reverse inequality, use the assumed contained uniform-design straight-interface submodel.  Put disjoint smooth bumps of width \(h\) and height \(c h^m\) at \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) trimmed centers, with Gaussian-noised Bernoulli outcomes and fixed score density.  Membership of this submodel in \(\mathcal C\) is part of the theorem's explicit containment premise; the bumps have the declared order-
\(m\) H\"older envelope.  Adjacent target values differ by \(ch^m\), and one-observation relative entropy is at most \(C h^{2m}h^2\).  Thus at the displayed bandwidth the product entropy is at most a sufficiently small multiple of \(\log M\).  The entropy identity
\[
I(J;O_{1:n})\leq M^{-1}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n}),
\qquad
I(J;O_{1:n})\geq(1-p_e)\log M-\log2
\]
shows that the error probability of every decoder is bounded away from zero.  Sup-norm accuracy below half the bump height would decode the center, so the risk is at least \(ch^m\).  This proves exactness under the stated containment condition.

If a class instead contains the shrinking angular-imbalance family, the target separation is \(ca_n\).  After Poissonization, its disjoint center processes and the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma give a probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\) of some bit error even with all cross-center observations admitted as ancillary side information.  Thresholding and de-Poissonization yield the lower frontier \(ca_n\).  For \(m=1\), the upper rate is exactly \(a_n\), agreeing with smooth-geometry saturation; for \(m\geq2\), the exponent \(m/(2m+2)>1/4\).

Finally, the proof shows why the operational premise is joint.  Continuity and boundedness of \(f_P\) alone permit the shrinking angular-imbalance family and therefore do not force a faster order.  Conversely, density differentiability is not necessary for the displayed remainder: for example, if an arm regression is locally constant on every relevant circle, then \(N_{t,P,x}/Q_{t,P,x}=\mu_{t,P}(x)\) for any bounded continuous positive density, regardless of a density derivative.  What the estimator and its Gram matrix use are exactly the radial mass bounds and the normalized product-integral remainder.  This establishes the claimed absence of a density-only operational threshold without identifying the causal target with the radial functional.
