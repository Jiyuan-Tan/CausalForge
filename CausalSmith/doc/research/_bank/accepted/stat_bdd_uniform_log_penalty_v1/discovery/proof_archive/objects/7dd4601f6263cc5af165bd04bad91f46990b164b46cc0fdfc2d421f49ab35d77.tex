We prove the statement and check all support, geometric, positivity, moment, and division conditions.

First use the unit square \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\), the lower and upper half-square assignment regions, and the interface
\[
\mathcal B=\{(u-1/2,0):0\leq u\leq1\},
\qquad \gamma(u)=(u-1/2,0).
\]
The base score density is \(f_*=1\).  The chart has length one, speed one, all derivatives of order at least two equal to zero, and \(C^{\iota+2}\)-norm at most one.  The line segment is closed and convex, hence every point in the plane has a unique metric projection onto it and its reach is infinite.  Thus the speed, derivative, and reach requirements hold for every \(L\geq4\), \(\iota\geq1\), and \(\rho_0>0\).  At an endpoint, the intersection of an arm and a sufficiently small boundary ball contains a quarter disk.  Since all densities constructed below are at least \(3/4\), for \(r\leq r_0\leq1/4\) each arm has mass at least \(3\pi r^2/16\), which exceeds \(c_-r^2\) because \(c_-\leq1/(16L)\).  Compact support and the assignment-partition conditions are immediate.

Choose a nonnegative radial \(C^\infty\) function \(\phi\), supported in the open unit disk, with \(\phi(0)=1\).  Put
\[
\Delta=d a_n,
\qquad
w=(d_1\Delta)^{1/q},
\]
where the fixed constants are selected below.  Pack the interval \(\gamma([\delta,1-\delta])\) by centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) separated by at least \(3w\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), the disks \(B_2(x_j,w)\) are disjoint and contained in the unit square at a positive distance from the chart endpoints, and elementary interval packing gives
\[
M\geq c w^{-1}\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}.
\]
Let \(\phi_j(z)=\phi((z-x_j)/w)\).  For every derivative order \(k\leq q\),
\[
\lVert D^k(\Delta\phi_j)\rVert_\infty
\leq C_\phi\Delta w^{-k}
\leq C_\phi d_1^{-1}.
\]
Since the supports are disjoint, the same bound holds for every signed sum.  Taking \(d_1\) sufficiently large and the fixed affine slope \(b>0\) sufficiently small therefore puts all regressions below in the prescribed \(q\)-H\"older ball.

For \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\), define
\[
p_{0,\omega}(z)=\frac12,
\qquad
p_{1,\omega}(z)=\frac12+bz_1+\Delta\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j\phi_j(z).
\]
After decreasing \(b\) and \(d\), these functions take values in \([1/4,3/4]\).  Conditional on \(X=z\), take independent variables
\[
Y(t)=B_t+\varepsilon_t,
\qquad
B_t\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_{t,\omega}(z)),
\qquad
\varepsilon_t\sim N(0,1).
\]
Then \(\mu_{t,P_\omega}=p_{t,\omega}\) and
\(1\leq\sigma^2_{t,P_\omega}\leq5/4\), which lies in \([L^{-1},L]\).  Hoeffding's lemma and the Gaussian moment-generating identity give
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[e^{\lambda\{Y(t)-p_{t,\omega}(X)\}}\mid X\right]
\leq e^{\lambda^2/8}e^{\lambda^2/2}
\leq e^{L^2\lambda^2/2},
\]
so the tail condition is uniform.

It remains to hide each regression bump in the radial experiment at its own center.  In polar coordinates \(z=x_j+r(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\), take a continuous cutoff \(\chi\) satisfying \(\chi(v)=0\) for \(v\leq1\) and \(\chi(v)=1\) for \(v\geq2\), and set
\[
A_n(r)=-\frac{2\Delta\phi(r/w)}{br}\,
\chi\!\left(\frac{br}{8\Delta}\right).
\]
There is no division at zero because the cutoff is identically zero on a neighborhood of zero.  On its active region \(br\geq8\Delta\), \(\lvert A_n(r)\rvert\leq1/4\), and smooth compact support of \(\phi\) makes the correction continuous at the outer edge.  Because the disks are disjoint, the formula
\[
f_\omega(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_jA_n(\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2)
\frac{z_1-(x_j)_1}{\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2}
\mathbf1\{z\in B_2(x_j,w)\}
\]
is unambiguous, with the summand defined as zero at \(x_j\).  Its angular correction integrates to zero on every full circle, so \(\int_Sf_\omega=1\); it is continuous and belongs to \([3/4,5/4]\subseteq[L^{-1},L]\).

Now compare adjacent vertices \(\omega\) and \(\omega^{(j)}\).  On either half-circle centered at \(x_j\), \(\int\cos\theta\,d\theta=0\).  Hence both arm-specific radial mass densities agree at \(x_j\) at every radius.  On the treated upper half-circle, whenever \(br\geq16\Delta\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^\pi p_{1,\omega}(x_j+r e_\theta)\{1+A_n(r)\cos\theta\}\,d\theta
-\int_0^\pi p_{1,\omega^{(j)}}(x_j+r e_\theta)\,d\theta\\
&\qquad=\pi\Delta\phi(r/w)+\frac\pi2brA_n(r)=0,
\end{aligned}
\]
after orienting the adjacent pair so that \(\omega_j=1\).  The control equality follows from its constant regression.  A Bernoulli variable followed by the common Gaussian channel is determined, after angular mixing at a fixed radius, by the averaged Bernoulli success probability.  Thus the entire arm-specific radial laws agree outside \(r\leq16\Delta/b\), not only their conditional means.

Inside this radius, the common radial density is bounded by \(Cr\), all Bernoulli probabilities remain in \([1/4,3/4]\), and their radially averaged success probabilities differ by at most \(C\Delta\).  The inequalities \(\log u\leq u-1\) and data processing through the common Gaussian channel give
\[
\operatorname{kl}(p,p')\leq\frac{(p-p')^2}{p'(1-p')},
\qquad
\operatorname{KL}(B+\varepsilon,B'+\varepsilon)\leq\operatorname{kl}(p,p').
\]
Consequently one compressed observation satisfies
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P^{\mathrm{dist}}_{\omega,x_j},P^{\mathrm{dist}}_{\omega^{(j)},x_j})
\leq C\int_0^{16\Delta/b}\Delta^2r\,dr
\leq C'\Delta^4.
\]
Tensorization gives \(C'n\Delta^4=C'd^4\log n\).  Moreover
\[
\log M=\left(\frac{1}{4q}+o(1)\right)\log n.
\]
Choosing \(d\) sufficiently small gives the asserted bound for a fixed \(\alpha<1/8\), with enough slack that Poissonization at mean \(2n\) still has exponent below one.  At \(x_j\), the adjacent target curves differ by \(\Delta\phi_j(x_j)=d a_n\), so \(c_1=d\).  The global density bounds already proved, together with the fixed half-square geometry, give common upper and lower arm-mass envelopes over the entire hypercube.  This completes membership in both \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\) and \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\).

For the canonical square \([-1,1]^2\), use the required chart \(\gamma(u)=(2u-1,0)\) and base density \(f_*=1/4\).  When \(L>4\), let \(\varepsilon_L=1-4/L>0\), replace the cutoff constant \(8\) by a constant \(C_L\geq4/\varepsilon_L\), and choose \(d_1\) sufficiently large and then \(d/b\) sufficiently small as functions of \(L\).  The first choice keeps the cancellation region inside every bump also when \(q=1\); the second keeps its radius and all bump disks away from the trimmed endpoints for all sufficiently large \(n\).  The relative density perturbation is then at most \(\varepsilon_L/2\), so
\[
L^{-1}\leq\frac14(1-\varepsilon_L/2)\leq f_\omega
\leq\frac14(1+\varepsilon_L/2)\leq L.
\]
The cancellation and divergence calculations are unchanged up to constants depending on \(L\).  This verifies the canonical-square clause for \(L>4\).  At \(L=4\), the lower envelope \(f\geq1/4\) on a square of area four and normalization force \(f\equiv1/4\), so no nonzero angular density perturbation is possible; no canonical-square claim is made in that boundary case.
