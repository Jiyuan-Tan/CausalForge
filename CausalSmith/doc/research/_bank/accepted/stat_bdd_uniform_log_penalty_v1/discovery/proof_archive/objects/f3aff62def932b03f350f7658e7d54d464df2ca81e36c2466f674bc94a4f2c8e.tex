Fix \(P\), \(x\), and \(t\) as in the statement.  Apply the coarea formula to the Lipschitz map \(z\mapsto\lVert z-x\rVert_2\) and the integrand \(f_P(z)\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P\}\).  Since the gradient of the distance has norm one away from the single Lebesgue-null point \(x\), for every \(r>0\),
\[
P\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,r)\}
=\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
For \(0<r\leq r_0\), uniform two-sided thickness supplies the lower bound \(c_{-}r^2\).  If the positive set of \(Q_{t,P,x}\) did not accumulate at zero, then \(Q_{t,P,x}=0\) almost everywhere on some interval \((0,r)\), contradicting this lower bound.

Apply coarea once more with the integrand \(\mu_{t,P}(z)f_P(z)\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P\}\).  Deterministic assignment and consistency imply that, on arm \(t\), \(Y=(1-t)Y(0)+tY(1)\) and its conditional mean given \(X=z\) is \(\mu_{t,P}(z)\).  Therefore the marginal density of the positive radius in arm \(t\) is \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\), while the corresponding first-moment density is \(N_{t,P,x}(s)\).  The Radon--Nikodym characterization of conditional expectation consequently gives, for Lebesgue-almost every \(s>0\) with \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
=\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}.
\]
The required integrability follows from the conditional sub-Gaussian member property of either class and the bounded H\"older envelope.

Because \(q\geq1\), the H\"older-regression assumption gives
\(\lvert\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\rvert\leq L\lVert z-x\rVert_2\).  When \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)>0\), the ratio \(N/Q\) is a probability-weighted average of \(\mu_{t,P}\) over points at distance \(s\), and hence
\[
\left|\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|\leq Ls.
\]
When \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)=0\), and also when \(s=0\), the canonical definition sets the value equal to \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\), so the same inequality holds, including every boundary case of the division.  Letting \(s\downarrow0\) separately for \(t=1\) and \(t=0\), and then using the definition of the causal target, yields
\[
\lim_{s\downarrow0}\{g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P,x}(s)-g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P,x}(s)\}
=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
