Fix \(P\), \(x\), and \(t\) as displayed, and put
\(R_x=\lVert X-x\rVert_2\).  By the definition of the observable radial
measure and its chosen coarea density,
\[
 P\{T=t,\ R_x\leq r\}=\nu_{t,P,x}((0,r])
 =\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
The point \(R_x=0\) has probability zero by score absolute continuity.  The
repaired observed-arm thickness condition bounds the last display below by
\(c_-r^2\) for \(0<r\leq r_0\).  If
\(\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\cap(0,r)\) were empty up to a Lebesgue-null set, the
nonnegative integral would be zero.  Thus every neighborhood of zero contains
positive-\(Q\) radii of positive \(Q(s)\,ds\)-measure, so
\(\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\) accumulates at zero.

For every bounded Borel function \(a:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\), the defining
Radon--Nikodym identities give
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)N_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
Conditional sub-Gaussianity and the H\"older envelope ensure integrability of
\(Y\).  Since \(P(R_x=0)=0\), the sign of
\(D_{P,x}=(2T-1)R_x\) recovers \(T\).  The Radon--Nikodym characterization of
conditional expectation therefore gives, for Lebesgue-almost every
\(s\in\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
 =\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
 =g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s).
\]
Division occurs only on \(\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\).

The chosen coarea representatives and consistency give, for every
\(s\in\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\),
\[
 g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)
 =\frac{\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap\mathcal E_{t,P}}
 \mu_{t,P}(z)f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)}
 {\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap\mathcal E_{t,P}}
 f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)}.
\]
Because \(q\geq1\), the H\"older condition implies
\(|\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\lVert z-x\rVert_2\).
The ratio is a probability-weighted average over points at distance \(s\), so
\[
 \left|g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \leq Ls.
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), taking
\(r<\varepsilon/L\) makes this inequality smaller than \(\varepsilon\) at
every positive-\(Q\) radius below \(r\).  The essential-limit definition and
the accumulation just proved therefore give
\(\ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P}(x)=\mu_{t,P}(x)\).  Applying this separately to
the two arms and then using the causal definition of \(\tau_P\) yields
\[
 \ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P}(x)-\ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P}(x)
 =\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
\]
No common sequence of positive radii and no value of the regression at a
zero-denominator radius has been used.
