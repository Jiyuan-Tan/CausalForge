Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\).  All constants below depend only on \(q,\iota,L,c_-,r_0,\delta\), and never on \(n\), \(P\), or the boundary point.  No derivative envelope, chart-speed lower bound, reach bound, or uniform curve-length estimate is used.

For the upper bound, use the strict signed-distance local-constant estimator from \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}estimator}\) on \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\).  At each fixed center, absolute continuity gives \(P(X=x)=0\), so its weights agree almost surely with the arm weights in \(\mathrm{lem:fixed\text{-}local\text{-}constant\text{-}stability}\).  That lemma gives a uniform population denominator lower bound \(\kappa_0>0\) for \(h\leq2r_0\).  The upper-mass lemma gives
\[
P\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,h)\}\leq\pi Lh^2,
\]
and \(q\geq1\) gives \(|\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq Lh\) on the kernel support.

The trimmed boundary is compact and contained in \([-L,L]^2\).  A maximal \(\varepsilon_n=n^{-3}\) separated subset has at most \(C_Ln^6\) points by ambient two-dimensional packing.  Exponential Markov optimization for the bounded kernel terms and the conditionally sub-Gaussian outcome terms, followed by a union bound over the net and both arms, gives an event of probability at least \(1-Cn^{-12}\) on which every net denominator is at least \(3\kappa_0/4\) and
\[
\left|\frac1{nh^2}\sum_{i=1}^nW_{i,t,x}(h)
\{Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i)\}\right|
\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}
\]
at every net point.  An injective \(C^{\iota+2}\) chart is in particular a \(C^1\) curve, whose planar Lebesgue measure is zero.  Hence, almost surely, no sample score lies on \(\mathcal B_P\), and the strict arm indicators remain constant as the center moves along the boundary.  The reverse triangle inequality and Lipschitz continuity of \(K_\triangle\) therefore give Gram oscillation \(C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\) and outcome-numerator oscillation
\[
C\varepsilon_nh^{-3}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|Y_i|.
\]
At \(h\asymp a_n\) these oscillations are negligible in expectation.  Thus every denominator is at least \(\kappa_0/2\), uniformly over the trimmed boundary.

Decomposing the weighted outcome around \(\mu_{t,P}(x)\), dividing only on the positive-denominator branch, and subtracting arms yields on this event
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B_{P,\delta}}
|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
On the complement, each nonzero arm estimate is a convex combination of observed outcomes and each zero-denominator arm estimate is zero.  The H\"older envelope and conditional sub-Gaussianity imply \(\mathbb E_P\max_{i\leq n}|Y_i|^2\leq C\log n\); Cauchy--Schwarz makes the complement contribution negligible.  With \(h\asymp a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), the bias and stochastic terms are both of order \(a_n\).  This proves the upper inequality with one explicit member of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\).  The canonical coarea lemma separately identifies the limit of radial regressions with the causal contrast.

For the lower bound, use the unit-square straight-interface family from \(\mathrm{lem:angular\text{-}density\text{-}packing}\).  Its displayed affine parameterization is an injective \(C^\infty\) chart, every center lies in \(\mathcal B_{P,\delta}\), and the packing proof verifies compact support, bounded continuous density, the \(q\)-H\"older regression envelope, common variance and tail bounds, and two-sided thickness.  Hence every vertex belongs to the enlarged \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\); none of the deleted quantitative geometry conditions is used.

Poissonize at mean \(2n\), attach independent continuous ordering marks, and split the process into the disjoint bump cells and their complement.  Cell \(j\) depends only on bit \(j\); the complement is bit-independent.  Compress cell \(j\) to the outcomes, signed radii about \(x_j\), and marks.  The local relative entropy is at most \(2\alpha\log M_n\), with \(2\alpha<1\), while adjacent target values differ by at least \(c_1a_n\).  The coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma gives probability at least \(1/2-o(1)\) of some bit error.  Midpoint thresholding would recover all bits whenever the trimmed-boundary sup loss were below \(c_1a_n/2\).  Thus the Poissonized risk is at least \(ca_n\).  On the event that the Poisson count is at least \(n\), the first \(n\) marked observations are iid; the complementary event has probability at most \(e^{-cn}\).  Since \(\sup_x|\tau_P(x)|\leq2L\), marked subsampling gives
\[
R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}})\geq c'a_n.
\]
Combining both bounds proves the proposed theorem.  Smooth injective assignment geometry alone therefore does not improve the coordinatewise-distance frontier over bounded continuous densities without a common modulus.
