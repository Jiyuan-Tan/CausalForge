Put \(S=[-1/2,1/2]^2\) and use its lower edge.  All centers below lie in the relative interior of that edge, so a sufficiently small circle centered at \(x_j\) meets \(S\) in the upper half-circle
\[
 x_j+r e_\theta,\qquad e_\theta=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),
 \qquad 0\leq\theta\leq\pi.
\]
The identities that drive the construction are
\[
 \int_0^\pi\cos\theta\,d\theta=0,
 \qquad
 \int_0^\pi\cos^2\theta\,d\theta=\frac\pi2.
\]

Choose \(\psi\in C_c^\infty((-1,1))\) with \(0\leq\psi\leq1\) and \(\psi(0)=1\), set
\[
 \phi(z)=\psi(\lVert z\rVert_2^2),
 \qquad \varphi(u)=\psi(u^2),
\]
and let
\[
 \Delta=d a_n,
 \qquad w=(d_1\Delta)^{1/q}.
\]
Choose points \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) on a fixed middle subinterval of the lower edge, separated by \(3w\).  The half-discs \(B_2(x_j,w)\cap S\) are disjoint and avoid the corners for all large \(n\), and elementary interval packing gives
\[
 M\geq c w^{-1}\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}.
\]
Write \(r_j(z)=\lVert z-x_j\rVert_2\) and \(\phi_j(z)=\phi((z-x_j)/w)\).

Fix a small affine slope \(b>0\).  For \(\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\), define
\[
 p_\omega(z)=\frac12+bz_1+\Delta\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j\phi_j(z).
\]
For every integer \(0\leq k\leq q\),
\[
 \lVert D^k(\Delta\phi_j)\rVert_\infty
 \leq C_\phi\Delta w^{-k}.
\]
The right side is bounded by a constant made arbitrarily small at \(k=q\) by increasing \(d_1\), and it tends to zero for \(k<q\).  Disjoint supports preserve these bounds for the sum.  We first choose \(b\) small, then \(d_1\) large, and finally \(d\) small.  This places every \(p_\omega\) in \(\mathcal H^q(S,L)\) and in \([1/4,3/4]\).

Let \(\chi\in C^\infty(\mathbb R)\) satisfy \(0\leq\chi\leq1\), \(\chi(v)=0\) for \(v\leq1\), and \(\chi(v)=1\) for \(v\geq2\).  With a fixed \(c_A\geq8\), define
\[
 A_n(r)=-\frac{2\Delta\varphi(r/w)}{br}
 \chi\!\left(\frac{br}{c_A\Delta}\right),
 \qquad A_n(0)=0.
\]
There is no singularity: the cutoff makes \(A_n\) identically zero on a neighborhood of zero.  On its active region, \(|A_n(r)|\leq2/c_A\leq1/4\), and the compact support of \(\varphi(r/w)\) makes it vanish smoothly before \(r=w\).  Define, relative to the compact support \(S\),
\[
 f_\omega(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^M\omega_j A_n(r_j(z))
 \frac{z_1-(x_j)_1}{r_j(z)}\mathbf1\{r_j(z)<w\},
\]
with each summand set to zero at \(x_j\).  The supports are disjoint, so \(f_\omega\) is unambiguous and continuous on \(S\), including at the centers and support edges.  It lies in \([3/4,5/4]\), which is contained in \([L^{-1},L]\) because \(L\geq4\).  Polar integration over each affected half-disc gives
\[
 \int_0^w A_n(r)r\,dr\int_0^\pi\cos\theta\,d\theta=0.
\]
Thus \(\int_S f_\omega=1\).  Positivity makes \(S\) the support, and the square boundary is a rectifiable curve of finite length.

Conditional on \(X=z\), let
\[
 Y=B+\varepsilon,
 \qquad B\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_\omega(z)),
 \qquad \varepsilon\sim N(0,1),
\]
with \(B\) and \(\varepsilon\) conditionally independent.  Then
\[
 \mu_{P_\omega}(z)=p_\omega(z),
 \qquad
 \sigma^2_{P_\omega}(z)=1+p_\omega(z)\{1-p_\omega(z)\}.
\]
The variance is continuous and belongs to \([1,5/4]\subseteq[L^{-1},L]\).  Taking \((Y_i,X_i)\) independently with this common law verifies, one by one, all four defining conditions of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\): independently and identically distributed sampling; a continuous density on the compact support \(S\subseteq[-L,L]^2\) with the displayed envelope and rectifiable boundary; the \(q\)-H\"older regression envelope; and the continuous conditional-variance envelope.

Fix adjacent vertices, oriented so that \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j^{(j)}=0\).  Other perturbations vanish in the \(j\)-th half-disc.  At every \(0<r<w\), the density difference integrates to zero on the half-circle, so the two radial densities agree and equal \(\pi r\).  The difference between their outcome-weighted angular integrals is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\int_0^\pi p_\omega(x_j+re_\theta)
       \{1+A_n(r)\cos\theta\}\,d\theta
 -\int_0^\pi p_{\omega^{(j)}}(x_j+re_\theta)\,d\theta\\
 &=\pi\Delta\varphi(r/w)+\frac\pi2brA_n(r).
\end{aligned}
\]
The terms involving the constant part of the affine regression and the product of the radial bump with \(A_n(r)\cos\theta\) vanish by the first angular identity.  When \(br\geq2c_A\Delta\), the cutoff equals one and the last display is zero.  For \(r\geq w\), both the density and regression perturbations vanish.  Therefore the conditional radial outcome laws agree outside \(r\leq2c_A\Delta/b\): a mixture of Bernoulli variables is Bernoulli with its averaged success probability, and convolution with the common Gaussian noise preserves equality.

On the exceptional interval, the common radial density is \(\pi r\), the two averaged Bernoulli probabilities remain in \([1/4,3/4]\), and their difference is at most \(C\Delta\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{kl}(p,p')\leq\frac{(p-p')^2}{p'(1-p')}
 \leq C\Delta^2.
\]
Data processing through addition of the common Gaussian and integration over the common radial marginal yield for one observation
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P^{\mathrm{rad}}_{\omega,j},P^{\mathrm{rad}}_{\omega^{(j)},j})
 \leq C\Delta^2\int_0^{2c_A\Delta/b}r\,dr
 \leq C'\Delta^4.
\]
Independent-product tensorization gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\!\left(\mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
 \mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}\right)
 \leq C'n\Delta^4=C'd^4\log n.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \log M=\left(\frac1{4q}+o(1)\right)\log n.
\]
Choose \(d\) sufficiently small that the preceding divergence is at most \(\alpha\log M\) for a fixed \(\alpha<1/8\), with enough slack for the later Poissonization at mean \(2n\).  Finally, \(\phi_j(x_j)=1\), so adjacent regressions differ at \(x_j\) by \(\Delta=da_n\).  This proves the lemma with \(c_1=d\).

To summarize the membership verification, every vertex and every sufficiently large \(n\) satisfies each of the four conditions in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\): condition \(\mathrm{(i)}\) holds by the independent product construction; condition \(\mathrm{(ii)}\) holds because \(S\) is compact with rectifiable boundary and \(f_\omega\) is continuous, normalized, supported on \(S\), and lies between \(L^{-1}\) and \(L\); condition \(\mathrm{(iii)}\) is the derivative-scaled \(q\)-H\"older calculation for \(p_\omega\); and condition \(\mathrm{(iv)}\) follows from the continuous formula \(\sigma^2_{P_\omega}=1+p_\omega(1-p_\omega)\in[1,5/4]\subseteq[L^{-1},L]\).
