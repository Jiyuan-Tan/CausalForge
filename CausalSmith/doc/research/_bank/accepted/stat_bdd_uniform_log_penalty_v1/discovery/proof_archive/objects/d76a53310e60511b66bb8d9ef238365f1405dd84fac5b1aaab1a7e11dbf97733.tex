Fix \(n\) sufficiently large for the packing lemma, abbreviate \(M=M_n\), and write \(\Delta=c_1a_n\).  Let \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) be an arbitrary fixed-sample rule.  We compare it with a marked-Poisson experiment before applying the direct-product lemma.

Generate a Poisson point process of mean \(2n\) from a packing law \(P_\omega\), and attach independent marks having a continuous distribution.  If the point count \(N\) is at least \(n\), retain the \(n\) observations with the smallest marks and apply \(\widehat\mu_n\) to them.  If \(N<n\), return the zero boundary function.  Denote the induced rule by \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\).  Conditional on \(N=k\geq n\), selection by independent marks makes the retained observations an independent sample with law \(P_\omega^{\otimes n}\).  The construction is law independent.  At a boundary point \(x\), its output is a measurable function only of the retained pairs \((Y_i,\lVert X_i-x\rVert_2)\), so it remains coordinatewise in the marked compressed experiment.

Partition the process into the disjoint half-disc cells supporting the bumps and their complement.  Under independent uniform bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\), restrictions of a Poisson process to these cells are conditionally independent.  Cell \(j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), and the complement is independent of all bits.  At center \(x_j\), threshold \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\) at the midpoint of the two possible regression values.  This defines a decoder of \(\Omega_j\).  Within its own cell, decoder \(j\) uses only the marked values \((Y_i,\lVert X_i-x_j\rVert_2)\).  Giving it the marked uncompressed restrictions of every other cell and the complement can only enlarge its information.  The enlarged experiment therefore has exactly the conditional-product form of \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\).

Independent marks contribute no relative entropy.  Poisson tensorization at mean \(2n\), together with the packing lemma's fixed-sample tensorization bound and its reserved slack, gives for every local compressed cell experiment
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
 \leq2\alpha\log M,
 \qquad 2\alpha<1.
\]
The direct-product lemma now yields
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one coordinate is decoded incorrectly}\}
 \geq\frac12-o(1).
\]
At every center, the regression value depends only on its own bit and the two possible values are separated by at least \(\Delta\).  Uniform boundary loss smaller than \(\Delta/2\) would therefore make every midpoint decoder correct.  Averaging over the uniform bit prior shows that the maximal expected boundary loss of the induced marked-Poisson rule over the finite packing family is at least
\[
 \frac\Delta2\left\{\frac12-o(1)\right\}
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

It remains to transfer this bound back to the fixed-sample experiment.  On \(\{N\geq n\}\), the retained data have law \(P_\omega^{\otimes n}\).  Every packing regression lies in \([1/4,3/4]\), so the zero default has uniform boundary loss at most one.  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 +\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Poisson Chernoff inequality gives
\[
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}\leq e^{-c'n}=o(a_n).
\]
Combining the preceding displays and reducing the constant if necessary proves, for every fixed-sample rule,
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq c a_n.
\]
The packing lemma verifies that every \(P_\omega\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), including independent sampling, compact support inside \([-L,L]^2\), a continuous normalized density in \([L^{-1},L]\), rectifiable support boundary, \(q\)-H\"older regression envelope, and continuous variance in \([L^{-1},L]\).  Therefore the supremum over the full published class is no smaller.  Taking the infimum over the law-independent decision class gives
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\geq c a_n.
\]
Since \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), division by \(a_n\) and passage to the lower limit establish both the expanded minimax display and
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}\geq c_{\mathrm{CTY}}>0.
\]
This is a same-class converse: no two-arm, thickness, or treatment-effect class is used.
