All constants below may depend on the frozen class parameters, \(q\), and \(\delta\), but not on \(n\) or \(P\).

First, the class is nonempty.  Take \(X\) uniform on \([-1,1]^2\), use the prescribed half-squares and the injective \(C^\infty\) chart \(\gamma(u)=(2u-1,0)\), take constant regressions in \((1/4,3/4)\), and let each potential outcome be a Bernoulli variable with that conditional mean plus an independent standard Gaussian.  The density is \(1/4\), and each arm contains a quarter disk at a boundary endpoint.  Thus arm mass is at least \(\pi r^2/16\geq c_-r^2\).  Conditional variances lie in \([1,5/4]\), and Hoeffding's lemma times the Gaussian moment-generating function gives the required sub-Gaussian envelope.  Every remaining support, positivity, assignment, smoothness, variance, tail, and chart condition is satisfied.

For the full-location upper bound, let \(r_q(u)\) collect the bivariate monomials of total degree at most \(q-1\), let \(K_2(u)=(1-\lVert u\rVert_2)_+\), and form the usual arm-specific local-polynomial Gram matrix and outcome vector, scaled by \((nh^2)^{-1}\).  For a trimmed boundary point and sufficiently small \(h\), the rescaled arm support contains a fixed open half-disk.  The density lower envelope gives, for every coefficient vector \(v\),
\[
v^\top\mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)v
\geq L^{-1}\int_{H_t\cap B_2(0,1/2)}K_2(u)\{v^\top r_q(u)\}^2\,du.
\]
A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on this open set.  Compactness of the unit sphere in the fixed-dimensional coefficient space therefore supplies a uniform positive minimum eigenvalue.  Use the intercept when the empirical minimum eigenvalue exceeds half this constant and zero otherwise.

Bounded kernel weights, the density upper envelope, and conditional sub-Gaussianity give, uniformly over a polynomial-size grid on the trimmed segment,
\[
\lVert\widehat G_{t,x}(h)-\mathbb E_P\widehat G_{t,x}(h)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
+\left\lVert\frac1{nh^2}\sum_iW_{i,t,x}(Y_i-\mu_{t,P}(X_i))r_q((X_i-x)/h)\right\rVert_2
\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}
\]
outside an event of probability at most \(Cn^{-12}\).  Kernel Lipschitz continuity extends the bound between grid points.  Taylor's formula through total degree \(q-1\) gives bias \(Ch^q\).  The cutoff bounds the inverse on its active branch; on its failed branch the estimate is zero.  The raw outcome vector is bounded by \(Ch^{-2}n^{-1}\sum_i|Y_i|\), so Cauchy--Schwarz makes the exceptional-event expectation negligible.  Hence
\[
R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
\leq C\left\{h^q+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^2}}\right\}.
\]
Balancing at \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2q+2)}\) gives the upper bound \(Cs_{n,q}\).

For the full-location converse, choose \(M\asymp h^{-1}\) trimmed centers with disjoint radius-\(h\) disks.  Keep the uniform density and control regression fixed, and perturb the treated regression in alternative \(j\) by
\[
\Delta\phi((z-x_j)/h),\qquad \Delta=\eta h^q,
\]
where \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2(0,1))\), \(\phi(0)=1\), and \(\eta>0\) is small.  Since \(\lVert D^k(\Delta\phi((\cdot-x_j)/h))\rVert_\infty\leq C\eta h^{q-k}\) for \(k\leq q\), every alternative lies in the same H\"older ball.  Gaussian-noised Bernoulli outcomes keep all variance and tail conditions fixed.  Relative entropy from the null obeys
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
\leq Cn\Delta^2h^2=C\eta^2nh^{2q+2}.
\]
At the chosen bandwidth this is at most a small multiple of \(\log M\).  For a uniform alternative index \(J\), the log-sum inequality gives \(I(J;O_{1:n})\leq\alpha\log M\), while conditioning entropy on the decoding-error indicator gives \(H(J\mid O_{1:n})\leq\log2+p_e\log M\).  Thus \(p_e\geq1-\alpha-o(1)\).  Sup-norm accuracy below \(\Delta/2\) decodes \(J\), proving
\[
R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})\geq c h^q=cs_{n,q}.
\]

Suppose \(L>4\).  Use the explicit rule \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{LC}}_{n,h}\) from \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}estimator}\).  Its strict signed-distance weights are functions of \(U_{n,P}(x)\) and assign \(D_{P,x}=0\) to neither arm.  The straight boundary is Lebesgue null, so these indicators are constant between mesh points almost surely.  The fixed-stability lemma gives a positive population denominator, the upper-mass lemma gives kernel-window probability \(O(h^2)\), the H\"older condition gives bias \(Ch\), and conditional sub-Gaussianity gives stochastic term \(C\sqrt{\log n/(nh^2)}\), uniformly on a polynomial mesh and hence on the segment by kernel Lipschitz continuity.  The complement is negligible because a nonzero estimate is a convex combination of outcomes.  Thus \(h\asymp a_n\) gives the upper bound \(Ca_n\).  The canonical-square angular-density packing is contained in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\).  Poissonization, the disjoint-cell factorization, the coordinatewise-overlap direct-product lemma, midpoint decoding, and marked subsampling back to exactly \(n\) observations give the matching lower bound \(ca_n\), because the adjacent Poisson compressed laws have relative entropy at most \(2\alpha\log M\) with \(2\alpha<1\).

Suppose instead \(L=4\).  The square has area four and \(f_P\geq1/4\), so
\[
1=\int_{[-1,1]^2}f_P(z)\,dz\geq\int_{[-1,1]^2}\frac14\,dz=1.
\]
Hence \(f_P=1/4\) almost everywhere, and continuity makes the equality pointwise.  Comparison of the member-property lists shows that every straight-class law is also in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{coarea}}(q,\iota,L)\).  The thickness-free radial coarea lemma therefore supplies the signed-radius conditional-mean identity.  At a trimmed boundary point and for all sufficiently small \(s\), each arm-specific circle portion is a half-circle contained in the square, so
\[
Q_{t,P,x}(s)=\frac\pi4s>0.
\]
Taylor expansion of \(\mu_{t,P}\) through degree \(q-1\), followed by half-circle integration and grouping equal powers of \(s\), gives uniformly bounded coefficients with
\[
\left|\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}-\mu_{t,P}(x)
-\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}b_{t,k,P}(x)s^k\right|\leq Cs^q.
\]
The one-sided radial polynomial Gram matrix is a positive multiple of
\[
\int_0^1uK_\triangle(u)v_q(u)v_q(u)^\top\,du,
\qquad v_q(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^{q-1})^\top,
\]
which is positive definite because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on \((0,1)\).  Instantiate \(\mathrm{def:stabilized\text{-}distance\text{-}local\text{-}polynomial}\) with \(m=q\), cutoff constant \(\pi/4\), and kernel \(K_\triangle\).  The admissibility lemma makes this a Borel member of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{dist}}\) and bounds its active branch by \(Ch^{-2}n^{-1}\sum_i|Y_i|\); the failed branch is zero.  Gram and radial-error concentration on a polynomial mesh, polynomial reproduction, and the displayed order-\(q\) remainder give bias \(Ch^q\), stochastic term \(C\sqrt{\log n/(nh^2)}\), and a negligible cutoff-exception contribution by Cauchy--Schwarz.  Balancing gives the distance upper bound \(Cs_{n,q}\).  Conversely, every distance-only rule can be implemented from full score locations, so
\[
R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
\geq R_{n,\delta}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}})
\geq cs_{n,q}.
\]
This proves the \(L=4\) equivalence.  Finally,
\[
\frac{q}{2q+2}>\frac14\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q>1,
\]
which proves the stated elbow and all boundary cases.
