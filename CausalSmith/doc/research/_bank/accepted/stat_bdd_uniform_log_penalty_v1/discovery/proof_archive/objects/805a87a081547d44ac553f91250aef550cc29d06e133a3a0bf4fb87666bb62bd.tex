Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{coarea}}(q,\iota,L)\), \(x\in\mathcal B_P\), and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and put
\[
 A_t=\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P,
 \qquad R_x=\lVert X-x\rVert_2.
\]
The density envelope gives an absolutely continuous score law supported on \(\mathcal X_P\).  Polar coordinates centered at \(x\) and Tonelli's theorem therefore yield, for every \(r>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,r)\}
 &=\int_{A_t\cap B_2(x,r)}f_P(z)\,dz\\
 &=\int_0^r\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap A_t}
 f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)\,ds\\
 &=\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
No positivity condition and no division is used in this identity.

Because a singleton has planar Lebesgue measure zero, \(P(X=x)=0\).  On \(\{X\neq x\}\), the signed-distance definition gives \(D_{P,x}=(2T-1)R_x\), so its sign recovers \(T\) almost surely.

For any bounded Borel \(a:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\), deterministic assignment, consistency, and the definition of \(\mu_{t,P}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 &=\int_{A_t}a(\lVert z-x\rVert_2)\mu_{t,P}(z)f_P(z)\,dz\\
 &=\int_0^\infty a(s)N_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
The same calculation with \(Y\) replaced by one gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
Thus, on the half-line of sign \(2t-1\), the outcome-weighted law of \(D_{P,x}\) has density \(N_{t,P,x}(s)\), whereas its probability law has density \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)\).  The Radon--Nikodym characterization of conditional expectation permits the version
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
 =\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(s>0\) with positive denominator.  The version may be assigned arbitrarily when the denominator is zero.

Finally, under the stated positivity and convergence conditions on \((0,r_*]\), subtraction of the two limits gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}\left\{
 \frac{N_{1,P,x}(s)}{Q_{1,P,x}(s)}-
 \frac{N_{0,P,x}(s)}{Q_{0,P,x}(s)}\right\}
 &=\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)\\
 &=\tau_P(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is an identification statement for the separately defined causal target.
