Fix \(P\), \(x\), and \(t\) as displayed, and put \(R_x=\lVert X-x\rVert_2\).  By the definition of the observable radial measure and its fixed Radon--Nikodym density,
\[
 P\{T=t,\ 0<R_x\leq r\}
 =\nu_{t,P,x}((0,r])
 =\int_0^rQ_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
Score absolute continuity gives \(P(R_x=0)=0\).  The actual-arm thickness condition bounds the last display below by \(c_-r^2\) for \(0<r\leq r_0\).  Therefore every neighborhood \((0,r]\) has positive \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)ds\)-measure.  This is exactly the assertion that zero belongs to the essential support; it makes no topological claim about a particular representative's pointwise positive set.

For every bounded Borel function \(a:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\), the defining Radon--Nikodym identities give
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)Q_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf1\{T=t\}a(R_x)]
 =\int_0^\infty a(s)N_{t,P,x}(s)\,ds.
\]
The class's conditional sub-Gaussian condition and H\"older envelope imply \(\mathbb E_P|Y|<\infty\).  Because \(P(R_x=0)=0\), the sign of \(D_{P,x}=(2T-1)R_x\) recovers \(T\).  The Radon--Nikodym characterization of conditional expectation then gives, for Lebesgue-almost every \(s\in\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid D_{P,x}=(2t-1)s]
 =\frac{N_{t,P,x}(s)}{Q_{t,P,x}(s)}
 =g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s).
\]
Division is performed only on \(\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\).

For Lebesgue-almost every \(s>0\), the fixed Radon--Nikodym versions agree with their observed-arm circle integrals.  At every such \(s\in\mathcal S_{t,P,x}\), consistency yields
\[
 g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)
 =\frac{\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap\mathcal E_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P}
 \mu_{t,P}(z)f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)}
 {\int_{\partial B_2(x,s)\cap\mathcal E_{t,P}\cap\mathcal X_P}
 f_P(z)\,d\mathcal H^1(z)}.
\]
Since \(q\geq1\), the H\"older condition implies \(|\mu_{t,P}(z)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq L\lVert z-x\rVert_2\).  The ratio is a probability-weighted average over points at distance \(s\), whence
\[
 |g^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P,x}(s)-\mu_{t,P}(x)|\leq Ls
\]
for \(Q_{t,P,x}(s)ds\)-almost every sufficiently small \(s\).  Given \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(r<\varepsilon/L\).  The exceptional set in the defining essential-limit display then has zero radial measure, and the essential-support result prevents the definition from being vacuous.  Hence
\[
 \ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{t,P}(x)=\mu_{t,P}(x).
\]
Applying this argument separately to \(t=0\) and \(t=1\), then using the causal definition of \(\tau_P\), gives
\[
 \ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{1,P}(x)-\ell^{\mathrm{dist}}_{0,P}(x)
 =\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)=\tau_P(x).
\]
No value at a zero-denominator radius and no common sequence of radii has been used.
