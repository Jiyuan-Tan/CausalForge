Fix \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\).  The cited transcription of Cattaneo, Titiunik, and Yu, Theorem~6, records that their distance class consists of rules \(T_n(U_n(x))\) for every evaluation point \(x\), with the infimum over \(T_n\) outside the supremum over laws.  In the present notation, \(U_n(x)=V_{n,P}(x)\).  Thus a member of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) is exactly a single measurable map selected independently of \(P\) and applied at every boundary point; the common-map formulation is not a new information restriction.

For all sufficiently large \(n\), take the family supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:cty\text{-}support\text{-}boundary\text{-}angular\text{-}packing}\).  Write \(M=M_n\), put \(\Delta=c_1a_n\), and let
\[
 C_j=B_2(x_j,w_n)\cap S,\qquad 1\leq j\leq M,
\]
be its pairwise disjoint half-disc cells.  The packing lemma supplies numbers \(u_{j,0},u_{j,1}\) satisfying
\[
 \mu_{P_\omega}(x_j)=u_{j,\omega_j},
 \qquad |u_{j,1}-u_{j,0}|\geq\Delta.
\]
It also gives \(M_n\geq c_0a_n^{-1/q}\).  Since \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\to0\),
\[
 M_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Let \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) be arbitrary.  By \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}distance\text{-}decision\text{-}class}\), there is one measurable map
\[
 T_n:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\]
selected independently of the law, such that
\[
 \widehat\mu_n(x)=T_n(V_{n,P}(x))
\]
at every boundary point.

We embed this fixed-sample rule in an experiment whose restrictions to the cells are independent.  Under \(P_\omega\), generate a Poisson point process of mean \(2n\) on the observation space and attach independent marks uniformly distributed on \((0,1)\).  If its point count \(N\) is at least \(n\), retain the \(n\) observations with the smallest marks, order them by their marks, form the corresponding distance sample at every queried boundary point, and apply the same map \(T_n\).  If \(N<n\), return the zero boundary function.  Denote this rule by \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}\).  Conditional on every \(N=k\geq n\), the retained observations are an independent sample from \(P_\omega\): the marks are independent of the observations, and their ordering induces a random permutation independent of the observation values.  The construction is independent of \(\omega\) and of the law within the packing family.

Put independent uniform bits \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_M\) on the packing vertices.  Let \(Z_j\) be the complete marked restriction of the Poisson process to \(C_j\), and let \(Z_0\) be its restriction to the complement of \(\bigcup_{j=1}^M C_j\).  The marked observation space is standard Borel, as are the spaces of its finite counting measures.  Conditional on the bits, \(Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M\) are independent because restrictions of a Poisson point process to disjoint Borel sets are independent.  By the cellwise locality conclusions of the packing lemma, the one-observation law on \(C_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), whereas the law on the complement of the union of the cells is bit-independent.  Therefore the law of \(Z_j\) depends only on \(\Omega_j\), and the law of \(Z_0\) is independent of all bits.

Let \(S_j\) retain from \(Z_j\) only each point's mark, outcome, and distance from \(x_j\).  At \(x_j\), the rule \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\) is measurable with respect to \((S_j,Z_{-j},Z_0)\): the full restrictions outside \(C_j\) reveal their marks, outcomes, and distances from \(x_j\), while \(S_j\) supplies exactly the information about points inside \(C_j\) permitted by \(V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\).  Sorting all revealed marks reconstructs the thinned ordered sample to which \(T_n\) is applied.

We verify the relative-entropy hypothesis of the direct-product lemma.  Adjacent packing laws are identical off \(C_j\), their common cell mass is \(m_n\), and every support point at distance below \(w_n\) from \(x_j\) lies in \(C_j\).  Let \(\nu_{j,b}\) be the subprobability law of one outcome--distance pair restricted to \(C_j\), under bit value \(b\in\{0,1\}\).  Both subprobabilities have mass \(m_n\).  The corresponding full one-observation compressed laws have the form
\[
 \nu_{j,b}+\lambda_j,
\]
where \(\lambda_j\) is common to the two bits and is supported on radii at least \(w_n\), while \(\nu_{j,b}\) is supported on radii below \(w_n\), up to null boundaries.  Define
\[
 D_j=\int\log\!\left(\frac{d\nu_{j,0}}{d\nu_{j,1}}\right)d\nu_{j,0}.
\]
The disjoint-support decomposition and product factorization of likelihood ratios give
\[
 nD_j
 =\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_\omega}\{V_{n,P_\omega}(x_j)\},
 \mathcal L_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}\{V_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)\}
 \right)
 \leq\alpha\log M.
\]
Finiteness supplies the needed absolute continuity, and the packing lemma gives the same bound after reversing the adjacent orientation.

Let \(Q_{j,b}=\mathcal L(S_j\mid\Omega_j=b)\).  Under either bit, the cell count satisfies \(K_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2nm_n)\), so the count law contributes no relative entropy.  Conditional on \(K_j=k\), the compressed cell points are independent with law \(\nu_{j,b}/m_n\), and the independent marks again contribute no relative entropy.  Since the two subprobabilities have the same mass,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\nu_{j,0}/m_n,\nu_{j,1}/m_n)=\frac{D_j}{m_n}.
\]
Conditioning on the common count and taking expectations therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,1})
 =\mathbb E[K_j]\frac{D_j}{m_n}
 =2nD_j
 \leq2\alpha\log M.
\]
The packing fixes \(0<\alpha<1/8\), so \(\kappa=2\alpha<1\).

At each \(x_j\), decode \(\Omega_j\) by thresholding \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x_j)\) at the midpoint of \(u_{j,0}\) and \(u_{j,1}\), reversing the inequality if \(u_{j,1}<u_{j,0}\).  The finite-\(M\) conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem:coordinatewise\text{-}overlap\text{-}direct\text{-}product}\) gives
\[
 \Pr\{\text{at least one coordinate is decoded incorrectly}\}
 \geq\frac12\left[1-\exp\{-M_n^{1-2\alpha}/2\}\right]
 =\frac12-o(1),
\]
because \(M_n\to\infty\) and \(2\alpha<1\).  If the uniform boundary loss is less than \(\Delta/2\), every midpoint decoder is correct.  Averaging over the uniform bit prior and using that a maximum dominates an average gives
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{4}
 \left[1-\exp\{-M_n^{1-2\alpha}/2\}\right]
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), where \(c>0\) depends only on \(q\) and \(L\).

It remains to return to the fixed-\(n\) experiment.  Membership of every packing regression in the radius-\(L\) H\"older ball gives \(\lVert\mu_{P_\omega}\rVert_\infty\leq L\), so the loss of the zero default on \(\{N<n\}\) is at most \(L\).  On \(\{N\geq n\}\), the selected data have law \(P_\omega^{\otimes n}\) and are processed by the original map \(T_n\).  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{Pois}}(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 +L\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-tN}\), with \(t=\log2\), gives
\[
 \Pr\{N<n\}
 \leq e^{tn}\mathbb E[e^{-tN}]
 =\exp\{-n(1-\log2)\}
 =o(a_n).
\]
Combining the last three displays and decreasing \(c\) if necessary proves, uniformly over the arbitrary rule \(\widehat\mu_n\),
\[
 \sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(S)}
 |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_{P_\omega}(x)|
 \right]
 \geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

The packing lemma verifies the independent-sampling, compact-support, density-positivity, rectifiable-boundary, H\"older-regression, and continuous-variance conditions required for every \(P_\omega\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\).  Hence the supremum over the full class is no smaller than the supremum over this finite family.  Taking the infimum over \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) gives
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\geq c a_n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Finally, \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\), while \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}distance\text{-}risk}\) identifies the expanded infimum--supremum display with \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).  Dividing by \(a_n\) and taking lower limits proves the theorem with \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}=c\).  Because the whole finite family lies in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), this is a same-class converse and not a transfer from a treatment-effect or two-sided-thickness subclass.
