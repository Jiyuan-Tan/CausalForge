Put \(a=a_n\).  We give the construction, verify membership, and then calculate the compressed divergence.

Choose a nonnegative radial function \(b\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\) which is one on \(B_2(0,1/4)\) and zero off \(B_2(0,1)\).  Choose a continuous function \(\chi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\) such that \(\chi(u)=0\) for \(u\leq K\) and \(\chi(u)=1\) for \(u\geq2K\), where \(K\) will be fixed below.  On polar angles define
\[
 \psi(\theta)=
 \begin{cases}
 \sin(2\theta),&0\leq\theta\leq\pi,\\
 0,&\pi<\theta<2\pi.
 \end{cases}
\]
The endpoint values agree, so this is continuous across the assignment interface.  Direct integration gives
\[
 \int_0^\pi\psi(\theta)\,d\theta=0,
 \qquad
 J:=\int_0^\pi\cos\theta\,\psi(\theta)\,d\theta=\frac43.
\]

First use the rectangle \(S=[-1,1]\times[-1/2,1/2]\), with straight interface \([-1,1]\times\{0\}\), lower half assigned to control, upper half assigned to treatment, and base density \(f_*=1/2\).  Fix a sufficiently small affine slope \(\beta>0\).  Select constants \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), \(K\), \(B\), and \(\eta>0\), in that order, so that
\[
 \frac{\pi}{\beta J K}\leq\varepsilon,
 \qquad B>4K\eta,
\]
and so that the H\"older and divergence inequalities verified below hold.  Set
\[
 \Delta=\eta a,
 \qquad w=Ba^{1/q}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), choose points \(x_j=(u_j,0)\), \(1\leq j\leq M_n\), in the trimmed interface, separated by \(3w\), with their radius-\(w\) disks contained in the interior of \(S\) relative to the two assignment halves.  A regular packing of the interval gives
\[
 M_n\geq cB^{-1}a^{-1/q}=:c_0a^{-1/q}.
\]

For \(r>0\), define
\[
 A_n(r)=-\frac{\pi\Delta}{\beta Jr}\,b(r/w)\chi(r/\Delta),
 \qquad A_n(0)=0.
\]
The cutoff makes \(A_n\) continuous at zero, and
\[
 \sup_{r\geq0}|A_n(r)|
 \leq\frac{\pi}{\beta JK}\leq\varepsilon.
\]
For \(z=x_j+r(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\), put
\[
 d_{j,n}(z)=A_n(r)\psi(\theta)
\]
inside \(B_2(x_j,w)\), and put \(d_{j,n}(z)=0\) outside that disk.  It is continuous at the center, at the disk boundary, and across the straight assignment interface.  The disks are disjoint.

For a vertex \(\omega=(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_{M_n})\in\{-1,1\}^{M_n}\), define
\[
 f_\omega(z)=f_*\left\{1+\sum_{j=1}^{M_n}\omega_jd_{j,n}(z)\right\},
\]
and define the regressions by
\[
 \mu_{0,\omega}(z)=0,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(z)=\beta z_1+Delta\sum_{j=1}^{M_n}\omega_j b\!\left(\frac{z-x_j}{w}\right).
\]
Because the supports are disjoint, \(f_*(1-\varepsilon)\leq f_\omega\leq f_*(1+\varepsilon)\).  Moreover, the perturbation integrates to zero on every treated half-circle and is zero on the control half-circle.  Hence each density integrates to one.  It is continuous and obeys both the one-sided primitive-class upper envelope and, after reducing \(\varepsilon\) if necessary, the displayed two-sided density envelope.

The derivative of order \(k\leq q\) of a regression bump is bounded by
\[
 C\Delta w^{-k}=C\eta B^{-k}a^{1-k/q}\leq C\eta B^{-k}.
\]
Thus sufficiently small \(\beta\) and \(\eta\) put both regressions in the common \(q\)-H\"older ball.  Conditional on \(X=z\), take each potential outcome in \(\{-1,1\}\) with conditional mean \(\mu_{t,\omega}(z)\), independently across arms.  The constants were chosen so that \(|\mu_{t,\omega}|\leq1/4\).  Consequently
\[
 \frac{15}{16}\leq\operatorname{Var}\{Y(t)\mid X=z\}\leq1,
\]
and the centered conditional moment-generating function is at most \(\exp(\lambda^2/2)\).  These bounds imply the required common variance and conditional sub-Gaussian envelopes because \(L\geq4\).  The minimum density and the rectangle's quarter-disk geometry give, uniformly at every interface point and for \(r\leq r_0\),
\[
 P_\omega\{X\in\mathcal A_{t,P_\omega}\cap B_2(x,r)\}
 \geq f_*(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\pi r^2}{4}
 \geq c_-r^2.
\]
All support, partition, assignment, consistency, and smooth-chart conditions are immediate from the construction.  Thus every vertex belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{rect}}\cap\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sm}}\), with common constants.

At its own center, the target separation for adjacent vertices is
\[
 |\tau_{P_\omega}(x_j)-\tau_{P_{\omega^{(j)}}}(x_j)|=2\Delta=2\eta a,
\]
so take \(c_1=2\eta\).  We next verify the radial cancellation.  On the treated half-circle about \(x_j\), the only changing terms are the \(j\)-th terms.  Since \(d\mathcal H^1=r\,d\theta\) and \(\int_0^\pi\psi=0\), flipping \(\omega_j\) does not change \(Q_{1,P,x_j}(r)\).  It plainly does not change the control radial mass either.  In the treated outcome integral, the coefficient of \(\omega_j\), apart from the common positive factor \(f_*r\), is
\[
 \pi\Delta b(r/w)+\beta rA_n(r)J
 =\pi\Delta b(r/w)\{1-\chi(r/\Delta)\}.
\]
The product of the regression bump and density tilt is independent of \(\omega_j\), because \(\omega_j^2=1\), and its angular integral is zero in any event.  Hence both \(Q\) and \(N\), and therefore the joint law of the binary outcome and signed radius, agree for the adjacent vertices whenever \(r\geq2K\Delta\).  They also agree when \(r>w\), where both perturbations vanish.  This proves agreement outside radii of order \(a_n\), as well as agreement of both arm-specific radial mass densities at every radius.

It remains to bound the information inside the uncancelled disk.  On \(0<r<2K\Delta\), the two radial conditional means differ by at most \(2\Delta\), and all conditional probabilities of the two-point outcome lie in \([3/8,5/8]\).  For \(p,p'\in[3/8,5/8]\), the elementary inequality \(\log u\leq u-1\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\{\operatorname{Bern}(p),\operatorname{Bern}(p')\}
 \leq C(p-p')^2.
\]
The common treated radial density in this range is \(f_*\pi r\).  Therefore the one-observation compressed divergence satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\!\left(\mathbb Q_{1,P_\omega,x_j},
 \mathbb Q_{1,P_{\omega^{(j)}},x_j}\right)
 \leq C\Delta^2\int_0^{2K\Delta}r\,dr
 \leq C\Delta^4.
\]
Tensorization for the \(n\) independent compressed observations yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\!\left(\mathbb Q_{n,P_\omega,x_j},
 \mathbb Q_{n,P_{\omega^{(j)}},x_j}\right)
 \leq Cn\Delta^4=C\eta^4\log n.
\]
Since
\[
 \log M_n\geq\frac{1}{8q}\log n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), choose \(\eta\) so that \(C\eta^4\leq\alpha/(8q)\), with any fixed \(0<\alpha<1/8\).  This proves the required divergence bound.

Finally, when \(L>4\), repeat the same construction on \([-1,1]^2\) with \(f_*=1/4\).  The strict slack \(1/4-1/L>0\) permits a positive choice of \(\varepsilon<1-4/L\); increasing \(K\) preserves the cancellation while enforcing that amplitude.  All vertices then satisfy the canonical-square definition of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{straight}}\).  When \(L=4\), this last perturbation is correctly not asserted, because the straight-class lower envelope has no slack.
