Let \(\beta^*_{t,P,x}(h)\) be the population local-polynomial coefficient and let \(\widehat O_{t,x}(h)\) be the centered empirical score at that coefficient.  On
\(\sup_{t,x}\|\widehat\Psi_{t,x}-\Psi_{t,P,x}\|\leq(2L)^{-1}\), the class Gram bound gives \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Psi_{t,x})\geq(2L)^{-1}\), and the normal equations imply
\[
 \|\widehat\beta_{t,x}-\beta^*_{t,P,x}\|\leq2L\|\widehat O_{t,x}(h)\|.
\]
The cited bias result bounds the difference between the two population intercepts and \(\tau_P(x)\) by \(Ch\).  Projection onto \([-2L,2L]\) is one-Lipschitz and the true contrast lies in that interval.  On the complementary Gram event the clipped loss is at most \(4L\); Markov's inequality and the cited expected Gram deviation bound its contribution by the same first stochastic term.  Consequently the cited maximal bounds give
\[
 \sup_P\mathbb E_P\sup_x|\widehat\tau^{12,\mathrm{LP}}_{n,h}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \leq C\left\{h+\sqrt{\log(1/h)\over nh^2}+{\log(1/h)\over n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)}h^2}\right\}.
\]
Put \(h\asymp b_{n,\nu}\).  Since \(h\geq a_n\), the square-root term is \(O(a_n)\); since \(h\geq(\log n/n^{(1+\nu)/(2+\nu)})^{1/3}\), the last term is at most that cube-root rate.  The source bandwidth condition holds, and the whole display is \(O(b_{n,\nu})\), proving the claim.
