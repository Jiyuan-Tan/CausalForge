Fix \(q\geq1\) and \(L\geq4\).  For a fixed \(n\geq2\), take any \(\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}distance\text{-}decision\text{-}class}\), there is one law-independent Borel map
\[
 T_n:(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty))^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R
\]
such that \(\widehat\mu_n(x)=T_n(V_{n,P}(x))\) at every boundary point.  Equivalently, define the point-indexed family by \(T_{n,x}=T_n\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^2\).  Every fixed-\(x\) section is Borel and the family is law-independent.  This is the inclusion part of \(\mathrm{lem:common\text{-}map\text{-}strict\text{-}in\text{-}point\text{-}indexed}\), so
\[
 \mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\subseteq\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}.
\]

We next verify that the ordinary expectation in the Cattaneo--Titiunik--Yu risk agrees with the outer expectation used by the point-indexed risk.  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\), write a full sample point as \(\mathbf z=((y_i,\xi_i):1\leq i\leq n)\), and put
\[
 D_n(x,\mathbf z)=((y_i,\lVert\xi_i-x\rVert_2):1\leq i\leq n),
 \qquad
 \ell_{P,n}(x,\mathbf z)
 =\left|T_n(D_n(x,\mathbf z))-\mu_P(x)\right|.
\]
The map \((x,\mathbf z)\mapsto D_n(x,\mathbf z)\) is Borel because each Euclidean-distance coordinate is continuous.  The map \(T_n\) is Borel by the decision-class definition, and \(\mu_P\) is continuous because it lies in \(\mathcal H^q(\mathcal X_P,L)\).  Hence \(\ell_{P,n}\) is jointly Borel on \(\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)\times(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2)^n\).  The boundary is compact because \(\mathcal X_P\) is compact.  Consequently, for every real \(t\),
\[
 \left\{\mathbf z:
   \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
   \ell_{P,n}(x,\mathbf z)>t
 \right\}
 =\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf z}
 \left\{(x,\mathbf z):
   x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P),\ 
   \ell_{P,n}(x,\mathbf z)>t
 \right\}.
\]
The set on the right before projection is Borel in a product of standard Borel spaces, so its projection is analytic and therefore belongs to the completion of every probability measure on the sample space.  Thus the nonnegative boundary supremum is upper semianalytic, hence universally measurable.  A universally measurable nonnegative function has a measurable version under the completed product law, and the completed expectation of that version equals its outer expectation.  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
   |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_P(x)|
 \right]
 =
 \mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
   \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
   |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_P(x)|
 \right].
\]
This argument uses joint measurability only for the common-map rule; it imposes no joint measurability on an arbitrary member of \(\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\).

The two minimax risks use the same law class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\).  The last equality and the inclusion of decision classes therefore give, for every \(n\geq2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
 &=\inf_{\widehat\mu_n\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}
   \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)}
   \mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
     \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
     |\widehat\mu_n(x)-\mu_P(x)|
   \right]\\
 &\geq
   \inf_{\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm{PI}}\in\mathcal T_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}
   \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)}
   \mathbb E_P^*\!\left[
     \sup_{x\in\operatorname{bd}(\mathcal X_P)}
     |\widehat\mu_{n,P}^{\mathrm{PI}}(x)-\mu_P(x)|
   \right]
 =R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The sibling result \(\mathrm{thm:point\text{-}indexed\text{-}distance\text{-}log\text{-}converse}\) supplies a constant \(c_{\mathrm{PI}}(q,L)>0\) satisfying
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{PI}}}{a_n}
 \geq c_{\mathrm{PI}}(q,L).
\]
Monotonicity of \(\liminf\) and the preceding pointwise risk inequality yield
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}}{a_n}
 \geq c_{\mathrm{PI}}(q,L).
\]
Finally, \(a_n=(\log n/n)^{1/4}\) by \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rates}\), while \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) is exactly the infimum--supremum functional displayed in the theorem by \(\mathrm{def:cty\text{-}distance\text{-}risk}\).  Therefore the two displayed lower limits in the statement are equal.  Taking \(c_{\mathrm{CTY}}(q,L)=c_{\mathrm{PI}}(q,L)>0\) proves the claim.  Because no law class was changed in the comparison, this is a converse on the exact class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NP}}(L,q)\); it neither invokes an Assumption~2 upper theorem nor asserts a matched two-sided frontier.
