We separate the boundary cases from the nondegenerate argument. If \(B=0\), every outcome in either class is identically zero, so the observed data, the TTE, both SNIPE estimators, and both minimax risks are zero. If \(d=0\), \({\rm ass:bounded-degree}\) makes every \(N_i\) empty. Hence every \(Y_i\) is assignment-invariant, \(\tau_n=0\), and \({\rm def:zero-degree-conventions}\) gives \(A_0=0\) and zero score. All conclusions follow. Assume henceforth that \(B>0\) and \(1\le d\le n\).

First compare the classes. If \(c\in\mathcal C_{n,d,\beta}(B)\), then for every \(i\) and \(z\),
\[
|Y_i(z)|
\le\sum_{S\subseteq N_i}|c_{i,S}|\prod_{j\in S}z_j
\le\sum_{S\subseteq N_i}|c_{i,S}|
\le B.
\]
The low-order restriction is common to the two classes, so
\[
\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\subseteq\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B),
\qquad
R_{n,\ell_1}^\star\le R_{n,\infty}^\star.
\]
The inclusion is strict when \(B>0\) and \(d\ge1\): take a graph with one self-loop and all remaining units isolated, put \(Y_1(z)=B(2z_1-1)\), and put \(Y_i(z)=0\) for \(i\ne1\). This schedule has order one and \(|Y_i(z)|\le B\), whereas its nonzero raw coefficients are \(c_{1,\varnothing}=-B\) and \(c_{1,\{1\}}=2B\), whose absolute mass is \(3B>B\).

We next prove the common upper bound. Put
\[
W_i:=Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}g_{i,\beta,p}(Z),
\qquad q_i:=|N_i|.
\]
For \(q_i=0\), the score and the causal contrast of unit \(i\) are both zero. For \(q_i\ge1\), relabel the coordinates in \(N_i\). The representer identity in \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\), applied in dimension \(q_i\), and \({\rm ass:low-order}\) give
\[
\mathbb E_Z[W_i]=Y_i(\mathbf1)-Y_i(\mathbf0).
\]
Thus \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}\) is design-unbiased for \(\tau_n\) on both classes. Centered-monomial orthogonality under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_Z[g_{i,\beta,p}(Z)^2]
=A_i:=\sum_{r=1}^{\min\{\beta,q_i\}}
\binom{q_i}{r}\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{[p(1-p)]^r}
\le A_d,
\]
because \(q_i\le d\) and each binomial coefficient is nondecreasing in its upper argument.

Every centered function of the binary vector \(Z_{N_i}\) has a unique Bernoulli Fourier expansion
\[
W_i-\mathbb E_ZW_i
=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}a_{i,S}
\prod_{j\in S}(Z_j-p).
\]
On the larger class, \(|Y_i(Z)|\le B\), so orthogonality yields
\[
\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}a_{i,S}^2[p(1-p)]^{|S|}
=\operatorname{Var}_Z(W_i)
\le\mathbb E_Z[W_i^2]
\le B^2A_i
\le B^2A_d.
\]
For nonempty \(S\), let \(I_S:=\{i:S\subseteq N_i\}\). Choose any \(j\in S\). The out-degree part of \({\rm ass:bounded-degree}\), equivalently the one-coordinate charge in \({\rm lem:overlap-count}\), gives
\[
|I_S|\le\#\{i:j\in N_i\}\le d.
\]
A second use of orthogonality and Cauchy--Schwarz now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_Z\!\left(\sum_{i\in V_n}W_i\right)
&=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}[p(1-p)]^{|S|}
\left(\sum_{i\in I_S}a_{i,S}\right)^2\\
&\le d\sum_{i\in V_n}\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}
a_{i,S}^2[p(1-p)]^{|S|}\\
&\le ndB^2A_d.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, on either class,
\[
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]
\le B^2\frac{dA_d}{n}.
\]
On the bounded-outcome class,
\[
|\tau_n|
\le\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}
\{|Y_i(\mathbf1)|+|Y_i(\mathbf0)|\}
\le2B.
\]
Projection onto \([-2B,2B]\) therefore cannot increase distance to \(\tau_n\), and the squared distance between two points of this interval is at most \(16B^2\). Hence
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_{n,\infty}^{\mathrm{up}}-\tau_n)^2]
\le B^2\min\!\left\{\frac{dA_d}{n},16\right\}
\le16B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.
\]
The same argument on the coefficient-mass class uses \(|\tau_n|\le B\) and projection onto \([-B,B]\), giving
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{up}}-\tau_n)^2]
\le4B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.
\]
Thus the theorem's upper claims hold with, for example, \(C_{\beta,p}=16\) before the later binomial conversion.

There is an exact equality behind the unclipped upper constant. Suppose \(d\mid n\), put \(m=n/d\), and take the disjoint union of \(m\) complete directed \(d\)-blocks. The preceding variance bound gives an upper bound \(B^2dA_d/n=B^2A_d/m\) for the global supremum on either class. For the reverse inequality, take the fixed schedule \(Y_i(z)\equiv B\) for every unit. It belongs to both classes and has \(\tau_n=0\). All units in block \(b\) share the score \(g_d(Z_b)\), so
\[
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}
=\frac{Bd}{n}\sum_{b=1}^m g_d(Z_b).
\]
The block assignments are independent, \(\mathbb E_Z[g_d(Z_b)]=0\), and \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\) gives \(\mathbb E_Z[g_d(Z_b)^2]=A_d\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]
=\frac{B^2d^2mA_d}{n^2}
=\frac{B^2A_d}{m}
=\frac{B^2dA_d}{n}.
\]
Since this graph and schedule are admissible, both global suprema equal the common upper bound.

It remains to establish the common converse. Use the construction in \({\rm def:block-family}\) and abbreviate
\[
\kappa:=\min\!\left\{(2H_{\beta,p})^{-1},(4\pi)^{-1}\right\},
\qquad
\delta=\kappa B\min\!\left\{1,\sqrt{A_d/m}\right\}.
\]
Every active vertex of the block graph has in-degree and out-degree exactly \(d\), including its loop, and every inactive vertex has both degrees zero. Hence the graph is in \(\mathcal G_{n,d}\). By \({\rm def:exposed-order}\), every coefficient of \(g_d\), and hence of \(h_d=g_d/A_d\), above order \(k_\star\) vanishes. Thus the block schedules have order at most \(k_\star\le\beta\). Moreover, for an active \(i\in V_b\), \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\), \(|U_b|\le B/2\), and \(\kappa H_{\beta,p}\le1/2\) give
\[
\sum_{T\subseteq V_b}|c_{i,T}|
\le |U_b|+\delta\sum_T|h_{d,T}|
\le\frac B2+\kappa BH_{\beta,p}
\le B.
\]
Inactive outcomes vanish. Thus each prior-realized schedule belongs to \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\), and hence also to \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)\).

For every active \(i\in V_b\),
\[
Y_i(z)=U_b+\sigma\delta h_d(z_{V_b}).
\]
Since \(h_d(\mathbf1_d)-h_d(\mathbf0_d)=1\), every schedule under \(\Pi_\sigma\) has
\[
\tau_n=\sigma\rho\delta,
\]
so the TTE separation is \(2\rho\delta\). Let \(\psi_s:=\sqrt{f_s}\), extended by zero outside \([-s,s]\). Direct differentiation and integration give
\[
\|\psi_s'\|_2^2
=\frac{\pi^2}{4s^3}\int_{-s}^{s}
\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi u}{2s}\right)du
=\frac{\pi^2}{4s^2}.
\]
The fundamental theorem of calculus and Minkowski's inequality imply
\[
\|\psi_s(\,\cdot-a)-\psi_s(\,\cdot-b)\|_2
\le|a-b|\,\|\psi_s'\|_2.
\]
Conditional on a block assignment \(Z_b=z\), the observed repeated block outcome is a translate of \(f_s\); the two signs differ by \(2\delta h_d(z)\). With \(s=B/2\) and \(\mathbb E_Z[h_d(Z)^2]=A_d^{-1}\), the one-block squared Hellinger distance satisfies
\[
H^2(P_{+,b},P_{-,b})
\le\frac{4\pi^2\delta^2}{B^2A_d}.
\]
Hellinger affinity tensorizes over the \(m\) independent blocks, and \(1-\prod_b a_b\le\sum_b(1-a_b)\). Therefore
\[
H^2(P_+,P_-)
\le\frac{4\pi^2m\delta^2}{B^2A_d}
\le4\pi^2\kappa^2
\le\frac14,
\qquad
\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac12.
\]
For any estimator, thresholding at zero tests the two signs. Put \(\theta:=\rho\delta\). On a testing error the squared estimation error is at least \(\theta^2\), whence
\[
\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_\sigma}[(\widehat\tau_n-\sigma\theta)^2]
\ge\frac{\theta^2}{2}
\{1-\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}
\ge\frac{\rho^2\delta^2}{4}.
\]
Because both priors are supported on the smaller model class, this lower-bounds both minimax risks. Finally, \(m=\lfloor n/d\rfloor\) implies
\[
\frac12\le\rho=\frac{md}{n}\le1,
\qquad
\frac{A_d}{m}=\frac{dA_d/n}{\rho}.
\]
It follows that
\[
R_{n,\ell_1}^\star(d,\beta,p,B)
\ge\frac{\kappa^2}{16}B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.
\]
Together with class inclusion, the same lower bound applies before \(R_{n,\infty}^\star\). Taking \(c_{\beta,p}=\kappa^2/16\), and enlarging the upper constant if necessary, proves the \(A_d\)-scale sandwich. Finally, \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\) gives \(A_d\asymp_{\beta,p}\binom d{k_\star}\). Substitution proves the binomial formulation and, together with the unclipped variance bound, the nonsaturated attainment claim.
