For every integer \(r\ge 1\), the definition of the Bernoulli contrast gives
\[
\Delta_r(1/2)=(1-1/2)^r-(-1/2)^r
=2^{-r}\{1-(-1)^r\}
=\begin{cases}
2^{1-r},&r\text{ odd},\\
0,&r\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Because \(d\ge1\) and \(\beta\ge1\), the integer \(\min\{\beta,d\}\) is at least one. Hence the nonvanishing indices in the maximum defining \(k_\star\) in \({\rm def:exposed-order}\) are exactly the odd integers in \(\{1,\ldots,\min\{\beta,d\}\}\). It follows that \(k_\star\) is the largest odd integer no greater than \(\min\{\beta,d\}\).

Now specialize to \(\beta=1\). Then \(k_\star=1\), and therefore
\[
d\binom{d}{k_\star}=d\binom d1=d^2.
\]
Applying \({\rm thm:degree-frontier}\) with \(p=1/2\) yields constants \(0<c_{1,1/2}\le C_{1,1/2}<\infty\), independent of \(n\), \(d\), and \(B\), such that
\[
c_{1,1/2}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n}\right\}
\le R_n^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
\le C_{1,1/2}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n}\right\}.
\]
This is precisely
\[
R_n^\star(d,1,1/2,B)\asymp B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n}\right\},
\]
including \(B=0\), and proves the claim.
