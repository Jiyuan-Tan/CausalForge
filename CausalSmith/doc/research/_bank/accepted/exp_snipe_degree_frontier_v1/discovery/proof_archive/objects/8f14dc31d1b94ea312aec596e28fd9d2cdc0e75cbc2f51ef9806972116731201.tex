Set \(p=1/2\). Directly from the definition of the Bernoulli contrast,
\[
\Delta_r(1/2)
=(1/2)^r-(-1/2)^r
=2^{-r}\{1-(-1)^r\}
=\begin{cases}
2^{1-r},&r\text{ odd},\\
0,&r\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Because \(1\le d\) and \(\beta\ge1\), the index set in \({\rm def:exposed-order}\) is nonempty and contains \(r=1\). The preceding display shows that its nonzero contrasts are exactly those of odd order. Therefore \(k_\star\) is the largest odd integer not exceeding \(\min\{\beta,d\}\).

In the specialization \(\beta=1\), this gives \(k_\star=1\). Applying \({\rm thm:degree-frontier}\) and using \(\binom d1=d\), for every \(B\ge0\) we obtain
\[
R_n^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
\asymp B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d\binom d1}{n}\right\}
=B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n}\right\}.
\]
This is the claimed fair-coin reduction.
