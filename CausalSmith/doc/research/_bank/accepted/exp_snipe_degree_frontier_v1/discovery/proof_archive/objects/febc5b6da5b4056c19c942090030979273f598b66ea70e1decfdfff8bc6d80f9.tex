The cases \(B=0\) and \(d=0\) are immediate. If \(B=0\), \({\rm ass:bounded-coefficient-mass}\) forces every coefficient to vanish. If \(d=0\), \({\rm ass:bounded-degree}\) forces every neighborhood to be empty, every causal contrast is zero, and the score and both SNIPE estimators are zero under \({\rm def:zero-degree-conventions}\). Hence assume \(B>0\) and \(1\le d\le n\).

For the upper bound, put
\[
W_i:=Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}g_{i,\beta,p}(Z),
\qquad q_i:=|N_i|.
\]
If \(q_i=0\), then both \(W_i\) and \(Y_i(\mathbf1)-Y_i(\mathbf0)\) are zero. If \(q_i\ge1\), relabeling the coordinates in \(N_i\) and applying the representer calculation in \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\) in dimension \(q_i\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_Z[W_i]=Y_i(\mathbf1)-Y_i(\mathbf0).
\]
Thus \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}\) is design-unbiased for \(\tau_n\). The coefficient-mass condition gives
\[
|Y_i(z)|\le\sum_{S\subseteq N_i}|c_{i,S}|\le B
\]
for every \(i\) and \(z\). Centered-monomial orthogonality under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\) gives the exact local score energy
\[
\mathbb E_Z[g_{i,\beta,p}(Z)^2]
=A_i:=\sum_{r=1}^{\min\{\beta,q_i\}}\binom{q_i}{r}
\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{[p(1-p)]^r}
\le A_d,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(q_i\le d\) and termwise monotonicity of \(\binom{q_i}{r}\) in \(q_i\).

Every centered function of the binary vector \(Z_{N_i}\) has a unique Bernoulli Fourier expansion
\[
W_i-\mathbb E_ZW_i
=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}a_{i,S}
\prod_{j\in S}(Z_j-p).
\]
By orthogonality and the outcome bound,
\[
\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}a_{i,S}^2[p(1-p)]^{|S|}
=\operatorname{Var}_Z(W_i)
\le\mathbb E_Z[W_i^2]
\le B^2A_i.
\]
For nonempty \(S\), let \(I_S:=\{i\in V_n:S\subseteq N_i\}\). Choosing any \(j\in S\), the out-degree part of \({\rm ass:bounded-degree}\), equivalently the single-coordinate charge isolated by \({\rm lem:overlap-count}\), gives
\[
|I_S|\le\#\{i\in V_n:j\in N_i\}\le d.
\]
A second use of orthogonality followed by Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_Z\!\left(\sum_{i\in V_n}W_i\right)
&=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}[p(1-p)]^{|S|}
\left(\sum_{i\in I_S}a_{i,S}\right)^2\\
&\le d\sum_{i\in V_n}\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}
a_{i,S}^2[p(1-p)]^{|S|}\\
&\le ndB^2A_d.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]
\le B^2\frac{dA_d}{n}
\le C_{\beta,p}^{(1)}B^2\frac{d\binom d{k_\star}}{n},
\]
where the last step is \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\). Moreover,
\[
|\tau_n|
\le\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}|c_{i,S}|
\le B.
\]
Projection onto \([-B,B]\) cannot increase distance to \(\tau_n\), while the squared distance between two points of this interval is at most \(4B^2\). Combining these two bounds gives
\[
\sup_{(G_n,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{up}}-\tau_n)^2]
\le C_{\beta,p}^{(2)}B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d\binom d{k_\star}}{n}\right\}.
\]
The minimax upper inequality follows by using this single data-dependent estimator. In the nonsaturated regime, the unclipped variance display and the lower bound below show that unclipped SNIPE has the same rate.

For the lower bound, use \({\rm def:block-family}\) and abbreviate
\[
\kappa:=\min\!\left\{(2H_{\beta,p})^{-1},(4\pi)^{-1}\right\}.
\]
By \({\rm lem:block-family-admissibility}\), both priors are supported on the announced model class. Every active unit in block \(b\) has
\[
Y_i(z)=U_b+\sigma\delta h_d(z_{V_b}).
\]
Since \(L_d(h_d)=1\), its all-one-versus-all-zero contrast is \(\sigma\delta\). Inactive units contribute zero, so every schedule under \(\Pi_\sigma\) has
\[
\tau_n=\sigma\rho\delta.
\]
The TTE separation is therefore exactly \(2\rho\delta\).

Let \(\psi_s:=\sqrt{f_s}\), extended by zero outside \([-s,s]\). Because the cosine vanishes at both endpoints, \(\psi_s\) has a square-integrable weak derivative, and direct integration gives
\[
\|\psi_s'\|_2^2
=\frac{\pi^2}{4s^3}\int_{-s}^{s}
\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi u}{2s}\right)du
=\frac{\pi^2}{4s^2}.
\]
For any translations \(a,b\), the fundamental theorem of calculus and Minkowski's inequality give
\[
\|\psi_s(\,\cdot-a)-\psi_s(\,\cdot-b)\|_2
\le |a-b|\,\|\psi_s'\|_2.
\]
Conditional on a block assignment \(Z_b=z\), the repeated observed block outcome is \(U_b+\sigma\delta h_d(z)\). Hence the conditional laws under the two signs are translates of \(f_s\) separated by \(2\delta h_d(z)\). With
\[
H^2(P,Q):=\int(\sqrt{dP}-\sqrt{dQ})^2,
\]
averaging over the common assignment law and using \(s=B/2\) and \(\mathbb E_Z[h_d(Z)^2]=A_d^{-1}\) yields
\[
H^2(P_{+,b},P_{-,b})
\le\frac{4\pi^2\delta^2}{B^2A_d}.
\]
Hellinger affinity tensorizes over the \(m\) independent active blocks, and \(1-\prod_b a_b\le\sum_b(1-a_b)\) for \(a_b\in[0,1]\). The inactive observations are a common factor. Therefore
\[
H^2(P_+,P_-)
\le\frac{4\pi^2m\delta^2}{B^2A_d}
\le4\pi^2\kappa^2
\le\frac14.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le H(P_+,P_-)
\le\frac12.
\]

For an arbitrary estimator, thresholding at zero produces a test of the two signs. With \(\theta:=\rho\delta\), a testing error forces squared estimation error at least \(\theta^2\). Hence the elementary two-point testing inequality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_\sigma}[(\widehat\tau_n-\sigma\theta)^2]
&\ge\frac{\theta^2}{2}
\{1-\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}\\
&\ge\frac{\rho^2\delta^2}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because the priors are supported on \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\), their mixture risks cannot exceed the worst-case risk entering \(R_n^\star\). Now \(m=\lfloor n/d\rfloor\), so
\[
\frac12\le\rho=\frac{md}{n}\le1,
\qquad
\frac{A_d}{m}=\frac{dA_d/n}{\rho}.
\]
Using the definition of \(\delta\) therefore gives
\[
R_n^\star(d,\beta,p,B)
\ge\frac{\kappa^2}{16}B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.
\]
Finally, define
\[
a_{\beta,p}:=
\min_{\substack{1\le r\le\beta\\\Delta_r(p)\ne0}}
\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{[p(1-p)]^r}>0.
\]
The order-\(k_\star\) summand gives \(A_d\ge a_{\beta,p}\binom d{k_\star}\). Thus
\[
R_n^\star(d,\beta,p,B)
\ge c_{\beta,p}B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d\binom d{k_\star}}{n}\right\},
\qquad
c_{\beta,p}:=\frac{\kappa^2}{16}\min\{1,a_{\beta,p}\}>0.
\]
Enlarging the upper constant if necessary ensures \(c_{\beta,p}\le C_{\beta,p}\), proving all three risk inequalities. The construction also proves the claimed exact separation, Hellinger bound, and no-divisibility relation.

As a boundary check, when \(\beta=1\) and \(p=1/2\), one has \(A_d=4d\). On a complete directed \(d\)-block the constant schedule \(Y_i\equiv B\) makes the unnormalized block error equal to \(Bd\,g_d(Z_b)\), whose variance is \(B^2d^2A_d\). Thus block replication produces the \(d^2/n\) risk scale without observation noise, in agreement with both sides of the theorem.
