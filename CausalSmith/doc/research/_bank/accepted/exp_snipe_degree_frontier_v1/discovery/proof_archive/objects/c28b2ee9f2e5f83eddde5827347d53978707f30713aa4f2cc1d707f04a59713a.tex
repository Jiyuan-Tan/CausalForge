At \(p=1/2\), direct substitution into \({\rm def:exposed-order}\) gives
\[
\Delta_r(1/2)=2^{-r}-(-1)^r2^{-r}
=\begin{cases}2^{1-r},&r\text{ odd},\\0,&r\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]
Thus the largest exposed order is exactly the largest odd integer at most \(\min\{\beta,d\}\). For an odd \(r\),
\[
\frac{\Delta_r(1/2)^2}{[(1/2)(1-1/2)]^r}
=\frac{2^{2-2r}}{4^{-r}}=4,
\]
whereas an even order contributes zero. Substitution into \({\rm def:block-score-energy}\) yields the exact identity
\[
A_d=4\sum_{\substack{1\le r\le\min\{\beta,d\}\\r\text{ odd}}}\binom dr.
\]
When \(\beta=1\), only \(r=1\) is present, so \(A_d=4d\). Apply \({\rm thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier}\) and \({\rm def:two-class-minimax-risks}\): its \(A_d\)-scale sandwich becomes
\[
R_n^\star(d,1,1/2,B)=R_{n,\ell_1}^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
\asymp R_{n,\infty}^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
\asymp B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{4d^2}{n}\right\}.
\]
The factor four is absorbed by the two-sided absolute constants, proving the displayed frontier. If \(d\mid n\), the exact complete-block clause of that theorem gives \(B^2dA_d/n=4B^2d^2/n\).
