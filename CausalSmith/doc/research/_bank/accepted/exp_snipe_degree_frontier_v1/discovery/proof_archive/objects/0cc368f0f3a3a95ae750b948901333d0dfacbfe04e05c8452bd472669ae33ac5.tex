Fix an index \(t\), and abbreviate \(n=n_t\), \(d=d_t\), \(m=m_t=n/d\), \(A=A_d\), \(g=g_d\), and \(\mathcal P=\mathcal P_d\). For \(S\subseteq V_b\), write \(Z_S=\prod_{j\in S}Z_j\), and identify the coordinates of each block with \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\). By \({\rm def:local-linear-class}\), every admissible weight \(w_i:=w_{i,t}\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_Z[w_i(Z_b)f(Z_b)]=L_d(f),\qquad f\in\mathcal P.
\]
Indeed, this follows by linearity from the constant and nonempty-monomial moment restrictions in that definition. By \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\), the same identity holds with \(g\) in place of \(w_i\), and \(\lVert g\rVert_2^2=A\). Consequently every admissible weight has the orthogonal decomposition
\[
w_i=g+u_i,\qquad u_i\perp\mathcal P.
\tag{1}
\]
All norms below are in the block space \(L^2(P_Z)\).

We first construct an attaining estimator. Set \(w_i=g\) for every unit. For \(S\subseteq V_b\) with \(|S|\le\bar\beta_d\), define
\[
X_S:=g(Z_b)Z_S-\mathbf 1\{S\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
If \(S=\varnothing\), then \(\lVert X_S\rVert_2^2=A\). If \(S\ne\varnothing\), the representer identity, \(Z_S^2=Z_S\), and \(0\le Z_S\le1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert X_S\rVert_2^2
&=\mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)^2Z_S]-2\mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)Z_S]+1\\
&=\mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)^2Z_S]-1
\le \mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)^2]=A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
For an admissible unit-level polynomial \(f_i=\sum_Sc_{i,S}Z_S\), the triangle inequality and the coefficient-mass restriction in \({\rm def:local-linear-class}\) therefore imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert gf_i-L_d(f_i)\rVert_2
&\le\sum_S|c_{i,S}|\,\lVert X_S\rVert_2
\le B\sqrt A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\), assignment subvectors belonging to distinct blocks are independent. Each block error is centered by the defining moment restrictions. Hence block variances add, and applying the triangle inequality within each block to (3) yields
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_g-\tau_n)^2]
\le \frac1{n^2}\sum_{b=1}^m(dB\sqrt A)^2
=\frac{B^2A}{m}.
\tag{4}
\]

For the matching converse, take the admissible constant schedule \(f_i\equiv B\) for every unit. It has coefficient mass \(B\) and \(L_d(f_i)=0\). For arbitrary admissible weights, (1), together with \(g\in\mathcal P\) and \(u_i\perp\mathcal P\), gives in each block
\[
\left\lVert\sum_{i\in V_b}w_i\right\rVert_2^2
=\left\lVert dg+\sum_{i\in V_b}u_i\right\rVert_2^2
=d^2A+\left\lVert\sum_{i\in V_b}u_i\right\rVert_2^2
\ge d^2A.
\tag{5}
\]
Independence across blocks then implies
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_n)^2]
\ge\frac{B^2}{n^2}\sum_{b=1}^m d^2A
=\frac{B^2A}{m}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (4) and (6), and restoring subscripts, proves for every \(t\) that
\[
R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}=\frac{B^2A_{d_t}}{m_t}
=\frac{B^2d_tA_{d_t}}{n_t}.
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(B>0\), \(d_t\ge1\), and \(A_{d_t}>0\) by \(\Delta_1(p)=1\), the denominator in the asserted ratio is positive; thus (7) also proves that this ratio equals one.

We next prove the exact finite extremal formula. For a fixed block \(V_b\), define its unscaled centered error by
\[
E_b(c,w):=\sum_{i\in V_b}\{w_i(Z_b)f_i(Z_b)-L_d(f_i)\}.
\]
As a function of the block coefficient vectors \((c_{i,S})_{i\in V_b}\), the map \(\lVert E_b(c,w)\rVert_2^2\) is convex. Each unit's feasible coefficient vector lies in a finite-dimensional \(\ell_1\)-ball of radius \(B\), which is the convex hull of the vectors \(\varepsilon_iB e_{S_i}\), where \(\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}\) and \(|S_i|\le\bar\beta_d\). The product of the unit-level balls is the convex hull of the corresponding product points. Jensen's inequality therefore bounds the value at every feasible coefficient array by the largest value at such a product point. At that point,
\[
E_b(c,w)=B\sum_{i\in V_b}\varepsilon_i
\{w_i(Z_b)Z_{S_i}-\mathbf1(S_i\ne\varnothing)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Conversely, every such product point is feasible, so this bound is attained. The block errors are centered and independent under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\), and the coefficient vectors may be chosen independently across blocks. Thus the supremum of the sum of block variances is the sum of their suprema. Equation (8) gives exactly
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_{w,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2]
=\frac{B^2}{n_t^2}\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}\Psi_{b,t}(w).
\tag{9}
\]
In the maximization defining \(\Psi_{b,t}\), choose \(S_i=\varnothing\) and \(\varepsilon_i=1\) for every \(i\in V_b\). Equation (5) then yields
\[
\Psi_{b,t}(w)\ge d_t^2A_{d_t}.
\tag{10}
\]
This proves all finite assertions.

By definition, a sequence is asymptotically minimax precisely when its worst-case risk divided by \(R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}\) tends to one. Equations (7), (9), and (10) imply, without requiring \(d_t\to\infty\),
\[
\frac{\sup_c\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_{w,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2]}
{R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}}
=1+\frac{1}{m_td_t^2A_{d_t}}
\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}\{\Psi_{b,t}(w)-d_t^2A_{d_t}\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Every summand in the last numerator is nonnegative by (10). Hence the risk ratio tends to one if and only if the normalized excess in the theorem tends to zero. This proves the asserted characterization for every admissible sequence.

We now prove the sufficient condition, still without assuming that \(d_t\) diverges. Suppress \(t\) again and set
\[
a_b^2:=\sum_{i\in V_b}\lVert u_i\rVert_2^2,
\qquad U:=\sum_{b=1}^m a_b^2.
\]
Every admissible polynomial satisfies \(|f_i(Z_b)|\le B\), because its raw coefficient mass is at most \(B\). Therefore Cauchy--Schwarz over the units gives
\[
\left\lVert\sum_{i\in V_b}u_if_i\right\rVert_2
\le B\sum_{i\in V_b}\lVert u_i\rVert_2
\le B\sqrt d\,a_b.
\tag{12}
\]
By (3), the part of the block error involving \(g\) has norm at most \(dB\sqrt A\). Combining this with (12), summing squared block bounds, and using \(\sum_ba_b\le\sqrt{mU}\), we obtain
\[
1\le
\frac{\sup_c\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_n)^2]}{B^2A/m}
\le1+2\sqrt{\frac{U}{mdA}}+\frac{U}{mdA}
=\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{U}{nA}}\right)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
The lower inequality is (6). By (1),
\[
U=\sum_{i\in V_n}\mathbb E_Z[(w_i(Z_{b(i)})-g(Z_{b(i)}))^2].
\]
Thus the normalized \(L^2\) condition in the theorem makes the right-hand side of (13) tend to one. The lower bound in (13) then proves asymptotic minimaxity.

It remains to prove non-necessity under the additional hypothesis \(d_t\to\infty\). Since \(\beta\) is fixed, for every sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
\dim(\mathcal P_{d_t})=\sum_{r=0}^{\beta}\binom{d_t}{r}<2^{d_t}=\dim L^2(P_Z).
\tag{14}
\]
The last equality holds because \(p\in(0,1)\) gives full support on the block assignment space. Hence \(\mathcal P_{d_t}^{\perp}\) contains a nonzero function. Also \(A_{d_t}>0\) because \(\Delta_1(p)=1\). Choose \(v_{d_t}\in\mathcal P_{d_t}^{\perp}\) normalized so that
\[
\lVert v_{d_t}\rVert_2^2=d_tA_{d_t}.
\tag{15}
\]
For every block and all sufficiently large \(t\), set
\[
u_1=v_{d_t},\qquad u_2=-v_{d_t},\qquad u_i=0\quad(i\ge3),
\qquad w_i=g_{d_t}+u_i.
\tag{16}
\]
There are at least two units per block eventually because \(d_t\to\infty\), and the orthogonality in (15) verifies every moment restriction in \({\rm def:local-linear-class}\). For arbitrary admissible unit polynomials, the additional block error contributed by (16) is \(v_{d_t}(f_1-f_2)\), whose norm is at most \(2B\sqrt{d_tA_{d_t}}\). The canonical \(g_{d_t}\)-part has norm at most \(d_tB\sqrt{A_{d_t}}\) by (3). Consequently
\[
1\le
\frac{\sup_c\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_{n_t})^2]}
{B^2A_{d_t}/m_t}
\le\left(1+\frac{2}{\sqrt{d_t}}\right)^2\longrightarrow1.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus these weights are asymptotically minimax. On the other hand, (15) and (16) give, for every sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
\frac{1}{n_tA_{d_t}}\sum_{i\in V_{n_t}}
\lVert w_i-g_{d_t}\rVert_2^2
=\frac{m_t(2d_tA_{d_t})}{m_td_tA_{d_t}}=2.
\tag{18}
\]
A relabeling of coordinates within a block preserves the product Bernoulli law, the symmetric function \(g_{d_t}\), and the sum of squared distances in (18). It therefore cannot make the sufficient condition hold. This proves the non-necessity and the distance-exactly-two witness under precisely the stated divergence hypothesis.
