Fix an index \(t\) satisfying the finite block-graph hypotheses, and suppress \(t\) by writing \(n=n_t\), \(d=d_t\), \(m=m_t=n/d\), \(A=A_d\), \(g=g_d\), and \(\mathcal P=\mathcal P_d\). For \(S\subseteq V_b\), write \(Z_S=\prod_{j\in S}Z_j\). By \({\rm def:local-linear-class}\), every admissible weight \(w_i\) obeys
\[
\mathbb E_Z[w_i(Z_b)f(Z_b)]=L_d(f),\qquad f\in\mathcal P.
\]
The representer identity in \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\) gives the same equality with \(g\) in place of \(w_i\). Hence
\[
w_i=g+u_i,\qquad u_i\perp\mathcal P,
\]
and that lemma also gives \(\|g\|_2^2=A\).

We first exhibit an estimator attaining the asserted finite risk. Set \(w_i=g\) for every unit. For every \(S\subseteq V_b\) with \(|S|\le\bar\beta_d\), define
\[
X_S:=g(Z_b)Z_S-\mathbf 1(S\ne\varnothing).
\]
When \(S=\varnothing\), \(\|X_S\|_2^2=A\). When \(S\ne\varnothing\), the representer identity, the identity \(Z_S^2=Z_S\), and \(0\le Z_S\le1\) imply
\[
\|X_S\|_2^2
=\mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)^2Z_S]-1
\le \mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)^2]
=A.
\]
For an admissible unit-level polynomial \(f_i=\sum_Sc_{i,S}Z_S\), the coefficient-mass restriction therefore yields
\[
\|g f_i-L_d(f_i)\|_2
\le\sum_S|c_{i,S}|\,\|X_S\|_2
\le B\sqrt A.
\]
The centered errors from distinct blocks are independent under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\). Applying the triangle inequality within each block and then adding the block variances gives
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_g-\tau_n)^2]
\le \frac1{n^2}\sum_{b=1}^m(dB\sqrt A)^2
=\frac{B^2A}{m}.
\]

For the converse, consider the admissible constant schedule \(f_i\equiv B\) for every unit. Its coefficient mass is \(B\), and its total-treatment effect is zero. For an arbitrary admissible collection of weights, orthogonality gives in each block
\[
\left\|\sum_{i\in V_b}w_i\right\|_2^2
=\left\|dg+\sum_{i\in V_b}u_i\right\|_2^2
=d^2A+\left\|\sum_{i\in V_b}u_i\right\|_2^2
\ge d^2A.
\]
Independence across blocks consequently implies
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_n)^2]
\ge \frac{B^2}{n^2}\sum_{b=1}^m d^2A
=\frac{B^2A}{m}.
\]
Together with the attaining choice \(w_i=g\), this proves
\[
R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}=\frac{B^2A_{d_t}}{m_t}
=\frac{B^2d_tA_{d_t}}{n_t}.
\]

We next prove the exact finite characterization. For a fixed block \(V_b\), its centered error is
\[
E_b(c,w):=\sum_{i\in V_b}\left\{w_i(Z_b)f_i(Z_b)-L_d(f_i)\right\}.
\]
The map from the block's coefficient vectors \((c_{i,S})\) to \(\|E_b(c,w)\|_2^2\) is a convex quadratic function. Each unit's feasible coefficient vector belongs to a finite-dimensional \(\ell_1\)-ball of radius \(B\), whose extreme points are \(\varepsilon_iB e_{S_i}\), where \(\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}\) and \(|S_i|\le\bar\beta_d\). Every point of the product of these balls is a convex combination of product extreme points, so convexity shows that the maximum is attained at such a product extreme point. At that point,
\[
E_b(c,w)=B\sum_{i\in V_b}\varepsilon_i
\left\{w_i(Z_b)Z_{S_i}-\mathbf1(S_i\ne\varnothing)\right\}.
\]
Because block assignments are independent, the block errors are centered and their variances add. Moreover, the coefficient schedules may be selected independently across blocks. It follows exactly that
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_n)^2]
=\frac{B^2}{n^2}\sum_{b=1}^m\Psi_b(w).
\]
Choosing \(S_i=\varnothing\) and \(\varepsilon_i=1\) for every \(i\in V_b\), and using the preceding orthogonal decomposition, gives
\[
\Psi_b(w)\ge\left\|\sum_{i\in V_b}w_i\right\|_2^2\ge d^2A.
\]
Thus the finite formula and lower bound for \(\Psi_{b,t}\) hold for every \(t\) under the block-graph hypotheses.

Now assume additionally that \(d_t\to\infty\). The exact risk formula and the exact minimax identity imply
\[
\frac{\sup_c\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_{w,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2]}
{R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}}
=\frac{1}{m_td_t^2A_{d_t}}\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}\Psi_{b,t}(w)
=1+\frac{1}{m_td_t^2A_{d_t}}
\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}\{\Psi_{b,t}(w)-d_t^2A_{d_t}\}.
\]
All summands in the final numerator are nonnegative. Hence the risk ratio tends to one if and only if the displayed normalized excess tends to zero, proving the asymptotic-minimaxity characterization.

To establish the sufficient condition, again suppress \(t\), put \(a_b^2:=\sum_{i\in V_b}\|u_i\|_2^2\), and put \(U:=\sum_ba_b^2\). Since the coefficient-mass restriction implies \(|f_i(Z_b)|\le B\),
\[
\left\|\sum_{i\in V_b}u_i f_i\right\|_2
\le B\sum_{i\in V_b}\|u_i\|_2
\le B\sqrt d\,a_b.
\]
The part using \(g\) has norm at most \(dB\sqrt A\), as already proved. Summing the resulting squared block bounds and applying \(\sum_ba_b\le\sqrt{mU}\) yields
\[
1\le
\frac{\sup_c\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_n)^2]}{B^2A/m}
\le1+2\sqrt{\frac{U}{mdA}}+\frac{U}{mdA}
=\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{U}{nA}}\right)^2.
\]
Since
\[
U=\sum_{i\in V_n}\mathbb E_Z[(w_i(Z_{b(i)})-g(Z_{b(i)}))^2],
\]
the stated averaged \(L^2\) condition is sufficient for asymptotic minimaxity.

It remains to show that this condition is not necessary. Because \(\beta\) is fixed and \(d_t\to\infty\), for every sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
\dim(\mathcal P_{d_t})=\sum_{r=0}^{\beta}\binom{d_t}{r}<2^{d_t}=\dim L^2(P_Z).
\]
The last equality uses the full support supplied by \(p\in(0,1)\). Thus \(\mathcal P_{d_t}^{\perp}\) contains a nonzero element. Since \(A_{d_t}>0\) by \(\Delta_1(p)=1\), choose \(v_{d_t}\in\mathcal P_{d_t}^{\perp}\) with
\[
\|v_{d_t}\|_2^2=d_tA_{d_t}.
\]
In every block set
\[
u_1=v_{d_t},\qquad u_2=-v_{d_t},\qquad u_i=0\quad(i\ge3),
\]
and let \(w_i=g_{d_t}+u_i\). Orthogonality to \(\mathcal P_{d_t}\) verifies every moment restriction in \({\rm def:local-linear-class}\). For arbitrary admissible polynomials \(f_i\), the additional block error is \(v_{d_t}(f_1-f_2)\), and hence its norm is at most \(2B\sqrt{d_tA_{d_t}}\). Combining this with the \(g_{d_t}\)-part gives
\[
1\le
\frac{\sup_c\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_{n_t})^2]}
{B^2A_{d_t}/m_t}
\le\left(1+\frac{2}{\sqrt{d_t}}\right)^2\longrightarrow1.
\]
These weights are therefore asymptotically minimax. On the other hand,
\[
\frac{1}{n_tA_{d_t}}\sum_{i\in V_{n_t}}
\|w_i-g_{d_t}\|_2^2
=\frac{m_t(2d_tA_{d_t})}{m_td_tA_{d_t}}=2
\]
for every sufficiently large \(t\). A coordinate relabeling within a block preserves the product Bernoulli law, the symmetric function \(g_{d_t}\), and the sum of these squared \(L^2\) distances. Thus relabeling cannot restore the sufficient condition. This proves its non-necessity and completes the theorem.
