\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}snipe\_\allowbreak{}degree\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The paper proves, to our knowledge, the first matched noiseless design-based minimax frontier for the finite-population total treatment effect simultaneously over the \(\ell_1\) coefficient-mass class and the uniformly bounded-outcome class, which is strictly larger when \(B>0\) and \(d\ge1\), under unit Bernoulli randomization and bounded-degree \(\beta\)-order neighborhood interference:
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle R_{n,\ell_1}^\star\asymp_{\beta,p}R_{n,\infty}^\star
\asymp_{\beta,p}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}
\asymp_{\beta,p}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d\binom d{k_\star}}{n}\right\}.\)}}
\]
Clipped SNIPE, projected onto \([-2B,2B]\) for the bounded-outcome class, attains the frontier, while unclipped SNIPE attains the linear branch. When \(d\mid n\), complete directed \(d\)-blocks give canonical unclipped SNIPE exact worst-case MSE \(B^2A_d/m=B^2dA_d/n\), so its global variance upper constant one is sharp. At \(p=1/2\), \(A_d=4\sum_{1\le r\le\min\{\beta,d\},\ r\text{ odd}}\binom dr\), and \(\beta=1\) yields \(B^2\min\{1,d^2/n\}\). On complete blocks, the paper additionally solves the \(\ell_1\) block-local linear design-unbiased problem exactly, with finite minimax constant \(B^2A_{d_t}/m_t\) and a complete representer characterization.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Under its \(Y_{\max}\) coefficient-mass normalization, Theorem 1 of \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023} gives a coarse \(d^{\beta+2}/n\)-type stated worst-case SNIPE variance bound. Its Theorem 2 has a degree-free lower scale generated by Gaussian priors with unbounded coefficient mass and unbounded outcomes, so it is not a converse for either bounded class studied here; on the bounded classes the true linear-branch risk instead carries the factor \(dA_d\asymp_{\beta,p}d\binom d{k_\star}\), of order \(d^{k_\star+1}\) when \(k_\star\) is fixed. Thus the printed degree-free diagnosis does not describe bounded-outcome minimax difficulty. To our knowledge, the literature lacked an exact finite minimax constant and a complete optimal-weight characterization for block-local linear design-unbiased estimation on complete directed blocks; that structural question is resolved here rather than left open.

\paragraph{Niche.} The local Bernoulli contrast representer has exact energy \(A_d\), with \(dA_d\asymp_{\beta,p}d\binom d{k_\star}\), where \(k_\star\) is the largest interaction order exposed by the all-one-versus-all-zero contrast. This separates within-neighborhood contrast complexity from the single additional out-degree overlap charge, works over both coefficient-mass-bounded and uniformly outcome-bounded schedules, and yields exact finite geometry on complete blocks.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves the simultaneous sandwich
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle c_{\beta,p}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}
\le R_{n,\ell_1}^\star\le R_{n,\infty}^\star
\le C_{\beta,p}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\},\)}}
\]
and then converts it using the globally valid comparison \(dA_d\asymp_{\beta,p}d\binom d{k_\star}\). A single clipped SNIPE estimator attains the bounded-outcome upper bound, and the noiseless block mixtures, already contained in the \(\ell_1\) subclass, give the converse for both classes. Relative to the anchor's stated scales, the upper bound removes one degree factor, with additional gains when Bernoulli contrasts cancel, while the bounded-class converse restores the missing \(dA_d\) degree amplification. On complete blocks with \(d\mid n\), canonical unclipped SNIPE has exact worst-case MSE \(B^2A_d/m\); the fair-coin specialization gives the exact odd-order energy identity and the \(\beta=1\) frontier \(B^2\min\{1,d^2/n\}\); and the separate \(\ell_1\) block-local theorem gives the exact finite minimax constant, the \(\Psi_{b,t}\) risk formula, and the full asymptotic representer characterization.

\section{Related work}

Cortez-Rodriguez, Eichhorn, and Yu introduce SNIPE for low-order neighborhood interference under unit randomization \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}. Their Theorem 1 is the like-for-like \(\ell_1\) comparator because \(Y_{\max}=\max_i\sum_{S'}|c_{i,S'}|\); the present variance argument tightens their stated worst-case bound by charging overlap only once and replacing a coarse order count by the exact energy \(A_d\). In the generic exposed-order regime this removes one full degree factor, with further gains when Bernoulli contrasts cancel. This is a comparison with their published upper bound, not a claim that SNIPE's actual variance always attains that display. Their Theorem 2 gives a degree-free lower scale using Gaussian coefficient priors with unbounded support; it is not a bounded-class converse, and the paper does not claim to improve that theorem on its own unrestricted class. Rather, the new bounded-class least-favourable family shows that the bounded problem has linear-branch degree amplification \(dA_d\asymp_{\beta,p}d\binom d{k_\star}\), of order \(d^{k_\star+1}\), which the printed degree-free diagnosis does not capture. Harshaw, Sävje, and Wang give the general design-based Riesz framework \cite{HarshawSavjeWang2025}, whereas the present paper derives the degree-sharp two-class frontier and the complete-block exact constant. Yu, Airoldi, Borgs, and Chayes provide an additive-interference benchmark under a different, unknown-network information set \cite{YuAiroldiBorgsChayes2022}; the fair-coin \(\beta=1\) theorem supplies the adjacent known-graph benchmark. Eichhorn, Khan, Ugander, and Yu use a variance bound to select among clusterings \cite{EichhornKhanUganderYu2026}. Their singleton-cluster corner \(N=1\), \(C=d_{\max}\) is exactly the coarse unit-Bernoulli candidate sharpened here: this paper sharply recalibrates that specific candidate in their selection procedure, not their general clustered-design bound, and at \(p=1/2\), \(\beta=1\) the linear-regime consistency condition becomes \(n\gg d^2\). Wang and Li develop covariate adjustment and conservative inference \cite{WangLi2026}; the unadjusted frontier and exact block-local benchmark here provide a scale against which such refinements can be judged.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bernoulli-design]\label{ass:bernoulli-design}
\(Z_j\) are mutually independent and \(\Pr(Z_j=1)=p\) for every \(j\in V_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (unit-level Bernoulli randomization, \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-degree]\label{ass:bounded-degree}
Self-loops are allowed and count in both degrees, with \(\max_{i\in V_n}|N_i|\le d\) and \(\max_{j\in V_n}|\{i\in V_n:j\in N_i\}|\le d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded in- and out-degree neighborhood interference with self-loops, \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:low-order]\label{ass:low-order}
\(c_{i,S}=0\) whenever \(|S|>\beta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded polynomial interaction order, \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-coefficient-mass]\label{ass:bounded-coefficient-mass}
\(\sum_{S\subseteq N_i}|c_{i,S}|\le B\) for every \(i\in V_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (SNIPE \(Y_{\max}\) coefficient-mass normalization, \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:exposed-order]\label{def:exposed-order}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{effective and exposed orders}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(\bar\beta_d:=\min\{\beta,d\}\) for every \(d\ge0\); in particular \(\bar\beta_0=0\). If \(\bar\beta_d\ge1\), set \(k_\star(d,\beta,p):=\max\{r\in\{1,\ldots,\bar\beta_d\}:\Delta_r(p)\ne0\}\); since \(\Delta_1(p)=1\), this index set contains \(r=1\), so its maximum is well defined. When \(\bar\beta_d=0\), equivalently \(d=0\) or \(\beta=0\), set \(k_\star(d,\beta,p):=0\) as a totality convention only, never as an attained interaction order. Thus the maximum case applies exactly when both \(d\ge1\) and \(\beta\ge1\); every statement below fixes \(\beta\ge1\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:zero-degree-conventions]\label{def:zero-degree-conventions}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{zero-\allowbreak{}degree conventions}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(\binom{0}{0}:=1\), \(g_0:=0\), and \(A_0:=0\). Set \(h_0:=0\) only as an empty-object convention, and never interpret it as \(g_0/A_0\).
\end{definition}

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:block-score-energy]\label{def:block-score-energy}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{block score,\allowbreak{} energy,\allowbreak{} and raw expansion}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(d\ge1\) and a \(d\)-coordinate Bernoulli vector, set \par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle g_d(z):=\sum_{r=1}^{\bar\beta_d}\frac{\Delta_r(p)}{[p(1-p)]^r}\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\\|S|=r}}\prod_{j\in S}(z_j-p)\)}}\par\noindent, \(A_d:=\sum_{r=1}^{\bar\beta_d}\binom{d}{r}\Delta_r(p)^2/[p(1-p)]^r\), and \(h_d:=g_d/A_d=\sum_{T\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}h_{d,T}\prod_{j\in T}z_j\), the last equality being the raw-monomial expansion.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:block-family]\label{def:block-family}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{continuous-\allowbreak{}baseline score-\allowbreak{}aligned block family}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(B>0\) and \(1\le d\le n\), set \(m:=\lfloor n/d\rfloor\), \(q:=md\), and \(\rho:=q/n\). Partition \(q\) units into \(m\) blocks \(V_b\) of size \(d\); include every within-block directed arrow, including loops, and leave the remaining \(n-q\) units isolated. Set \(s:=B/2\), \(f_s(u):=s^{-1}\cos^2(\pi u/(2s))\mathbf1\{|u|\le s\}\), and draw \(U_1,\ldots,U_m\) independently from \(f_s\). Let \(H_{\beta,p}:=\sup_{d\ge1}\sum_T|h_{d,T}|\) and choose \(\delta:=B\min\{(2H_{\beta,p})^{-1},(4\pi)^{-1}\}\min\{1,\sqrt{A_d/m}\}\). Under \(\Pi_\sigma\), active \(i\in V_b\) has \(c_{i,T}:=U_b\mathbf1\{T=\varnothing\}+\sigma\delta h_{d,T}\) for \(|T|\le k_\star\), and all other coefficients, including those of inactive units, are zero. The \(U_b\) randomize fixed potential-outcome schedules only; they are not observation noise.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:block-energy-representer]\label{lem:block-energy-representer}
For fixed \(\beta\ge1\), \(p\in(0,1)\), and \(d\ge1\), \(A_d\asymp_{\beta,p}\binom{d}{k_\star}\), \(\mathbb E_Z[g_d(Z)f(Z)]=L_d(f)\) for every \(f\in\mathcal P_d\), \(h_d(\mathbf1_d)-h_d(\mathbf0_d)=1\), and \(\mathbb E_Z[h_d(Z)^2]=A_d^{-1}\). The unique minimizers of \({\rm def:perturbation-program}\) and \({\rm def:weight-program}\) are respectively \(h_d=g_d/A_d\), with value \(A_d^{-1}\), and \(w=g_d\), with value \(A_d\). Moreover \(H_{\beta,p}=\sup_{d\ge1}\sum_T|h_{d,T}|<\infty\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(v:=p(1-p)\), \(X_j:=Z_j-p\), \(X_S:=\prod_{j\in S}X_j\), and \(Z_T:=\prod_{j\in T}Z_j\). Independence and centering under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\) give
\[
\mathbb E_Z[X_SX_T]=\mathbf 1\{S=T\}v^{|S|}.
\]
Moreover, for \(S\ne\varnothing\),
\[
\mathbb E_Z[X_SZ_T]=\mathbf 1\{S\subseteq T\}v^{|S|}p^{|T|-|S|}.
\]
Thus, if \(1\le t:=|T|\le\bar\beta_d\), then
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_Z[g_d(Z)Z_T]
&=\sum_{r=1}^{t}\binom tr\Delta_r(p)p^{t-r}\\
&=\sum_{r=1}^{t}\binom tr(1-p)^rp^{t-r}-\sum_{r=1}^{t}\binom tr(-p)^rp^{t-r}\\
&=\{(p+1-p)^t-p^t\}-\{(p-p)^t-p^t\}=1.
\end{aligned}\)}}
\]
For \(T=\varnothing\), the expectation is zero. Since the raw monomials of degree at most \(\bar\beta_d\) span \(\mathcal P_d\), linearity proves
\[
\mathbb E_Z[g_d(Z)f(Z)]=L_d(f),\qquad f\in\mathcal P_d.
\]
Orthogonality of the centered monomials also gives
\[
\mathbb E_Z[g_d(Z)^2]=\sum_{r=1}^{\bar\beta_d}\binom dr\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{v^r}=A_d.
\]
Let \(k:=k_\star(d,\beta,p)\). By \({\rm def:exposed-order}\), every term above order \(k\) vanishes, whereas the order-\(k\) coefficient is positive. For fixed \(\beta\) and \(p\), the finitely many nonzero coefficients \(\Delta_r(p)^2/v^r\) are bounded above and away from zero. Also
\[
\sum_{r=1}^{k}\binom dr\le C_\beta\binom dk.
\]
Indeed, this follows from monotonicity of the binomial coefficients through order \(\beta\) when \(d\ge2\beta\), and the finitely many remaining values of \(d\) are absorbed into \(C_\beta\). The order-\(k\) summand supplies the reverse inequality. Hence
\[
A_d\asymp_{\beta,p}\binom d{k_\star}.
\]
For any feasible \(h\) in \({\rm def:perturbation-program}\), the representer identity and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
1=L_d(h)^2=\langle g_d,h\rangle_{L^2(P_Z)}^2\le A_d\,\mathbb E_Z[h(Z)^2].
\]
Equality holds only when \(h\) is proportional to \(g_d\), and the constraint fixes the proportionality constant as \(A_d^{-1}\). Therefore the unique minimizer is \(h_d=g_d/A_d\), its value is \(A_d^{-1}\), and
\[
h_d(\mathbf1_d)-h_d(\mathbf0_d)=L_d(h_d)=1,
\qquad
\mathbb E_Z[h_d(Z)^2]=A_d^{-1}.
\]
If \(w\) is feasible in \({\rm def:weight-program}\), its moment restrictions and linearity give
\[
\langle w,f\rangle_{L^2(P_Z)}=L_d(f)=\langle g_d,f\rangle_{L^2(P_Z)},\qquad f\in\mathcal P_d.
\]
Consequently \(w-g_d\) is orthogonal to \(\mathcal P_d\). Since \(g_d\in\mathcal P_d\),
\[
\mathbb E_Z[w(Z)^2]=A_d+\mathbb E_Z[(w(Z)-g_d(Z))^2]\ge A_d,
\]
with equality only for \(w=g_d\). This proves the second unique-minimizer assertion.

Finally,
\[
X_S=\sum_{T\subseteq S}(-p)^{|S|-|T|}Z_T.
\]
The triangle inequality for raw coefficients therefore yields
\[
\sum_T|h_{d,T}|
\le \frac{1}{A_d}\sum_{r=1}^{\bar\beta_d}\binom dr\frac{|\Delta_r(p)|}{v^r}(1+p)^r.
\]
Only orders at most \(k_\star\) with nonzero contrast contribute. The same binomial comparison used for \(A_d\), together with the positive order-\(k_\star\) term in its denominator, bounds the last display uniformly over \(d\ge1\) by a finite constant depending only on \(\beta,p\). Thus \(H_{\beta,p}<\infty\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:overlap-count]\label{lem:overlap-count}
For every \(r\ge1\), including \(d=0\), and every \(i\in V_n\), \[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \sum_{l\in V_n}\binom{|N_i\cap N_l|}{r}=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq N_i\\|S|=r}}\#\{l\in V_n:S\subseteq N_l\}\le d\binom{|N_i|}{r}\le d\binom{d}{r}.\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Double-count the pairs \((l,S)\) such that \(l\in V_n\), \(S\subseteq N_i\cap N_l\), and \(|S|=r\). Counting first by \(l\) and then by \(S\) gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sum_{l\in V_n}\binom{|N_i\cap N_l|}{r}
=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq N_i\\|S|=r}}\#\{l\in V_n:S\subseteq N_l\}.\)}}
\]
For each nonempty \(S\), choose \(j_S\in S\). The out-degree part of \({\rm ass:bounded-degree}\) implies
\[
\#\{l:S\subseteq N_l\}\le\#\{l:j_S\in N_l\}\le d.
\]
Summing this inequality over the \(\binom{|N_i|}{r}\) eligible sets and then using \(|N_i|\le d\) proves both inequalities. If \(d=0\), every neighborhood is empty and all displayed quantities are zero, so the same conclusion holds.
\end{proof}

\] In the nonsaturated regime \(d\binom{d}{k_\star}/n\lesssim_{\beta,p}1\), unclipped SNIPE attains the same rate. \end{theorem}

\le R_n^\star(d,\beta,p,B)

\]

\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-local-linear-constant-and-representers]\label{thm:sharp-local-linear-constant-and-representers}
Fix \(\beta\ge1\), \(p\in(0,1)\), and \(B>0\). For every index \(t\), let \(n_t\) and \(d_t\) be positive integers such that \(d_t\mid n_t\), write \(m_t:=n_t/d_t\), and let \(G_t\) be the fixed directed graph that is the disjoint union of \(m_t\) complete directed blocks \(V_b\) of size \(d_t\), including all self-loops. Then, for every \(t\),
\[
R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}=\frac{B^2A_{d_t}}{m_t}=\frac{B^2d_tA_{d_t}}{n_t}.
\]
In particular, \(R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}/(B^2d_tA_{d_t}/n_t)=1\) for every \(t\). More precisely, put \(Z_S:=\prod_{j\in S}Z_j\) and \(Z_b:=(Z_j)_{j\in V_b}\), and for every block \(V_b\) define
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \Psi_{b,t}(w):=\max_{\substack{(\varepsilon_i,S_i)_{i\in V_b}\\\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\},\ S_i\subseteq V_b,\ |S_i|\le\bar\beta_{d_t}}}
\mathbb E_Z\!\left[\left(\sum_{i\in V_b}\varepsilon_i\{w_{i,t}(Z_b)Z_{S_i}-\mathbf1(S_i\ne\varnothing)\}\right)^2\right].\)}}
\]
Then, for every \(t\),
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_{w,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2]
=\frac{B^2}{n_t^2}\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}\Psi_{b,t}(w),
\qquad
\Psi_{b,t}(w)\ge d_t^2A_{d_t}.\)}}
\]
A sequence in \(\mathcal E_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}\) is asymptotically minimax if and only if
\[
\frac{1}{m_td_t^2A_{d_t}}\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}\{\Psi_{b,t}(w)-d_t^2A_{d_t}\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The condition
\[
\frac{1}{n_tA_{d_t}}\sum_{i\in V_{n_t}}\mathbb E_Z[(w_{i,t}(Z_{b(i)})-g_{d_t}(Z_{b(i)}))^2]\longrightarrow0
\]
is sufficient for asymptotic minimaxity. Suppose in addition that \(d_t\to\infty\). This sufficient condition is not necessary, even up to relabeling coordinates within blocks. There are asymptotically minimax weights for which the last display equals \(2\) for every sufficiently large \(t\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an index \(t\), and abbreviate \(n=n_t\), \(d=d_t\), \(m=m_t=n/d\), \(A=A_d\), \(g=g_d\), and \(\mathcal P=\mathcal P_d\). For \(S\subseteq V_b\), write \(Z_S=\prod_{j\in S}Z_j\), and identify the coordinates of each block with \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\). By \({\rm def:local-linear-class}\), every admissible weight \(w_i:=w_{i,t}\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_Z[w_i(Z_b)f(Z_b)]=L_d(f),\qquad f\in\mathcal P.
\]
Indeed, this follows by linearity from the constant and nonempty-monomial moment restrictions in that definition. By \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\), the same identity holds with \(g\) in place of \(w_i\), and \(\lVert g\rVert_2^2=A\). Consequently every admissible weight has the orthogonal decomposition
\[
w_i=g+u_i,\qquad u_i\perp\mathcal P.
\tag{1}
\]
All norms below are in the block space \(L^2(P_Z)\).

We first construct an attaining estimator. Set \(w_i=g\) for every unit. For \(S\subseteq V_b\) with \(|S|\le\bar\beta_d\), define
\[
X_S:=g(Z_b)Z_S-\mathbf 1\{S\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
If \(S=\varnothing\), then \(\lVert X_S\rVert_2^2=A\). If \(S\ne\varnothing\), the representer identity, \(Z_S^2=Z_S\), and \(0\le Z_S\le1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert X_S\rVert_2^2
&=\mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)^2Z_S]-2\mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)Z_S]+1\\
&=\mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)^2Z_S]-1
\le \mathbb E_Z[g(Z_b)^2]=A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
For an admissible unit-level polynomial \(f_i=\sum_Sc_{i,S}Z_S\), the triangle inequality and the coefficient-mass restriction in \({\rm def:local-linear-class}\) therefore imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert gf_i-L_d(f_i)\rVert_2
&\le\sum_S|c_{i,S}|\,\lVert X_S\rVert_2
\le B\sqrt A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\), assignment subvectors belonging to distinct blocks are independent. Each block error is centered by the defining moment restrictions. Hence block variances add, and applying the triangle inequality within each block to (3) yields
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_g-\tau_n)^2]
\le \frac1{n^2}\sum_{b=1}^m(dB\sqrt A)^2
=\frac{B^2A}{m}.
\tag{4}
\]

For the matching converse, take the admissible constant schedule \(f_i\equiv B\) for every unit. It has coefficient mass \(B\) and \(L_d(f_i)=0\). For arbitrary admissible weights, (1), together with \(g\in\mathcal P\) and \(u_i\perp\mathcal P\), gives in each block
\[
\left\lVert\sum_{i\in V_b}w_i\right\rVert_2^2
=\left\lVert dg+\sum_{i\in V_b}u_i\right\rVert_2^2
=d^2A+\left\lVert\sum_{i\in V_b}u_i\right\rVert_2^2
\ge d^2A.
\tag{5}
\]
Independence across blocks then implies
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_n)^2]
\ge\frac{B^2}{n^2}\sum_{b=1}^m d^2A
=\frac{B^2A}{m}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (4) and (6), and restoring subscripts, proves for every \(t\) that
\[
R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}=\frac{B^2A_{d_t}}{m_t}
=\frac{B^2d_tA_{d_t}}{n_t}.
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(B>0\), \(d_t\ge1\), and \(A_{d_t}>0\) by \(\Delta_1(p)=1\), the denominator in the asserted ratio is positive; thus (7) also proves that this ratio equals one.

We next prove the exact finite extremal formula. For a fixed block \(V_b\), define its unscaled centered error by
\[
E_b(c,w):=\sum_{i\in V_b}\{w_i(Z_b)f_i(Z_b)-L_d(f_i)\}.
\]
As a function of the block coefficient vectors \((c_{i,S})_{i\in V_b}\), the map \(\lVert E_b(c,w)\rVert_2^2\) is convex. Each unit's feasible coefficient vector lies in a finite-dimensional \(\ell_1\)-ball of radius \(B\), which is the convex hull of the vectors \(\varepsilon_iB e_{S_i}\), where \(\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}\) and \(|S_i|\le\bar\beta_d\). The product of the unit-level balls is the convex hull of the corresponding product points. Jensen's inequality therefore bounds the value at every feasible coefficient array by the largest value at such a product point. At that point,
\[
E_b(c,w)=B\sum_{i\in V_b}\varepsilon_i
\{w_i(Z_b)Z_{S_i}-\mathbf1(S_i\ne\varnothing)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Conversely, every such product point is feasible, so this bound is attained. The block errors are centered and independent under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\), and the coefficient vectors may be chosen independently across blocks. Thus the supremum of the sum of block variances is the sum of their suprema. Equation (8) gives exactly
\[
\sup_{c\in\mathcal M_t(G_t)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_{w,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2]
=\frac{B^2}{n_t^2}\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}\Psi_{b,t}(w).
\tag{9}
\]
In the maximization defining \(\Psi_{b,t}\), choose \(S_i=\varnothing\) and \(\varepsilon_i=1\) for every \(i\in V_b\). Equation (5) then yields
\[
\Psi_{b,t}(w)\ge d_t^2A_{d_t}.
\tag{10}
\]
This proves all finite assertions.

By definition, a sequence is asymptotically minimax precisely when its worst-case risk divided by \(R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}\) tends to one. Equations (7), (9), and (10) imply, without requiring \(d_t\to\infty\),
\[
\frac{\sup_c\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_{w,t}-\tau_{n_t})^2]}
{R_t^{\mathrm{loc,lin}}}
=1+\frac{1}{m_td_t^2A_{d_t}}
\sum_{b=1}^{m_t}\{\Psi_{b,t}(w)-d_t^2A_{d_t}\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Every summand in the last numerator is nonnegative by (10). Hence the risk ratio tends to one if and only if the normalized excess in the theorem tends to zero. This proves the asserted characterization for every admissible sequence.

We now prove the sufficient condition, still without assuming that \(d_t\) diverges. Suppress \(t\) again and set
\[
a_b^2:=\sum_{i\in V_b}\lVert u_i\rVert_2^2,
\qquad U:=\sum_{b=1}^m a_b^2.
\]
Every admissible polynomial satisfies \(|f_i(Z_b)|\le B\), because its raw coefficient mass is at most \(B\). Therefore Cauchy--Schwarz over the units gives
\[
\left\lVert\sum_{i\in V_b}u_if_i\right\rVert_2
\le B\sum_{i\in V_b}\lVert u_i\rVert_2
\le B\sqrt d\,a_b.
\tag{12}
\]
By (3), the part of the block error involving \(g\) has norm at most \(dB\sqrt A\). Combining this with (12), summing squared block bounds, and using \(\sum_ba_b\le\sqrt{mU}\), we obtain
\[
1\le
\frac{\sup_c\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_n)^2]}{B^2A/m}
\le1+2\sqrt{\frac{U}{mdA}}+\frac{U}{mdA}
=\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{U}{nA}}\right)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
The lower inequality is (6). By (1),
\[
U=\sum_{i\in V_n}\mathbb E_Z[(w_i(Z_{b(i)})-g(Z_{b(i)}))^2].
\]
Thus the normalized \(L^2\) condition in the theorem makes the right-hand side of (13) tend to one. The lower bound in (13) then proves asymptotic minimaxity.

It remains to prove non-necessity under the additional hypothesis \(d_t\to\infty\). Since \(\beta\) is fixed, for every sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
\dim(\mathcal P_{d_t})=\sum_{r=0}^{\beta}\binom{d_t}{r}<2^{d_t}=\dim L^2(P_Z).
\tag{14}
\]
The last equality holds because \(p\in(0,1)\) gives full support on the block assignment space. Hence \(\mathcal P_{d_t}^{\perp}\) contains a nonzero function. Also \(A_{d_t}>0\) because \(\Delta_1(p)=1\). Choose \(v_{d_t}\in\mathcal P_{d_t}^{\perp}\) normalized so that
\[
\lVert v_{d_t}\rVert_2^2=d_tA_{d_t}.
\tag{15}
\]
For every block and all sufficiently large \(t\), set
\[
u_1=v_{d_t},\qquad u_2=-v_{d_t},\qquad u_i=0\quad(i\ge3),
\qquad w_i=g_{d_t}+u_i.
\tag{16}
\]
There are at least two units per block eventually because \(d_t\to\infty\), and the orthogonality in (15) verifies every moment restriction in \({\rm def:local-linear-class}\). For arbitrary admissible unit polynomials, the additional block error contributed by (16) is \(v_{d_t}(f_1-f_2)\), whose norm is at most \(2B\sqrt{d_tA_{d_t}}\). The canonical \(g_{d_t}\)-part has norm at most \(d_tB\sqrt{A_{d_t}}\) by (3). Consequently
\[
1\le
\frac{\sup_c\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_w-\tau_{n_t})^2]}
{B^2A_{d_t}/m_t}
\le\left(1+\frac{2}{\sqrt{d_t}}\right)^2\longrightarrow1.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus these weights are asymptotically minimax. On the other hand, (15) and (16) give, for every sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
\frac{1}{n_tA_{d_t}}\sum_{i\in V_{n_t}}
\lVert w_i-g_{d_t}\rVert_2^2
=\frac{m_t(2d_tA_{d_t})}{m_td_tA_{d_t}}=2.
\tag{18}
\]
A relabeling of coordinates within a block preserves the product Bernoulli law, the symmetric function \(g_{d_t}\), and the sum of squared distances in (18). It therefore cannot make the sufficient condition hold. This proves the non-necessity and the distance-exactly-two witness under precisely the stated divergence hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier]\label{thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier}
Fix \(\beta\ge1\), \(p\in(0,1)\), \(B\ge0\), integers \(n\ge1\), and \(0\le d\le n\). The coefficient-mass model is contained in the uniformly bounded-outcome model, and the inclusion is strict whenever \(B>0\) and \(d\ge1\). There are \(0<c_{\beta,p}\le C_{\beta,p}<\infty\) such that
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle c_{\beta,p}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}
\le R_{n,\ell_1}^\star(d,\beta,p,B)=R_n^\star(d,\beta,p,B)
\le R_{n,\infty}^\star(d,\beta,p,B)
\le \sup_{(G_n,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_{n,\infty}^{\mathrm{up}}-\tau_n)^2]
\le C_{\beta,p}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.\)}}

Moreover,
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sup_{(G_n,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}

\le C_{\beta,p}B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\},\)}}

and on either model class unclipped SNIPE is design-unbiased and has worst-case mean squared error at most \(B^2dA_d/n\). If \(1\le d\le n\) and \(d\mid n\), put \(m=n/d\) and take the disjoint union of \(m\) complete directed \(d\)-blocks, including loops. Then canonical unclipped SNIPE has the exact global worst-case risk on both classes,

\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sup_{(G_n,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]
=\sup_{(G_n,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)}\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]

\]
so the constant one in the global unclipped variance bound is sharp. The upper-bound calculation uses
\[
A_i=\sum_{r=1}^{\min\{\beta,|N_i|\}}\binom{|N_i|}{r}\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{[p(1-p)]^r}\le A_d
\]
and charges out-degree only once. \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first dispose of the boundary cases. If \(B=0\), all potential outcomes in either class vanish, so the observed data, the TTE, both clipped estimators, and both minimax risks are zero. If \(d=0\), \({\rm ass:bounded-degree}\) makes every \(N_i\) empty. Every potential outcome is then assignment-invariant, \(\tau_n=0\), and \({\rm def:zero-degree-conventions}\) gives \(A_0=0\) and zero SNIPE score. All conclusions follow. Henceforth assume \(B>0\) and \(1\le d\le n\).

\emph{Comparison of the model classes.} If \(c\in\mathcal C_{n,d,\beta}(B)\), then for every \(i\in V_n\) and every assignment \(z\),
\[
|Y_i(z)|
\le\sum_{S\subseteq N_i}|c_{i,S}|\prod_{j\in S}z_j
\le\sum_{S\subseteq N_i}|c_{i,S}|
\le B.
\]
The low-order restriction is common to the two classes. Therefore
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)
\subseteq\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B),
\qquad
R_{n,\ell_1}^{\star}(d,\beta,p,B)
\le R_{n,\infty}^{\star}(d,\beta,p,B).\)}}
\]
The inclusion is strict. Indeed, take a graph having the single self-loop at unit \(1\) and no other edge, set
\[
Y_1(z)=B(2z_1-1),
\qquad
Y_i(z)=0\quad(i\ne1).
\]
This schedule has order one and satisfies \(|Y_i(z)|\le B\). Its two nonzero raw coefficients are \(c_{1,\varnothing}=-B\) and \(c_{1,\{1\}}=2B\), whose absolute mass is \(3B>B\). Thus it belongs to the bounded-outcome class but not the coefficient-mass class.

\emph{Unbiasedness and the common variance bound.} Define
\[
W_i:=Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}g_{i,\beta,p}(Z),
\qquad q_i:=|N_i|.
\]
If \(q_i=0\), both the score and unit \(i\)'s causal contrast vanish. If \(q_i\ge1\), relabel the coordinates in \(N_i\). The representer identity in \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\), applied in dimension \(q_i\), and \({\rm ass:low-order}\) imply
\[
\mathbb E_Z[W_i]=Y_i(\mathbf1)-Y_i(\mathbf0).
\]
Summing over \(i\) proves
\[
\mathbb E_Z[\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}]=\tau_n
\]
on both classes. Centered-monomial orthogonality under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\) also gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_Z[g_{i,\beta,p}(Z)^2]
=A_i:=\sum_{r=1}^{\min\{\beta,q_i\}}
\binom{q_i}{r}\frac{\Delta_r(p)^2}{[p(1-p)]^r}
\le A_d,\)}}
\]
because \(q_i\le d\) and \(\binom qr\) is nondecreasing in \(q\) for fixed \(r\).

Every centered function of the binary vector \(Z_{N_i}\) has a unique Bernoulli Fourier expansion
\[
W_i-\mathbb E_ZW_i
=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}a_{i,S}
\prod_{j\in S}(Z_j-p).
\]
On the larger class, \(|Y_i(Z)|\le B\). Orthogonality consequently yields
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}
a_{i,S}^2[p(1-p)]^{|S|}
=\operatorname{Var}_Z(W_i)
\le\mathbb E_Z[W_i^2]
\le B^2A_i
\le B^2A_d.\)}}
\]
For a nonempty set \(S\), let \(I_S:=\{i\in V_n:S\subseteq N_i\}\). Choose any \(j\in S\). The out-degree restriction in \({\rm ass:bounded-degree}\), equivalently the one-coordinate charge underlying \({\rm lem:overlap-count}\), gives
\[
|I_S|\le\#\{i\in V_n:j\in N_i\}\le d.
\]
Another use of orthogonality, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz for the sum over \(I_S\), gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_Z\!\left(\sum_{i\in V_n}W_i\right)
&=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}[p(1-p)]^{|S|}
\left(\sum_{i\in I_S}a_{i,S}\right)^2\\
&\le d\sum_{i\in V_n}
\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq N_i}
a_{i,S}^2[p(1-p)]^{|S|}\\
&\le ndB^2A_d.
\end{aligned}\)}}
\]
Design unbiasedness therefore implies, on either class,
\[
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]
\le B^2\frac{dA_d}{n}.
\]

On the bounded-outcome class,
\[
|\tau_n|
\le\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}
\{|Y_i(\mathbf1)|+|Y_i(\mathbf0)|\}
\le2B.
\]
Projection onto \([-2B,2B]\) cannot increase distance to \(\tau_n\), and the squared distance between two points in this interval is at most \(16B^2\). Hence
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sup_{(G_n,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_{n,\infty}^{\mathrm{up}}-\tau_n)^2]
\le B^2\min\!\left\{\frac{dA_d}{n},16\right\}
\le16B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.\)}}
\]
On the coefficient-mass class, each unit contrast has absolute value at most \(B\), so \(|\tau_n|\le B\). Projection onto \([-B,B]\) similarly gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sup_{(G_n,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{up}}-\tau_n)^2]
\le4B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.\)}}
\]

\emph{Exact complete-block worst-case risk.} Suppose \(d\mid n\), put \(m=n/d\), and take the disjoint union of \(m\) complete directed blocks of size \(d\), including loops. The variance inequality just proved gives the upper bound
\[
B^2\frac{dA_d}{n}=\frac{B^2A_d}{m}
\]
for the supremum on either class. For the reverse inequality, take the fixed schedule \(Y_i(z)\equiv B\) for every unit. It belongs to both classes and has \(\tau_n=0\). All units in block \(b\) share the score \(g_d(Z_b)\), whence
\[
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}
=\frac{Bd}{n}\sum_{b=1}^m g_d(Z_b).
\]
The block assignments are independent, \(\mathbb E_Z[g_d(Z_b)]=0\), and \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\) gives \(\mathbb E_Z[g_d(Z_b)^2]=A_d\). Therefore
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]
=\frac{B^2d^2mA_d}{n^2}
=\frac{B^2A_d}{m}
=\frac{B^2dA_d}{n}.\)}}
\]
Thus both global suprema equal the claimed value, proving sharpness of the constant one.

\emph{Least-favourable family and converse.} Use \({\rm def:block-family}\) and abbreviate
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \kappa:=\min\!\left\{(2H_{\beta,p})^{-1},(4\pi)^{-1}\right\},
\qquad
\delta=\kappa B\min\!\left\{1,\sqrt{A_d/m}\right\}.\)}}
\]
The complete active blocks have in-degree and out-degree exactly \(d\), including the loop, and inactive vertices have both degrees zero. Thus the graph is in \(\mathcal G_{n,d}\). By \({\rm def:exposed-order}\), all coefficients of \(g_d\), hence of \(h_d=g_d/A_d\), above order \(k_\star\) vanish. The schedules therefore have order at most \(k_\star\le\beta\). For active \(i\in V_b\), \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\), \(|U_b|\le B/2\), and \(\kappa H_{\beta,p}\le1/2\) imply
\[
\sum_{T\subseteq V_b}|c_{i,T}|
\le |U_b|+\delta\sum_T|h_{d,T}|
\le\frac B2+\kappa BH_{\beta,p}
\le B.
\]
Inactive outcomes vanish. Every schedule in either prior's support consequently belongs to \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}(B)\), and hence also to \(\mathcal M_{n,d,\beta}^{\infty}(B)\).

For active \(i\in V_b\),
\[
Y_i(z)=U_b+\sigma\delta h_d(z_{V_b}).
\]
Since \(h_d(\mathbf1_d)-h_d(\mathbf0_d)=1\), every schedule under \(\Pi_\sigma\) has
\[
\tau_n=\sigma\rho\delta.
\]
The two prior mixtures thus have TTE separation \(2\rho\delta\).

It remains to bound their statistical distance. Let \(\psi_s:=\sqrt{f_s}\), extended by zero outside \([-s,s]\). It is weakly differentiable, and direct calculation gives
\[
\|\psi_s'\|_2^2
=\frac{\pi^2}{4s^3}\int_{-s}^{s}
\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi u}{2s}\right)du
=\frac{\pi^2}{4s^2}.
\]
For any real \(a,b\), the fundamental theorem of calculus followed by Minkowski's integral inequality gives
\[
\|\psi_s(\,\cdot-a)-\psi_s(\,\cdot-b)\|_2
\le |a-b|\,\|\psi_s'\|_2.
\]
Conditional on a block assignment \(Z_b=z\), the repeated observed block outcome has a translate of density \(f_s\), and the translations under the two signs differ by \(2\delta h_d(z)\). Under the convention
\[
H^2(P,Q):=\int(\sqrt{dP}-\sqrt{dQ})^2,
\]
the one-block squared Hellinger distance is therefore bounded by
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_Z\!\left[4\delta^2h_d(Z)^2\|\psi_s'\|_2^2\right]
=\frac{\pi^2\delta^2}{s^2}\mathbb E_Z[h_d(Z)^2]
=\frac{4\pi^2\delta^2}{B^2A_d},\)}}
\]
where \(s=B/2\) and \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\) supplies \(\mathbb E_Z[h_d(Z)^2]=A_d^{-1}\). Hellinger affinity multiplies over the \(m\) independent blocks. Since \(1-\prod_{b=1}^m a_b\le\sum_{b=1}^m(1-a_b)\) for \(a_b\in[0,1]\),
\[
H^2(P_+,P_-)
\le\frac{4\pi^2m\delta^2}{B^2A_d}
\le4\pi^2\kappa^2
\le\frac14.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the densities gives \(\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le H(P_+,P_-)\), and hence
\[
\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac12.
\]

For any estimator, thresholding it at zero defines a test of the two signs. Put \(\theta:=\rho\delta\). On a testing error, the squared estimation error is at least \(\theta^2\). The sum of the two testing-error probabilities is at least \(1-\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\). Consequently
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_\sigma}[(\widehat\tau_n-\sigma\theta)^2]
\ge\frac{\theta^2}{2}
\{1-\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}
\ge\frac{\rho^2\delta^2}{4}.\)}}
\]
Each prior is supported on deterministic schedules in the smaller model class, so its integrated risk cannot exceed the corresponding worst-case risk. This inequality therefore lower-bounds both minimax risks.

Finally, \(m=\lfloor n/d\rfloor\) and \(1\le d\le n\) imply
\[
\frac12\le\rho=\frac{md}{n}\le1,
\qquad
\frac{A_d}{m}=\frac{dA_d/n}{\rho}.
\]
Using the definition of \(\delta\),
\[
R_{n,\ell_1}^{\star}(d,\beta,p,B)
\ge\frac{\kappa^2}{16}B^2
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dA_d}{n}\right\}.
\]
Class inclusion gives the same lower bound for \(R_{n,\infty}^{\star}\). Together with the upper bounds, this proves the \(A_d\)-scale sandwich with \(c_{\beta,p}=\kappa^2/16\) and, for example, \(C_{\beta,p}=16\).

For \(d\ge1\), \({\rm lem:block-energy-representer}\) gives \(A_d\asymp_{\beta,p}\binom d{k_\star}\). For \(d=0\), \({\rm def:zero-degree-conventions}\) gives \(dA_d=0=d\binom d{k_\star}\). Hence, uniformly for \(0\le d\le n\),
\[
dA_d\asymp_{\beta,p}d\binom d{k_\star}.
\]
Substitution proves the binomial frontier. The unclipped risk bound \(B^2dA_d/n\) is of the same order as the minimax risk whenever \(d\binom d{k_\star}/n\lesssim_{\beta,p}1\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem gives the simultaneous matched design-based minimax frontier over the \(\ell_1\) coefficient-mass class and the larger uniformly bounded-outcome class, with the exact energy \(A_d\) leading, one clipped attaining estimator, a noiseless least-favourable family, and the exact worst-case risk of canonical unclipped SNIPE on complete blocks. \textit{Gap.} Theorem 1 of \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023} has a coarser stated worst-case variance bound, while its Gaussian-prior Theorem 2 is not a converse for either bounded class. This theorem supplies the missing cancellation-sensitive two-class frontier without claiming to improve that unrestricted Gaussian-prior result. \textit{Consumer.} For the singleton-cluster candidate \(N=1\), \(C=d_{\max}\) in the clustering-selection procedure of \cite{EichhornKhanUganderYu2026}, the theorem replaces the coarse unit-Bernoulli calibration by \(dA_d/n\); at \(p=1/2\), \(\beta=1\), the linear-regime threshold is \(n\gg d^2\). This recalibrates that candidate, not the paper's general clustered-design bound.

\begin{theorem}[thm:fair-coin-energy-frontier]\label{thm:fair-coin-energy-frontier}
Fix \(n\ge1\), \(1\le d\le n\), \(\beta\ge1\), \(B\ge0\), and \(p=1/2\). Then
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \Delta_r(1/2)=\begin{cases}2^{1-r},&r\text{ odd},\\0,&r\text{ even},\end{cases}
\qquad
A_d=4\sum_{\substack{1\le r\le\min\{\beta,d\}\\r\text{ odd}}}\binom dr.\)}}
\]
Consequently \(k_\star\) is the largest odd integer no greater than \(\min\{\beta,d\}\). For \(\beta=1\), \(A_d=4d\) and
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle R_n^\star(d,1,1/2,B)=R_{n,\ell_1}^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
\asymp R_{n,\infty}^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
\asymp B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n}\right\}.\)}}
\]
If additionally \(d\mid n\), canonical unclipped SNIPE has exact worst-case mean squared error \(4B^2d^2/n\) on both model classes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At \(p=1/2\), for every integer \(r\ge1\),
\[
\Delta_r(1/2)
=2^{-r}\{1-(-1)^r\}
=\begin{cases}
2^{1-r},&r\text{ odd},\\
0,&r\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Substitute this identity and \(p(1-p)=1/4\) into \({\rm def:block-score-energy}\). For odd \(r\),
\[
\frac{\Delta_r(1/2)^2}{[p(1-p)]^r}
=\frac{2^{2-2r}}{4^{-r}}=4,
\]
whereas for even \(r\) the summand is zero. Therefore
\[
A_d=4\sum_{\substack{1\le r\le\min\{\beta,d\}\\r\text{ odd}}}\binom dr.
\]
Because \(d\ge1\) and \(\beta\ge1\), the exposed set is nonempty, and \({\rm def:exposed-order}\) now shows that \(k_\star\) is the largest odd integer not exceeding \(\min\{\beta,d\}\).

If \(\beta=1\), only \(r=1\) occurs, so
\[
A_d=4\binom d1=4d.
\]
The identities in \({\rm def:two-class-minimax-risks}\) and the two-class frontier in Theorem \({\rm thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier}\) then give
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle R_n^\star(d,1,1/2,B)
=R_{n,\ell_1}^{\star}(d,1,1/2,B)
\asymp R_{n,\infty}^{\star}(d,1,1/2,B)
\asymp B^2\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n}\right\}.\)}}
\]
Finally, if \(d\mid n\), the exact complete-block assertion of that theorem gives
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \sup_{(G_n,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,1}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]
=\sup_{(G_n,c)\in\mathcal M_{n,d,1}^{\infty}(B)}
\mathbb E_Z[(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{SNIPE}}-\tau_n)^2]
=\frac{B^2dA_d}{n}
=\frac{4B^2d^2}{n}.\)}}
\]
This is the claimed exact worst-case mean squared error.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This public fair-coin theorem makes the exposed-order cancellation and exact energy transparent: only odd orders contribute, and \(\beta=1\) gives the motivating \(d^2/n\) frontier. \textit{Gap.} The general bounds in \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023} do not state this cancellation-sensitive minimax specialization or the exact fair-coin energy identity. \textit{Consumer.} It gives the most legible known-graph additive-interference benchmark adjacent to \cite{YuAiroldiBorgsChayes2022} and the concrete singleton-cluster calibration used in comparison with \cite{EichhornKhanUganderYu2026}.

\section{Interpretation}

The exact local energy \(A_d\) measures the squared norm of the Bernoulli representer for the all-one-versus-all-zero contrast, while the extra factor \(d\) records how many outcome neighborhoods one assignment coordinate can affect. At \(p=1/2\), even-order contrast terms vanish from this representer and \(A_d\) is four times the sum of the eligible odd-order binomial counts; for \(\beta=1\), the matched linear branch is therefore \(d^2/n\). Complete blocks show that the unit upper constant one for canonical unclipped SNIPE is genuinely attained, not merely an artifact of the proof. Within the narrower block-local linear problem, \(g_d\) attains the exact minimax constant, but asymptotic minimaxity need not force every unit weight to approach \(g_d\): opposite perturbations in \(\mathcal P_d^\perp\) can cancel within blocks while the normalized aggregate \(L^2\) distance remains two.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The main upper bound over the uniformly bounded-outcome class uses only \(|Y_i(z)|\le B\); this yields \(|\tau_n|\le2B\), so the attaining estimator is projected onto \([-2B,2B]\). The converse is inherited from the smaller \(\ell_1\) class because the continuous-baseline least-favourable schedules have coefficient mass at most \(B\). The exact block-local linear theorem is not enlarged to bounded outcomes: its within-block triangle argument and finite \(\ell_1\)-extreme-point formula for \(\Psi_{b,t}\) genuinely use coefficient-mass boundedness. The lower bound uses Hellinger distance because compactly supported translates of \(f_s\) may have infinite one-sided Kullback--Leibler divergence, while their squared Hellinger distance is quadratic in the translation. The anchor's Theorem 2 is not a bounded-class converse, and no comparison is made with its unrestricted class.

\section{Honest scope}

The two-class frontier covers all integers \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le d\le n\), fixed \(\beta\ge1\), fixed \(p\in(0,1)\), and \(B\ge0\). At \(d=0\), every neighborhood is empty and the TTE and both minimax risks are zero; at \(B=0\), every bounded outcome is zero. If \(\beta>d\), only orders through \(\bar\beta_d=\min\{\beta,d\}\) exist. Constants are not uniform as \(p\to0\) or \(p\to1\), and can deteriorate as a nonzero \(\Delta_r(p)\) approaches zero. At \(p=1/2\), a vanishing even-order contrast means only that the centered order is absent from this TTE representer, not that the corresponding coefficient is unobservable. The paper tightens the stated worst-case variance bound of \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}, not SNIPE's actual variance, and it does not claim to beat that paper's Theorem 2 on its unrestricted class. The exact \(\Psi_{b,t}\) characterization and block-local minimax constant remain confined to the \(\ell_1\) class on fixed complete directed blocks. Future work includes the exact leading constant for the all-measurable-estimator minimax risk and a matched frontier under heterogeneous propensities \(p_j\in[p,1-p]\): there \(\Delta_S=\prod_{j\in S}(1-p_j)-(-1)^{|S|}\prod_{j\in S}p_j\) may vanish for some subsets of one order but not others, so no scalar exposed order \(k_\star\) generally exists; the upper bound extends, but a matched design-specific converse requires new packing work. Variance estimation, studentization, and limit-law theory are outside the present theorem scope.
