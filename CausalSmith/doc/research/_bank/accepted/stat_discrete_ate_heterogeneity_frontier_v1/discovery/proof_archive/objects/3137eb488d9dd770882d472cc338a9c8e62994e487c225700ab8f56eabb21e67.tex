Set
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad a:=\frac1n,\qquad t:=\frac d{n^2},
 \qquad b:=a+t,\qquad u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},
 \qquad s:=\sigma^2.
\tag{1}
\]
First consider \(L^2<d<nL\).  Then
\[
 t<u<1,
\tag{2}
\]
because \(u/t=d/L^2>1\) and \(d/(nL)<1\).  The upper benchmark is therefore
\[
 q=a+\min\{u,s+t\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For any nonnegative \(s,t,u\) with \(t\le u\),
\[
 \frac12\{t+\min(u,s)\}
 \le \min\{u,s+t\}
 \le t+\min(u,s).
\tag{4}
\]
For the upper inequality, \(\min(u,s+t)\le u\) and \(\min(u,s+t)\le s+t\), hence it is at most \(t+\min(u,s)\).  For the lower inequality, if \(u\le s+t\), its left-hand side is at most \((t+u)/2\le u\); if \(u>s+t\), then \(s<u\) and its left-hand side is \((t+s)/2\le s+t\).  Adding \(a\) to (4), and using \(a\ge0\), gives
\[
 \frac12\{b+\min(u,s)\}
 \le q\le b+\min(u,s).
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(u<1\), the converse benchmark is exactly
\[
 \ell=b+s\min(1,u)=b+su.
\tag{6}
\]
This proves the first display.

We next compare the two theorem-level bounds on the entire stipulated dimension range.  Put \(m:=\min(1,u)\).  Apart from their constants and the common factor \(M^2\), Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) has benchmark
\[
 r:=a+\min\{m,s+t\},
\tag{7}
\]
whereas Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) has benchmark
\[
 B:=b+sm.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(\sigma\ge\underline\sigma>0\), then
\[
 r\le a+m
 \le \max\{1,\underline\sigma^{-2}\}(a+sm)
 \le \max\{1,\underline\sigma^{-2}\}B.
\tag{9}
\]
The upper and lower theorems combined with (9) prove the fixed-positive-radius equivalence, with constants depending only on \(\epsilon\) and \(\underline\sigma\).

The three uniform matching regimes follow from equally direct inequalities.  If \(u\ge1\), then \(m=1\) and
\[
 r=a+\min(1,s+t)\le a+s+t=b+s=B.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(u\le Cb\), then
\[
 r\le a+u\le(1+C)b\le(1+C)B.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(s\le Cb\), then
\[
 r\le a+s+t=b+s\le(1+C)b\le(1+C)B.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus in each regime the converse lower bound controls the selector rate, while Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) supplies the reverse risk inequality.  The resulting constants are independent of \(\sigma\in[0,2]\).

Multiplying the inequality \(u\le b\) by \(n^2L^2\) gives the exact equivalence
\[
 u\le b
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d^2\le nL^2+dL^2.
\tag{13}
\]
The condition \(d\le\sqrt n\,L\) implies (13) immediately.  Also, if \(d\le L^2\), then \(u=t(d/L^2)\le t\le b\), so the earlier small-alphabet regime is included.  Finally, (12) applies to every admissible alphabet when \(\sigma^2=s\le Cb\).  These are the two parametric-dominance elbows claimed in the statement.

It remains to identify where a divergent ratio can occur.  If along a sequence the ratio \(r/B\) is unbounded, (10)--(12) imply, for every fixed \(C\), that eventually
\[
 u<1,\qquad u>Cb,\qquad s>Cb.
\tag{14}
\]
Hence \(b=o(u)\) and \(b=o(s)\).  Equation (9) shows that no subsequence with \(s\) bounded away from zero can have an unbounded ratio, so \(s\to0\).  It follows that \(b\to0\), and hence \(t\to0\), because \(0\le t\le b\).  If \(u\) were bounded below by some \(\eta>0\) on a further subsequence, then \(s+t\to0\), so eventually \(s+t<u\), and (7)--(8) would give
\[
 r=a+s+t=b+s
 \le\max\{1,\eta^{-1}\}(b+\eta s)
 \le\max\{1,\eta^{-1}\}B,
\tag{15}
\]
a contradiction.  Therefore \(u\to0\).  This proves that divergence is possible only when
\[
 b\ll u\ll1,
 \qquad b\ll s\ll1.
\tag{16}
\]
Since
\[
 \frac ub=\frac{d^2}{L^2(n+d)},
\tag{17}
\]
condition (16) is, up to boundary constants in the triangular asymptotic range, \(\sqrt n\,L\ll d\ll nL\) together with \(\sigma^2\gg n^{-1}+d/n^2\).

The wedge is nonempty.  Take \(d_n=n\), \(L_n:=\log(en)\), and \(\sigma_n:=L_n^{-1/2}\).  Then
\[
 b_n=\frac2n,\qquad u_n=\frac1{L_n^2},\qquad s_n=\frac1{L_n}.
\tag{18}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the normalized selector and converse benchmarks are
\[
 r_n=\frac1n+\frac1{L_n^2}\asymp\frac1{L_n^2},
 \qquad
 B_n=\frac2n+\frac1{L_n^3}\asymp\frac1{L_n^3},
\tag{19}
\]
because \(L_n^3/n\to0\).  Consequently
\[
 \frac{r_n}{B_n}\asymp L_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{20}
\]
The endpoint cases are included in (9)--(12): \(s=0\) is covered by (12), and every fixed positive radius, including \(\sigma=2\), is covered by (9); saturation is (10), and the alphabet and elbow statements follow from (11)--(13).  Thus (16) is the only regime not decided by the present pair of bounds.  The proof compares proved benchmarks only and does not assert that either one is the exact minimax rate inside that wedge.
