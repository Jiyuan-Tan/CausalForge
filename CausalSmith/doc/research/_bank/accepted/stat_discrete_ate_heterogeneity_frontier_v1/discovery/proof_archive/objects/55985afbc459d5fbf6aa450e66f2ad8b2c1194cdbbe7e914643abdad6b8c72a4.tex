The answer is complete for fixed interior radii but remains partial for a radius allowed to shrink with \(n\).  For every fixed \(s\in(0,2]\), there are constants \(0<c_{\epsilon,s}\le C_{\epsilon,s}<\infty\) such that, uniformly over \(s\le\sigma\le2\) and \(1\le d\le c_{\epsilon}n^2/\log(en)\),
\[
 c_{\epsilon,s}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}
 \le \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \le C_{\epsilon,s}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\]
The same equivalence holds with constants independent of \(\sigma\in[0,2]\) whenever either
\[
 \frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\ge1
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 d\le\log^2(en).
\]
Thus the product lower remainder is minimax-tight at every fixed interior radius.  The frozen bounds do not, however, decide the fully uniform shrinking-radius problem.  Indeed, with \(L_n:=\log(en)\), \(d_n:=n\), and \(\sigma_n:=L_n^{-1/2}\), the selector benchmark divided by the proved lower benchmark satisfies
\[
 \frac{n^{-1}+\min\{1,d_n^2/(n^2L_n^2),\sigma_n^2+d_n/n^2\}}
 {n^{-1}+d_n/n^2+\sigma_n^2\min\{1,d_n^2/(n^2L_n^2)\}}
 \asymp L_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Consequently a uniform resolution still requires either a stronger constrained converse or a sharper estimator in the triangular regime \(\sigma_n\to0\), \(\log^2(en)<d<n\log(en)\); neither endpoint-only reasoning nor the existing radius channel decides between them.
