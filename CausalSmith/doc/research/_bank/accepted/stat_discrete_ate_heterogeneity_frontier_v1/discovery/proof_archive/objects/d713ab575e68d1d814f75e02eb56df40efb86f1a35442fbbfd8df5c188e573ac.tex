Put
\[
 a:=\frac1n,\qquad v:=\frac d{n^2},\qquad
 L:=\log(en),\qquad u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},
 \qquad s:=\sigma^2,qquad b:=a+v.
\tag{1}
\]
The normalized upper and lower benchmarks supplied by Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) are, respectively,
\[
 r=a+\min(1,u,s+v),
 \qquad B=b+s\min(1,u).
\tag{2}
\]

First suppose \(L^2<d<nL\).  Then \(v<u<1\).  With \(m:=\min(u,s)\) and \(t:=\min(u,s+v)\), elementary monotonicity gives
\[
 t\le m+v,qquad t\ge m,qquad t\ge v.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the first inequality follows by considering whether \(u\le s\), and the last two use \(s+v\ge s,v\) and \(u>v\).  Hence \(t\ge\max(m,v)\ge(m+v)/2\), and therefore
\[
 \frac12(b+m)le a+tle b+m.
\tag{4}
\]
Since \(u<1\), (2) also gives \(B=b+su\).  This proves the first display of the theorem.

We next compare (2) globally.  Fix \(\underline\sigma>0\) and suppose \(s\ge\underline\sigma^2\).  Writing \(m_1:=\min(1,u)\),
\[
 r\le a+m_1
 \le \max\{1,\underline\sigma^{-2}\}\{a+s m_1\}
 \le \max\{1,\underline\sigma^{-2}\}B.
\tag{5}
\]
The upper theorem and the converse theorem now sandwich the risk between constant multiples of \(M^2r\), uniformly over \(\underline\sigma\le\sigma\le2\).

The comparison constants can instead be made independent of \(\sigma\) in each of the three stated regimes.  If \(u\ge1\), then
\[
 r=a+\min(1,s+v)\le a+s+v=B.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(u\le Cb\), then
\[
 r\le a+u\le(C+1)b\le(C+1)B.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(s\le Cb\), then
\[
 r\le a+s+v=b+s\le(C+1)b\le(C+1)B.
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (5), (6), (7), or (8), as applicable, with the same upper and lower theorems proves every asserted risk equivalence.

The algebraic description of the first parametric elbow is exact:
\[
 u\le b
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\le\frac1n+\frac d{n^2}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d^2\le nL^2+dL^2.
\tag{9}
\]
The inequality \(d\le\sqrt n\,L\) implies (9).  If \(d\le L^2\), then \(u=v(d/L^2)\le v\le b\), so this former small-alphabet regime is also included.  Equation (8) gives the asserted matching whenever \(s\le Cb\).

For completeness, consider any triangular sequence along which the ratio \(r/B\) diverges.  Equations (6)--(8) force
\[
 u<1,qquad \frac bu\longrightarrow0,qquad
 \frac bs\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]
Equation (5) forces \(s\to0\).  Also \(u\to0\): otherwise, on a subsequence with \(u\ge\eta>0\),
\[
 r\le b+s
 \le\max(1,\eta^{-1})\{b+su\}
 =\max(1,\eta^{-1})B,
\tag{11}
\]
contradicting divergence.  Thus (10)--(11) give precisely the residual wedge \(b\ll u\ll1\) and \(b\ll s\ll1\).  Since
\[
 \frac ub=\frac{d^2}{L^2(n+d)},
\tag{12}
\]
the conditions \(b\ll u\) and \(u\ll1\) are, up to boundary constants, \(\sqrt n\,L\ll d\ll nL\); the first also implies \(d\gg L^2\), as needed when \(d\) exceeds \(n\).  The remaining condition is \(s\gg b\).

Finally take \(d_n=n\), \(L_n=log(en)\), and \(s_n=\sigma_n^2=L_n^{-1}\).  Then
\[
 b_n=\frac2n,qquad u_n=\frac1{L_n^2},qquad
 r_n=\frac1n+\frac1{L_n^2}\asymp\frac1{L_n^2},
\tag{13}
\]
whereas
\[
 B_n=\frac2n+\frac1{L_n^3}\asymp\frac1{L_n^3}.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore \(r_n/B_n\asymp L_n\to\infty\).  Equations (5)--(14) show that the endpoints, fixed positive radii, saturation, the small-alphabet regime, and the two parametric-dominance elbows are matched, and that only the displayed wedge remains undecided by the current selector and channel converse.
