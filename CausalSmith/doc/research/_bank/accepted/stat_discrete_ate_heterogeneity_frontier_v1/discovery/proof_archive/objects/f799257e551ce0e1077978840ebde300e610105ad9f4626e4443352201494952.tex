Put \(L:=\log(en)\), \(u:=d^2/(n^2L^2)\), and \(h:=\sigma^2+d/n^2\).  By Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:robust\text{-}upper\text{-}construction\text{-}resolution}\), after taking the smaller of its two dimension constants and the larger of its two risk constants, the two explicit total estimators satisfy simultaneously
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2\{n^{-1}+\min(1,u)\}
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2(n^{-1}+h).
\tag{2}
\]
The same theorem verifies that both maps belong to \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\), including all positive-denominator indicators, zero fallbacks, and final clipping.

It remains only to inspect the deterministic selector in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}estimator}\).  If \(h\le\min(1,u)\), the selector is \(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\), and (2) is exactly
\[
 C_{\epsilon}M^2\{n^{-1}+\min(1,u,h)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(u<\min(1,h)\), the selector is \(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}\); here \(u<1\), so (1) again equals (3).  Finally, suppose neither selection inequality holds.  If \(h<1\) and \(h\le u\), the first inequality would hold.  If \(u<1\) and \(u<h\), the second inequality would hold.  Hence in the remaining case \(\min(1,u,h)=1\).  The selector is then zero.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives \(|\tau(P)|\le M\), and consequently
\[
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(0-\tau(P))^2]\le M^2
 \le M^2\{n^{-1}+\min(1,u,h)\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4), together with the definition of \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\), prove the asserted uniform risk bound.  They also cover all ties and the boundary values \(\sigma=0\) and \(\sigma=2\).

For the comparison, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}collision\text{-}upper}\) records the collision remainder of order \(\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}^2+d/n^2\), in addition to \(n^{-1}\), for the binary model.  On the common normalized scale, the selector remainder is
\[
 \min(1,u,h)\le h.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus it is never larger in order than the collision remainder.  If \(u=o(h)\) and \(d=o(nL)\), then \(u=o(1)\), whence \(\min(1,u,h)\le u=o(h)\).  This is the stated strict order improvement.  The comparison concerns the estimator guarantee: the anchor's formal binary image is a strict subclass, while its text also states rate validity under a bounded conditional second moment, so no novelty is attributed merely to allowing real outcomes.
