Put \(\lambda=\sigma/2\in[0,1]\).  Start from the binary one-arm subclass in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}one\text{-}arm\text{-}lower}\), with treated response \(B\in\{0,1\}\), treated cell mean \(\eta_k\in[0,1]\), and control response identically zero.  Conditional on every binary potential response \(B(a)\), independently draw
\[
 B'(a)\mid B(a)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left\{\frac12+\lambda\left(B(a)-\frac12\right)\right\},
 \qquad Y(a)=M\{B'(a)-1/2\},
\tag{1}
\]
and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  The randomization in (1) is independent of treatment conditional on the original potential outcomes and \(X\), so Assumptions~\(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:conditional\text{-}exchangeability}\) are preserved.  Propensities and cell masses are unchanged, hence Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) is preserved.

For the control and treated arms, respectively,
\[
 \mu_{0k}=-\frac{M\lambda}{2},
 \qquad
 \mu_{1k}=M\lambda(\eta_k-1/2).
\tag{2}
\]
Both means lie in \([-M/2,M/2]\).  Since \(Y(a)\in\{-M/2,M/2\}\), the absolute centered outcome is at most \(M\), so every conditional fourth central moment is at most \(M^4\).  Equations (2) give
\[
 \tau_k=M\lambda\eta_k,
 \qquad
 \tau(P)=M\lambda\sum_{k=1}^dp_k\eta_k.
\tag{3}
\]
Writing \(\psi:=\sum_kp_k\eta_k\),
\[
 |\tau_k-\tau(P)|=M\lambda|\eta_k-\psi|le M\lambda\le\sigma M.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus every transformed law belongs to \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\).  The verification is realization-wise, includes \(\sigma=0\), and imposes no condition on zero-mass cells.

The transformation from \((X,A,B)\) to \((X,A,Y)\) is a Markov kernel identical under the two fuzzy hypotheses.  Total variation between the induced mixtures can only decrease under this kernel.  Equivalently, for \(\lambda>0\), any estimator \(T\) in the transformed experiment can be applied after simulating (1) from the source sample, and \(T/(M\lambda)\) estimates \(\psi\).  Conditional expectation given the source sample can only reduce squared loss.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}one\text{-}arm\text{-}lower}\) therefore gives, for \(d\le b_{\epsilon}n\log n\),
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\lambda^2\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(d>b_{\epsilon}n\log n\), take \(d_0=\lfloor b_{\epsilon}n\log n\rfloor\) and embed the \(d_0\)-cell construction by assigning zero mass to the remaining cells.  After decreasing the dimension constant and absorbing fixed cutoffs, (5) implies
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\sigma^2
 \min\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
At \(\lambda=0\), (6) is the zero inequality.

Independently, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scaled\text{-}binary\text{-}exact\text{-}lower\text{-}transfer}\) yields throughout the stated range
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\left(\frac1n+\frac d{n^2}\right).
\tag{7}
\]
The maximum of the right sides of (6) and (7) is at least half their sum, which proves the theorem's displayed lower bound.  The finitely many sample sizes below the source lemmas' cutoffs are absorbed by decreasing \(c_{\epsilon}\): the displayed bracket divided by \(n^{-1}\) is then uniformly bounded on the stipulated dimension and radius ranges, while Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\) supplies a fixed multiple of \(M^2/n\).

Finally, (3) shows that the target separation is exactly \(M\lambda\) times the source separation.  A source separation of order \(\min\{1,d/[n\log(en)]\}\) therefore becomes \(M\sigma\min\{1,d/[n\log(en)]\}\) up to constants, and its square is precisely the product term in (6).  Hence this channel proves no standalone interior separation of order \(M\min\{\sigma,d/[n\log(en)]\}\).
