Put
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad x:=\frac d{n^2},\qquad
 b:=\frac1n+x,\qquad u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},
 \qquad s:=\sigma^2,\qquad v:=\min(1,u),
\]
and define the all-regime upper and lower benchmarks
\[
 q:=\frac1n+\min\{v,s+x\},\qquad \ell:=b+sv.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(q=r_{n,d,\sigma}\).  Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\), on the same arbitrary-cell-mass conditional-second-moment model, give
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\le C_\epsilon M^2q,
 \qquad
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\ge c_\epsilon M^2\ell.
\tag{2}
\]

In the triangular regime \(L^2<d<nL\), one has \(0<x<u<1\).  With \(m:=\min(u,s)\),
\[
 \frac{x+m}{2}\le\min\{u,s+x\}\le x+m.
\tag{3}
\]
For the upper inequality, use the second argument when \(m=s\) and the first when \(m=u\).  For the lower inequality, \(s+x\ge m+x\), while \(x+m<2u\).  Adding \(n^{-1}\) proves
\[
 \frac12\{b+\min(u,s)\}\le q\le b+\min(u,s),
 \qquad \ell=b+su.
\tag{4}
\]

If \(\sigma\ge\underline\sigma>0\), then
\[
 q\le n^{-1}+v,\qquad
 \ell\ge n^{-1}+\underline\sigma^2v,
\]
and hence
\[
 q\le\max\{1,\underline\sigma^{-2}\}\ell.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining (2) and (5) proves the fixed-interior minimax equivalence, with constants allowed to depend on \(\underline\sigma\).

Suppose \(u\ge1\).  Then \(v=1\).  If \(s+x\le1\), equation (1) gives \(q=\ell\).  If \(s+x>1\), it gives
\[
 q=n^{-1}+1\le\ell,
 \qquad
 \ell=n^{-1}+x+s\le n^{-1}+5\le5q,
\tag{6}
\]
where, after taking the dimension constant no larger than one, \(x=d/n^2\le1\) on the stated dimension range, and \(0\le s=\sigma^2\le4\).  Thus the benchmarks are uniformly equivalent throughout the saturation regime.

For fixed \(C<\infty\), if \(u\le Cb\), then
\[
 q\le n^{-1}+v\le b+u\le(1+C)b\le(1+C)\ell.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(s\le Cb\), then
\[
 q\le n^{-1}+s+x=b+s\le(1+C)b\le(1+C)\ell.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (2), (7), and (8) prove both parametric-dominance elbows with constants independent of \(\sigma\).  Direct multiplication by \(n^2L^2\) shows
\[
 u\le b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d^2\le nL^2+dL^2.
\tag{9}
\]
The condition \(d\le\sqrt n\,L\) makes \(d^2\le nL^2\), and \(d\le L^2\) makes \(d^2\le dL^2\), proving the stated sufficient regimes.

If \(q/\ell\) diverges along a sequence, (7)--(8) force \(u/b\to\infty\) and \(s/b\to\infty\) along a subsequence.  Such divergence also forces \(u\to0\) and \(s\to0\).  Saturation \(u\ge1\) is ruled out by (6).  If instead \(c\le u<1\) along a subsequence for some \(c>0\), then
\[
 q\le b+s\le b+c^{-1}su\le\max(1,c^{-1})\ell,
\]
so the ratio cannot diverge.  If \(s\ge c>0\), (5) likewise bounds the ratio.  Consequently divergence is possible only when
\[
 b\ll u\ll1,
 \qquad
 b\ll s\ll1.
\tag{10}
\]
The first pair is, up to boundary constants, \(\sqrt n\,L\ll d\ll nL\); the second is \(\sigma^2\gg n^{-1}+d/n^2\) with \(\sigma\to0\).

Finally take \(d_n=n\), \(L_n:=\log(en)\), and \(\sigma_n:=L_n^{-1/2}\).  Then
\[
 b_n=\frac2n,\qquad u_n=L_n^{-2},\qquad s_n=L_n^{-1},
\]
so
\[
 q_n=n^{-1}+L_n^{-2}\asymp L_n^{-2},
 \qquad
 \ell_n=2n^{-1}+L_n^{-3}\asymp L_n^{-3}.
\]
Hence \(q_n/\ell_n\asymp L_n\to\infty\).  This proves the exact localization and the claimed witness.  The argument concerns minimax mean-squared error only and makes no inference, coverage, or unknown-scale or unknown-radius adaptation claim.
