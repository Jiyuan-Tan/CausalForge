\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(\lambda:=\sigma/2\in[0,1]\).  Start from the binary one-arm subclass in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}one\text{-}arm\text{-}lower}\), with treated response \(B\in\{0,1\}\), treated cell mean \(\eta_k\in[0,1]\), and control response identically zero.  Conditional on every binary potential response \(B(a)\), independently draw
\[
 B'(a)\mid B(a)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\{1/2+\lambda(B(a)-1/2)\},
 \qquad Y(a):=M\{B'(a)-1/2\},
\tag{1}
\]
and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  The auxiliary randomization in (1) is independent of treatment given the original potential outcomes and \(X\), so consistency and conditional exchangeability are preserved.  Propensities and cell masses are unchanged, hence fixed overlap is preserved.

For the control and treated arms, respectively,
\[
 \mu_{0k}=-\frac{M\lambda}{2},\qquad
 \mu_{1k}=M\lambda(\eta_k-1/2).
\tag{2}
\]
Both means belong to \([-M/2,M/2]\).  Since \(Y(a)\in\{-M/2,M/2\}\), the conditional fourth central moment is at most \(M^4\).  Equation (2) gives
\[
 \tau_k=M\lambda\eta_k,qquad
 \tau(P)=M\lambda\sum_{k=1}^dp_k\eta_k.
\tag{3}
\]
Writing \(\psi:=\sum_kp_k\eta_k\), we obtain
\[
 |\tau_k-\tau(P)|=M\lambda|\eta_k-\psi|le M\lambda\le\sigma M.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus every transformed law is in \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), including the boundary values \(\sigma=0,2\), and all cellwise claims concern only \(p_k>0\).

The passage from \((X,A,B)\) to \((X,A,Y)\) is a Markov kernel identical under the two fuzzy hypotheses.  Total variation between their induced mixtures cannot increase.  Equivalently, for \(\lambda>0\), any estimator in the transformed experiment can be simulated from the source experiment and divided by \(M\lambda\); conditioning the simulated estimator on the source sample cannot increase squared loss.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}one\text{-}arm\text{-}lower}\) therefore implies, for \(d\le b_{\epsilon}n\log n\),
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\lambda^2\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Replacing \(\log n\) by \(\log(en)\) only changes a universal constant on the source range.  If \(d>b_{\epsilon}n\log n\), take \(d_0=\lfloor b_{\epsilon}n\log n\rfloor\) and assign zero mass to the remaining cells.  Decreasing constants to cover the integer cutoff gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\sigma^2
 \min\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
At \(\lambda=0\), (6) is the zero inequality.

Independently, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scaled\text{-}binary\text{-}exact\text{-}lower\text{-}transfer}\), after reducing the dimension constant if necessary, gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\left(\frac1n+\frac d{n^2}\right).
\tag{7}
\]
The maximum of the right sides of (6) and (7) is at least half their sum, proving the theorem's display.  The finitely many sample sizes below the two binary lemmas' cutoffs are absorbed by decreasing \(c_{\epsilon}\): over that finite set and the stated dimension range, the bracket divided by \(1/n\) is bounded, while Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\) supplies a uniform multiple of \(M^2/n\).

Finally, (3) shows that the channel multiplies the source target separation by exactly \(M\sigma/2\).  A source separation of order \(\min\{1,d/[n\log(en)]\}\) therefore becomes order \(M\sigma\min\{1,d/[n\log(en)]\}\), whose square is the product term in (6).  The same channel cannot yield a separation of order \(M\min\{\sigma,d/[n\log(en)]\}\), so it does not establish the stronger interior minimum. \(\square\)
