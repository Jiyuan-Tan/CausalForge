\paragraph{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and write \(L:=\log(en)\).  Let \(\rho_{U,\epsilon}>0\) and \(C_{U,\epsilon}<\infty\) be respectively a dimension-range constant and a risk constant furnished by Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\).  Let \(\rho_{L,\epsilon}>0\) and \(c_{L,\epsilon}>0\) be the corresponding constants furnished by Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\).  Define
\[
 \rho_{\epsilon}:=\min\{\rho_{U,\epsilon},\rho_{L,\epsilon},1\}.
\]
For every \(0\leq\sigma\leq2\) and \(1\leq d\leq \rho_{\epsilon}n^2/L\), Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}-\tau(P))^2\right]
 \leq C_{U,\epsilon}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:robust\text{-}upper\text{-}construction\text{-}answer}\), as used in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\), verifies that both component maps belong to \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\); Definition~\(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}estimator}\) therefore gives \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\).  Taking this one data-driven map as the witness in the infimum of Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\), rather than choosing a law-dependent map, turns (1) into
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \leq C_{U,\epsilon}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
 \tag{2}
\]
All ratios in the two component estimators have the zero fallbacks specified in their constructions, and clipping makes the selector a total map into \([-M,M]\).

On exactly the same radius and dimension range, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \geq c_{L,\epsilon}M^2\left\{
 \frac1n+\frac d{n^2}
 +\sigma^2\min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right)
 \right\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Its proof restricts the supremum in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\) to full-data laws satisfying every condition in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\); it does not replace that class by a different observed experiment.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\) supplies the \(n^{-1}\) component also at every finite boundary value of \(n\), \(d\), and \(\sigma\), while the converse absorbs the finite cutoffs of its remaining construction.  Thus (2) and (3) concern the same real-outcome class, with arbitrary masses \(p_k\), fixed overlap on every positive-mass cell, the stated conditional-mean and fourth-central-moment bounds, and no condition on zero-mass cells.

Set
\[
 c_{\epsilon}:=\min\{\rho_{\epsilon},c_{L,\epsilon},1\},
 \qquad
 C_{\epsilon}:=\max\{C_{U,\epsilon},c_{\epsilon}\}.
\]
Then \(0<c_{\epsilon}\leq C_{\epsilon}<\infty\).  Restricting to \(d\leq c_{\epsilon}n^2/L\), decreasing the coefficient in (3) to \(c_{\epsilon}\), and increasing the coefficient in (2) to \(C_{\epsilon}\) proves the displayed two-sided bracket.

For completeness, the algebra requested for interpreting, but not strengthening, the bracket is recorded next.  Put
\[
 a:=\frac1n,
 \qquad q:=\frac d{n^2},
 \qquad b:=a+q,
 \qquad u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},
 \qquad s:=\sigma^2.
 \tag{4}
\]
In the nonsaturated regime \(u\leq1\), the dimensionless upper and lower benchmarks in (2) and (3) are
\[
 U:=a+\min\{u,s+q\},
 \qquad
 D:=b+su.
 \tag{5}
\]
Let \(m:=\min\{u,s\}\) and \(t:=\min\{u,s+q\}\).  Since \(L=1+\log n\leq2\sqrt n\) for \(n\geq1\), completing the square gives
\[
 n+\frac{d^2}{L^2}-d
 =\frac{(d-L^2/2)^2}{L^2}+n-\frac{L^2}{4}
 \geq0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 q\leq a+u.
 \tag{6}
\]
Also \(m\leq t\) and \(t\leq q+m\).  If \(t=s+q\), then \(q\leq t\); if \(t=u\), (6) gives \(q\leq a+t\).  Hence in both cases \(q\leq U=a+t\).  It follows that
\[
 \frac12\{b+\min(u,s)\}
 \leq U
 \leq b+\min(u,s),
 \qquad
 D=b+su.
 \tag{7}
\]
Thus the upper and lower benchmarks are, up to universal numerical constants, respectively \(b+\min(u,s)\) and \(b+su\) in the nonsaturated triangular regime.

These expressions immediately expose the additional parametric-dominance elbow.  More generally, throughout the theorem's dimension range one has \(q\leq1\).  For the full upper benchmark \(U_{\mathrm{full}}:=a+\min\{1,u,s+q\}\), the same three-case argument according as its minimum is \(1\), \(u\), or \(s+q\) gives \(b\leq2U_{\mathrm{full}}\).  If \(u\leq Cb\), then, using \(0\leq s\leq4\),
\[
 U_{\mathrm{full}}\leq a+u\leq(1+C)b,
 \qquad
 b+s\min(1,u)\leq b+4u\leq(1+4C)b.
 \tag{8}
\]
If instead \(s\leq Cb\), then
\[
 U_{\mathrm{full}}\leq a+s+q\leq(1+C)b,
 \qquad
 b+s\min(1,u)\leq b+s\leq(1+C)b.
 \tag{9}
\]
Both benchmarks are bounded below by a fixed multiple of \(b\), by the preceding inequality for \(U_{\mathrm{full}}\) and trivially for the lower benchmark.  Therefore they are order-equivalent whenever either \(u\lesssim b\) or \(s\lesssim b\).  In particular, the constant-free all-radius condition \(u\leq b\) is exactly
\[
 d^2\leq nL^2+dL^2.
 \tag{10}
\]
The simpler condition \(d\leq\sqrt n\,L\) implies (10); conversely (10) yields
\[
 d\leq\frac{L^2+\sqrt{L^4+4nL^2}}2
 \leq L^2+\sqrt n\,L
 \lesssim\sqrt n\,L.
\]
Equation (9) also proves matching for every alphabet in the theorem's range whenever \(\sigma^2\lesssim n^{-1}+d/n^2\).

If \(u\geq1\), then the two benchmarks are order-equivalent for every radius: indeed the upper benchmark is \(a+\min\{1,s+q\}\), the lower benchmark is \(b+s\), and the cases \(s+q\leq1\) and \(s+q>1\), together with \(q\leq1\) and \(s\leq4\), compare them within a universal constant.  The endpoint \(s=0\) is covered by (9), and radii bounded away from zero also match by (5)--(7) and the saturation argument.  Hence possible divergence is localized, up to boundary constants, to
\[
 b\ll u\ll1,
 \qquad
 b\ll s\ll1,
 \tag{11}
\]
which is equivalently the wedge
\[
 \sqrt n\,L\ll d\ll nL,
 \qquad
 \sigma^2\gg \frac1n+\frac d{n^2},
 \qquad
 \sigma^2\ll1.
\]
This remaining non-equivalence is genuine for the displayed benchmarks.  For example, along \(d=n\) and \(s=L^{-2}\), one has \(b=2/n\) and \(u=s=L^{-2}\), so (7) gives an upper benchmark of order \(L^{-2}\) and a lower benchmark of order \(L^{-4}\); their ratio is of order \(L^2\).  This witness explains why the theorem asserts a bracket rather than uniform order equivalence and does not purport to solve the residual shrinking-radius wedge.  No inference statement and no adaptation to unknown \(M\) or \(\sigma\) is used.  \(\square\)
