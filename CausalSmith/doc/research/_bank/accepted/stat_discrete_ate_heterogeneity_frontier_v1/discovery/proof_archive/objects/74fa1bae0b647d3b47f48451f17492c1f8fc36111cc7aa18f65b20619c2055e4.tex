Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) and write
\[
 \vartheta:=\tau(P)/M,qquad
 \vartheta_k:=\tau_k/M,qquad
 s_{ak}:=P(X=k,A=a),qquad
 z_{ak}:=E[(Y/M)\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}].
\tag{1}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives \(\vartheta\in[-1,1]\), and approximate homogeneity gives \(|\vartheta_k-\vartheta|\le\sigma\) on every positive-mass cell.

The three blocks in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:centered\text{-}product\text{-}estimator}\) are independent and each has size between \(n/4\) and \(n/2\) for \(n\ge4\).  Apply Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:continuous\text{-}occupancy\text{-}collision\text{-}upper}\) to the pilot block.  After changing constants because its size is a constant fraction of \(n\), the normalized pilot \(t\in[-1,1]\) satisfies
\[
 E[(t-\vartheta)^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left\{\frac1n+\frac d{n^2}+\sigma^2\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
The range condition of that lemma follows from \(u\le\kappa_{\epsilon}\) after choosing \(\kappa_{\epsilon}\) sufficiently small and absorbing finitely many \(n<N_{\epsilon}\) into the fallback constant.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the classification event on which every empirically light cell has \(p_k\le B/4\), while every empirically heavy cell has \(p_k\ge c_{\epsilon}L/n\).  The binomial Chernoff inequalities
\[
 P\{D\ge2E D\}\le(e/4)^{E D},
 \qquad
 P\{D\le E D/2\}\le e^{-E D/8}
\tag{3}
\]
and a union bound over at most \(d\le\sqrt{\kappa_{\epsilon}},nL\) cells give, for the displayed \(\lambda_{\epsilon}\) and \(\beta_{\epsilon}\),
\[
 Q_P^{(n)}(\mathcal A^c)\le C_{\epsilon}n^{-8}.
\tag{4}
\]
The numerical choices also ensure that, on \(\mathcal A\), every selected heavy cell obeys \(mp_k\ge C\epsilon^{-1}L\).  Hence the probability of a missing estimation-block arm in such a cell is at most \(2e^{-\epsilon mp_k}\le2n^{-12}\).

Condition on the pilot and classifier and suppose \(\mathcal A\) occurs.  For \(r_{K,B}(s):=B^{-1}G_K(s/B)\), put
\[
 c_k:=s_{0k}s_{1k}\{r_{K,B}(s_{0k})+r_{K,B}(s_{1k})\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The shifted-Chebyshev identity from Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\) is
\[
 0\le x\{1-xG_K(x)\}\le K^{-2},qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{6}
\]
Since overlap makes \(s_{0k}\) and \(s_{1k}\) comparable and every light cell has \(p_k\le B/4\), equation \((6)\) gives
\[
 |c_k-p_k|
 \le C_{\epsilon}B/K^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, expand
\[
 c_k-p_k=s_{1k}\{s_{0k}r_{K,B}(s_{0k})-1\}
 +s_{0k}\{s_{1k}r_{K,B}(s_{1k})-1\}
\]
and apply \((6)\) to the two terms.

The population centering identity is exact for every partition \(\mathcal H\cup\mathcal L=\{1,\ldots,d\}\):
\[
 t+\sum_{k\in\mathcal H}p_k(\vartheta_k-t)
 +\sum_{k\in\mathcal L}c_k(\vartheta_k-t)-\vartheta
 =\sum_{k\in\mathcal L}(c_k-p_k)(\vartheta_k-t).
\tag{8}
\]
By \((7)\), \(dB/K^2\le C_{epsilon}d/(nL)=C_{epsilon}\sqrt u\), and
\[
 \max_k|\vartheta_k-t|^2
 \le2\{\sigma^2+(t-\vartheta)^2\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus the squared deterministic error in \((8)\) is at most
\[
 C_{epsilon}u\{\sigma^2+(t-\vartheta)^2\}.
\tag{10}
\]

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:centered\text{-}mixed\text{-}arm\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\), with the light set deterministic after conditioning, gives the same radius-dependent term plus \(C_{epsilon}(n^{-1}+d/n^2)\) for its variance.  For the heavy sum, condition further on the estimation-block category and treatment indicators.  The conditional outcome variance is bounded by \(C_{epsilon}/n\), because each normalized arm mean has variance at most the reciprocal arm count and overlap controls the expected reciprocal.  Its conditional mean, after removing missing-arm terms, is the empirical average of \(\vartheta_X-t\) over heavy cells, whose variance is at most \(4/n\).  The missing-arm contribution has squared expectation at most \(C_{epsilon}n^{-8}\) by the observation following \((4)\) and \(\sum_kp_k=1\).  Therefore
\[
 E\left[\left.
 \left\{
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\widehat h_k(t)
 -\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}p_k(\vartheta_k-t)
 \right\}^2\right|t,I_{\mathrm{cls}},\mathcal A\right]
 \le C_{\epsilon}/n+C_{epsilon}n^{-8}.
\tag{11}
\]

Combine \((8)\)--\((11)\), using \((x+y+z)^2\le3(x^2+y^2+z^2)\), to obtain for the normalized, unclipped estimator \(Z(t)\)
\[
 E[\{Z(t)-\vartheta\}^2\mid t,I_{\mathrm{cls}},\mathcal A]
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left[
 b+u\{\sigma^2+(t-\vartheta)^2\}
 \right],qquad b:=n^{-1}+d/n^2.
\tag{12}
\]
Projection onto \([-1,1]\) cannot increase loss from \(\vartheta\in[-1,1]\), and its squared loss is at most four on \(\mathcal A^c\).  Integrating \((12)\), then applying \((2)\), \((4)\), and \(u\le\kappa_{\epsilon}<1\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E[(T_n^{\mathrm{ctr}}/M-\vartheta)^2]
 &\le C_{\epsilon}\{b+u\sigma^2+uE(t-\vartheta)^2+n^{-8}\}\\
 &\le C_{\epsilon}\{b+u\sigma^2+u(b+\sigma^2)\}\\
 &\le C_{\epsilon}(b+\sigma^2u).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Multiplication by \(M^2\) proves the claim.  Every statistic in the construction has a displayed zero fallback, and final clipping maps it into \([-M,M]\), so \(T_n^{\mathrm{ctr}}\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\).  The sole outcome-tail input is the conditional variance bound used in the centered covariance lemma and in \((11)\).
