\textit{Proof.}
The upper inequality is Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\), applied to its single explicit total selector \(\widehat\tau_n^\star\).  Since that estimator belongs to \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\), Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(\widehat\tau_n^\star-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_\epsilon M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\tag{1}
\]

For the lower inequality, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\), whose parametric component is supplied by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\), gives on the same range
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_\epsilon M^2\left\{
 \frac1n+\frac d{n^2}
 +\sigma^2\min\left(1,
 \frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right)
 \right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Decrease the common dimension constant and the lower constant, and increase the upper constant, so that (1)--(2) hold simultaneously, including the finitely many sample sizes absorbed in the cited results.  By Definitions~\(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), \(\mathrm{def:sample\text{-}experiment}\), and \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\), both displays take their supremum over the same arbitrary-cell-mass real-outcome class and use the same product sampling law.  No binary subclass is substituted into the upper inequality; the binary construction appears only inside the valid subclass restriction used by the converse.

Equations (1)--(2) prove the stated bracket.  Since no comparison between the two right-hand benchmark expressions was used, the conclusion is exactly a two-sided bracket and makes no additional claim of uniform order equivalence at shrinking interior radii. \(\square\)
