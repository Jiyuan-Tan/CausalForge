Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), \(0\le\sigma\le2\), and integers \(n,d\ge1\).  Shrink the constants supplied by Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\), if necessary, so that
\[
 1\le d\le c_{\epsilon}\frac{n^2}{\log(en)}
\tag{1}
\]
implies the range conditions of both theorems.  Enlarge the common upper constant and decrease the common lower constant once more, if necessary, so that \(0<c_{\epsilon}\le C_{\epsilon}<\infty\).

By Definition~\(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}estimator}\), the selected map \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}\) is an element of \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\).  It is therefore an admissible witness in the infimum defining \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\).  Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\) and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 &=\inf_{T\in\mathcal T_{n,M}}
   \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
   E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T-\tau(P))^2]\\
 &\le
   \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
   E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}-\tau(P))^2]\\
 &\le C_{\epsilon}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
This is a data-driven minimax upper bound: the estimator depends only on the observed sample and the fixed known inputs \(n,d,M,\sigma\), and its clipping and division-boundary fallbacks are part of Definition~\(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}estimator}\) and its two construction inputs.

Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) yields on the same range
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\left\{
 \frac1n+\frac d{n^2}
 +\sigma^2\min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right)
 \right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The parametric component used in that converse is also valid uniformly by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\), including every boundary value of \(\sigma\) and every positive integer \(n\).

Both (2) and (3) quantify over the identical class \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\).  By Definition~\(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), this is the arbitrary-cell-mass real-outcome class with fixed overlap, the conditional-mean normalization, and the conditional-second-central-moment envelope; zero-mass cells require no conditional quantities.  Thus no class substitution, bounded-outcome restriction, positivity strengthening, or rank condition enters either side.  Combining (2) and (3) proves the displayed bracket.

The lower radius term is the product \(\sigma^2\min(1,u)\), whereas the selected upper radius term is \(\min(1,u,\sigma^2+d/n^2)\), where \(u=d^2/[n^2\log^2(en)]\).  Hence the theorem proves exactly the claimed bracket and does not resolve the possible shrinking-radius mismatch.  The final paragraph of the statement records provenance and positioning; it does not enlarge the mathematical claim proved in (2)--(3).
