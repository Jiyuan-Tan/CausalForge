\textbf{Proof.}
Write
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad q:=\frac d{n^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Take the dimension constant in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:two\text{-}sided\text{-}minimax\text{-}bracket}\) no larger than one.  The stipulated range then gives
\[
 q\le\frac{c_{\epsilon}}L\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
For every integer \(n\ge1\), \(L^2\le4n\): indeed \(\log n\le\sqrt n\) and \(1+\sqrt n\le2\sqrt n\).

At \(\sigma=0\), the definition of \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\) gives
\[
 r_{n,d,0}=\frac1n+\min\{1,u,q\}.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(d\ge L^2\), then \(u=q(d/L^2)\ge q\), and (2) makes the minimum in (3) equal to \(q\).  If \(d<L^2\), then
\[
 q<\frac{L^2}{n^2}\le\frac4n,
\tag{4}
\]
so
\[
 \frac15\left(\frac1n+q\right)\le\frac1n\le r_{n,d,0}\le\frac1n+q.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(r_{n,d,0}\asymp n^{-1}+d/n^2\) uniformly.  At this endpoint the lower expression in the bracket is exactly \(n^{-1}+q\).

At \(\sigma=2\), \(4+q\ge1\), and hence
\[
 r_{n,d,2}=\frac1n+\min\{1,u\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives the support-qualified class identity
\[
 \mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,2}=\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M}^{\mathrm{unr}}.
\tag{7}
\]
Put \(m:=\min\{1,u\}\).  The normalized lower expression is
\[
 B:=\frac1n+q+4m.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(d\ge L^2\), then \(q\le m\): this follows from \(q\le u=m\) when \(u\le1\), and from (2) when \(u>1\).  Hence \(B\le5(n^{-1}+m)\).  If \(d<L^2\), (4) gives the same conclusion.  Conversely, \(B\ge n^{-1}+m\).  Equations (6)--(8) prove \(B\asymp r_{n,d,2}\).  These comparisons include \(n=1\), \(d=1\), and arbitrary zero-mass cells; the common multiplier \(M^2\) does not affect them. \(\square\)
