Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), put \(\nu_{ak}:=\mu_{ak}/M\), and recall \(V=Y/M\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}normalization}\) gives \(|\nu_{ak}|\le1/2\), while Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:second\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\) gives
\[
 E[(V-\nu_{ak})^2\mid A=a,X=k]\le1.
\]
Consequently
\[
 E[V^2\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}]
 =s_{ak}\{\operatorname{Var}(V\mid A=a,X=k)+\nu_{ak}^2\}
 \le\frac54s_{ak}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is the only outcome-moment inequality used below.

Every summand defining \(U_{k,a,j}\) contains exactly one marked selector
\(V\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}\); all its other factors are indicators.  Independence of distinct sample indices therefore gives
\[
 E[U_{k,a,j}]=z_{ak}p_ks_{ak}^{j}.
\tag{2}
\]
To calculate a covariance, multiply the two ordered sums defining \(U_{k,a,j}\) and \(U_{\ell,b,r}\) and partition their terms by the partial matching of their index tuples.  A matching of size \(h\) leaves \(j+r+4-h\) distinct observations.  Since \(j+2,r+2\le K\) and \(4K^2\le m\), cancellation of falling factorials yields
\[
 \frac{(m)_{j+r+4-h}}{(m)_{j+2}(m)_{r+2}}
 \le\frac e{m^h},
\tag{3}
\]
and the number of size-\(h\) matchings is at most \(K^{2h}/h!\).

This partition also verifies the promised moment audit.  If the two marked positions are not identified, each surviving observation carries at most one mark and factors by independence.  If a marked position is matched to an unmarked selector, the expectation is \(z_{ak}\), or zero when the selectors are incompatible, and \(|z_{ak}|\le s_{ak}/2\).  The two marked positions can be identified only when \(k=\ell\) and \(a=b\); that observation then contributes exactly \(E[V^2\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}]\), which is bounded by (1).  Thus no term contains a third power, fourth power, or product of marks at distinct selectors after factorization.

It remains to sum the deterministic coefficients.  Define
\[
 G_K(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}g_{Kj}x^j,
 \qquad G_K^+(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}|g_{Kj}|x^j=G_K(-x).
\]
The shifted-Chebyshev identity gives
\[
 G_K^+(x)=\frac{T_K(1+2x)-1-2K^2x}{2K^2x^2},
\]
with the continuous value at zero, and the closed form for \(T_K(t)\) on \(t\ge1\) implies
\[
 G_K^+(x)\le6^K\max\{1,x^{K-2}\},\qquad x\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]
Because \(p_k\le B/4\) on \(S\) and \(4(K+2)/m\le3B/4\), every intensity argument produced by a matching in (3) is at most \(B\).  Summing absolute overlap contributions with (4) gives three classes.  A matching that identifies the marked positions is bounded by \(C_\epsilon/m\) using (1), overlap, and \(\sum_{k,a}s_{ak}=1\).  The remaining within-cell matchings are bounded by \(C_\epsilon6^{2K}dB^2\).  For different cells no cross-tuple index can be shared; after subtracting the product of expectations, only the fixed-sample falling-factorial correction remains, and
\[
 \left|\frac{(m)_{j+r+4}}{(m)_{j+2}(m)_{r+2}}-1\right|
 \le\frac{2K^2}{m}.
\tag{5}
\]
Its total is at most \(C_\epsilon6^{2K}d^2K^2B^2/m\).  Adding the three classes proves
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{k\in S}\widehat P_k\right)
 \le \frac{C_\epsilon}{m}+C_\epsilon6^{2K}
 \left\{dB^2+\frac{d^2K^2B^2}{m}\right\}.
\]
The proof uses only (1), hence only the conditional second-central-moment envelope; boundedness of \(Y\) is nowhere invoked.
