\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Let \(c_{U,\epsilon}>0\) and \(c_{L,\epsilon}>0\) be dimension-range constants for Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\), respectively, and set
\[
 c_{0,\epsilon}:=\min\{c_{U,\epsilon},c_{L,\epsilon},1\}.
\]
For every \(0\le\sigma\le2\) and \(1\le d\le c_{0,\epsilon}n^2/\log(en)\), Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \le C_{U,\epsilon}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\tag{1}
\]
The witness for (1) is the single data-dependent total selector in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}estimator}\), so (1) is a valid upper bound for the infimum in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\), rather than a law-dependent oracle bound.

On the same support, radius, and dimension range, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) yields
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{L,\epsilon}'M^2\left\{
 \frac1n+\frac d{n^2}
 +\sigma^2\min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right)
 \right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
The parametric boundary component in that theorem is valid for every \(n,d\ge1\) by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\).  Its other components are lower bounds on subclasses of the model in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), so restricting the supremum in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\) transfers them to the full class.

Choose the displayed dimension constant no larger than \(c_{0,\epsilon}\) and the two risk constants no smaller or larger, as appropriate, than the constants in (1)--(2).  This gives finite \(0<c_{\epsilon}\le C_{\epsilon}\) and proves the display.  Both sides use the same iid product experiment in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:sample\text{-}experiment}\), the same fixed overlap, the same conditional-mean and fourth-moment envelopes, and the same radius restriction.  All cellwise conditions are imposed only for \(p_k>0\), so arbitrary zero-mass cells cause no boundary exception.  Equations (1)--(2) prove only the stated bracket and do not identify the two interior remainders. \(\square\)
