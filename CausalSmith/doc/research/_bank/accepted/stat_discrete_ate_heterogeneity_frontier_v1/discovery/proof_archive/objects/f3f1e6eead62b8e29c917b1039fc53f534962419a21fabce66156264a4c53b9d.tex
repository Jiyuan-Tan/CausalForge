Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:continuous\text{-}ratio\text{-}polynomial\text{-}upper}\) defines the linear-mark Chebyshev factorial estimator on every sample: it uses the displayed empirical formula in its active regime and the value zero otherwise, guards every empirical denominator by a positive-count indicator, and finally clips into \([-M,M]\).  It is therefore a measurable map into \([-M,M]\), hence an element of \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\).  The same lemma proves, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\),
\[
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{1,\epsilon}M^2\left\{n^{-1}+
 \min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right)\right\}                       \tag{1}
\]
throughout its stated dimension range.  This proof uses the fixed-sample distinct-index factorial identity, so (1) requires neither Poissonization nor a de-Poissonization loss.

Next define \(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\) by Definition~\(\mathrm{def:collision\text{-}handle}\).  Its inner value is explicitly zero on \(\{R_n=0\}\), each arm mean is used only on \(\{N_{ak}>0\}\), and its outer projection lies in \([-M,M]\).  Thus it too belongs to \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\).  Its formula is exactly the estimator denoted \(T_n^{\mathrm{occ}}\) in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:continuous\text{-}occupancy\text{-}collision\text{-}upper}\).  That lemma therefore gives
\[
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{2,\epsilon}M^2\left(n^{-1}+\sigma^2+\frac d{n^2}\right)               \tag{2}
\]
uniformly over the same model class and its stated dimension range.

Take \(c_\epsilon\) to be the smaller of the dimension-range constants in the two lemmas and take \(C_\epsilon:=\max(C_{1,\epsilon},C_{2,\epsilon})\), increasing it if necessary to cover their finitely many fallback cases.  Then (1) and (2) hold simultaneously for every \(0\le\sigma\le2\) and every \(1\le d\le c_\epsilon n^2/\log(en)\).  The construction and the two cited proofs explicitly cover \(\sigma=0\), \(\sigma=2\), zero-mass population cells, empty empirical arms, \(R_n=0\), and the inactive polynomial regime.  Neither display has an additional logarithmic loss, which proves every assertion of the theorem.
