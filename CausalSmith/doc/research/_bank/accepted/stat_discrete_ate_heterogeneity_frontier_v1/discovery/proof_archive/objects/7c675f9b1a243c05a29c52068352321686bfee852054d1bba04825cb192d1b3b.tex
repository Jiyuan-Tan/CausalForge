Set
\[
 L:=\log(en),\quad a:=\frac1n,\quad t:=\frac d{n^2},
 \quad b:=a+t,\quad u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\quad s:=\sigma^2,
\tag{1}
\]
and let
\[
 t_c:=\min(1,t),\qquad m:=\min(1,u),\qquad
 r:=a+\min\{m,s+t\},\qquad B:=a+t_c+sm.
\tag{2}
\]
Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper\text{-}all\text{-}d}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse\text{-}all\text{-}d}\) bound the risk above by \(C_{\epsilon}M^2r\) and below by \(c_{\epsilon}M^2B\), respectively.

First suppose \(L^2<d<nL\).  Then \(t<u<1\), because \(u/t=d/L^2>1\) and \(u=(d/(nL))^2<1\).  In particular \(t_c=t\), \(m=u\), and (2) reduces to the stated \(q=r\) and \(\ell=B=b+su\).  For nonnegative \(s,t,u\) with \(t\le u\),
\[
 \frac12\{t+\min(u,s)\}
 \le\min\{u,s+t\}
 \le t+\min(u,s).
\tag{3}
\]
For the upper inequality, the middle expression is at most both \(u\) and \(s+t\).  For the lower inequality, if \(u\le s+t\), its left side is at most \((t+u)/2\le u\); if \(u>s+t\), then \(s<u\) and its left side is \((t+s)/2\le s+t\).  Adding \(a\) proves the triangular display.

If \(s\ge\underline\sigma^2>0\), then
\[
 r\le a+m
 \le\max(1,\underline\sigma^{-2})(a+sm)
 \le\max(1,\underline\sigma^{-2})B.
\tag{4}
\]
This proves the fixed-positive-radius equivalence uniformly over every \(d\ge1\).

If \(u\ge1\), then \(m=1\) and
\[
 r=a+\min(1,s+t)\le a+s+t_c=B.
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, when \(t<1\) the right side contains \(s+t\), and when \(t\ge1\) it contains \(1\).  If \(u<1\), then \(d<nL\), so \(t<L/n\le1\), and hence \(t_c=t\) and \(B=b+sm\).  Under \(u\le Cb\),
\[
 r\le a+u\le(1+C)b\le(1+C)B.
\tag{6}
\]
Under \(s\le Cb\),
\[
 r\le a+s+t=b+s\le(1+C)b\le(1+C)B.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the three matching regimes hold with constants independent of \(\sigma\).  Multiplication by \(n^2L^2\) gives
\[
 u\le b
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d^2\le nL^2+dL^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Hence \(d\le\sqrt n\,L\) is sufficient; if \(d\le L^2\), then \(u=t(d/L^2)\le t\le b\).  Equation~(7) is the second parametric-dominance elbow.

Now suppose along a sequence that \(r/B\to\infty\).  Equations~\((5)\)--\((7)\) imply, for every fixed \(C<\infty\), eventually
\[
 u<1,\qquad u>Cb,\qquad s>Cb.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(b=o(u)\), \(b=o(s)\), and, by (4), \(s\to0\).  Since \(u<1\), the cap on \(t\) is inactive.  If \(u\) were bounded below by \(\eta>0\) on a subsequence, then eventually \(s+t<u\), so
\[
 r=b+s\le\max(1,\eta^{-1})(b+\eta s)
 \le\max(1,\eta^{-1})B,
\tag{10}
\]
a contradiction.  Hence \(u\to0\), proving that divergence is possible only when
\[
 b\ll u\ll1,\qquad b\ll s\ll1.
\tag{11}
\]
Furthermore
\[
 \frac ub=\frac{d^2}{L^2(n+d)},
\tag{12}
\]
and \(u=(d/(nL))^2\), which translate (11), up to boundary constants, to \(\sqrt n\,L\ll d\ll nL\) and \(\sigma^2\gg b\).

For \(d_n=n\), \(L_n=\log(en)\), and \(\sigma_n=L_n^{-1/2}\),
\[
 b_n=\frac2n,\qquad u_n=\frac1{L_n^2},\qquad s_n=\frac1{L_n}.
\tag{13}
\]
The upper benchmark is asymptotic to \(L_n^{-2}\), whereas the lower benchmark is asymptotic to \(L_n^{-3}\), because \(L_n^3/n\to0\).  Their ratio is therefore of order \(L_n\to\infty\).  Equations~\((4)\)--\((12)\) establish all stated matching regimes and localize the only unresolved wedge without asserting that either benchmark is exact inside it.
