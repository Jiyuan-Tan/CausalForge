Put
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad
 x:=\frac d{n^2},\qquad
 v:=\min\{1,u\}.
\]
Define the normalized upper and lower benchmarks
\[
 q:=\frac1n+\min\{v,\sigma^2+x\},
 \qquad
 \ell:=\frac1n+x+\sigma^2v.
\tag{1}
\]
By the definition of \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\), \(q=r_{n,d,\sigma}\).  Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\), respectively, give constants depending only on \(\epsilon\) such that
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\le C_{\epsilon}M^2q,
 \qquad
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\ell.
\tag{2}
\]
The support, overlap, moment, radius, and dimension conditions in (2) are exactly those of the two cited theorems.  The remaining argument is deterministic.

Fix \(s\in(0,2]\) and suppose \(s\le\sigma\le2\).  Since a minimum is no larger than either argument,
\[
 q\le\frac1n+v.
\tag{3}
\]
Since \(\sigma^2\ge s^2\) and \(x\ge0\),
\[
 \ell\ge\frac1n+s^2v.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (3)--(4),
\[
 q\le\max\{1,s^{-2}\}\ell.
\tag{5}
\]
The lower inequality in (2) and (5) give
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge \frac{c_{\epsilon}}{\max\{1,s^{-2}\}}M^2q,
\]
while the upper inequality in (2) gives the reverse comparison.  This proves matching for every fixed lower radius \(s>0\), including the boundaries \(d=1\), \(d=c_{\epsilon}n^2/L\), \(\sigma=s\), and \(\sigma=2\).

If \(u\ge1\), then \(v=1\).  When \(\sigma^2+x\le1\), (1) gives \(q=\ell\).  When \(\sigma^2+x>1\),
\[
 q=\frac1n+1<\frac1n+x+\sigma^2=\ell.
\]
The dimension range, after taking \(c_{\epsilon}\le1\), also gives
\[
 x=\frac d{n^2}\le\frac{c_{\epsilon}}L\le1.
\]
Together with \(n^{-1}\le1\) and \(\sigma^2\le4\),
\[
 q\le\ell\le6\le6q.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus (2) and (6) prove uniform matching in the saturation regime \(u\ge1\).

If \(d\le L^2\), then
\[
 u=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\le\frac d{n^2}=x,
\]
so \(v\le x\).  Therefore
\[
 q\le\frac1n+v\le\frac1n+x\le\ell.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (2) and (7) prove uniform matching in the small-alphabet regime.

It remains to test whether the two established benchmarks are uniformly comparable over all shrinking radii.  Let \(n\to\infty\), set \(d_n=n\), put \(L_n=\log(en)\), and take \(\sigma_n=L_n^{-1/2}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(0<\sigma_n<2\), the dimension condition holds, and
\[
 u_n=L_n^{-2},\qquad x_n=n^{-1},\qquad \sigma_n^2=L_n^{-1}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 q_n=\frac1n+\min\{L_n^{-2},L_n^{-1}+n^{-1}\}
 =\frac1n+L_n^{-2}\asymp L_n^{-2},
\tag{8}
\]
whereas
\[
 \ell_n=\frac2n+L_n^{-1}L_n^{-2}
 =\frac2n+L_n^{-3}\asymp L_n^{-3}.
\tag{9}
\]
Dividing (8) by (9) gives \(q_n/\ell_n\asymp L_n\to\infty\).  Since eventually \(L_n^2<d_n<nL_n\), the witness lies in the stated triangular regime.  This proves the localization claim: fixed interior radii and the two displayed dimension regimes are settled, but the proved bounds alone do not settle the fully uniform shrinking-radius problem.  No inference, coverage, or adaptation to unknown \(M\) or \(\sigma\) is asserted.
