Write
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad b:=\frac1n+\frac d{n^2},\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad \rho:=\sqrt u=\frac d{nL}.
\]
The already established upper and lower bounds imply, in the residual wedge where the relevant caps are inactive,
\[
 c_{\epsilon}M^2\{b+\sigma^2u\}
 \le \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2\{b+\min(u,\sigma^2)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Two rigorous calculations narrow the unresolved alternatives.

First, consider a binary one-arm source experiment with \(B(0)=0\), \(B(1)\mid X=k\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\eta_k)\), and \(\psi:=\sum_kp_k\eta_k\). Let \(K_a(\mathrm dy\mid b)\) be an arm-dependent but cell- and hypothesis-independent outcome channel whose output has the integrable conditional means required for membership in the target class. Put
\[
 \nu_{ab}:=\int y\,K_a(\mathrm dy\mid b),\qquad
 c:=\nu_{10}-\nu_{00},\qquad \gamma:=\nu_{11}-\nu_{10}.
\]
Linearity of integration gives
\[
 \mu_{0k}=\nu_{00},\qquad
 \mu_{1k}=\nu_{10}+\gamma\eta_k,\qquad
 \tau_k=c+\gamma\eta_k,\qquad
 \tau=c+\gamma\psi.
\tag{2}
\]
If this fixed channel maps the entire source class into \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), apply it to the supported two-cell law \(p_1=p_2=1/2\), \(\eta_1=0\), and \(\eta_2=1\). Equation~\((2)\) and approximate homogeneity give
\[
 \frac{|\gamma|}{2}
 =\max_{k\in\{1,2\}}|\tau_k-\tau|
 \le \sigma M,
 \qquad |\gamma|\le2\sigma M.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus every source target gap \(\Delta_{\psi}\) is sent to a gap at most \(2\sigma M\Delta_{\psi}\). The frozen one-arm hard family has \(\Delta_{\psi}\asymp\rho\) in the nonsaturated range, so data processing retains its total-variation certificate but produces only separation of order \(M\sigma\rho\). In the wedge, \(\sigma,\rho\to0\) and
\[
 \frac{\min(\sigma,\rho)}{\sigma\rho}
 =\frac1{\max(\sigma,\rho)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently the established hypothesis-independent channel architecture cannot deliver the requested nonproduct separation. This is not an impossibility theorem for hypothesis-dependent or jointly moment-matched paired constructions.

Second, the natural centered mixed-arm factorial lift has the desired deterministic approximation bias but not the required covariance. Take an estimation block of size \(m\), set \(d=m\), \(M=1\),
\[
 p_k=\frac1m,\qquad \pi_k=\frac12,\qquad
 \mu_{0k}=\mu_{1k}=0,
\]
and let the potential outcomes, conditionally on \(X\), be independent Rademacher variables independent of \(A\), with \(Y=Y(A)\). This law belongs to the frozen class at exact homogeneity. Give the construction its oracle pilot \(t=\tau=0\). For \(c\in\{0,1\}\) and \(j\ge0\), define
\[
 \widehat H_{k,c,j}(0)
 :=\frac1{(m)_{j+2}}
 \sum_{i_0,\ldots,i_{j+1}\ {\rm distinct}}
 \mathbf1\{X_{i_0}=k,A_{i_0}=0\}
 \mathbf1\{X_{i_1}=k,A_{i_1}=1\}
 (Y_{i_1}-Y_{i_0})
 \prod_{q=2}^{j+1}\mathbf1\{X_{i_q}=k,A_{i_q}=c\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The light-cell lift sums these monomials against \(g_{Kj}/B^{j+1}\), and its constant coefficient is
\[
 g_{K0}=\frac{K^2-1}{3}.
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(J_k:=\mathbf1\{N_{0k}=N_{1k}=1\}\). On \(J_k=1\), every term with \(j\ge1\) vanishes, while the two \(j=0\) terms coincide. Conditional independence of the Rademacher marks therefore yields, for the full light statistic \(Z\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Z\mid X,A)
 \ge
 2\left\{\frac{2g_{K0}}{B(m)_2}\right\}^{\!2}
 \sum_{k=1}^m J_k.
\tag{7}
\]
The multinomial occupancy law gives
\[
 E\!\left[\sum_{k=1}^mJ_k\right]
 =m\frac{(m)_2}{4m^2}
 \left(1-\frac1m\right)^{m-2}
 \ge c_0m.
\tag{8}
\]
With the reciprocal-approximation calibration \(K\asymp\log(em)\) and \(B\asymp\log(em)/m\), equations~\((6)\)--\((8)\) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Z)
 \ge c_1\frac{g_{K0}^2}{B^2m^3}
 \asymp \frac{\log^2(em)}m.
\tag{9}
\]
At \(d=m\) and \(\sigma=0\), the covariance inequality needed for a product-rate proof would be only of order \(m^{-1}+d/m^2\asymp m^{-1}\); equation~\((9)\) contradicts it by a factor of order \(\log^2(em)\). Oracle centering and independent sample splitting do not remove this diagonal, while deleting \(g_{K0}\) destroys the reciprocal approximation.

The valid population identity for that attempted upper route is
\[
 t+S_K(t)-\vartheta
 =\sum_k(\vartheta_k-\vartheta)(c_k-p_k)
 +(t-\vartheta)\left(1-\sum_kc_k\right),
\tag{10}
\]
where
\[
 \vartheta:=\frac{\tau(P)}M,\quad
 H_k(t):=s_{0k}s_{1k}(\vartheta_k-t),\quad
 S_K(t):=\sum_kH_k(t)\{r_K(s_{0k})+r_K(s_{1k})\},
\]
\[
 s_{ak}:=P(X=k,A=a),\quad \vartheta_k:=\frac{\tau_k}{M},\quad
 c_k:=s_{0k}s_{1k}\{r_K(s_{0k})+r_K(s_{1k})\}.
\]
Reciprocal approximation and overlap imply
\[
 |t+S_K(t)-\vartheta|^2
 \le C_{\epsilon}u\{\sigma^2+(t-\vartheta)^2\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the product-scaled deterministic bias is genuine, but this stochastic lift cannot realize it at product-rate variance. A stronger converse must use a radius-coupled, hypothesis-dependent construction with joint moment matching and exact fixed-sample normalization; a sharper upper bound must avoid amplifying singleton-pair noise. Neither object is supplied by the frozen core, so the OEQ remains open.
