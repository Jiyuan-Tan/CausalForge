\textbf{Proof.}
Put
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad
 x:=\frac d{n^2},\qquad
 v:=\min\{1,u\},
\]
and define the normalized upper and lower benchmarks
\[
 q:=\frac1n+\min\{v,\sigma^2+x\},
 \qquad
 \ell:=\frac1n+x+\sigma^2v.
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(q=r_{n,d,\sigma}\).  Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) give constants depending only on \(\epsilon\) such that
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\le C_{\epsilon}M^2q,
 \qquad
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\ell.
\tag{2}
\]
Every remaining step is a deterministic comparison of (1), so all iid sampling, support, overlap, fourth-moment, radius, and dimension conditions are precisely those in the two cited theorems.

Fix \(s\in(0,2]\) and suppose \(s\le\sigma\le2\).  Since a minimum is no larger than either argument,
\[
 q\le\frac1n+v.
\tag{3}
\]
Since \(\sigma^2\ge s^2\) and \(x\ge0\),
\[
 \ell\ge\frac1n+s^2v.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(q\le\max\{1,s^{-2}\}\ell\).  Combining this with (2) proves the first two-sided rate equivalence, with constants allowed to depend on \(s\).

Suppose next that \(u\ge1\), so \(v=1\).  If \(\sigma^2+x\le1\), then \(q=\ell\).  If \(\sigma^2+x>1\), then \(q=n^{-1}+1<\ell\).  The dimension range, reduced so that \(c_{\epsilon}\le1\), also gives \(x\le c_{\epsilon}/L\le1\).  With \(n^{-1}\le1\) and \(\sigma^2\le4\),
\[
 q\le\ell\le6\le6q.
\tag{5}
\]
Hence (2) yields uniform matching when \(u\ge1\).  If instead \(d\le L^2\), then
\[
 u=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\le\frac d{n^2}=x,
\]
so \(v\le x\) and
\[
 q\le\frac1n+v\le\frac1n+x\le\ell.
\tag{6}
\]
Together with (2), this proves uniform matching in the second displayed subregime.

To show why these comparisons do not settle all shrinking radii, let \(d_n:=n\), \(L_n:=\log(en)\), and \(\sigma_n:=L_n^{-1/2}\).  For sufficiently large \(n\), all support, radius, and dimension restrictions hold, and
\[
 u_n=L_n^{-2},\qquad x_n=n^{-1},\qquad \sigma_n^2=L_n^{-1}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 q_n=\frac1n+\min\{L_n^{-2},L_n^{-1}+n^{-1}\}
 =\frac1n+L_n^{-2}\asymp L_n^{-2},
\tag{7}
\]
whereas
\[
 \ell_n=\frac2n+L_n^{-3}\asymp L_n^{-3}.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(q_n/\ell_n\asymp L_n\to\infty\), exactly as claimed.

More precisely, in the unresolved region \(L^2<d<nL\) with \(\sigma\to0\), a lower-side resolution would require a realization-wise radius-constrained paired-cell or fuzzy-hypothesis construction with target separation of order
\[
 M\min\left\{\sigma,\frac d{nL}\right\},
\]
while retaining bounded total variation; squaring gives the selector's minimum term.  The existing channel instead gives only
\(M\sigma\min\{1,d/(nL)\}\), the already-proved product separation.  The alternative is a single data-driven estimator improving the selector to the product-scale lower benchmark.  Neither construction follows from the frozen dependencies, so the displayed sequence proves a gap between established bounds, not a lower bound on the true ratio. \(\square\)
