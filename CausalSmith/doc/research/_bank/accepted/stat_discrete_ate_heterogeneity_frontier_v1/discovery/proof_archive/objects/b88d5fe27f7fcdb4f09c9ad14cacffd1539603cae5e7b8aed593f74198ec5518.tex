Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and put \(L:=\log(en)\).  Choose \(c_\epsilon>0\) no larger than the dimension-range constants in Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\).  The upper theorem supplies the single data-driven map \(\widehat\tau_n^\star\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\) and gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E[(\widehat\tau_n^\star-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_\epsilon M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\tag{1}
\]
Using this same map as a witness in the infimum defining \(\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) yields the claimed minimax upper bound; no law-dependent oracle is used.

On exactly the same class and range, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_\epsilon M^2\left\{\frac1n+\frac d{n^2}
 +\sigma^2\min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right)\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Its binary subclasses have bounded outcomes and therefore satisfy the enlarged conditional second-central-moment condition.  The parametric component remains valid for the same reason.  Taking the smaller positive lower constant and the larger finite upper constant in (1)--(2) proves the display.  Both bounds use arbitrary cell masses, fixed overlap on positive-mass cells, the common mean envelope, and the conditional second-moment envelope; zero-mass cells impose no conditioning requirement.  Since the two numerical expressions are not asserted to be uniformly equivalent, the theorem is precisely a same-class bracket.
