For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there are constants \(0<c_{\epsilon}\le C_{\epsilon}<\infty\) such that, uniformly over every pair of integers \(n,d\ge1\) and every \(0\le\sigma\le2\),
\[
 c_{\epsilon}M^2\left\{\frac1n+\min\left(1,\frac d{n^2}\right)+\sigma^2\min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right)\right\}
 \le\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\]
Both inequalities hold on the same arbitrary-cell-mass real-outcome class under fixed overlap, the conditional-mean normalization, and the conditional-second-central-moment envelope.  This is an all-alphabet two-sided minimax bracket, not a claim that the two displayed benchmarks are uniformly order-equivalent at every shrinking interior radius.

The accepted-bank theorem \texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match/thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} establishes the unrestricted binary-outcome hybrid rate \(n^{-1}+d^2/[n^2\log^2 n]\).  The present theorem is not a restatement of that endpoint.  Its increment is the all-alphabet radius-indexed same-class bracket, the explicit weak-moment signed-mark construction and known-radius selector, the capped exact-homogeneity and radius-channel embeddings, and localization of every remaining possible mismatch to the shrinking-radius wedge; real outcomes alone are not claimed as novel.
