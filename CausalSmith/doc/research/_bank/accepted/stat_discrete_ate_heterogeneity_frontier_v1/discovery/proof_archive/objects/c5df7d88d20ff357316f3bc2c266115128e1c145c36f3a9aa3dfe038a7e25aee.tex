\textbf{Proof.}
Take a binary law in \(\mathcal D_d(\epsilon,\sigma)\).  If it is specified only by observed arm means, augment it by drawing \(B(0),B(1)\) conditionally on \(X\), independently of \(A\), with the prescribed Bernoulli means, and put \(B=B(A)\).  This augmentation preserves the observed law and gives consistency and conditional exchangeability.  Apply the affine map in the proposition.  For every positive-mass cell,
\[
 \mu_{ak}=M(\eta_{ak}-1/2)\in[-M/2,M/2],
\tag{1}
\]
and \(Y(a)\in\{-M/2,M/2\}\).  Hence
\[
 E[(Y-\mu_{ak})^4\mid A=a,X=k]\le M^4.
\tag{2}
\]
The map does not change \(p_k\) or \(\pi_k\), so overlap is preserved.  Moreover,
\[
 \tau_k=M(\eta_{1k}-\eta_{0k}),\qquad
 \tau(P)=M\sum_{k=1}^dp_k(\eta_{1k}-\eta_{0k}),
\tag{3}
\]
and therefore the defining radius inequality for \(\mathcal D_d(\epsilon,\sigma)\) gives
\[
 \max_{k:p_k>0}|\tau_k-\tau(P)|\le\sigma M.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (1)--(4), together with the constructed causal augmentation, verify every membership condition in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).  The inverse map on the image is \(B=Y/M+1/2\), so the embedding is injective.  Uniform masses and a common binary arm contrast give exact homogeneity, proving the assertion for \(\mathcal H_d(\epsilon)\).

The containment is strict.  Put all mass on \(X=1\), take \(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) independently of \((Y(0),Y(1))\), let \(Y(0),Y(1)\) be independent \(N(0,M^2/4)\) variables, and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  The conditional means are zero, the fourth central moment is \(3M^4/16\le M^4\), overlap holds because \(\pi_1=1/2\), and the sole effect deviation is zero.  This law belongs to \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) for every \(0\le\sigma\le2\), but its unbounded conditional outcome support excludes it from the two-point affine binary image.

On \(\mathcal H_d(\epsilon)\), the target and squared loss are multiplied by \(M\) and \(M^2\), respectively.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scaled\text{-}binary\text{-}exact\text{-}lower\text{-}transfer}\) therefore transfers the exact-homogeneity lower bound \(M^2\{n^{-1}+d/n^2\}\), with its stated fixed-sample range and constants.  For the one-arm family, conditionally replacing each binary response by
\[
 B'\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left(\frac12+\frac\sigma2(B-1/2)\right),
 \qquad Y=M(B'-1/2),
\tag{5}
\]
is a hypothesis-independent Markov kernel.  Data processing preserves the fuzzy-hypothesis total-variation bound, (3) is scaled by \(\sigma/2\), and (4) holds realization by realization.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}one\text{-}arm\text{-}lower}\), followed by the zero-mass-cell embedding used in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\), gives the displayed product lower term.  This records exactly which binary subclasses supply the two converse components.  The strict-superclass claim concerns the class on which the present upper estimator is proved; it does not claim that allowing real outcomes by itself was absent from the anchor's discussion. \(\square\)
