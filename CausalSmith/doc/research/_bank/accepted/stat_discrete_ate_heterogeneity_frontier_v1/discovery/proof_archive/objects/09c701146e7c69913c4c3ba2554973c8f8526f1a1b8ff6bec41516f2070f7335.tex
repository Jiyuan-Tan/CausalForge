Take any binary law in \(\mathcal H_{n,d}^{\mathrm{bin}}(\epsilon)\), write its binary potential outcomes as \(B(0),B(1)\), and define
\[
 Y(a)=M\{B(a)-1/2\},\qquad Y=Y(A).
\tag{1}
\]
If the source law is specified only through its observed conditional Bernoulli distributions, augment it by drawing \(B(0),B(1)\) conditionally on \(X\), independently of \(A\), with the prescribed arm means.  This gives consistency and conditional exchangeability without changing the observed experiment.  Overlap is unchanged.  Since \(Y(a)\in\{-M/2,M/2\}\), its conditional mean lies in \([-M/2,M/2]\) and its centered second moment is at most \(M^2\).  Cell effects and the ATE are multiplied by \(M\); exact homogeneity is preserved, so the resulting law belongs to \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) for every \(\sigma\ge0\).

The observed transformation \(B=Y/M+1/2\) is invertible.  Consequently, every \(T\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\) induces the binary estimator \(T/M\), and
\[
 E[(T-\tau(P))^2]
 =M^2E[(T/M-\psi(P^{\mathrm{bin}}))^2].
\tag{2}
\]
Restricting the supremum in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\) to these scaled laws, taking the infimum over \(T\), and applying Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}exact\text{-}homogeneity\text{-}lower}\) proves the claim.
