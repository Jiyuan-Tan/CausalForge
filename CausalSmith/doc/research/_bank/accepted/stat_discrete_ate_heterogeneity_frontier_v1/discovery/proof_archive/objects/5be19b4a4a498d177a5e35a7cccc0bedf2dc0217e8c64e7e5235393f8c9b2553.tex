Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), and let
\[
S_P:=\{k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:p_k>0\}.
\]
For \(k\in S_P\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) gives
\[
P(A=1,X=k)=p_k\pi_k\ge p_k\epsilon>0
\]
and
\[
P(A=0,X=k)=p_k(1-\pi_k)\ge p_k\epsilon>0.
\]
Thus both treatment-arm conditional laws given \(X=k\), and hence both observed conditional means appearing in the proposition, are determined by \(P_O\).

The replacement of \(\mathrm{ass:fourth\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\) by \(\mathrm{ass:second\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\), incorporated into \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), is sufficient for all integrability used here.  Indeed, for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(k\in S_P\), Cauchy--Schwarz, \(\mathrm{ass:second\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}normalization}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[|Y|\mid A=a,X=k]
&\le |\mu_{ak}|+E_P[|Y-\mu_{ak}|\mid A=a,X=k]\\
&\le \frac M2+
\left\{E_P[(Y-\mu_{ak})^2\mid A=a,X=k]\right\}^{1/2}\\
&\le \frac{3M}{2}<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because there are finitely many cells and two treatment arms, the same display and the law of total expectation also give \(E_P|Y|<\infty\).

Fix \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(k\in S_P\).  Consistency yields equality of the conditional laws of \(Y\) and \(Y(a)\) given \((A=a,X=k)\).  Conditional exchangeability yields equality of the conditional laws of \(Y(a)\) given \((A=a,X=k)\) and given \(X=k\).  The first-moment bound above therefore also supplies the required integrability of \(Y(a)\) given \(X=k\), and the two assumptions give the equation
\[
E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=k]
=E_P[Y(a)\mid A=a,X=k]
=E_P[Y(a)\mid X=k].
\]
Applying the law of total expectation over the finite support of \(X\), separately to the two integrable potential outcomes, now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)]
&=\sum_{k\in S_P}p_k
\left\{E_P[Y(1)\mid X=k]-E_P[Y(0)\mid X=k]\right\}\\
&=\sum_{k\in S_P}p_k
\left\{E_P[Y\mid A=1,X=k]-E_P[Y\mid A=0,X=k]\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(k\notin S_P\), choose arbitrary finite versions of the two conditional means.  Their summands vanish because \(p_k=0\), so the last sum equals the sum over \(k=1,\ldots,d\) and is independent of the chosen null-cell versions.  By \(\mathrm{def:ate\text{-}functional}\), its left-hand side is \(\tau(P)\).  Finally, \(p_k=P_O(X=k)\), and the two positive-probability conditional means in every \(k\in S_P\) are functionals of \(P_O\).  Hence
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k\{E_P[Y\mid A=1,X=k]-E_P[Y\mid A=0,X=k]\}
\]
is an observed-law functional and identifies the causal average treatment effect.  No fourth moment is used.
