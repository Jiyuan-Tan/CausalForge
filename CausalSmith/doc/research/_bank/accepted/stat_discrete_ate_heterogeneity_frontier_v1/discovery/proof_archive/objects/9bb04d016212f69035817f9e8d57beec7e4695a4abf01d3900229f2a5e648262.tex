Put \(\nu_{ak}:=\mu_{ak}/M\) and, on \(\{X=k,A=a\}\), write
\[
 V=\nu_{ak}+\xi,
 \qquad E[\xi\mid X=k,A=a]=0,
 \qquad E[\xi^2\mid X=k,A=a]\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
The last inequality is Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:second\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\), after normalization by \(M\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}normalization}\) gives \(|\nu_{ak}|\le1/2\).  Hence
\[
 E[\xi^2\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}]\le s_{ak},
 \qquad |z_{ak}|=s_{ak}|\nu_{ak}|\le s_{ak}/2.
\tag{2}
\]

Independence of the distinct sample indices in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:centered\text{-}product\text{-}estimator}\) gives
\[
 E[U_{k,j}^{a\mid b,c}]=z_{ak}s_{bk}s_{ck}^{j},
 \qquad E[F_{k,j}^{c}]=s_{0k}s_{1k}s_{ck}^{j}.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(z_{1k}s_{0k}-z_{0k}s_{1k}=s_{0k}s_{1k}\vartheta_k\), summing \((3)\) with the signed coefficients gives
\[
 E[\widehat P_k(t)]
 =s_{0k}s_{1k}(\vartheta_k-t)
 \sum_{c=0}^1\frac1B G_K(s_{ck}/B),
\tag{4}
\]
which is the asserted expectation identity.

We next prove the variance bound, retaining the signs that are essential to the radius factor.  Symmetrize each ordered statistic over its \(j+2\) arguments before multiplying two kernels.  In each symmetrized kernel first regroup
\[
 U_{k,j}^{1\mid0,c}-U_{k,j}^{0\mid1,c}-tF_{k,j}^{c}.
\tag{5}
\]
For the two distinguished opposite-arm observations, substitution of \((1)\) into \((5)\) gives the exact decomposition
\[
 \mathcal K_{k,c,j}(t)
 =\mathcal N_{k,c,j}+(\vartheta_k-t)\mathcal F_{k,c,j},
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\mathcal N_{k,c,j}\) is the signed sum of kernels having one centered mark \(\xi\), and \(\mathcal F_{k,c,j}\) is an unmarked opposite-arm factorial kernel.  Indeed, on an unordered pair containing one treated and one control observation, the symmetrized distinguished part of \((5)\) is one half of
\[
 (\xi_1-\xi_0)+(\nu_{1k}-\nu_{0k}-t)
 = (\xi_1-\xi_0)+(\vartheta_k-t).
\]
Thus the nuisance levels occur only through their difference; no triangle inequality has been applied before this cancellation.

Let \(\mathcal N_S\) and \(\mathcal M_S(t)\) be the centered sums generated by the first and second terms in \((6)\), respectively, after applying all coefficients \(g_{Kj}/B^{j+1}\) and summing over \(c,j,k\in S\).  Then
\[
 \sum_{k\in S}\{\widehat P_k(t)-E\widehat P_k(t)\}
 =\mathcal N_S+\mathcal M_S(t).
\tag{7}
\]

We audit the two variances by the ordered-index overlap expansion.  A product of two centered-mark kernels has expectation zero unless their marked sample indices are identified: if the marked indices are distinct, conditioning on all cell and arm selectors and then using \(E[\xi\mid X,A]=0\) annihilates the term.  When the marks are identified, the matched observation costs at most \((2)\).  For a partial matching of total size \(h\), cancellation of falling factorials gives
\[
 \frac{(m)_{j+r+4-h}}{(m)_{j+2}(m)_{r+2}}
 \le \frac e{m^h},
\tag{8}
\]
and there are at most \(K^{2h}/h!\) such matchings.  In the terms with only the two marks identified, the remaining sums preserve their signs and factor through \(G_K(s_{ck}/B)\).  The shifted-Chebyshev identity used in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\) implies
\[
 0\le xG_K(x)\le1,
 \qquad 0\le x\le1,
\tag{9}
\]
and overlap makes \(s_{0k}/s_{1k}\) and its reciprocal at most \((1-\epsilon)/\epsilon\).  Consequently the signed, single-match contribution is at most \(C_{\epsilon}/m\) after summing \(\sum_{k,a}s_{ak}=1\).

Every other surviving centered-mark term has at least one additional identified index and therefore an additional factor \(m^{-1}\) in \((8)\).  For these terms absolute summation is harmless.  With
\[
 G_K^+(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}|g_{Kj}|x^j
 \le6^K\max\{1,x^{K-2}\},
\tag{10}
\]
the same matching count as in \((8)\), together with \(p_k\le B/4\) and \(K/(mB)\le\alpha/\beta_{\epsilon}\), bounds all additional-match contributions by
\[
 \frac{C_{\epsilon}6^{2K}dB^2}{m}.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations \((8)\)--\((11)\) are an exhaustive partition: different cells cannot share the marked observation, and without a shared mark conditional centering makes their covariance zero.  Put \(\gamma:=2\alpha\log6<1/32\).  Since \(6^{2K}\le m^{\gamma}\), \(B\le C_{\epsilon}L_m/m\), and \(d\le\rho_{\epsilon}mL_m\), decreasing \(\rho_{\epsilon}\) and increasing \(N_{\epsilon}\) give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\mathcal N_S)
 \le \frac{C_{\epsilon}}m
 C_{\epsilon}m^{\gamma}\frac{dL_m^2}{m^3}
 \le \frac{C_{\epsilon}}m.
\tag{12}
\]

For the unmarked part, Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:approximate\text{-}homogeneity}\) yields
\[
 q_t:=\max_{k:p_k>0}|\vartheta_k-t|
 \le\sigma+|t-\vartheta|,
 \qquad q_t^2\le2\{\sigma^2+(t-\vartheta)^2\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Apply the same matching partition to \(\mathcal M_S(t)\), and only now take absolute values.  Within-cell overlaps use \((8)\)--\((10)\).  Distinct cells share no observation, and after subtraction of the product of expectations their sole covariance is the fixed-sample correction
\[
 \left|\frac{(m)_{j+r+4}}{(m)_{j+2}(m)_{r+2}}-1\right|
 \le\frac{2K^2}{m}.
\tag{14}
\]
It follows that
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{\mathcal M_S(t)\}
 \le C_{\epsilon}q_t^2\left[
 \frac1m+\frac d{m^2}
 +6^{2K}\left\{dB^2+\frac{d^2K^2B^2}{m}\right\}
 \right].
\tag{15}
\]
This is the ordinary unmarked factorial overlap sum; the first two terms are the one-index and order-two overlaps, the first term in braces collects the remaining within-cell matchings, and the second is \((14)\) summed over distinct cells.

The calibration converts the last display to the required scale.  Since \(q_t\le4\), \(6^{2K}\le m^{\gamma}\), \(B\le C_{\epsilon}L_m/m\), and \(K\le C L_m\), splitting at \(d=m^{\gamma}L_m^4\) gives
\[
 m^{\gamma}\frac{dL_m^2}{m^2}
 \le C\left\{\frac1m+\frac{d^2}{m^2L_m^2}\right\},
 \qquad
 m^{\gamma}\frac{d^2L_m^4}{m^3}
 \le C\frac{d^2}{m^2L_m^2},
\tag{16}
\]
once \(N_{\epsilon}\) is large enough that \(m^{2\gamma}L_m^6\le m\) and \(m^{\gamma}L_m^6\le m\).  Combining \((13)\)--\((16)\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{\mathcal M_S(t)\}
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left\{
 \frac1m+\frac d{m^2}
 +\frac{d^2}{m^2L_m^2}
 [\sigma^2+(t-\vartheta)^2]
 \right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Finally, \(\operatorname{Var}(U+W)\le2\operatorname{Var}(U)+2\operatorname{Var}(W)\).  Apply this identity to \((7)\), and use \((12)\) and \((17)\), to obtain the asserted variance bound.  The proof uses the outcome only through \((1)\)--\((2)\); hence no moment above the conditional second moment, and no boundedness of \(Y\), is required.
