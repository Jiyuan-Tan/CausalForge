Put
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad
 h:=\sigma^2+\frac d{n^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:robust\text{-}upper\text{-}construction\text{-}resolution}\) supplies total measurable maps \(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}},T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\) and, after taking the smaller dimension constant and the larger risk constant in its two displays, gives simultaneously
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2\left\{\frac1n+\min(1,u)\right\}
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2\left(\frac1n+h\right)
\tag{3}
\]
throughout the stated range.  The clipping and zero fallbacks established in that theorem cover empty empirical arms, a zero collision denominator, and every boundary value of \(\sigma\).

We inspect the three exhaustive branches of Definition~\(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}estimator}\).  If
\[
 h\le\min(1,u),
\tag{4}
\]
then \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}=T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\), and (3) is the desired bound because \(\min(1,u,h)=h\).  If
\[
 u<\min(1,h),
\tag{5}
\]
then \(u<1\), \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}=T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}\), and (2) is the desired bound because \(\min(1,u,h)=u\).  If neither (4) nor (5) holds, the tie convention in the first branch implies either \(1<u\) and \(1<h\), or \(1=u<h\).  Indeed, if \(\min(1,u)<h\) and \(u\ge\min(1,h)\), then \(u<1\) is impossible, and if \(u=1\) the strict failure of (4) gives \(h>1\).  Thus \(\min(1,u,h)=1\) in the fallback branch.  There \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}=0\), and Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) yields
\[
 (\widehat\tau_n^{\star}-\tau(P))^2=\tau(P)^2
 \le M^2
 \le M^2\left\{\frac1n+\min(1,u,h)\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (2)--(6), and enlarging \(C_{\epsilon}\) once, proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2
 \left\{\frac1n+\min(1,u,h)\right\}
 =C_{\epsilon}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\tag{7}
\]

For the comparison, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}collision\text{-}upper}\) records the collision remainder of order \(h\), apart from the common parametric term.  Since \(\min(1,u,h)\le h\), the selected remainder is never larger.  If \(u=o(h)\) and \(d=o(nL)\), then \(u=o(1)\), so eventually \(\min(1,u,h)=u\), and
\[
 \frac{\min(1,u,h)}{h}=\frac uh\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
\]
This proves the asserted strict order improvement.  The comparison uses the published binary collision guarantee only as a benchmark; (7) holds on the frozen real-outcome class under the conditional-second-moment envelope.
