Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\).  Put \(\nu_{ak}:=\mu_{ak}/M\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}normalization}\) gives \(|\nu_{ak}|\le1/2\).  Lyapunov's inequality and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:fourth\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\) give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(V\mid A=a,X=k)
 \le \{E_P[(V-\nu_{ak})^4\mid A=a,X=k]\}^{1/2}\le1.
\]
Consequently
\[
 E_P[V^2\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}]
 =s_{ak}\{\operatorname{Var}(V\mid A=a,X=k)+\nu_{ak}^2\}
 \le\frac54s_{ak}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is the only moment inequality needed for a repeated marked selector.

The distinct-index definition and independence show directly that
\[
 E_P[U_{k,a,j}]=z_{ak}p_ks_{ak}^{j}.
\tag{2}
\]
We next give the covariance count.  Multiply two ordered sums defining \(U_{k,a,j}\) and \(U_{\ell,b,r}\), and partition the product according to the partial matching between their two ordered index tuples.  A matching of size \(h\) leaves \(j+r+4-h\) distinct sample indices.  Since \(j+2,r+2\le K\), cancellation of falling factorials and \(4K^2\le m\) give
\[
 \frac{(m)_{j+r+4-h}}{(m)_{j+2}(m)_{r+2}}
 \le \frac{e}{m^h}.
\tag{3}
\]
The number of matchings of size \(h\) is at most \(K^{2h}/h!\).  If no two marked positions are identified, all repeated factors are indicators and the usual falling-factorial identity applies.  If one marked position is identified with an unmarked position, the corresponding expectation is \(z_{ak}\) or zero and satisfies \(|z_{ak}|\le s_{ak}/2\).  The two marked positions can be identified only when \(k=\ell\) and \(a=b\); their expectation is then bounded by (1).  Thus every overlap term is bounded by the same nonnegative coefficient calculation as for unmarked factorial monomials, except for a factor at most \(5/4\) on the common marked selector.

For completeness, the coefficient calculation is as follows.  Define
\[
 G_K(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}g_{Kj}x^j.
\]
The alternating signs imply that its absolute-coefficient majorant is \(G_K^+(x):=G_K(-x)\).  From the Chebyshev identity
\[
 G_K^+(x)=\frac{T_K(1+2x)-1-2K^2x}{2K^2x^2}
\]
and the closed form for \(T_K(t)\) on \(t\ge1\),
\[
 G_K^+(x)\le6^K\max\{1,x^{K-2}\},\qquad x\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]
Because \(p_k\le B/4\) on \(S\) and \(4K/m\le3B/4\), every intensity vector arising after the overlap replacements has total mass at most \(B\).  Summing the absolute values of the overlap terms in (3) against the coefficients and applying (4) therefore gives the following three contributions.  The matching containing the two marked positions is, by (1), Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\), and \(\sum_{k,a}s_{ak}=1\), at most \(C_{\epsilon}/m\).  All remaining within-category matchings are at most \(C_{\epsilon}6^{2K}dB^2\).  For distinct categories an overlap is impossible; subtracting the product of expectations leaves only the finite-population falling-factorial correction, and
\[
 \left|\frac{(m)_{j+r+4}}{(m)_{j+2}(m)_{r+2}}-1\right|
 \le \frac{2K^2}{m},
\tag{5}
\]
so their total is at most \(C_{\epsilon}6^{2K}d^2K^2B^2/m\).  Adding these three bounds proves the display.  Equations (1)--(5) also show explicitly why a fourth central moment is more than sufficient: the proof uses only the induced conditional second moment of the single linear mark, and never multiplies two outcomes unless their marked sample indices coincide.
