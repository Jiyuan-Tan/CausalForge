Put
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad
 h:=\sigma^2+\frac d{n^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
By Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:robust\text{-}upper\text{-}construction\text{-}resolution}\), after taking the smaller of its two dimension constants and the larger of its two risk constants, the two explicit total estimators satisfy simultaneously
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2\left\{\frac1n+\min(1,u)\right\}
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2\left(\frac1n+h\right).
\tag{3}
\]
The theorem cited above also proves that both maps belong to \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\), including every zero-denominator and out-of-calibration fallback.

We now examine the three mutually exhaustive branches in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}estimator}\).  If \(h\le\min\{1,u\}\), then \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}=T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\), the minimum of \(1,u,h\) is \(h\), and (3) gives
\[
 \sup_P E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2\left\{\frac1n+min(1,u,h)\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(u<\min\{1,h\}\), then \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}=T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}\), the minimum is \(u\), and (2) gives (4) again.

It remains to check the zero branch.  If \(\min\{1,u,h\}<1\), then either \(h\le u\), in which case \(h\le\min\{1,u\}\) and the first branch applies, or \(u<h\), in which case \(u<\min\{1,h\}\) and the second branch applies.  Hence the zero branch can occur only when
\[
 \min\{1,u,h\}=1.
\tag{5}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives \(|\tau(P)|\le M\).  Therefore the zero estimator has squared loss at most \(M^2\), which, using (5), is bounded by
\[
 M^2\le M^2\left\{\frac1n+min(1,u,h)\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (4)--(6) with the definition of \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\) proves the asserted uniform risk bound.  The selector is total and measurable because its branch choice depends only on the known numbers \(n,d,\sigma\), while each selected component is a total measurable map into \([-M,M]\).

For the comparison, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}collision\text{-}upper}\) records the collision remainder of order
\[
 h=\sigma^2+\frac d{n^2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The selector's nonparametric remainder is \(\min\{1,u,h\}\le h\), so it is never larger in order.  If \(u=o(h)\) and \(d=o(nL)\), then \(u=o(1)\), and for all sufficiently large indices the minimum equals \(u\).  Thus the selector remainder is \(u=o(h)\), proving the claimed strict rate comparison.  This last assertion compares the displayed guarantees; it does not extend the binary theorem's model by citation.
