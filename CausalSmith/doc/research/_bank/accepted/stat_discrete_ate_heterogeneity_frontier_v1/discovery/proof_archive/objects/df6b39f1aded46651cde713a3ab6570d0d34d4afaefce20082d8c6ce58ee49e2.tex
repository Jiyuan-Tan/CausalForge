Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) and work first in normalized outcome units.  Put
\[
 V:=Y/M,\qquad \nu_{ak}:=\mu_{ak}/M,\qquad
 s_{ak}:=P(X=k,A=a),\qquad z_{ak}:=E_P[V\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}],
\]
and
\[
 \varphi_k:=p_k(\nu_{1k}-\nu_{0k}).
\]
Then \(z_{ak}=s_{ak}\nu_{ak}\) and, by Definition~\(\mathrm{def:ate\text{-}functional}\),
\[
 \frac{\tau(P)}M=\sum_{k=1}^d\varphi_k.
\tag{1}
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}normalization}\) gives \(|\nu_{ak}|\le1/2\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) gives \(s_{ak}\ge\epsilon p_k\) on every positive-mass cell.  Lyapunov's inequality and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:fourth\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\) also give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(V\mid A=a,X=k)
 \le \{E_P[(V-\nu_{ak})^4\mid A=a,X=k]\}^{1/2}\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus all marked statistics below have finite second moments.  Zero-mass cells contribute zero and need no conditional quantity.

We first establish the approximation identity.  Let \(T_K\) be the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind and define, with polynomial continuation at zero,
\[
 H_K(x):=\frac{1-T_K(1-2x)}{2K^2},\qquad
 E_K(x):=\frac{H_K(x)}x,\qquad
 G_K(x):=\frac{1-E_K(x)}x.
\]
Expanding the shifted Chebyshev polynomial gives
\[
 G_K(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}(-1)^j
 \frac{2^{2j+3}}{K(K+j+2)}\binom{K+j+2}{2j+4}x^j
 =\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}g_{Kj}x^j.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(0\le x\le1\), the elementary inequality \(|T_K(1-2x)|\le1\) implies
\[
 0\le x\{1-xG_K(x)\}=H_K(x)\le K^{-2}.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(p_k\le B\), then \(0\le s_{ak}/B\le1\).  Using \(|z_{ak}|\le s_{ak}/2\), \(s_{ak}\ge\epsilon p_k\), and (4), we obtain
\[
 \left|p_k\nu_{ak}-\frac{p_kz_{ak}}B
 G_K(s_{ak}/B)\right|
 \le \frac{B}{2\epsilon K^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
The distinct-index identity in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\) states that
\[
 E_P[U_{k,a,j}]=z_{ak}p_ks_{ak}^{j}.
\]
Consequently, (3) shows that \(\widehat P_k\) is unbiased for
\[
 \frac{p_kz_{1k}}B G_K(s_{1k}/B)
 -\frac{p_kz_{0k}}B G_K(s_{0k}/B).
\tag{6}
\]

We next control the independent pilot classification.  Write
\[
 B_-:=\frac{\lambda_0L}{2m_0},\qquad
 B_+:=\frac{2\lambda_0L}{m_0}
\]
and let
\[
 \mathcal A:=
 \left\{\widehat{\mathcal H}\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge B_-\},\quad
 \widehat{\mathcal L}\subseteq\{k:p_k\le B_+\}\right\}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the balanced split and \(b_0=16\lambda_0\) imply \(B_+\le B/4\).  If \(p_k<B_-\), the pilot count has mean less than \(\lambda_0L/2\); if \(p_k>B_+\), its mean exceeds \(2\lambda_0L\).  The exponential-moment proof of the binomial Chernoff inequalities therefore yields
\[
 Q_P^{(n)}(\mathcal A^c)
 \le 2d\exp(-c\lambda_0L)\le Cn^{-6}
\tag{7}
\]
uniformly for \(d\le\rho_\epsilon nL\), after reducing \(\rho_\epsilon\le1\).  Here and below numerical constants are absolute unless decorated by \(\epsilon\).

Condition on the pilot block and suppose \(\mathcal A\) holds.  The selected light set is then deterministic relative to block \(I_1\) and is contained in \(\{k:p_k\le B/4\}\).  For sufficiently large \(N_\epsilon\),
\[
 K\ge2,\qquad 4(K+2)^2\le m_1,
 \qquad \frac{4(K+2)}{m_1}\le\frac{3B}{4},
\tag{8}
\]
so Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\) applies.  Equations (5)--(6), the variance--bias identity, and that lemma give
\[
 \begin{split}
 E\left[\left\{
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\widehat P_k
 -\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\varphi_k
 \right\}^2\middle|I_0\right]
 &\le C_\epsilon\left[\frac1{m_1}
 +6^{2K}\left\{dB^2+\frac{d^2K^2B^2}{m_1}\right\}
 +\frac{d^2B^2}{K^4}\right].
\end{split}
\tag{9}
\]
Put \(\kappa:=2\alpha_0\log6\le1/32\).  Since \(6^{2K}\le n^\kappa\), \(B\le CL/n\), and \(K\le CL\),
\[
 n^\kappa\frac{dL^2}{n^2}
 \le C\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\},
 \qquad
 n^\kappa\frac{d^2L^4}{n^3}
 \le C\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
For the first inequality, set \(q=d/(\sqrt nL)\), use \(2q\le1+q^2\), and note that \(n^{\kappa-1/2}L^3\) is bounded.  The second follows because \(n^{\kappa-1}L^6\) is bounded.  Moreover \(dB/K^2\le Cd/(nL)\).  Hence (9)--(10) prove
\[
 E\left[\left\{
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\widehat P_k
 -\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\varphi_k
 \right\}^2\middle|I_0\right]
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}
\tag{11}
\]
on \(\mathcal A\).

We now prove the analogous heavy-cell bound, including the missing-arm boundary.  Conditional on the pilot, write \(\mathcal H:=\widehat{\mathcal H}\), which is deterministic for block \(I_1\), and let \(m=m_1\).  Conditional on the category and treatment indicators in this block, (2) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathcal H}\widehat h_k
 \middle|(X_i,A_i)_{i\in I_1}\right)
 \le \frac1{m^2}\sum_{k\in\mathcal H}N_k^2
 \left\{\frac{\mathbf1\{N_{1k}>0\}}{N_{1k}}
 +\frac{\mathbf1\{N_{0k}>0\}}{N_{0k}}\right\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Given \(N_k=t\), each arm count is binomial with success probability in \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\).  The identity
\[
 E[(D+1)^{-1}]=\frac{1-(1-q)^{t+1}}{(t+1)q},
 \qquad D\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(t,q),
\]
and \(D^{-1}\mathbf1\{D>0\}\le2(D+1)^{-1}\) imply that the expectation of (12) is at most
\[
 \frac{C_\epsilon}{m^2}\sum_{k=1}^dE[N_k]le\frac{C_\epsilon}{m}.
\tag{13}
\]

The conditional outcome mean of the heavy sum equals
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{k\in\mathcal H}N_k(\nu_{1k}-\nu_{0k})
 -R_1+R_0,
 \qquad
 R_a:=\frac1m\sum_{k\in\mathcal H}N_k\nu_{ak}\mathbf1\{N_{ak}=0\}.
\tag{14}
\]
The first term in (14) is the empirical mean of the bounded function
\(\mathbf1\{X\in\mathcal H\}(\nu_{1X}-\nu_{0X})\).  It is unbiased for \(\sum_{k\in\mathcal H}\varphi_k\) and has variance at most \(1/m\).

It remains to bound \(R_a\).  Put \(q_{ak}:=P(A=a\mid X=k)\), \(s_{ak}=p_kq_{ak}\), and \(r_{ak}:=p_k(1-q_{ak})\).  Exact multinomial factorial moments give
\[
 \begin{split}
 E[N_k^2\mathbf1\{N_{ak}=0\}]
 &=mr_{ak}(1-s_{ak})^{m-1}
 +m(m-1)r_{ak}^2(1-s_{ak})^{m-2},\\
 E[N_kN_\ell\mathbf1\{N_{ak}=N_{a\ell}=0\}]
 &=m(m-1)r_{ak}r_{a\ell}
 (1-s_{ak}-s_{a\ell})^{m-2},\qquad k\ne\ell.
 \end{split}
\tag{15}
\]
Using \(|\nu_{ak}|\le1/2\), \(s_{ak}\ge\epsilon p_k\), and \(r_{ak}\le p_k\), (15) yields
\[
 E[R_a^2]
 \le C_\epsilon\left[\frac1m+
 \left\{\sum_{k\in\mathcal H}p_k e^{-c_\epsilon mp_k}\right\}^2\right].
\tag{16}
\]
On \(\mathcal A\), every \(k\in\mathcal H\) has \(p_k\ge B_-\ge cL/n\).  Once \(N_\epsilon\) is enlarged, the function \(x\mapsto xe^{-c_\epsilon mx}\) is decreasing on this interval and
\[
 \sup_{x\ge B_-}xe^{-c_\epsilon mx}
 \le \frac{C_\epsilon}{nL}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 E[R_a^2]\le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (13)--(14) and (17) gives
\[
 E\left[\left\{
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\widehat h_k
 -\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\varphi_k
 \right\}^2\middle|I_0\right]
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}
\tag{18}
\]
on \(\mathcal A\).

Let \(Z\) denote the normalized, unclipped sum of the heavy and light terms.  The selected sets partition \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\), so (1), (11), (18), and \((u+v)^2\le2u^2+2v^2\) show
\[
 E[\mathbf1_{\mathcal A}\{Z-\tau(P)/M\}^2]
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.
\tag{19}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives \(\tau(P)/M\in[-1,1]\).  Projection onto \([-1,1]\) cannot increase loss on \(\mathcal A\), and its loss is at most four on \(\mathcal A^c\).  Equations (7) and (19), followed by restoration of \(M^2\), prove
\[
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_\epsilon M^2\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}
\tag{20}
\]
whenever \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon nL\).  Taking \(\rho_\epsilon\le1\) makes the second term in (20) equal to the required minimum branch in this construction regime.

If \(d>\rho_\epsilon nL\), the prescribed estimator is zero and Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives squared loss at most \(M^2\).  Since
\[
 \min\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}\ge\rho_\epsilon^2,
\]
this loss is bounded by the asserted right-hand side after enlarging \(C_\epsilon\).  If \(n<N_\epsilon\), the same zero fallback is bounded because \(1\le N_\epsilon/n\).  These arguments cover, in particular, all \(1\le d\le c_\epsilon n^2/L\), after choosing any positive \(c_\epsilon\) no larger than the dimension constant in the statement.

Finally, every displayed empirical arm ratio is explicitly multiplied by its positive-denominator indicator, and the outer clipping is defined on all real inputs.  The factorial statistic can be evaluated after aggregation from the marked cell sum and falling-factorial cell counts; for example its unnormalized ordered sum is
\[
 \left(\sum_{i\in I_1}V_i\mathbf1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\}\right)
 (N_{ak}^{(1)}-1)_j(N_k^{(1)}-j-1),
\]
with value zero when there are insufficient distinct indices.  Computing the Chebyshev coefficients and all degrees therefore takes at most \(O(dK^2)\) arithmetic operations after cell aggregation.  The construction is measurable, clipped into \([-M,M]\), and has the stated bounded fallback at every division boundary.
