Set
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad a:=\frac1n,\qquad t:=\frac d{n^2},
 \qquad b:=a+t,\qquad u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},
 \qquad s:=\sigma^2.
\tag{1}
\]
First suppose that \(L^2<d<nL\).  Then
\[
 t<u<1,
\tag{2}
\]
because \(u/t=d/L^2>1\) and \(u=(d/(nL))^2<1\).  Hence the selector benchmark is
\[
 q=a+\min\{u,s+t\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For arbitrary nonnegative \(s,t,u\) satisfying \(t\le u\),
\[
 \frac12\{t+\min(u,s)\}
 \le \min\{u,s+t\}
 \le t+\min(u,s).
\tag{4}
\]
For the upper inequality, the middle term is at most both \(u\) and \(s+t\), and hence at most \(t+\min(u,s)\).  For the lower inequality, if \(u\le s+t\), then its left side is at most \((t+u)/2\le u\).  If \(u>s+t\), then \(s<u\), so its left side is \((t+s)/2\le s+t\).  Adding \(a\) to (4) and using \(a\ge0\) gives
\[
 \frac12\{b+\min(u,s)\}
 \le q\le b+\min(u,s).
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(u<1\), the converse benchmark is exactly
\[
 \ell=b+s\min(1,u)=b+su.
\tag{6}
\]

For the full stipulated dimension range, write
\[
 m:=\min(1,u),\qquad
 r:=a+\min\{m,s+t\},\qquad
 B:=b+sm.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(r=r_{n,d,\sigma}\).  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) gives risk at most \(C_\epsilon M^2r\), while Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) gives risk at least \(c_\epsilon M^2B\).

If \(\sigma\ge\underline\sigma>0\), then \(s\ge\underline\sigma^2\), whence
\[
 r\le a+m
 \le \max\{1,\underline\sigma^{-2}\}(a+sm)
 \le \max\{1,\underline\sigma^{-2}\}B.
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (8) with the two risk bounds proves the fixed-positive-radius equivalence, after changing constants only as a function of \(\epsilon\) and \(\underline\sigma\).

The three uniform matching regimes follow from direct comparisons.  If \(u\ge1\), then \(m=1\) and
\[
 r=a+\min(1,s+t)\le a+s+t=b+s=B.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(u\le Cb\) for a fixed \(C\ge0\), then
\[
 r\le a+m\le a+u\le(1+C)b\le(1+C)B.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(s\le Cb\) for a fixed \(C\ge0\), then
\[
 r\le a+s+t=b+s\le(1+C)b\le(1+C)B.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(C<0\), the latter two conditions are impossible, while (9) remains unchanged.  Thus whenever at least one displayed regime holds, the constants are independent of \(\sigma\in[0,2]\).

Multiplication by the positive number \(n^2L^2\) gives
\[
 u\le b
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d^2\le nL^2+dL^2.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, \(d\le\sqrt n\,L\) implies (12).  If \(d\le L^2\), then \(u=t(d/L^2)\le t\le b\), so the former small-alphabet regime is included.  Equation~(11) applies for every admissible alphabet whenever \(\sigma^2=s\le Cb\).  Equations~(10) and (11) are the two parametric-dominance elbows.

It remains to localize possible divergence.  Consider any subsequence along which \(r/B\to\infty\).  Equations~(9)--(11) imply that, for every fixed \(C<\infty\), eventually
\[
 u<1,\qquad u>Cb,\qquad s>Cb.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently \(b=o(u)\) and \(b=o(s)\).  Equation~(8) rules out every further subsequence on which \(s\) is bounded away from zero, so \(s\to0\).  It follows that \(b\to0\), and hence \(t\to0\), because \(0\le t\le b\).  If \(u\) were bounded below by some \(\eta>0\) on a further subsequence, then eventually \(s+t<u\), and (7) would yield
\[
 r=a+s+t=b+s
 \le \max\{1,\eta^{-1}\}(b+\eta s)
 \le \max\{1,\eta^{-1}\}B,
\tag{14}
\]
contradicting divergence.  Hence \(u\to0\).  Thus divergence can occur only when
\[
 b\ll u\ll1,
 \qquad b\ll s\ll1.
\tag{15}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac ub=\frac{d^2}{L^2(n+d)}.
\tag{16}
\]
Together with \(u=(d/(nL))^2\), relation (16) gives, up to boundary constants in the triangular asymptotic range, \(\sqrt n\,L\ll d\ll nL\); the second condition in (15) is \(\sigma^2\gg n^{-1}+d/n^2\).

Finally, take \(d_n=n\), \(L_n:=\log(en)\), and \(\sigma_n:=L_n^{-1/2}\).  Then
\[
 b_n=\frac2n,\qquad u_n=\frac1{L_n^2},
 \qquad s_n=\frac1{L_n}.
\tag{17}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the numerator and denominator in the theorem's ratio are, respectively,
\[
 r_n=\frac1n+\frac1{L_n^2}\asymp\frac1{L_n^2},
 \qquad
 B_n=\frac2n+\frac1{L_n^3}\asymp\frac1{L_n^3},
\tag{18}
\]
because \(L_n^3/n\to0\).  Therefore
\[
 \frac{r_n}{B_n}\asymp L_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{19}
\]
Exact homogeneity is covered by (11), every fixed positive radius, including \(\sigma=2\), is covered by (8), saturation is covered by (9), and the alphabet and elbow claims follow from (10)--(12).  Thus (15) is the only regime not decided by the current upper and lower bounds.  This conclusion compares proved benchmarks only and does not assert that either benchmark is the exact minimax rate inside the wedge.
