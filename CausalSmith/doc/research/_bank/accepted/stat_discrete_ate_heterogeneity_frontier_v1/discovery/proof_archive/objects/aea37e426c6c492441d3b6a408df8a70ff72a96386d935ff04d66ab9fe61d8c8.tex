Put
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad
 h:=\sigma^2+\frac d{n^2},
 \qquad q:=\min\{1,u,h\}.
\]
First identify the two quantities being compared.  For a binary-outcome law in the radius-\(\sigma\) class, the heterogeneity parameter in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}collision\text{-}upper}\) obeys \(\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}\le\sigma\).  Hence the polynomial heterogeneity-and-occupancy remainder in that published guarantee, stated uniformly over this radius class, is
\[
 \sigma^2+\frac d{n^2}=h.
\]
The exponentially small term in the cited bias bound is a separate empty-occupancy boundary term; it is not a heterogeneity-dependent remainder and is not part of the comparison asserted here.

By Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper\text{-}all\text{-}d}\), the selector's corresponding remainder is \(q\).  Since a minimum is no larger than any of its entries,
\[
 q=\min\{1,u,h\}\le h.
\]
This proves the first assertion for every \(n,d\ge1\) and every \(0\le\sigma\le2\); no positivity issue arises because \(h\ge d/n^2>0\).

For the strict comparison, consider any sequence along which
\[
 u=o(h)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 d=o\!\left(n\log(en)\right).
\]
The second condition gives
\[
 u=\left\{\frac{d}{n\log(en)}\right\}^2=o(1).
\]
The first condition and \(h>0\) give \(u/h\to0\), so eventually \(u<h\); the preceding display also gives eventually \(u<1\).  Consequently the minimum is eventually attained at \(u\), and therefore
\[
 q=u,
 \qquad
 \frac qh=\frac uh\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the selector remainder is strictly smaller in order than the published radius-uniform collision remainder in the stated regime.  This argument uses the published result only to name the external binary benchmark; the selector bound itself is supplied independently by Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper\text{-}all\text{-}d}\).
