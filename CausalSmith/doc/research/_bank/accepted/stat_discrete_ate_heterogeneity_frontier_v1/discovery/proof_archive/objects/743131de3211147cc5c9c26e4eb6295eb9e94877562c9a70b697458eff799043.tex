Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\).  Normalize the outcome and the arm means by
\[
 V:=Y/M,\qquad \nu_{ak}:=\mu_{ak}/M,
\]
and put
\[
 s_{ak}:=P(X=k,A=a),\qquad
 z_{ak}:=E_P[V\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}],\qquad
 \varphi_k:=p_k(\nu_{1k}-\nu_{0k}).
\]
For \(j\in\{0,1\}\), define the block counts
\[
 N_k^{(j)}:=\sum_{i\in I_j}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k\},
 \qquad
 N_{ak}^{(j)}:=\sum_{i\in I_j}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\}.
\]
Definition~\(\mathrm{def:ate\text{-}functional}\), mean normalization, and overlap give
\[
 \frac{\tau(P)}M=\sum_{k=1}^d\varphi_k,\qquad
 z_{ak}=s_{ak}\nu_{ak},\qquad
 |\nu_{ak}|\le\frac12,\qquad
 s_{ak}\ge\epsilon p_k.
\tag{1}
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:second\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(V\mid A=a,X=k)\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
This is the only tail control used below.

Let \(T_K\) be the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind and, with continuous extensions at zero, define
\[
 H_K(x):=\frac{1-T_K(1-2x)}{2K^2},\qquad
 E_K(x):=\frac{H_K(x)}x,\qquad
 G_K(x):=\frac{1-E_K(x)}x.
\]
Expanding the shifted Chebyshev polynomial gives
\[
 G_K(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}(-1)^j
 \frac{2^{2j+3}}{K(K+j+2)}\binom{K+j+2}{2j+4}x^j
 =\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}g_{Kj}x^j.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(0\le x\le1\), write \(1-2x=\cos\theta\).  The identity \(T_K(\cos\theta)=\cos(K\theta)\) implies \(|T_K(1-2x)|\le1\), and therefore
\[
 0\le x\{1-xG_K(x)\}=H_K(x)\le K^{-2}.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(p_k\le B\), equations~\((1)\) and \((4)\), with \(x=s_{ak}/B\in[0,1]\), imply
\[
 \left|p_k\nu_{ak}-\frac{p_kz_{ak}}B G_K(s_{ak}/B)\right|
 =p_k|\nu_{ak}|\,|1-xG_K(x)|
 \le\frac{B}{2\epsilon K^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Independence of the ordered distinct indices in \(U_{k,a,j}\) gives
\[
 E_P[U_{k,a,j}]=z_{ak}p_ks_{ak}^{j}.
\]
Consequently \(\widehat P_k\) is unbiased for
\[
 \frac{p_kz_{1k}}B G_K(s_{1k}/B)-
 \frac{p_kz_{0k}}B G_K(s_{0k}/B).
\tag{6}
\]

For the independent pilot block put
\[
 B_-:=\frac{\lambda_0L}{2m_0},\qquad
 B_+:=\frac{2\lambda_0L}{m_0},
\]
and define the event
\[
 \mathcal A:=\left\{
 \widehat{\mathcal H}\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge B_-\},\quad
 \widehat{\mathcal L}\subseteq\{k:p_k\le B_+\}
 \right\}.
\]
The balanced split and \(b_0=16\lambda_0\) imply \(B_+\le B/4\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  For completeness, if \(D\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(t,q)\), exponential Markov applied to \(E[e^{uD}]=(1-q+qe^u)^t\) at \(u=\log2\) and \(u=-\log2\) gives
\[
 P(D\ge2tq)\le(e/4)^{tq},
 \qquad
 P(D\le tq/2)\le e^{-tq/8}.
\]
Applying these inequalities on the two sides of the pilot threshold and taking a union bound over the \(d\) cells yields
\[
 Q_P^{(n)}(\mathcal A^c)
 \le2d\exp(-c\lambda_0L)
 \le Cn^{-6}
\tag{7}
\]
uniformly for \(d\le\rho_{\epsilon}nL\), after decreasing \(\rho_{\epsilon}\) if necessary.

Condition on the pilot block and suppose \(\mathcal A\) occurs.  Then the light set is deterministic relative to \(I_1\) and is contained in \(\{k:p_k\le B/4\}\).  Since \(K=\lfloor\alpha_0L\rfloor\), \(B=b_0L/m_1\), and \(\alpha_0,b_0\) are the displayed fixed constants, increasing \(N_{\epsilon}\) ensures
\[
 K\ge2,\qquad 4(K+2)^2\le m_1,\qquad
 \frac{4(K+2)}{m_1}\le\frac{3B}{4}.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations~\((5)\)--\((6)\) and Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\) therefore give
\[
\begin{split}
 E_P\left[\left\{
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\widehat P_k-
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\varphi_k
 \right\}^2\middle|I_0\right]
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left[
 \frac1{m_1}+6^{2K}\left\{dB^2+
 \frac{d^2K^2B^2}{m_1}\right\}
 +\frac{d^2B^2}{K^4}
 \right].
\end{split}
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, the final term is the square of the sum of the cellwise biases in \((5)\), and the other terms are precisely the covariance lemma's variance bound.

Let \(\kappa:=2\alpha_0\log6\le1/32\).  Since \(6^{2K}\le n^\kappa\), \(B\le CL/n\), and \(K\le CL\), elementary completion of squares gives, uniformly in \(d\ge1\),
\[
 n^\kappa\frac{dL^2}{n^2}
 \le C\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\},
 \qquad
 n^\kappa\frac{d^2L^4}{n^3}
 \le C\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Also \(dB/K^2\le Cd/(nL)\).  Substitution in \((9)\) proves
\[
 E_P\left[\left\{
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\widehat P_k-
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\varphi_k
 \right\}^2\middle|I_0\right]
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left\{\frac1n+
 \frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]

We next treat the heavy cells.  Conditional on the pilot and on the category and treatment indicators in \(I_1\), equation~\((2)\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P\left(
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\widehat h_k
 \middle|(X_i,A_i)_{i\in I_1}\right)
 \le\frac1{m_1^2}\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}(N_k^{(1)})^2
 \left\{
 \frac{\mathbf1\{N_{1k}^{(1)}>0\}}{N_{1k}^{(1)}}+
 \frac{\mathbf1\{N_{0k}^{(1)}>0\}}{N_{0k}^{(1)}}
 \right\}.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(D\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(t,q)\), integration of \(E[x^D]=(1-q+qx)^t\) over \([0,1]\) gives
\[
 E[(D+1)^{-1}]=\frac{1-(1-q)^{t+1}}{(t+1)q}.
\]
Together with \(D^{-1}\mathbf1\{D>0\}\le2(D+1)^{-1}\), overlap, and conditioning first on \(N_k^{(1)}\), this shows that the expectation of the right side of \((12)\) is at most \(C_{\epsilon}/m_1\).

The conditional outcome mean of the heavy sum is
\[
 \frac1{m_1}\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}N_k^{(1)}
 (\nu_{1k}-\nu_{0k})-R_1+R_0,
\]
where
\[
 R_a:=\frac1{m_1}\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}
 N_k^{(1)}\nu_{ak}\mathbf1\{N_{ak}^{(1)}=0\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Conditional on the pilot, the first term is an empirical average of a function bounded in absolute value by one; it is unbiased for \(\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\varphi_k\) and has variance at most \(1/m_1\).  Exact multinomial factorial moments, \(|\nu_{ak}|\le1/2\), and \(s_{ak}\ge\epsilon p_k\) give
\[
 E_P[R_a^2\mid I_0]
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left[
 \frac1{m_1}+
 \left\{\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}
 p_ke^{-c_{\epsilon}m_1p_k}\right\}^2
 \right].
\tag{14}
\]
On \(\mathcal A\), every selected heavy cell has \(p_k\ge B_-\ge cL/n\).  Hence \(p_ke^{-c_{\epsilon}m_1p_k}\le C_{\epsilon}/(nL)\) after increasing \(N_{\epsilon}\), and
\[
 E_P[R_a^2\mid I_0]
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left\{\frac1n+
 \frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Combining \((12)\)--\((15)\) proves the heavy-cell analogue of \((11)\).

The selected heavy and light sets partition all cells.  Let \(Z\) denote the normalized sum before clipping.  The heavy and light bounds, equation~\((1)\), and \((x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\) yield
\[
 E_P\left[\mathbf1_{\mathcal A}
 \left\{Z-\frac{\tau(P)}M\right\}^2\right]
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left\{\frac1n+
 \frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives \(\tau(P)/M\in[-1,1]\).  Projection onto \([-1,1]\) therefore cannot increase squared loss, and the projected squared loss is at most four on \(\mathcal A^c\).  Equations~\((7)\) and \((16)\) prove the asserted rate when \(n\ge N_{\epsilon}\) and \(d\le\rho_{\epsilon}nL\).

If \(d>\rho_{\epsilon}nL\), the zero fallback has loss at most \(M^2\), while
\[
 \min\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}
 \ge\min\{1,\rho_{\epsilon}^2\}.
\]
If \(n<N_{\epsilon}\), the same conclusion follows from \(1\le N_{\epsilon}/n\).  Enlarging \(C_{\epsilon}\) proves the displayed risk inequality throughout \(1\le d\le c_{\epsilon}n^2/L\).

All sample-mean ratios are multiplied by positive-denominator indicators.  Moreover, after cell aggregation, the unnormalized ordered sum in \(U_{k,a,j}\) equals
\[
 \left(\sum_{i\in I_1}V_i\mathbf1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\}\right)
 (N_{ak}^{(1)}-1)_j(N_k^{(1)}-j-1),
\]
with value zero whenever there are too few distinct indices.  Thus the estimator is measurable and total.  Computing all coefficients and factorial terms takes \(O(dK^2)\) arithmetic operations, and no boundary division is left undefined.

Finally, equations~\((1)\)--\((2)\) imply for every law in the note's real-outcome class that
\[
 E_P[Y^2\mid A=a,X=k]
 =\operatorname{Var}_P(Y\mid A=a,X=k)+\mu_{ak}^2
 \le M^2+\frac{M^2}{4}=\frac{5M^2}{4}.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the broader formal outcome support does not itself supply a novelty claim relative to the weak-second-moment rate-valid envelope stated by Zeng--Balakrishnan--Han--Kennedy.  The construction-level increment proved here is the explicit fixed-sample signed one-mark Chebyshev/factorial estimator, together with the covariance control that avoids an extra logarithmic loss; downstream it is paired with the collision estimator in the known-radius selector and converse comparison.
