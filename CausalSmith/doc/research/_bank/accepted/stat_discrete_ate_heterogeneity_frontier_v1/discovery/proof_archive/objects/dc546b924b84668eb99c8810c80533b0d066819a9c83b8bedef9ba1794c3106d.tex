 q:=\min\{u,\sigma^2+x\},
 \qquad

\]
then
\[
 \frac{x+m}{2}\le q\le x+m.
\]
Indeed, \(q\le x+m\) follows by considering whether \(m=\sigma^2\) or \(m=u\).  Conversely, both arguments of the minimum defining \(q\) are at least \((x+m)/2\): one has \(u>(x+m)/2\) because \(x<u\) and \(m\le u\), while \(\sigma^2+x\ge x+m\).  Thus the current selector already has, up to a factor two, the minimum-type remainder \(x+\min\{u,\sigma^2\}\).  The established converse has remainder \(x+\sigma^2u\).

There is also an exact population cancellation for a product-rate upper candidate.  Normalize \(V=Y/M\), put \(\vartheta=\tau(P)/M\), \(s_{ak}=P(X=k,A=a)\), \(z_{ak}=E_P[V\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}]\), and \(\vartheta_k=\tau_k/M\).  For a scalar \(t\), define
\[
 H_k(t):=s_{0k}z_{1k}-s_{1k}z_{0k}-t s_{0k}s_{1k}
 =s_{0k}s_{1k}(\vartheta_k-t).
\]
Let \(r_K\) be any degree-\(K\) reciprocal polynomial on the light-cell interval, and set
\[
 e_{ak}:=1-s_{ak}r_K(s_{ak}),
 \qquad
 R_{Kk}:=r_K(s_{0k})+r_K(s_{1k}),
 \qquad
 c_k:=s_{0k}s_{1k}R_{Kk}.
\]
The identity
\[
 \frac{p_k}{s_{0k}s_{1k}}=\frac1{s_{0k}}+\frac1{s_{1k}}
\]
gives
\[
 c_k-p_k=-s_{1k}e_{0k}-s_{0k}e_{1k}.
\]
Writing \(S_K(t):=\sum_kH_k(t)R_{Kk}\) and using \(\sum_kp_k(\vartheta_k-\vartheta)=0\), direct expansion yields
\[
 t+S_K(t)-\vartheta
 =\sum_k(\vartheta_k-\vartheta)(c_k-p_k)
 +(t-\vartheta)\left(1-\sum_kc_k\right).
\]
Consequently, if the reciprocal polynomial obeys
\[
 |e_{ak}|\le \frac{B}{K^2s_{ak}},
\]
then overlap and \(|\vartheta_k-\vartheta|\le\sigma\) give
\[
 |t+S_K(t)-\vartheta|
 \le C_{\epsilon}\{\sigma+|t-\vartheta|\}\frac{dB}{K^2}.
\]
Taking \(B\asymp L/n\), \(K\asymp L\), and an independent collision pilot for \(t\) therefore gives the desired product-radius order for the squared population bias.
