Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and put
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad
 v:=\min\left\{1,\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) supplies the explicit selector \(\widehat\tau_n^\star\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\) and proves, uniformly on the stated class and dimension range,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(\widehat\tau_n^\star-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_\epsilon M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\tag{2}
\]
Because the minimax risk is the infimum over every member of \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\), inserting this one data-driven estimator in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \le C_\epsilon M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\tag{3}
\]

On the same model class, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_\epsilon M^2\left\{\frac1n+\frac d{n^2}+\sigma^2v\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Its proof combines the radius channel with the exact-homogeneity subclass and uses Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\) to cover the parametric component and finite source cutoffs.  Thus no estimator-side hypothesis or different outcome class enters (4).  Taking the smaller dimension constant and, respectively, the smaller lower-bound constant and larger upper-bound constant in (3)--(4) proves the displayed bracket simultaneously.  Both sides refer to the same \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), so the conclusion is a same-class bracket.  No step asserts uniform order equivalence of its endpoints at shrinking interior radii.
