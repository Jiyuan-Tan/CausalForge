Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:second\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\), incorporated into \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), is sufficient for all integrability used here.  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), and define the positive-mass support
\[
S_P:=\{k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:p_k>0\}.
\]
For every \(k\in S_P\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) gives
\[
P(A=1,X=k)=p_k\pi_k\ge p_k\epsilon>0
\]
and
\[
P(A=0,X=k)=p_k(1-\pi_k)\ge p_k\epsilon>0.
\]
Consequently, both treatment-arm conditional laws given \(X=k\), and hence both conditional means in the claimed formula, are uniquely determined by the observed law \(P_O\).

We next verify that these means and the causal contrast are integrable.  For each \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(k\in S_P\), the triangle inequality, conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:second\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\), and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}normalization}\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[|Y|\mid A=a,X=k]
&\le |\mu_{ak}|+E_P[|Y-\mu_{ak}|\mid A=a,X=k]\\
&\le \frac{M}{2}
+\left\{E_P[(Y-\mu_{ak})^2\mid A=a,X=k]\right\}^{1/2}\\
&\le \frac{3M}{2}<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
There are only finitely many positive-mass cells and two treatment arms.  The law of total expectation therefore gives \(E_P|Y|<\infty\).

Fix \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(k\in S_P\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) identifies the conditional law of \(Y\) given \((A=a,X=k)\) with that of \(Y(a)\) given \((A=a,X=k)\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:conditional\text{-}exchangeability}\) identifies the latter conditional law with the law of \(Y(a)\) given \(X=k\).  The preceding finite-first-moment bound is consequently inherited by \(Y(a)\) conditional on \(X=k\), and
\[
E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=k]
=E_P[Y(a)\mid A=a,X=k]
=E_P[Y(a)\mid X=k].
\]
Summing these conditional expectations over the finite support of \(X\), separately for the two integrable potential outcomes, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)]
&=\sum_{k\in S_P}p_k
\left\{E_P[Y(1)\mid X=k]-E_P[Y(0)\mid X=k]\right\}\\
&=\sum_{k\in S_P}p_k
\left\{E_P[Y\mid A=1,X=k]-E_P[Y\mid A=0,X=k]\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(k\notin S_P\), choose arbitrary finite versions of the two conditional means.  The corresponding summands are zero because \(p_k=0\); hence the last display equals the sum over all \(k=1,\ldots,d\) and is independent of those null-cell versions.  By \(\mathrm{def:ate\text{-}functional}\), the left-hand side is \(\tau(P)\).  Finally, \(p_k=P_O(X=k)\), while overlap makes both arm-specific conditional means observed-law functionals in every positive-mass cell.  Thus
\[
\tau(P)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k
\left\{E_P[Y\mid A=1,X=k]-E_P[Y\mid A=0,X=k]\right\}
\]
is a computable functional of \(P_O\) and identifies the causal average treatment effect.  Only conditional second moments are used.
