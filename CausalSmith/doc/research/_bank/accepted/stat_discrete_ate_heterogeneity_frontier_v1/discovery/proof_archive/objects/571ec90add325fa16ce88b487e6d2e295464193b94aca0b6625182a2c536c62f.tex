We prove the claim in normalized outcome units.  Thus write \(V=Y/M\), \(\nu_{ak}=\mu_{ak}/M\), and
\[
 \varphi_k:=p_k(\nu_{1k}-\nu_{0k}),\qquad
 \frac{\tau(P)}M=\sum_{k=1}^d\varphi_k.
\tag{1}
\]
The support and division conditions are explicit: Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) gives \(s_{ak}=p_kP(A=a\mid X=k)\ge\epsilon p_k\) whenever \(p_k>0\); every empirical ratio is multiplied by the indicator that its denominator is positive; and the outer clipping is a total measurable map.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives \(\tau(P)/M\in[-1,1]\), so clipping cannot increase squared error.

Define
\[
 H_K(x):=\frac{1-T_K(1-2x)}{2K^2},\qquad
 E_K(x):=\frac{H_K(x)}x,\qquad
 G_K(x):=\frac{1-E_K(x)}x,
\]
using polynomial continuation at zero.  Direct expansion of the shifted Chebyshev polynomial yields
\[
 G_K(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}(-1)^j
 \frac{2^{2j+3}}{K(K+j+2)}\binom{K+j+2}{2j+4}x^j,
\tag{2}
\]
which is exactly the coefficient sequence used in the estimator.  Since \(|T_K(1-2x)|\le1\) on \([0,1]\),
\[
 x|1-xG_K(x)|=H_K(x)\le K^{-2}.
\tag{3}
\]
For a cell with \(p_k\le B\), put \(z_{ak}=s_{ak}\nu_{ak}\).  By (3), \(|z_{ak}|\le s_{ak}/2\), and \(s_{ak}\ge\epsilon p_k\),
\[
 \left|\varphi_k-\left\{\frac{p_kz_{1k}}B G_K(s_{1k}/B)-
 \frac{p_kz_{0k}}B G_K(s_{0k}/B)\right\}\right|
 \le\frac{B}{\epsilon K^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
The distinct-index identity in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\) shows that \(\widehat P_k\) is unbiased for the expression in braces.

We next control pilot classification.  Put \(B_+=2\lambda_0L/m_0\) and \(B_-=\lambda_0L/(2m_0)\).  The balanced split and \(b_0=16\lambda_0\) give \(B_+\le B/4\).  Binomial Chernoff inequalities, followed by a union bound over \(d\le\rho_{\epsilon}nL\) cells, give
\[
 Q_P^{(n)}\!\left(\widehat{\mathcal H}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge B_-\}
 \text{ or }\widehat{\mathcal L}\not\subseteq\{k:p_k\le B_+\}\right)
 \le Cn^{-4}.
\tag{5}
\]
The same calculation also controls a cell with \(p_k>B_+\) that is falsely selected as light.  Indeed, its pilot probability is at most \(\exp(-m_0p_k/8)\), while the absolute-coefficient identity in the proof of the covariance lemma gives
\[
 \|\widehat P_k\|_2+|\varphi_k|
 \le CB\{6(1+p_k/B)\}^{K}.
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(x^{-1}\log\{6(1+x)\}\) decreases on \([1/8,\infty)\), the definition of \(D_6\), the choice \(K\le\alpha_0L\), and \(m_0B\asymp b_0L\) make the sum over falsely light cells in (6) at most \(CBd e^{-15L}\) in \(L_2\).

Condition on the pilot and on the event in (5).  The genuinely light set is deterministic for block \(I_1\) and lies in \(\{k:p_k\le B/4\}\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\), (4), and the triangle inequality give
\[
 E\left[\left\{\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}(\widehat P_k-\varphi_k)\right\}^2
 \middle| I_0\right]
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
To see the rate in (7) without suppressing a logarithm, let \(\kappa=2\alpha_0\log6\le1/32\).  Since \(6^{2K}\le Cn^{\kappa}\), \(B\asymp L/n\), and \(K\asymp L\), the two nonparametric covariance terms satisfy
\[
 n^{\kappa}d\frac{L^2}{n^2}
 \le C\left(\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right),\qquad
 n^{\kappa}d^2\frac{L^4}{n^3}
 \le C\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}.
\tag{8}
\]
For the first inequality use \(2x\le1+x^2\) with \(x=d/(\sqrt nL)\); both inequalities then use boundedness of the remaining powers of \(n\) times powers of \(L\).  The accumulated approximation bias in (4) is at most \(dB/(\epsilon K^2)\asymp d/(nL)\), whose square is the second term of (7).  Equations (5)--(8) prove the complete light-cell bound.

It remains to treat heavy cells.  Given the category and treatment counts in \(I_1\), the normalized outcome-noise variance in a nonempty arm is at most \(1/N_{ak}^{(1)}\).  If \(D\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(t,\rho)\), then
\[
 E\left[\frac{\mathbf 1\{D>0\}}D\right]
 \le2E[(D+1)^{-1}]\le\frac{2}{(t+1)\rho}.
\tag{9}
\]
Using (9), overlap, and \(\sum_kE[N_k^{(1)}]=m_1\), the aggregate conditional outcome-noise variance is at most \(C_{\epsilon}/n\).  The conditional mean equals the empirical category average of \(\nu_{ak}\) minus the missing-arm remainder.  The former has variance at most \(1/(4m_1)\).  Exact multinomial counting, \(p_kP(A=a\mid X=k)\ge\epsilon p_k\), and \(p_k\ge B_-\) on the heavy set yield for each arm
\[
 E[\{\text{missing-arm remainder}\}^2]
 \le\frac{C_{\epsilon}}n+C_{\epsilon}\left\{
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}p_ke^{-c_{\epsilon}np_k}\right\}^2
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the heavy contribution obeys the same bound as (7).  On the complement of (5), clipping bounds squared error by four, so its contribution is \(O(n^{-4})\).  Combining (7) and (10), restoring the factor \(M^2\), proves the asserted risk whenever \(n\ge N_{\epsilon}\) and \(d\le\rho_{\epsilon}nL\).

If \(d>\rho_{\epsilon}nL\), then \(d^2/(n^2L^2)>\rho_{\epsilon}^2\), while the zero fallback has squared error at most \(M^2\); increasing \(C_{\epsilon}\) proves the truncated bound.  If \(n<N_{\epsilon}\), the same conclusion follows from \(M^2\le N_{\epsilon}M^2/n\).  Finally, each \(U_{k,a,j}\) is computable from falling-factorial cell counts and one marked cell sum, so all degrees can be evaluated in \(O(dK^2)\) arithmetic operations.  No median or Catoni step is needed for mean-squared error because (1) supplies the exact second-moment control.
