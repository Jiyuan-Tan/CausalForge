Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\).  We work first in normalized outcome units.  Put
\[
 V:=Y/M,\qquad \nu_{ak}:=\mu_{ak}/M,\qquad
 s_{ak}:=P(X=k,A=a),\qquad z_{ak}:=E_P[V\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}],
\]
and
\[
 \varphi_k:=p_k(\nu_{1k}-\nu_{0k}).
\]
By Definition~\(\mathrm{def:ate\text{-}functional}\),
\[
 \frac{\tau(P)}M=\sum_{k=1}^d\varphi_k.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}normalization}\) gives \(|\nu_{ak}|\le1/2\), while Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) gives
\[
 s_{ak}\ge\epsilon p_k\quad\text{whenever }p_k>0.                              \tag{2}
\]
Lyapunov's inequality and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:fourth\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\) also give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(V\mid A=a,X=k)\le1.                                      \tag{3}
\]
All empirical arm ratios below are multiplied by the indicator that their denominators are positive.  The outer clipping is therefore a total measurable map.  Moreover, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives \(\tau(P)/M\in[-1,1]\), so projection onto \([-1,1]\) cannot increase squared loss.

We first establish the polynomial approximation used on light cells.  Let \(T_K\) be the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind and define
\[
 H_K(x):=\frac{1-T_K(1-2x)}{2K^2},\qquad
 E_K(x):=\frac{H_K(x)}x,\qquad
 G_K(x):=\frac{1-E_K(x)}x,
\]
where the quotients at zero are defined by polynomial continuation.  Expanding the shifted Chebyshev polynomial gives
\[
 G_K(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}(-1)^j
 \frac{2^{2j+3}}{K(K+j+2)}\binom{K+j+2}{2j+4}x^j.                              \tag{4}
\]
Thus (4) is precisely the coefficient sequence \(g_{Kj}\) in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\).  Since \(|T_K(t)|\le1\) for \(t\in[-1,1]\),
\[
 \left|x\{1-xG_K(x)\}\right|=|H_K(x)|\le K^{-2},
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.                                                            \tag{5}
\]
For a cell with \(0<p_k\le B\), the identity \(z_{ak}=s_{ak}\nu_{ak}\), (2), and (5) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left|p_k\nu_{ak}-\frac{p_kz_{ak}}B G_K(s_{ak}/B)\right|\\
 &\qquad=p_k|\nu_{ak}|\left|1-\frac{s_{ak}}B G_K(s_{ak}/B)\right|
 \le \frac{B}{2\epsilon K^2}.
 \end{aligned}                                                                  \tag{6}
\]
The same assertion is trivial when \(p_k=0\).  The distinct-index expectation identity in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\) is
\[
 E_P[U_{k,a,j}]=z_{ak}p_ks_{ak}^{,j}.                                           \tag{7}
\]
Consequently \(\widehat P_k\) is unbiased for
\[
 \frac{p_kz_{1k}}B G_K(s_{1k}/B)-
 \frac{p_kz_{0k}}B G_K(s_{0k}/B),                                               \tag{8}
\]
and (6), applied to both arms, bounds the absolute error between (8) and \(\varphi_k\) by \(B/(\epsilon K^2)\).

We next control the independent pilot classification.  Write
\[
 B_-:=\frac{\lambda_0L}{2m_0},\qquad
 B_+:=\frac{2\lambda_0L}{m_0},
\]
and let \(\mathcal A_0\) be the event
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal H}\subseteq\{k:p_k\ge B_-\},
 \qquad
 \widehat{\mathcal L}\subseteq\{k:p_k\le B_+\}.                              \tag{9}
\]
For \(N_k^{(0)}:=\sum_{i\in I_0}\mathbf 1\{X_i=k\}\), the binomial Chernoff inequalities give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sup_{p_k<B_-}P\{N_k^{(0)}>\lambda_0L\}&\le e^{-\lambda_0L/6},\\
 \sup_{p_k>B_+}P\{N_k^{(0)}\le\lambda_0L\}&\le e^{-\lambda_0L/4}.
 \end{split}                                                                    \tag{10}
\]
Reduce \(\rho_\epsilon\), if necessary, so that \(0<\rho_\epsilon\le1\).  Hence, after a union bound, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and all \(d\le\rho_\epsilon nL\),
\[
 Q_P^{(n)}(\mathcal A_0^c)
 \le d\{e^{-\lambda_0L/6}+e^{-\lambda_0L/4}\}
 \le Cn^{-4}.                                                                   \tag{11}
\]
The numerical choices \(b_0=16\lambda_0\) and the balanced split imply, after enlarging the finite threshold \(N_\epsilon\) if necessary,
\[
 B_+\le B/4.                                                                    \tag{12}
\]
The same enlargement ensures \(K\ge2\), \(4(K+2)^2\le m_1\), and \(4(K+2)/m_1\le3B/4\).  These are exactly the support and rank conditions needed to invoke Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\).

Condition on the pilot block and on a realization for which \(\mathcal A_0\) holds.  The set \(\widehat{\mathcal L}\) is then deterministic relative to block \(I_1\), and by (12) it is contained in \(\{k:p_k\le B/4\}\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\), (6)--(8), and the bias--variance identity yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &E\left[\left\{\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\widehat P_k
       -\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\varphi_k\right\}^2
       \middle| I_0\right]\\
 &\quad\le \frac{C_\epsilon}{m_1}
 +C_\epsilon6^{2K}\left\{dB^2+\frac{d^2K^2B^2}{m_1}\right\}
 +\frac{d^2B^2}{\epsilon^2K^4}.                                                 \tag{13}
\end{aligned}
\]
Put \(\kappa:=2\alpha_0\log6\le1/32\).  Since \(6^{2K}\le n^\kappa\), \(B\asymp L/n\), and \(K\asymp L\), elementary algebra gives, uniformly in \(d\ge1\),
\[
 n^\kappa d\frac{L^2}{n^2}
 \le C\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\},
 \qquad
 n^\kappa d^2\frac{L^4}{n^3}
 \le C\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}.                                                       \tag{14}
\]
For the first inequality in (14), apply \(2x\le1+x^2\) to \(x=d/(\sqrt nL)\) and use \(n^{\kappa-1/2}L^3=O(1)\); the second follows from \(n^{\kappa-1}L^6=O(1)\).  The last term in (13) is at most \(C_\epsilon d^2/(n^2L^2)\).  Thus
\[
 E\left[\left\{\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\widehat P_k
       -\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal L}}\varphi_k\right\}^2
       \middle| I_0\right]
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}                  \tag{15}
\]
on \(\mathcal A_0\).

It remains to bound the heavy-cell term on the same pilot realization.  For notational brevity, all counts in the next display are computed on \(I_1\), and \(m=m_1\).  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), decompose
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\frac{N_k}{m}\overline V_{ak}
       \mathbf 1\{N_{ak}>0\}
 &=\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\frac{N_k}{m}\nu_{ak}
   +\Xi_a-\mathcal R_a,\\
 \Xi_a&:=\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\frac{N_k}{m}
   (\overline V_{ak}-\nu_{ak})\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}>0\},\\
 \mathcal R_a&:=\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\frac{N_k}{m}
   \nu_{ak}\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}=0\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                                  \tag{16}
\]
Conditional on all category and treatment counts, \(E(\Xi_a)=0\).  If \(D\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(t,q)\), then
\[
 E\left[\frac{\mathbf 1\{D>0\}}D\right]
 \le2E[(D+1)^{-1}]
 =\frac{2\{1-(1-q)^{t+1}\}}{(t+1)q}
 \le\frac{2}{(t+1)q}.                                                          \tag{17}
\]
Using (2), (3), and (17), first conditionally on \(N_k\) and then summing over cells, gives
\[
 E(\Xi_a^2\mid I_0)
 \le \frac{C_\epsilon}{m^2}\sum_kE[N_k]
 \le\frac{C_\epsilon}{m}.                                                       \tag{18}
\]

We record the missing-arm calculation explicitly.  Conditional on the pilot, the heavy set is deterministic.  Multinomial factorial moments, \(|\nu_{ak}|\le1/2\), and \(s_{ak}\ge\epsilon p_k\) imply
\[
 E(\mathcal R_a^2\mid I_0)
 \le \frac{C_\epsilon}{m}
 +C_\epsilon\left\{\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}
 p_ke^{-c_\epsilon mp_k}\right\}^2.                                            \tag{19}
\]
Indeed, the diagonal terms use
\[
 E[N_k^2\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}=0\}]
 \le mp_ke^{-c_\epsilon mp_k}+m^2p_k^2e^{-c_\epsilon mp_k},
\]
and the off-diagonal terms use
\[
 E[N_kN_\ell\mathbf 1\{N_{ak}=N_{a\ell}=0\}]
 \le C m^2p_kp_\ell e^{-c_\epsilon m(p_k+p_\ell)},\qquad k\ne\ell.
\]
The term \(\sum_kp_k^2e^{-c_\epsilon mp_k}\) is at most \(C_\epsilon/m\), proving (19).  On \(\mathcal A_0\), every heavy cell has \(p_k\ge B_-\asymp L/n\).  Since, for each fixed \(c>0\),
\[
 \sup_{t\ge cL} t e^{-c_\epsilon t}\le C_\epsilon/L
\]
after another finite enlargement of \(N_\epsilon\), (19) gives
\[
 E(\mathcal R_a^2\mid I_0)
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.                   \tag{20}
\]
Finally,
\[
 \sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\frac{N_k}{m}
 (\nu_{1k}-\nu_{0k})
\]
is the empirical average of the bounded function
\(\mathbf 1\{X\in\widehat{\mathcal H}\}(\nu_{1X}-\nu_{0X})\), whose absolute value is at most one.  Its variance is at most \(1/m\), and its expectation is \(\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\varphi_k\).  Combining this observation with (16), (18), and (20) yields
\[
 E\left[\left\{\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\widehat h_k
       -\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal H}}\varphi_k\right\}^2
       \middle| I_0\right]
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}                  \tag{21}
\]
on \(\mathcal A_0\).

Let \(Z_n\) denote the normalized, unclipped sum of the heavy and light terms.  Equations (1), (15), and (21), together with \((x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\), imply
\[
 E\left[(Z_n-\tau(P)/M)^2\mathbf 1_{\mathcal A_0}\right]
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}.                 \tag{22}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0^c\), the clipped estimator and its target both lie in \([-1,1]\), so their squared difference is at most four.  By (11), projection contraction, and (22),
\[
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_\epsilon M^2\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right\}              \tag{23}
\]
whenever \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon nL\).

If \(d>\rho_\epsilon nL\), the stipulated zero fallback has squared error at most \(M^2\), whereas
\[
 \min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right)\ge\min(1,\rho_\epsilon^2).
\]
Increasing \(C_\epsilon\) proves the claimed truncated bound in this regime, including every \(d\le c_\epsilon n^2/L\).  If \(n<N_\epsilon\), the same fallback is bounded by \(N_\epsilon M^2/n\).  Thus (23) gives the displayed assertion in all stated regimes after one final increase of \(C_\epsilon\).

To verify the computational claim, let \(S_{ak}^{V}:=\sum_{i\in I_1}V_i\mathbf 1\{X_i=k,A_i=a\}\).  Direct counting of the ordered distinct tuples gives, when \(N_{ak}^{(1)}>0\),
\[
 U_{k,a,j}=\frac{S_{ak}^{V}(N_{ak}^{(1)}-1)_j
 (N_k^{(1)}-j-1)}{(m_1)_{j+2}},                                                 \tag{24}
\]
with value zero when the relevant falling factorial vanishes.  Hence all \(U_{k,a,j}\), heavy ratios, and the final sum are obtained from aggregated cell counts and one marked sum per arm.  Evaluating the coefficient table and the degree sequence costs at most \(O(dK^2)\) arithmetic operations.  The inactive-regime zero rule, the indicators in every ratio, the rank conditions preceding (13), and the final clipping cover every division and support boundary.  Equation (23) has no logarithmic factor beyond the claimed \(L^{-2}\) gain.
