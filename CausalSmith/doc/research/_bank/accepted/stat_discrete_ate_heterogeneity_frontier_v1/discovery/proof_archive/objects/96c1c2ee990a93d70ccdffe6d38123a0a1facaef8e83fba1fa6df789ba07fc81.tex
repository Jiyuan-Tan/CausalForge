Write
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad b:=\frac1n+\frac d{n^2},\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad \rho:=\sqrt u=\frac d{nL}.
\]
The established upper and lower theorems imply, in the residual wedge where the relevant caps are inactive,
\[
 c_{\epsilon}M^2\{b+\sigma^2u\}
 \le \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2\{b+\min(u,\sigma^2)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Two route-specific conclusions are proved.

First, let a binary one-arm source have \(B(0)=0\), \(B(1)\mid X=k\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\eta_k)\), and \(\psi:=\sum_kp_k\eta_k\).  For an arm-dependent but cell- and hypothesis-independent outcome channel \(K_a(\mathrm dy\mid b)\), put
\[
 \nu_{ab}:=\int y\,K_a(\mathrm dy\mid b),\qquad
 c:=\nu_{10}-\nu_{00},\qquad \gamma:=\nu_{11}-\nu_{10}.
\]
Linearity gives
\[
 \mu_{0k}=\nu_{00},\qquad
 \mu_{1k}=\nu_{10}+\gamma\eta_k,qquad
 \tau_k=c+\gamma\eta_k,qquad
 \tau=c+\gamma\psi.
\tag{2}
\]
If the channel maps the whole source class into \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), apply it to \(p_1=p_2=1/2\), \(\eta_1=0\), and \(\eta_2=1\).  Approximate homogeneity and (2) yield
\[
 \frac{|\gamma|}{2}
 =\max_{k\in\{1,2\}}|\tau_k-\tau|
 \le \sigma M,
 \qquad |\gamma|\le2\sigma M.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus a source target gap \(\Delta_{\psi}\) becomes at most \(2\sigma M\Delta_{\psi}\).  The one-arm hard family has \(\Delta_{\psi}\asymp\rho\) in the nonsaturated range, so data processing retains its testing certificate but produces only order \(M\sigma\rho\) separation.  In the residual wedge,
\[
 \frac{\min(\sigma,\rho)}{\sigma\rho}
 =\frac1{\max(\sigma,\rho)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{4}
\]
Hence no channel of this hypothesis-independent form can attain the requested order \(M\min(\sigma,\rho)\).

Second, the natural centered mixed-arm factorial lift has a false product-rate covariance bound.  On an estimation block of size \(m\), take \(d=m\), \(M=1\), \(p_k=1/m\), \(\pi_k=1/2\), and \(\mu_{0k}=\mu_{1k}=0\), with conditionally independent Rademacher potential outcomes independent of treatment.  This law belongs to the frozen class at exact homogeneity.  Even with the oracle pilot \(t=\tau=0\), for
\[
 \widehat H_{k,c,j}(0)
 :=\frac1{(m)_{j+2}}
 \sum_{i_0,\ldots,i_{j+1}\ {\rm distinct}}
 \mathbf1\{X_{i_0}=k,A_{i_0}=0\}
 \mathbf1\{X_{i_1}=k,A_{i_1}=1\}
 (Y_{i_1}-Y_{i_0})
 \prod_{q=2}^{j+1}\mathbf1\{X_{i_q}=k,A_{i_q}=c\},
\tag{5}
\]
the Chebyshev constant coefficient is \(g_{K0}=(K^2-1)/3\).  If \(J_k:=\mathbf1\{N_{0k}=N_{1k}=1\}\), then the terms with \(j\ge1\) vanish on \(J_k=1\), whereas the two \(j=0\) terms coincide.  Conditional independence of the marks therefore gives for the full light statistic \(Z\)
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Z\mid X,A)
 \ge 2\left\{\frac{2g_{K0}}{B(m)_2}\right\}^{\!2}
 \sum_{k=1}^mJ_k.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 E\!\left[\sum_{k=1}^mJ_k\right]
 =m\frac{(m)_2}{4m^2}
 \left(1-\frac1m\right)^{m-2}
 \ge c_0m.
\tag{7}
\]
Under the required reciprocal-approximation calibration \(K\asymp\log(em)\) and \(B\asymp\log(em)/m\), (6)--(7) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Z)
 \ge c_1\frac{g_{K0}^2}{B^2m^3}
 \asymp\frac{\log^2(em)}m,
\tag{8}
\]
contradicting the order \(m^{-1}+d/m^2\asymp m^{-1}\) needed for the proposed product-rate proof.  Oracle centering and independent sample splitting do not remove this singleton-pair diagonal.

The population approximation calculation for that lift nevertheless remains valid.  With
\[
 \vartheta:=\frac{\tau(P)}M,quad
 H_k(t):=s_{0k}s_{1k}(\vartheta_k-t),quad
 S_K(t):=\sum_kH_k(t)\{r_K(s_{0k})+r_K(s_{1k})\},
\]
and \(c_k:=s_{0k}s_{1k}\{r_K(s_{0k})+r_K(s_{1k})\}\), one has
\[
 t+S_K(t)-\vartheta
 =\sum_k(\vartheta_k-\vartheta)(c_k-p_k)
 +(t-\vartheta)\left(1-\sum_kc_k\right),
\]
so reciprocal approximation and overlap give
\[
 |t+S_K(t)-\vartheta|^2
 \le C_{\epsilon}u\{\sigma^2+(t-\vartheta)^2\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus the product-scaled deterministic bias is genuine, but the attempted stochastic lift cannot exploit it at the product risk.  The remaining alternatives require either a hypothesis-dependent paired construction with joint moment matching and exact normalization, or a rare-cell estimator that avoids the singleton-pair amplification; neither has been constructed or ruled out.
