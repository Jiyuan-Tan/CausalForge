Let \(a_{\epsilon},b_{\epsilon}>0\) and \(N_{\epsilon}<\infty\) be supplied by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}exact\text{-}homogeneity\text{-}lower}\), decreasing \(b_{\epsilon}\) so that \(b_{\epsilon}\le1/2\).  Suppose first that \(n\ge N_{\epsilon}\) and set
\[
 d_0:=\min\{d,\max(1,\lfloor b_{\epsilon}n^2\rfloor)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For every source binary law, augment the observed arm laws by potential outcomes \(B(0),B(1)\) drawn conditionally on \(X\) and independently of \(A\), with the prescribed Bernoulli arm means.  Define
\[
 Y(a):=M\{B(a)-1/2\},\qquad Y:=Y(A).
\]
Consistency holds by construction and conditional exchangeability follows from the conditional independence of \(A\).  Overlap and cell masses are unchanged.  The conditional means lie in \([-M/2,M/2]\), and because \(Y(a)\in\{-M/2,M/2\}\), every conditional second central moment is at most \(M^2\).  The ATE and every cell effect are multiplied by \(M\), while exact homogeneity is preserved.  Embed this \(d_0\)-cell experiment into the \(d\)-cell model by assigning zero mass to cells \(d_0+1,\ldots,d\).  Every model assumption is support-qualified, so the image belongs to \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) for every \(\sigma\ge0\).

The observed transformation is invertible through \(B=Y/M+1/2\).  Thus every estimator \(T\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\) induces the binary estimator \(T/M\), and its squared loss is \(M^2\) times the source squared loss.  Subclass restriction and the source lemma yield
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge a_{\epsilon}M^2\left(\frac1n+\frac{d_0}{n^2}\right).
\tag{2}
\]
For \(n\) above a larger fixed cutoff, \(\lfloor b_{\epsilon}n^2\rfloor\ge b_{\epsilon}n^2/2\).  Hence
\[
 \frac1n+\frac{d_0}{n^2}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}\left\{\frac1n+\min\left(1,\frac d{n^2}\right)\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, if \(d\le b_{\epsilon}n^2/2\), then \(d_0=d\); otherwise \(d_0/n^2\ge b_{\epsilon}/2\), which dominates \(c_{\epsilon}\min(1,d/n^2)\).

For the finitely many smaller sample sizes, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\) gives \(c_{\epsilon}M^2/n\).  Since
\[
 \frac1n+\min(1,d/n^2)\le \frac{1+N_{\epsilon}}n
 \qquad (n<N_{\epsilon}),
\tag{4}
\]
decreasing the constant absorbs those cases.  Equations~\((2)\)--\((4)\) prove the all-alphabet capped lower bound, including \(d=1\), zero-mass unused cells, and both boundary radii.
