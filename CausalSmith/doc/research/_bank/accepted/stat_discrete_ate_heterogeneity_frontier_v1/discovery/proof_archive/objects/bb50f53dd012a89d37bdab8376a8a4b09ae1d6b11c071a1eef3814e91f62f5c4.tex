Put
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad
 x:=\frac d{n^2},\qquad
 v:=\min\{1,u\}.
\]
Define the normalized upper and lower benchmarks
\[
 q:=\frac1n+\min\{v,\sigma^2+x\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \ell:=\frac1n+x+\sigma^2v.
\tag{1}
\]
By the definition of \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\), \(q=r_{n,d,\sigma}\).  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\), respectively, give constants depending only on \(\epsilon\) such that
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\le C_{\epsilon}M^2q
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\ell.
\tag{2}
\]
All subsequent steps are deterministic comparisons of the two quantities in (1), so the support, overlap, moment, radius, and dimension conditions are exactly those already checked in the two cited core theorems.

Fix \(s\in(0,2]\) and suppose \(s\le\sigma\le2\).  Since a minimum is no larger than either of its arguments,
\[
 q\le \frac1n+v.
\tag{3}
\]
On the other hand, \(\sigma^2\ge s^2\) and \(x\ge0\), whence
\[
 \ell\ge\frac1n+s^2v.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (3) and (4) yields
\[
 q\le \max\{1,s^{-2}\}\ell.
\tag{5}
\]
The lower inequality in (2) and (5) give
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge \frac{c_{\epsilon}}{\max\{1,s^{-2}\}}M^2q,
\]
while the upper inequality in (2) supplies the reverse comparison.  This proves the first display, including all boundaries \(d=1\), \(d=c_{\epsilon}n^2/L\), \(\sigma=s\), and \(\sigma=2\).

We next prove the two uniform subregimes.  If \(u\ge1\), then \(v=1\).  If \(\sigma^2+x\le1\), (1) gives \(q=\ell\).  If \(\sigma^2+x>1\), then
\[
 q=\frac1n+1<\frac1n+x+\sigma^2=\ell.
\]
The stipulated dimension range, \(c_{\epsilon}\le1\), and \(L\ge1\) also imply
\[
 x=\frac d{n^2}\le\frac{c_{\epsilon}}L\le1.
\]
Together with \(n^{-1}\le1\) and \(\sigma^2\le4\), this gives
\[
 q\le\ell=\frac1n+x+\sigma^2\le6\le6q.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus (2) and (6) prove uniform matching when \(u\ge1\).

If \(d\le L^2\), then
\[
 u=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\le\frac d{n^2}=x,
\]
and hence \(v\le x\).  Therefore
\[
 q\le\frac1n+v\le\frac1n+x\le\ell.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (2) and (7) prove uniform matching in the second subregime.

It remains to verify that these comparisons cannot be made uniform over the entire shrinking-radius range from the frozen benchmarks alone.  Let \(n\to\infty\), set \(d_n=n\), put \(L_n=\log(en)\), and take \(\sigma_n=L_n^{-1/2}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), the support and dimension conditions hold, \(0<\sigma_n<2\), and
\[
 u_n=L_n^{-2},\qquad x_n=n^{-1},\qquad \sigma_n^2=L_n^{-1}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 q_n=\frac1n+\min\left\{L_n^{-2},L_n^{-1}+n^{-1}\right\}
 =\frac1n+L_n^{-2}\asymp L_n^{-2},
\tag{8}
\]
whereas
\[
 \ell_n=\frac2n+L_n^{-1}L_n^{-2}
 =\frac2n+L_n^{-3}\asymp L_n^{-3}.
\tag{9}
\]
Dividing (8) by (9) gives \(q_n/\ell_n\asymp L_n\to\infty\).  This proves the asserted localization: fixed interior radii and the two displayed dimension regimes are settled, while the established inequalities alone leave a genuine triangular shrinking-radius gap.  No claim of inference, coverage, or adaptation to unknown \(M\) or \(\sigma\) is made.
