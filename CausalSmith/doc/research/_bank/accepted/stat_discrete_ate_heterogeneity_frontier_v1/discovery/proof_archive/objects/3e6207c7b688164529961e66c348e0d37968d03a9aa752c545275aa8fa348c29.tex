Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and take the smaller of the dimension constants and the larger of the risk constants in Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\).  By Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\), the infimum over all \(T\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\) is no larger than the risk of the explicit total map \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}\).  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) therefore yields
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every quantity in (1) is defined on the same observed product experiment from Definition~\(\mathrm{def:sample\text{-}experiment}\), and the estimator is total on all division and occupancy boundaries by Definition~\(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}estimator}\) and its construction theorem.

On that identical model class, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\left\{
 \frac1n+\frac d{n^2}
 +\sigma^2\min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right)
 \right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
The lower theorem already incorporates the parametric and exact-homogeneity components; the parametric component is also recorded independently in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\).  Thus (2) includes all boundary values \(0\le\sigma\le2\) and all sample sizes in the displayed range after the lower theorem's stated constant adjustment.  Equations (1) and (2) prove the bracket.  Because both suprema are over \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), no binary subclass is substituted for the real-outcome class in either asserted inequality; the binary experiments enter only as valid subclasses for the converse.

For theorem-level positioning, the external accepted-bank result \texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match/thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} supplies the unrestricted binary hybrid endpoint \(n^{-1}+d^2/[n^2\log^2 n]\).  Here the mathematical addition is not the admission of real outcomes by itself.  Rather, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\) selects explicitly between the weak-moment signed-mark polynomial branch and the occupancy branch using the known radius, while (2) supplies a radius-indexed same-class converse.  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:fixed\text{-}interior\text{-}tightness\text{-}and\text{-}shrinking\text{-}radius\text{-}gap}\) then proves that their only potentially divergent regime is
\[
 n^{-1}+d/n^2\ll d^2/[n^2\log^2(en)]\ll1,
 \qquad
 n^{-1}+d/n^2\ll\sigma^2\ll1.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the accepted endpoint does not imply the radius-indexed bracket or the localization (3).  Conversely, the explicit divergence example in that theorem shows why (1)--(2) must be described as a bracket rather than as a uniformly matched frontier.
