Put \(\nu_{ak}:=\mu_{ak}/M\) and, on \(\{X=k,A=a\}\), write
\[
 V=\nu_{ak}+\xi,qquad E[\xi\mid X=k,A=a]=0,qquad
 E[\xi^2\mid X=k,A=a]\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
The last inequality is Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:second\text{-}central\text{-}moment}\), after normalization by \(M\).  Mean normalization gives \(|\nu_{ak}|\le1/2\).  In particular,
\[
 E[\xi^2\mathbf 1\{X=k,A=a\}]\le s_{ak},
 \qquad |z_{ak}|=s_{ak}|\nu_{ak}|\le s_{ak}/2.
\tag{2}
\]

Independence of the distinct sample indices in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:centered\text{-}product\text{-}estimator}\) gives
\[
 E[U_{k,j}^{a\mid b,c}]=z_{ak}s_{bk}s_{ck}^{j},qquad
 E[F_{k,j}^{c}]=s_{0k}s_{1k}s_{ck}^{j}.
\tag{3}
\]
Since \(z_{1k}s_{0k}-z_{0k}s_{1k}=s_{0k}s_{1k}\vartheta_k\), summing \((3)\) with the signed coefficients yields
\[
 E[\widehat P_k(t)]
 =s_{0k}s_{1k}(\vartheta_k-t)
 \sum_{c=0}^1\frac1B G_K(s_{ck}/B),
\tag{4}
\]
which is the asserted expectation identity.

We next prove the variance inequality.  This is the step for which the signs must be retained.  Symmetrize each ordered statistic over its \(j+2\) arguments before multiplying two kernels.  Within every symmetrized kernel, first regroup
\[
 U_{k,j}^{1\mid0,c}-U_{k,j}^{0\mid1,c}-tF_{k,j}^{c}.
\tag{5}
\]
For the two distinguished opposite-arm observations, substitution of \((1)\) into \((5)\) separates the kernel exactly as
\[
 \mathcal K_{k,c,j}(t)=\mathcal N_{k,c,j}
 +(\vartheta_k-t)\mathcal F_{k,c,j},
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\mathcal N_{k,c,j}\) contains one centered mark, equal up to sign to \(\xi\) on one of the two distinguished observations, and \(\mathcal F_{k,c,j}\) is an unmarked opposite-arm factorial kernel.  Equality \((6)\), rather than a triangle inequality applied to the three terms in \((5)\), is the cancellation: the nuisance levels \(\nu_{0k}\) and \(\nu_{1k}\) occur only through \(\nu_{1k}-\nu_{0k}=\vartheta_k\).

Let \(\mathcal N_S\) and \(\mathcal M_S(t)\) be the sums of the first and second terms in \((6)\), respectively, after applying all coefficients \(g_{Kj}/B^{j+1}\) and summing over \(c,j,k\in S\).  Thus
\[
 \sum_{k\in S}\{\widehat P_k(t)-E\widehat P_k(t)\}
 =\mathcal N_S+\mathcal M_S(t).
\tag{7}
\]

For completeness, we audit both covariances by the ordered-index overlap expansion.  If two centered-mark kernels in \(\mathcal N_S\) have different marked sample indices after the partial matching of their tuples, conditional centering in \((1)\) makes their expectation zero.  A surviving term must identify the two centered marks.  The matched observation then costs at most the first inequality in \((2)\); no higher outcome moment appears.  The remaining selectors are unmarked.  The falling-factorial ratio for a matching of size \(h\ge1\) obeys
\[
 \frac{(m)_{j+r+4-h}}{(m)_{j+2}(m)_{r+2}}
 \le \frac e{m^h},
\tag{8}
\]
and the number of such matchings is at most \(K^{2h}/h!\).  The coefficient identity used in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:linear\text{-}mark\text{-}factorial\text{-}covariance}\),
\[
 G_K^+(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}|g_{Kj}|x^j
 =\frac{T_K(1+2x)-1-2K^2x}{2K^2x^2},
\tag{9}
\]
together with overlap \(s_{ak}\ge\epsilon p_k\), \(p_k\le B/4\), and \(4(K+2)/m\le3B/4\), sums the surviving marked-match terms.  Exactly as in the marked-position class of that lemma, \(\sum_{k,a}s_{ak}=1\) removes the cell count, and we obtain
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\mathcal N_S)\le C_{\epsilon}/m.
\tag{10}
\]
This is where centering is essential: unmatched marks vanish before absolute values, so no unscaled polynomial remainder is produced.

For the mean part, approximate homogeneity gives
\[
 q_t:=\max_{k:p_k>0}|\vartheta_k-t|
 \le\sigma+|t-\vartheta|,qquad
 q_t^2\le2\{\sigma^2+(t-\vartheta)^2\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Applying the same overlap partition to the unmarked kernels in \(\mathcal M_S(t)\), and only now taking absolute values, factors out \(q_t^2\).  Within-cell overlaps use \((8)\)--\((9)\); different cells share no observation, and their sole covariance is the fixed-multinomial falling-factorial correction
\[
 \left|\frac{(m)_{j+r+4}}{(m)_{j+2}(m)_{r+2}}-1\right|
 \le\frac{2K^2}{m}.
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{\mathcal M_S(t)\}
 \le C_{\epsilon}q_t^2\left[
 \frac1m+6^{2K}\left\{dB^2+\frac{d^2K^2B^2}{m}\right\}
 \right].
\tag{13}
\]
The same expansion for the cross covariance between \(\mathcal N_S\) and \(\mathcal M_S(t)\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, is bounded by one half the sum of the right sides of \((10)\) and \((13)\).

It remains only to insert the calibration.  Put \(\kappa:=2\alpha\log6<1/32\).  Then \(6^{2K}\le m^{\kappa}\), \(B\le C_{\epsilon}L_m/m\), and \(K\le C L_m\).  Splitting the first inequality at \(d=m^{\kappa}L_m^4\), and using \(m^{\kappa}L_m^6/m\le1\) for \(m\ge N_{\epsilon}\), gives
\[
 m^{\kappa}\frac{dL_m^2}{m^2}
 \le C\left\{\frac1m+\frac{d^2}{m^2L_m^2}\right\},
 \qquad
 m^{\kappa}\frac{d^2L_m^4}{m^3}
 \le C\frac{d^2}{m^2L_m^2}.
\tag{14}
\]
The harmless term \(d/m^2\) is added to cover the order-two unmarked overlaps uniformly down to \(d=1\).  Combining \((7)\), \((10)\)--\((14)\), and \((x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{k\in S}\widehat P_k(t)\right)
 \le C_{\epsilon}\left\{
 \frac1m+\frac d{m^2}
 +\frac{d^2}{m^2L_m^2}
 [\sigma^2+(t-\vartheta)^2]
 \right\}.
\]
All divisions are deterministic factorial normalizations; the stipulated zero fallback covers their boundary.  Equations \((1)\), \((2)\), and \((10)\) show explicitly that only conditional second moments of the outcome are used.
