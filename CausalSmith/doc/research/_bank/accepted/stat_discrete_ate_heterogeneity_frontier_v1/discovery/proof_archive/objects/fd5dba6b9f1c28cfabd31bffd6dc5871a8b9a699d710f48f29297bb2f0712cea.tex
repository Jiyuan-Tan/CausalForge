\textit{Proof.}
Write
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad
 h:=\sigma^2+\frac d{n^2},
 \qquad t:=\min\{1,u,h\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:robust\text{-}upper\text{-}construction\text{-}resolution}\) supplies total measurable maps \(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}},T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\in\mathcal T_{n,M}\), with all empirical divisions totalized and both final outputs clipped to \([-M,M]\), such that, after taking the smaller dimension constant and the larger risk constant,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_\epsilon M^2\left\{\frac1n+\min(1,u)\right\}
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_\epsilon M^2\left(\frac1n+h\right)
\tag{3}
\]
uniformly on the range in the theorem.  These are statements about explicit maps of the observed sample under \(Q_P^{(n)}\); no oracle quantity enters either map.

There are three cases in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}estimator}\).  If \(h\le\min\{1,u\}\), then \(t=h\), the selector equals \(T_n^{\mathrm{col}}\), and (3) is exactly the desired bound.  If \(u<\min\{1,h\}\), then \(t=u\), the selector equals \(T_n^{\mathrm{poly}}\), and (2) is exactly the desired bound.

It remains to check the zero fallback.  Suppose neither preceding inequality holds.  If \(t<1\) and \(t=h\), then \(h\le\min\{1,u\}\), contradicting failure of the first case.  If \(t<1\) and \(t=u<h\), then \(u<\min\{1,h\}\), contradicting failure of the second case.  A tie \(u=h<1\) belongs to the first case by its weak inequality.  Hence failure of both nonzero branches forces
\[
 t=1.
\tag{4}
\]
On this branch \(\widehat\tau_n^\star=0\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) gives \(|\tau(P)|\le M\), so
\[
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(\widehat\tau_n^\star-\tau(P))^2]
 =\tau(P)^2\le M^2
 \le M^2\left(\frac1n+t\right).
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (2)--(5) therefore prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(\widehat\tau_n^\star-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_\epsilon M^2
 \left\{\frac1n+\min\left(1,
 \frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)},\sigma^2+\frac d{n^2}
 \right)\right\}
 =C_\epsilon M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\tag{6}
\]

For the comparison, the corrected cited form of Zeng, Balakrishnan, Han, and Kennedy's collision theorem has algebraic bias--variance remainder of order \(h=\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}^2+d/n^2\), in addition to the common \(n^{-1}\) term and its displayed exponentially small bias fallback.  The selector's algebraic nonparametric remainder is \(t\le h\), so it is never larger in order.  If
\[
 u=o(h)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad d=o\{n\log(en)\},
\tag{7}
\]
then the second condition gives \(u=o(1)\), and the first gives \(u<h\) eventually.  Thus \(t=u=o(h)\), which is the claimed strict order improvement of the remainder. \(\square\)
