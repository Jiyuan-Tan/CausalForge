Write
\[
 L:=\log(en),\qquad
 u:=\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2},\qquad
 q:=\frac d{n^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
We may take the dimension-range constant in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:matched\text{-}minimax\text{-}frontier}\) to satisfy \(c_{\epsilon}\le1\).  Thus the stipulated range implies
\[
 q\le\frac{c_{\epsilon}}L\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
We shall also use the elementary inequality \(L^2\le4n\) for integers \(n\ge1\), which follows from \(\log n\le\sqrt n\) and \(1+\sqrt n\le2\sqrt n\).

At \(\sigma=0\), the definition of \(r_{n,d,\sigma}\) gives
\[
 r_{n,d,0}=\frac1n+\min\{1,u,q\}.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(d\ge L^2\), then \(u=q(d/L^2)\ge q\), and (2) shows that the minimum in (3) equals \(q\).  If \(d<L^2\), then
\[
 q<\frac{L^2}{n^2}\le\frac4n.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently, in the latter case,
\[
 \frac15\left(\frac1n+q\right)
 \le \frac1n
 \le r_{n,d,0}
 \le \frac1n+q.
\tag{5}
\]
Together with the equality in the first case, (5) proves uniformly that
\[
 r_{n,d,0}\asymp \frac1n+\frac d{n^2}.
\tag{6}
\]
At this endpoint the expression inside braces on the lower side of Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:matched\text{-}minimax\text{-}frontier}\) is exactly \(n^{-1}+q\), so the two sides match in order.

At \(\sigma=2\), one has \(4+q\ge1\), and hence
\[
 r_{n,d,2}=\frac1n+\min\{1,u\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scale\text{-}sanity}\) supplies the support-qualified class identity
\[
 \mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,2}
 =\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M}^{\mathrm{unr}}.
\tag{8}
\]
Let \(m:=\min\{1,u\}\).  The expression inside braces on the lower side of the theorem is now
\[
 B:=\frac1n+q+4m.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(d\ge L^2\), then \(q\le m\): when \(u\le1\), this follows from \(q\le u=m\), and when \(u>1\), it follows from (2) and \(m=1\).  Thus \(B\le5(n^{-1}+m)\).  If \(d<L^2\), (4) instead gives the same conclusion,
\[
 B\le\frac5n+4m\le5\left(\frac1n+m\right).
\tag{10}
\]
Conversely, \(B\ge n^{-1}+m\) in both cases.  Equations (7), (9), and (10) therefore show
\[
 B\asymp r_{n,d,2}.
\tag{11}
\]
The common multiplier \(M^2\) does not affect these comparisons.  Equations (6), (8), and (11) prove matching at both boundary radii uniformly on the stated dimension range, including \(n=1\), \(d=1\), and arbitrary zero-mass cells.
