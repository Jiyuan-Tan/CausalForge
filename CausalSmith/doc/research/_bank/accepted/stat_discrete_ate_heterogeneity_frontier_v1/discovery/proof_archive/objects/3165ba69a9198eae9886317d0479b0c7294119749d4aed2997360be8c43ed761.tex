Let \(L:=\log(en)\).  By Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:frontier\text{-}upper}\), the explicit selector \(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}\) belongs to \(\mathcal T_{n,M}\) and satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[(\widehat\tau_n^{\star}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\tag{1}
\]
The defining infimum in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\) is no larger than the risk of this particular data-driven estimator.  Thus (1) proves the upper inequality.

On the same class and over the same range, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:radius\text{-}channel\text{-}converse}\), including its parametric component from Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\), gives
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\left\{
 \frac1n+\frac d{n^2}
 +\sigma^2\min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2L^2}\right)
 \right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Choose the common dimension constant to be the smaller constant in (1) and (2), and adjust the two risk constants in the harmless direction.  Equations (1)--(2) prove the bracket uniformly, including \(\sigma=0\), \(\sigma=2\), all zero-mass cells, and every boundary value of the stated dimension range.  Both sides quantify over exactly \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\) and its induced iid observed experiment; no binary subclass replaces the class in the conclusion.  Since no comparison between the two different right-hand expressions was used, the result is a bracket and makes no uniform shrinking-radius equivalence claim.
