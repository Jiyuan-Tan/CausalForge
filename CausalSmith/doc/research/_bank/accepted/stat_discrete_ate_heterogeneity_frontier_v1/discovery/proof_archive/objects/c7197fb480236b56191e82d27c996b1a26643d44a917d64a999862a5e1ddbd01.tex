There are two estimable signals.  Collisions exploit nearly common cell effects and pay the radius-side cost \(\sigma^2+d/n^2\); the signed-mark polynomial estimator enlarges the effective sample size for rare cells and pays \(u=d^2/[n^2\log^2(en)]\).  The known-radius selector takes the smaller cost.  The product lower channel is already sufficient whenever either cost is hidden by the parametric floor \(b=n^{-1}+d/n^2\), and fixed positive heterogeneity or saturation also forces matching.  Only when both \(u\) and \(\sigma^2\) vanish more slowly than \(b\) can the selector and converse separate.
