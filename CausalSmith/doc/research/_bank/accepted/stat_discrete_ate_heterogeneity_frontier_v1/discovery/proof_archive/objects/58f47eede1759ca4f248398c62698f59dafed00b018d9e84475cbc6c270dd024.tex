Put \(\lambda=\sigma/2\in[0,1]\).  Start from the binary one-arm subclass in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}one\text{-}arm\text{-}lower}\), with treated response \(B\in\{0,1\}\), treated cell mean \(\eta_k\in[0,1]\), and control response identically zero.  Conditional on every binary potential response \(B(a)\), independently draw
\[
 B'(a)\mid B(a)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\{1/2+\lambda(B(a)-1/2)\},
 \qquad Y(a)=M\{B'(a)-1/2\},
\tag{1}
\]
and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  The randomization in (1) is independent of treatment given the original potential outcomes and \(X\), so consistency and conditional exchangeability are preserved.  Propensities and cell masses are unchanged, hence overlap is preserved.

For the control arm and treated arm respectively,
\[
 \mu_{0k}=-\frac{M\lambda}{2},\qquad
 \mu_{1k}=M\lambda(\eta_k-1/2).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus both means lie in \([-M/2,M/2]\), and, since \(Y(a)\in\{-M/2,M/2\}\), every conditional fourth central moment is at most \(M^4\).  Equations (2) give
\[
 \tau_k=M\lambda\eta_k,\qquad
 \tau(P)=M\lambda\sum_{k=1}^dp_k\eta_k.
\tag{3}
\]
Writing \(\psi=\sum_kp_k\eta_k\), we have
\[
 |\tau_k-\tau(P)|=M\lambda|\eta_k-\psi|\le M\lambda\le\sigma M.
\tag{4}
\]
Therefore every transformed law belongs to \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}\), with all support and boundary conditions checked realization by realization.

The transformation from the source observation \((X,A,B)\) to \((X,A,Y)\) is a Markov kernel that is identical under the two fuzzy hypotheses.  Total variation between their induced mixtures can therefore only decrease.  Equivalently, given any estimator \(T\) in the transformed experiment and \(\lambda>0\), simulate (1) from the source sample and use \(T/(M\lambda)\) to estimate \(\psi\).  Conditional expectation with respect to the source sample can only reduce squared loss, so allowing the simulation does not evade the fixed-sample minimax lower bound.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:zeng\text{-}binary\text{-}one\text{-}arm\text{-}lower}\) consequently gives, for \(d\le b_{\epsilon}n\log n\),
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\lambda^2\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(d>b_{\epsilon}n\log n\), take \(d_0=\lfloor b_{\epsilon}n\log n\rfloor\) and embed the \(d_0\)-cell construction by assigning zero mass to the remaining cells.  After decreasing \(b_{\epsilon}\) and handling the finite cutoff, (5) becomes
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\sigma^2
 \min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right).
\tag{6}
\]
At \(\lambda=0\), (6) is the trivial zero inequality and follows by continuity of its right-hand side.

Independently, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:scaled\text{-}binary\text{-}exact\text{-}lower\text{-}transfer}\) gives, throughout the stated dimension range after decreasing \(c_{\epsilon}\),
\[
 \mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \ge c_{\epsilon}M^2\left(\frac1n+\frac d{n^2}\right).
\tag{7}
\]
The maximum of the right sides of (6) and (7) is at least half their sum.  This proves the displayed lower bound.  The finitely many sample sizes below the source lemmas' cutoffs are absorbed by decreasing \(c_{\epsilon}\): on that finite set, the bracket divided by \(1/n\) is uniformly bounded over the stipulated dimension and radius ranges, while Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:parametric\text{-}lower}\) supplies \(cM^2/n\).

Finally, the separation in (3) is exactly \(M\lambda\) times the source separation.  Hence a source separation of order \(\min\{1,d/[n\log(en)]\}\) becomes order \(M\sigma\min\{1,d/[n\log(en)]\}\), and its square is the product in (6).  This explicitly records why this valid channel construction does not prove a standalone interior \(\sigma^2\) phase.
