For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there are constants \(0<c_{\epsilon}\le C_{\epsilon}<\infty\) such that, uniformly over \(0\le\sigma\le2\) and \(1\le d\le c_{\epsilon}n^2/\log(en)\),
\[
 c_{\epsilon}M^2\left\{\frac1n+\frac d{n^2}+\sigma^2\min\left(1,\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right)\right\}
 \le\mathsf R_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\sigma}
 \le C_{\epsilon}M^2r_{n,d,\sigma}.
\]
Both inequalities hold on the same real-outcome, arbitrary-cell-mass class.  This is a two-sided minimax bracket, not a claim that the two displayed benchmarks are uniformly order-equivalent at every shrinking interior radius.

The accepted-bank theorem \texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match/thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior} establishes the unrestricted binary-outcome hybrid rate \(n^{-1}+d^2/[n^2\log^2 n]\).  The present theorem is not a restatement of that endpoint: on one arbitrary-cell-mass real-outcome class under conditional-second-central-moment control, it gives a known-radius upper and lower minimax bracket.  Its increment is the radius-indexed same-class bracket, the explicit weak-moment signed-mark construction and known-radius selector, the radius-channel converse, and the localization of every remaining possible mismatch to the explicit shrinking-radius wedge in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:fixed\text{-}interior\text{-}tightness\text{-}and\text{-}shrinking\text{-}radius\text{-}gap}\); real outcomes alone are not claimed as novel.
