Fix a treatment-noise transform \(M\) represented in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  By \(\text{lem:zero-localization}\), it has a zero in \(\{|z|\le R_0\}\), so every bank circle encloses at least one zero.  The transform bound proved in that lemma gives, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 |M(z)|\le \exp(4\psi_{\eta}^2|z|^2). \tag{1}
\]

We first audit the zero count used by the finite search.  Let \(s\in(R_{\mathrm{fac}},R_{\mathrm{out}})\) be a radius on which \(M\) has no zero.  Factoring the finitely many zeros in \(\{|z|<s\}\), using
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|se^{it}-a|\,dt=\log s
 \quad (|a|<s),
\]
and applying the mean-value identity to the logarithm of the modulus of the remaining zero-free factor gives
\[
 \sum_{|a|<s}\log\frac{s}{|a|}
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|M(se^{it})|\,dt-\log|M(0)|.
\]
Here zeros are repeated according to multiplicity and \(M(0)=1\).  Every zero with \(|a|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\) contributes at least \(\log(s/R_{\mathrm{fac}})\).  By (1), if \(N\) is the number of those zeros, then
\[
 N\log\frac{s}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}}
 \le4\psi_{\eta}^2s^2.
\]
Letting such radii \(s\) increase to \(R_{\mathrm{out}}\) yields
\[
 N\le
 Q_{\star}:=
 \frac{4\psi_{\eta}^2R_{\mathrm{out}}^2}
      {\log(R_{\mathrm{out}}/R_{\mathrm{fac}})}
 \le N_{\mathrm{cert}}. \tag{2}
\]

The grid \(\mathcal Q_{m_{\star}}\) is finite, lies in \((R_0,R_1)\), has more than \(N_{\mathrm{cert}}\) points, and has spacing \(d_{\star}\).  Since \(2h_{\star}=2d_{\star}/3<d_{\star}\), the modulus of a fixed zero can lie at distance less than \(h_{\star}\) from at most one grid point.  Any zero that can violate this separation satisfies \(|a|<R_1+h_{\star}<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), so it is among the at most \(N_{\mathrm{cert}}\) zeros counted in (2).  The pigeonhole principle therefore supplies a grid radius \(\rho_j\) such that
\[
 \bigl||a|-\rho_j\bigr|\ge h_{\star} \tag{3}
\]
for every zero \(a\) strictly inside the disk of radius \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\).  In particular, \(M\) is zero-free on \(C_j\).  Because \(\rho_j>R_0\), that circle encloses the zero localized in \(\{|z|\le R_0\}\), and hence \(N_{C_j}(M)\ge1\).

Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\), repeated according to multiplicity, be precisely the zeros strictly inside the disk of radius \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), and form the disk Blaschke product
\[
 Q(z)=\prod_{\nu=1}^N
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\nu)}
      {R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline{a_\nu}z}.
\]
Set \(f=M/Q\), filling the removable singularities.  The function \(f\) is holomorphic and zero-free on the open disk of radius \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\).  On its boundary, every displayed factor has modulus one, so \(|f|=|M|\).  Cauchy's integral formula applied to \(f^m\), followed by taking the \(m\)-th root and letting \(m\to\infty\), gives from (1)
\[
 |f(z)|\le \exp(4\psi_{\eta}^2R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2),
 \qquad |z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}. \tag{4}
\]
Moreover, \(|Q(0)|\le1\) and \(M(0)=1\), whence \(|f(0)|\ge1\).

Put \(A=4\psi_{\eta}^2R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2\) and \(u=A-\log|f|\).  By (4), \(u\) is a nonnegative harmonic function and \(u(0)\le A\).  To make the required interior bound explicit, apply the Poisson formula on a circle of radius \(s\in(R_1,R_{\mathrm{fac}})\).  Its kernel is at most \((s+|z|)/(s-|z|)\), while the mean-value identity makes the average boundary value equal to \(u(0)\).  Thus
\[
 u(z)\le\frac{s+|z|}{s-|z|}u(0).
\]
Letting \(s\uparrow R_{\mathrm{fac}}\) and using \(|z|\le R_1\) yields
\[
 u(z)\le\Lambda_{\star}A,
 \qquad
 |f(z)|\ge
 \exp\{-4(\Lambda_{\star}-1)\psi_{\eta}^2R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2\}. \tag{5}
\]
For \(z\in C_j\), (3) implies for every disk factor
\[
 \left|
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\nu)}
      {R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline{a_\nu}z}
 \right|
 \ge \frac{|z-a_\nu|}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|}
 \ge \frac{h_{\star}}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+R_1}.
\]
The last base belongs to \((0,1)\), and \(N\le N_{\mathrm{cert}}\).  Multiplication and (5) therefore give
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_j}|M(z)|
 \ge
 \exp\{-4(\Lambda_{\star}-1)\psi_{\eta}^2R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2\}
 \left(\frac{h_{\star}}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+R_1}\right)^{N_{\mathrm{cert}}}
 =a_{\mathrm{an}}
 \ge a_{\star}>0. \tag{6}
\]

It remains to verify effectiveness.  The canonical certified names of \((k,\delta,\psi_{\eta},R_0,R_1)\) give outward rational intervals for every displayed arithmetic expression.  Since \(\log(R_{\mathrm{out}}/R_{\mathrm{fac}})>0\), refinement eventually gives that denominator a positive rational lower endpoint and gives \(Q_{\star}\) a finite rational upper endpoint \(U_{\star}\).  Hence \(N_{\mathrm{cert}}=\lceil U_{\star}\rceil\) is computed after finitely many operations.  The search for \(m_{\star}\) and enumeration of its finite grid terminate.  Since \(a_{\mathrm{an}}>0\), refinement eventually supplies a positive rational lower endpoint \(L_{\star}\); the integer search for the least \(u_{\star}\ge1\) with \(2^{-u_{\star}}\le L_{\star}\) also terminates and returns the prescribed positive dyadic rational \(a_{\star}\).  No operation queries \(M\), \(\eta\), or their law.  Equations (1)--(6) apply to every represented transform and contain no \(n\)-dependent choice, proving the uniform claim.
