We prove the proposed statement, which adds only the necessary boundary condition \(n\ge2\).

\emph{Upper bound.}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  By the finite-contour-bank lemma there is an index \(j_0\) such that
\[
 N_{C_{j_0}}(M)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{j_0}}|M|\ge a_{\star}.
\]
The nuisance-zero-free lemma and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\) imply that \(|H_n|\ge3/4\) on the bank disk and that \(F_n\) and \(M\) have the same zeros there.  Therefore
\[
 N_{C_{j_0}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{j_0}}|F_n|\ge3a_{\star}/4.
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 \Delta_{F,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{G,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|.
\]
Let
\[
 \mathcal E=\{\Delta_{F,0}\le a_{\star}/8,\ \Delta_{F,1}\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
Since both half-sample sizes are bounded below by a fixed multiple of \(n\) for \(n\ge2\), the empirical-transform lemma and Markov's inequality give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}P(\mathcal E^c)\le K/n.
\]

On \(\mathcal E\), the favorable contour \(j_0\) has pilot boundary modulus at least \(5a_{\star}/8\).  The straight-line homotopy between \(F_n\) and \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) is zero-free on that boundary, so their winding numbers agree and \(j_0\) is pilot-admissible.  By the maximizing rule, the selected index \(\widehat j_n\) satisfies
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|
 \ge5a_{\star}/8.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_n|\ge a_{\star}/2,
 \qquad
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)\ge1.
\]
The evaluation transform then obeys
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{1,n}|
 \ge3a_{\star}/8>a_{\star}/4,
\]
so the evaluation fallback is not used.  This also proves measurability of the operational selection: the bank is finite, each boundary infimum equals the infimum over a fixed countable dense parametrization of its circle, the contour integrals use fixed parametrizations, and ties are deterministic.

The exact-contour theorem applies on the selected contour.  Put \(N=N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)=\widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}\).  Uniformly over the class and the bank,
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|
 \le E_P[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]\le K_G<\infty,
\]
because \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), and \(\eta,\xi\) have the stated sub-Gaussian envelopes.  Since \(N\ge1\) and every bank circle has radius at most \(R_1\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left|\frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_{C_{\widehat j_n}}
 \left\{\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}}{\widehat F_{1,n}}-
 \frac{G_n}{F_n}\right\}\,dz\right|\\
 &\quad\le R_1\left\{
 \frac{8}{3a_{\star}}\Delta_{G,1}
 +\frac{16K_G}{3a_{\star}^{2}}\Delta_{F,1}
 \right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Projection onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) and taking real parts cannot increase distance from the real number \(\theta_0\).  The exact-contour identity and the empirical-transform lemma therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0)^2\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E}]
 \le K/n.
\]
On \(\mathcal E^c\), both the estimator and the target lie in \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\), so the squared loss is at most \(4C_{\theta}^2\).  Combining this fact with the probability bound for \(\mathcal E^c\) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le C/n.
\]
The proof never uses \(\bar q_n\) or \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\), and the finite bank depends on the class constants rather than the unknown law of \(\eta\).  Since the minimax infimum ranges over all data-dependent estimators using the common codes, this estimator is an admissible witness and
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
 \le \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2].
\]

\emph{Lower bound.}
Use the Gaussian-outcome submodel from \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\).  Take two parameters \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\) in its interior with separation \(d=h/\sqrt n\), where \(h>0\) is a sufficiently small class-dependent constant.  Conditional on \((X,T)\), the outcome distributions are normals of common variance \(\tau^2\) and their means differ by \(d\eta\).  Their one-observation Hellinger affinity is
\[
 A_1=E_P\!\left[\exp\{-d^2\eta^2/(8\tau^2)\}\right]
 \ge\exp\{-d^2E_P[\eta^2]/(8\tau^2)\},
\]
where the inequality is Jensen's inequality.  Product structure and the uniform second-moment bound give
\[
 A_n=A_1^n\ge\exp\{-h^2C\psi_{\eta}^2/(8\tau^2)\}=:a_0>0.
\]
For densities \(p_0,p_1\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0,P_1)
 \le\sqrt{1-\left(\int\sqrt{p_0p_1}\right)^2},
\]
so the sum of the two testing errors is at least
\[
 1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n},P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})
 \ge1-\sqrt{1-a_0^2}\ge a_0^2/2.
\]
Turning any estimator into the test that selects the closer of \(\vartheta_0\) and \(\vartheta_1\), at least one of its two squared risks is therefore at least
\[
 \frac{d^2}{8}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n},P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})\}
 \ge \frac{h^2a_0^2}{16n}.
\]
Taking the infimum over estimators proves \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\ge c/n\).

\emph{Quantile and ACE comparison.}
For \(W=|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|\), Markov's inequality at \(t=\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}\) yields \(P(W>t)\le\gamma\).  Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}Q_{P,1-\gamma}(W)
 \le\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}.
\]
On the displayed class intersection and eligible indices, \(\text{lem:jms-ace-quantile-upper}\) supplies the published ACE guarantee.  Since \(6^r\le16^r\) and the coefficient \(4\) in its sampling term is at most \(64\), that published right-hand side is bounded above by \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\).  The spectral bound has no terms involving \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\) or powers of \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\).  Finally, if
\[
 A_n=\varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
 +C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}\gg n^{-1/2},
\]
then \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\ge c_1A_n\) while the spectral guarantee is \(O(n^{-1/2})\); their ratio is \(O(n^{-1/2}/A_n)=o(1)\).  This proves every clause of the proposed theorem.
