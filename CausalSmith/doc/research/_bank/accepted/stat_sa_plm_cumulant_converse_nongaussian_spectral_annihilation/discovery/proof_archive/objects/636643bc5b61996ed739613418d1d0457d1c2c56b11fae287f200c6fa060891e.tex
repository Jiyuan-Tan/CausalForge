The frozen assumptions control only a cumulant separation \(|\kappa_k(\eta_n)|\ge\delta_n\). Zero localization then controls how far a transform zero may lie from the origin, but it supplies no quantitative lower bound on the boundary modulus of a useful contour as \(\delta_n\downarrow0\). The compactness proof of the fixed-separation bank is non-effective and its cardinality, radii, and conditioning modulus may all deteriorate. Consequently the current primitives do not bound empirical contour variance or selector mistakes uniformly in \(\delta_n\), do not state a valid ordinary-DML rate or limit law to switch to, and contain no local least-favorable family from which a matching minimax converse could be derived.
