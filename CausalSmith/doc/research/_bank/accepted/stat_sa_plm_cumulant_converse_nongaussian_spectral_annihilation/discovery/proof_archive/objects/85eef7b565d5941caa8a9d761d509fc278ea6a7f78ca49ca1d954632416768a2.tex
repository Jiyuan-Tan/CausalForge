\emph{Local ACE benchmark.}
Fix \(n\).  The sequence \(\delta_n\) is positive, so the result
\(\text{lem:jms-ace-theorem-five-four}\) may be applied with its positive
cumulant-separation parameter specialized to \(\delta_n\).  Under this
specialization, the model class in \(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\) is precisely
\(\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)\): the restriction
\[
 \lvert\kappa_{r+1}(\eta)\rvert\geq\delta
\]
is replaced by
\[
 \lvert\kappa_{r+1}(\eta)\rvert\geq\delta_n,
\]
and every other member property is unchanged.  The quantities \(a_{1,n}\),
\(b_{1,n}\), and \(a_2\) do not involve the separation parameter.  The
quantity \(b_{2,n}\) becomes exactly
\[
 \widetilde b_{2,n}
 =200\min\{1,C_{\theta}\}\delta_n
 \max\!\left\{
   \varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n},
   (\gamma n)^{-1/2}(\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta})
 \right\}^{-1}.
\]
Consequently, the displayed local eligibility condition in the theorem is
exactly the condition in \(\text{def:jms-eligibility}\) after this
specialization.  The hypotheses \(\varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\),
together with the explicit requirement that all logarithms and denominators
be defined, supply the positivity and boundary conditions required by
\(\text{lem:jms-ace-theorem-five-four}\).  That result therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)\right|\right)\\
 &\quad\leq
 C_{\gamma}r!16^r\delta_n^{-1}
 \Bigl[
   \varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
   +C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}\\
 &\hspace{42mm}
   +64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
    \{r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\}
    (\gamma n)^{-1/2}
 \Bigr],
\end{aligned}
\]
which is the claimed \(U_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{loc}}\).  If an ordinary
DML upper bound \(U_{\mathrm{DML},n}\) is separately valid, the smaller of
the two numerical bounds is an oracle upper envelope.  The two inequalities
alone specify neither a sample-measurable rule that selects between their
estimators nor a lower bound for \(\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)\).  Hence this
comparison establishes neither adaptation nor a minimax phase diagram.

\emph{Gaussian--Rademacher path.}
Set \(k=4\), so \(r=3\), and fix \(0<a\leq1\).  Independence of
\(G_{\circ}\) and \(S\) gives, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_a(z)
 &=E[\exp\{z\sqrt{1-a^2}G_{\circ}\}]E[e^{zaS}]\\
 &=\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since
\[
 \log M_a(z)=\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2+\log\cosh(az)
\]
in a neighborhood of zero and the fourth derivative of
\(\log\cosh(az)\) at zero is \(-2a^4\), the cumulant definition yields
\[
 \kappa_4(\eta_a)=-2a^4.
\]
The characteristic function is
\[
 \varphi_a(t)=M_a(it)
 =\exp\!\left(-\frac{1-a^2}{2}t^2\right)\cos(at).
\]
Its exponential factor is strictly positive on the real line, so its first
positive zero is \(t_a=\pi/(2a)\).  For every real \(d\),
\[
 E[\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+d)\}]
 =\operatorname{Im}\{e^{it_ad}\varphi_a(t_a)\}=0.
\]

We next establish relevance.  Direct differentiation at \(t_a\) gives
\[
 \varphi_a'(t_a)
 =-a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\}
 =-A_a.
\]
The variable \(\eta_a\) has moments of every order, so differentiation under
the expectation is justified.  For every fixed \(d\in\mathbb R\), writing
\(f_d(t)=e^{itd}\varphi_a(t)\), the identity
\(\operatorname{Re} f_d'(t)=-E[(\eta_a+d)\sin\{t(\eta_a+d)\}]\)
implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E[(\eta_a+d)\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+d)\}]
 &=-\operatorname{Re} f_d'(t_a)\\
 &=-\operatorname{Re}\{e^{it_ad}\varphi_a'(t_a)\}\\
 &=A_a\cos(t_ad),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the term involving \(\varphi_a(t_a)\) vanishes.  Because
\(\eta_a\perp X\) and \(D_n\) is measurable with respect to \(X\),
conditioning on \(D_n\) gives
\[
 m_a:=E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =A_aE_P[\cos(t_aD_n)].
\]
Here \(s\geq r+1=4\).  Lyapunov's inequality, including its usual
\(L^{\infty}\) interpretation when \(s=\infty\), and
\(\text{ass:treatment-code-radius}\) yield
\[
 E_P|D_n|
 \leq\|D_n\|_{L^s(P_X)}
 \leq\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
The elementary inequality \(1-\cos u\leq |u|\) and the condition
\(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\leq1/2\) then imply
\[
 E_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
 \geq1-t_aE_P|D_n|
 \geq1-t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}
 \geq\frac12.
\]
Thus \(m_a\geq A_a/2>0\), which proves both the stated identity and the
denominator overlap condition.

It remains to prove the uniform risk bound.  Define
\[
 W=Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n),\qquad
 V=Y\sin(t_aZ_n),\qquad
 R=(Y-\theta_0Z_n)\sin(t_aZ_n).
\]
The residual identity gives \(Y-\theta_0Z_n=b_n(X)+\xi\).  Conditional
shift annihilation and \(\eta_a\perp X\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[b_n(X)\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 &=E_P\!\left[b_n(X)E_P\{\sin(t_aZ_n)\mid X\}\right]\\
 &=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, \(\sin(t_aZ_n)\) is measurable with respect to \((X,T)\), and
\(\text{ass:outcome-mean-independence}\) therefore gives
\[
 E_P[\xi\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P\!\left[\sin(t_aZ_n)E_P(\xi\mid X,T)\right]
 =0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 E_P[R]=0,
 \qquad
 E_P[V]=\theta_0m_a.
\]
This population identifying moment is distinct from the sample ratio used
below.

We now verify the uniform second-moment conditions.  Clipping gives
\(\|\bar g_n\|_{\infty}\leq C_g\), while
\(\text{ass:g-range}\) gives \(\|g_0\|_{\infty}\leq C_g\); hence
\(|D_n|\leq2C_g\).  Also \(E[\eta_a]=0\),
\(E[\eta_a^2]=1\), and \(\eta_a\perp D_n\).  Therefore
\[
 E_P[W^2]
 \leq E_P[Z_n^2]
 =1+E_P[D_n^2]
 \leq1+4C_g^2
 =:K_W.
\]
The range conditions similarly imply
\[
 |b_n(X)|
 \leq C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g.
\]
The conditional sub-Gaussian condition entails
\(E_P[\xi^2]\leq c_{\psi}\psi_{\xi}^2\), where \(c_{\psi}<\infty\)
is the universal constant determined by the convention for the
\(\psi_2\)-norm.  Since \(|\sin|\leq1\),
\[
 E_P[R^2]
 \leq2(C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g)^2+2c_{\psi}\psi_{\xi}^2
 =:K_R.
\]
The constants \(K_W\) and \(K_R\) are finite and independent of \(a\),
\(n\), and the particular path law.

Write \(\overline W=\mathbb P_nW\),
\(\overline V=\mathbb P_nV\), and
\(\mathcal E=\{\overline W\geq A_a/4\}\).  On \(\mathcal E\), projection
onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) is a contraction and
\(\theta_0\in[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\), so
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0|
 &\leq\left|\frac{\overline V}{\overline W}-\theta_0\right|\\
 &=\frac{|\mathbb P_nR|}{\overline W}
 \leq\frac{4}{A_a}|\mathbb P_nR|.
\end{aligned}
\]
By \(\text{ass:iid-sampling}\) and \(E_P[R]=0\),
\[
 E_P[(\mathbb P_nR)^2]
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(R)}{n}
 \leq\frac{K_R}{n}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E^c\), the estimator is zero and its squared error is at most
\(C_{\theta}^2\).  Since \(m_a\geq A_a/2\), Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P(\mathcal E^c)
 &\leq P\!\left(|\overline W-m_a|\geq\frac{A_a}{4}\right)\\
 &\leq\frac{16K_W}{nA_a^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining the regular-event and complement bounds yields
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \leq\frac{16(K_R+C_{\theta}^2K_W)}{nA_a^2}.
\]
Independently, both the clipped estimator and the target lie in
\([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\), so the risk is at most
\(4C_{\theta}^2\).  Taking the smaller bound and using
\[
 A_a^{-2}
 =a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\}
\]
proves the first risk display with a finite constant depending only on
\(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\).  All bounds are uniform over the
displayed path laws, so taking their supremum is valid.

Finally, \(\Delta_a=2a^4\) implies
\[
 a=2^{-1/4}\Delta_a^{1/4},
 \qquad
 a^{-2}=\sqrt2\,\Delta_a^{-1/2}.
\]
The relevance condition is equivalent to
\[
 t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\leq\frac12
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \varepsilon_{1,n}\leq\frac{a}{\pi}
 =\frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi}.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\}
 &=\sqrt2e^{-\pi^2/4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left\{\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Absorbing the fixed factor \(\sqrt2e^{-\pi^2/4}\) into \(C\) proves the
last risk display.  This derivation uses the explicit
Gaussian--Rademacher path only; it supplies no converse over the larger class
described solely by fourth-cumulant separation.  It therefore proves the
stated construction-specific upper benchmark, not a full minimax phase
diagram.
