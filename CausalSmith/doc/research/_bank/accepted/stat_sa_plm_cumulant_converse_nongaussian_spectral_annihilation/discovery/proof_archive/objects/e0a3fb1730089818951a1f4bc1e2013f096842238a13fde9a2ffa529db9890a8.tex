The strongest established result is the following three-part benchmark.  First, at every fixed \(\delta>0\), \(\text{thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}\) gives a computable certified statistic satisfying
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P\!\left[
   \bigl(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)\bigr)^2
 \right]
 \le \frac{C}{n}
\]
whenever \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), and the hard-submodel argument gives the matching lower bound \(c/n\).  Second, along \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), \(\text{thm:local-to-gaussian-partial-benchmarks}\) gives the explicit local ACE generalized-quantile upper bound proportional to \(\delta_n^{-1}\) under its displayed eligibility condition.  Third, on the Gaussian--Rademacher path, with \(\Delta_a=2a^4\), that theorem gives the computable mean-squared-error bound
\[
 C\min\!\left\{
  1,
  n^{-1}\Delta_a^{-1/2}
  \exp\!\left(
    \frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}
  \right)
 \right\}
\]
provided \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\Delta_a^{1/4}/(2^{1/4}\pi)\).  These results close the fixed-separation problem and provide two honest local upper benchmarks, but they do not answer the full shrinking-separation question.
