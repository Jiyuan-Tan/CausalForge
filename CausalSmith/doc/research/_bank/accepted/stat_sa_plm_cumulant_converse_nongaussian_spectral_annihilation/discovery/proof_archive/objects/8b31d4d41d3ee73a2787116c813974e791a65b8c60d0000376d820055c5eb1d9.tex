We prove the proposed statement.  Throughout the proof all constants are
uniform over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and may depend only on the
fixed class constants displayed in the theorem.

\emph{Upper bound.}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  By
\(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\), there is an index \(j_0\) such that
\[
 N_{C_{j_0}}(M)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_0}}|M(z)|\ge a_{\star}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:nuisance-zero-free}\) and
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), the function \(H_n\) is zero-free
on \(\{|z|\le R_1\}\), satisfies \(|H_n|\ge3/4\) there, and multiplication
by \(H_n\) preserves zeros and multiplicities.  Hence
\[
 N_{C_{j_0}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_0}}|F_n(z)|\ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4.       \tag{1}
\]

For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), put
\[
 \Delta_{F,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
   |\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{G,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
   |\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|.
\]
Let
\[
 \mathcal E_n=
 \{\Delta_{F,0}\le a_{\star}/8,\ 
   \Delta_{F,1}\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
For \(n\ge2\), both half-sample sizes are at least \(n/3\).
The bound in \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\), followed by
Markov's inequality at the fixed threshold \(a_{\star}/8\), gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}P(\mathcal E_n^c)
 \le \frac{K_0}{n}.                                    \tag{2}
\]

On \(\mathcal E_n\), (1) implies
\[
 \inf_{C_{j_0}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8.
\]
The straight-line homotopy between \(F_n\) and
\(\widehat F_{0,n}\) is zero-free on \(C_{j_0}\), since its modulus is
at least \(3a_{\star}/4-a_{\star}/8>0\).  Its winding number is therefore
constant, so \(j_0\) is pilot-admissible and its empirical winding number
is positive.  By the maximizing and deterministic tie-breaking rule in
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\), the selected index
\(\widehat j_n\) exists and satisfies
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8.                                 \tag{3}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_n|
 \ge\frac{a_{\star}}2.                                  \tag{4}
\]
The homotopy from \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) to \(F_n\) is zero-free on the
selected circle by (3), and hence
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}
 =N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)\ge1.                        \tag{5}
\]
Equations (4) and the evaluation-half part of \(\mathcal E_n\) also give
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{1,n}|
 \ge\frac{3a_{\star}}8>\frac{a_{\star}}4,              \tag{6}
\]
so the evaluation fallback is not used.  The selector is measurable:
the bank is finite, each boundary infimum is the infimum along a fixed
countable dense parametrization of its circle, the contour integrals use
fixed parametrizations, and all ties and failures have deterministic
rules.

The hypotheses of \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) now hold on
the selected contour: (4) gives boundary zero-freeness, the nuisance
lemma gives zero-freeness of \(H_n\) throughout its interior, and (5)
gives positive zero count.  Write
\(N=N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)=\widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}\).
The class restrictions imply
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|
 \le E_P[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]
 \le K_G<\infty.                                        \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\),
\(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), and Cauchy--Schwarz together with
the two sub-Gaussian envelopes bounds every term in (7).

For every \(z\) on the selected circle, (4), (6), and (7) yield
\[
 \left|
  \frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}
  -\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}
 \right|
 \le
 \frac{8}{3a_{\star}}\Delta_{G,1}
 +\frac{16K_G}{3a_{\star}^2}\Delta_{F,1}.              \tag{8}
\]
Since \(N\ge1\), every bank-circle radius is at most \(R_1\), and taking
real parts and projecting onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot
increase distance from the real number \(\theta_0(P)\), the exact contour
identity and (8) imply
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|
 \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}
 \le R_1\left\{
   \frac{8}{3a_{\star}}\Delta_{G,1}
   +\frac{16K_G}{3a_{\star}^2}\Delta_{F,1}
 \right\}.                                             \tag{9}
\]
Applying \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) to (9) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2
      \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}]
 \le\frac{K_1}{n}.                                     \tag{10}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n^c\), both the estimator and target belong to
\([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\), so their squared distance is at most
\(4C_{\theta}^2\).  Combining (2) and (10) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac Cn.                                          \tag{11}
\]
Neither the estimator nor this proof uses \(\bar q_n\) or
\(\varepsilon_{2,n}\), and the finite bank depends only on fixed class
constants, not on the unknown law of \(\eta\).  Thus the declared
statistic is a single data-driven minimax witness, and the definition of
\(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]. \tag{12}
\]

\emph{Lower bound.}
Use the submodel in \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\), and let
\(\tau^2>0\) be its fixed Gaussian innovation variance.  Choose a fixed
\(h>0\), depending only on the class constants, small enough that for
every \(n\ge2\) two parameters \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\) in the
interior of the submodel can be chosen with
\[
 d:=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=\frac h{\sqrt n}.
\]
Conditional on \((X,T)\), their outcome laws are normal with common
variance \(\tau^2\) and means differing by \(d\eta\).  Their
one-observation Hellinger affinity is therefore
\[
 A_1=E_P\!\left[\exp\{-d^2\eta^2/(8\tau^2)\}\right]
 \ge \exp\{-d^2E_P[\eta^2]/(8\tau^2)\},                \tag{13}
\]
where the inequality is Jensen's inequality.  Affinity tensorizes under
independent products, and the treatment sub-Gaussian bound gives
\(E_P[\eta^2]\le K_2\psi_{\eta}^2\).  Hence
\[
 A_n=A_1^n
 \ge \exp\{-h^2K_2\psi_{\eta}^2/(8\tau^2)\}
 =:a_0>0.                                               \tag{14}
\]
For any two dominated probability laws with affinity \(A\),
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\((\sqrt{p_0}-\sqrt{p_1})(\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt{p_1})\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0,P_1)\le\sqrt{1-A^2}.            \tag{15}
\]
Turn an arbitrary estimator into the test choosing the closer of
\(\vartheta_0\) and \(\vartheta_1\).  Its maximum squared risk over the
two points is at least
\[
 \frac{d^2}{8}
 \{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n},
                       P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})\}
 \ge \frac{d^2a_0^2}{16}
 =\frac{h^2a_0^2}{16n},                                 \tag{16}
\]
where (14), (15), and
\(1-\sqrt{1-u}\ge u/2\) for \(u\in[0,1]\) were used.  Both points are
members of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  Taking the infimum over all
estimators in (16) proves \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\ge c/n\).

\emph{Quantile and exact ACE comparison.}
Let
\(W=|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|\).  From (11) and
Markov's inequality, at \(t=\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}\),
\[
 P(W>t)\le\gamma.
\]
Thus \(P(W\le t)\ge1-\gamma\), and the definition of the generalized
quantile gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}(W)\le\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}.               \tag{17}
\]

By \(\text{prop:jms-ace-alignment}\), norm monotonicity supplies the two
\(L^r(P_X)\) code controls for every member of
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  Comparing the remaining member
conditions in \(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\) and
\(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\) gives the one-way inclusion
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}.   \tag{18}
\]
This does not assert equality: the first class uses the stricter
\(L^s(P_X)\) balls, whereas the second uses \(L^r(P_X)\) balls.

Suppose now that \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) holds.  Its definition
requires every displayed logarithm to be defined and therefore implies
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\).  If \(\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\), the corrected
cited leaf \(\text{lem:jms-ace-quantile-upper}\) gives exactly
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
 \le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}.                         \tag{19}
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{2,n}=0\), choose
\(u\in(0,\varepsilon_{1,n})\) and enlarge only the outcome-code radius
from zero to \(u\).  The original class is contained in the enlarged
class, and the maximum defining \(b_{2,n}\) is unchanged because
\(u<\varepsilon_{1,n}\).  Thus the same eligibility condition holds.
Applying the cited positive-radius theorem to the enlarged class gives
the right-hand side
\[
 C_{\gamma}r!16^r\delta^{-1}
 \Big[\varepsilon_{1,n}^r u+C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
 +64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
 \{r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\}
 (\gamma n)^{-1/2}\Big].
\]
Letting \(u\downarrow0\) proves (19) at the zero-radius boundary as
well.  Restricting the supremum in (19) by (18) shows that the exact
published \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\) comparison holds throughout
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), not merely on an unspecified
intersection.

Finally, set
\[
 A_n=\varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
     +C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}.
\]
The definition of \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\) has the exact published
coefficients \(16^r\) and \(64\), and its remaining sampling term is a
fixed multiple of \(n^{-1/2}\).  If \(A_n\gg n^{-1/2}\), then the ratio
of the spectral guarantee (17), which is \(O(n^{-1/2})\), to
\(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\), which is bounded below by a fixed positive
multiple of \(A_n\), tends to zero.  This proves the claimed strict
order improvement and completes the proof.
