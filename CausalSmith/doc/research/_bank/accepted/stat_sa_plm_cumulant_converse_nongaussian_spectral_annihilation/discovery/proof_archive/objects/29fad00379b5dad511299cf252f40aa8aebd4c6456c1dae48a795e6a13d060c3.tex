For the ACE claim, fix \(n\).  The number \(\delta_n\) is strictly positive.  Apply the cited Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis Theorem 5.4 in \(\text{lem:jms-ace-theorem-five-four}\) with its class-separation constant set equal to \(\delta_n\).  Every other class restriction is unchanged by the definition of \(\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)\).  Under that substitution, the theorem's quantity \(b_{2,n}\) is exactly \(\widetilde b_{2,n}\), so its condition (21) is exactly the displayed local eligibility inequality.  Substitution of \(\delta_n\) for \(\delta\) in its bound (22) gives the displayed inequality term by term.  This is a pointwise-in-\(n\) application of a published estimator bound.  Taking the smaller of this deterministic bound and any separately justified DML bound merely records which oracle guarantee is numerically smaller; it supplies neither a simultaneous proof of the DML hypotheses nor a data-dependent switching rule.

We next prove every assertion for the Gaussian--Rademacher path.  Independence of \(G_{\circ}\) and \(S\) gives, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_a(z)
 &=E[e^{z\sqrt{1-a^2}G_{\circ}}]E[e^{zaS}]\\
 &=\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az).
\end{aligned}
\]
The fourth cumulant is additive over independent summands, vanishes for the Gaussian summand, and is homogeneous of degree four.  Since
\[
 \kappa_4(S)=E[S^4]-3E[S^2]^2=1-3=-2,
\]
we obtain \(\kappa_4(\eta_a)=a^4\kappa_4(S)=-2a^4\).  Also,
\[
 \phi_a(t):=M_a(it)
 =\exp\!\left\{-\frac{1-a^2}{2}t^2\right\}\cos(at).
\]
The exponential factor is strictly positive on the real line, so the first positive zero is \(t_a=\pi/(2a)\).  The path is uniformly sub-Gaussian because, for real \(u\),
\[
 E[e^{u\eta_a}]
 =e^{(1-a^2)u^2/2}\cosh(au)
 \le e^{(1-a^2)u^2/2}e^{a^2u^2/2}
 =e^{u^2/2}.
\]

Symmetry makes \(\phi_a\) real.  For any real shift \(d\),
\[
 E[e^{it_a(\eta_a+d)}]
 =e^{it_ad}\phi_a(t_a)=0.
\]
Taking imaginary parts proves exact sine annihilation.  Differentiation of the characteristic function is justified by the displayed sub-Gaussian bound, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E[\eta_a\sin(t_a\eta_a)]
 &=-\phi_a'(t_a)\\
 &=a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\}
 =A_a.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(D_n\) is measurable with respect to \(X\) and \(\eta_a\perp X\), conditioning on \(D_n=d\), expanding the sine of a sum, and using symmetry together with \(\phi_a(t_a)=0\) yields
\[
 E_P[(\eta_a+D_n)\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+D_n)\}\mid D_n]
 =A_a\cos(t_aD_n).
\]
Thus the asserted relevance identity follows after expectation.  The clipping definition and \(\lVert g_0\rVert_{\infty}\le C_g\) give \(\lvert D_n\rvert\le2C_g\), while the \(L^s(P_X)\) radius and \(s\ge1\) give \(E_P\lvert D_n\rvert\le\varepsilon_{1,n}\).  The elementary inequality \(1-\cos x\le\lvert x\rvert\) therefore gives
\[
 E_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
 \ge1-t_aE_P\lvert D_n\rvert
 \ge\frac12.
\]
This proves the positive denominator bound and states the boundary condition used by every subsequent division.

To identify the ratio's target, recall \(Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi\).  Conditional sine annihilation gives
\[
 E_P[b_n(X)\sin(t_aZ_n)]=0,
\]
and the conditional-mean restriction gives
\[
 E_P[\xi\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P[E_P\{\xi\mid X,T\}\sin(t_aZ_n)]=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 E_P[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =\theta_0E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)].
\]
This distinguishes the causal target \(\theta_0\) from the identifying ratio and proves that they agree on the displayed path class.

It remains to prove the uniform risk bound.  Put
\[
 V=Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n),
 \qquad
 W=(Y-\theta_0Z_n)\sin(t_aZ_n),
 \qquad
 \mu_a=E_P[V].
\]
The preceding identities give \(E_P[W]=0\) and \(\mu_a\ge A_a/2>0\).  Moreover,
\[
 E_P[V^2]\le E_P[Z_n^2]
 \le2E[\eta_a^2]+2E_P[D_n^2]
 \le2+8C_g^2.
\]
Since \(b_n=q_0-\theta_0D_n\), the support restrictions imply \(\lvert b_n\rvert\le C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g\).  Conditional sub-Gaussianity supplies a constant \(c<\infty\), independent of \(a,n,P\), such that \(E_P[\xi^2]\le c\psi_{\xi}^2\).  Hence
\[
 E_P[W^2]
 \le E_P[(b_n+\xi)^2]
 \le2(C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g)^2+2c\psi_{\xi}^2=:K_W,
\]
while \(\operatorname{Var}_P(V)\le K_V:=2+8C_g^2\).

On the event \(\mathbb P_nV\ge A_a/4\), projection onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) is a contraction at \(\theta_0\), so
\[
 \left|\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0\right|
 \le\frac{4}{A_a}\left|\mathbb P_nW\right|.
\]
Independence of the sample and \(E_P[W]=0\) give \(E_P[(\mathbb P_nW)^2]=\operatorname{Var}_P(W)/n\le K_W/n\).  On the complementary event, \(\mu_a\ge A_a/2\) implies \(\lvert\mathbb P_nV-\mu_a\rvert\ge A_a/4\); Chebyshev's inequality therefore gives
\[
 P\!\left(\mathbb P_nV<A_a/4\right)
 \le\frac{16K_V}{nA_a^2}.
\]
Both the estimator and target lie in \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\), so the squared loss on this event is at most \(4C_{\theta}^2\).  Combining the two events yields
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \le\frac{16K_W+64C_{\theta}^2K_V}{nA_a^2}.
\]
The deterministic clipping bound also gives risk at most \(4C_{\theta}^2\).  Taking the smaller of these two bounds and substituting
\[
 A_a^{-2}
 =a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\}
\]
proves the first risk display uniformly over the stated laws.

Finally, \(\Delta_a=2a^4\) implies \(a=(\Delta_a/2)^{1/4}\), so
\[
 a^{-2}=\sqrt2\,\Delta_a^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}
 \le\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}.
\]
Also \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le1/2\) is equivalent to \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le a/\pi=\Delta_a^{1/4}/(2^{1/4}\pi)\).  Absorbing the factor \(\sqrt2\) into the path constant gives the construction-specific cumulant parametrization.  Because this derivation uses the complete Gaussian--Rademacher transform rather than only the inequality \(\lvert\kappa_4\rvert\ge\Delta_a\), it supplies no converse and no full-class sharpness claim.
