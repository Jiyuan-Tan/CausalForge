The strongest result presently derivable is a collection of three
benchmarks, not a sharp full-class phase diagram.  First,
\(\text{thm:local-to-gaussian-partial-benchmarks}\) applies the
Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis finite-order ACE theorem pointwise in \(n\), with
the separation constant replaced by \(\delta_n\).  Under its exact local
eligibility and positivity conditions it gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)\right|\right)\\
 &\quad\le
 C_{\gamma}r!16^r\delta_n^{-1}
 \Bigl[
   \varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
   +C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}\\
 &\hspace{38mm}
   +64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
    \{r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\}
    (\gamma n)^{-1/2}
 \Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]
Putting this beside a separately valid DML bound yields only an oracle
upper envelope; it neither constructs a data-driven selector nor proves a
minimax rate.

Second, on the explicit path with \(k=4\),
\[
 \eta_a=\sqrt{1-a^2}\,G_{\circ}+aS,
 \qquad
 \Delta_a=|\kappa_4(\eta_a)|=2a^4,
\]
the same theorem constructs an exact sine-annihilation estimator.  When
\[
 \varepsilon_{1,n}
 \le \frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi},
\]
it obeys the pathwise risk bound
\[
 \sup_P E_P\!\left[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0(P))^2\right]
 \le
 C\min\!\left\{
  1,\frac1n\Delta_a^{-1/2}
  \exp\!\left(
    \frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}
  \right)
 \right\}.
\]
This uses the full Gaussian--Rademacher transform and is not uniform over
the class defined only by a fourth-cumulant lower bound.

Third, \(\text{lem:zero-localization}\) gives the proof-intermediate
radius
\[
 R_{0,n}
 \le A_k\left(\frac{\psi_{\eta}^2}{\delta_n}\right)^{1/(k-2)}.
\]
Together with the nuisance-factor calculation, exact contour
identification persists whenever
\[
 \varepsilon_{1,n}
 \le
 \left[4(R_{0,n}+1)e^{2C_g(R_{0,n}+1)}\right]^{-1}.
\]
This last condition is an identification benchmark rather than a uniform
risk bound: fixed-separation compactness does not quantify the contour
boundary modulus as \(\delta_n\downarrow0\).  These results supply no
cumulant-only matching lower bound, full-class elbow, adaptive selector,
or uniform coverage theorem.
