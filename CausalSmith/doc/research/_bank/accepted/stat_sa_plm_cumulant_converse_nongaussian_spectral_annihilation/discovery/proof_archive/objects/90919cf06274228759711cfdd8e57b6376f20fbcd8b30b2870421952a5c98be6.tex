The non-Gaussian assertions are immediate from \(\text{thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}\): on every stated index it gives \(c/n\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\le C/n\).  The class definition and estimator construction use \(\bar g_n\), but impose no restriction involving, and make no use of, \(\widehat q_n,\bar q_n\), or \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\).  This remains true for a fixed nonzero treatment-code radius.  Separately, \(\text{prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy}\) proves that the target is zero throughout the simultaneous Gaussian intersection and computes both losses as zero when it is nonempty.  Since these conclusions concern separately defined classes and no inclusion or equality has been proved, they imply neither a Gaussian-versus-non-Gaussian minimax dichotomy nor a shrinking-separation frontier.
