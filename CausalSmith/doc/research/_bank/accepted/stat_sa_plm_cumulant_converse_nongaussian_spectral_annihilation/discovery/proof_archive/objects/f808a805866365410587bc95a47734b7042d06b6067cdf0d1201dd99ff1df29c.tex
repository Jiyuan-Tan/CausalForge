\emph{Canonical computation.}  The bank algorithm in \(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\) terminates by \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\) and uses only the canonical certified names of \((k,\delta,\psi_{\eta},R_0,R_1)\).  Given the represented sample, subtraction, multiplication, absolute value, and the interval Taylor algorithm for the exponential convert the canonical dyadic names of \(T_i,Y_i,\bar g_n(X_i)\) into outward rational enclosures for every finite sum defining \(\widehat F_{a,n}^{(p)}\) and \(\widehat G_{a,n}^{(p)}\).  Refinement makes every such enclosure arbitrarily narrow.

For a fixed circle, the derived bound \(L^F_{aj}\) is finite.  Hence a finite uniform mesh makes its Lipschitz allowance at most \(a_{\star}/128\), and finite refinement makes the aggregate node error at most \(a_{\star}/128\).  Thus every modulus certificate terminates.  A winding computation is attempted only after a positive rational denominator certificate has been obtained.  The displayed derivative bound for \(W_{0j}\) is then finite, so finite mesh and precision refinement produce an enclosure of radius below \(1/4\).  Because \(\widehat F_{0,n}(0)=1\), the empirical entire function is not identically zero.  Its zeros inside a zero-free bank circle are isolated and finite, and the argument principle makes the logarithmic-derivative integral their nonnegative integer count.  Therefore the radius-below-\(1/4\) enclosure contains a unique nonnegative integer.  The finite bank, rational comparisons, and prescribed smallest-index tie-break make pilot selection terminate.  The same argument applies to the evaluation modulus.  If that certificate passes, the derivative bound for \(V\) is finite, so finite refinement reaches the required radius \(1/n\).  Every branch therefore returns either the prescribed fallback or one rational midpoint after finitely many operations.

The dyadic name \(\mathcal I_m(x)\) is uniquely determined by \(x\).  Equal primitive constants and equal represented sample values consequently generate the same interval queries, rational trace, and output, so the algorithm is extensional.  For Borel measurability, fix a finite trace.  Each queried endpoint is a floor of a scaled sample coordinate and hence is Borel; \(\bar g_n(X_i)\) is Borel because \(\bar g_n\) is measurable.  Finite interval operations and rational branch comparisons preserve Borel dependence.  There are countably many finite traces, and the output on each terminal trace is a Borel rational expression followed by real part and continuous projection.  The countable union over terminal traces proves that \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) is a total Borel measurable observed-sample map.  It uses \(\bar g_n\), no supplied outcome code, and no treatment-noise law.

\emph{Uniform upper bound.}  Put \(m_a=|I_a|\).  For \(n\ge2\), \(m_a\ge n/3\).  Fix, as required by the repaired identification theorem, an arbitrary \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and choose \(j_{\star}\) from \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\).  By \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\),
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|F_n|\ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4.
 \tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
 d_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \qquad
 e_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_a=\{d_a\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\), the canonical modulus interval for \(j_{\star}\), whose width is at most \(a_{\star}/64\), has lower endpoint at least
\[
 \left(\frac34-\frac18-\frac1{64}\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{39}{64}a_{\star}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The straight-line homotopy from \(F_n\) to \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) is nonzero on \(C_{j_{\star}}\), so their winding counts coincide and are positive.  Thus \(j_{\star}\) is pilot-admissible.

Let \(C=C_{\widehat j_n}\).  Maximality of the selected certified lower endpoint and (2), followed by subtraction of \(d_0\), imply
\[
 \inf_C|F_n|
 \ge\left(\frac{39}{64}-\frac18\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{31}{64}a_{\star}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The same homotopy argument on the selected circle gives
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_C(F_n)=:N\ge1.
 \tag{4}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), (3) gives an evaluation empirical modulus of at least \(23a_{\star}/64\).  Subtracting the enclosure width gives the certified lower endpoint
\[
 \frac{22}{64}a_{\star}>\frac14a_{\star}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus no fallback occurs on this event.

There is a finite class-uniform constant \(C_G\) such that
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|\le C_G.
 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\).  The exact Luxemburg inequality gives
\[
 E_Pe^{t|\eta|}
 \le\sqrt2\exp(t^2\psi_{\eta}^2/2),
\]
by \(t|\eta|\le\eta^2/(2\psi_{\eta}^2)+t^2\psi_{\eta}^2/2\).  It also gives uniform second moments for \(\eta\) and \(\xi\).  Cauchy--Schwarz therefore bounds \(E_P[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]\) using only \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\), and \(R_1\), proving (6) without requiring independence between \(\xi\) and \(\eta\).

On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), subtract the empirical and population ratios on \(C\).  Equations (3), (5), and (6), together with \(\operatorname{len}(C)\le2\pi R_1\) and \(N\ge1\), give
\[
 \left|
 \frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C
 \frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}\,dz
 -
 \frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C
 \frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
 \right|
 \le L_{\star}(d_1+e_1),
 \tag{7}
\]
where, with \(\mu=31a_{\star}/64\) and \(\nu=23a_{\star}/64\), one may take
\[
 L_{\star}=R_1\max\{\nu^{-1},C_G(\mu\nu)^{-1}\}.
\]
The repaired \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) applies to the fixed law \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\): (3) makes \(F_n\) zero-free on \(C\), \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) makes \(H_n\) zero-free on and inside \(C\), and (4) gives the positive zero count.  Hence the second contour functional in (7) equals the causal target \(\theta_0(P)\).  The certified midpoint adds complex error at most \(1/n\); taking its real part and projecting onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase its distance from \(\theta_0(P)\).

By \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\), uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 E_P(d_a^2+e_a^2)\le\frac{3K}{n},
 \qquad
 P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)
 \le\frac{384K}{a_{\star}^2n}.
 \tag{8}
\]
On the exceptional event, projection bounds squared error by \(4C_{\theta}^2\).  On the good event, (7), \((d_1+e_1)^2\le2(d_1^2+e_1^2)\), and the numerical error contribute at most \(12L_{\star}^2K/n+2/n^2\) in expectation.  Consequently
\[
 C_{\mathrm{up}}
 =12L_{\star}^2K
 +\frac{1536C_{\theta}^2K}{a_{\star}^2}+2
\]
is valid, and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac{C_{\mathrm{up}}}{n}.
 \tag{9}
\]
The bank, \(a_{\star}\), \(C_G\), \(K\), and \(L_{\star}\) depend only on the displayed fixed class constants.  The statistic is therefore one admissible data-driven minimax rule, proving \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\le C_{\mathrm{up}}/n\).

\emph{Broad-class converse.}  Invoke the first branch of \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\).  Choose fixed \(h>0\), depending only on the class constants, small enough that \(\vartheta_{\pm}=\pm h/(2\sqrt n)\) belong to the path for every \(n\ge2\) and
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_-^{\otimes n}\Vert P_+^{\otimes n})\le\frac18.
\]
Pinsker's inequality gives total variation at most \(1/4\).  Testing by the sign of an arbitrary estimator yields
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-,+\}}
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}}[(\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 \ge\frac{h^2}{8n}
 \{1-\|P_-^{\otimes n}-P_+^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}
 \ge\frac{3h^2}{32n}.
\]
Taking the class supremum and estimator infimum proves \(c_{\mathrm{NG}}/n\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\), with \(c_{\mathrm{NG}}=3h^2/32\).

\emph{Published ACE-class converse and upper bound.}  By \(\text{lem:jms-ace-class-relations}\),
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}.
 \tag{10}
\]
When the published class is nonempty and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), restriction of (9) to (10) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac{C_{\mathrm{up}}}{n}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The second branch of \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\) gives, on this same class,
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\theta(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\bar g_n,\bar q_n)}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 E_P[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \ge\frac{c_{\mathrm{ACE}}}{n}.
 \tag{12}
\]
The middle inequality in the claimed ACE bracket is the definition of an infimum: the certified statistic is one admissible estimator.  Set
\[
 c=\min\{c_{\mathrm{NG}},c_{\mathrm{ACE}}\}>0,
 \qquad
 C=C_{\mathrm{up}}.
\]
Equations (9), (11), and (12) prove both matched minimax mean-squared-error brackets with the same displayed constants.

Finally, Markov's inequality applied to (9) gives
\[
 P\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|
 >\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}
 \right)\le\gamma,
\]
which is the broad-class generalized-quantile bound; restriction through (10) proves the full published ACE-class version.  The same class-relation lemma proves the stated relation with \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\), while it gives no reverse inclusion of the broad spectral class in the published ACE class.

The lower bound in (12) concerns the minimax mean-squared error over all estimators on the published class.  It says nothing about the risk of the particular ACE estimator and does not turn the cited generalized-quantile upper bound into a lower bound.  Thus the ACE-versus-spectral generalized-quantile comparison remains upper-versus-upper.  All computed constants are fixed only for fixed \(\delta>0\); no shrinking-separation rate, adaptive selector, uniform inference result, coverage guarantee, or matching local lower bound follows.
