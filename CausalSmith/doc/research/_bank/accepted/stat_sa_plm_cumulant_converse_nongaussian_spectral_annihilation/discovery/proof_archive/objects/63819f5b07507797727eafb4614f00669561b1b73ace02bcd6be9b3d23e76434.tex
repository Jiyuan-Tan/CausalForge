We prove the shared-record representation claim, the uniform upper bound,
the two minimax converses, and the quantile and comparator conclusions.

\emph{1. Mathematical and represented-data layers.}
Fix the exact records \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) and
\(\mathfrak c_{\theta,\star}\) from
\(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\) and
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\).  They are fixed before the law,
sample size, observation records, and execution trace vary.  Unfolding
\(\text{def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate}\), the real interval
formulas are sound because the extrema of a bilinear function on a rectangle
occur at its corners.  Substitution in
\[
 (x+iy)(u+iv)=(xu-yv)+i(xv+yu)
\]
then proves complex-multiplication containment.  The square bounds enclose
each coordinate square, rational bisection preserves the square-root
bracketing invariant, and a positive modulus lower bound makes the squared
denominator strictly positive.  The displayed conjugate formula and outward
division therefore prove guarded complex-division containment.

Machin's identity together with the displayed alternating remainder proves
the certified \(\pi\) contract at a finite cutoff.  The Lagrange remainders
and explicit cutoffs prove the bounded-interval contracts for exponential,
sine, and cosine, and hence for
\(e^{x+iy}=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)\).  Finite induction proves the sum and
product contracts.  On a mesh cell, a function with rational Lipschitz bound
\(L\) differs from its node value by at most
\(L/N_{\mathrm{mesh}}\); integration and finite endpoint minima prove the
quadrature and boundary-infimum enclosures.  Every cutoff and mesh size is
an explicit integer and all ensuing arithmetic is finite.

Consequently, if a concrete module implementing these algorithms and
contracts is added and compiled, the represented-data procedure in
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\), on inputs whose primitive
components are exactly the two records fixed above, is a total transducer.
Its final output is a certified-real record because
\[
 \frac{|y+C_{\theta}|-|y-C_{\theta}|}{2}
 =\Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}(y)                               \tag{1}
\]
in the three cases \(y<-C_{\theta}\),
\(|y|\le C_{\theta}\), and \(y>C_{\theta}\), and the left side uses only
certified arithmetic and interval absolute value.  Identical full input
records cause the same finite modulus calls and rational comparisons, hence
the same trace and output record.  This conditional statement neither
quantifies over alternative primitive records nor asserts extensionality
across arbitrary names.

Independently of compilation, the ordinary-sample interpretation uses these
same primitive records and canonicalizes only the observation coordinates.
On a fixed terminal trace, every queried observation endpoint is a floor of
a scaled sample coordinate and is Borel measurable.  Rational arithmetic
and endpoint comparisons preserve Borel measurability.  There are only
countably many finite traces, and the procedure is total, so partitioning the
sample space by terminal trace proves that
\(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) is a total Borel statistic.  By the
definition of the two layers, the represented output has the same value.
Inspection of the construction shows that it uses \(\bar g_n\), no supplied
outcome code, and no treatment-noise law.

\emph{2. Uniform upper bound.}
Fix \(n\ge2\), \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\).  Let \(j_{\star}\) be supplied by
\(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\).  By
\(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\),
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|F_n|\ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4.                 \tag{2}
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), put
\[
 d_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \quad
 e_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|,
 \quad
 \mathcal A_a=\{d_a\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\), soundness and the width
\(a_{\star}/64\) of the certified modulus interval give the candidate
\(j_{\star}\) a lower endpoint at least
\[
 \left(\frac34-\frac18-\frac1{64}\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{39a_{\star}}{64}.                                        \tag{3}
\]
The straight-line homotopy between \(F_n\) and
\(\widehat F_{0,n}\) is nonzero on this circle, so their winding numbers
agree and are positive.  The sound enclosure contains that integer in its
real coordinate and zero in its imaginary coordinate, with coordinate
widths below \(1/4\).  Thus \(j_{\star}\) is admissible.

Let \(C=C_{\widehat j_n}\).  Maximality of the selected lower endpoint,
(3), and subtraction of \(d_0\) give
\[
 \inf_C|F_n|\ge\frac{31a_{\star}}{64}.                            \tag{4}
\]
The same homotopy and the sound winding enclosure give
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_C(F_n)=:N\ge1.                   \tag{5}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), the evaluation empirical modulus is
at least \(23a_{\star}/64\); after subtracting the enclosure width, its
certified lower endpoint is at least \(22a_{\star}/64>a_{\star}/4\).
Hence the evaluation branch does not fall back.

There is a finite constant \(C_G\), depending only on
\(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},R_1\), such that
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|\le C_G.                               \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and
\(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\).  From the exact Luxemburg convention and
Young's inequality,
\[
 E e^{t|\eta|}\le\sqrt2\,e^{t^2\psi_{\eta}^2/2},
\]
and the same convention bounds the second moments of \(\eta\) and \(\xi\).
Cauchy--Schwarz then bounds
\(E[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]\), without requiring independence of \(\xi\) and
\(\eta\), proving (6).

Subtract the empirical and population ratios on \(C\).  Equations
(4)--(6), \(\operatorname{len}(C)\le2\pi R_1\), and \(N\ge1\) give
\[
 \left|
 \frac1{N2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}}{\widehat F_{1,n}}\,dz
 -\frac1{N2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{G_n}{F_n}\,dz
 \right|
 \le L_{\mathrm{rat}}(d_1+e_1),                                  \tag{7}
\]
where, with \(\mu=31a_{\star}/64\) and
\(\nu=23a_{\star}/64\), one may take
\[
 L_{\mathrm{rat}}
 =R_1\max\{\nu^{-1},C_G(\mu\nu)^{-1}\}.                         \tag{8}
\]
The hypotheses of \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) now hold:
(4) makes \(F_n\) zero-free on \(C\),
\(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) makes \(H_n\) zero-free on and inside
\(C\), and (5) supplies a positive count.  The population contour in (7)
therefore equals the causal target \(\theta_0(P)\).  The final rational
midpoint adds at most \(1/n\), and taking the real part followed by
projection on \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase distance from
\(\theta_0(P)\).

Both fold sizes are at least \(n/3\).  Therefore
\(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) and Markov's inequality give,
for its constant \(K\),
\[
 E_P(d_a^2+e_a^2)\le\frac{3K}{n},
 \qquad
 P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)
 \le\frac{384K}{a_{\star}^2n}.                                  \tag{9}
\]
Projection bounds squared error by \(4C_{\theta}^2\) on the exceptional
event.  On the good event, (7),
\((d_1+e_1)^2\le2(d_1^2+e_1^2)\), and the numerical error yield
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac{C_{\mathrm{up}}}{n},                                  \tag{10}
\]
where, for example,
\[
 C_{\mathrm{up}}
 =12L_{\mathrm{rat}}^2K
 +\frac{1536C_{\theta}^2K}{a_{\star}^2}+2.                      \tag{11}
\]
The bank, \(a_{\star}\), \(C_G\), \(K\), and
\(L_{\mathrm{rat}}\) depend only on
\(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\).  In particular,
a width-one refinement of any fixed valid primitive record has upper endpoint
at most its represented value plus one, so the integer bounds used to form
\(a_{\star}\) are bounded solely by those values.  Thus
\(C_{\mathrm{up}}\) is outside all quantifiers over \(P\), \(n\), samples,
traces, and laws, and is independent of \(\gamma\).  Since the Borel
statistic is one data-driven rule, (10) also proves the minimax upper bound.

\emph{3. Converses and quantiles.}
Apply the first branch of \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\).  Choose
a fixed \(h>0\), depending only on class constants, such that
\(\vartheta_{\pm}=\pm h/(2\sqrt n)\) lie on its path and the product
relative entropy is at most \(1/8\).  Pinsker's inequality gives total
variation at most \(1/4\).  Testing by the sign of an arbitrary estimator
then yields
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-,+\}}
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}}[(\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 \ge\frac{h^2}{8n}(1-1/4)
 =\frac{3h^2}{32n}.                                               \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(c_{\mathrm{NG}}/n\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\) for a fixed
\(c_{\mathrm{NG}}>0\).

By \(\text{lem:jms-ace-class-relations}\),
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}.                            \tag{13}
\]
Restriction of (10) proves the spectral upper bound on the full published
ACE class.  The second branch of
\(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\) gives on this same class
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\theta}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 E_P[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \ge\frac{c_{\mathrm{ACE}}}{n}.                                  \tag{14}
\]
The Borel statistic is admissible in this infimum.  Taking
\[
 c=\min\{c_{\mathrm{NG}},c_{\mathrm{ACE}}\}>0,
 \qquad C=C_{\mathrm{up}}
\]
proves both stated mean-squared-error brackets.

Finally, (10) and Markov's inequality give
\[
 P\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|
 >\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}
 \right)\le\gamma,
\]
which is exactly the stated generalized-quantile bound; (13) gives its
ACE-class restriction.  The class-relation lemma also proves the
\(L^s(P_X)\) subclass statement and equality at \(s=r\), but supplies no
reverse inclusion for the broad spectral class.  The lower bound (14) is
over all estimators, not specifically the ACE estimator.  Every constant may
deteriorate as the fixed \(\delta\) decreases; no shrinking-separation rate,
adaptive switch, uniform inference result, or local converse follows.
