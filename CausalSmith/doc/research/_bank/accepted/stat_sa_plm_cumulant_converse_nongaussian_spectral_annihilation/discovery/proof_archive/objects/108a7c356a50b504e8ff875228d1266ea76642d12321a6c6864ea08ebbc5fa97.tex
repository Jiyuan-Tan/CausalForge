Write \(\psi=\psi_{\eta}\).  The exact Luxemburg premise gives
\(E_P\exp(\eta^2/\psi^2)\le2\), and centering gives \(E_P\eta=0\).  Markov's inequality yields
\[
 P(|\eta|>u)\le2e^{-u^2/\psi^2},\qquad u\ge0.
\]
Expanding the exponential, using this tail bound to control the moments, and pairing positive and negative terms gives the elementary transform estimate
\[
 |M(z)|\le E_Pe^{|z||\eta|},
 \qquad
 \log |M(z)|\le4\psi^2|z|^2,qquad z\in\mathbb C. \tag{1}
\]
The deliberately nonoptimal constant four is valid for precisely the stated Luxemburg normalization.

Suppose for contradiction that \(M\) has no zero in the closed disk of radius \(R_0\).  Since \(M(0)=1\), an analytic logarithm \(f=\log M\) exists on a neighborhood of that disk and can be normalized by \(f(0)=0\).  Centering gives \(f'(0)=0\).  For \(0<R<R_0\), (1) implies
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R}\operatorname{Re}f(z)\le4\psi^2R^2.
\]
The Carath\'eodory coefficient estimate, obtained directly by applying the Cauchy coefficient formula to the positive-real-part function \(4\psi^2R^2-f\), gives
\[
 |f^{(k)}(0)|
 \le 2k!R^{-k}\,4\psi^2R^2
 =8k!\psi^2R^{2-k}. \tag{2}
\]
Letting \(R\uparrow R_0\), using \(f^{(k)}(0)=\kappa_k(\eta)\), and invoking cumulant separation gives
\[
 \delta\le8k!\psi^2R_0^{2-k}.
\]
For the asserted numerical choice,
\[
 8k!\psi^2R_0^{2-k}
 =\frac{8k!}{2^{k+4}k!}\,\delta
 =2^{-k-1}\delta<\delta,
\]
a contradiction.  Therefore \(M\) has a zero in \(|z|\le R_0\), as claimed.
