We prove the theorem in four steps.  All constants below are uniform over
\(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and may depend only on the fixed class
constants displayed in the theorem.

\emph{Step 1: a population-good contour and a regular empirical event.}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  By
\(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\), there is an index \(j_0\) such that
\[
 N_{C_{j_0}}(M)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_0}}|M(z)|\ge a_{\star}.
\]
The hypothesis \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\) and
\(\text{lem:nuisance-zero-free}\) imply that \(H_n\) is zero-free on
\(\{|z|\le R_1\}\), that \(|H_n|\ge3/4\) there, and that multiplication
by \(H_n\) preserves zeros and their multiplicities.  Hence
\[
 N_{C_{j_0}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_0}}|F_n(z)|\ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4.       \tag{1}
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define the proof intermediates
\[
 \Delta_{F,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
   |\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{G,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
   |\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|
\]
and the event
\[
 \mathcal E_n=
 \{\Delta_{F,0}\le a_{\star}/8,\ 
   \Delta_{F,1}\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
When \(n\ge2\), both half-sample sizes are at least \(n/3\).  Applying
\(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) on both halves and then
Markov's inequality at the fixed threshold \(a_{\star}/8\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}P(\mathcal E_n^c)
 \le \frac{K_0}{n}.                                    \tag{2}
\]

\emph{Step 2: the selector is stable on \(\mathcal E_n\).}
On \(\mathcal E_n\), equation (1) gives
\[
 \inf_{C_{j_0}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8.
\]
For the straight-line homotopy between \(F_n\) and
\(\widehat F_{0,n}\), the boundary modulus on \(C_{j_0}\) is at least
\(3a_{\star}/4-a_{\star}/8>0\).  Therefore the winding number is
constant along this homotopy.  Thus \(j_0\) is pilot-admissible and its
empirical winding number is positive.  The maximizing rule in
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\) consequently selects an index
\(\widehat j_n\) satisfying
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8.                                 \tag{3}
\]
It follows that
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_n|
 \ge\frac{a_{\star}}2.                                  \tag{4}
\]
The homotopy from \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) to \(F_n\) is zero-free on the
selected circle by (3), so
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}
 =N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)\ge1.                        \tag{5}
\]
Equations (4) and the evaluation-half part of \(\mathcal E_n\) also give
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{1,n}|
 \ge\frac{3a_{\star}}8>\frac{a_{\star}}4,              \tag{6}
\]
so the evaluation fallback is not used.  Measurability follows directly
from the finite bank and the deterministic rules in
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\): each boundary infimum may be
taken over a fixed countable dense parametrization, every contour integral
uses a fixed circle parametrization, and all ties and failures are resolved
deterministically.

\emph{Step 3: uniform upper risk.}
The hypotheses of \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) hold on the
selected contour: (4) supplies boundary zero-freeness, the nuisance lemma
supplies zero-freeness of \(H_n\) on its interior, and (5) supplies a
positive zero count.  Write
\[
 N=N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)
   =\widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}.
\]
The class restrictions give a uniform finite constant \(K_G\) such that
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|
 \le E_P[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]
 \le K_G.                                               \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\),
\(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and \(|D_n|\le2C_g\).  Cauchy--Schwarz,
the range restrictions, and the sub-Gaussian bounds on \(\eta\) and
\(\xi\) therefore bound the last expectation by a constant depending
only on \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},R_1\).

For each \(z\) on the selected circle, (4), (6), and (7) imply the
algebraic perturbation bound
\[
 \left|
  \frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}
  -\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}
 \right|
 \le
 \frac{8}{3a_{\star}}\Delta_{G,1}
 +\frac{16K_G}{3a_{\star}^2}\Delta_{F,1}.              \tag{8}
\]
Since \(N\ge1\), every bank radius is at most \(R_1\), and taking real
parts and projecting onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase
distance from the real number \(\theta_0(P)\), the exact contour identity
and (8) yield
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|
 \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}
 \le R_1\left\{
   \frac{8}{3a_{\star}}\Delta_{G,1}
   +\frac{16K_G}{3a_{\star}^2}\Delta_{F,1}
 \right\}.                                             \tag{9}
\]
Using \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) in (9) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P\!\left[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2
      \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}\right]
 \le\frac{K_1}{n}.                                     \tag{10}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n^c\), both the estimator and the target lie in
\([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\), so their squared distance is at most
\(4C_{\theta}^2\).  Combining (2) and (10) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac Cn.                                          \tag{11}
\]
Neither the estimator nor this argument uses \(\bar q_n\) or
\(\varepsilon_{2,n}\).  The finite bank depends only on fixed class
constants and not on the unknown law of \(\eta\).  Hence the displayed
statistic is one data-driven minimax witness, and
\(\text{def:minimax-risks}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]. \tag{12}
\]

\emph{Step 4: the matching lower bound and the quantile comparison.}
Use the submodel in \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\), whose
Gaussian innovation has a fixed variance \(\tau^2>0\).  Choose a fixed
\(h>0\), depending only on the class constants, sufficiently small that
for every \(n\ge2\) the submodel contains two parameters
\(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\) with
\[
 d=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=\frac h{\sqrt n}.
\]
Conditional on \((X,T)\), the two outcome laws are normal with common
variance \(\tau^2\) and means differing by \(d\eta\).  Their
one-observation Hellinger affinity is therefore
\[
 A_1=E_P\!\left[\exp\{-d^2\eta^2/(8\tau^2)\}\right]
 \ge \exp\{-d^2E_P[\eta^2]/(8\tau^2)\},                \tag{13}
\]
where Jensen's inequality gives the last step.  Direct multiplication of
product densities shows that affinity tensorizes.  Since the treatment
sub-Gaussian bound gives \(E_P[\eta^2]\le K_2\psi_{\eta}^2\),
\[
 A_n=A_1^n
 \ge \exp\{-h^2K_2\psi_{\eta}^2/(8\tau^2)\}
 =:a_0>0.                                               \tag{14}
\]
For any two dominated laws with affinity \(A\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied
to \((\sqrt{p_0}-\sqrt{p_1})(\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt{p_1})\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0,P_1)\le\sqrt{1-A^2}.            \tag{15}
\]
Turn an arbitrary estimator into the test that selects the nearer of
\(\vartheta_0\) and \(\vartheta_1\).  On a testing error its squared
estimation error is at least \(d^2/4\).  The elementary two-point testing
identity therefore yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta_u}^{\otimes n}}
 [ (\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_u)^2]
 &\ge \frac{d^2}{8}
 \{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n},
                       P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})\}\\
 &\ge \frac{d^2a_0^2}{16}
 =\frac{h^2a_0^2}{16n},                                 \tag{16}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where (14), (15), and \(1-\sqrt{1-u}\ge u/2\) for
\(u\in[0,1]\) were used.  Both laws are in
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  Taking the infimum over estimators in
(16) proves \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\ge c/n\).

For \(W=|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|\), equation
(11) and Markov's inequality at \(t=\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}\) give
\(P(W>t)\le\gamma\).  Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}(W)\le\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}.               \tag{17}
\]
By \(\text{prop:jms-ace-alignment}\), norm monotonicity and the remaining
common class restrictions give
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}.   \tag{18}
\]
This is only a one-way inclusion: the former class uses the stricter
\(L^s(P_X)\) code balls, while the latter uses \(L^r(P_X)\) balls.

Suppose \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) holds.  Its definition requires
the displayed logarithms to be defined, so in particular
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\).  When \(\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\), the cited leaf
\(\text{lem:jms-ace-quantile-upper}\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
 \le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}.                         \tag{19}
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{2,n}=0\), choose
\(u\in(0,\varepsilon_{1,n})\) and enlarge only the outcome-code radius
from zero to \(u\).  The original class is contained in the enlarged
class, and the maximum defining \(b_{2,n}\) is unchanged because
\(u<\varepsilon_{1,n}\).  Thus the same eligibility condition holds.
Applying the cited positive-radius theorem to the enlarged class gives
the right-hand side
\[
 C_{\gamma}r!16^r\delta^{-1}
 \Big[\varepsilon_{1,n}^r u+C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
 +64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
 \{r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\}
 (\gamma n)^{-1/2}\Big].
\]
Letting \(u\downarrow0\) proves (19) at the boundary.  Restricting its
supremum by (18) proves the claimed ACE inequality over all of
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).

Finally set
\[
 A_n=\varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
     +C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}.
\]
The exact definition of \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\) is a fixed positive
multiple of \(A_n\) plus a fixed multiple of \(n^{-1/2}\).  If
\(A_n\gg n^{-1/2}\), the ratio of the spectral guarantee (17), which is
\(O(n^{-1/2})\), to \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\) tends to zero.  This
proves the asserted strict order improvement and completes the proof.
