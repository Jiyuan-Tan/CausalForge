By \(\text{lem:jms-gaussian-quantile-lower}\), the stated asymptotic hypotheses imply, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}\ge L_{\mathrm G,n,\gamma}.
\]
It remains to convert the loss without changing the experiment.  Let \(W\ge0\) and write
\[
 q=Q_{P,1-\gamma}(W)
 =\inf\{t\ge0:P(W\le t)\ge1-\gamma\}.
\]
For every \(t<q\), \(P(W\le t)<1-\gamma\).  Letting \(t\uparrow q\) and using continuity from below gives
\[
 P(W<q)\le1-\gamma,
 \qquad
 P(W\ge q)\ge\gamma.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 E_P[W^2]\ge q^2P(W\ge q)\ge\gamma q^2.
\]
Apply this inequality to the error of an arbitrary estimator \(\widetilde\theta\).  Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) yields
\[
 \sup_P E_P[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \ge\gamma\left\{
   \sup_PQ_{P,1-\gamma}(|\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P)|)
 \right\}^2.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\widetilde\theta\) and using monotonicity of squaring on \([0,\infty)\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}
 \ge\gamma(\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G})^2
 \ge\gamma L_{\mathrm G,n,\gamma}^2.
\]
The bounded-outcome, known-variance Gaussian class, the common deterministic codes, and the stated generalized-quantile convention remain unchanged throughout.
