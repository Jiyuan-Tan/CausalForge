Fix \(n\ge2\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and write
\[
 m_a=|I_a|,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{F,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{G,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|.
\]
The split sizes satisfy \(m_0\ge n/3\) and \(m_1\ge n/2\).  All constants below are uniform in \(n\) and \(P\).

By \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\), some bank index \(j_{\circ}\) satisfies
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\circ}}}(M)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_{\circ}}}|M(z)|\ge a_{\star}.
\]
The hypothesis \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\) and
\(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) give
\[
 \inf_{|z|\le R_1}|H_n(z)|\ge\frac34,
\]
and show that \(F_n=M H_n\) and \(M\) have the same zeros with multiplicity throughout the bank disk.  Consequently,
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\circ}}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_{\circ}}}|F_n(z)|\ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, all divisions and winding numbers in (1) are licensed by a strictly positive boundary modulus.

Define the regular event
\[
 \mathcal E_n
 =\left\{\Delta_{F,0}\le\frac{a_{\star}}8,
              \ \Delta_{F,1}\le\frac{a_{\star}}8\right\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n\), (1) implies
\[
 \inf_{C_{j_{\circ}}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{C_{j_{\circ}}}|\widehat F_{0,n}-F_n|
 \le\frac{a_{\star}}8
 <\inf_{C_{j_{\circ}}}|F_n|.
\]
For \(t\in[0,1]\), the boundary function
\(F_n+t(\widehat F_{0,n}-F_n)\) is nonzero on
\(C_{j_{\circ}}\).  Its argument-principle integral is continuous in \(t\) and integer-valued, hence constant.  Thus \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) and \(F_n\) have equal zero counts inside \(C_{j_{\circ}}\), and the argument principle shows
\[
 \widehat N_{0j_{\circ},n}=N_{C_{j_{\circ}}}(F_n)\ge1.
\]
Thus \(j_{\circ}\) is pilot-admissible.  By the maximization rule in
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\), the selected index \(\widehat j_n\) satisfies
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_n|
 \ge\frac{a_{\star}}2. \tag{2}
\]
The second inequality and \(\Delta_{F,0}\le a_{\star}/8\) permit the same nonvanishing-boundary homotopy, which yields
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}
 =N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)\ge1. \tag{3}
\]
Likewise, (2) and the evaluation-half bound on \(\mathcal E_n\) give
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{1,n}|
 \ge\frac{3a_{\star}}8>\frac{a_{\star}}4. \tag{4}
\]
Hence the estimator does not use its fallback on \(\mathcal E_n\).

The population transform \(G_n\) also has a class-uniform boundary envelope.  Indeed, the class definition gives
\(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and \(|D_n|\le2C_g\).  The range and sub-Gaussian conditions therefore imply uniform bounds on \(E_P[Y^2]\) and on \(E_P[e^{2R_1|\eta|}]\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives a finite constant \(K_G\), depending only on the displayed class constants and \(R_1\), such that
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|
 \le E_P[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]
 \le e^{2C_gR_1}\{E_P[Y^2]E_P[e^{2R_1|\eta|}]\}^{1/2}
 \le K_G. \tag{5}
\]
This also verifies the integrability required by every contour integral below.

Put \(j=\widehat j_n\) and \(N=N_{C_j}(F_n)\) on \(\mathcal E_n\).  By (2), (3), the zero-freeness of \(H_n\) on the enclosed disk, and
\(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\),
\[
 \theta_0
 =\frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz. \tag{6}
\]
For complex numbers with the denominators in (2) and (4), the elementary quotient identity gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}}{\widehat F_{1,n}}-
             \frac{G_n}{F_n}\right|
 \le
 \frac{|\widehat G_{1,n}-G_n|}{|\widehat F_{1,n}|}
 +\frac{|G_n|\,|\widehat F_{1,n}-F_n|}
       {|\widehat F_{1,n}|\,|F_n|}.
\]
Using (2), (4), (5), \(N\ge1\), and the fact that the length of \(C_j\) is at most \(2\pi R_1\), equations (3)--(6) imply
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0|
 \le R_1\left\{
       \frac{8}{3a_{\star}}\Delta_{G,1}
       +\frac{16K_G}{3a_{\star}^2}\Delta_{F,1}
     \right\}
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal E_n. \tag{7}
\]
Here projection onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) is a contraction because
\(\theta_0\) lies in that interval, and taking the real part cannot increase the complex modulus.

By \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\), (7) yields
\[
 E_P\!\left[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0)^2
             \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}\right]
 \le \frac{K_1}{m_1}. \tag{8}
\]
The same lemma and Markov's inequality give
\[
 P(\mathcal E_n^c)
 \le\frac{64}{a_{\star}^2}
     \{E_P[\Delta_{F,0}^2]+E_P[\Delta_{F,1}^2]\}
 \le K_2\left(\frac1{m_0}+\frac1{m_1}\right). \tag{9}
\]
Both the target and the estimator lie in \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\), so their squared distance is at most \(4C_{\theta}^2\) on the complement.  Combining (8), (9), \(m_0\ge n/3\), and \(m_1\ge n/2\) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac Cn. \tag{10}
\]
The quantities \(R_1\), \(a_{\star}\), and the empirical-transform constants depend only on
\(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\); hence so does \(C\).  The bank is fixed once for the entire class, rather than for the unknown law.  After that non-effective once-for-all choice, the statistic is the measurable sample map in
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\); no effective procedure for constructing the bank is being asserted.

We next prove the converse.  By \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\), nonemptiness of the class supplies a submodel
\(\{P_{n,\vartheta}:|\vartheta|\le c_0\}\) with
\(\theta_0(P_{n,\vartheta})=\vartheta\), common deterministic codes, and constants
\(c_0,C_0>0\) depending only on the class constants.  Let
\[
 h=\min\left\{\frac{c_0}{2},\frac{1}{\sqrt{4C_0+1}}\right\}>0,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_0=0,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_1=\frac h{\sqrt n}.
\]
Both points belong to the submodel, and the lemma gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n}\Vert
                  P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})
 \le C_0h^2<\frac14.
\]
For completeness, the preceding KL bound implies the required total-variation bound directly.  Let \(p\) and \(q\) be densities of the two product laws with respect to their sum, and put
\[
 A=\int\sqrt{pq}.
\]
Jensen's inequality for \(-\log\), followed by \(-\log x\ge1-x\), gives
\[
 2(1-A)\le-2\log A
 \le D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n}\Vert
                     P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n}).
\]
Since
\[
 \int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2=2(1-A),
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n},
                    P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})
 &=\frac12\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\\
 &\le\left\{2(1-A)\right\}^{1/2}
 <\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\]
For any estimator \(\widetilde\theta\), classify the observation according to which of \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\) is closer to \(\widetilde\theta\).  On a classification error, the squared estimation error is at least
\(h^2/(4n)\).  The sum of the two testing errors is at least one minus total variation.  It follows that
\[
 \max_{v\in\{0,1\}}
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}^{\otimes n}}
 [ (\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 \ge\frac{h^2}{8n}
       \{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n},
                              P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})\}
 \ge\frac{h^2}{16n}. \tag{11}
\]
Taking the infimum over estimators and using that the two-point experiment is contained in
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) proves the lower bound with \(c=h^2/16\).  The middle inequality in the theorem follows directly from
\(\text{def:minimax-risks}\), since \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) is one admissible statistic.

For the generalized-quantile conclusion, (10) and Markov's inequality give, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 P\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|
 >\sqrt{\frac{C}{\gamma n}}
 \right)
 \le\gamma.
\]
By the definition of \(Q_{P,1-\gamma}\), this is exactly
\[
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 (|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|)
 \le\sqrt{\frac{C}{\gamma n}}.
\]
Finally, the estimator definition uses \(\bar g_n\) and the empirical transforms of \((Y,Z_n)\), but never \(\bar q_n\); the non-Gaussian class likewise contains no outcome-code restriction.  The fixed bank depends on the class constants, not on the individual law of \(\eta\).  The ACE comparison is restricted by definition to
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\), so no inclusion of the broad class in the published ACE class has been used or asserted.
