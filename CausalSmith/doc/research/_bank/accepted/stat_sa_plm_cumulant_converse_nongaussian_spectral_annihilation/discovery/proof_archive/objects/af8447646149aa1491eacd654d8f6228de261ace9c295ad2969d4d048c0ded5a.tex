For the ACE conclusion, fix \(n\).  Since \(\delta_n>0\), apply \(\text{lem:jms-ace-theorem-five-four}\) with the separation constant in that result set equal to \(\delta_n\).  By construction, \(\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)\) changes no other member property of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\).  Under this substitution the quantity called \(b_{2,n}\) in \(\text{def:jms-eligibility}\) becomes exactly
\[
 \widetilde b_{2,n}=200\min\{1,C_\theta\}\delta_n
 \max\!\left\{\varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n},
   (\gamma n)^{-1/2}(\psi_\xi+C_\theta\psi_\eta)\right\}^{-1}.
\]
Thus the displayed hypothesis is precisely condition (21) after this substitution.  The same substitution in the cited bound gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)}Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)\right|\right)\\
 &\quad\le C_\gamma r!16^r\delta_n^{-1}
 \Bigl[\varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
 +C_\theta\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
 +64(C_g+\psi_\eta)^r
 \{r^2(C_g+\psi_\eta)+\psi_\xi+C_\theta\psi_\eta\}
 (\gamma n)^{-1/2}\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]
The strict positivity of \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\), together with the stated domain conditions for the logarithms and divisions, supplies every boundary condition needed for this application.  Taking the numerical minimum of this deterministic guarantee and a separately established DML guarantee produces only an oracle envelope: no statistic implementing a switch has been defined, and the hypotheses of the DML guarantee have not been asserted here.  Hence this comparison implies neither a minimax rate nor an adaptive selector.

We now prove the Gaussian--Rademacher path assertions.  Independence of \(G_\circ\) and \(S\) gives, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_a(z)
 &=E[e^{z\sqrt{1-a^2}G_\circ}]E[e^{zaS}]\\
 &=\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az).
\end{aligned}
\]
Cumulants add across independent summands and are homogeneous under scaling.  The Gaussian fourth cumulant is zero, while
\[
 \kappa_4(S)=E[S^4]-3E[S^2]^2=1-3=-2.
\]
Therefore \(\kappa_4(\eta_a)=-2a^4\).  The characteristic function is
\[
 \phi_a(t)=M_a(it)
 =\exp\!\left\{-\frac{1-a^2}{2}t^2\right\}\cos(at).
\]
Its exponential factor is strictly positive on \(\mathbb R\), so its first positive zero is \(t_a=\pi/(2a)\).  Moreover, for real \(u\),
\[
 E[e^{u\eta_a}]
 =e^{(1-a^2)u^2/2}\cosh(au)
 \le e^{(1-a^2)u^2/2}e^{a^2u^2/2}=e^{u^2/2},
\]
where \(\cosh x\le e^{x^2/2}\).  This uniform sub-Gaussian bound justifies all differentiations below and gives \(E[\eta_a^2]=1\).

The law of \(\eta_a\) is symmetric.  Hence, for every real \(d\),
\[
 E[e^{it_a(\eta_a+d)}]=e^{it_ad}\phi_a(t_a)=0,
\]
and taking imaginary parts proves the claimed exact sine annihilation.  Differentiating \(\phi_a\) at \(t_a\) yields
\[
 E[\eta_a\sin(t_a\eta_a)]
 =-\phi_a'(t_a)
 =a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\}=A_a.
\]
Because \(D_n\) is measurable with respect to \(X\) and \(\eta_a\perp X\), conditioning on \(D_n=d\) and expanding the sine of a sum gives
\[
 E_P[(\eta_a+D_n)\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+D_n)\}\mid D_n]
 =A_a\cos(t_aD_n).
\]
Indeed, the other terms vanish because \(E[\eta_a\cos(t_a\eta_a)]=0\), \(E[\sin(t_a\eta_a)]=0\), and \(E[\cos(t_a\eta_a)]=\phi_a(t_a)=0\).  Taking expectations and using \(Z_n=\eta_a+D_n\) proves the relevance identity.

The clipping definition of \(\bar g_n\) and \(\lVert g_0\rVert_\infty\le C_g\) imply \(\lvert D_n\rvert\le2C_g\).  Since \(P_X\) is a probability law and \(s\ge r+1\ge1\), norm monotonicity and the treatment-code radius give
\[
 E_P\lvert D_n\rvert=\lVert D_n\rVert_{L^1(P_X)}
 \le\lVert D_n\rVert_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
The elementary inequality \(1-\cos x\le\lvert x\rvert\) now gives
\[
 E_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
 \ge1-t_aE_P\lvert D_n\rvert\ge\frac12,
\]
which proves the positive denominator bound and records the boundary condition required for the ratio.

To identify the ratio's target, use \(Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi\).  Conditional sine annihilation and boundedness of \(b_n\) give
\[
 E_P[b_n(X)\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P\!\left[b_n(X)E_P\{\sin(t_aZ_n)\mid X\}\right]=0.
\]
Since \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) is measurable with respect to \((X,T)\), outcome mean independence gives
\[
 E_P[\xi\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P\!\left[E_P(\xi\mid X,T)\sin(t_aZ_n)\right]=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 E_P[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =\theta_0E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)].
\]
Thus the computable population ratio is distinct from, but equals on this path class, the causal coefficient \(\theta_0\).

It remains to establish the uniform risk bound.  Define
\[
 V=Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n),\qquad
 W=(Y-\theta_0Z_n)\sin(t_aZ_n),\qquad
 \mu_a=E_P[V].
\]
The preceding identities imply \(E_P[W]=0\) and \(\mu_a\ge A_a/2>0\).  Since \(E[\eta_a^2]=1\) and \(\lvert D_n\rvert\le2C_g\),
\[
 E_P[V^2]\le E_P[Z_n^2]
 \le2E[\eta_a^2]+2E_P[D_n^2]\le2+8C_g^2=:K_V.
\]
Also \(b_n=q_0-\theta_0D_n\), so \(\lvert b_n\rvert\le C_q+2C_\theta C_g\).  The conditional sub-Gaussian norm bound implies \(E_P[\xi^2]\le c_2\psi_\xi^2\), where \(c_2<\infty\) is the universal constant fixed by the convention for \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\psi_2}\).  Therefore
\[
 E_P[W^2]
 \le E_P[(b_n+\xi)^2]
 \le2(C_q+2C_\theta C_g)^2+2c_2\psi_\xi^2=:K_W.
\]

On the event \(\mathbb P_nV\ge A_a/4\), projection onto \([-C_\theta,C_\theta]\) is a contraction at \(\theta_0\), and the sample identity \(\mathbb P_n[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]=\theta_0\mathbb P_nV+\mathbb P_nW\) gives
\[
 \lvert\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0\rvert
 \le\frac{4}{A_a}\lvert\mathbb P_nW\rvert.
\]
By independent sampling and \(E_P[W]=0\),
\[
 E_P[(\mathbb P_nW)^2]=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(W)}{n}
 \le\frac{K_W}{n}.
\]
On the complementary event, \(\mu_a\ge A_a/2\) implies \(\lvert\mathbb P_nV-\mu_a\rvert\ge A_a/4\).  Chebyshev's inequality and \(\operatorname{Var}_P(V)\le K_V\) give
\[
 P(\mathbb P_nV<A_a/4)\le\frac{16K_V}{nA_a^2}.
\]
Both the estimator and target belong to \([-C_\theta,C_\theta]\), so their squared distance is at most \(4C_\theta^2\).  Splitting over the two events yields
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \le\frac{16K_W+64C_\theta^2K_V}{nA_a^2}.
\]
Clipping separately gives the deterministic upper bound \(4C_\theta^2\).  Taking the smaller bound and using
\[
 A_a^{-2}=a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\}
\]
proves the first risk display with a constant depending only on \(C_\theta,C_g,C_q,\psi_\xi\), uniformly over the displayed path laws.

Finally, \(\Delta_a=2a^4\) implies
\[
 a=\left(\frac{\Delta_a}{2}\right)^{1/4},\qquad
 a^{-2}=\sqrt2\,\Delta_a^{-1/2},\qquad
 \frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}
 \le\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}.
\]
Also
\[
 t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac12
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac{a}{\pi}
 =\frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi}.
\]
Absorbing the factor \(\sqrt2\) into \(C\) proves the cumulant-parametrized risk bound.  This proof uses the full Gaussian--Rademacher transform, not merely a fourth-cumulant lower bound; it therefore supplies neither a converse nor a full-class elbow, adaptive selector, or coverage statement.
