Independence of \(G_{\circ}\) and \(S\) gives
\[
 M_a(z)
 =E[e^{z\sqrt{1-a^2}G_{\circ}}]E[e^{zaS}]
 =\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az).
\]
Since the fourth derivative at zero of \(\log\cosh(az)\) is \(-2a^4\), the cumulant definition gives \(\kappa_4(\eta_a)=-2a^4\).  Its characteristic function is
\[
 \varphi_a(t)=M_a(it)
 =\exp\!\left(-\frac{1-a^2}{2}t^2\right)\cos(at).
\]
The exponential factor is positive, so the first positive zero is \(t_a=\pi/(2a)\), and for every real \(d\),
\[
 E[\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+d)\}]
 =\operatorname{Im}\{e^{it_ad}\varphi_a(t_a)\}=0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \varphi_a'(t_a)
 =-a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\}=-A_a.
\]
Differentiation under the expectation is justified because \(\eta_a\) has moments of every order.  Differentiating \(e^{itd}\varphi_a(t)\) and taking real parts at \(t_a\) yields
\[
 E[(\eta_a+d)\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+d)\}]=A_a\cos(t_ad).
\]
Since \(D_n\) is measurable in \(X\) and \(\eta_a\perp X\), conditioning on \(D_n\) gives
\[
 m_a:=E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]=A_aE_P[\cos(t_aD_n)].
\]
The direct \(L^1(P_X)\) premise is exactly \(E_P|D_n|\le\varepsilon_{1,n}\).  Hence \(1-\cos u\le|u|\) and \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le1/2\) imply
\[
 E_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
 \ge1-t_aE_P|D_n|
 \ge\frac12,
\]
so \(m_a\ge A_a/2>0\).  This is the required overlap condition.

Put
\[
 W=Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n),\qquad
 V=Y\sin(t_aZ_n),\qquad
 R=(Y-\theta_0Z_n)\sin(t_aZ_n).
\]
The residual identity is \(Y-\theta_0Z_n=b_n(X)+\xi\).  Conditional shift annihilation gives
\[
 E_P[b_n(X)\sin(t_aZ_n)]=0,
\]
and outcome mean independence gives
\[
 E_P[\xi\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P[\sin(t_aZ_n)E_P(\xi\mid X,T)]=0.
\]
Therefore \(E_P[R]=0\) and \(E_P[V]=\theta_0m_a\), establishing the population identifying moment separately from the sample ratio.

Clipping and the range condition give \(|D_n|\le2C_g\).  Since \(E[\eta_a^2]=1\) and \(\eta_a\perp D_n\),
\[
 E_P[W^2]\le E_P[Z_n^2]
 =1+E_P[D_n^2]\le1+4C_g^2=:K_W.
\]
Also \(|b_n(X)|\le C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g\).  The conditional sub-Gaussian premise implies \(E_P[\xi^2]\le c_{\psi}\psi_{\xi}^2\) for a universal convention-dependent \(c_{\psi}<\infty\).  Thus
\[
 E_P[R^2]
 \le2(C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g)^2+2c_{\psi}\psi_{\xi}^2=:K_R.
\]
Both constants are uniform in \(a,n,P\).

Write \(\overline W=\mathbb P_nW\), \(\overline V=\mathbb P_nV\), and \(\mathcal E=\{\overline W\ge A_a/4\}\).  Projection onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) is a contraction and \(\theta_0\) belongs to that interval.  On \(\mathcal E\),
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0|
 \le\frac{|\mathbb P_nR|}{\overline W}
 \le\frac4{A_a}|\mathbb P_nR|.
\]
Independence and \(E_P[R]=0\) yield
\[
 E_P[(\mathbb P_nR)^2]\le K_R/n.
\]
On \(\mathcal E^c\), the squared error is at most \(C_{\theta}^2\), while Chebyshev's inequality and \(m_a\ge A_a/2\) give
\[
 P(\mathcal E^c)
 \le P(|\overline W-m_a|\ge A_a/4)
 \le\frac{16K_W}{nA_a^2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \le\frac{16(K_R+C_{\theta}^2K_W)}{nA_a^2}.
\]
The independent deterministic bound \(|\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0|^2\le4C_{\theta}^2\), together with
\[
 A_a^{-2}=a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\},
\]
proves the first risk display.  Finally \(\Delta_a=2a^4\) gives
\[
 a=2^{-1/4}\Delta_a^{1/4},
 \qquad
 t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac12
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi},
\]
and
\[
 a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\}
 =\sqrt2e^{-\pi^2/4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left\{\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}\right\}.
\]
Absorbing the fixed prefactor into \(C\) completes the proof.  No supplied outcome code is used.
