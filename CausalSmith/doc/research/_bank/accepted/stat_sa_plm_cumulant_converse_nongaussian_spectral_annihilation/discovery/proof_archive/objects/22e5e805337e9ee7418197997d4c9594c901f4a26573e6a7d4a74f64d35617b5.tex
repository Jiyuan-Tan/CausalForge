On \(C_j\), the factorization lemma and the assumed absence of zeros give
\[
 \frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}
 =\theta_0\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}+\frac{B_n(z)}{H_n(z)}.
\]
The functions \(B_n\) and \(H_n\) are entire.  Since \(H_n\) is zero-free on and inside \(C_j\), the quotient \(B_n/H_n\) is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk bounded by \(C_j\).  The Cauchy integral identity therefore gives
\[
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{B_n(z)}{H_n(z)}\,dz=0.
\]
By the definition of \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\),
\[
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz=N_{C_j}(F_n).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
 =\theta_0N_{C_j}(F_n).
\]
The assumed positivity \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\ge1\) permits division.  The result is exactly
\[
 \theta_0
 =\{N_{C_j}(F_n)2\pi i\}^{-1}
   \oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
 =\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n).
\]
Thus the causal coefficient is distinct from, but under the displayed conditions equal to, the computable observed-transform contour functional.
