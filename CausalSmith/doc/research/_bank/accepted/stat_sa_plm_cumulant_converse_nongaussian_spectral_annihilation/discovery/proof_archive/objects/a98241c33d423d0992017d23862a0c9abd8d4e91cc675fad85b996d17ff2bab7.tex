Write \(m_a=|I_a|\).  For \(n\ge2\), both half-samples are nonempty and \(m_a\ge n/3\).  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and suppose \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\).

By \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\), there is an index \(j_{\star}=j_{\star}(P)\) such that
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(M)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_{\star}}}|M(z)|\ge a_{\star}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\), \(H_n\) is zero-free on the disk \(|z|\le R_1\), \(|H_n(z)|\ge3/4\) there, and \(F_n=M H_n\) has the same zeros as \(M\), with multiplicity.  Consequently,
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_{\star}}}|F_n(z)|\ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4. \tag{1}
\]

For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), put
\[
 d_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \qquad
 e_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|,
\]
and define the regular events \(\mathcal A_a=\{d_a\le a_{\star}/8\}\).  On \(\mathcal A_0\), (1) gives
\[
 \inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8.
\]
Rouch\'e's theorem applies because \(d_0\le a_{\star}/8<3a_{\star}/4\le\inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|F_n|\).  Thus
\[
 \widehat N_{0j_{\star},n}=N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\ge1,
\]
so \(j_{\star}\) is pilot-admissible.  The maximizing rule in \(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\) therefore selects an index \(\widehat j_n\) satisfying
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_n|
 \ge\frac{a_{\star}}2. \tag{2}
\]
Another application of Rouch\'e's theorem, now using \(d_0\le a_{\star}/8<a_{\star}/2\), gives
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)\ge1. \tag{3}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), (2) also yields
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{1,n}|
 \ge\frac{3a_{\star}}8>\frac{a_{\star}}4, \tag{4}
\]
so the evaluation fallback is not used.

The selector is measurable.  Indeed, under the fixed circle parameterization, each empirical transform and its derivative are jointly measurable in the sample and continuous in the circle parameter.  Hence each boundary infimum is the infimum over a fixed countable dense set of angles and is measurable.  On the measurable zero-free event, the winding integral is a measurable integral of a jointly measurable continuous integrand.  The bank is finite, and the smallest-index tie and failure rules are deterministic.  Thus \(\widehat j_n\) and \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) are sample-measurable once the non-effective deterministic bank has been fixed.

There is a class-uniform constant \(C_G<\infty\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|\le C_G. \tag{5}
\]
To see this, use \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and Cauchy--Schwarz to obtain
\[
 |G_n(z)|
 \le E_P[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]
 \le e^{2C_gR_1}\lVert Y\rVert_{L^2(P)}
       \{E_P[e^{2R_1|\eta|}]\}^{1/2}.
\]
The range bounds, \(|\theta_0|\le C_{\theta}\), and the two sub-Gaussian assumptions bound both factors uniformly, proving (5).

On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), let \(C=C_{\widehat j_n}\) and let the common positive integer in (3) be \(N\).  Since \(H_n\) is zero-free on and inside \(C\), \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) gives
\[
 \theta_0
 =\frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz. \tag{6}
\]
Equations (2), (4), (5), \(N\ge1\), and the fact that the circle length is at most \(2\pi R_1\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left|
 \frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C
 \frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}\,dz
 -\theta_0
 \right|\\
 &\quad\le R_1\left\{
 \frac{e_1}{3a_{\star}/8}
 +\frac{C_Gd_1}{(3a_{\star}/8)(a_{\star}/2)}
 \right}
 \le L_{\star}(d_1+e_1), \tag{7}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where \(L_{\star}<\infty\) depends only on the fixed class constants and the fixed bank.  Projection onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) after taking the real part cannot increase distance from the real number \(\theta_0\), so (7) also bounds \(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0|\).

By \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) and \(m_a\ge n/3\), for a uniform \(K_1<\infty\),
\[
 E_P[d_a^2+e_a^2]\le\frac{K_1}{n}. \tag{8}
\]
Markov's inequality and (8) give
\[
 P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)
 \le\frac{K_2}{n}. \tag{9}
\]
On the complement of the regular event, clipping gives the deterministic loss bound \((\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0)^2\le4C_{\theta}^2\).  Combining (7)--(9),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0)^2]
 &\le 2L_{\star}^2E_P[d_1^2+e_1^2]
   +4C_{\theta}^2P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)\\
 &\le\frac{C}{n}. \tag{10}
 \end{aligned}
\]
All constants are uniform in \(P\) and \(n\).  The bank constants depend only on \((r,\delta,\psi_{\eta})\), while the other bounds use only the class constants displayed in the theorem.  Taking the supremum proves the upper risk bound.  Since the minimax infimum ranges over this estimator as one permissible sample map,
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]. \tag{11}
\]

For the lower bound, invoke \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\), and let \(c_0,C_0\) be its constants.  Choose a fixed
\[
 0<h\le\min\{c_0,1,(8C_0)^{-1/2}\},
 \qquad
 \vartheta_-=-\frac{h}{2\sqrt n},
 \qquad
 \vartheta_+=\frac{h}{2\sqrt n}.
\]
Both parameter values lie in the submodel and have separation \(h/\sqrt n\).  The lemma gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{n,\vartheta_-}^{\otimes n}
 \|P_{n,\vartheta_+}^{\otimes n})\le\frac18. \tag{12}
\]
For completeness, (12) implies total variation at most \(1/4\).  Indeed, for \(A=\{dP_-/d\mu\ge dP_+/d\mu\}\), binary partitioning and nonnegativity of conditional relative entropy give
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_-\|P_+)
 \ge u\log\frac uv+(1-u)\log\frac{1-u}{1-v},
\]
where \(u=P_-(A)\), \(v=P_+(A)\), and \(u-v=\lVert P_--P_+\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\).  As a function of \(u\), the right-hand side has value and first derivative zero at \(u=v\), while its second derivative is \(1/\{u(1-u)\}\ge4\).  Therefore it is at least \(2(u-v)^2\), proving
\[
 \lVert P_--P_+\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_-\|P_+)/2}\le\frac14. \tag{13}
\]

For any estimator \(\widetilde\theta\), use the test \(A_{\widetilde\theta}=\{\widetilde\theta\ge0\}\).  On this event under \(P_-\), or on its complement under \(P_+\), the estimation error is at least \(h/(2\sqrt n)\).  Hence (13) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &E_{P_-}[(\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_-)^2]
 +E_{P_+}[(\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_+)^2]\\
 &\quad\ge\frac{h^2}{4n}
 \{P_-(A_{\widetilde\theta})+P_+(A_{\widetilde\theta}^c)\}\\
 &\quad\ge\frac{h^2}{4n}
 \{1-\lVert P_--P_+\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}
 \ge\frac{3h^2}{16n}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the larger of the two risks is at least \(3h^2/(32n)\).  Taking the supremum over the class and then the infimum over estimators proves the lower bound with \(c=3h^2/32>0\).

The construction in \(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\) uses \(Y\), \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\), and the once-for-all bank, but not \(\bar q_n\), \(\widehat q_n\), or the law of \(\eta\).  Finally, (10) and Markov's inequality imply
\[
 P\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|
 >\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}
 \right)\le\gamma.
\]
By the definition of \(Q_{P,1-\gamma}\), this is exactly the asserted generalized-quantile bound.  The class-inclusion disclaimer follows directly from the separate definitions of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\).
