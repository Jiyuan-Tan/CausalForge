Write \(m_a=|I_a|\) and let \(\mathbb P_a\) denote the empirical average on that half-sample.  Uniformly on \(|z|\le R_1\), the exponential series and the class moment bounds permit termwise subtraction:
\[
 \widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)
 =\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^m}{m!}(\mathbb P_a-P)Z_n^m,
\]
and
\[
 \widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)
 =\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^m}{m!}(\mathbb P_a-P)(YZ_n^m).
\]
For every square-integrable \(W\), independence within the half-sample gives
\[
 \left\|(\mathbb P_a-P)W\right\|_{L^2(P^{\otimes m_a})}
 \le m_a^{-1/2}\|W\|_{L^2(P)}.
\]
The sub-Gaussian moment inequality, \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), and \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\) imply, for a class-uniform \(C<\infty\),
\[
 \|Z_n\|_{L^p(P)}\le C\sqrt p,
 \qquad p\ge2.
\]
Also \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\).  The boundedness of \(q_0\) and \(\theta_0\), together with the unconditional consequence of the conditional \(\psi_2\) bound on \(\xi\), gives \(\|Y\|_{L^4(P)}\le C\).  Hence
\[
 \|Z_n^m\|_{L^2(P)}\le(C\sqrt m)^m
\]
and, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \|YZ_n^m\|_{L^2(P)}
 \le\|Y\|_{L^4(P)}\|Z_n^m\|_{L^4(P)}
 \le C(C\sqrt m)^m.
\]
Minkowski's inequality now yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left\|\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|\right\|_2
 &\le m_a^{-1/2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
   \frac{R_1^m(C\sqrt m)^m}{m!},\\
 \left\|\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|\right\|_2
 &\le Cm_a^{-1/2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
   \frac{R_1^m(C\sqrt m)^m}{m!}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The series is finite by the ratio test, or directly from \(m!\ge(m/e)^m\).  Squaring both inequalities and enlarging the class-uniform constant proves the claim.  This argument also justifies all earlier termwise operations and introduces no entropy or logarithmic loss.
