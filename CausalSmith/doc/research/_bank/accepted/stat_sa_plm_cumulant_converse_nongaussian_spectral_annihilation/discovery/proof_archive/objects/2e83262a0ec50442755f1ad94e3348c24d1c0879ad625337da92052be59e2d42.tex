Fix \(n\).  We prove the two conclusions separately.

For the first conclusion, \(\delta_n>0\) by the definition of the sequence \((\delta_n)_{n\geq 1}\).  Apply \(\text{lem:jms-ace-theorem-five-four}\) with its positive separation constant instantiated as \(\delta_n\).  By the definition in the theorem statement, \(\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)\) retains every member condition of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) except that the cumulant restriction is \(\lvert\kappa_{r+1}(\eta)\rvert\geq\delta_n\).  Under the same substitution, the quantity denoted by \(b_{2,n}\) in \(\text{def:jms-eligibility}\) is
\[
 \widetilde b_{2,n}
 =200\min\{1,C_{\theta}\}\delta_n
 \max\!\left\{
   \varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n},
   (\gamma n)^{-1/2}(\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta})
 \right\}^{-1}.
\]
Consequently, the displayed local eligibility condition is exactly the eligibility condition of \(\text{def:jms-eligibility}\) after this substitution.  The hypotheses \(\varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\) and the stated requirement that all logarithms and denominators be defined supply the positivity and boundary conditions of the cited theorem.  Its conclusion is therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)\right|\right)\\
 &\quad\leq C_{\gamma}r!16^r\delta_n^{-1}
 \Bigl[
   \varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
   +C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}\\
 &\hspace{42mm}
   +64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
    \{r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\}
    (\gamma n)^{-1/2}
 \Bigr],
\end{aligned}
\]
which is the claimed local ACE benchmark.  Taking the numerical minimum of this deterministic bound and a separately established DML bound produces an oracle envelope.  No statistic that selects between the two procedures has been defined, and no DML hypotheses have been imposed here.  Hence this numerical comparison establishes neither a minimax rate nor a data-driven selector.

We turn to the Gaussian--Rademacher path.  Let \(0<a\leq1\).  Independence of \(G_{\circ}\) and \(S\), together with their respective moment generating functions, gives for every \(z\in\mathbb C\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_a(z)
 &=E\!\left[e^{z\sqrt{1-a^2}G_{\circ}}\right]
   E\!\left[e^{zaS}\right]\\
 &=\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az).
\end{aligned}
\]
The cumulant generating function is
\[
 \log M_a(z)=\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2+\log\cosh(az).
\]
The fourth derivative at zero of the first term is zero, while the fourth derivative at zero of \(\log\cosh(az)\) is \(-2a^4\).  Thus
\[
 \kappa_4(\eta_a)=-2a^4.
\]
The characteristic function is
\[
 \phi_a(t)=M_a(it)
 =\exp\!\left(-\frac{1-a^2}{2}t^2\right)\cos(at).
\]
Its exponential factor is strictly positive for real \(t\), and therefore its first positive zero is
\[
 t_a=\frac{\pi}{2a}.
\]
The random variable \(\eta_a\) is symmetric.  Hence, for every real \(d\),
\[
 E\!\left[e^{it_a(\eta_a+d)}\right]
 =e^{it_ad}\phi_a(t_a)=0,
\]
and taking imaginary parts proves
\[
 E\!\left[\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+d)\}\right]=0.
\]

Differentiating the explicit characteristic function at \(t_a\) is justified by the Gaussian--Rademacher construction and yields
\[
 E[\eta_a\sin(t_a\eta_a)]
 =-\phi_a'(t_a)
 =a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\}
 =A_a.
\]
Symmetry and \(\phi_a(t_a)=0\) also give
\[
 E[\eta_a\cos(t_a\eta_a)]=0,
 \qquad E[\sin(t_a\eta_a)]=0,
 \qquad E[\cos(t_a\eta_a)]=0.
\]
Because \(D_n\) is measurable with respect to \(X\) and \(\eta_a\perp X\) by \(\text{ass:independent-treatment-noise}\), conditioning on \(D_n=d\) and expanding the sine of a sum gives
\[
 E_P\!\left[(\eta_a+d)\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+d)\}\mid D_n=d\right]
 =A_a\cos(t_ad).
\]
Since \(Z_n=\eta_a+D_n\), integration over \(D_n\) proves the exact relevance identity
\[
 E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =A_aE_P[\cos(t_aD_n)].
\]

By \(\text{ass:g-range}\), both \(g_0(X)\) and the clipped code \(\bar g_n(X)\) take values in \([-C_g,C_g]\), so \(\lvert D_n\rvert\leq2C_g\) almost surely.  Since \(P_X\) is a probability law and \(s\geq r+1\geq1\), monotonicity of probability-space \(L^p\) norms and \(\text{ass:treatment-code-radius}\) imply
\[
 E_P\lvert D_n\rvert
 =\lVert D_n\rVert_{L^1(P_X)}
 \leq\lVert D_n\rVert_{L^s(P_X)}
 \leq\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
The elementary inequality \(1-\cos x\leq\lvert x\rvert\) now gives
\[
 E_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
 \geq1-t_aE_P\lvert D_n\rvert
 \geq\frac12
\]
whenever \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\leq1/2\).  Therefore
\[
 E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]\geq\frac{A_a}{2}>0,
\]
which proves the asserted positivity and overlap condition for the population denominator.

We next verify that the observable ratio targets the causal coefficient rather than defining it.  From the residual decomposition,
\[
 Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi,
 \qquad b_n(X)=q_0(X)-\theta_0D_n.
\]
Conditional sine annihilation and \(\eta_a\perp X\) imply
\[
 E_P[b_n(X)\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P\!\left[b_n(X)E_P\{\sin(t_aZ_n)\mid X\}\right]=0.
\]
Moreover, \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) is measurable with respect to \((X,T)\), so \(\text{ass:outcome-mean-independence}\) gives
\[
 E_P[\xi\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P\!\left[E_P(\xi\mid X,T)\sin(t_aZ_n)\right]=0.
\]
It follows that
\[
 E_P[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =\theta_0(P)E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)].
\]
Thus the displayed population ratio is a computable identifying functional and equals the distinct causal target \(\theta_0(P)\) on the stated path class.

It remains to bound the risk of the explicit empirical ratio.  Put
\[
 V=Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n),
 \qquad
 W=(Y-\theta_0Z_n)\sin(t_aZ_n),
 \qquad
 \mu_a=E_P[V].
\]
The preceding identities show that \(E_P[W]=0\) and \(\mu_a\geq A_a/2\).  Directly from the construction,
\[
 E[\eta_a^2]=(1-a^2)E[G_{\circ}^2]+a^2E[S^2]=1.
\]
Since \(\lvert\sin u\rvert\leq1\) and \(\lvert D_n\rvert\leq2C_g\),
\[
 E_P[V^2]
 \leq E_P[Z_n^2]
 \leq2E[\eta_a^2]+2E_P[D_n^2]
 \leq2+8C_g^2=:K_V.
\]
The bounds \(\lVert q_0\rVert_\infty\leq C_q\), \(\lvert\theta_0\rvert\leq C_\theta\), and \(\lvert D_n\rvert\leq2C_g\) imply
\[
 \lvert b_n(X)\rvert\leq C_q+2C_\theta C_g.
\]
The conditional sub-Gaussian bound \(\text{ass:xi-subgaussian}\) implies
\[
 E_P[\xi^2]\leq c_2\psi_\xi^2
\]
for the universal constant \(c_2<\infty\) determined by the convention for the \(\psi_2\)-norm.  Hence
\[
 E_P[W^2]
 \leq E_P[(b_n(X)+\xi)^2]
 \leq2(C_q+2C_\theta C_g)^2+2c_2\psi_\xi^2
 =:K_W.
\]

Let \(\mathcal E_n=\{\mathbb P_nV\geq A_a/4\}\).  On \(\mathcal E_n\), projection onto \([-C_\theta,C_\theta]\) is a contraction at \(\theta_0(P)\), and the sample identity
\[
 \mathbb P_n[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =\theta_0(P)\mathbb P_nV+\mathbb P_nW
\]
therefore gives
\[
 \lvert\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0(P)\rvert
 \leq\frac{4}{A_a}\lvert\mathbb P_nW\rvert.
\]
Under \(\text{ass:iid-sampling}\),
\[
 E_P[(\mathbb P_nW)^2]
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(W)}{n}
 \leq\frac{K_W}{n}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n^c\), the inequality \(\mu_a\geq A_a/2\) entails
\[
 \lvert\mathbb P_nV-\mu_a\rvert\geq A_a/4.
\]
Chebyshev's inequality and \(\operatorname{Var}_P(V)\leq K_V\) yield
\[
 P(\mathcal E_n^c)\leq\frac{16K_V}{nA_a^2}.
\]
Both \(\widehat\theta_{a,n}\) and \(\theta_0(P)\) lie in \([-C_\theta,C_\theta]\), so their squared distance is at most \(4C_\theta^2\).  Splitting the expectation over \(\mathcal E_n\) and \(\mathcal E_n^c\) consequently gives
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \leq\frac{16K_W+64C_\theta^2K_V}{nA_a^2}.
\]
The same clipping gives the deterministic bound \(4C_\theta^2\).  Since
\[
 A_a^{-2}
 =a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\},
\]
taking the smaller of these two bounds proves, uniformly over the displayed path laws,
\[
 \sup_P E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \leq C\min\!\left\{
  1,\frac{1}{n}a^{-2}
  \exp\!\left(\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right)
 \right\},
\]
where \(C<\infty\) depends only on \(C_\theta,C_g,C_q,\psi_\xi\).

Finally, write \(\Delta_a=2a^4=\lvert\kappa_4(\eta_a)\rvert\).  Then
\[
 a=\left(\frac{\Delta_a}{2}\right)^{1/4},
 \qquad
 a^{-2}=\sqrt2\,\Delta_a^{-1/2},
\]
and
\[
 \frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}
 \leq\frac{\pi^2}{4a^2}
 =\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\leq\frac12
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \varepsilon_{1,n}\leq\frac{a}{\pi}
 =\frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi}.
\]
Absorbing the factor \(\sqrt2\) into \(C\) proves the final cumulant-parametrized risk display.  Every step of this second argument uses the full Gaussian--Rademacher transform, not merely the scalar fourth-cumulant lower bound.  It therefore proves only the stated construction-specific upper benchmark and leaves the full cumulant-only minimax frontier unresolved.
