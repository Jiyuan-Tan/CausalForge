Fix the effective bank, write \(a_{\star}=a_{\star}^{\mathrm{eff}}\), and put \(m_a=|I_a|\).  For \(n\ge2\), both splits are nonempty and \(m_a\ge n/3\).  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  By \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\), some grid index \(j_{\star}\) satisfies
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(M)\ge1,\qquad \inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|M|\ge a_{\star}. \tag{1}
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) gives \(|H_n|\ge3/4\) on \(|z|\le R_1\), makes \(H_n\) zero-free there, and shows that \(F_n=MH_n\) and \(M\) have the same zeros.  Hence
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\ge1,\qquad \inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|F_n|\ge3a_{\star}/4. \tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
 d_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,\quad
 e_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|,\quad
 \mathcal A_a=\{d_a\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
The certified lower endpoint differs from the true boundary infimum by at most \(a_{\star}/64\).  On \(\mathcal A_0\), (2) makes the lower endpoint for \(j_{\star}\) at least \(39a_{\star}/64>a_{\star}/2\).  Rouch\'e's theorem shows that its rounded winding number equals \(N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\), so it is pilot-admissible.  The maximizing rule selects \(\widehat j_n\) with lower endpoint at least \(39a_{\star}/64\), whence
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_n|\ge31a_{\star}/64,\qquad
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)\ge1. \tag{3}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), the evaluation-fold true infimum is at least \(23a_{\star}/64\), and its certified lower endpoint is at least \(22a_{\star}/64>a_{\star}/4\); no fallback occurs.

The construction is measurable and computable: the bank is a finite explicitly enumerated list; empirical transforms are finite sums of elementary functions; observable derivative bounds certify boundary infima; certified quadrature and integer rounding compute winding numbers; and finite maxima, tie rules, and fallbacks are measurable.  All refinement loops terminate on admitted circles.

There is a class-uniform \(C_G<\infty\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|\le C_G. \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 |G_n(z)|\le e^{2C_gR_1}\|Y\|_2\{E[e^{2R_1|\eta|}]\}^{1/2},
\]
and the class bounds uniformly control both factors.

On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), let \(C=C_{\widehat j_n}\) and \(N=N_C(F_n)\).  By (3), zero-freeness of \(H_n\), and \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\),
\[
 \theta_0=\frac1{N2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz.
\]
The circle length is at most \(2\pi R_1\), \(N\ge1\), and the two denominator lower bounds together with (4) imply, after subtracting ratios and adding the certified numerical error,
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0|
 \le L_{\star}(d_1+e_1)+n^{-1}, \tag{5}
\]
for an explicit finite \(L_{\star}\) depending only on fixed class constants.  Real parts and projection cannot increase distance from \(\theta_0\).

By \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) and \(m_a\ge n/3\),
\[
 E_P[d_a^2+e_a^2]\le K_1/n. \tag{6}
\]
Markov's inequality gives
\[
 P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)\le K_2/n. \tag{7}
\]
On the exceptional event, clipping bounds squared error by \(4C_{\theta}^2\).  Combining (5)--(7) and \(n^{-2}\le n^{-1}\) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0)^2]\le C/n. \tag{8}
\]
This one common computable statistic witnesses the minimax upper bound.

For the converse, apply \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\).  Let \(c_0,C_0\) be its constants, choose
\[
 0<h_0\le\min\{c_0,1,(8C_0)^{-1/2}\},\qquad
 \vartheta_{\pm}=\pm h_0/(2\sqrt n),
\]
and use the two corresponding submodel laws.  Their target separation is \(h_0/\sqrt n\) and their product Kullback--Leibler divergence is at most \(1/8\), so Pinsker bounds total variation by \(1/4\).  Testing by the sign of an arbitrary estimator yields
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-,+\}}E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}}
 [(\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 \ge\frac{h_0^2}{8n}(1-\|P_-^{\otimes n}-P_+^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}})
 \ge\frac{3h_0^2}{32n}.
\]
Taking the supremum and infimum proves \(c/n\).  Finally, (8) and Markov's inequality imply
\[
 P\{|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0|>\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}\}\le\gamma,
\]
which proves the generalized-quantile claim.  The estimator uses no outcome code, and the separate class definitions give the ACE disclaimer.  Because the explicit constants deteriorate rapidly as \(\delta\downarrow0\), this fixed-separation proof does not settle the local frontier or uniform inference.
