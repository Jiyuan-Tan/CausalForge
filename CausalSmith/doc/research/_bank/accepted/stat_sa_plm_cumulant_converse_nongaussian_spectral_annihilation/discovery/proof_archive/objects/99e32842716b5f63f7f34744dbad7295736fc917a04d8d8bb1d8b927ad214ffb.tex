We prove executable correctness, the uniform upper bound, the two minimax converses, and the quantile and comparator conclusions.

\emph{1. Certified execution.}  Unfold \(\text{def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate}\).  The real endpoint operations are sound because the extrema of a bilinear map on a rectangle occur at corners.  For complex multiplication, applying those real formulas to
\[
 (x+iy)(u+iv)=(xu-yv)+i(xv+yu)
\]
proves rectangle containment.  For division, a certified lower modulus \(m>0\) gives \(u^2+v^2\ge m^2>0\), so the same multiplication formulas followed by outward real division prove containment.  Rational square-root bisection preserves its bracketing invariant.  After rational range reduction, the alternating sine and cosine sums bracket their values by the first omitted term; the real exponential series is bounded by its explicit positive remainder.  Therefore
\[
 e^{x+iy}=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)
\]
shows that the complex-exponential rectangles are sound.  The stated Taylor remainders tend effectively to zero, and the algebraic composition rules divide any requested error among finitely many inputs; hence every returned name has the claimed effective modulus.  Finite induction proves the soundness of the sum rectangles.  Finally, on each mesh cell a Lipschitz function differs from its node value by at most \(L/N\); summing the cell integrals proves the infimum and trapezoidal enclosures.

These arguments establish the operation-level invariants of the concrete object, rather than postulating a theorem-local numerical oracle.  In \(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\), the coarse rational magnitude bounds are finite because every supplied name refines at width one.  The bound \(e^x\le3^{\lceil x\rceil}\) for \(x\ge0\) makes every displayed derivative budget rational and finite.  Each mesh size is a ceiling of a rational ratio, and every node precision is a direct call to an effective modulus.  The bank and the finite list of circles are finite by \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\).  Consequently every branch has a finite rational execution trace; every division is guarded; and the prescribed fallback, maximum-score rule, and smallest-index tie-break make the output total and single-valued.

At the interpretation level, equal real samples have equal canonical floor-dyadic names, hence identical traces and outputs.  For any fixed finite trace, every queried canonical endpoint is a floor of a scaled sample coordinate and is Borel measurable.  Finite rational arithmetic and rational comparisons preserve Borel measurability.  There are countably many finite traces, so partitioning the sample space by terminal trace proves that \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) is Borel measurable.  This also proves trace extensionality.  Inspection of the executable input record shows that it uses \(\bar g_n\), no outcome code, and no treatment-noise law.

\emph{2. Uniform upper bound.}  Fix \(n\ge2\), \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\).  Let \(j_{\star}\) be supplied by \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\).  By \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\),
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|F_n|\ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4. \tag{1}
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), put
\[
 d_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \quad
 e_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|,
 \quad
 \mathcal A_a=\{d_a\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\), soundness and the width \(a_{\star}/64\) of the certified modulus interval give the candidate \(j_{\star}\) a lower endpoint at least
\[
 \left(\frac34-\frac18-\frac1{64}\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{39a_{\star}}{64}. \tag{2}
\]
The straight-line homotopy between \(F_n\) and \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) is nonzero on this circle, so their winding numbers agree and are positive.  Thus \(j_{\star}\) is admissible.

Let \(C=C_{\widehat j_n}\).  Maximality of the selected rational lower endpoint, (2), and subtraction of \(d_0\) give
\[
 \inf_C|F_n|\ge\frac{31a_{\star}}{64}. \tag{3}
\]
The same homotopy and the sound certified winding rectangle give
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_C(F_n)=:N\ge1. \tag{4}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), the evaluation empirical modulus is at least \(23a_{\star}/64\); after the enclosure width is subtracted, its lower endpoint exceeds \(a_{\star}/4\).  Hence the evaluation branch does not fall back.

There is a constant \(C_G<\infty\), depending only on \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},R_1\), such that
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|\le C_G. \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\).  The Luxemburg inequality and Young's inequality give
\[
 E e^{t|\eta|}\le\sqrt2e^{t^2\psi_{\eta}^2/2},
\]
and the same convention bounds the second moments of \(\eta\) and \(\xi\).  Cauchy--Schwarz then bounds \(E[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]\), proving (5) without assuming independence of \(\xi\) and \(\eta\).

Subtract the empirical and population ratios on \(C\).  From (3)--(5), \(\operatorname{len}(C)\le2\pi R_1\), and \(N\ge1\),
\[
 \left|
 \frac1{N2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}}{\widehat F_{1,n}},dz
 -\frac1{N2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{G_n}{F_n},dz
 \right|
 \le L_{\mathrm{rat}}(d_1+e_1), \tag{6}
\]
where, with \(\mu=31a_{\star}/64\) and \(\nu=23a_{\star}/64\), one may take
\[
 L_{\mathrm{rat}}=R_1\max\{\nu^{-1},C_G(\mu\nu)^{-1}\}. \tag{7}
\]
The hypotheses of \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) hold: (3) makes \(F_n\) zero-free on \(C\), \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) makes \(H_n\) zero-free on and inside \(C\), and (4) supplies a positive count.  Hence the population contour in (6) equals the causal target \(\theta_0(P)\).  The final certified midpoint adds at most \(1/n\), and real part followed by projection onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase distance from \(\theta_0(P)\).

Both fold sizes are at least \(n/3\).  Therefore \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) and Markov's inequality give, for its constant \(K\),
\[
 E_P(d_a^2+e_a^2)\le\frac{3K}{n},
 \qquad
 P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)
 \le\frac{384K}{a_{\star}^2n}. \tag{8}
\]
Projection bounds squared error by \(4C_{\theta}^2\) on the exceptional event.  On the good event, (6), \((d_1+e_1)^2\le2(d_1^2+e_1^2)\), and the numerical error give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac{C_{\mathrm{up}}}{n}, \tag{9}
\]
with, for example,
\[
 C_{\mathrm{up}}
 =12L_{\mathrm{rat}}^2K
 +\frac{1536C_{\theta}^2K}{a_{\star}^2}+2. \tag{10}
\]
The bank, \(a_{\star}\), \(C_G\), \(K\), and \(L_{\mathrm{rat}}\) depend only on \(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\).  In particular, \(C_{\mathrm{up}}\) is chosen once outside the quantifiers over \(P,n\), represented names, and traces, and is independent of \(\gamma\).  Since the certified statistic is one data-driven rule, (9) proves the minimax upper bound.

\emph{3. Converses.}  Apply the first branch of \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\).  Choose fixed \(h>0\), depending only on the class constants, such that \(\vartheta_{\pm}=\pm h/(2\sqrt n)\) lie on its path and the product relative entropy is at most \(1/8\).  Pinsker's inequality gives total variation at most \(1/4\).  Testing by the sign of an arbitrary estimator yields
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-,+\}}
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}}[(\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 \ge\frac{h^2}{8n}(1-1/4)
 =\frac{3h^2}{32n}. \tag{11}
\]
Thus \(c_{\mathrm{NG}}/n\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\).

By \(\text{lem:jms-ace-class-relations}\),
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}. \tag{12}
\]
Restriction of (9) proves the spectral upper bound on the full published ACE class.  The second branch of \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\) gives on this same class
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\theta}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 E_P[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \ge\frac{c_{\mathrm{ACE}}}{n}. \tag{13}
\]
The certified statistic is admissible in this infimum.  Taking
\[
 c=\min\{c_{\mathrm{NG}},c_{\mathrm{ACE}}\}>0,
 \qquad C=C_{\mathrm{up}}
\]
proves both MSE brackets with constants fixed exactly as claimed.

Finally, (9) and Markov's inequality give
\[
 P\left(
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|
 >\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}
 \right)\le\gamma,
\]
which is the stated generalized-quantile bound; (12) gives its ACE-class restriction.  The class-relation lemma also proves the \(L^s(P_X)\) subclass statement and equality at \(s=r\), but no reverse inclusion for the broad spectral class.  The constant \(C_{\gamma}\) belongs only to the cited ACE theorem: for fixed \(\gamma\) it is one witness chosen outside all \(P,n\) quantifiers, is not an algorithm input, and does not enter (9)--(10).  The lower bound (13) is over all estimators, not the ACE estimator.  Every constant here may deteriorate as fixed \(\delta\) decreases; no shrinking-separation rate, adaptive switch, uniform inference result, or local converse has been proved.
