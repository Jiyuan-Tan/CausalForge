Fix \(n\) and suppose that \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\neq\varnothing\).  Choose \(P_{\star}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  Retain from \(P_{\star}\) the joint law of \((X,T)\), the covariate marginal, the functions \(g_0\) and \(q_0\), and hence the residual \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\).  The supplied codes and their clipped versions \(\bar g_n\) and \(\bar q_n\) are deterministic experiment inputs, so they are retained as well.

Let
\[
 c_{\mathrm N}=\lVert N(0,1)\rVert_{\psi_2},
 \qquad
 \tau=\frac{\psi_{\xi}}{2c_{\mathrm N}}>0,
 \qquad
 c_0=\min\{C_{\theta},1\}>0.
\]
On a product extension, let \(\xi_{\star}\sim N(0,\tau^2)\) be independent of \((X,T)\).  For every \(\vartheta\in[-c_0,c_0]\), define
\[
 Y_{\vartheta}=q_0(X)+\vartheta\eta+\xi_{\star},
\]
and let \(P_{n,\vartheta}\) denote the resulting law of \((X,T,Y_{\vartheta})\).

We first verify every membership condition in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  The law of \((X,T)\) is unchanged, so \(g_0\), \(\eta\), the independence \(\eta\perp X\), the treatment sub-Gaussian bound, the order-\(k\) cumulant separation, the range bound on \(g_0\), and the direct treatment-code condition
\[
 \lVert\bar g_n-g_0\rVert_{L^1(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}
\]
are inherited from \(P_{\star}\).  Moreover, centering and independence of \(\eta\) give
\[
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta}}[Y_{\vartheta}\mid X]
 =q_0(X)+\vartheta E_{P_{\star}}[\eta\mid X]
 =q_0(X).
\]
Thus the outcome regression remains exactly \(q_0\), including its bound \(\lVert q_0\rVert_{\infty}\le C_q\).  Conditional on \((X,T)\),
\[
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta}}[Y_{\vartheta}\mid X,T]
 =q_0(X)+\vartheta\eta,
 \qquad
 E[\xi_{\star}\mid X,T]=0.
\]
Hence the partially linear coefficient is \(\theta_0(P_{n,\vartheta})=\vartheta\), and the outcome innovation is \(\xi_{\star}\).  This coefficient is unique: cumulant separation implies that \(\eta\) is not almost surely zero, so two distinct coefficients cannot give the same conditional mean.  The boundary condition is valid because \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\le C_{\theta}\).  Finally, Gaussian scaling and independence yield
\[
 \lVert\xi_{\star}\rVert_{\psi_2\mid X}
 =c_{\mathrm N}\tau
 =\frac{\psi_{\xi}}{2}
 \le\psi_{\xi}
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Its variance \(\tau^2\) is strictly positive and depends only on the fixed class constant \(\psi_{\xi}\) and the universal norm convention.  Therefore \(P_{n,\vartheta}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) for every \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\).  This verification uses no outcome-code restriction: \(\bar q_n\) is unchanged, but the broad class imposes no uncertainty ball involving it.

It remains to calculate the divergence.  The two laws \(P_{n,\vartheta_0}\) and \(P_{n,\vartheta_1}\) have the same \((X,T)\) marginal.  Given \((X,T)\), their outcome distributions are Gaussian with common variance \(\tau^2>0\) and means differing by \((\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0)\eta\).  The conditional chain rule for relative entropy and the elementary equal-variance Gaussian identity therefore give
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(P_{n,\vartheta_0}\middle\|P_{n,\vartheta_1}\right)
 =\frac{(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0)^2}{2\tau^2}
   E_{P_{\star}}[\eta^2].
\]
By the defining exponential-moment inequality for the \(\psi_2\)-norm, there is a universal constant \(c_2<\infty\) such that
\[
 E_{P_{\star}}[\eta^2]\le c_2\psi_{\eta}^2.
\]
For example, the usual Luxemburg convention gives this by integrating the bound \(P_{\star}(|\eta|>t)\le2\exp(-t^2/\psi_{\eta}^2)\).  Additivity of relative entropy for product measures now shows that, whenever \(|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=h/\sqrt n\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n}
          \middle\|P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n}\right)
 &=nD_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(P_{n,\vartheta_0}
          \middle\|P_{n,\vartheta_1}\right)\\
 &=\frac{h^2}{2\tau^2}E_{P_{\star}}[\eta^2]\\
 &\le \frac{c_2\psi_{\eta}^2}{2\tau^2}h^2
 =:C_0h^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The constants \(c_0\) and \(C_0\) depend only on the fixed class constants (and universal norm conventions).  No support-overlap or rank condition is required; the only nondegeneracy used is \(\tau^2>0\) for the conditional Gaussian divergence and the cumulant separation for a genuinely one-dimensional parameterization.
