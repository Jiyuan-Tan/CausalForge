The construction in \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\) computes once for all a finite certified translated-dyadic bank and a positive rational certificate \(a_{\star}\) from the certified-real primitive-constant names. Put \(m_a=|I_a|\). For \(n\ge2\), \(m_a\ge n/3\). Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and choose an index \(j_{\star}\) supplied by that lemma. By \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\),
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|F_n|
 \ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4. \tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
 d_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \quad
 e_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|,
 \quad
 \mathcal A_a=\{d_a\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\), the certified pilot lower endpoint for \(j_{\star}\) is at least
\[
 \left(\frac34-\frac18-\frac1{64}\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{39}{64}a_{\star}. \tag{2}
\]
The straight-line homotopy from \(F_n\) to \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) remains nonzero on \(C_{j_{\star}}\), so winding number is invariant. The certified pilot count is therefore positive and \(j_{\star}\) is admissible.

Let \(C=C_{\widehat j_n}\) be the selected circle. Since the selector maximizes the certified lower modulus over admissible indices, its certified pilot lower endpoint is at least (2). Its actual pilot empirical modulus is no smaller than that endpoint, and subtracting \(d_0\le a_{\star}/8\) gives
\[
 \inf_C|F_n|
 \ge\left(\frac{39}{64}-\frac18\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{31}{64}a_{\star}. \tag{3}
\]
The selected circle is pilot-admissible, so its certified count is at least one. The homotopy between its pilot transform and \(F_n\) remains nonzero by (3), whence
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_C(F_n)=:N\ge1. \tag{4}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), (3) and \(d_1\le a_{\star}/8\) imply that the evaluation empirical modulus is at least \(23a_{\star}/64\). After subtracting the enclosure width, the certified evaluation lower endpoint is at least
\[
 \left(\frac{23}{64}-\frac1{64}\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{22}{64}a_{\star}>\frac14a_{\star}. \tag{5}
\]
Thus the definition does not invoke its fallback on this event.

Uniformly over the class, Cauchy--Schwarz and the sub-Gaussian bounds give a finite constant \(C_G\), depending only on the displayed class constants, such that
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|\le C_G. \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\); the range and Luxemburg bounds therefore uniformly control \(E_P[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]\). On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), (3), (5), (6), subtraction of two ratios, and the contour-length bound \(\operatorname{len}(C)\le2\pi R_1\) give
\[
 \left|
 \frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C
 \frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}\,dz
 -
 \frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C
 \frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
 \right|
 \le L_{\star}(d_1+e_1), \tag{7}
\]
where, with \(\mu=31a_{\star}/64\) and \(\nu=23a_{\star}/64\), one may take
\[
 L_{\star}=R_1\max\{\nu^{-1},C_G(\mu\nu)^{-1}\}.
\]
The hypotheses of \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) hold: (3) makes \(F_n\) zero-free on \(C\), \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) makes \(H_n\) zero-free on and inside it, and (4) gives the positive zero count. Therefore the population contour functional in (7) equals the causal target \(\theta_0\). The certified ratio midpoint adds complex error at most \(1/n\); taking its real part and projecting onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase its distance from \(\theta_0\).

By \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\), uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 E_P(d_a^2+e_a^2)\le\frac{3K}{n},
 \qquad
 P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)
 \le\frac{384K}{a_{\star}^2n}. \tag{8}
\]
On the exceptional event, projection bounds squared error by \(4C_{\theta}^2\). On the good event, (7) and the numerical error give a contribution at most \(12L_{\star}^2K/n+2/n^2\). Hence, for example,
\[
 C=12L_{\star}^2K+\frac{1536C_{\theta}^2K}{a_{\star}^2}+2
\]
is valid and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac Cn. \tag{9}
\]
All constants depend only on the fixed primitive class constants, because \(R_1\), the finite bank, and \(a_{\star}\) do. The estimator uses finite sums, deterministic interval refinements with positive stopping margins, deterministic tie-breaking, projection, and fallback. Each operation is Borel measurable and terminates. Thus it is a total computable sample map under the certified-real convention. It uses \(\bar g_n\), but neither a supplied outcome code nor the unknown law of \(\eta\). It is therefore an admissible minimax rule and \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\le C/n\).

For the converse, invoke \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\). Choose a fixed \(h>0\), depending only on the class constants, sufficiently small that \(\vartheta_{\pm}=\pm h/(2\sqrt n)\) belong to the path for every \(n\ge2\) and the relative entropy between the corresponding product laws is at most \(1/8\). Pinsker's inequality gives total variation at most \(1/4\). Testing by the sign of an arbitrary estimator yields
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-,+\}}E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}}
 [ (\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 \ge\frac{h^2}{8n}
 \{1-\|P_-^{\otimes n}-P_+^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}
 \ge\frac{3h^2}{32n}.
\]
Taking the supremum over the class and then the infimum over estimators proves \(c/n\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\), with \(c=3h^2/32\). Finally, (9) and Markov's inequality give
\[
 P\!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|>
 \sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}\right)\le\gamma,
\]
which proves the generalized-quantile bound. The separate definitions of the broad class and the ACE comparison subclass prove the scope disclaimer. Since the computed constants depend on the fixed separation \(\delta\), this argument supplies no useful rate as \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), no adaptive selector across methods, and no uniform inference theorem.
