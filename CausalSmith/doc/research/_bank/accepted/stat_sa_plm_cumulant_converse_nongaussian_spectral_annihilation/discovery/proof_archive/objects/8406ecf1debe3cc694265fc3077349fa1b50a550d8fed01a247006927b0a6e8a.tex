Applying the zero-free-disk argument with \(\delta\) replaced by \(\delta_n\) localizes a zero at a radius of order at most \((\psi_{\eta}^2/\delta_n)^{1/(k-2)}\).  Repeating the compactness proof at each \(n\) gives a finite bank, but the finite-subcover argument is nonquantitative and does not control its smallest boundary modulus as \(\delta_n\to0\).  Directly inserting the growing radius into the empirical exponential envelope produces an unspecified, potentially severe variance constant and therefore cannot certify a sharp phase diagram.
