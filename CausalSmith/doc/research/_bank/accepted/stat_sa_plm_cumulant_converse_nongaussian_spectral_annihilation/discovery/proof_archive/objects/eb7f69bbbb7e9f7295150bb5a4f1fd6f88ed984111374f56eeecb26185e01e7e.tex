Fix \(n\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  All norms below are
with respect to the probability measure \(P_X\).  The code errors are
measurable and bounded: \(g_0\) and \(\bar g_n\) take values in
\([-C_g,C_g]\), while \(q_0\) and \(\bar q_n\) take values in
\([-C_q,C_q]\).  Thus every norm and expectation used below is finite,
including at the endpoint \(s=\infty\).

First suppose \(r\le s<\infty\).  For any measurable \(f\), Jensen's
inequality for the concave map \(u\mapsto u^{r/s}\) on
\([0,\infty)\) gives
\[
 E_P[|f(X)|^r]
 =E_P\!\left[(|f(X)|^s)^{r/s}\right]
 \le \left(E_P[|f(X)|^s]\right)^{r/s}.
\]
Taking \(r\)-th roots yields
\[
 \|f\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \|f\|_{L^s(P_X)}.                 \tag{1}
\]
If \(s=\infty\), then \(|f(X)|\le\|f\|_{L^\infty(P_X)}\)
almost surely, so integration and an \(r\)-th root give (1) again.
The standing range \(s\ge r+1\) in particular ensures \(s>r\), while
only \(s\ge r\) was needed for (1).

Apply (1) first to \(f=\bar g_n-g_0\) and then to
\(f=\bar q_n-q_0\).  The two \(L^s(P_X)\) restrictions in
\(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\) imply
\[
 \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n},
 \qquad
 \|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}.       \tag{2}
\]
These are precisely the two code controls in
\(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\).  Every other member property displayed
in that definition is also a member property of
\(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\).  Hence the definitions and (2) give
the one-way inclusion
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}
 \subseteq \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}.   \tag{3}
\]
This does not identify the two classes as equal.  Indeed, norm
monotonicity has no converse with the same radius: on a probability
space containing an event \(A\) with \(0<P_X(A)<1\), the bounded
function \(f=c\mathbf 1_A\) satisfies
\[
 \frac{\|f\|_{L^s(P_X)}}{\|f\|_{L^r(P_X)}}
 =P_X(A)^{1/s-1/r}>1
\]
when \(s<\infty\), and the analogous ratio is
\(P_X(A)^{-1/r}>1\) when \(s=\infty\).  Thus (1) supplies only the
direction in (3); it does not supply equality of the uncertainty sets.

It remains to verify the displayed comparator bound, including its
constants.  Suppose \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) holds.  By
\(\text{def:jms-eligibility}\), all logarithms and divisions in
condition (21) are defined.  In particular, the case
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}=0\) is excluded whenever the antecedent holds.
Define the nonnegative proof intermediates
\[
 A_n=\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r}\varepsilon_{2,n}
       +C_\theta\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
\]
and
\[
 V_n=(C_g+\psi_\eta)^r
 \left\{r^2(C_g+\psi_\eta)+\psi_\xi+C_\theta\psi_\eta\right\}
 (\gamma n)^{-1/2}.
\]
The cited specialization \(\text{lem:jms-ace-theorem-five-four}\)
gives, for positive \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
 \le C_\gamma r!6^r\delta^{-1}(A_n+4V_n).                \tag{4}
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{2,n}=0\), apply the same cited theorem after replacing
that radius by an arbitrary \(u\in(0,\varepsilon_{1,n})\).  The original
radius-zero uncertainty set is contained in the radius-\(u\) set, and
the maximum defining \(b_{2,n}\) is unchanged, so the same eligibility
condition holds.  Taking the infimum of the resulting right-hand sides
over \(u\downarrow0\) proves (4) at the boundary as well.

Since \(A_n,V_n\ge0\), \(r\ge2\), \(6^r\le16^r\), and
\(A_n+4V_n\le A_n+64V_n\), equation (4) implies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
 \le C_\gamma r!16^r\delta^{-1}(A_n+64V_n)
 =B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}.                              \tag{5}
\]
The last equality is the definition of \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\).
The supremum in (5) is therefore over the published base-class and
\(L^r(P_X)\)-uncertainty intersection encoded by
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\); the deterministic
eligibility predicate is imposed at the displayed index \(n\), not
silently added as a property of an individual law.
