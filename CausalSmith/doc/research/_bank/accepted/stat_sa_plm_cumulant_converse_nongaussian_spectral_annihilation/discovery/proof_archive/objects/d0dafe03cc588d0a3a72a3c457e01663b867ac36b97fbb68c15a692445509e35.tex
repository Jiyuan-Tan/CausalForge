For the first conclusion, fix \(n\).  The number \(\delta_n\) is strictly positive by the definition of the sequence \((\delta_n)_{n\geq 1}\).  Apply the cited Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis bound in \(\text{lem:jms-ace-theorem-five-four}\) with its positive separation constant instantiated as \(\delta_n\).  By the definition in the theorem statement, \(\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)\) retains every member condition of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) except that the cumulant restriction is \(\lvert\kappa_{r+1}(\eta)\rvert\geq\delta_n\).  Under the same substitution, the quantity denoted by \(b_{2,n}\) in \(\text{def:jms-eligibility}\) is
\[
 \widetilde b_{2,n}
 =200\min\{1,C_\theta\}\delta_n
 \max\!\left\{
   \varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n},
   (\gamma n)^{-1/2}(\psi_\xi+C_\theta\psi_\eta)
 \right\}^{-1}.
\]
Consequently the theorem's displayed local eligibility inequality is exactly condition (21) after that substitution.  The hypotheses \(\varepsilon_{1,n}>0\) and \(\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\), together with the stated requirement that all logarithms and denominators be defined, supply the positivity and boundary conditions required by the cited result.  Substitution of \(\delta_n\) for \(\delta\) in its proof-closing bound gives, term by term,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)\right|\right)\\
 &\quad\leq
 C_\gamma r!16^r\delta_n^{-1}
 \Bigl[
   \varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
   +C_\theta\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}\\
 &\hspace{42mm}
   +64(C_g+\psi_\eta)^r
    \{r^2(C_g+\psi_\eta)+\psi_\xi+C_\theta\psi_\eta\}
    (\gamma n)^{-1/2}
 \Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]
This is precisely \(U_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{loc}}\).  Taking the numerical minimum of this deterministic guarantee and a separately valid DML guarantee yields an oracle upper envelope only.  Indeed, no statistic implementing a switch has been defined here, and the hypotheses needed for the separate DML guarantee have not been imposed here.  Therefore this numerical comparison supplies neither a minimax conclusion nor a data-driven selector.

We next prove every assertion for the Gaussian--Rademacher path.  Let \(0<a\leq1\).  Independence of \(G_\circ\) and \(S\) gives, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_a(z)
 &=E[e^{z\sqrt{1-a^2}G_\circ}]E[e^{zaS}]\\
 &=\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az).
\end{aligned}
\]
The fourth cumulant is additive over independent summands, vanishes for the Gaussian summand, and is homogeneous of degree four.  Since a symmetric Rademacher variable obeys
\[
 \kappa_4(S)=E[S^4]-3E[S^2]^2=1-3=-2,
\]
we obtain
\[
 \kappa_4(\eta_a)=a^4\kappa_4(S)=-2a^4.
\]
The characteristic function is
\[
 \phi_a(t):=M_a(it)
 =\exp\!\left\{-\frac{1-a^2}{2}t^2\right\}\cos(at).
\]
Its exponential factor is strictly positive for real \(t\).  Hence its first positive zero is
\[
 t_a=\frac{\pi}{2a}.
\]
The path is uniformly sub-Gaussian: for real \(u\), the elementary inequality \(\cosh x\leq e^{x^2/2}\) gives
\[
 E[e^{u\eta_a}]
 =e^{(1-a^2)u^2/2}\cosh(au)
 \leq e^{(1-a^2)u^2/2}e^{a^2u^2/2}
 =e^{u^2/2}.
\]
In particular, all moments used below are finite, differentiation of \(\phi_a\) is justified, and \(E[\eta_a^2]=1\).

The distribution of \(\eta_a\) is symmetric.  For every real shift \(d\),
\[
 E[e^{it_a(\eta_a+d)}]
 =e^{it_ad}\phi_a(t_a)=0.
\]
Taking imaginary parts proves
\[
 E[\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+d)\}]=0.
\]
Differentiating the characteristic function and evaluating at \(t_a\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E[\eta_a\sin(t_a\eta_a)]
 &=-\phi_a'(t_a)\\
 &=a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\}
 =A_a.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(D_n\) is measurable with respect to \(X\) and \(\eta_a\perp X\), condition on \(D_n=d\) and expand the sine of a sum.  Symmetry and \(\phi_a(t_a)=0\) imply
\[
 E[\eta_a\cos(t_a\eta_a)]=0,
 \qquad
 E[\sin(t_a\eta_a)]=0,
 \qquad
 E[\cos(t_a\eta_a)]=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
 E_P[(\eta_a+D_n)\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+D_n)\}\mid D_n]
 =A_a\cos(t_aD_n).
\]
Taking expectations and using \(Z_n=\eta_a+D_n\) proves the relevance identity
\[
 E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =A_aE_P[\cos(t_aD_n)].
\]

The clipping definition of \(\bar g_n\), together with \(\lVert g_0\rVert_\infty\leq C_g\), gives the support bound
\[
 \lvert D_n\rvert\leq2C_g
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Since \(P_X\) is a probability measure and \(s\geq r+1\geq1\), monotonicity of probability-space \(L^p\) norms and \(\text{ass:treatment-code-radius}\) give
\[
 E_P\lvert D_n\rvert
 =\lVert D_n\rVert_{L^1(P_X)}
 \leq\lVert D_n\rVert_{L^s(P_X)}
 \leq\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
The elementary inequality \(1-\cos x\leq\lvert x\rvert\) and the hypothesis \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\leq1/2\) now yield
\[
 E_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
 \geq1-t_aE_P\lvert D_n\rvert
 \geq\frac12.
\]
Because \(A_a>0\), this proves
\[
 E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]\geq\frac{A_a}{2}>0,
\]
which is the positivity condition needed for the population ratio and for the empirical threshold below.

We distinguish the causal target from its identifying ratio.  The structural decomposition is
\[
 Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi.
\]
Exact sine annihilation conditional on \(X\) gives
\[
 E_P[b_n(X)\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P\!\left[b_n(X)E_P\{\sin(t_aZ_n)\mid X\}\right]
 =0.
\]
The integrand is integrable because \(b_n(X)=q_0(X)-\theta_0D_n\) is bounded by the support restrictions.  Moreover, \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) is measurable with respect to \((X,T)\), so \(\text{ass:outcome-mean-independence}\) gives
\[
 E_P[\xi\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P\!\left[E_P(\xi\mid X,T)\sin(t_aZ_n)\right]
 =0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 E_P[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =\theta_0E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)].
\]
Thus the computable population ratio is an observed-law functional distinct from the causal coefficient by definition, and the preceding equation proves that it equals \(\theta_0(P)\) on the displayed path class.

It remains to prove the uniform mean-squared-error bound for the explicit sample ratio.  Define
\[
 V=Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n),
 \qquad
 W=(Y-\theta_0Z_n)\sin(t_aZ_n),
 \qquad
 \mu_a=E_P[V].
\]
The identification calculation gives \(E_P[W]=0\), and the relevance calculation gives \(\mu_a\geq A_a/2>0\).  Since \(\lvert\sin x\rvert\leq1\), \(E[\eta_a^2]=1\), and \(\lvert D_n\rvert\leq2C_g\),
\[
 E_P[V^2]
 \leq E_P[Z_n^2]
 \leq2E[\eta_a^2]+2E_P[D_n^2]
 \leq2+8C_g^2
 =:K_V.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \lvert b_n(X)\rvert
 =\lvert q_0(X)-\theta_0D_n\rvert
 \leq C_q+2C_\theta C_g.
\]
The conditional sub-Gaussian norm bound supplies a universal finite constant \(c_2\), determined only by the convention for \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\psi_2}\), such that
\[
 E_P[\xi^2]\leq c_2\psi_\xi^2.
\]
Since \(Y-\theta_0Z_n=b_n(X)+\xi\), it follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[W^2]
 &\leq E_P[(b_n(X)+\xi)^2]\\
 &\leq2(C_q+2C_\theta C_g)^2+2c_2\psi_\xi^2
 =:K_W.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both \(K_V\) and \(K_W\) are uniform in \(a\), \(n\), and the displayed path law \(P\).

Let
\[
 \mathcal E_a=\{\mathbb P_nV\geq A_a/4\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_a\), projection onto \([-C_\theta,C_\theta]\) is a contraction at \(\theta_0\), and the sample identity
\[
 \mathbb P_n[Y\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =\theta_0\mathbb P_nV+\mathbb P_nW
\]
gives
\[
 \lvert\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0\rvert
 \leq\frac{4}{A_a}\lvert\mathbb P_nW\rvert.
\]
By \(\text{ass:iid-sampling}\) and \(E_P[W]=0\),
\[
 E_P[(\mathbb P_nW)^2]
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(W)}{n}
 \leq\frac{K_W}{n}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_a^c\), the inequality \(\mu_a\geq A_a/2\) implies
\[
 \lvert\mathbb P_nV-\mu_a\rvert\geq A_a/4.
\]
Chebyshev's inequality and \(\operatorname{Var}_P(V)\leq K_V\) therefore yield
\[
 P(\mathcal E_a^c)
 \leq\frac{16K_V}{nA_a^2}.
\]
Both the estimator and the target lie in \([-C_\theta,C_\theta]\), so their squared distance on \(\mathcal E_a^c\) is at most \(4C_\theta^2\).  Splitting the risk over \(\mathcal E_a\) and its complement gives
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \leq\frac{16K_W+64C_\theta^2K_V}{nA_a^2}.
\]
The range restriction separately gives the deterministic risk bound \(4C_\theta^2\).  Taking the smaller of these two inequalities and using
\[
 A_a^{-2}
 =a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\}
\]
proves
\[
 \sup_P E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \leq
 C\min\!\left\{
  1,\frac{1}{n}a^{-2}
  \exp\!\left(\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right)
 \right\},
\]
where \(C<\infty\) depends only on \(C_\theta,C_g,C_q,\psi_\xi\).

Finally, set \(\Delta_a=2a^4\).  Then
\[
 a=\left(\frac{\Delta_a}{2}\right)^{1/4},
 \qquad
 a^{-2}=\sqrt2\,\Delta_a^{-1/2},
\]
and, because \(1-a^2\leq1\),
\[
 \frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}
 \leq\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\leq\frac12
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \varepsilon_{1,n}\leq\frac{a}{\pi}
 =\frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi}.
\]
Absorbing the factor \(\sqrt2\) into \(C\) proves the final cumulant-parametrized risk display.  This derivation uses the complete Gaussian--Rademacher transform, rather than only a fourth-cumulant lower bound.  It therefore proves a construction-specific upper benchmark, but no cumulant-only converse, full-class phase diagram, adaptive selector, or coverage statement.
