The usable distinction established here is not a positive Gaussian-versus-non-Gaussian lower-bound dichotomy under one common law class.  It is a constructive statement about the separated non-Gaussian experiment: complex transform zeros eliminate arbitrary outcome-side contamination exactly, and a fixed finite contour bank turns that identity into a uniform root-\(n\) estimator.  Gaussian transforms are zero-free, but the particular frozen Gaussian intersection cannot quantify the resulting difficulty because its bounded-outcome restriction already collapses the target to zero.
