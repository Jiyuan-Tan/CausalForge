Let \(\mathscr M\) be the locally uniform closure of the moment generating functions of centered laws satisfying \(\lVert\eta\rVert_{\psi_2}\le\psi_\eta\) and \(\lvert\kappa_k(\eta)\rvert\ge\delta\).  We first verify the compactness and closure properties used by \(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\).

The sub-Gaussian bound supplies a constant \(c_\psi<\infty\), independent of the law, such that
\[
 |M(z)|
 \le E[e^{\operatorname{Re}(z)\eta}]
 \le\exp(c_\psi\psi_\eta^2|z|^2),
 \qquad z\in\mathbb C.
\]
Thus the family is uniformly bounded on every compact disk.  Cauchy's formula on a slightly larger disk makes it equicontinuous on the smaller disk.  Successive finite-net extraction on disks of integer radius, followed by a diagonal choice, shows that every sequence has a subsequence converging locally uniformly to an entire function \(M_\infty\).  The underlying laws are tight because their second moments are uniformly bounded.  Along a further weakly convergent subsequence, the uniform sub-Gaussian tails make \(e^{t\eta}\) uniformly integrable for each real \(t\); hence \(M_\infty(t)\) is the moment generating function of the weak limit.  In particular,
\[
 M_\infty(0)=1,
 \qquad
 M_\infty'(0)=0.
\]
Local uniform convergence implies convergence of all derivatives at zero.  Since the \(k\)-th cumulant is a polynomial in the first \(k\) derivatives of the moment generating function at zero,
\[
 |\kappa_k(M_\infty)|
 =\lim_m|\kappa_k(M_m)|\ge\delta.
\]
The uniform sub-Gaussian exponential bound also passes to the limit.  Hence the zero-localization argument in \(\text{lem:zero-localization}\) applies to every member of \(\mathscr M\): each has a zero in \(\{|z|\le R_0\}\).  The preceding diagonal compactness shows that \(\mathscr M\) is compact for locally uniform convergence.

Fix \(M_\circ\in\mathscr M\).  Because \(M_\circ(0)=1\), it is not identically zero, so it has only finitely many zeros in the compact annulus \(R_0\le|z|\le R_1\).  Choose a rational radius
\[
 \rho(M_\circ)\in(R_0,R_1)
\]
which is not the modulus of any of those zeros.  The circle \(C(M_\circ)=\{|z|=\rho(M_\circ)\}\) is therefore zero-free, encloses the zero supplied by localization, and has
\[
 m(M_\circ):=\inf_{z\in C(M_\circ)}|M_\circ(z)|>0,
 \qquad
 N_{C(M_\circ)}(M_\circ)\ge1.
\]
Consider the compact-open neighborhood
\[
 \mathcal U(M_\circ)
 =\left\{\widetilde M\in\mathscr M:
   \sup_{z\in C(M_\circ)}|\widetilde M(z)-M_\circ(z)|
   <\frac{m(M_\circ)}2
  \right\}.
\]
For \(\widetilde M\in\mathcal U(M_\circ)\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \inf_{z\in C(M_\circ)}|\widetilde M(z)|
 \ge\frac{m(M_\circ)}2.
\]
Moreover, on the boundary the straight-line homotopy between \(M_\circ\) and \(\widetilde M\) never meets zero.  Its winding number about zero is therefore constant, and the argument principle yields
\[
 N_{C(M_\circ)}(\widetilde M)
 =N_{C(M_\circ)}(M_\circ)\ge1.
\]

The neighborhoods \(\mathcal U(M_\circ)\) cover the compact set \(\mathscr M\).  Select a finite subcover centered at \(M_1,\ldots,M_J\), put
\[
 \rho_j=\rho(M_j),
 \qquad
 a_\star=\frac12\min_{1\le j\le J}m(M_j)>0,
\]
and let \(C_j=\{|z|=\rho_j\}\).  Every admissible treatment transform belongs to some \(\mathcal U(M_j)\), and for that index
\[
 N_{C_j}(M)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_j}|M(z)|\ge a_\star.
\]
If the admissible transform class is empty, the conclusion is vacuous and one may take any single radius in \((R_0,R_1)\) and any positive \(a_\star\).  Otherwise the construction above gives \(J\ge1\).  Only \(k,\delta,\psi_\eta,R_0,R_1\) enter this construction, so its bank and modulus do not depend on \(n\).
