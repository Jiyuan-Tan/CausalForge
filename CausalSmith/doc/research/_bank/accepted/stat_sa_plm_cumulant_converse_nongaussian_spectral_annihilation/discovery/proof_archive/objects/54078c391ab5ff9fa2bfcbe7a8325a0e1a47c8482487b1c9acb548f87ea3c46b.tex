The deterministic codes, their clipped versions, and the radii are external inputs shared only as notation; the two branches are separate law classes.

Along the sequence of non-Gaussian experiments in the statement,
\(\text{thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}\) supplies constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
 \frac cn\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\le\frac Cn.
\]
Therefore \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\asymp n^{-1}\).  The same theorem identifies
\(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) as the upper-bound witness and shows that its guarantee remains valid for a fixed nonzero
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\).  By
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\), the statistic uses \(\bar g_n\) through \(Z_n\) but never uses \(\widehat q_n\) or \(\bar q_n\).  By
\(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\), membership in the broad class likewise contains no condition involving \(\widehat q_n\), \(\bar q_n\), or
\(\varepsilon_{2,n}\).  This proves the non-Gaussian branch, including the asserted common-input scope.

For the Gaussian branch,
\(\text{prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy}\) proves that every
\(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) has \(\theta_0(P)=0\).  Whenever the intersection is nonempty, the same proposition applies the zero statistic to the losses in \(\text{def:minimax-risks}\) and gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}
 =\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}
 =0.
\]
A strictly positive nuisance-product lower bound is therefore false on this displayed intersection.  The conclusion follows from its simultaneous bounded-outcome and Gaussian conditional-mean restrictions, not from Jin--Mackey--Syrgkanis Theorem 3.2.  No equality or inclusion between the two branches was used.
