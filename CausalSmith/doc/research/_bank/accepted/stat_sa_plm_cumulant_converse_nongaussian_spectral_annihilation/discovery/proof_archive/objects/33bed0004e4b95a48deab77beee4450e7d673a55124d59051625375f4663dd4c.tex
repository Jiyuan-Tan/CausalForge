We prove the upper bound, the matching lower bound, and the generalized-quantile consequence.  Every constant below is uniform over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and may depend only on the fixed constants displayed in the theorem.

\emph{Step 1: a population-good contour and a regular empirical event.}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  By \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\), some \(j_0\in\{1,\ldots,J\}\) satisfies
\[
 N_{C_{j_0}}(M)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_0}}|M(z)|\ge a_{\star}.
\]
The hypothesis \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\) and \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) give \(|H_n|\ge3/4\) on \(\{|z|\le R_1\}\), show that \(H_n\) is zero-free there, and show that multiplication by \(H_n\) preserves zeros and multiplicities.  Therefore
\[
 N_{C_{j_0}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_0}}|F_n(z)|\ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4. \tag{1}
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), introduce
\[
 \Delta_{F,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
 |\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{G,a}=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}
 |\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|
\]
and the event
\[
 \mathcal E_n=
 \{\Delta_{F,0}\le a_{\star}/8,\ 
   \Delta_{F,1}\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
For \(n\ge2\), both half-sample sizes are at least \(n/3\).  Applying \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) to both halves and Markov's inequality at the fixed threshold \(a_{\star}/8\) yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}P(\mathcal E_n^c)
 \le \frac{K_0}{n}. \tag{2}
\]

\emph{Step 2: stability of the finite-bank selector.}
On \(\mathcal E_n\), equation \((1)\) implies
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_{j_0}}|\widehat F_{0,n}(z)|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8.
\]
The straight-line homotopy from \(F_n\) to \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) is zero-free on \(C_{j_0}\), because its boundary modulus is at least \(3a_{\star}/4-a_{\star}/8>0\).  Its winding number is consequently constant.  Thus \(j_0\) is pilot-admissible.  The maximizing rule in \(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\) selects an index \(\widehat j_n\) satisfying
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{0,n}(z)|
 \ge\frac{5a_{\star}}8. \tag{3}
\]
It follows that
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_n(z)|
 \ge\frac{a_{\star}}2. \tag{4}
\]
The homotopy from \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) to \(F_n\) is zero-free on the selected circle by \((3)\), so
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}
 =N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)\ge1. \tag{5}
\]
Equations \((4)\) and the evaluation-half part of \(\mathcal E_n\) also give
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{1,n}(z)|
 \ge\frac{3a_{\star}}8>\frac{a_{\star}}4, \tag{6}
\]
so the evaluation fallback is not used.  The selector is measurable because the bank is finite, each boundary infimum can be taken over a fixed countable dense parametrization of its circle, every contour integral has a fixed circle parametrization, and all ties and failures are resolved by the deterministic rules in \(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\).

\emph{Step 3: uniform upper risk.}
On the selected contour, \((4)\) gives boundary zero-freeness of \(F_n\), \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) gives zero-freeness of \(H_n\) on the enclosed disk, and \((5)\) gives a positive zero count.  Hence \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) applies.  Write
\[
 N=N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)
   =\widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}.
\]
The class restrictions give a finite uniform constant \(K_G\) such that
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|
 \le E_P[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]
 \le K_G. \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and \(|D_n|\le2C_g\).  Cauchy--Schwarz, the range restrictions, and the sub-Gaussian bounds on \(\eta\) and \(\xi\) bound the last expectation by a constant depending only on \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},R_1\).

For every \(z\in C_{\widehat j_n}\), equations \((4)\), \((6)\), and \((7)\) imply
\[
 \left|
  \frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}
  -\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}
 \right|
 \le
 \frac{8}{3a_{\star}}\Delta_{G,1}
 +\frac{16K_G}{3a_{\star}^2}\Delta_{F,1}. \tag{8}
\]
Since \(N\ge1\), every bank radius is at most \(R_1\), and taking the real part and projecting onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase distance from the real number \(\theta_0(P)\), the exact contour identity and \((8)\) yield
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|
 \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}
 \le R_1\left{
   \frac{8}{3a_{\star}}\Delta_{G,1}
   +\frac{16K_G}{3a_{\star}^2}\Delta_{F,1}
 \right}. \tag{9}
\]
The uniform \(L^2\) transform lemma applied to \((9)\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P\!\left[
  (\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2
  \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}
 \right]
 \le\frac{K_1}{n}. \tag{10}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n^c\), both the estimator and the target lie in \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\), so their squared distance is at most \(4C_{\theta}^2\).  Combining \((2)\) and \((10)\) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac Cn. \tag{11}
\]
The statistic is a single data-driven witness: it uses only \(\bar g_n\), its bank depends only on fixed class constants, and it does not know the law of \(\eta\).  Therefore \(\text{def:minimax-risks}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]. \tag{12}
\]

\emph{Step 4: the matching two-point lower bound.}
Use the submodel in \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\), whose outcome innovation has a fixed variance \(\tau^2>0\).  Choose a fixed \(h>0\), depending only on the class constants, small enough that the two parameters
\[
 \vartheta_0=-\frac{h}{2\sqrt n},
 \qquad
 \vartheta_1=\frac{h}{2\sqrt n}
\]
belong to the submodel for every \(n\ge2\).  Their separation is \(d=h/\sqrt n\).  Conditional on \((X,T)\), the outcome laws are normal with common variance \(\tau^2\) and means differing by \(d\eta\).  Hence their one-observation Hellinger affinity is
\[
 A_1=E_P\!\left[\exp\{-d^2\eta^2/(8\tau^2)\}\right]
 \ge \exp\{-d^2E_P[\eta^2]/(8\tau^2)\}, \tag{13}
\]
where Jensen's inequality was used.  Affinity tensorizes under independent products.  Since the treatment sub-Gaussian bound implies \(E_P[\eta^2]\le K_2\psi_{\eta}^2\),
\[
 A_n=A_1^n
 \ge \exp\{-h^2K_2\psi_{\eta}^2/(8\tau^2)\}
 =:a_0>0. \tag{14}
\]
For dominated laws with affinity \(A\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to \((\sqrt{p_0}-\sqrt{p_1})(\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt{p_1})\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0,P_1)\le\sqrt{1-A^2}. \tag{15}
\]
Turn an arbitrary estimator into the test selecting the nearer of \(\vartheta_0\) and \(\vartheta_1\).  On a testing error, its squared estimation error is at least \(d^2/4\).  The elementary two-point testing identity, \((14)\), \((15)\), and \(1-\sqrt{1-u}\ge u/2\) for \(u\in[0,1]\) therefore yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_{v\in\{0,1\}}
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}^{\otimes n}}
 [ (\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 &\ge \frac{d^2}{8}
 \{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n},
                       P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})\}\\
 &\ge \frac{d^2a_0^2}{16}
 =\frac{h^2a_0^2}{16n}. \tag{16}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Both laws belong to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  Taking the infimum over estimators proves \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\ge c/n\).

Finally, set \(W=|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|\).  Equation \((11)\) and Markov's inequality at \(t=\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}\) give \(P(W>t)\le\gamma\).  Thus \(P(W\le t)\ge1-\gamma\), and the definition of the generalized quantile gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}(W)
 \le\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}.
\]
This completes the proof.  In particular, no supplied outcome code, outcome-code radius, or inclusion in the JMS ACE class was used.
