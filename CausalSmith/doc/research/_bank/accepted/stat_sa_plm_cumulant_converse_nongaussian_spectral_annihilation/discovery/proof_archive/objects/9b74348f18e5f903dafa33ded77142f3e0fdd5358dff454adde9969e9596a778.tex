Put \(a=\pi/2\).  A random variable with the stipulated mixture law can be represented as \(\eta=R+E\), where \(R\) is Rademacher, \(E\sim N(0,1)\), and \(R\) and \(E\) are independent.  Therefore
\[
M(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}]=e^{z^2/2}\cosh z,
\qquad
M(ia)=e^{-a^2/2}\cos a=0.
\]
The variable \(\eta\) is centered, \(E_P[\eta^2]=2\), and
\[
E_P[\eta^4]
=E_P[(R+E)^4]
=E_P[R^4]+6E_P[R^2]E_P[E^2]+E_P[E^4]
=1+6+3=10.
\]
The fourth cumulant identity \(\kappa_4(U)=E[U^4]-3E[U^2]^2\) for a centered random variable now gives
\[
\kappa_4(\eta)=10-3\cdot 2^2=-2.
\]

We next establish the denominator identity and its boundary modulus.  The characteristic function of \(\eta\) is
\[
\phi(t)=E_P[e^{it\eta}]=e^{-t^2/2}\cos t.
\]
For every deterministic \(d\in\mathbb R\), symmetry of \(\eta\) and differentiation under the expectation, justified by the Gaussian-mixture moments, imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[(\eta+d)\sin\{t(\eta+d)\}]
&=-\frac{d}{dt}E_P[\cos\{t(\eta+d)\}]\\
&=-\frac{d}{dt}\{\phi(t)\cos(td)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(\phi(a)=0\) and \(\phi'(a)=-e^{-a^2/2}\), evaluation at \(t=a\) yields
\[
E_P[(\eta+d)\sin\{a(\eta+d)\}]
=e^{-a^2/2}\cos(ad).
\]
The independence condition \(\eta\perp X\), and hence independence of \(\eta\) and the \(X\)-measurable variable \(D_n\), permits conditioning on \(D_n\).  Since \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), this gives
\[
E_P[Z_n\sin(aZ_n)]
=e^{-a^2/2}E_P[\cos(aD_n)]
=e^{-\pi^2/8}E_P[\cos(\pi D_n/2)].
\]

By construction \(\bar g_n\) takes values in \([-C_g,C_g]\), and the treatment-regression range assumption gives \(|D_n|\le2C_g\) almost surely.  Because \(s\ge1\) and \(P_X\) is a probability measure, the treatment-code radius and H\"older's inequality give
\[
E_P|D_n|\le \|D_n\|_{L^s(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
The elementary inequality \(1-\cos u\le |u|\) therefore implies
\[
E_P[\cos(aD_n)]
\ge1-aE_P|D_n|
\ge1-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac1\pi
=\frac12.
\]
Thus the certified population denominator lower bound is
\[
c_0:=\frac12e^{-\pi^2/8}>0.
\]
This is the required overlap, relevance, and division condition; the estimator's cutoff ensures that it never divides by an empirical denominator smaller than \(c_0/2\).

It remains to prove the uniform risk bound.  Define
\[
W_i:=Z_{n,i}\sin(aZ_{n,i}),
\qquad
V_i:=\{Y_i-\theta_0(P)Z_{n,i}\}\sin(aZ_{n,i}),
\]
and write \(\bar W_n=\mathbb P_nW\) and \(\bar V_n=\mathbb P_nV\).  From the definitions of \(b_n\), \(Z_n\), and \(\xi\),
\[
Y-\theta_0(P)Z_n=b_n(X)+\xi,
\qquad
b_n(X)=q_0(X)-\theta_0(P)D_n.
\]
Also, for every \(d\),
\[
E_P[\sin\{a(\eta+d)\}]
=\operatorname{Im}\{e^{iad}\phi(a)\}=0.
\]
Consequently, treatment-noise independence gives
\[
E_P[b_n(X)\sin(aZ_n)]=0.
\]
Because \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) is measurable with respect to \((X,T)\), outcome mean independence gives
\[
E_P[\xi\sin(aZ_n)]
=E_P\!\left[E_P[\xi\mid X,T]\sin(aZ_n)\right]=0.
\]
Hence \(E_P[V_i]=0\).

All second moments used below are uniform.  Indeed, with
\[
b_{\max}:=C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g,
\]
the support conditions give \(|b_n(X)|\le b_{\max}\).  The conditional sub-Gaussian assumption implies \(E_P[\xi^2]\le K_{\xi}\psi_{\xi}^2\) for an absolute constant \(K_{\xi}<\infty\) fixed by the convention for the \(\psi_2\)-norm.  Since \(|\sin u|\le1\),
\[
E_P[V_i^2]
\le2b_{\max}^2+2K_{\xi}\psi_{\xi}^2
=:K_V.
\]
Likewise,
\[
E_P[W_i^2]
\le E_P[Z_n^2]
\le2E_P[\eta^2]+2E_P[D_n^2]
\le4+8C_g^2
=:K_W.
\]
These bounds also verify every integrability condition used in the conditioning, differentiation, and variance steps above.

Let \(\mathcal A_n=\{\bar W_n\ge c_0/2\}\), the event on which the sample ratio is used.  On \(\mathcal A_n\), projection onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase distance from \(\theta_0(P)\), and therefore
\[
|\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}-\theta_0(P)|^2
\le \frac{\bar V_n^2}{\bar W_n^2}
\le \frac{4\bar V_n^2}{c_0^2}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_n^c\), both the clipped estimator and the target lie in \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\), so the squared loss is at most \(4C_{\theta}^2\).  If \(m_W=E_P[W_i]\), the denominator calculation gives \(m_W\ge c_0\); hence the independent and identically distributed sampling assumption and Chebyshev's inequality yield
\[
P(\mathcal A_n^c)
\le P\!\left(|\bar W_n-m_W|\ge\frac{c_0}{2}\right)
\le\frac{4\operatorname{Var}_P(W_i)}{nc_0^2}
\le\frac{4K_W}{nc_0^2}.
\]
The same sampling assumption and \(E_P[V_i]=0\) give
\[
E_P[\bar V_n^2]=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(V_i)}{n}\le\frac{K_V}{n}.
\]
Combining the regular and fallback events proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
&\le \frac{4K_V}{nc_0^2}
+4C_{\theta}^2\frac{4K_W}{nc_0^2}\\
&=\frac{4K_V+16C_{\theta}^2K_W}{c_0^2}\,\frac1n.
\end{aligned}
\]
The displayed constant depends only on \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\), not on \(P\), \(n\), the supplied outcome code, or \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\).  This establishes the asserted uniform root-\(n\) mean-squared-error rate, including all support, positivity, overlap, and boundary conditions.
