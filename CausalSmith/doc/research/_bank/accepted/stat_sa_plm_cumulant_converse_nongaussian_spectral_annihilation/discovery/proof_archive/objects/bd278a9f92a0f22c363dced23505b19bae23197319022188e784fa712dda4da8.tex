Fix a zero \(z_0\) of multiplicity \(\ell\).  Since \(M\) is an entire moment transform, differentiation under the expectation is valid and, for every integer \(j\geq 0\),
\[
M^{(j)}(z)=E_P\!\left[\eta^j e^{z\eta}\right].
\]
Indeed, for \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), sub-Gaussianity of \(\eta\) gives finite exponential moments of every real order and hence an integrable dominating function for each derivative on every compact subset of \(\mathbb C\).  The multiplicity condition is therefore
\[
M^{(j)}(z_0)=0\quad(0\leq j<\ell),
\qquad
M^{(\ell)}(z_0)\neq 0.
\]
For an arbitrary real \(d\), the binomial identity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P\!\left[J_{z_0,\ell}(\eta+d)\right]
&=E_P\!\left[(\eta+d)^{\ell-1}e^{z_0(\eta+d)}\right]\\
&=e^{z_0d}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
  {\ell-1\choose j}d^{\ell-1-j}
  E_P\!\left[\eta^j e^{z_0\eta}\right]\\
&=e^{z_0d}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
  {\ell-1\choose j}d^{\ell-1-j}M^{(j)}(z_0)
=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves exact annihilation for every shift, including shifts on the boundary of the support of \(D_n\); no support-interior or overlap condition is used.

Now fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  The random variable \(D_n=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\) is measurable with respect to \(X\), and \(\eta\perp X\).  Thus the conditional law of \(\eta\) given \(X\) equals its marginal law, and substitution of \(d=D_n\) in the preceding identity yields, componentwise for the complex conditional expectation,
\[
E_P\!\left[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\mid X\right]
=E_P\!\left[J_{z_0,\ell}(\eta+D_n)\mid X\right]
=0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Applying the same calculation with exponent \(\ell\) rather than \(\ell-1\), and again conditioning on \(X\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P\!\left[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\mid X\right]
&=E_P\!\left[(\eta+D_n)^\ell e^{z_0(\eta+D_n)}\mid X\right]\\
&=e^{z_0D_n}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose j}
  D_n^{\ell-j}M^{(j)}(z_0)\\
&=e^{z_0D_n}M^{(\ell)}(z_0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking expectations and using the definition \(H_n(z)=E_P[e^{zD_n}]\),
\[
E_P\!\left[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\right]
=M^{(\ell)}(z_0)H_n(z_0).
\]

It remains to establish the outcome moment.  From the residual decomposition,
\[
Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi.
\]
The first contamination term vanishes by the conditional annihilation already proved:
\[
E_P\!\left[b_n(X)J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\right]
=E_P\!\left[b_n(X)E_P\!\left[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\mid X\right]\right]
=0.
\]
Because \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\), the variable \(J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\) is measurable with respect to \(\sigma(X,T)\).  The outcome mean-independence assumption consequently gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P\!\left[\xi J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\right]
&=E_P\!\left[
J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)E_P[\xi\mid X,T]
\right]
=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
E_P\!\left[YJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\right]
=\theta_0E_P\!\left[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\right].
\]
Whenever \(M^{(\ell)}(z_0)H_n(z_0)\neq0\), division is valid and proves
\[
\theta_0
=\frac{E_P[YJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]}
       {E_P[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]}.
\]
This ratio is an observed-population functional because \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) and the instrument is fixed once \((z_0,\ell)\) is supplied; its equality to the causal coefficient uses the independent-treatment-noise and outcome mean-independence assumptions.  All displayed expectations are finite: \(|D_n|\leq2C_g\), \(b_n\) is bounded, and the sub-Gaussian bounds on \(\eta\) and \(\xi\) dominate every fixed complex-exponential polynomial weight appearing above.  The sole rank condition is the explicitly stated nonvanishing denominator; no positivity or treatment-overlap condition is required.
