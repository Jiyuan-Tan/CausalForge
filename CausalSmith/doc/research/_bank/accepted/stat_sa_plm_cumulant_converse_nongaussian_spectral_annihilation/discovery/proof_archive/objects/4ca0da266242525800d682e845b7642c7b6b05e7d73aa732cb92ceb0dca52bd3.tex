Write \(\psi=\psi_{\eta}\).  The exact Luxemburg premise gives \(E_P\exp(\eta^2/\psi^2)\le2\), and centering gives \(E_P\eta=0\).  Expanding the exponential, bounding the moments by integration of
\(P(|\eta|>u)\le2e^{-u^2/\psi^2}\), and pairing the positive and negative terms yields the elementary centered transform bound
\[
 |M(z)|\le E_Pe^{|z||\eta|},
 \qquad
 \log |M(z)|\le4\psi^2|z|^2,
 \quad z\in\mathbb C.
 \tag{1}
\]
The constant four is deliberately retained; it is valid under precisely the displayed Luxemburg normalization.

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(M\) has no zero in the closed disk of radius \(R_0\).  Since \(M(0)=1\), there is an analytic logarithm \(f=\log M\) on a neighborhood of that disk with \(f(0)=0\) and \(f'(0)=E_P\eta=0\).  For \(0<R<R_0\), (1) gives \(\sup_{|z|\le R}\operatorname{Re}f(z)\le4\psi^2R^2\).  Applying the Cauchy formula to the positive-real-part function \(4\psi^2R^2-f\) gives the coefficient estimate
\[
 |f^{(k)}(0)|\le 2k!R^{-k}\,4\psi^2R^2
 =8k!\psi^2R^{2-k}.
 \tag{2}
\]
(This estimate follows directly by inserting the Poisson--Cauchy representation and taking the \(k\)-th coefficient.)  Letting \(R\uparrow R_0\), using \(f^{(k)}(0)=\kappa_k(\eta)\), and the separation assumption yields
\[
 \delta\le8k!\psi^2R_0^{2-k}.
\]
But the authoritative choice
\[
 R_0=A_k(\psi^2/\delta)^{1/(k-2)},
 \qquad A_k=(2^{k+4}k!)^{1/(k-2)},
\]
makes the right side equal to \(2^{-k-1}\delta<\delta\), a contradiction.  Thus a zero lies in \(|z|\le R_0\).  The displayed equality for \(R_0\) in particular implies the claimed inequality with this finite \(A_k\), which depends only on \(k\).
