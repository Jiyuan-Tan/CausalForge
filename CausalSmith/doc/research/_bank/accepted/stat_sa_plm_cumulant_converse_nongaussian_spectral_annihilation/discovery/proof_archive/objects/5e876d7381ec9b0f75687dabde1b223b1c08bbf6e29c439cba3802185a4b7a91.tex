The fixed-separated theorem uses a finite contour bank obtained nonconstructively by compactness. Its radii, cardinality, and boundary modulus are not computed or effectively enumerable, although one arbitrary bank is fixed once for all laws and sample sizes before the measurable sample map is defined. As \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), zero localization alone does not control the bank's conditioning or the selector's variance, so the sharp local-to-Gaussian rate, a uniform limit law and coverage theorem, and matching local lower bounds remain open. The formal Gaussian intersection is degenerate and therefore cannot be used to fill that missing local converse.
