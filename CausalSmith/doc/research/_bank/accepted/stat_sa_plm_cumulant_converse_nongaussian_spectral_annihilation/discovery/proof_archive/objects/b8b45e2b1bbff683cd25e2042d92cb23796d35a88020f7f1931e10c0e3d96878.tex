Fix \(n\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  All norms in the
first part of the proof are taken with respect to the probability measure
\(P_X\).  The code errors are measurable and bounded: \(g_0\) and
\(\bar g_n\) take values in \([-C_g,C_g]\), while \(q_0\) and
\(\bar q_n\) take values in \([-C_q,C_q]\).  Thus the norms below are
finite, including at the endpoint \(s=\infty\).

Suppose first that \(r\le s<\infty\).  For any measurable \(f\), Jensen's
inequality applied to the concave map \(u\mapsto u^{r/s}\) gives
\[
 E_P[|f(X)|^r]
 =E_P\!\left[(|f(X)|^s)^{r/s}\right]
 \le \left(E_P[|f(X)|^s]\right)^{r/s}.
\]
Taking \(r\)-th roots yields
\[
 \|f\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \|f\|_{L^s(P_X)}.                 \tag{1}
\]
If \(s=\infty\), then
\(|f(X)|\le\|f\|_{L^\infty(P_X)}\) almost surely, and integration
followed by an \(r\)-th root proves (1) again.  The assumed inequality
\(s\ge r+1\) therefore suffices; in fact, this step only needs
\(s\ge r\).

Apply (1) to \(f=\bar g_n-g_0\) and to
\(f=\bar q_n-q_0\).  The two radius conditions in
\(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\) imply
\[
 \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n},
 \qquad
 \|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}.       \tag{2}
\]
These are exactly the two code restrictions in
\(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\).  Every remaining member condition in that
definition---mean exclusion, the target and nuisance range restrictions,
the two sub-Gaussian restrictions, independence of \(\eta\) and \(X\),
and the order-\(r+1\) cumulant separation---is also a member condition in
\(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\).  Hence (2) and the two class
definitions prove the one-way inclusion
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}
 \subseteq \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}.  \tag{3}
\]

The norm implication alone does not prove equality.  Indeed, on any
probability space containing an event \(A\) with
\(0<P_X(A)<1\), for \(c\ne0\) the bounded function
\(f=c\mathbf 1_A\) obeys
\[
 \frac{\|f\|_{L^s(P_X)}}{\|f\|_{L^r(P_X)}}
 =P_X(A)^{1/s-1/r}>1
\]
when \(s<\infty\), whereas for \(s=\infty\) the ratio is
\(P_X(A)^{-1/r}>1\).  Thus no reverse implication with the same radius
follows from norm monotonicity.  This establishes precisely the claimed
one-way alignment without asserting equality of the two base classes.

It remains to establish the published comparator bound with its exact
constants.  Assume \(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\).  By
\(\text{def:jms-eligibility}\), every logarithm and denominator in the
eligibility display is defined.  Since \(a_{1,n}\) contains
\(\log\{6(C_g+\psi_\eta)\varepsilon_{1,n}^{-1}\}\), this antecedent
in particular excludes \(\varepsilon_{1,n}=0\).  Define the
nonnegative quantities
\[
 A_n=\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r}\varepsilon_{2,n}
       +C_\theta\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
\]
and
\[
 V_n=(C_g+\psi_\eta)^r
 \left\{r^2(C_g+\psi_\eta)+\psi_\xi+C_\theta\psi_\eta\right\}
 (\gamma n)^{-1/2}.
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\), the corrected cited specialization
\(\text{lem:jms-ace-theorem-five-four}\), with condition (21) encoded by
\(\mathsf E_n^{\mathrm{JMS}}\), gives the exact bound (22)
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
 \le C_\gamma r!16^r\delta^{-1}(A_n+64V_n).             \tag{4}
\]
There is no change of constants in this step.

For completeness, suppose \(\varepsilon_{2,n}=0\).  For each
\(u\in(0,\varepsilon_{1,n})\), enlarge only the outcome-code ball at
index \(n\) from radius zero to radius \(u\).  The original uncertainty
class is contained in this enlarged class.  Moreover, the maximum in
\(b_{2,n}\) is unchanged, because \(u<\varepsilon_{1,n}\), so the same
eligibility predicate holds.  Applying the cited positive-radius bound
to the enlarged class gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)\\
 &\quad\le C_\gamma r!16^r\delta^{-1}
 \left[\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r}u+C_\theta\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}
 +64V_n\right].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Letting \(u\downarrow0\) proves (4) also at the boundary
\(\varepsilon_{2,n}=0\).

By the definition of \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\), the right-hand side
of (4) is exactly \(B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}\).  Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
 \le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}.                         \tag{5}
\]
The supremum in (5) is over the published base-class and
\(L^r(P_X)\)-uncertainty intersection represented by
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\); eligibility is a
deterministic condition at index \(n\), not an additional property of a
law.  Finally, (3) and (5) give the actionable comparison on the entire
non-Gaussian class:
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}
 \!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|\right)
 \le B_{\mathrm{ACE},n,\gamma}.
\]
