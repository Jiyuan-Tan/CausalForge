For the first conclusion, fix \(n\).  Since \(\delta_n>0\), apply \(\text{lem:jms-ace-theorem-five-four}\) with its positive separation constant replaced by \(\delta_n\).  The resulting law class is exactly \(\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)\), because every other restriction in \(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\) is unchanged.  The quantities \(a_{1,n}\), \(b_{1,n}\), and \(a_2\) are unchanged, whereas the source theorem's \(b_{2,n}\) becomes
\[
 \widetilde b_{2,n}
 =200\min\{1,C_{\theta}\}\delta_n
 \max\!\left\{\varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n},
 (\gamma n)^{-1/2}(\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta})\right\}^{-1}.
\]
Therefore the displayed local eligibility condition is precisely the eligibility condition of the cited theorem after this substitution.  The hypotheses \(\varepsilon_{1,n},\varepsilon_{2,n}>0\), together with the requirement that all logarithms and denominators be defined, supply its positivity and boundary conditions.  The cited theorem consequently gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal C_n(\delta_n)}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}-\theta_0(P)|)\\
 &\quad\le C_{\gamma}r!16^r\delta_n^{-1}
 \Bigl[\varepsilon_{1,n}^r\varepsilon_{2,n}
 +C_{\theta}\varepsilon_{1,n}^{r+1}\\
 &\hspace{35mm}+64(C_g+\psi_{\eta})^r
 \{r^2(C_g+\psi_{\eta})+\psi_{\xi}+C_{\theta}\psi_{\eta}\}
 (\gamma n)^{-1/2}\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]
This is \(U_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{loc}}\).  Taking the smaller of this numerical bound and a separately valid DML bound is only an oracle comparison: no sample-measurable selector or minimax lower bound follows from comparing two upper bounds.

For the second conclusion, every displayed path hypothesis is exactly a hypothesis of \(\text{lem:gaussian-rademacher-l1-benchmark}\), including the direct condition
\[
 \lVert\bar g_n-g_0\rVert_{L^1(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
That lemma therefore gives, without an appeal to Lyapunov's inequality, the formulas for \(M_a\), \(\kappa_4(\eta_a)\), \(t_a\), and \(A_a\); the exact shift-annihilation identity; the denominator lower bound under \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le1/2\); the displayed computable clipped ratio; and its uniform mean-squared-error bound.  Substituting \(\Delta_a=2a^4\) gives
\[
 a=(\Delta_a/2)^{1/4},
 \qquad
 t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac12
 \iff
 \varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi},
\]
and elementary algebra converts the first risk display into
\[
 C\min\!\left\{1,\frac1n\Delta_a^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}\right)\right\},
\]
after enlarging the constant \(C\) by the fixed factor arising from \(a^{-2}=\sqrt2\,\Delta_a^{-1/2}\).  This proves the second conclusion under the stated \(L^1(P_X)\) premise.  The first result is only an ACE upper benchmark and the second concerns one explicit Gaussian--Rademacher path; neither supplies a converse or a uniform theorem over the class defined solely by cumulant separation.  Hence the full minimax phase diagram is not determined.
