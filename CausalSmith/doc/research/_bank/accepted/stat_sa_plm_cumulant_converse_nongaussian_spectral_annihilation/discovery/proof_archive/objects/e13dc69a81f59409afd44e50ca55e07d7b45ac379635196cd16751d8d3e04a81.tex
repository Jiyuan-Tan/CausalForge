Fix \(n\).  First let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\).  Every structural, range, tail, independence, and cumulant condition in \(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\) occurs verbatim in \(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\).  The only condition of the former definition that is not literally displayed with the same exponent in the latter is the direct treatment-code restriction.  Since \(P_X\) is a probability law and \(r\ge2\), H\"older's inequality gives
\[
 \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^1(P_X)}
 \le \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}
 \le \varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
The broad class imposes no supplied outcome-code restriction.  Hence \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), proving
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}. \tag{1}
\]

Now suppose \(s\ge r\) and let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\).  On the probability space \((\mathcal X,\mathcal A,P_X)\), norm monotonicity gives
\[
 \|f\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \|f\|_{L^s(P_X)},
\]
with the essential-supremum interpretation when \(s=\infty\).  Apply this inequality to \(f=\bar g_n-g_0\) and to \(f=\bar q_n-q_0\).  The two \(L^s(P_X)\) restrictions in \(\text{def:ace-comparison-subclass}\) therefore imply the two \(L^r(P_X)\) restrictions in \(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\), while all other conditions are inherited from \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  Thus
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}. \tag{2}
\]

Finally suppose \(s=r\).  Inclusion (2) remains valid.  Conversely, (1) places every member of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and its two defining \(L^r(P_X)\) code bounds are exactly the two additional restrictions defining \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) when \(s=r\).  Because the same clipped functions \(\bar g_n\) and \(\bar q_n\) occur in both definitions, this proves the reverse inclusion and hence equality.
