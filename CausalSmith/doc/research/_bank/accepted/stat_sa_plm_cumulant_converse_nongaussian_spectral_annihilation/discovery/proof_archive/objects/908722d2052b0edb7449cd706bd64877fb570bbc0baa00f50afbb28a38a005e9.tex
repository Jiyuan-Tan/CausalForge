Let \(\mathscr M\) be the locally uniform closure appearing in \(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\).  We first verify the compactness and closure facts needed by the finite-subcover argument.

The sub-Gaussian assumption gives a universal constant \(c_{\psi}<\infty\) such that every transform in the original family obeys
\[
 |M(z)|
 \le E[e^{\operatorname{Re}(z)\eta}]
 \le \exp(c_{\psi}\psi_{\eta}^{2}|z|^{2}),
 \qquad z\in\mathbb C.
\]
Thus the family is uniformly bounded on every compact disk.  Cauchy's formula on a slightly larger disk makes it equicontinuous on the smaller disk.  Successive finite-net extraction on disks of integer radius, followed by a diagonal argument, gives a locally uniformly convergent subsequence from every sequence in the original family.

Every locally uniform limit is again the moment generating function of an admissible law.  Indeed, the corresponding laws have uniformly bounded second moments and hence are tight.  Along a weakly convergent subsequence, the common sub-Gaussian envelope makes \(e^{t\eta}\) uniformly integrable for each real \(t\).  Therefore the moment generating function of the weak limit agrees with the locally uniform analytic limit on the real axis, and the identity theorem extends the agreement to \(\mathbb C\).  Centering passes to the limit by uniform integrability.  The sub-Gaussian norm bound is weakly closed: for every \(u>\psi_{\eta}\), Portmanteau's inequality applied to \(x\mapsto e^{x^{2}/u^{2}}\), followed by \(u\downarrow\psi_{\eta}\), gives \(\|\eta_{\infty}\|_{\psi_2}\le\psi_{\eta}\).  Finally, locally uniform convergence implies convergence of every derivative at zero.  Since the \(k\)-th cumulant is a polynomial in the first \(k\) derivatives of an MGF whose value at zero is one,
\[
 |\kappa_k(M_{\infty})|
 =\lim_m|\kappa_k(M_m)|
 \ge\delta.
\]
The same conclusion applies to sequences in the closure by choosing, for their \(m\)-th terms, original-family approximants within \(m^{-1}\) on the disk of radius \(m\).  Since the compact-open topology on entire functions is metrizable, the preceding sequential compactness proves that \(\mathscr M\) is compact.  It also proves that every \(M\in\mathscr M\) satisfies the hypotheses of \(\text{lem:zero-localization}\), so every such \(M\) has a zero in \(\{|z|\le R_0\}\).

Suppose first that \(\mathscr M\neq\varnothing\), and fix \(M_{\circ}\in\mathscr M\).  Because \(M_{\circ}(0)=1\), it is not identically zero, and its zeros are isolated.  It therefore has only finitely many zeros in the compact annulus \(R_0\le |z|\le R_1\).  Choose
\[
 \rho(M_{\circ})\in(R_0,R_1)
\]
which is not the modulus of any of those zeros, and put \(C(M_{\circ})=\{z:|z|=\rho(M_{\circ})\}\).  This circle is zero-free, encloses the zero supplied by \(\text{lem:zero-localization}\), and satisfies
\[
 m(M_{\circ})
 :=\inf_{z\in C(M_{\circ})}|M_{\circ}(z)|>0,
 \qquad
 N_{C(M_{\circ})}(M_{\circ})\ge1.
\]
Define the compact-open neighborhood
\[
 \mathcal U(M_{\circ})
 =\left\{\widetilde M\in\mathscr M:
 \sup_{z\in C(M_{\circ})}
 |\widetilde M(z)-M_{\circ}(z)|<\frac{m(M_{\circ})}{2}
 \right\}.
\]
For \(\widetilde M\in\mathcal U(M_{\circ})\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \inf_{z\in C(M_{\circ})}|\widetilde M(z)|
 \ge\frac{m(M_{\circ})}{2}.
\]
Moreover, for \(t\in[0,1]\), the boundary function \(M_{\circ}+t(\widetilde M-M_{\circ})\) is nonzero.  Its argument-principle integral is continuous in \(t\) and integer-valued, hence constant.  Consequently
\[
 N_{C(M_{\circ})}(\widetilde M)
 =N_{C(M_{\circ})}(M_{\circ})
 \ge1.
\]

The neighborhoods \(\mathcal U(M_{\circ})\) cover the compact set \(\mathscr M\).  Choose a finite subcover centered at \(M_1,\ldots,M_J\), and set
\[
 \rho_j=\rho(M_j),
 \qquad
 a_{\star}=\frac12\min_{1\le j\le J}m(M_j)>0,
 \qquad
 C_j=\{z:|z|=\rho_j\}.
\]
For every \(M\in\mathscr M\), membership in one selected neighborhood gives, for the corresponding \(j\),
\[
 N_{C_j}(M)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{z\in C_j}|M(z)|\ge a_{\star}.
\]
Every treatment-noise transform represented in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) belongs to the original family and hence to \(\mathscr M\), so the conclusion holds uniformly in \(n\).  If the original family is empty, take \(J=1\), any \(\rho_1\in(R_0,R_1)\), and any \(a_{\star}>0\); the conclusion is then vacuous.

Only \(k,\delta,\psi_{\eta},R_0,R_1\) enter this existence argument.  The finite subcover and its radii are nonconstructive.  Once any resulting tuple is fixed, it is deterministic and independent of \(n\), \(P\), and the sample, but no effective enumeration, numerical modulus, or algorithm for finding it has been proved.
