The two branches are separate law classes that share the displayed deterministic inputs.  Along every indicated sequence of non-Gaussian indices, \(\text{thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}\) supplies constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
 \frac cn\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\le\frac Cn.
\]
The only supplied-code restriction in that theorem is
\[
 \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^1(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0.
\]
It therefore permits a fixed positive treatment-code radius and does not constrain or use any supplied outcome code.  This proves \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\asymp n^{-1}\).  The definition of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) restores the \(L^s(P_X)\) treatment- and outcome-code restrictions only on the narrower comparator interface; it does not alter the minimax conclusion on \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).

Separately, for every sufficiently large index satisfying the Gaussian hypotheses, \(\text{thm:gaussian-quantile-to-mse}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}\ge\gamma L_{\mathrm G,n,\gamma}^2.
\]
Substitution of the definition of \(L_{\mathrm G,n,\gamma}\) yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}
 \ge\gamma c_{\gamma}^2
 \left[
  \sigma^{-2}\varepsilon_{1,n}\varepsilon_{2,n}
  \left\{\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon_{1,n})}{\log n}\right\}^3
  +\sigma^{-1}n^{-1/2}
 \right]^2.
\]
This invocation leaves unchanged the bounded-outcome Gaussian class, its known variance, its positivity and logarithmic conditions, its generalized-quantile convention, and the common deterministic codes.  No equality or inclusion between the Gaussian and non-Gaussian classes is used.
