The first conclusion is an immediate specialization of \(\text{thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}\).  By \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\), that theorem selects one deterministic witness bank once and for all and fixes it independently of \(n\), \(P\), and the sample.  For every index satisfying \(n\ge2\), \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\neq\varnothing\), and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), it supplies constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on the displayed fixed class constants and the selected witness, such that
\[
 \frac{c}{n}
 \le \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
 \le \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P\!\left[
   \{\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)\}^{2}
 \right]
 \le \frac{C}{n}.
\]
The two positive constants do not depend on \(n\) along the stated sequence, so the first and last inequalities are exactly \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\asymp n^{-1}\).  The restriction \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\) permits a constant positive sequence, so no convergence of the treatment-code error to zero is required.

The asserted input restrictions follow by inspection of the declared objects, and this inspection is why both definitions belong to the proof spine.  The class in \(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\) constrains \(\bar g_n\) through the direct \(L^1(P_X)\) radius \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\), but its membership conditions contain neither \(\widehat q_n\), \(\bar q_n\), nor \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\).  After the witness bank has been fixed, \(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\) constructs \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) from the two empirical transforms of \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) and \(Y\), together with the fixed bank.  Its formula likewise contains no supplied outcome code or outcome-code radius.  The finite-subcover argument in \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\) is existential, so neither this theorem nor the estimator definition supplies an algorithm for obtaining the bank, its radii, or \(a_{\star}\).

For the second, separate conclusion, fix \(n\) and \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\).  Proposition \(\text{prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy}\), applied to the class in \(\text{def:gaussian-class}\), gives
\[
 \theta_0(P)=0.
\]
Its proof uses the simultaneous bounded-observed-outcome restriction, the exact conditional-mean restriction, and \(\eta\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma>0\): boundedness of \(Y\) bounds \(E_P[Y\mid X,T]\), whereas \(q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta\) has unbounded support when \(\theta_0\neq0\).  Thus the conclusion is a compatibility diagnostic for the simultaneous source assumptions and does not use the source's positive lower-bound claim.

If \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\neq\varnothing\), the same proposition, together with the loss definitions in \(\text{def:minimax-risks}\), shows that the constant-zero statistic has zero squared error and zero generalized-quantile error at every member.  Nonnegativity of both losses then yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}
 =\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}
 =0.
\]
No overlap, rank, positivity, division, inverse, or limiting argument is used in this second part; nonemptiness is needed only to interpret the displayed minimax criteria on a nonempty class.  Finally, \(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\) and \(\text{def:gaussian-class}\) define distinct classes, and no cited result proves an inclusion or equality between them.  The two conclusions are therefore separate facts, not a Gaussian-versus-non-Gaussian minimax dichotomy and not a shrinking-separation phase diagram.
