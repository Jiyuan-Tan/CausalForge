Fix the deterministic codes, their clipped versions, and the nested radii as in the statement.  These are common external inputs, but the two law classes remain distinct.

Along any sequence satisfying \(n\ge2\), \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\ne\varnothing\), and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), the already established \(\text{thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}\) supplies constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
 \frac{c}{n}
 \le \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
 \le \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le \frac{C}{n}. \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\asymp n^{-1}\).  The class definition uses \(\bar g_n\) and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\), but no supplied outcome code; the witnessing estimator uses \(\bar g_n\) only through \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\).  Consequently neither the class restriction nor the estimator uses \(\widehat q_n\), \(\bar q_n\), or \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\).  Equation \((1)\) therefore remains valid when \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\) is a fixed nonzero number not exceeding \(\varepsilon_0\), with arbitrary outcome-code error.

For the separate simultaneous bounded-outcome Gaussian intersection, \(\text{thm:gaussian-quantile-to-mse}\) proves pointwise that
\[
 \theta_0(P)=0
 \qquad
 (P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}). \tag{2}
\]
Whenever that class is nonempty, the same theorem uses the common zero statistic to obtain
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}=0. \tag{3}
\]
No asymptotic restriction on \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\varepsilon_{2,n}\) is needed for \((2)\)--\((3)\).  In particular, this Gaussian intersection cannot supply a positive nuisance-product lower arm for the comparison.  Equations \((1)\) and \((3)\) establish exactly the corrected contrast stated in the theorem; they neither assert nor require inclusion or equality between the two branches.
