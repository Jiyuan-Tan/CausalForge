By \(\text{lem:observable-factorization}\), on the contour \(C_j\),
\[
 \frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}
 =\theta_0\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}
   +\frac{M(z)B_n(z)}{M(z)H_n(z)}
 =\theta_0\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}+\frac{B_n(z)}{H_n(z)}.
\]
The first equality is valid because \(F_n\) is zero-free on \(C_j\).  The quotient \(B_n/H_n\) is analytic on and inside \(C_j\), because both functions are entire and \(H_n\) is zero-free there.  Cauchy's integral theorem therefore gives
\[
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{B_n(z)}{H_n(z)}\,dz=0.
\]
The argument principle and the assumed boundary zero-freeness give
\[
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
 =N_{C_j}(F_n).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
 =\theta_0N_{C_j}(F_n).
\]
The assumed positive zero count licenses division by \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\).  The resulting expression is exactly \(\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n)\) from \(\text{def:contour-functional}\), proving the claim.
