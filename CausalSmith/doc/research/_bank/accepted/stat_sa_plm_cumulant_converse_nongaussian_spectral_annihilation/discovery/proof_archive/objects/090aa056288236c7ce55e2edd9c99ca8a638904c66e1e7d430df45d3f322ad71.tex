We separate the computation audit, the upper bound, the converse, and the maximal comparison scope.

\emph{Canonical computation.}  The bank algorithm in \(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\) terminates by \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\) and uses only the canonical certified names of \((k,\delta,\psi_{\eta},R_0,R_1)\).  Given the represented sample, subtraction, multiplication, absolute value, and the interval Taylor algorithm for the exponential convert the canonical dyadic names of \(T_i,Y_i,\bar g_n(X_i)\) into outward rational enclosures for every finite sum defining \(\widehat F_{a,n}^{(p)}\) and \(\widehat G_{a,n}^{(p)}\).  Refinement makes each enclosure arbitrarily narrow.

For a fixed circle, the derived upper bound \(L^F_{aj}\) is finite.  Hence a finite uniform mesh makes its Lipschitz allowance at most \(a_{\star}/128\), and finite refinement makes the aggregate node error at most \(a_{\star}/128\).  Thus every modulus certificate terminates.  A winding computation is attempted only after a positive rational denominator certificate \(m\) has been obtained.  The displayed derivative bound for \(W_{0j}\) is then finite, so a finite mesh and finite node refinement produce an enclosure of radius less than \(1/4\).  The empirical entire function equals one at zero and is not identically zero.  Its zeros inside the circle are isolated and finite in number.  Factoring them locally and summing the changes of their logarithms shows directly that
\[
 \frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}
 \frac{\widehat F_{0,n}'(z)}{\widehat F_{0,n}(z)}\,dz
\]
is their nonnegative integer count.  Therefore the enclosure contains a unique nonnegative integer once its radius is below \(1/4\).  The finite bank, rational comparisons, and prescribed smallest-index tie-break make pilot selection terminate.  The same argument applies to the evaluation modulus.  If it passes, the displayed derivative bound for \(V\) is finite, so finite refinement reaches the positive target radius \(1/n\).  Every branch returns either the prescribed fallback or one rational midpoint after finitely many operations.

The dyadic name \(\mathcal I_m(x)\) is uniquely determined by \(x\).  Equal primitive constants and equal represented sample values therefore generate the same interval queries, rational trace, and output.  Thus the algorithm is extensional in the represented real values.  For Borel measurability, fix a finite trace.  Each queried canonical endpoint is a floor of a scaled coordinate and hence is Borel; \(\bar g_n(X_i)\) is Borel because \(\bar g_n\) is measurable.  Finite interval operations and each rational branch comparison preserve Borel dependence.  There are only countably many finite traces, and the output on every terminal trace is a Borel rational expression followed by real part and continuous projection.  The countable union over terminal traces proves that \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) is a total Borel measurable observed-sample map.  Inspection of the algorithm shows that it uses \(\bar g_n\), no supplied outcome code, and no treatment-noise law.

\emph{Uniform upper bound.}  Put \(m_a=|I_a|\).  For \(n\ge2\), \(m_a\ge n/3\).  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and choose \(j_{\star}\) from \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\).  By \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\),
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|F_n|
 \ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4. \tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
 d_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \quad
 e_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|,
 \quad
 \mathcal A_a=\{d_a\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\), the certified pilot lower endpoint for \(j_{\star}\) is at least
\[
 \left(\frac34-\frac18-\frac1{64}\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{39}{64}a_{\star}. \tag{2}
\]
The straight-line homotopy from \(F_n\) to \(\widehat F_{0,n}\) is nonzero on \(C_{j_{\star}}\).  Its logarithmic-derivative integral is integer-valued and continuous along the homotopy, so the empirical count equals the positive population count.  Thus \(j_{\star}\) is pilot-admissible.

Let \(C=C_{\widehat j_n}\).  Maximality of the selected certified lower endpoint and (2), followed by subtraction of \(d_0\), imply
\[
 \inf_C|F_n|
 \ge\left(\frac{39}{64}-\frac18\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{31}{64}a_{\star}. \tag{3}
\]
The same homotopy argument on the selected circle gives
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_C(F_n)=:N\ge1. \tag{4}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), (3) gives an evaluation empirical modulus of at least \(23a_{\star}/64\).  Subtracting the enclosure width gives the certified lower endpoint
\[
 \frac{22}{64}a_{\star}>\frac14a_{\star}. \tag{5}
\]
Therefore no fallback occurs on this event.

There is a finite class-uniform constant \(C_G\) such that
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|\le C_G. \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\).  The exact Luxemburg inequality implies
\[
 E_P e^{t|\eta|}
 \le \sqrt2\exp(t^2\psi_{\eta}^2/2),
\]
by \(t|\eta|\le \eta^2/(2\psi_{\eta}^2)+t^2\psi_{\eta}^2/2\).  It also gives uniform second moments for \(\eta\) and \(\xi\).  Cauchy--Schwarz then bounds \(E_P[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]\) using only \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\), and \(R_1\), proving (6) without any independence between \(\xi\) and \(\eta\).

On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), subtracting the two ratios, using (3), (5), and (6), and using \(\operatorname{len}(C)\le2\pi R_1\) gives
\[
 \left|
 \frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C
 \frac{\widehat G_{1,n}(z)}{\widehat F_{1,n}(z)}\,dz
 -
 \frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C
 \frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
 \right|
 \le L_{\star}(d_1+e_1), \tag{7}
\]
where, with \(\mu=31a_{\star}/64\) and \(\nu=23a_{\star}/64\), one may take
\[
 L_{\star}=R_1\max\{\nu^{-1},C_G(\mu\nu)^{-1}\}.
\]
The hypotheses of \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) hold: (3) makes \(F_n\) zero-free on \(C\), \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) makes \(H_n\) zero-free on and inside \(C\), and (4) gives the positive zero count.  Hence the population contour functional in (7) is the causal target \(\theta_0\).  The numerical midpoint adds complex error at most \(1/n\), and taking its real part and projecting onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase distance from \(\theta_0\).

By \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\), uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 E_P(d_a^2+e_a^2)\le\frac{3K}{n},
 \qquad
 P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)
 \le\frac{384K}{a_{\star}^2n}. \tag{8}
\]
On the exceptional event, projection bounds squared error by \(4C_{\theta}^2\).  On the good event, (7), \((d_1+e_1)^2\le2(d_1^2+e_1^2)\), and the numerical error contribute at most \(12L_{\star}^2K/n+2/n^2\) in expectation.  Consequently
\[
 C=12L_{\star}^2K+\frac{1536C_{\theta}^2K}{a_{\star}^2}+2
\]
is valid and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac Cn. \tag{9}
\]
The bank, \(a_{\star}\), \(C_G\), \(K\), and \(L_{\star}\) depend only on the displayed fixed class constants.  The statistic is therefore one admissible data-driven minimax rule, proving \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\le C/n\).

\emph{Converse and generalized-quantile consequence.}  Invoke \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\).  Choose fixed \(h>0\), depending only on the class constants, small enough that \(\vartheta_{\pm}=\pm h/(2\sqrt n)\) belong to the path for every \(n\ge2\) and
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_-^{\otimes n}\Vert P_+^{\otimes n})\le\frac18. \tag{10}
\]
For completeness, the total-variation consequence used below follows directly from (10).  For a likelihood-ratio set \(A\) attaining total variation, the log-sum inequality maps relative entropy to the binary divergence between \(p=P_-(A)\) and \(q=P_+(A)\).  As a function of \(p\), that binary divergence vanishes with zero derivative at \(p=q\) and has second derivative \(1/[p(1-p)]\ge4\).  Taylor's formula therefore gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_-^{\otimes n}\Vert P_+^{\otimes n})
 \ge2(p-q)^2
 =2\|P_-^{\otimes n}-P_+^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^2.
\]
Thus the total variation is at most \(1/4\).  Testing by the sign of an arbitrary estimator gives
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-,+\}}E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}}
 [ (\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 \ge\frac{h^2}{8n}
 \{1-\|P_-^{\otimes n}-P_+^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}
 \ge\frac{3h^2}{32n}.
\]
Taking the supremum over the class and the infimum over estimators proves \(c/n\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\), with \(c=3h^2/32\).

Finally, (9) and Markov's inequality give
\[
 P\!\left(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|>
 \sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}\right)\le\gamma,
\]
which is exactly the displayed generalized-quantile bound.

\emph{Maximal ACE comparison scope.}  By \(\text{lem:jms-ace-class-relations}\),
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}.
\]
If the published ACE class is nonempty, then so is the broad spectral class.  Under the same conditions \(n\ge2\) and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), restriction of the last generalized-quantile inequality to that subset proves the displayed full-class spectral guarantee.  The same lemma proves the stated relation with \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\), while it gives no reverse inclusion of the broad spectral class in the published ACE class.  The constants depend on fixed \(\delta\), so no useful shrinking-\(\delta_n\) rate, cross-regime selector, uniform inference result, or matching local lower bound follows.
