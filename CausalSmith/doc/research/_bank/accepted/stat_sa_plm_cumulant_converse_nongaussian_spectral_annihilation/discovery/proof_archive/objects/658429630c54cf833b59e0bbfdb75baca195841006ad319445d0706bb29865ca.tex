Write \(m_a=|I_a|\) and let \(\mathbb P_a\) denote the empirical average on that half-sample.  The observations within \(I_a\) are independent under \(\text{ass:iid-sampling}\).  Uniformly on \(|z|\le R_1\), the exponential series gives
\[
 \widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)
 =\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^p}{p!}(\mathbb P_a-P)Z_n^p
\]
and
\[
 \widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)
 =\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^p}{p!}(\mathbb P_a-P)(YZ_n^p).
\]
For every square-integrable random variable \(W\), independence and centering give
\[
 \left\|(\mathbb P_a-P)W\right\|_{L^2(P^{\otimes m_a})}
 =m_a^{-1/2}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_P(W)}
 \le m_a^{-1/2}\lVert W\rVert_{L^2(P)}.
\]

Membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), the clipping bound \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), and \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\) imply that, for a class-uniform constant \(C_Z<\infty\),
\[
 \lVert Z_n\rVert_{L^p(P)}\le C_Z\sqrt p,
 \qquad p\ge2.
\]
Also \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\).  The boundedness of \(q_0\) and \(\theta_0\), the treatment sub-Gaussian bound, and the unconditional moment consequence of the conditional sub-Gaussian bound on \(\xi\) give
\[
 \lVert Y\rVert_{L^4(P)}\le C_Y
\]
uniformly over the class.  Consequently, for every integer \(p\ge1\),
\[
 \lVert Z_n^p\rVert_{L^2(P)}
 =\lVert Z_n\rVert_{L^{2p}(P)}^p
 \le(C\sqrt p)^p
\]
and, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \lVert YZ_n^p\rVert_{L^2(P)}
 \le\lVert Y\rVert_{L^4(P)}
     \lVert Z_n\rVert_{L^{4p}(P)}^p
 \le C(C\sqrt p)^p.
\]
The terms at \(p=0\) are bounded by the same conclusion after enlarging \(C\).

Minkowski's inequality now yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left\|\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|\right\|_2
 &\le m_a^{-1/2}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}
   \frac{R_1^p(C\sqrt p)^p}{p!},\\
 \left\|\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|\right\|_2
 &\le Cm_a^{-1/2}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}
   \frac{R_1^p(C\sqrt p)^p}{p!}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here the \(p=0\) summand is interpreted as a separate finite constant.  For \(p\ge1\), the bound \(p!\ge(p/e)^p\) shows that the \(p\)-th summand is at most \((CR_1e/\sqrt p)^p\); hence the series is finite.  The same summable \(L^2\) majorant justifies the preceding termwise operations.  Squaring the two inequalities and enlarging the uniform constant proves
\[
 E_P\!\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|^2\right]
 +E_P\!\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|^2\right]
 \le\frac K{m_a}.
\]
