Fix once and for all any deterministic witness bank supplied by \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\), and write \(m_a=|I_a|\).  For \(n\ge2\), both halves are nonempty and \(m_a\ge n/3\).  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  By the bank lemma there is an index \(j_\star=j_\star(P)\) such that
\[
 N_{C_{j_\star}}(M)\ge1,
 \qquad \inf_{C_{j_\star}}|M|\ge a_\star.
\]
Because \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), \(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) gives \(|H_n|\ge3/4\) on \(|z|\le R_1\), says that \(H_n\) is zero-free there, and says that \(F_n=M H_n\) and \(M\) have the same zeros with multiplicity.  Hence
\[
 N_{C_{j_\star}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad \inf_{C_{j_\star}}|F_n|\ge3a_\star/4. \tag{1}
\]

Put
\[
 d_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \qquad
 e_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|,
\]
and \(\mathcal A_a=\{d_a\le a_\star/8\}\).  On \(\mathcal A_0\), (1) implies \(\inf_{C_{j_\star}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|\ge5a_\star/8\).  Rouch\'e's theorem gives
\[
 \widehat N_{0j_\star,n}=N_{C_{j_\star}}(F_n)\ge1,
\]
so \(j_\star\) is pilot-admissible.  The finite maximizing rule therefore selects \(\widehat j_n\) with
\[
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{0,n}|\ge5a_\star/8,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_n|\ge a_\star/2. \tag{2}
\]
A second application of Rouch\'e's theorem gives
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)\ge1. \tag{3}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), (2) also gives \(\inf_{C_{\widehat j_n}}|\widehat F_{1,n}|\ge3a_\star/8>a_\star/4\), so the evaluation fallback is not used.

Once the bank is fixed, this selector and statistic are measurable.  Indeed, under each fixed circle parameterization, the empirical transforms and their derivatives are jointly measurable in the sample and continuous in the angle.  A boundary infimum equals the infimum over a fixed countable dense set of angles.  On the measurable zero-free event, the winding integral is the integral of a jointly measurable continuous integrand.  Finiteness of the bank and the deterministic tie and failure rules complete the measurability argument.  This proves measurability only conditional on the non-effectively fixed bank; it supplies no procedure for constructing that bank.

There is a uniform \(C_G<\infty\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|\le C_G. \tag{4}
\]
To verify (4), use \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and Cauchy--Schwarz:
\[
 |G_n(z)|\le e^{2C_gR_1}\|Y\|_{L^2(P)}
 \{E_P[e^{2R_1|\eta|}]\}^{1/2}.
\]
The bounds on \(q_0,\theta_0,\eta,\xi\) uniformly bound both factors.

On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), let \(C=C_{\widehat j_n}\) and \(N=N_{C_{\widehat j_n}}(F_n)\).  Equations (2)--(3), zero-freeness of \(H_n\), and \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) give
\[
 \theta_0=\frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz.
\]
The circle length is at most \(2\pi R_1\), \(N\ge1\), and the preceding denominator and numerator bounds imply
\[
 \left|\frac{1}{N2\pi i}\oint_C
 \frac{\widehat G_{1,n}}{\widehat F_{1,n}}\,dz-\theta_0\right|
 \le L_\star(d_1+e_1), \tag{5}
\]
where \(L_\star<\infty\) depends only on the fixed class constants and the fixed witness bank.  Taking real parts and projecting onto \([-C_\theta,C_\theta]\) cannot increase distance from the real number \(\theta_0\).

By \(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) and \(m_a\ge n/3\),
\[
 E_P[d_a^2+e_a^2]\le K_1/n. \tag{6}
\]
Markov's inequality therefore yields
\[
 P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)\le K_2/n. \tag{7}
\]
On this exceptional event clipping bounds the squared error by \(4C_\theta^2\).  Combining (5)--(7) proves, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \le2L_\star^2E_P[d_1^2+e_1^2]
 +4C_\theta^2P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)
 \le C/n. \tag{8}
\]
The witness bank depends only on the fixed class constants, so after any one such bank is fixed, \(C\) has exactly the dependence claimed.  Since this fixed-bank statistic is one sample-measurable decision rule, (8) also gives \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\le C/n\).

For the converse, use \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\).  Let \(c_0,C_0\) be its constants and choose fixed
\[
 0<h\le\min\{c_0,1,(8C_0)^{-1/2}\},
 \qquad \vartheta_\pm=\pm h/(2\sqrt n).
\]
Both laws belong to the submodel, their target separation is \(h/\sqrt n\), and
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{n,\vartheta_-}^{\otimes n}\|P_{n,\vartheta_+}^{\otimes n})\le1/8.
\]
Pinsker's inequality gives total variation at most \(1/4\).  For an arbitrary estimator \(\widetilde\theta\), test with \(A=\{\widetilde\theta\ge0\}\).  On \(A\) under the negative law, or on \(A^c\) under the positive law, squared error is at least \(h^2/(4n)\).  Thus
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-,+\}}E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}}[(\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 \ge \frac{h^2}{8n}\{1-\|P_-^{\otimes n}-P_+^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}
 \ge\frac{3h^2}{32n}.
\]
Taking the class supremum and estimator infimum proves the lower bound.

The statistic uses only \(Y\), \(T-\bar g_n(X)\), and the fixed witness bank, not an outcome code or the law of \(\eta\).  Finally, (8) and Markov's inequality give
\[
 P\{|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0|>\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}\}\le\gamma,
\]
which is the asserted generalized-quantile bound.  The class disclaimer follows from the separate definitions of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\).
