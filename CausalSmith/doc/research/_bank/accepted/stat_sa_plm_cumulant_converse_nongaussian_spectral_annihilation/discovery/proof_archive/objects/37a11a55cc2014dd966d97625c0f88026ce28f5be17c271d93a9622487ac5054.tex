The once-for-all contour bank, its radii, and its conditioning modulus are obtained nonconstructively from compactness and are not claimed to be effectively enumerable or computable from the class constants.  The fixed-separation risk theorem is an existence-level statistical result conditional on one such deterministic bank.  When \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), the present arguments give only zero-radius and nuisance-stability benchmarks; they do not control the contour modulus, construct a uniformly valid adaptive switch, prove a limit law or Wald coverage, or provide matching lower bounds.  The frozen Gaussian class is degenerate and cannot serve as that missing local converse.
