We prove the proposed statement.  Along every indicated non-Gaussian sequence of indices, \(\text{thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}\) gives fixed constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) such that
\[
 \frac cn\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\le\frac Cn.
\]
The constants are uniform in \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\), and the only restriction on the treatment radius is \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\).  This proves \(\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\asymp n^{-1}\), including the admissible case of a fixed nonzero \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\).

Separately, for all sufficiently large indices satisfying the Gaussian hypotheses, \(\text{thm:gaussian-quantile-to-mse}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}\ge\gamma L_{\mathrm G,n,\gamma}^2.
\]
Substitution of the displayed definition of \(L_{\mathrm G,n,\gamma}\) yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}\ge
 \gamma c_{\gamma}^2\left[
 \sigma^{-2}\varepsilon_{1,n}\varepsilon_{2,n}
 \left\{\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon_{1,n})}{\log n}\right\}^{3}
 +\sigma^{-1}n^{-1/2}\right]^2.
\]
Both conclusions use the same deterministic supplied codes and radii, but they concern the two separately defined law classes; no equality or inclusion of those classes is asserted.
