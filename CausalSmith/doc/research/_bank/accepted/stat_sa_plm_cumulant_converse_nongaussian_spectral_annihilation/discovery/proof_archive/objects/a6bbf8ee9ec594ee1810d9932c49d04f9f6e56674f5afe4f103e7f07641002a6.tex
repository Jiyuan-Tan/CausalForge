Let \(z_0\) be a zero of \(M\) of multiplicity \(\ell\).  Thus
\[
M^{(j)}(z_0)=0\quad(0\le j<\ell),
\qquad
M^{(\ell)}(z_0)\ne0.
\]
All differentiations below are legitimate.  Indeed, on \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), sub-Gaussianity of \(\eta\), boundedness of \(D_n\), and conditional sub-Gaussianity of \(\xi\) give every polynomial--exponential moment used below; dominated convergence therefore permits differentiation under the expectation.  Equivalently, for the first identity one may use directly that \(M\) is the displayed entire moment generating function.

Fix \(d\in\mathbb R\) and define \(L_d(z)=E_P[e^{z(\eta+d)}]\).  Since \(d\) is deterministic,
\[
L_d(z)=e^{zd}M(z).
\]
By the derivative representation of an entire moment generating function and the Leibniz rule,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(\eta+d)]
&=E_P[(\eta+d)^{\ell-1}e^{z_0(\eta+d)}]\\
&=L_d^{(\ell-1)}(z_0)\\
&=e^{z_0d}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
  {\ell-1\choose j}d^{\ell-1-j}M^{(j)}(z_0)\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality is precisely the multiplicity condition.  It also covers the boundary case \(\ell=1\), when the sum consists only of \(M(z_0)=0\).

Now fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  By the clipped range of \(\bar g_n\) and the class condition \(\lVert g_0\rVert_\infty\le C_g\),
\[
|D_n|=|g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)|\le2C_g
\quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
In particular, all following conditional expectations exist.  The variable \(D_n\) is measurable with respect to \(X\), and \(\eta\perp X\) by the independent-treatment-noise assumption.  Applying the deterministic-shift identity conditionally with \(d=D_n\) gives
\[
E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\mid X]
=E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(\eta+D_n)\mid X]
=0
\quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]

For relevance, independence of \(\eta\) and \(D_n\) yields, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
F_n(z)=E_P[e^{z(\eta+D_n)}]
=M(z)H_n(z).
\]
Because \(Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)=Z_n^\ell e^{z_0Z_n}\), differentiation and then the Leibniz rule give
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]
&=F_n^{(\ell)}(z_0)\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose j}
  M^{(j)}(z_0)H_n^{(\ell-j)}(z_0)\\
&=M^{(\ell)}(z_0)H_n(z_0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every summand with \(j<\ell\) vanishes by multiplicity, and \(j=\ell\) is the only remaining summand.  Since \(M^{(\ell)}(z_0)\ne0\), the scalar relevance condition is equivalently \(H_n(z_0)\ne0\); no overlap or additional rank condition is used.

Finally, put \(b_n(X)=q_0(X)-\theta_0D_n\).  The residual definitions give the structural decomposition
\[
Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi.
\]
The boundedness assumptions on \(q_0\), \(\theta_0\), and \(D_n\) ensure that \(b_n\) is bounded.  Hence the conditional annihilation just proved implies
\[
E_P[b_n(X)J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]
=E_P\!\left[b_n(X)E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\mid X]\right]
=0.
\]
Moreover, \(J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\) is measurable with respect to \((X,T)\), because \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\).  The outcome-mean-independence assumption therefore gives
\[
E_P[\xi J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]
=E_P\!\left[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)E_P[\xi\mid X,T]\right]
=0.
\]
Multiplying the structural decomposition by \(J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\) and taking expectations now yields
\[
E_P[YJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]
=\theta_0E_P[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)].
\]
Whenever the displayed denominator is nonzero, division is valid and proves
\[
\theta_0
=\frac{E_P[YJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]}
        {E_P[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]}.
\]
Thus the moment ratio is an identifying functional for the causal coefficient under the stated structural assumptions and the explicit nonzero-relevance boundary condition; the equality is not imposed by definition.
