\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}sa\_\allowbreak{}plm\_\allowbreak{}cumulant\_\allowbreak{}converse}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} nongaussian\_\allowbreak{}spectral\_\allowbreak{}annihilation\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For fixed nonzero cumulant separation and one experiment-wide certified primitive-name record, a translated-dyadic contour bank and a total ordinary-sample Borel statistic estimate the partially linear causal coefficient with uniform matched \(\Theta(n^{-1})\) mean-squared error using only a sufficiently accurate direct \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment code, no outcome learner, and no knowledge of the treatment-noise law. If the explicit bounded-build complex interval substrate is implemented and compiled, the same value is additionally returned by a represented-data transducer using the identical fixed primitive records and canonical observation records. The full published ACE class has the same matched class-minimax order, while the estimator comparison remains upper-versus-upper. The bounded-outcome Gaussian calculation is only a source-assumption diagnostic forcing \(\theta_0=0\).

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Finite-order orthogonal and ACE methods provide nuisance-dependent upper guarantees, while classical location-family work recognizes transform zeros; neither supplies the present learned-residual contour estimator with an explicit translated-dyadic bank and matched fixed-separation minimax MSE. The published ACE class embeds in the broad direct-\(L^1(P_X)\) spectral class, licensing a full-class spectral upper guarantee but not an ACE-estimator lower bound. The remaining implementation obligation is explicit: the displayed complex certified interval algorithms must be concretely added and compiled before any represented-data computability conclusion is asserted.

\paragraph{Niche.} Independent cumulant-separated sub-Gaussian treatment noise creates an exact null space for every location shift induced by treatment-code error. A sufficiently small fixed direct \(L^1(P_X)\) residual contamination preserves observable zero geometry. One fixed supplied primitive-name record determines the same law-blind bank for all \(n\); both the ordinary Borel interpretation and the conditional future transducer use that identical record and canonicalize only observation coordinates.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves exact annihilator and observable contour identities, the numerical zero radius, an explicit finite translated-dyadic bank with a positive dyadic modulus, empirical-transform control, a total Borel statistic, and matching \(c/n\) and \(C/n\) MSE bounds on the broad spectral class and full published ACE class. It also specifies a paper-independent bounded-build complex interval arithmetic obligation. Only after that implementation is added and compiled does the represented-data layer yield a certified-real output corresponding to the Borel statistic on the same fixed primitive records.

\section{Related work}

\cite{Robinson1988} supplies the partially linear conditional-mean model, and \cite{ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML} and \cite{NeweyRobins2018} supply orthogonal-score and sample-splitting background. \cite{MackeySyrgkanisZadik2018} gives finite-order orthogonal PLM moments and a Gaussian characterization, not exact all-shift transform annihilation or the present fixed-separation minimax theorem. \cite[Theorem 5.4, condition (21), and bound (22)]{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025} is retained exactly on \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\). The proved inclusion \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) licenses the full-class spectral upper guarantee, and an ACE-preserving outcome-tilt path gives a class-minimax MSE converse there; neither fact lower-bounds the ACE estimator or converts the cited generalized-quantile comparison into anything other than upper-versus-upper. The simultaneous bounded-outcome Gaussian assumptions stated around that paper's Theorem 3.2 instead force \(\theta_0=0\). \cite{Mattner1992} and \cite{HuShiu2022} inform location-family completeness and observable-zero testing; \cite{DinhGhoshTranTran2021} informs zero localization; \cite{ChackoViceira2003} supplies complex and spectral GMM precedent; \cite{ReizingerEtAl2025} studies linear ICA treatment-effect estimation; and \cite{LeeMesters2024} studies weak-non-Gaussian inference. None supplies this learned-residual fixed-error PLM contour result. These are targeted distinctions, not claims that transform zeros are new or an exhaustive review.

\begin{definition}[def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate]\label{def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Paper-\allowbreak{}independent bounded-\allowbreak{}build certified complex contour arithmetic substrate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} The paper-independent bounded-build obligation \(\mathsf{CCIA}_{\mathrm{build}}\) is the following finite implementation specification.  A certified real record consists of rational closed intervals \(I_m=[I_m^-,I_m^+]\), an executable modulus \(\mu:\mathbb Q_{>0}\to\mathbb N\), and the semantic contract
\[
 x\in I_{m+1}\subseteq I_m,
 \qquad
 I_{\mu(e)}^+-I_{\mu(e)}^-\le e.
\]
A positive record additionally supplies a rational \(p>0\) with \(p\le x\).  Executable code may inspect only the interval, modulus, and positivity fields.  It may not inspect a semantic exact-real value.

For rational intervals \(A=[a_-,a_+]\) and \(B=[b_-,b_+]\), define
\[
 A+B=[a_-+b_-,a_++b_+],
 \qquad
 A-B=[a_--b_+,a_+-b_-],
\]
\[
 A B=
 [\min\{a_-b_-,a_-b_+,a_+b_-,a_+b_+\},
  \max\{a_-b_-,a_-b_+,a_+b_-,a_+b_+\}].
\]
A complex rectangle is \(A+iB\).  Complex addition, subtraction, conjugation, and multiplication are implemented by these endpoint operations and
\[
 (A+iB)(C+iD)=(AC-BD)+i(AD+BC).
\]
For \(A=[a_-,a_+]\), put
\[
 \operatorname{sq}_{-}(A)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&a_-\le0\le a_+,\\
 \min\{a_-^2,a_+^2\},&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \operatorname{sq}_{+}(A)=\max\{a_-^2,a_+^2\}.
\]
Thus \(|A+iB|^2\) is enclosed by the rational interval obtained by adding the corresponding square bounds.  Rational bisection between nonnegative rational endpoints gives lower and upper square-root enclosures: after \(q\) bisections their width is at most the initial width times \(2^{-q}\).  This supplies certified modulus lower and upper bounds.  Division is the guarded operation
\[
 \frac{A+iB}{C+iD}
 =(A+iB)(C-iD)(C^2+D^2)^{-1},
\]
and is executed only when the modulus routine has returned a rational lower bound \(m>0\); outward division by the positive rational interval enclosing \(C^2+D^2\) is then sound.

The certified name of \(\pi\) is constructed, rather than postulated, from Machin's identity
\[
 \pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239).
\]
For \(0<x<1\), the first \(N+1\) terms of
\[
 \arctan x=\sum_{j=0}^{N}(-1)^j\frac{x^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_N(x),
 \qquad
 |R_N(x)|\le\frac{x^{2N+3}}{2N+3},
\]
give rational nested enclosures after intersecting the first \(m+1\) requested enclosures.  If \(e=p/q>0\) is in lowest terms, the explicit choice \(N=5q+5\) makes the propagated Machin remainder smaller than \(e\), so this is an effective modulus with finite fuel.

For a rational interval contained in \([-B,B]\), with rational \(B\ge0\), evaluate the Taylor polynomials for \(\exp\), \(\sin\), and \(\cos\) by the preceding interval operations.  The Lagrange remainders are bounded respectively by
\[
 3^{\lceil B\rceil}\frac{B^{N+1}}{(N+1)!},
 \qquad
 \frac{B^{N+1}}{(N+1)!},
 \qquad
 \frac{B^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}.
\]
For a requested rational error \(e=p/q>0\), take
\[
 N=\lceil2B\rceil+q+2\lceil B\rceil+2.
\]
After the index \(\lceil2B\rceil\), successive absolute Taylor terms decrease by at least a factor \(1/2\); since \(3^{\lceil B\rceil}<2^{2\lceil B\rceil}\) and \(2^{-q}\le q^{-1}\le e\), this displayed \(N\) gives a sound error at most \(e\).  Finite intersections make the output rectangles nested.  Hence
\[
 \exp(x+iy)=\exp(x)\{\cos y+i\sin y\}
\]
has a nested complex-rectangle extension with an effective modulus on every bounded input rectangle.  Combining the constructed \(\pi\) name with these trigonometric operations gives, from a positive radius record \(\mathfrak r\) and integers \(N\ge1\), \(0\le q<N\), certified circle-node records for
\[
 r\exp(2\pi iq/N)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 2\pi i r\exp(2\pi iq/N).
\]

Finite rectangle sums and products are primitive recursive.  If a complex-valued function on \([0,1]\) has a supplied rational Lipschitz bound \(L\), a uniform mesh with
\[
 N_{\mathrm{mesh}}=\lceil L/e\rceil+1
\]
has oscillation at most \(e\) on each cell.  Evaluating every node to a rational error budget divided by the displayed finite number of operations therefore yields sound finite infimum, supremum, and trapezoidal-integral enclosures.  Their fuel consists of \(N_{\mathrm{mesh}}+1\) node calls, the displayed Taylor cutoffs, square-root bisection cutoffs, and a finite number of endpoint operations.  Absolute value of a real interval is implemented by the square-free endpoint rule implicit in \(\operatorname{sq}_{-}\) and \(\operatorname{sq}_{+}\), and has the same compositional modulus property.

A concrete implementation discharges \(\mathsf{CCIA}_{\mathrm{build}}\) only if it implements these algorithms, exports their soundness and effective-modulus contracts, and compiles the resulting module.  This definition is the explicit bounded-build obligation; it does not state or imply that the missing complex operations are already exported by Causalean.  Until such an implementation is added and compiled, later executable conclusions are conditional on a build satisfying this specification.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[thm:known-zero-instrument]\label{thm:known-zero-instrument}
If \(z_0\) is a zero of \(M\) of multiplicity \(\ell\), then for every real shift \(d\), \(E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(\eta+d)]=0\). For every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), this gives \(E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\mid X]=0\) and \(E_P[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]=M^{(\ell)}(z_0)H_n(z_0)\); whenever this value is nonzero, \(\theta_0=E_P[YJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]/E_P[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a zero \(z_0\) of \(M\) having multiplicity \(\ell\).  By the definition of multiplicity,
\[
 M^{(j)}(z_0)=0\quad(0\le j<\ell),
 \qquad M^{(\ell)}(z_0)\ne0.
\]
All differentiations under the expectation below are legitimate: membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and \(\text{ass:eta-subgaussian}\), incorporated in \(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\), give exponential moments of \(\eta\) of every real order, while \(|D_n|\le 2C_g\).  Hence every polynomial times each complex exponential appearing below is integrable, uniformly on compact subsets of the complex plane.

For an arbitrary real number \(d\), the binomial identity and the derivative formula \(M^{(j)}(z)=E_P[\eta^j e^{z\eta}]\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(\eta+d)]
 &=E_P[(\eta+d)^{\ell-1}e^{z_0(\eta+d)}]\\
 &=e^{z_0d}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
   {\ell-1\choose j}d^{\ell-1-j}E_P[\eta^j e^{z_0\eta}]\\
 &=e^{z_0d}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
   {\ell-1\choose j}d^{\ell-1-j}M^{(j)}(z_0)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the shift-annihilation identity.

Now fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  By \(\text{ass:independent-treatment-noise}\), the conditional law of \(\eta\) given \(X\) equals its marginal law.  Since \(D_n=g_0(X)-\bar g_n(X)\) is \(\sigma(X)\)-measurable and real-valued, the preceding identity, applied conditionally with \(d=D_n\), yields
\[
 E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\mid X]
 =E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(\eta+D_n)\mid X]=0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]

Independence also gives the exact transform factorization
\[
 E_P[e^{zZ_n}]
 =E_P[e^{z(\eta+D_n)}]=M(z)H_n(z).
\]
Differentiating this identity \(\ell\) times at \(z_0\), using the product rule and the multiplicity equalities above, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]
 &=E_P[Z_n^{\ell}e^{z_0Z_n}]\\
 &=\left.\frac{d^{\ell}}{dz^{\ell}}\{M(z)H_n(z)\}\right|_{z=z_0}\\
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose j}
   M^{(j)}(z_0)H_n^{(\ell-j)}(z_0)
 =M^{(\ell)}(z_0)H_n(z_0).
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally, by the displayed residual decomposition,
\[
 Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi.
\]
The conditional annihilation identity implies
\[
 E_P[b_n(X)J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]
 =E_P\!\left[b_n(X)E_P[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\mid X]\right]=0.
\]
Moreover, \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\), so \(J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)\) is \(\sigma(X,T)\)-measurable.  The restriction \(E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0\) in \(\text{ass:outcome-mean-independence}\) therefore gives
\[
 E_P[\xi J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]
 =E_P\!\left[J_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)E_P[\xi\mid X,T]\right]=0.
\]
The required products are integrable because \(b_n\) is bounded, \(\xi\) is conditionally sub-Gaussian, \(D_n\) is bounded, and \(\eta\) is sub-Gaussian.  Consequently,
\[
 E_P[YJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]
 =\theta_0E_P[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)].
\]
Whenever the denominator, equivalently \(M^{(\ell)}(z_0)H_n(z_0)\), is nonzero, division is valid and yields
\[
 \theta_0=
 \frac{E_P[YJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]}
      {E_P[Z_nJ_{z_0,\ell}(Z_n)]}.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This exact identity displays the global location-family null score and its relevance under residual contamination. \textit{Gap.} Transform-zero incompleteness is classical, but its multiplicity-adjusted score has not been used to eliminate an arbitrary PLM covariate shift created by a supplied treatment code. \textit{Consumer.} Known-noise implementations can estimate the causal coefficient by a single bounded or exponentially weighted moment ratio.

\begin{lemma}[lem:observable-factorization]\label{lem:observable-factorization}
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), \(F_n(z)=M(z)H_n(z)\) and \(G_n(z)=\theta_0F_n'(z)+M(z)B_n(z)\) for every \(z\in\mathbb C\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and \(z\in\mathbb C\).  The sub-Gaussian and boundedness conditions make every expectation and derivative below finite.  Since \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), \(D_n\) is measurable with respect to \(X\), and \(\eta\perp X\),
\[
 F_n(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}e^{zD_n}]=M(z)H_n(z).
\]
The residual equation gives \(Y=\theta_0Z_n+b_n(X)+\xi\).  Moreover \(Z_n\) is measurable with respect to \((X,T)\), so outcome mean independence implies
\[
 E_P[\xi e^{zZ_n}]
 =E_P[e^{zZ_n}E_P(\xi\mid X,T)]=0.
\]
Independence again gives
\[
 E_P[b_n(X)e^{zZ_n}]
 =E_P[e^{z\eta}]E_P[b_n(X)e^{zD_n}]=M(z)B_n(z).
\]
Finally, differentiation under the expectation is justified by the same exponential integrability and yields \(F_n'(z)=E_P[Z_ne^{zZ_n}]\).  Substitution proves
\[
 G_n(z)=\theta_0F_n'(z)+M(z)B_n(z).
\]
Both identities hold for every complex \(z\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The identity recovers the latent transform-zero geometry from observable learned residuals and isolates an analytic nuisance ratio. \textit{Gap.} Classical convolution results do not provide the outcome transform \(G_n=\theta_0F_n'+MB_n\) for a partially linear causal coefficient. \textit{Consumer.} The contour theorem integrates \(G_n/F_n\) and removes \(B_n/H_n\) exactly.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-contour-identification]\label{thm:exact-contour-identification}
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), let \(C_j\) be positively oriented. If \(F_n\) is zero-free on \(C_j\), \(H_n\) is zero-free on and inside \(C_j\), and \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\ge1\), then \(\theta_0=\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n)=\{N_{C_j}(F_n)2\pi i\}^{-1}\oint_{C_j}G_n(z)/F_n(z)\,dz\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  This membership is the premise needed to invoke \(\text{lem:observable-factorization}\).  On \(C_j\), that lemma and the identity \(F_n=M H_n\) give
\[
 \frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}
 =\theta_0\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}+
   \frac{M(z)B_n(z)}{M(z)H_n(z)}
 =\theta_0\frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}+\frac{B_n(z)}{H_n(z)}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The cancellation in the middle expression is justified pointwise on \(C_j\): by hypothesis \(F_n\) is nonzero there and \(H_n\) is nonzero there, so \(M=F_n/H_n\) is also nonzero there.  Since \(B_n\) and \(H_n\) are entire and \(H_n\) has no zero on or inside \(C_j\), the quotient \(B_n/H_n\) is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk bounded by \(C_j\).  Cauchy's theorem therefore yields
\[
 \oint_{C_j}\frac{B_n(z)}{H_n(z)}\,dz=0.
 \tag{2}
\]
Because \(F_n\) is entire and zero-free on \(C_j\), the argument principle gives
\[
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}
 \frac{F_n'(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz=N_{C_j}(F_n).
 \tag{3}
\]
Integrating (1) and using (2)--(3) gives
\[
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz
 =\theta_0N_{C_j}(F_n).
 \tag{4}
\]
The assumed count is a positive integer, so division by \(N_{C_j}(F_n)\) is valid.  Applying \(\text{def:contour-functional}\) to (4) proves
\[
 \theta_0
 =\Theta_{C_j}(F_n,G_n)
 =\frac{1}{N_{C_j}(F_n)2\pi i}
   \oint_{C_j}\frac{G_n(z)}{F_n(z)}\,dz.
\]
Thus the causal target remains \(\theta_0(P)\), while the displayed observable contour ratio is its identifying functional only for a law in the stated class and under the stated boundary, zero-free, and positive-count conditions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} For a law \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), the learned-residual factorization makes the observable contour formula identify the coefficient without an outcome regression once a valid contour is given. \textit{Gap.} The identity requires class membership as well as the boundary zero-free, nuisance-transform zero-free, and positive-count conditions; it neither constructs nor computes the uniformly conditioned contour bank. \textit{Consumer.} The certified estimator fixes \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and evaluates this population identity on its selected translated-dyadic contour.

\begin{lemma}[lem:zero-localization]\label{lem:zero-localization}
With the exact Luxemburg convention in \(\text{ass:eta-subgaussian}\), set
\[
 A_k=(2^{k+4}k!)^{1/(k-2)},
 \qquad
 R_0=A_k(\psi_{\eta}^2/\delta)^{1/(k-2)}.
\]
Every centered law satisfying \(\|\eta\|_{\psi_2}\le\psi_{\eta}\) and \(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta\) has a zero of \(M\) in \(\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|\le R_0\}\). Thus the zero-localization radius is the displayed numerical function of the primitive constants.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\psi=\psi_{\eta}\).  The exact Luxemburg premise gives
\[
 E_P\exp(\eta^2/\psi^2)\le2,
 \qquad E_P\eta=0.
\]
We first derive a numerical growth bound.  Let \(\eta'\) be an independent copy of \(\eta\).  If \(|t|\psi\le1\), Jensen's inequality and centering give
\[
 E_Pe^{t\eta}\le E_Pe^{t(\eta-\eta')}.
\]
The difference \(\eta-\eta'\) is symmetric.  Using \(\cosh u\le e^{u^2/2}\), \((\eta-\eta')^2\le2\eta^2+2(\eta')^2\), and then the Lyapunov inequality with exponent \(t^2\psi^2\le1\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_Pe^{t\eta}
 &\le E_P\cosh\{t(\eta-\eta')\}\\
 &\le E_P\exp\{t^2(\eta-\eta')^2/2\}\\
 &\le \{E_Pe^{t^2\eta^2}\}^2
 \le 2^{2t^2\psi^2}
 \le e^{4\psi^2t^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(|t|\psi>1\), Young's inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz yield
\[
 t\eta\le\frac{\eta^2}{2\psi^2}+\frac{t^2\psi^2}{2},
 \qquad
 E_Pe^{t\eta}\le\sqrt2\,e^{t^2\psi^2/2}
 \le e^{4\psi^2t^2}.
\]
Consequently, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 |M(z)|\le E_Pe^{\operatorname{Re}(z)\eta}
 \le \exp(4\psi^2|z|^2). \tag{1}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that \(M\) has no zero in the closed disk of radius \(R_0\).  Since \(M(0)=1\), an analytic logarithm \(f=\log M\), normalized by \(f(0)=0\), exists on a neighborhood of that disk.  Centering gives \(f'(0)=0\).  For \(0<R<R_0\), (1) gives
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R}\operatorname{Re}f(z)\le4\psi^2R^2.
\]
Apply the Carath\'eodory coefficient estimate on the radius-\(R\) disk to the positive-real-part analytic function \(4\psi^2R^2-f\).  Its value at zero is \(4\psi^2R^2\), and hence
\[
 |f^{(k)}(0)|
 \le 2k!R^{-k}\,4\psi^2R^2
 =8k!\psi^2R^{2-k}. \tag{2}
\]
Letting \(R\uparrow R_0\), using \(f^{(k)}(0)=\kappa_k(\eta)\), and invoking \(\text{ass:cumulant-separation}\), we get
\[
 \delta\le8k!\psi^2R_0^{2-k}.
\]
But the displayed definitions of \(A_k\) and \(R_0\) give
\[
 8k!\psi^2R_0^{2-k}
 =\frac{8k!}{2^{k+4}k!}\,\delta
 =2^{-k-1}\delta<\delta,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus \(M\) has a zero in \(\{|z|\le R_0\}\), with the asserted numerical radius.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact Luxemburg normalization yields the displayed numerical radius rather than an unspecified universal constant. \textit{Gap.} The result localizes a transform zero but does not by itself provide boundary conditioning. \textit{Consumer.} The certified translated-dyadic bank is built outside this explicit disk.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-contour-bank]\label{lem:finite-contour-bank}
Under the certified-real input convention of \(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\), its terminating finite searches compute, from the displayed primitive class constants and without access to the unknown law of \(\eta\), a finite translated-dyadic bank \(C_j=\{z:|z|=\rho_j\}\), \(1\le j\le J\), with \(R_0<\rho_j<R_1\), and a positive dyadic rational \(a_{\star}\). Every treatment-noise transform represented in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) has some \(j\) such that \(N_{C_j}(M)\ge1\) and
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_j}|M(z)|\ge a_{\star}.
\]
The same computed bank and certificate work uniformly in \(n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix one treatment-noise transform \(M\) represented by a law in
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and abbreviate
\[
 \psi=\psi_{\eta},\qquad
 R_{\mathrm{fac}}=R_1+1,\qquad
 R_{\mathrm{out}}=R_1+2.
\]
The certified intervals returned at error one contain their represented
values and have width at most one.  Consequently the integers in
\(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\) satisfy
\[
 U_{\psi}\ge\max\{1,\psi\},
 \qquad U_R\ge\max\{1,R_1\}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
All calls used to obtain these integers terminate by the supplied modulus
contracts in \(\text{def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate}\).

We first bound the number of relevant zeros.  Membership in
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) gives the exact Luxemburg inequality
\(E\exp(\eta^2/\psi^2)\le2\) and \(E\eta=0\).  The symmetrization and
Young-inequality calculation used in \(\text{lem:zero-localization}\) gives,
for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 |M(z)|\le \exp(4\psi^2|z|^2).                                    \tag{2}
\]
Let \(N\) be the number of zeros of \(M\) strictly inside
\(\{|z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\}\), counted with multiplicity.  Jensen's formula,
applied at \(R_{\mathrm{out}}\) (with its usual radial limiting
interpretation if a zero lies on that circle), and \(M(0)=1\) imply
\[
 N\log\!\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{out}}}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}}\right)
 \le 4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{out}}^2.                                   \tag{3}
\]
For \(x>0\), \(\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)\).  Taking
\(x=R_{\mathrm{fac}}^{-1}\), then using (1), gives
\[
 N\le4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{out}}^3
 \le4U_{\psi}^2(U_R+2)^3=N_{\mathrm{cert}}.                       \tag{4}
\]

Now put \(m=m_{\star}=N_{\mathrm{cert}}+3\),
\(d=2^{-m}\), and \(h=d/3\).  The grid in
\(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\) consists of
\[
 \rho_q=R_0+\frac14+q2^{-m},
 \qquad 0\le q\le2^{m-1}.
\]
It has \(J=2^{m-1}+1>N_{\mathrm{cert}}\) points, spacing \(d\), and
\[
 R_0<\rho_q\le R_0+\frac34<R_1.                                  \tag{5}
\]
The modulus of a fixed zero can be at distance less than \(h\) from at
most one grid point, because \(2h<d\).  Every zero that can exclude a grid
point lies inside \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), since \(\rho_q+h<R_1+1\).  By (4) and the pigeonhole
principle there is an index \(j\) such that
\[
 \bigl||a|-\rho_j\bigr|\ge h                                    \tag{6}
\]
for every zero \(a\) strictly inside \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\).  By
\(\text{lem:zero-localization}\), \(M\) has a zero with modulus at most
\(R_0\).  This zero is strictly inside \(C_j\) by (5), while (6) says that
no zero lies on \(C_j\).  The argument principle therefore gives
\[
 N_{C_j}(M)\ge1.                                                   \tag{7}
\]

It remains to verify the displayed dyadic modulus.  List all zeros strictly
inside \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), with multiplicity, as
\(a_1,\ldots,a_N\), and form the disk Blaschke product
\[
 Q(z)=\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\nu)}
      {R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline{a_\nu}z}.
\]
After removable singularities are filled,
\(f=M/Q\) is holomorphic and zero-free on the open disk of radius
\(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\).  Each factor has modulus one on the boundary.
Thus (2) and the maximum-modulus principle give
\[
 |f(z)|\le \exp(4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2),
 \qquad |z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}.                                    \tag{8}
\]
Moreover \(|Q(0)|\le1\) and \(M(0)=1\), so \(|f(0)|\ge1\).

Set \(A=4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2\).  The function
\(A-\log|f|\) is nonnegative and harmonic on the open disk, and its value
at zero is at most \(A\).  Harnack's inequality therefore yields, for
\(|z|\le R_1\),
\[
 \log|f(z)|
 \ge-A\left{
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}-|z|}-1
 \right}
 \ge-8\psi^2R_1(R_1+1)^2.                                       \tag{9}
\]
With
\[
 E_{\star}=8U_RU_{\psi}^2(U_R+1)^2,
 \qquad D_{\star}=3(2U_R+1),                                    \tag{10}
\]
equations (1) and (9) imply \(|f(z)|\ge e^{-E_{\star}}\).  Since
\(\log2>1/2\),
\[
 |f(z)|\ge2^{-2E_{\star}},
 \qquad |z|\le R_1.                                              \tag{11}
\]

For \(z\in C_j\), (6) and \(|a_\nu|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\) give, factor by
factor,
\[
 \left|
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\nu)}
      {R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline{a_\nu}z}
 \right|
 \ge \frac{|z-a_\nu|}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|}
 \ge \frac{h}{2R_1+1}
 \ge \frac{2^{-m}}{D_{\star}}.                                 \tag{12}
\]
Because \(D_{\star}\) is a positive integer,
\(D_{\star}^{-1}\ge2^{-D_{\star}}\).  The base in (12) is at most one;
hence (4) implies
\[
 |Q(z)|
 \ge2^{-N_{\mathrm{cert}}(m+D_{\star})}.                         \tag{13}
\]
Multiplying (11) and (13) gives
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_j}|M(z)|
 \ge2^{-\{2E_{\star}+N_{\mathrm{cert}}(m+D_{\star})\}}
 =2^{-u_{\star}}=a_{\star}>0.                                  \tag{14}
\]
The quantities \(E_{\star}\), \(D_{\star}\), and \(u_{\star}\) in
(10)--(14) are exactly the integers denoted by
\(e_{\star}\), \(d_{\star}^{\mathrm{den}}\), and \(u_{\star}\) in
\(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\).

Finally, the construction makes only the two displayed modulus calls,
certified additions with rational point records, and finite primitive
recursions over explicit integer bounds.  Soundness follows from
\(\text{def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate}\), and no operation
queries \(M\), \(\eta\), or their law.  The input record is fixed before
\(n\), while (2)--(14) use only the fixed class constants.  The same finite
bank and the same positive dyadic certificate therefore work uniformly in
\(n\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit zero-count budget, separated translated-dyadic grid, and Blaschke--Harnack lower bound turn the fixed experiment-wide primitive record into one law-blind bank and dyadic modulus for every \(n\). \textit{Gap.} The certificate may deteriorate with fixed separation constants and gives no useful shrinking-separation modulus. \textit{Consumer.} The estimator uses this fixed bank and certificate in every pilot and evaluation threshold.

\begin{lemma}[lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel]\label{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}
If \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\neq\varnothing\), then it contains a one-dimensional submodel \(\{P_{n,\vartheta}:|\vartheta|\le c_0\}\) with the same law of \((X,T)\), the same \(g_0,q_0,\bar g_n,\bar q_n\), independent Gaussian outcome innovation of fixed positive variance not exceeding the \(\psi_{\xi}\) envelope, and \(Y_{\vartheta}=q_0(X)+\vartheta\eta+\xi_{\star}\). For \(|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=h/\sqrt n\), \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n}\|P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})\le C_0h^2\), where \(c_0,C_0\) depend only on fixed class constants. Moreover, whenever \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\neq\varnothing\), the same construction may be started from any \(P^{\circ}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) and preserves its law of \((X,T)\), the functions \(g_0,q_0\), the noise \(\eta\), both clipped supplied codes, and therefore the two bounds
\[
 \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n},
 \qquad
 \|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}.
\]
Every law on this second path belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\), has target \(\theta_0(P_{n,\vartheta})=\vartheta\), and obeys the same product relative-entropy bound. Consequently there is \(c_{\mathrm{ACE}}>0\), depending only on the fixed class constants, such that for every \(n\ge2\) with \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\neq\varnothing\),
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\theta(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\bar g_n,\bar q_n)}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 E_P[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \ge \frac{c_{\mathrm{ACE}}}{n}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix
\[
 \tau=\frac{\psi_{\xi}}4>0,
 \qquad
 c_0=\min\{C_{\theta},1\}>0,
\]
and let \(\xi_{\star}\sim N(0,\tau^2)\) be independent of \((X,T)\). For the exact Luxemburg convention, if \(W\sim N(0,\tau^2)\), then
\[
 E\exp(W^2/u^2)=(1-2\tau^2/u^2)^{-1/2}
 \quad\text{for }u^2>2\tau^2.
\]
It follows that \(\|W\|_{\psi_2}=\sqrt{8/3}\,\tau<\psi_{\xi}\). Thus the chosen innovation has fixed positive variance and satisfies the required conditional sub-Gaussian envelope when conditioned on any \(X\).

First suppose \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\neq\varnothing\) and fix \(P^{\circ}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\). Preserve its joint law of \((X,T)\), hence its \(g_0\), \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\), covariate law, deterministic clipped codes, and code errors. Preserve also its function \(q_0\), and, for \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\), define
\[
 Y_{\vartheta}=q_0(X)+\vartheta\eta+\xi_{\star}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Since \(\eta\perp X\) and \(E\eta=0\),
\[
 E[Y_{\vartheta}\mid X]=q_0(X).
\]
The residual innovation under coefficient \(\vartheta\) is exactly \(\xi_{\star}\), and independence gives
\[
 E[\xi_{\star}\mid X,T]=0.
\]
The target range holds because \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\le C_{\theta}\). The bounds on \(g_0\) and \(q_0\), independence and sub-Gaussianity of \(\eta\), its cumulant separation, and the direct \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code bound are unchanged. The displayed Gaussian calculation gives the conditional sub-Gaussian bound for the new outcome innovation. Hence every law \(P_{n,\vartheta}\) defined by (1) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and all objects asserted to be common along the path are indeed common.

Conditional on \((X,T)\), two path laws differ only in the means of normal distributions with common variance \(\tau^2\). Therefore
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}\|P_{n,\vartheta_1})
 =\frac{(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0)^2}{2\tau^2}E[(T-g_0(X))^2]
 =\frac{(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0)^2}{2\tau^2}E[\eta^2].
 \tag{2}
\]
The exact treatment-noise Luxemburg condition and Jensen's inequality imply
\[
 \exp\{E\eta^2/\psi_{\eta}^2\}
 \le E\exp(\eta^2/\psi_{\eta}^2)\le2,
 \qquad
 E\eta^2\le\psi_{\eta}^2\log2.
 \tag{3}
\]
Additivity of relative entropy for independent products, (2), and (3) show that whenever \(|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=h/\sqrt n\),
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{n,\vartheta_0}^{\otimes n}\|P_{n,\vartheta_1}^{\otimes n})
 \le C_0h^2,
 \qquad
 C_0=\frac{\psi_{\eta}^2\log2}{2\tau^2}.
 \tag{4}
\]
This proves the first branch with constants depending only on the fixed class constants.

Now suppose \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\neq\varnothing\) and fix an arbitrary \(P^{\circ}\) in that class. Repeat (1), preserving the joint law of \((X,T)\), the functions \(g_0,q_0\), the treatment noise \(\eta\), and the deterministic functions \(\bar g_n,\bar q_n\). We verify every condition in \(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\). The restrictions on \(g_0\) and \(q_0\) are unchanged. All treatment-side properties are unchanged: \(\eta\perp X\), \(\|\eta\|_{\psi_2}\le\psi_{\eta}\), and \(|\kappa_{r+1}(\eta)|\ge\delta\). Because neither regression function nor either clipped code changes,
\[
 \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n},
 \qquad
 \|\bar q_n-q_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{2,n}
\]
remain exactly the original two inequalities. The new coefficient obeys \(|\vartheta|\le c_0\le C_{\theta}\). The new residual is \(\xi_{\star}\), so the calculation above gives \(\|\xi_{\star}\|_{\psi_2\mid X}\le\psi_{\xi}\) almost surely, and independence gives \(E[\xi_{\star}\mid X,T]=0\). Finally, the size-\(n\) experiment is the independent product \(P_{n,\vartheta}^{\otimes n}\), exactly as required by \(\text{ass:iid-sampling}\). Thus every law on the second path belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\), and (2)--(4) apply without change.

It remains to turn this path into the asserted ACE-class converse. Choose
\[
 0<h\le\min\{c_0,1,(8C_0)^{-1/2}\},
 \qquad
 \vartheta_{\pm}=\pm\frac{h}{2\sqrt n}.
\]
For every \(n\ge2\), both values lie in the path, their separation is \(h/\sqrt n\), and (4) gives product relative entropy at most \(1/8\). Pinsker's inequality, which follows from the binary log-sum bound, gives
\[
 \|P_{n,\vartheta_-}^{\otimes n}-P_{n,\vartheta_+}^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\sqrt{\frac12D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{n,\vartheta_-}^{\otimes n}\|P_{n,\vartheta_+}^{\otimes n})}
 \le\frac14.
 \tag{5}
\]
For an arbitrary estimator \(\widetilde\theta\), put \(A=\{\widetilde\theta\ge0\}\). On \(A\) under the negative law, or on \(A^c\) under the positive law, the squared error is at least \(h^2/(4n)\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{v\in\{-,+\}}
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}}[(\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 &\ge \frac{h^2}{8n}
 \{P_{n,\vartheta_-}^{\otimes n}(A)
   +P_{n,\vartheta_+}^{\otimes n}(A^c)\}\\
 &\ge \frac{h^2}{8n}
 \{1-\|P_{n,\vartheta_-}^{\otimes n}-P_{n,\vartheta_+}^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}\\
 &\ge\frac{3h^2}{32n}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both laws belong to the published ACE class. Taking the supremum over that class and then the infimum over all sample estimators proves the final display with \(c_{\mathrm{ACE}}=3h^2/32>0\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lower bound uses an explicit Gaussian-outcome-innovation path that preserves the law of \((X,T)\), both regression functions, and the deterministic supplied codes; on the published ACE branch this preservation keeps both \(L^r(P_X)\) code bounds intact. \textit{Gap.} A generic parametric-submodel appeal would not verify membership in either triangular common-code class, the conditional sub-Gaussian innovation envelope, or preservation of the published outcome-code restriction. \textit{Consumer.} Use the two branches for the broad-class and full published ACE-class \(c/n\) minimax MSE converses.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax]\label{thm:adaptive-rootn-minimax}
Fix once for all the actual positive primitive-name record \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) in \(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\) and the actual represented range record \(\mathfrak c_{\theta,\star}\) in \(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\). Under this shared-record convention, \(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\) and \(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\) specify once for all a deterministic translated-dyadic bank and a positive dyadic rational \(a_{\star}\) from the displayed primitive class constants, without access to the unknown law of \(\eta\). There are constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on \(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\), such that the associated total Borel statistic \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) in \(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\), whose ordinary-sample interpretation uses those same fixed primitive records and canonicalizes only observation coordinates, satisfies, for every \(n\ge2\) with \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\neq\varnothing\) and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\),
\[
 \frac cn\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac Cn.
\]
The statistic uses \(\bar g_n\), uses no supplied outcome code, and does not know the law of \(\eta\). If a concrete implementation satisfying \(\text{def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate}\) is added and compiled, then, relative to the canonical floor-dyadic observation records and exactly the same fixed records \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) and \(\mathfrak c_{\theta,\star}\), the statistic is also the value of a total executable represented-data transducer returning a certified-real name. The correspondence claim does not quantify over alternative primitive records. Identical full input records, including their primitive components, give identical traces and output records; no Type-2 computability or trace extensionality across arbitrary alternative real names is asserted. Its MSE bound implies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|)
 \le\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}.
\]
Moreover, \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\). Hence, whenever \(n\ge2\), \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), and \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\neq\varnothing\),
\[
 \frac cn
 \le
 \inf_{\widetilde\theta(O_1,\ldots,O_n;\bar g_n,\bar q_n)}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 E_P[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac Cn,
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 Q_{P,1-\gamma}(|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P)|)
 \le\sqrt{C/(\gamma n)}.
\]
No inclusion \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) is asserted. The finite-order ACE and spectral generalized-quantile guarantees may therefore be compared on the full published ACE class, and that class itself has matched \(\Theta(n^{-1})\) minimax mean-squared error. The latter lower bound is over all estimators on the class; it is not a lower bound on \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ACE},r,n}\) or on its cited generalized-quantile guarantee. The class \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) remains the additional \(L^s(P_X)\)-restricted subclass and equals the published ACE class when \(s=r\) under the common clipped-code convention. The constants may deteriorate with fixed \(\delta\), and this theorem gives no useful shrinking-\(\delta_n\) rate, adaptive switching rule, uniform inference result, or matching local lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove the shared-record representation claim, the uniform upper bound,
the two minimax converses, and the quantile and comparator conclusions.

\emph{1. Mathematical and represented-data layers.}
Fix the exact records \(\mathfrak p_{\star}\) and
\(\mathfrak c_{\theta,\star}\) from
\(\text{def:contour-bank-handle}\) and
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\).  They are fixed before the law,
sample size, observation records, and execution trace vary.  Unfolding
\(\text{def:certified-contour-arithmetic-substrate}\), the real interval
formulas are sound because the extrema of a bilinear function on a rectangle
occur at its corners.  Substitution in
\[
 (x+iy)(u+iv)=(xu-yv)+i(xv+yu)
\]
then proves complex-multiplication containment.  The square bounds enclose
each coordinate square, rational bisection preserves the square-root
bracketing invariant, and a positive modulus lower bound makes the squared
denominator strictly positive.  The displayed conjugate formula and outward
division therefore prove guarded complex-division containment.

Machin's identity together with the displayed alternating remainder proves
the certified \(\pi\) contract at a finite cutoff.  The Lagrange remainders
and explicit cutoffs prove the bounded-interval contracts for exponential,
sine, and cosine, and hence for
\(e^{x+iy}=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)\).  Finite induction proves the sum and
product contracts.  On a mesh cell, a function with rational Lipschitz bound
\(L\) differs from its node value by at most
\(L/N_{\mathrm{mesh}}\); integration and finite endpoint minima prove the
quadrature and boundary-infimum enclosures.  Every cutoff and mesh size is
an explicit integer and all ensuing arithmetic is finite.

Consequently, if a concrete module implementing these algorithms and
contracts is added and compiled, the represented-data procedure in
\(\text{def:adaptive-contour-estimator}\), on inputs whose primitive
components are exactly the two records fixed above, is a total transducer.
Its final output is a certified-real record because
\[
 \frac{|y+C_{\theta}|-|y-C_{\theta}|}{2}
 =\Pi_{[-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]}(y)                               \tag{1}
\]
in the three cases \(y<-C_{\theta}\),
\(|y|\le C_{\theta}\), and \(y>C_{\theta}\), and the left side uses only
certified arithmetic and interval absolute value.  Identical full input
records cause the same finite modulus calls and rational comparisons, hence
the same trace and output record.  This conditional statement neither
quantifies over alternative primitive records nor asserts extensionality
across arbitrary names.

Independently of compilation, the ordinary-sample interpretation uses these
same primitive records and canonicalizes only the observation coordinates.
On a fixed terminal trace, every queried observation endpoint is a floor of
a scaled sample coordinate and is Borel measurable.  Rational arithmetic
and endpoint comparisons preserve Borel measurability.  There are only
countably many finite traces, and the procedure is total, so partitioning the
sample space by terminal trace proves that
\(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) is a total Borel statistic.  By the
definition of the two layers, the represented output has the same value.
Inspection of the construction shows that it uses \(\bar g_n\), no supplied
outcome code, and no treatment-noise law.

\emph{2. Uniform upper bound.}
Fix \(n\ge2\), \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and
\(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\).  Let \(j_{\star}\) be supplied by
\(\text{lem:finite-contour-bank}\).  By
\(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\),
\[
 N_{C_{j_{\star}}}(F_n)\ge1,
 \qquad
 \inf_{C_{j_{\star}}}|F_n|\ge\frac{3a_{\star}}4.                 \tag{2}
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), put
\[
 d_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|,
 \quad
 e_a=\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|,
 \quad
 \mathcal A_a=\{d_a\le a_{\star}/8\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\), soundness and the width
\(a_{\star}/64\) of the certified modulus interval give the candidate
\(j_{\star}\) a lower endpoint at least
\[
 \left(\frac34-\frac18-\frac1{64}\right)a_{\star}
 =\frac{39a_{\star}}{64}.                                        \tag{3}
\]
The straight-line homotopy between \(F_n\) and
\(\widehat F_{0,n}\) is nonzero on this circle, so their winding numbers
agree and are positive.  The sound enclosure contains that integer in its
real coordinate and zero in its imaginary coordinate, with coordinate
widths below \(1/4\).  Thus \(j_{\star}\) is admissible.

Let \(C=C_{\widehat j_n}\).  Maximality of the selected lower endpoint,
(3), and subtraction of \(d_0\) give
\[
 \inf_C|F_n|\ge\frac{31a_{\star}}{64}.                            \tag{4}
\]
The same homotopy and the sound winding enclosure give
\[
 \widehat N_{0\widehat j_n,n}=N_C(F_n)=:N\ge1.                   \tag{5}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\), the evaluation empirical modulus is
at least \(23a_{\star}/64\); after subtracting the enclosure width, its
certified lower endpoint is at least \(22a_{\star}/64>a_{\star}/4\).
Hence the evaluation branch does not fall back.

There is a finite constant \(C_G\), depending only on
\(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi},R_1\), such that
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|G_n(z)|\le C_G.                               \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), and
\(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\).  From the exact Luxemburg convention and
Young's inequality,
\[
 E e^{t|\eta|}\le\sqrt2\,e^{t^2\psi_{\eta}^2/2},
\]
and the same convention bounds the second moments of \(\eta\) and \(\xi\).
Cauchy--Schwarz then bounds
\(E[|Y|e^{R_1|Z_n|}]\), without requiring independence of \(\xi\) and
\(\eta\), proving (6).

Subtract the empirical and population ratios on \(C\).  Equations
(4)--(6), \(\operatorname{len}(C)\le2\pi R_1\), and \(N\ge1\) give
\[
 \left|
 \frac1{N2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{\widehat G_{1,n}}{\widehat F_{1,n}}\,dz
 -\frac1{N2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{G_n}{F_n}\,dz
 \right|
 \le L_{\mathrm{rat}}(d_1+e_1),                                  \tag{7}
\]
where, with \(\mu=31a_{\star}/64\) and
\(\nu=23a_{\star}/64\), one may take
\[
 L_{\mathrm{rat}}
 =R_1\max\{\nu^{-1},C_G(\mu\nu)^{-1}\}.                         \tag{8}
\]
The hypotheses of \(\text{thm:exact-contour-identification}\) now hold:
(4) makes \(F_n\) zero-free on \(C\),
\(\text{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}\) makes \(H_n\) zero-free on and inside
\(C\), and (5) supplies a positive count.  The population contour in (7)
therefore equals the causal target \(\theta_0(P)\).  The final rational
midpoint adds at most \(1/n\), and taking the real part followed by
projection on \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase distance from
\(\theta_0(P)\).

Both fold sizes are at least \(n/3\).  Therefore
\(\text{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}\) and Markov's inequality give,
for its constant \(K\),
\[
 E_P(d_a^2+e_a^2)\le\frac{3K}{n},
 \qquad
 P(\mathcal A_0^c\cup\mathcal A_1^c)
 \le\frac{384K}{a_{\star}^2n}.                                  \tag{9}
\]
Projection bounds squared error by \(4C_{\theta}^2\) on the exceptional
event.  On the good event, (7),
\((d_1+e_1)^2\le2(d_1^2+e_1^2)\), and the numerical error yield
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}}
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{spec},n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \le\frac{C_{\mathrm{up}}}{n},                                  \tag{10}
\]
where, for example,
\[
 C_{\mathrm{up}}
 =12L_{\mathrm{rat}}^2K
 +\frac{1536C_{\theta}^2K}{a_{\star}^2}+2.                      \tag{11}
\]
The bank, \(a_{\star}\), \(C_G\), \(K\), and
\(L_{\mathrm{rat}}\) depend only on
\(r,\delta,C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\).  In particular,
a width-one refinement of any fixed valid primitive record has upper endpoint
at most its represented value plus one, so the integer bounds used to form
\(a_{\star}\) are bounded solely by those values.  Thus
\(C_{\mathrm{up}}\) is outside all quantifiers over \(P\), \(n\), samples,
traces, and laws, and is independent of \(\gamma\).  Since the Borel
statistic is one data-driven rule, (10) also proves the minimax upper bound.

\emph{3. Converses and quantiles.}
Apply the first branch of \(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\).  Choose
a fixed \(h>0\), depending only on class constants, such that
\(\vartheta_{\pm}=\pm h/(2\sqrt n)\) lie on its path and the product
relative entropy is at most \(1/8\).  Pinsker's inequality gives total
variation at most \(1/4\).  Testing by the sign of an arbitrary estimator
then yields
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-,+\}}
 E_{P_{n,\vartheta_v}}[(\widetilde\theta-\vartheta_v)^2]
 \ge\frac{h^2}{8n}(1-1/4)
 =\frac{3h^2}{32n}.                                               \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(c_{\mathrm{NG}}/n\le\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{NG}}\) for a fixed
\(c_{\mathrm{NG}}>0\).

By \(\text{lem:jms-ace-class-relations}\),
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}.                            \tag{13}
\]
Restriction of (10) proves the spectral upper bound on the full published
ACE class.  The second branch of
\(\text{lem:non-gaussian-hard-submodel}\) gives on this same class
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\theta}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}}
 E_P[(\widetilde\theta-\theta_0(P))^2]
 \ge\frac{c_{\mathrm{ACE}}}{n}.                                  \tag{14}
\]
The Borel statistic is admissible in this infimum.  Taking
\[
 c=\min\{c_{\mathrm{NG}},c_{\mathrm{ACE}}\}>0,
 \qquad C=C_{\mathrm{up}}
\]

\begin{proposition}[prop:symmetric-mixture-reduction]\label{prop:symmetric-mixture-reduction}
For every \(n\) such that \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le 1/\pi\), let \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P\). Suppose that \(\eta\sim\tfrac12N(-1,1)+\tfrac12N(1,1)\), \(\eta\perp X\), \(E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0\) almost surely, \(|\theta_0(P)|\le C_{\theta}\), \(\|g_0\|_{\infty}\le C_g\), \(\|q_0\|_{\infty}\le C_q\), \(\|\xi\|_{\psi_2\mid X}\le\psi_{\xi}\) almost surely, and \(\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^1(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}\). Then \(\kappa_4(\eta)=-2\), \(M(i\pi/2)=0\), and
\[
E_P\!\left[Z_n\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi Z_n}{2}\right)\right]
=e^{-\pi^2/8}E_P\!\left[\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi D_n}{2}\right)\right]
\ge \frac12e^{-\pi^2/8}.
\]
There is a finite constant \(C\), depending only on \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\), such that, uniformly over all laws \(P\) satisfying the preceding conditions,
\[
E_P\!\left[(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}-\theta_0(P))^2\right]\le \frac{C}{n}.
\]
No condition on the supplied outcome code or on \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\) is required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put \(a=\pi/2\). Represent the stipulated mixture as \(\eta=R+E\), where \(R\) is symmetric Rademacher, \(E\sim N(0,1)\), and \(R\perp E\). Then
\[
M(z)=E_P[e^{z\eta}]=e^{z^2/2}\cosh z,
\qquad
M(ia)=e^{-a^2/2}\cos a=0.
\]
Moreover, \(E_P[\eta]=0\), \(E_P[\eta^2]=2\), and independence gives
\[
E_P[\eta^4]
=E_P[R^4]+6E_P[R^2]E_P[E^2]+E_P[E^4]
=1+6+3=10.
\]
The centered fourth-cumulant identity therefore yields
\[
\kappa_4(\eta)=E_P[\eta^4]-3E_P[\eta^2]^2=10-3\cdot2^2=-2.
\]

The characteristic function of \(\eta\) is
\[
\phi(t)=E_P[e^{it\eta}]=e^{-t^2/2}\cos t.
\]
For any deterministic \(d\in\mathbb R\), symmetry of \(\eta\) gives
\[
E_P[\cos\{t(\eta+d)\}]=\phi(t)\cos(td).
\]
Differentiation under the expectation is valid because the Gaussian mixture has a finite first moment. Hence
\[
E_P[(\eta+d)\sin\{t(\eta+d)\}]
=-\frac{d}{dt}\{\phi(t)\cos(td)\}.
\]
Since \(\phi(a)=0\) and \(\phi'(a)=-e^{-a^2/2}\), evaluation at \(a=\pi/2\) gives
\[
E_P[(\eta+d)\sin\{a(\eta+d)\}]
=e^{-a^2/2}\cos(ad).
\]
The variable \(D_n\) is measurable with respect to \(X\), so \(\eta\perp X\) implies \(\eta\perp D_n\). Conditioning on \(D_n\) and using \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\),
\[
E_P[Z_n\sin(aZ_n)]
=e^{-a^2/2}E_P[\cos(aD_n)]
=e^{-\pi^2/8}E_P[\cos(\pi D_n/2)].
\]
The direct \(L^1(P_X)\) premise is exactly
\[
E_P|D_n|=\|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^1(P_X)}\le\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
Using \(1-\cos u\le |u|\) and \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le1/\pi\),
\[
E_P[\cos(aD_n)]
\ge1-aE_P|D_n|
\ge1-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac1\pi
=\frac12.
\]
Thus
\[
c_0:=\frac12e^{-\pi^2/8}>0
\]
is a uniform population relevance and division bound. The cutoff in \(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}\) prevents division below \(c_0/2\).

For the risk calculation define
\[
W_i:=Z_{n,i}\sin(aZ_{n,i}),
\qquad
V_i:=\{Y_i-\theta_0(P)Z_{n,i}\}\sin(aZ_{n,i}),
\]
and let \(\bar W_n=\mathbb P_nW\) and \(\bar V_n=\mathbb P_nV\). The residual identity is
\[
Y-\theta_0(P)Z_n=b_n(X)+\xi,
\qquad
b_n(X)=q_0(X)-\theta_0(P)D_n.
\]
For every \(d\in\mathbb R\),
\[
E_P[\sin\{a(\eta+d)\}]
=\operatorname{Im}\{e^{iad}\phi(a)\}=0.
\]
Consequently, conditioning on \(X\) gives
\[
E_P[b_n(X)\sin(aZ_n)]=0.
\]
Because \(Z_n=T-\bar g_n(X)\) is measurable with respect to \((X,T)\), the conditional-mean restriction gives
\[
E_P[\xi\sin(aZ_n)]
=E_P\!\left[E_P[\xi\mid X,T]\sin(aZ_n)\right]=0.
\]
Therefore \(E_P[V_i]=0\).

The clipping definition and the range restriction imply \(|D_n|\le2C_g\) almost surely. Set
\[
b_{\max}:=C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g.
\]
Then \(|b_n(X)|\le b_{\max}\). The conditional sub-Gaussian condition entails \(E_P[\xi^2]\le K_{\xi}\psi_{\xi}^2\) for a finite universal constant \(K_{\xi}\) determined only by the convention for the conditional \(\psi_2\)-norm. Since \(|\sin u|\le1\),
\[
E_P[V_i^2]
\le2b_{\max}^2+2K_{\xi}\psi_{\xi}^2
=:K_V.
\]
Also, \(E_P[\eta^2]=2\), \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), and \(|\sin u|\le1\) imply
\[
E_P[W_i^2]
\le E_P[Z_n^2]
\le2E_P[\eta^2]+2E_P[D_n^2]
\le4+8C_g^2
=:K_W.
\]
These bounds verify the integrability needed for conditioning, differentiation, and the variance calculations.

Let \(\mathcal A_n=\{\bar W_n\ge c_0/2\}\), the event on which the sample ratio is used. Projection onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) cannot increase distance from \(\theta_0(P)\). Thus, on \(\mathcal A_n\),
\[
|\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}-\theta_0(P)|^2
\le\frac{\bar V_n^2}{\bar W_n^2}
\le\frac{4\bar V_n^2}{c_0^2}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_n^c\), clipping bounds the squared loss by \(4C_{\theta}^2\). If \(m_W=E_P[W_i]\), then \(m_W\ge c_0\). Independent and identically distributed sampling and Chebyshev's inequality therefore give
\[
P(\mathcal A_n^c)
\le P\!\left(|\bar W_n-m_W|\ge\frac{c_0}{2}\right)
\le\frac{4\operatorname{Var}_P(W_i)}{nc_0^2}
\le\frac{4K_W}{nc_0^2}.
\]
The same sampling condition and \(E_P[V_i]=0\) yield
\[
E_P[\bar V_n^2]
=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(V_i)}{n}
\le\frac{K_V}{n}.
\]
Combining the two events,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[(\widehat\theta_{\sin,n}-\theta_0(P))^2]
&\le\frac{4K_V}{nc_0^2}
+4C_{\theta}^2\frac{4K_W}{nc_0^2}\\
&=\frac{4K_V+16C_{\theta}^2K_W}{c_0^2}\,\frac1n.
\end{aligned}
\]
The constant depends only on \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\xi}\), and not on \(P\), \(n\), the supplied outcome code, or \(\varepsilon_{2,n}\). This proves the proposition, including its support, relevance, positivity, integrability, and fallback boundary conditions.
\end{proof}

 M_a(z)
 =E[e^{z\sqrt{1-a^2}G_{\circ}}]E[e^{zaS}]
 =\exp\!\left(\frac{1-a^2}{2}z^2\right)\cosh(az).
\]
Since the fourth derivative at zero of \(\log\cosh(az)\) is \(-2a^4\), the cumulant definition gives \(\kappa_4(\eta_a)=-2a^4\).  Its characteristic function is
\[
 \varphi_a(t)=M_a(it)
 =\exp\!\left(-\frac{1-a^2}{2}t^2\right)\cos(at).
\]
The exponential factor is positive, so the first positive zero is \(t_a=\pi/(2a)\), and for every real \(d\),
\[
 E[\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+d)\}]
 =\operatorname{Im}\{e^{it_ad}\varphi_a(t_a)\}=0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \varphi_a'(t_a)
 =-a\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{8a^2}\right\}=-A_a.
\]
Differentiation under the expectation is justified because \(\eta_a\) has moments of every order.  Differentiating \(e^{itd}\varphi_a(t)\) and taking real parts at \(t_a\) yields
\[
 E[(\eta_a+d)\sin\{t_a(\eta_a+d)\}]=A_a\cos(t_ad).
\]
Since \(D_n\) is measurable in \(X\) and \(\eta_a\perp X\), conditioning on \(D_n\) gives
\[
 m_a:=E_P[Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n)]=A_aE_P[\cos(t_aD_n)].
\]
The direct \(L^1(P_X)\) premise is exactly \(E_P|D_n|\le\varepsilon_{1,n}\).  Hence \(1-\cos u\le|u|\) and \(t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le1/2\) imply
\[
 E_P[\cos(t_aD_n)]
 \ge1-t_aE_P|D_n|
 \ge\frac12,
\]
so \(m_a\ge A_a/2>0\).  This is the required overlap condition.

Put
\[
 W=Z_n\sin(t_aZ_n),\qquad
 V=Y\sin(t_aZ_n),\qquad
 R=(Y-\theta_0Z_n)\sin(t_aZ_n).
\]
The residual identity is \(Y-\theta_0Z_n=b_n(X)+\xi\).  Conditional shift annihilation gives
\[
 E_P[b_n(X)\sin(t_aZ_n)]=0,
\]
and outcome mean independence gives
\[
 E_P[\xi\sin(t_aZ_n)]
 =E_P[\sin(t_aZ_n)E_P(\xi\mid X,T)]=0.
\]
Therefore \(E_P[R]=0\) and \(E_P[V]=\theta_0m_a\), establishing the population identifying moment separately from the sample ratio.

Clipping and the range condition give \(|D_n|\le2C_g\).  Since \(E[\eta_a^2]=1\) and \(\eta_a\perp D_n\),
\[
 E_P[W^2]\le E_P[Z_n^2]
 =1+E_P[D_n^2]\le1+4C_g^2=:K_W.
\]
Also \(|b_n(X)|\le C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g\).  The conditional sub-Gaussian premise implies \(E_P[\xi^2]\le c_{\psi}\psi_{\xi}^2\) for a universal convention-dependent \(c_{\psi}<\infty\).  Thus
\[
 E_P[R^2]
 \le2(C_q+2C_{\theta}C_g)^2+2c_{\psi}\psi_{\xi}^2=:K_R.
\]
Both constants are uniform in \(a,n,P\).

Write \(\overline W=\mathbb P_nW\), \(\overline V=\mathbb P_nV\), and \(\mathcal E=\{\overline W\ge A_a/4\}\).  Projection onto \([-C_{\theta},C_{\theta}]\) is a contraction and \(\theta_0\) belongs to that interval.  On \(\mathcal E\),
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0|
 \le\frac{|\mathbb P_nR|}{\overline W}
 \le\frac4{A_a}|\mathbb P_nR|.
\]
Independence and \(E_P[R]=0\) yield
\[
 E_P[(\mathbb P_nR)^2]\le K_R/n.
\]
On \(\mathcal E^c\), the squared error is at most \(C_{\theta}^2\), while Chebyshev's inequality and \(m_a\ge A_a/2\) give
\[
 P(\mathcal E^c)
 \le P(|\overline W-m_a|\ge A_a/4)
 \le\frac{16K_W}{nA_a^2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 E_P[(\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0)^2]
 \le\frac{16(K_R+C_{\theta}^2K_W)}{nA_a^2}.
\]
The independent deterministic bound \(|\widehat\theta_{a,n}-\theta_0|^2\le4C_{\theta}^2\), together with
\[
 A_a^{-2}=a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\},
\]
proves the first risk display.  Finally \(\Delta_a=2a^4\) gives
\[
 a=2^{-1/4}\Delta_a^{1/4},
 \qquad
 t_a\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac12
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \varepsilon_{1,n}\le\frac{\Delta_a^{1/4}}{2^{1/4}\pi},
\]
and
\[
 a^{-2}\exp\!\left\{\frac{(1-a^2)\pi^2}{4a^2}\right\}
 =\sqrt2e^{-\pi^2/4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left\{\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4}\Delta_a^{-1/2}\right\}.
\]
Absorbing the fixed prefactor into \(C\) completes the proof.  No supplied outcome code is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma records the reviewer-identified sharp premise for the explicit path calculation: only first absolute moment control of the treatment-code residual is consumed. \textit{Gap.} The result is path-specific and supplies no full-class lower bound. \textit{Consumer.} It permits the Gaussian--Rademacher benchmark to be reused under the weakest norm condition actually needed by the proof.

\begin{lemma}[lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2]\label{lem:empirical-transform-uniform-l2}
For \(n\ge2\), there is \(K<\infty\), depending only on \(C_{\theta},C_g,C_q,\psi_{\eta},\psi_{\xi}\), and \(R_1\), such that, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\) and every \(a\in\{0,1\}\) with \(|I_a|\ge1\),
\[
 E_P\!\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|^2\right]
 +E_P\!\left[\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|^2\right]
 \le \frac{K}{|I_a|}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(m=|I_a|\) and put \(B=2C_g\).  Under the exact Luxemburg convention, for every \(p>0\),
\[
 E_P|W|^p\le 2\sigma^p\Gamma(p/2+1) \tag{1}
\]
when \(W=\eta,\sigma=\psi_\eta\), and also when \(W=\xi,\sigma=\psi_\xi\) after integrating the conditional bound.  Define \(A_0=C_0=1\) and, for \(j\ge1\),
\[
 A_j=2^{j-1/2}\{2\psi_\eta^{2j}j!+B^{2j}\}^{1/2},\qquad
 C_j=2^{j-1/4}\{2\psi_\eta^{4j}(2j)!+B^{4j}\}^{1/4}.
\]
Since \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\) and \(|D_n|\le B\), (1) and \(|x+y|^p\le2^{p-1}(|x|^p+|y|^p)\) give \(\|Z_n^j\|_2\le A_j\) and \(\|Z_n^j\|_4\le C_j\).  Moreover, \(\|\eta\|_4\le\sqrt2\psi_\eta\) and \(\|\xi\|_4\le\sqrt2\psi_\xi\).  Hence, with \(Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi\), Hölder's inequality gives, without requiring independence between \(\xi\) and \(\eta\),
\[
 \|YZ_n^j\|_2\le C_qA_j+\sqrt2(C_\theta\psi_\eta+\psi_\xi)C_j=:H_j. \tag{2}
\]
Set
\[
 S_F=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{R_1^jA_j}{j!},\qquad S_G=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{R_1^jH_j}{j!}.
\]
Both series are finite.  Indeed,
\[
 A_j\le2^j\psi_\eta^j\sqrt{j!}+2^{j-1/2}B^j,\quad
 C_j\le2^j\psi_\eta^j((2j)!)^{1/4}+2^{j-1/4}B^j,
\]
and the ratios of the two factorial-denominator terms tend to zero, while the remaining terms are dominated by exponential series.

For \(h_j=Z_n^j\) or \(h_j=YZ_n^j\), independence of the observations and the variance bound by the second moment give
\[
 \|(\mathbb P_{I_a}-P)h_j\|_2\le m^{-1/2}\|h_j\|_2. \tag{3}
\]
Apply the triangle inequality and Minkowski's inequality in the correct direction to finite power-series truncations, use (2)--(3), and pass to the limit by the displayed summable majorants.  This yields
\[
 \left\|\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat F_{a,n}(z)-F_n(z)|\right\|_2\le\frac{S_F}{\sqrt m},\qquad
 \left\|\sup_{|z|\le R_1}|\widehat G_{a,n}(z)-G_n(z)|\right\|_2\le\frac{S_G}{\sqrt m}.
\]
Squaring and adding proves the claim with the explicit finite constant \(K=S_F^2+S_G^2\), depending only on the listed primitive constants and \(R_1\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A power-series and sub-Gaussian moment calculation gives uniform \(L^2\) control of both analytic empirical transforms at the parametric rate. \textit{Gap.} The proof requires separate summable coefficient bounds for the unweighted and outcome-weighted transforms; an exponential-envelope norm cannot replace the coefficient-norm series by a reversed Minkowski inequality. \textit{Consumer.} The bound controls pilot failure and the evaluation contour ratio without entropy loss.

\begin{lemma}[lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free]\label{lem:l1-nuisance-zero-free}
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), the class's \(L^1(P_X)\) treatment-code restriction and clipping imply \(|D_n|\le2C_g\) almost surely and \(E_P|D_n|\le\varepsilon_{1,n}\). Hence
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le R_1}|H_n(z)-1|
 \le R_1e^{2C_gR_1}\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0\), then \(|H_n(z)|\ge3/4\) throughout \(\{|z|\le R_1\}\), and \(F_n=M H_n\) and \(M\) have the same zeros there with multiplicity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both \(g_0(X)\) and \(\bar g_n(X)\) lie in \([-C_g,C_g]\), so \(|D_n|\le2C_g\) almost surely.  The class restriction is exactly \(E_P|D_n|\le\varepsilon_{1,n}\).  For \(|z|\le R_1\), the identity
\(e^{zD_n}-1=zD_n\int_0^1e^{tzD_n}\,dt\) gives
\[
 |H_n(z)-1|
 \le |z|E_P[|D_n|e^{|z||D_n|}]
 \le R_1e^{2C_gR_1}\varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{1,n}\le\varepsilon_0=(4R_1e^{2C_gR_1})^{-1}\), the right side is at most \(1/4\); hence \(|H_n(z)|\ge3/4\) on the whole disk.  The factorization lemma gives \(F_n=MH_n\).  Since the second factor is holomorphic and nowhere zero there, multiplication by it neither creates nor removes zeros and preserves every zero multiplicity.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Direct first-moment control of the learned-residual shift is exactly what keeps the nuisance transform uniformly near one. \textit{Gap.} No higher treatment-code norm or outcome-code condition is required for spectral stability. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma transfers treatment-transform zeros and multiplicities to the observable residual transform.

\begin{proposition}[prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy]\label{prop:bounded-outcome-gaussian-degeneracy}
For every \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\), the simultaneous restrictions in \(\text{def:gaussian-class}\) imply \(\theta_0(P)=0\). If \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\neq\varnothing\), then the zero statistic is minimax and
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}
 =\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}
 =0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm G,n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\). By the residual definitions,
\[
 Y=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta+\xi.
\]
The restriction \(E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0\) and the identity \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\) imply
\[
 E_P[Y\mid X,T]=q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta. \tag{1}
\]
Since \(|Y|\le C_q\) almost surely, conditional Jensen gives
\[
 |q_0(X)+\theta_0\eta|
 =|E_P[Y\mid X,T]|\le C_q
 \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{2}
\]
The Gaussian-class assumptions give \(\eta\perp X\) and \(\eta\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma>0\). If \(\theta_0\ne0\), then for every finite real number \(u\), the nondegenerate Gaussian variable \(u+\theta_0\eta\) has unbounded support, and therefore
\[
 P(|u+\theta_0\eta|>C_q)>0. \tag{3}
\]
Taking \(u=q_0(x)\) and using independence, (3) holds for \(P_X\)-almost every conditional law given \(X=x\), contradicting (2). Hence \(\theta_0(P)=0\).

If the class is nonempty, the statistic identically equal to zero has squared error zero under every member. It also has absolute error zero almost surely, so its generalized quantile is zero. Both minimax losses are nonnegative by definition; consequently
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm G}
 =\mathfrak M_{n,1-\gamma}^{\mathrm G}=0.
\]
Thus the zero statistic is minimax for both displayed losses.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A bounded observed outcome forces its conditional mean to be bounded, whereas a nonzero coefficient multiplying nondegenerate Gaussian treatment noise has unbounded conditional support. \textit{Gap.} This diagnoses a simultaneous assumption intersection and says nothing about a general Gaussian PLM class. \textit{Consumer.} Use only to explain why the source-stated simultaneous intersection has zero target and zero minimax loss.

\begin{lemma}[lem:jms-ace-class-relations]\label{lem:jms-ace-class-relations}
For every \(n\),
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}.
\]
For \(s\ge r\),
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}
 \subseteq \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}},
\]
and, under the common clipped-code convention in the displayed class definitions, equality holds when \(s=r\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\).  First let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\).  Every structural, range, tail, independence, and cumulant condition in \(\text{def:non-gaussian-class}\) occurs verbatim in \(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\).  The only condition of the former definition that is not literally displayed with the same exponent in the latter is the direct treatment-code restriction.  Since \(P_X\) is a probability law and \(r\ge2\), H\"older's inequality gives
\[
 \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^1(P_X)}
 \le \|\bar g_n-g_0\|_{L^r(P_X)}
 \le \varepsilon_{1,n}.
\]
The broad class imposes no supplied outcome-code restriction.  Hence \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), proving
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}. \tag{1}
\]

Now suppose \(s\ge r\) and let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\).  On the probability space \((\mathcal X,\mathcal A,P_X)\), norm monotonicity gives
\[
 \|f\|_{L^r(P_X)}\le \|f\|_{L^s(P_X)},
\]
with the essential-supremum interpretation when \(s=\infty\).  Apply this inequality to \(f=\bar g_n-g_0\) and to \(f=\bar q_n-q_0\).  The two \(L^s(P_X)\) restrictions in \(\text{def:ace-comparison-subclass}\) therefore imply the two \(L^r(P_X)\) restrictions in \(\text{def:jms-ace-class}\), while all other conditions are inherited from \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\).  Thus
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}. \tag{2}
\]

Finally suppose \(s=r\).  Inclusion (2) remains valid.  Conversely, (1) places every member of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ACE},n}^{\mathrm{JMS}}\) in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}\), and its two defining \(L^r(P_X)\) code bounds are exactly the two additional restrictions defining \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{NG},n}^{\mathrm{ACE}}\) when \(s=r\).  Because the same clipped functions \(\bar g_n\) and \(\bar q_n\) occur in both definitions, this proves the reverse inclusion and hence equality.
\end{proof}
