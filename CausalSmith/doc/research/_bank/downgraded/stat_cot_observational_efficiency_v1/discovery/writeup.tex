\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}cot\_\allowbreak{}observational\_\allowbreak{}efficiency}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Smooth conditional-optimal-transport inference already includes the wavelet stability and randomized marginal-sample asymptotic normality of Lin--Gao--Blanchet--Glynn. For the sharp lower endpoint on mean squared individual treatment effects, this note establishes three different observational results: the covariate-composition plus inverse-propensity canonical gradient under ignorability and overlap; the semiparametric information lower bound together with a propensity-corrected cross-fitted one-step estimator that attains it; and uniform studentized normality and Wald coverage on admissible positive-variance collections, including \(\sigma_n\to0\), under \(\sqrt n\,\delta_n/\sigma_n\to0\), \(r_{S,n}/\sigma_n\to0\), and \(\sqrt n\,\sigma_n\to\infty\). At equality, the first-order pivot fails and the unregularized full-tangent Gaussian spectral route is obstructed by a noncompact, non-Hilbert--Schmidt Hessian; an honest full-parent adaptive procedure using controlled regularization or a different calibration remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Lin--Gao--Blanchet--Glynn already provide smooth-density wavelet conditional-optimal-transport stability and pointwise asymptotic normality for randomized marginal samples. Their theorem does not provide the observational tangent-space gradient with both covariate-composition and inverse-propensity terms, an information lower bound with an attaining observational estimator, or uniform studentized coverage along admissible sequences with \(\sigma_n\to0\). Other conditional-optimal-transport work supplies covariate-aware identification, observational direct-value rates, or AIPW inference for fitted dual bounds, but not this three-part efficiency-and-coverage result for the sharp smooth primal endpoint. The spectral existence criterion is settled; the remaining inference gap is a full-parent adaptive confidence procedure outside the ruled-out unregularized full-tangent Gaussian route.

\paragraph{Niche.} The note treats the sharp conditional-coupling endpoint as an identified causal estimand observed through one i.i.d. observational sample. Relative to randomized smooth COT asymptotic normality, it derives the propensity-corrected observed-data canonical gradient. Relative to observational direct-COT value estimation and dual-bound AIPW inference, it proves the information lower bound and constructs a cross-fitted one-step estimator attaining it. Relative to positive-variance pointwise Gaussian theory, it retains the shrinking \(\sigma_n\) scale in the variance expansion and proves uniform studentized validity under explicit relative-rate conditions.

\paragraph{Fill.} The results give the observed-data canonical gradient and efficiency bound, the matching information lower bound, an attaining cross-fitted estimator, the exact signed transport and propensity bias decomposition, a scale-aware variance error of order \(\sigma_n r_{S,n}+r_{S,n}^2+(\sigma_n+r_{S,n})/\sqrt n\), uniform normality and Wald coverage under the three displayed relative-rate conditions, and explicit H\"older phase conditions. These conclusions complement rather than replace earlier smooth COT wavelet inference, observational direct-value estimation, and dual-bound AIPW inference. The note also proves a route-specific equality obstruction: the full-tangent conditional Hessian can contain an infinite-dimensional identity compression and hence be noncompact and non-Hilbert--Schmidt, so uniform unregularized Gaussian spectral calibration is unavailable without covariate-direction regularization or another derivative-aware method.

\section{Related work}

Lin, Gao, Blanchet, and Glynn's 2026 smooth-density paper supplies wavelet conditional-optimal-transport stability and a pointwise asymptotic-normality result for randomized marginal samples; it does not supply the observational covariate-composition plus inverse-propensity canonical gradient, an observed-data information lower bound with an attaining cross-fitted estimator, or the present uniform studentized coverage theorem for admissible \(\sigma_n\to0\) collections. Their covariate-aware tightening paper supplies conditional-transport identification and bound-tightening structure, not observational semiparametric efficiency or Wald theory. Their direct conditional-optimal-transport estimation paper supplies adapted-Wasserstein continuity and an observational finite-sample value-rate route using cross-fitting and self-normalized propensity weighting; it does not derive the efficient influence function, efficiency attainment, studentizer consistency, or uniform Wald coverage. Ji--Lei--Spector supply observational cross-fitted AIPW inference for fitted dual transport bounds and the 401(k) SIPP application; that construction can retain weak-duality slack and does not establish efficiency for the sharp smooth primal endpoint or vanishing-variance studentization. Manole--Balakrishnan--Niles-Weed--Wasserman supply the smooth unconditional Brenier stability, plug-in limit theory, Wald inference away from equality, and marginal-sample efficiency substrate; their model has neither the causal covariate-composition term nor observational inverse-propensity correction. Tan--Blanchet--Syrgkanis study optimal-transport partial identification of policy-relevant treatment effects with instrumental variables, a different estimand and identifying design, and do not provide the present conditional observational efficiency theorem. Chernozhukov and coauthors and Newey--Robins provide the general orthogonal-score and cross-fitting templates; here the transport-specific ingredients are the signed quadratic Brenier remainder and the variance expansion that retains the shrinking canonical-gradient scale. At exact equality, the weighted-Neumann second variation and non-Hilbert--Schmidt full-tangent obstruction leave regularized or alternative adaptive calibration unresolved; the Gaussian-series existence and uniform-tail criteria themselves are settled.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-iid]\label{ass:observed-iid}
\(O_{1,n},\ldots,O_{n,n}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (i.i.d. sampling, \cite{chernozhukov2018double}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y_n=A_nY_n(1)+(1-A_n)Y_n(0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (consistency, \cite{imbens2015causal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:ignorability]\label{ass:ignorability}
\((Y_n(0),Y_n(1))\perp A_n\mid X_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (conditional ignorability, \cite{imbens2015causal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-overlap]\label{ass:fixed-overlap}
\(\varepsilon\le\pi_n(X_n)\le1-\varepsilon\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (strong overlap, \cite{crump2009dealing}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:compact-support]\label{ass:compact-support}
\(P_n(X_n\in\mathcal X,Y_n\in\mathcal Y)=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (compact support, \cite{lin2026statistical}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-arm-densities]\label{ass:bounded-arm-densities}
\(c\le q_{a,n}(x,y)\le C\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) and every \((x,y)\in\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded conditional densities, \cite{lin2026statistical}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-arm-densities]\label{ass:holder-arm-densities}
\(q_{a,n}\in\Sigma(s,L;\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y)\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder-ball conditional densities, \cite{lin2026statistical}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:variance-separated]\label{ass:variance-separated}
\(\sigma_n\ge\sigma_\star\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform nondegenerate variance, \cite{chernozhukov2018double}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:regular-support-geometry]\label{ass:regular-support-geometry}
\(\mathcal X\) is the closure of a bounded connected Lipschitz domain in \(\mathbb R^{d_X}\), and \(\mathcal Y\) is the closure of a bounded connected uniformly convex \(C^2\) domain with nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{d_Y}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} standard: connected-support first variation in Manole et al. (2024), Lemma 23, and standard global Brenier regularity on uniformly convex domains
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-conditional-brenier-smoothness]\label{ass:uniform-conditional-brenier-smoothness}
With \(\mathfrak F\) denoting the feasible conditional-density class in \(def:coupled-fold-learners\), the quadratic Brenier potential \(b_{f_0,f_1,x}\) transporting \(f_0(x,\cdot)\) to \(f_1(x,\cdot)\) belongs to \(C^2(\mathcal Y)\) for every \(f_0,f_1\in\mathfrak F\) and almost every \(x\).

\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-conditional-brenier-lower-curvature]\label{ass:uniform-conditional-brenier-lower-curvature}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform A1-type lower Brenier curvature, manole2024plugin, \cite{manole2024plugin}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-conditional-brenier-upper-curvature]\label{ass:uniform-conditional-brenier-upper-curvature}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform A1-type upper Brenier curvature, manole2024plugin, \cite{manole2024plugin}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-propensity]\label{ass:holder-propensity}

\begin{definition}[def:coupled-fold-learners]\label{def:coupled-fold-learners}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Normalized coupled foldwise transport learners}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Choose deterministic folds satisfying \(\max_{1\le k\le K}||I_{k,n}|-n/K|\le1\). Let \(\mathfrak F\) be the class of \(f\in\Sigma(s,L;\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y)\) such that \(c\le f\le C\) and \(\int_{\mathcal Y}f(x,y)d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(y)=1\) for almost every \(x\). Let \(\mathfrak F_{J_n}\) be a fixed finite \(L^\infty\)-net of \(\mathfrak F\), constructed by quantizing boundary-adapted tensor-wavelet coefficients through level \(J_n\), with mesh order \(2^{-J_ns}\) and logarithmic cardinality order \(2^{J_nD}\). Put \(N_{a,-k,n}=\sum_{j\notin I_{k,n}}1\{A_{j,n}=a\}\). If \(N_{a,-k,n}>0\), define \(\widehat q_{a,-k,n}\) as the lexicographically first minimizer over \(f\in\mathfrak F_{J_n}\) of \[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \frac1{N_{a,-k,n}}\sum_{j\notin I_{k,n}:A_{j,n}=a}\left\{\int_{\mathcal Y}f(X_{j,n},y)^2d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(y)-2f(X_{j,n},Y_{j,n})\right\}.\)}}\par\noindent}}
\] If \(N_{a,-k,n}=0\), use the first element of \(\mathfrak F_{J_n}\). For every \(k,n\), fix before observing the data a rule \(\mathsf A_{\pi,k,n}\) such that \((t,x)\mapsto\mathsf A_{\pi,k,n}(t)(x)\in(0,1)\) is jointly measurable for a training sample \(t\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\). Set \(\widehat\pi_{-k,n}=\mathsf A_{\pi,k,n}(\mathcal T_{-k,n})\), set \(\bar\pi_{-k,n}=\min\{1-\varepsilon,\max\{\varepsilon,\widehat\pi_{-k,n}\}\}\), and define \(\widehat Q_{a,-k,n}(B\mid x)=\int_B\widehat q_{a,-k,n}(x,y)d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(y)\). Require dual feasibility, contact on \(\widehat\gamma_{-k,n}\), and \(\widehat V_{-k,n}(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\int\widehat u_{a,-k,n}(x,y)\widehat Q_{a,-k,n}(dy\mid x)\). \section{Main results}

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:degeneracy-adaptive-inference]\label{oeq:degeneracy-adaptive-inference}
The Gaussian spectral subproblem is settled: square summability is necessary and sufficient for \(L^2\) existence of the centered Gaussian quadratic series, and a uniform square-summable tail is necessary and sufficient for uniform finite-rank approximation. Does there exist an honest, degeneracy-adaptive confidence procedure uniformly over the full variance-unrestricted parent, outside the ruled-out unregularized full-tangent Gaussian spectral route, that is first-order efficient away from equality and correctly calibrated at equality; and which covariate-direction regularization, regularized-Hessian estimability, or alternative derivative-aware calibration conditions are necessary and sufficient?
\end{openendedquestion}
The exact frontier currently proved is as follows.  At every fixed positive-variance regular law, \(\psi_n\) is the canonical gradient and \(\sigma_n^2\) is the efficiency bound.  On positive-variance triangular collections satisfying \[\frac{\sqrt n\,\delta_n}{\sigma_n}\to0,\qquad \frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}\to0,\qquad \sqrt n\,\sigma_n\to\infty\] uniformly, the cross-fitted estimator is asymptotically linear with influence function \(\psi_n\), its studentizer is ratio consistent, and its Wald interval has uniform asymptotic coverage.  At conditional equality, \(\theta_n=0\), \(\psi_n=0\), and purely first-order Gaussian calibration fails.  For a centered Gaussian quadratic calibration, square summability is necessary and sufficient for \(L^2\) existence, and a uniformly vanishing square-summable tail is necessary and sufficient for uniform finite-rank approximation.  The full parent violates these requirements at the identity-compression equality witness.  Consequently, removing that compression, obtaining a uniform Hilbert--Schmidt tail, and uniformly estimating the regularized Hessian are necessary for the regularized spectral route; their primitive sufficiency, and the existence or impossibility of an alternative derivative-aware route, remain unresolved.
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact spectral obstruction identifies what a successful adaptive procedure must overcome, but the frozen parent supplies no uniformly estimable regularization or alternative calibration. \textit{Gap.} Necessary and sufficient primitive conditions for full-parent honest adaptation remain unknown. \textit{Consumer.} A solution would determine uncertainty reporting when the conditional outcome laws are nearly or exactly equal.

\begin{lemma}[lem:manole-unconditional-smooth-wasserstein-efficiency]\label{lem:manole-unconditional-smooth-wasserstein-efficiency}
For the unconditional functional \(W_2^2(P,Q)\) based on one sample from one marginal or two independent marginal samples, Manole, Balakrishnan, Niles-Weed, and Wasserman derive centered plug-in central limit theorems, consistent plug-in variance estimators and Wald intervals away from \(P=Q\), the efficient one-sample influence function given by the centered Kantorovich potential, and one- and two-sample local asymptotic minimax lower bounds matching the corresponding potential variances under their smooth positive-density conditions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:canonical-gradient-information-inequality]\label{lem:canonical-gradient-information-inequality}
In a dominated semiparametric model, if a scalar parameter has canonical gradient \(\psi\in L_0^2(P)\), then every regular asymptotically linear estimator has asymptotic variance at least \(E_P[\psi^2]\). An asymptotically linear estimator whose influence function is \(\psi\) attains this lower bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-net-conditional-density-rate]\label{lem:finite-net-conditional-density-rate}
For the constrained finite-net empirical-risk learners in \(def:coupled-fold-learners\), under i.i.d. sampling, fixed overlap, bounded arm densities, and Hölder arm densities,
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \max_{k,a}\lVert\widehat q_{a,-k,n}-q_{a,n}\rVert_{L^2(P_{X,n}\otimes\ell_{\mathcal Y})}=O_{P_n}(2^{-J_ns}+\sqrt{2^{J_nD}/n})=O_{P_n}(n^{-s/(2s+D)})\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]uniformly over \(\mathbf P\in\mathscr P\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a fold and arm and condition on the training treatment labels. If \(N\) is the arm count and \(m\asymp n\) the training size, overlap gives \(P(N<\varepsilon m/2)\le e^{-c_0m}\) uniformly. Conditional on \(N\), the selected observations are independent with law \(P_n(\cdot\mid A=a)\). For
\[\ell_f(x,y)=\int f(x,z)^2d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(z)-2f(x,y),\]
conditioning on \(X\) yields the exact excess-risk identity
\[\mathcal R(f)=E[\ell_f-\ell_q\mid A=a]=\lVert f-q\rVert_{L^2(P_{X\mid A=a}\otimes\ell_{\mathcal Y})}^2.\tag{1}\]
Uniform boundedness gives \(|\ell_f-\ell_q|\le B\) and a Bernstein variance bound \(\operatorname{Var}(\ell_f-\ell_q\mid A=a)\le B\mathcal R(f)\). Bernstein's inequality and a union bound over \(\mathfrak F_{J_n}\) therefore imply, with probability at least \(1-2e^{-u}\), simultaneously for every candidate,
\[|({\mathbb P}_N-P)(\ell_f-\ell_q)|\le\frac14\mathcal R(f)+C_1\frac{\log|\mathfrak F_{J_n}|+u}{N}.\tag{2}\]
By the net construction there exists \(f^\circ\) with \(\lVert f^\circ-q\rVert_\infty\le C_2 2^{-J_ns}\), while \(\log|\mathfrak F_{J_n}|\lesssim2^{J_nD}\). The empirical minimizing property, applied to (2) for \(\widehat q\) and \(f^\circ\), gives
\[\mathcal R(\widehat q)\le C_3\left\{2^{-2J_ns}+\frac{2^{J_nD}+u}{n}\right\}.\tag{3}\]
Overlap makes the \(L^2(P_{X\mid A=a}\otimes\ell_{\mathcal Y})\) and \(L^2(P_X\otimes\ell_{\mathcal Y})\) norms uniformly equivalent. Taking square roots, a union bound over fixed \(2K\) fold--arm pairs, and \(2^{J_nD}\asymp n^{D/(2s+D)}\) yield
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \max_{k,a}\lVert\widehat q_{a,-k,n}-q_{a,n}\rVert_2=O_{P_n}\!\left(2^{-J_ns}+\sqrt{2^{J_nD}/n}\right)=O_{P_n}\!\left(n^{-s/(2s+D)}\right)\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathscr P\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:conditional-coupling-sharpness]\label{lem:conditional-coupling-sharpness}
Let \(P_X\) be a probability law on \(\mathcal X\), and let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be jointly measurable probability kernels from \(\mathcal X\) to the compact metric space \(\mathcal Y\). Among all probability laws \(\widetilde P\) on \(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y^2\) whose \(X\)-marginal is \(P_X\) and whose conditional \(Y_a\)-marginal given \(X=x\) is \(Q_a(\cdot\mid x)\),
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \inf_{\widetilde P}E_{\widetilde P}[\lVert Y_1-Y_0\rVert_2^2]=\int_{\mathcal X}W_2^2(Q_0(\cdot\mid x),Q_1(\cdot\mid x))dP_X(x).\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
The infimum is attained by a jointly measurable conditional optimal-coupling kernel. If, in addition, \(A\mid X=x\) is drawn independently of \((Y_0,Y_1)\mid X=x\) with probabilities \(\pi_a(x)>0\), and \(Y=Y_A\), the resulting law is a causal extension satisfying consistency and conditional ignorability and having observed arm kernels \(Q_a\). Hence the displayed value is a sharp lower endpoint over causal extensions with these conditional marginals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Disintegrate any admissible law as \(P_X(dx)\gamma_x(dy_0,dy_1)\). The marginal restrictions imply \(\gamma_x\in\Gamma(Q_0(\cdot\mid x),Q_1(\cdot\mid x))\) almost surely, so pointwise optimality and Tonelli give
\[E\lVert Y_1-Y_0\rVert_2^2\ge\int W_2^2(Q_0(\cdot\mid x),Q_1(\cdot\mid x))dP_X(x).\tag{1}\]
For attainment, \(\mathcal P(\mathcal Y^2)\) is compact in the weak topology because \(\mathcal Y\) is compact. For each \(x\), the coupling constraint is a nonempty compact section and the continuous quadratic objective has a nonempty compact argmin section. Its graph is measurable: marginal equalities may be tested on a fixed countable dense subset of \(C(\mathcal Y)\), and the optimal value is the limit of finite-partition transport programs with measurable coefficients. A measurable selector can be constructed by successively intersecting each section with the first ball, from a countable dense family, of radius \(2^{-m}\) that meets the preceding section. The nested sections have diameter tending to zero, and their limit \(\gamma_x^\star\) is measurable and optimal. Thus \(P_X(dx)\gamma_x^\star(dy_0,dy_1)\) attains equality in (1).

Finally draw \(A\mid X=x\) independently of \((Y_0,Y_1)\mid X=x\), with probabilities \(\pi_a(x)>0\), and set \(Y=Y_A\). Then consistency and conditional ignorability hold and \(\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a,X=x)=Q_a(\cdot\mid x)\). Hence the minimizing coupling is a compatible causal extension, proving sharpness.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:uniform-canonical-gradient-bound]\label{lem:uniform-canonical-gradient-bound}
There is a finite constant \(M_\psi\), depending only on \(\varepsilon\) and \(\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal Y)\), such that every \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) satisfying \(ass:regular-support-geometry\) obeys \(E_{P_n}[\psi_n(O_{1,n})]=0\) and \(|\psi_n(O_{1,n})|\le M_\psi\) almost surely. Consequently, \(\sup_{n,P_n}E_{P_n}[|\psi_n|^r]\le M_\psi^r\) for every \(r\ge1\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(R=\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal Y)\). Since the quadratic cost lies in \([0,R^2]\), \(0\le V_n(x),\theta_n\le R^2\). For \(y,y',z\in\mathcal Y\),
\[|\lVert y-z\rVert_2^2-\lVert y'-z\rVert_2^2|\le2R\lVert y-y'\rVert_2\le2R^2.\]
Taking the relevant conjugacy infimum or supremum preserves this oscillation bound, so \(\operatorname{osc}(u_{a,n}(x,\cdot))\le2R^2\), and centering gives \(|\phi_{a,n}|le2R^2\). Exactly one arm indicator is nonzero and \(\pi_{a,n}\ge\varepsilon\), hence
\[|\psi_n(O)|\le R^2+\frac{2R^2}{\varepsilon}=:M_\psi.\]
Moreover,
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle E\!\left[\frac{1\{A=a\}}{\pi_{a,n}(X)}\phi_{a,n}(X,Y)\,\middle|\,X\right]=\int\phi_{a,n}(X,y)Q_{a,P_n}(dy\mid X)=0,\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
and \(E[V_n(X)-\theta_n]=0\). Thus \(E\psi_n=0\). The almost-sure envelope gives \(E|\psi_n|^r\le M_\psi^r\) for every \(r\ge1\), uniformly.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:wasserstein-l2-density-control]\label{lem:wasserstein-l2-density-control}
If \(\mathcal Y\) is a bounded connected Lipschitz domain and \(f,g\in L^2(\mathcal Y)\) are probability densities satisfying \(f,g\ge c>0\), then
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle W_2^2(f\,d\ell_{\mathcal Y},g\,d\ell_{\mathcal Y})\le c^{-1}C_{\mathcal Y}^2\lVert f-g\rVert_{L^2(\ell_{\mathcal Y})}^2,\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
where \(C_{\mathcal Y}\) is a Poincar\'e constant of \(\mathcal Y\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(h=g-f\), so \(\int_{\mathcal Y}h\,d\ell_{\mathcal Y}=0\). On the mean-zero subspace of \(H^1(\mathcal Y)\), the Poincar\'e inequality makes the functional
\[\eta\mapsto\frac12\int\lVert\nabla\eta\rVert_2^2d\ell_{\mathcal Y}-\int h\eta\,d\ell_{\mathcal Y}\]
coercive and strictly convex. Its minimizer \(\varphi\) satisfies
\[\int\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\eta=\int h\eta,\qquad \lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_2\le C_{\mathcal Y}\lVert h\rVert_2.\tag{1}\]
Set \(\rho_t=(1-t)f+tg\), \(j_t=\nabla\varphi\), and \(v_t=j_t/\rho_t\). Then \(\partial_t\rho_t+\nabla\cdot j_t=0\), the weak boundary flux is zero, and \(\rho_t\ge c\). The dynamic transport bound obtained by regularizing this weak continuity equation and passing to the limit gives
\[W_2^2(f\,d\ell_{\mathcal Y},g\,d\ell_{\mathcal Y})\le\int_0^1\!\int\rho_t\lVert v_t\rVert_2^2d\ell_{\mathcal Y}dt.\tag{2}\]
Using \(\rho_t\ge c\) and (1) in (2) yields
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle W_2^2(f\,d\ell_{\mathcal Y},g\,d\ell_{\mathcal Y})\le c^{-1}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_2^2\le c^{-1}C_{\mathcal Y}^2\lVert f-g\rVert_2^2.\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:equality-degeneracy-and-second-variation]\label{lem:equality-degeneracy-and-second-variation}
Let \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) satisfy \(\texttt{ass:regular-support-geometry}\). Then \(\sigma_n^2=0\) if and only if \(Q_{0,P_n}(\cdot\mid x)=Q_{1,P_n}(\cdot\mid x)\) for \(P_{X,n}\)-almost every \(x\). At such a law, \(\theta_n=0\), \(\psi_n=0\) almost surely, and the zero-denominator oracle first-order pivot is identically zero and has Kolmogorov distance \(1/2\) from \(N(0,1)\). Moreover, suppress the row index and consider a path that fixes \(P_X\) and \(\pi\), starts at conditional equality \(q_{0,0}=q_{1,0}=q\), and satisfies \(q_{a,t}=q+t h_a\), where \(\int_{\mathcal Y}h_a(x,y)d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(y)=0\). Suppose the path remains in \(\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), the functions \(q,h_0,h_1\) are twice continuously differentiable in \(y\) with uniform bounds, and the mean-zero weighted Neumann solution \(w_x\) satisfies
\[
-\nabla_y\cdot\{q(x,y)\nabla_yw_x(y)\}=h_1(x,y)-h_0(x,y),\qquad q(x,y)\nabla_yw_x(y)\cdot\mathbf n(y)=0,
\]
with \(w_x\), \(\nabla_yw_x\), and \(\nabla_y^2w_x\) jointly measurable and uniformly bounded. Then
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \theta(P_t)=t^2\mathcal E+o(t^2),\qquad \mathcal E=\int_{\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y}q(x,y)\lVert\nabla_yw_x(y)\rVert_2^2d(P_X\otimes\ell_{\mathcal Y})(x,y),\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
so the second directional derivative at equality is \(2\mathcal E\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppress \(n\). Suppose first that \(Q_0(\cdot\mid x)=Q_1(\cdot\mid x)=Q(\cdot\mid x)\). The diagonal coupling has zero cost, so \(V(x)=0\) and \(\theta=0\). Complementary slackness for any continuous mutually conjugate optimal pair gives \(u_0(x,y)+u_1(x,y)=0\) on the support of \(Q\), hence everywhere because the density is positive and the potentials are continuous. Dual feasibility gives
\[|u_1(x,z)-u_1(x,y)|\le\lVert z-y\rVert_2^2.\tag{1}\]
Partitioning the segment from \(y\) to \(z\) into \(m\) equal pieces makes the right side \(\lVert z-y\rVert_2^2/m\); letting \(m\to\infty\) shows that both potentials are constant. Thus \(\phi_a=0\), \(\psi=0\), and \(\sigma^2=0\).

Conversely, if \(\sigma^2=0\), then \(\psi=0\) almost surely. Conditional on \((X,A)=(x,a)\), fixed overlap gives
\[V(x)-\theta+\frac{\phi_a(x,Y)}{\pi_a(x)}=0\quad Q_a(\cdot\mid x)\text{-almost surely}.\tag{2}\]
Integrating (2) against \(Q_a\) gives \(V(x)=\theta\), and substituting back gives \(\phi_a(x,\cdot)=0\). Positivity of \(q_a\) and continuity make each \(u_a(x,\cdot)\) constant. Their sum is the dual value \(V(x)\), while dual feasibility at \(y_0=y_1\) makes the sum nonpositive. Since \(V(x)\ge0\), one has \(V(x)=0\), and the metric property of \(W_2\) gives \(Q_0(\cdot\mid x)=Q_1(\cdot\mid x)\). Also \(\theta=0\). The frozen convention makes the oracle pivot identically zero, and therefore its Kolmogorov distance from \(N(0,1)\) is \(1/2\).

For the second variation put \(\delta_x=h_1(x,\cdot)-h_0(x,\cdot)\),
\[\rho_{r,t}=q+t\{(1-r)h_0+rh_1\},\qquad j_{r,t}=tq\nabla_yw_x,\qquad 0\le r\le1.\]
The weighted Neumann equation gives \(\partial_r\rho_{r,t}+\nabla_y\cdot j_{r,t}=0\) and zero normal flux. Because \(\rho_{r,t}\) is uniformly bounded below, the flow of \(v_{r,t}=j_{r,t}/\rho_{r,t}\) couples the two endpoint densities. Cauchy--Schwarz along flow lines yields
\[W_2^2(q_{0,t}d\ell_{\mathcal Y},q_{1,t}d\ell_{\mathcal Y})\le\int_0^1\!\int\frac{\lVert j_{r,t}\rVert_2^2}{\rho_{r,t}}d\ell_{\mathcal Y}dr=t^2E_x+O(|t|^3),\tag{3}\]
where \(E_x=\int q(x,y)\lVert\nabla_yw_x(y)\rVert_2^2d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(y)\), and the remainder is uniform in \(x\). For the reverse bound let \(\gamma_{x,t}\) be optimal and \(W_{x,t}^2=\int\lVert y_1-y_0\rVert_2^2d\gamma_{x,t}\). The marginal constraints and weak Neumann identity give
\[tE_x=\int\{w_x(y_1)-w_x(y_0)\}d\gamma_{x,t}.\tag{4}\]
Taylor expansion on convex \(\mathcal Y\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, gives
\[|t|E_x\le\{E_x+O(|t|)\}^{1/2}W_{x,t}+\frac12\lVert\nabla_y^2w_x\rVert_\infty W_{x,t}^2.\tag{5}\]
Equation (3) gives \(W_{x,t}=O(|t|)\). If \(E_x>0\), divide (5) by \(|t|\) and take a lower limit; if \(E_x=0\), positivity of \(q\) gives \(\nabla w_x=0\) and then \(\delta_x=0\). In either case, together with (3),
\[W_{x,t}^2=t^2E_x+o(t^2).\tag{6}\]
The uniform upper bound from (3) permits dominated convergence over \(P_X\), so \(\theta(P_t)=t^2\int E_xdP_X(x)+o(t^2)=t^2\mathcal E+o(t^2)\). Hence the second directional derivative is \(2\mathcal E\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:gaussian-quadratic-spectral-criterion]\label{lem:gaussian-quadratic-spectral-criterion}
Let \(Z_1,Z_2,\ldots\) be independent \(N(0,1)\) variables and let \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\ldots\in\mathbb R\). The centered quadratic series \(\sum_{j\ge1}\lambda_j(Z_j^2-1)\) converges in \(L^2\) if and only if \(\sum_{j\ge1}\lambda_j^2<\infty\). If \(\lambda_j\ge0\), the uncentered series \(\sum_{j\ge1}\lambda_jZ_j^2\) converges in \(L^1\) if and only if \(\sum_{j\ge1}\lambda_j<\infty\). For a family \(\Lambda\) of square-summable sequences, common rank-\(J\) truncations approximate the centered series uniformly in \(L^2\) if and only if \(\lim_{J\to\infty}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sum_{j>J}\lambda_j^2=0\). If \(\lambda_j=\lambda_\circ>0\) for infinitely many \(j\), the centered partial sums have no weakly convergent subsequence.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(S_J=\sum_{j=1}^J\lambda_j(Z_j^2-1)\). Independence, centering, and \(E[(Z_j^2-1)^2]=2\) give, for \(J>L\),
\[E[(S_J-S_L)^2]=2\sum_{j=L+1}^J\lambda_j^2.\tag{1}\]
Thus \((S_J)\) is Cauchy in \(L^2\) exactly when \(\sum_j\lambda_j^2<\infty\). Completeness of \(L^2\) proves the first assertion. Taking the supremum over \(\lambda\in\Lambda\) in (1) proves both directions of the uniform truncation criterion.

If \(\lambda_j\ge0\), monotone convergence gives
\[E\!\left[\sum_{j\ge1}\lambda_jZ_j^2\right]=\sum_{j\ge1}\lambda_j.\tag{2}\]
A finite right side makes the nonnegative tails converge to zero in \(L^1\); conversely, \(L^1\) convergence bounds the expectations of the partial sums, so (2) forces summability.

Finally, the characteristic function of the full centered partial sum is
\[\varphi_{S_J}(t)=\prod_{j\le J}e^{-it\lambda_j}(1-2it\lambda_j)^{-1/2},\qquad |\varphi_{S_J}(t)|=\prod_{j\le J}(1+4t^2\lambda_j^2)^{-1/4}.\tag{3}\]
If \(m_J=|\{j\le J:\lambda_j=\lambda_\circ\}|\), then \(m_J\to\infty\) and, for every \(t\ne0\),
\[|\varphi_{S_J}(t)|\le(1+4t^2\lambda_\circ^2)^{-m_J/4}\longrightarrow0.\tag{4}\]
If a subsequence converged weakly to a real variable \(S\), its characteristic functions would converge to \(E[e^{itS}]\), which by (4) would vanish for every \(t\ne0\). This contradicts continuity at zero of every characteristic function. Hence no weakly convergent subsequence exists.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The full centered partial sums are controlled by their actual characteristic-function product, not by a selected subseries. \textit{Gap.} A selected-subseries argument does not by itself exclude convergence of the full partial sums. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the exact spectral compactness criterion and the full-partial-sum obstruction used by the degeneracy theorem.

\begin{lemma}[lem:conditional-hessian-spectral-obstruction]\label{lem:conditional-hessian-spectral-obstruction}
Suppose the variance-unrestricted parent class contains an equality law \(P^\circ\) for which \(P_X^\circ\) is nonatomic, \(\pi^\circ(x)=1/2\), and \(q_0^\circ(x,y)=q_1^\circ(x,y)=q^\circ\) is a constant density lying in the interior of the frozen density bounds and H\"older ball. Then the equality Hessian of \(\theta\), viewed on the closure of regular observed-data scores in \(L_0^2(P^\circ)\), is not compact. More precisely, there is an infinite-dimensional closed score subspace \(\mathcal E\) and a constant \(\lambda_\circ>0\) such that the compression to \(\mathcal E\) of any bounded self-adjoint Riesz representative \(H^\circ\) of the second derivative equals \(\lambda_\circ I_{\mathcal E}\). Consequently \(H^\circ\) is neither compact nor Hilbert--Schmidt, and no unregularized square-summable spectral representation exists at this law.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose nonconstant \(w\in C_c^\infty(\operatorname{int}\mathcal Y)\) and put
\[g(y)=-q^\circ\Delta w(y),\qquad \kappa=q^\circ\int_{\mathcal Y}\lVert\nabla w(y)\rVert_2^2d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(y)>0.\tag{1}\]
Then \(\int g=0\) and the zero-flux boundary condition holds. For bounded smooth \(e\) on \(\mathcal X\), perturb only arm one by \(q_{0,t}=q^\circ\) and \(q_{1,t}(x,y)=q^\circ+te(x)g(y)\). The interior condition keeps this path in the model for sufficiently small \(|t|\). Its score is
\[s_e(x,a,y)=1\{a=1\}\frac{e(x)g(y)}{q^\circ}.\tag{2}\]
Since \(\pi^\circ=1/2\),
\[\langle s_e,s_f\rangle_{L^2(P^\circ)}=c_g\langle e,f\rangle_{L^2(P_X^\circ)},\qquad c_g=\frac12\int\frac{g(y)^2}{q^\circ}d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(y)>0.\tag{3}\]
The Neumann solution for direction \(e(x)g(y)\) is \(e(x)w(y)\). Polarizing the energy formula in \(\texttt{lem:equality-degeneracy-and-second-variation}\) yields
\[D^2\theta_{P^\circ}[s_e,s_f]=2\kappa\langle e,f\rangle_{L^2(P_X^\circ)}=\lambda_\circ\langle s_e,s_f\rangle_{L^2(P^\circ)},\qquad \lambda_\circ=2\kappa/c_g>0.\tag{4}\]
The full outcome-score Hessian is bounded. Indeed, if \(-q^\circ\Delta v_x=d(x,\cdot)\) with zero flux and mean zero, integration by parts and Poincar\'e give
\[q^\circ\lVert\nabla v_x\rVert_2^2=\int d(x,y)v_x(y)d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(y)\le C_{\mathcal Y}\lVert d(x,\cdot)\rVert_2\lVert\nabla v_x\rVert_2.\tag{5}\]
Fixed overlap and the density bounds control the integrated energy by the squared observed-score norm. Polarization and the Riesz representation theorem therefore supply a bounded self-adjoint representative \(H^\circ\).

Because \(P_X^\circ\) is nonatomic, \(L^2(P_X^\circ)\) is infinite dimensional, and bounded smooth functions are dense. Hence \(\mathcal E=\overline{\{s_e:e\text{ bounded and smooth}\}}\) is infinite dimensional. Continuity extends (4) to \(\mathcal E\), giving
\[\Pi_{\mathcal E}H^\circ|_{\mathcal E}=\lambda_\circ I_{\mathcal E}.\tag{6}\]
If \(H^\circ\) were compact, the left side of (6) would be compact, but a positive multiple of the identity on an infinite-dimensional space is not compact. Thus \(H^\circ\) is not compact. Every Hilbert--Schmidt operator is compact, so it is not Hilbert--Schmidt and cannot possess an unregularized square-summable spectral representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:two-sample-quadratic-remainder]\label{lem:two-sample-quadratic-remainder}
Fix \(x\) in the common full-measure set from the three uniform conditional Brenier assumptions. Let \(\overline P=(\overline P_0,\overline P_1)\) and \(P=(P_0,P_1)\) be probability-law pairs on the convex set \(\mathcal Y\) such that every component has a density equal to the \(x\)-section of an element of \(\mathfrak F\) in \(\texttt{def:coupled-fold-learners}\); hence all four densities are normalized, belong to the common H\"older class, and lie between \(c\) and \(C\). Assume that for every ordered pair generated by these four densities, the corresponding quadratic Brenier potential belongs to \(C^2(\mathcal Y)\) and has Hessian between \(m_{\mathrm{OT}}I_{d_Y}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{OT}}I_{d_Y}\). Let \((\overline u_0,\overline u_1)\) be a mutually conjugate optimal quadratic dual pair at \(\overline P\), and define
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(P,\overline P)
=W_2^2(P_0,P_1)-W_2^2(\overline P_0,\overline P_1)
-\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\int\overline u_a\,d(P_a-\overline P_a).\)}}
\]
There is a finite constant \(C_{\mathrm{OT}}\), depending only on \(c,C,m_{\mathrm{OT}},M_{\mathrm{OT}}\) and the geometry of \(\mathcal Y\), such that
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle 0\le\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(P,\overline P)
\le C_{\mathrm{OT}}\{W_2(P_0,\overline P_0)+W_2(P_1,\overline P_1)\}^2.\)}}
\]
The assertion is unchanged by centering the base potentials.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(F(P)=W_2^2(P_0,P_1)\), \(d_a=W_2(P_a,\overline P_a)\), and let \((u_0,u_1)\) be an optimal dual pair at \(P\). Feasibility of the base optimizer at \(P\) gives \(\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(P,\overline P)\ge0\). Feasibility of the perturbed optimizer at \(\overline P\), subtracted from optimality at \(P\), gives
\[\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(P,\overline P)\le\sum_a\int(u_a-\overline u_a)d(P_a-\overline P_a).\tag{1}\]
The forward and reverse conclusions of \(\texttt{lem:two-sample-map-stability}\), the map--potential identities, and the density lower bound imply
\[\sum_a\lVert\nabla(u_a-\overline u_a)\rVert_{L^2(\ell_{\mathcal Y})}^2\le C_1(d_0+d_1)^2.\tag{2}\]
For each arm, let \(\gamma_a\) be an optimal coupling of \(\overline P_a\) and \(P_a\). Displacement interpolation remains in convex \(\mathcal Y\), and the endpoint density upper bound implies that every interpolating density is at most \(C\). Therefore, for every \(v\in H^1(\mathcal Y)\), the fundamental theorem of calculus along transport segments and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[\left|\int v\,d(P_a-\overline P_a)\right|\le C^{1/2}d_a\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^2(\ell_{\mathcal Y})}.\tag{3}\]
Apply (3) with \(v=u_a-\overline u_a\), sum over arms, and use (1)--(2). This yields \(0\le\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(P,\overline P)\le C_{\mathrm{OT}}(d_0+d_1)^2\). Adding constants to the base potentials integrates to zero against \(P_a-\overline P_a\), so centering leaves the assertion unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:two-sample-map-stability]\label{lem:two-sample-map-stability}
Fix \(x\) in the common full-measure set from \(ass:uniform-conditional-brenier-smoothness\), \(ass:uniform-conditional-brenier-lower-curvature\), and \(ass:uniform-conditional-brenier-upper-curvature\). Let \(P,Q,\widehat P,\widehat Q\) be probability laws on the convex set \(\mathcal Y\), each having a density that is the \(x\)-section of an element of the class \(\mathfrak F\) in \(\texttt{def:coupled-fold-learners}\); in particular, all four densities are normalized, belong to the common H\"older class, and lie between \(c\) and \(C\). Let \(b\in C^2(\mathcal Y)\) be a quadratic Brenier potential from \(P\) to \(Q\) satisfying
\[
m_{\mathrm{OT}}I_{d_Y}\preceq\nabla^2b(y)\preceq M_{\mathrm{OT}}I_{d_Y}\qquad(y\in\mathcal Y),
\]
put \(T=\nabla b\), and let \(\widehat\gamma\in\Gamma(\widehat P,\widehat Q)\) be an optimal quadratic coupling. Then
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \frac1{M_{\mathrm{OT}}}\int\lVert\zeta-T(\xi)\rVert_2^2d\widehat\gamma(\xi,\zeta)
\le \frac1{m_{\mathrm{OT}}}W_2^2(Q,\widehat Q)+M_{\mathrm{OT}}W_2^2(P,\widehat P)
+2W_2(P,\widehat P)W_2(Q,\widehat Q).\)}}
\]
The analogous assertion holds for the reverse maps after swapping the marginals, provided the corresponding reverse Brenier potential is in \(C^2(\mathcal Y)\) and satisfies the same all-feasible-pairs lower- and upper-curvature bounds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the half-squared-cost semi-dual
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle S_{\widehat P,\widehat Q}(b)=\int b\,d\widehat P+\int b^*\,d\widehat Q,\qquad \Delta=S_{\widehat P,\widehat Q}(b)-\inf_{\widetilde b}S_{\widehat P,\widehat Q}(\widetilde b).\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
Because \(b^*\) is \(M_{\mathrm{OT}}^{-1}\)-strongly convex and \(T=\nabla b\), Fenchel equality on an optimal fitted coupling gives
\[\Delta\ge\frac1{2M_{\mathrm{OT}}}\int\lVert\zeta-T(\xi)\rVert_2^2d\widehat\gamma(\xi,\zeta).\tag{1}\]
For the upper bound, draw \(X\sim P\), put \(Y=T(X)\), couple \(X\) optimally to \(U\sim\widehat P\), and \(Y\) optimally to \(V\sim\widehat Q\). The resulting law of \((U,V)\) is feasible. Taylor's inequalities from \(\nabla^2b\preceq M_{\mathrm{OT}}I\) and \(\nabla^2b^*\preceq m_{\mathrm{OT}}^{-1}I\) give
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle b(U)+b^*(V)-\langle U,V\rangle\le\frac{M_{\mathrm{OT}}}{2}\lVert U-X\rVert_2^2+\frac1{2m_{\mathrm{OT}}}\lVert V-Y\rVert_2^2-\langle U-X,V-Y\rangle.\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
Taking expectations and applying Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[\Delta\le\frac{M_{\mathrm{OT}}}{2}W_2^2(P,\widehat P)+\frac1{2m_{\mathrm{OT}}}W_2^2(Q,\widehat Q)+W_2(P,\widehat P)W_2(Q,\widehat Q).\tag{2}\]
Combining (1)--(2) and multiplying by two proves the displayed inequality. Repeating the argument with the conjugate potential and swapped marginals proves the reverse-map assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:assignment-score-rates]\label{lem:assignment-score-rates}
Let \(\mathscr C\subseteq\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vu}}\) be predetermined, and assume that the generic propensity learner-rate condition in \(\texttt{def:propensity-rate-regimes}\) holds uniformly over \(\mathscr C\).  Under the conclusions of \(\texttt{lem:conditional-transport-stability-rates}\), uniformly over \(\mathbf P\in\mathscr C\),
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \max_k\left|E_{P_{X,n}}\sum_a\left(\frac{\pi_{a,n}}{\bar\pi_{a,-k,n}}-1\right)\int\widehat\phi_{a,-k,n}\,d(Q_{a,P_n}-\widehat Q_{a,-k,n})\right|=O_{P_n}(r_{\pi,n}r_{q,n}),\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
and
\[
\max_k\left\|\widehat S_{-k,n}-(\theta_n+\psi_n)\right\|_{L^2(P_n)}=O_{P_n}(r_{\pi,n}+2r_{q,n}).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a fold and suppress indices. Define \(g_a(x)=\int\widehat\phi_a(x,y)d(Q_a-\widehat Q_a)(y\mid x)\). Exact fitted-kernel centering and true centering imply
\[g_a(x)=\int(\widehat\phi_a-\phi_a)(x,y)Q_a(dy\mid x).\]
Jensen's inequality and \(\texttt{lem:conditional-transport-stability-rates}\) give \(\lVert g_a\rVert_{L^2(P_X)}=O_{P_n}(r_{q,n})\). Fixed overlap and clipping give
\[\left|\frac{\pi_a}{\bar\pi_a}-1\right|\le\varepsilon^{-1}|\pi_a-\bar\pi_a|.\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and the generic propensity premise therefore prove the first displayed product order.

For the score rate, let \(S^\circ=\theta+\psi\). Then
\[\widehat S-S^\circ=(\widehat V-V)+\sum_a1\{A=a\}\left\{\frac{\widehat\phi_a-\phi_a}{\bar\pi_a}+\phi_a\left(\frac1{\bar\pi_a}-\frac1{\pi_a}\right)\right\}.\tag{1}\]
The compact-cost conjugacy bound gives \(|\phi_a|\le2\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal Y)^2\). Taking \(L^2(P)\) norms in (1), using overlap, the transport-value and potential rates, and the propensity premise, yields
\[\lVert\widehat S-S^\circ\rVert_{L^2(P)}=O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}+r_{\pi,n}).\]
The harmless notation \(r_{\pi,n}+2r_{q,n}\) records the two transport arms. Taking a maximum over fixed \(K\) proves the assertion uniformly.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This new lemma places the generic propensity learner premise exactly where it is used and proves both the propensity--density product bias and the full coupled-score \(L^2(P_n)\) rate. \textit{Gap.} The transport-only stability conclusions do not logically imply assignment-score rates without an independently stated propensity rate. \textit{Consumer.} It is the propensity-dependent bridge from transport stability to the cross-fit remainder and variance bounds.

\begin{lemma}[lem:conditional-transport-stability-rates]\label{lem:conditional-transport-stability-rates}
Uniformly over \(\mathbf P\in\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vu}}\), the conditional-density rate \(r_{q,n}\) implies
\[
\max_{k,a}\int W_2^2(\widehat Q_{a,-k,n},Q_{a,P_n})\,dP_{X,n}=O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}^2),
\]
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \max_{k,a}\lVert\widehat\phi_{a,-k,n}-\phi_{a,n}\rVert_{L^2(\nu_{a,n})}=O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}),\qquad
\max_k\lVert\widehat V_{-k,n}-V_n\rVert_{L^2(P_{X,n})}=O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}).\)}}
\]
With the fitted pair as base and the truth as perturbation,
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \widehat V_{-k,n}-V_n+\sum_a\int\widehat\phi_{a,-k,n}\,d(Q_{a,P_n}-\widehat Q_{a,-k,n})=-\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(Q,\widehat Q)\le0,\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
and, explicitly,
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \max_k\left\|\widehat V_{-k,n}-V_n+\sum_a\int\widehat\phi_{a,-k,n}\,d(Q_{a,P_n}-\widehat Q_{a,-k,n})\right\|_{L^1(P_{X,n})}
=\max_kE_{P_{X,n}}\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(Q,\widehat Q)=O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}^2).\)}}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a fold and suppress indices. Put \(e_a=\widehat q_a-q_a\) and \(d_a(x)=W_2(\widehat Q_a(\cdot\mid x),Q_a(\cdot\mid x))\). By \(\texttt{lem:wasserstein-l2-density-control}\),
\[d_a(x)^2\le c^{-1}C_{\mathcal Y}^2\int e_a(x,y)^2d\ell_{\mathcal Y}(y).\tag{1}\]
Integrating and invoking \(\texttt{lem:finite-net-conditional-density-rate}\) proves the first assertion.

At a fixed admissible \(x\), write \(h_a=\widehat u_a-u_a\). Applying the forward and reverse forms of \(\texttt{lem:two-sample-map-stability}\), using the map--potential gradient identities and the density lower bound, gives
\[\sum_a\lVert\nabla_yh_a(x,\cdot)\rVert_{L^2(\ell_{\mathcal Y})}^2\le C_1\{d_0(x)+d_1(x)\}^2.\tag{2}\]
Exact centering gives
\[\widehat\phi_a-\phi_a=h_a-Q_ah_a+(Q_a-\widehat Q_a)\widehat u_a.\tag{3}\]
Weighted Poincar\'e controls the first two terms in (3) by the gradient in (2). The common oscillation bound for quadratic dual potentials and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[|(Q_a-\widehat Q_a)\widehat u_a|\le C_2\lVert e_a(x,\cdot)\rVert_{L^2(\ell_{\mathcal Y})}.\tag{4}\]
Squaring, integrating, and using the density rate proves the potential rate. If \(w=W_2(Q_0,Q_1)\) and \(\widehat w=W_2(\widehat Q_0,\widehat Q_1)\), the metric triangle inequality gives \(|\widehat w-w|\le d_0+d_1\). Since all distances are at most \(\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal Y)\),
\[|\widehat V-V|\le2\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal Y)(d_0+d_1),\tag{5}\]
which proves the value rate.

Finally apply \(\texttt{lem:two-sample-quadratic-remainder}\), pointwise in \(x\), with the fitted pair as base and truth as perturbation. Centering does not alter the linear term, so
\[\widehat V-V+\sum_a\int\widehat\phi_a\,d(Q_a-\widehat Q_a)=-\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(Q,\widehat Q)\le0.\tag{6}\]
Moreover \(E_{P_X}\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}\le C E_{P_X}(d_0+d_1)^2=O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}^2)\). Because the left side of (6) is nonpositive, its \(L^1(P_X)\)-norm is exactly that expectation. Taking maxima over the fixed folds proves all conclusions uniformly.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:regular-analytic-gradient-conditions]\label{lem:regular-analytic-gradient-conditions}
For every \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) satisfying \(ass:regular-support-geometry\), the unique-normalized-potential property \(\mathsf U(P_n)\) in \(\texttt{def:unique-normalized-conditional-potentials}\) and the square-integrable-gradient property \(\mathsf G(P_n)\) in \(\texttt{def:gradient-square-integrability}\) hold.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P_n\) and suppress \(n\). For almost every \(x\), both conditional laws have densities bounded below on compact convex \(\mathcal Y\). For the continuous quadratic cost, successive quadratic transforms of a feasible maximizing sequence, normalized by \(\min u_0=0\), are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. Arzel\`a--Ascoli gives a uniformly convergent subsequence; feasibility and the dual objective pass to the limit, proving existence of a normalized mutually conjugate optimal pair.

Each potential is Lipschitz and differentiable almost everywhere. Positivity of the source density and differentiation of the contact identity show that every optimal plan is concentrated on the uniquely determined graph associated with \(\nabla u_0\). The average of two optimal graph plans is optimal, and hence must again lie on one graph; therefore their maps agree almost surely. Two normalized optimal first potentials consequently have equal gradients almost everywhere. Their Lipschitz difference has zero weak gradient on connected \(\mathcal Y\), so it is constant, and normalization makes it zero. Conjugacy then uniquely determines the second potential.

For joint measurability, place the normalized equibounded, equicontinuous pairs in the compact uniform-metric space. Feasibility is testable on countable dense subsets, and the dual objective is measurable in \(x\). The same nested-first-ball construction as in \(\texttt{lem:conditional-coupling-sharpness}\) supplies a measurable selector; uniqueness makes it the normalized pair just constructed. Hence \(\mathsf U(P_n)\) holds. Finally, \(\texttt{lem:uniform-canonical-gradient-bound}\) gives \(E\psi_n=0\) and \(|\psi_n|\le M_\psi\), so \(\psi_n\in L_0^2(P_n)\), which is \(\mathsf G(P_n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-net-propensity-rate]\label{lem:finite-net-propensity-rate}
Let \(\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vs,H}}_{\sigma_\star,t_\pi,L_\pi}\) be the structural H\"older-propensity subclass in \(\texttt{def:holder-propensity-triangular-law-class}\).  On every training complement use the clipped finite-net least-squares learner specified in \(\texttt{def:propensity-rate-regimes}\).  Then, uniformly over \(\mathbf P\in\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vs,H}}_{\sigma_\star,t_\pi,L_\pi}\),
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \max_{1\le k\le K}\lVert\bar\pi_{-k,n}-\pi_n\rVert_{L^2(P_{X,n})}
=O_{P_n}\!\left(n^{-t_\pi/(2t_\pi+d_X)}\right).\)}}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a fold with training size \(m\asymp n\), and write \(\ell_g(X,A)=(A-g(X))^2\). Conditional expectation gives the exact excess-risk identity
\[\mathcal R(g)=P(\ell_g-\ell_\pi)=\lVert g-\pi\rVert_{L^2(P_X)}^2.\tag{1}\]
Writing \(d_g=g-\pi\), one has \(\ell_g-\ell_\pi=d_g(X)^2-2\{A-\pi(X)\}d_g(X)\). Since \(g,\pi\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), the loss difference is bounded and
\[E[(\ell_g-\ell_\pi)^2\mid X]\le4d_g(X)^2.\tag{2}\]
Bernstein's inequality, Young's inequality, and a union bound over the finite net and fixed folds imply that, with probability at least \(1-2Ke^{-u}\),
\[|({\mathbb P}_{k,n}-P)(\ell_g-\ell_\pi)|\le\frac14\mathcal R(g)+C_1\frac{\log(2|\mathfrak G_J|)+u}{m}\tag{3}\]
simultaneously for all candidates. The Holder assumption and net construction provide \(g^\circ\) with \(\lVert g^\circ-\pi\rVert_\infty\lesssim2^{-Jt_\pi}\) and \(\log|\mathfrak G_J|\lesssim2^{Jd_X}\). Comparing the empirical minimizer to \(g^\circ\) in (3) yields
\[\mathcal R(\widetilde\pi_{-k,n})\lesssim2^{-2Jt_\pi}+\frac{2^{Jd_X}+u}{n}.\tag{4}\]
Projection onto \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) is one-Lipschitz and fixes \(\pi\), so clipping cannot increase this error. With \(2^{Jd_X}\asymp n^{d_X/(2t_\pi+d_X)}\), taking square roots in (4) gives the claimed \(n^{-t_\pi/(2t_\pi+d_X)}\) order uniformly over laws and folds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:abstract-observed-canonical-gradient]\label{thm:abstract-observed-canonical-gradient}
Let \(P_n\) be an observed-data law satisfying the three analytic properties \(\mathsf U(P_n)\), \(\mathsf D(P_n)\), and \(\mathsf G(P_n)\) in \(\texttt{def:unique-normalized-conditional-potentials}\), \(\texttt{def:conditional-transport-pathwise-differentiability}\), and \(\texttt{def:gradient-square-integrability}\). Then \(\psi_n\) is the canonical gradient of \(\theta_n\) in the nonparametric observed-data model, and \(E_{P_n}[\psi_n(O)^2]=\sigma_n^2\) is the semiparametric efficiency bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\), suppress the row index, and write \(P=P_n\). Property \(\mathsf U(P)\) makes the normalized conditional dual potentials and their centered versions \(\phi_a\) well defined. Let \(P_t\) be an arbitrary regular dominated one-dimensional submodel through \(P\), with score \(h\in L_0^2(P)\). Put \(h_X(x)=E[h(O)\mid X=x]\), \(m_a(x)=E[h(O)\mid A=a,X=x]\), and \(h_{Y,a}(x,y)=h(x,a,y)-m_a(x)\). By \(\mathsf D(P)\),
\[\dot\theta_P(h)=E[(V(X)-\theta)h_X(X)]+\sum_aE_{P_X}\!\left[\int\phi_a(X,y)h_{Y,a}(X,y)Q_a(dy\mid X)\right].\tag{1}\]
Because \(V(X)-\theta\) is \(X\)-measurable, the first term equals \(E[(V(X)-\theta)h(O)]\). For each arm, iterated expectation and positivity give
\[E\!\left[\frac{1\{A=a\}}{\pi_a(X)}\phi_a(X,Y)h(O)\right]=E_{P_X}\!\left[\int\phi_a(X,y)h(x,a,y)Q_a(dy\mid X)\right].\tag{2}\]
Exact centering, \(\int\phi_a(x,y)Q_a(dy\mid x)=0\), allows \(h(x,a,y)\) in (2) to be replaced by \(h_{Y,a}(x,y)\). Substitution into (1) yields \(\dot\theta_P(h)=E[\psi(O)h(O)]\) for every regular score \(h\). The same centering identities give \(E[\psi]=0\), and \(\mathsf G(P)\) gives \(\psi\in L_0^2(P)\).

It remains only to identify the tangent space. For every bounded \(g\in L_0^2(P)\), the path \(dP_t=(1+tg)dP\), for \(|t|\lVert g\rVert_\infty<1/2\), is regular with score \(g\). Bounded mean-zero functions are dense in \(L_0^2(P)\), by truncation followed by recentering. Hence the nonparametric observed-data tangent space is all of \(L_0^2(P)\). Therefore \(\psi\) is the unique tangent-space representer of the derivative, namely the canonical gradient. Its squared norm is
\[\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^2(P)}^2=E_P[\psi(O)^2]=\sigma_n^2,\]
which is the semiparametric efficiency bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:integrated-arm-path-differentiation]\label{lem:integrated-arm-path-differentiation}
Let \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) satisfy the unique-normalized-potential property \(\mathsf U(P_n)\) in \(\texttt{def:unique-normalized-conditional-potentials}\). Then the conditional-transport pathwise differentiability property \(\mathsf D(P_n)\) in \(\texttt{def:conditional-transport-pathwise-differentiability}\) holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a row, write \(P=P_n\), and let \(P_t\) be a regular dominated submodel with score \(h\). If \(L_t=dP_t/dP\), regularity gives \(L_t=1+th+o_{L^1(P)}(t)\). Conditional expectation and quotient expansion imply
\[\frac{dP_{X,t}/dP_X-1}{t}\to h_X\quad\text{in }L^1(P_X),\tag{1}\]
\[\int\!\left|\frac{dQ_{a,t}/dQ_a-1}{t}-h_{Y,a}\right|d(P_X\otimes Q_a)\to0.\tag{2}\]
Fixed overlap justifies the arm-conditioned change of measure in (2). In particular the conditional marginals converge in integrated total variation.

Let \((u_0,u_1)\) be the unique normalized base pair and \((u_{0,t},u_{1,t})\) normalized optimizers along the path. Compact quadratic conjugacy makes these families uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. Integrated total-variation convergence, compactness, and uniqueness at \(t=0\) imply
\[\sum_a\int|u_{a,t}-u_a|^2d(P_X\otimes Q_a)\to0.\tag{3}\]
Indeed, any violating subsequence has an almost-everywhere conditionally convergent subsubsequence; Arzel\`a--Ascoli supplies uniform potential limits, and passing to dual feasibility and value makes each limit a normalized base optimizer, contradicting uniqueness.

Put \(V_t(x)=W_2^2(Q_{0,t},Q_{1,t})\). Dual feasibility gives the envelope inequalities
\[\sum_a\int u_a\,d(Q_{a,t}-Q_a)\le V_t-V\le\sum_a\int u_{a,t}\,d(Q_{a,t}-Q_a).\tag{4}\]
Divide by \(t\), integrate over \(P_X\), and use (2)--(3). The two bounds have the same limit, namely
\[\sum_aE_{P_X}\!\left[\int\phi_a(X,y)h_{Y,a}(X,y)Q_a(dy\mid X)\right],\tag{5}\]
where centering is valid because \(Q_ah_{Y,a}=0\). The values are uniformly bounded, and (4) plus total-variation convergence gives \(V_t-V\to0\) in \(P_X\)-probability. Combining this with (1) shows that the product term \(t^{-1}\int(V_t-V)d(P_{X,t}-P_X)\) tends to zero. Decomposing
\[\theta(P_t)-\theta(P)=\int(V_t-V)dP_X+\int Vd(P_{X,t}-P_X)+\int(V_t-V)d(P_{X,t}-P_X)\]
and applying (1) and (5) yields exactly the derivative displayed in \(\mathsf D(P_n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:degeneracy-frontier-and-spectral-obstruction]\label{thm:degeneracy-frontier-and-spectral-obstruction}
Theorem (degeneracy frontier and obstruction to the unregularized full-tangent spectral route).  Let \(P_n\) be a row law of the variance-unrestricted parent \(\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vu}}\), equivalently \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) satisfying \(ass:regular-support-geometry\) and the three all-feasible-pairs conditional Brenier conditions.  Then \(\sigma_n^2=0\) if and only if \(Q_{0,P_n}(\cdot\mid x)=Q_{1,P_n}(\cdot\mid x)\) for \(P_{X,n}\)-almost every \(x\).  At equality, \(\theta_n=0\), \(\psi_n=0\), and the oracle first-order Gaussian pivot has Kolmogorov distance \(1/2\) from \(N(0,1)\).  Along every smooth equality path described in \(\texttt{lem:equality-degeneracy-and-second-variation}\), the second directional derivative is the displayed weighted Neumann energy.  A centered Gaussian spectral calibration for a second-order Hessian exists in \(L^2\) exactly when its eigenvalues are square summable, and uniform finite-rank calibration requires a uniform square-summable tail.  The parent class does not imply this condition: whenever it contains the equality law in \(\texttt{lem:conditional-hessian-spectral-obstruction}\) with nonatomic \(P_X\), the conditional Hessian has an infinite-dimensional compression equal to a positive multiple of the identity and is neither compact nor Hilbert--Schmidt.  Consequently, the unregularized \(n\)-scale full-tangent spectral/Gaussian route named in \(\texttt{def:degeneracy-handle}\) cannot yield a uniformly tight equality calibration over \(\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vu}}\).  A positive theorem using that route must impose or construct covariate-direction regularization, a uniform Hilbert--Schmidt tail, and uniform estimation of the regularized Hessian.  On collections with \(\sigma_n>0\) satisfying the estimator and scale-aware variance expansion hypotheses of \(\texttt{lem:vanishing-variance-studentization-scale-aware}\), the first-order studentized limit remains uniformly valid whenever
\[
\frac{\sqrt n\,\delta_n}{\sigma_n}\to0,\qquad
\frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}\to0,\qquad
\sqrt n\,\sigma_n\to\infty
\]
uniformly.  This is a route-specific negative result and does not rule out slower regularized or other derivative-aware confidence procedures; honest degeneracy-adaptive inference over the entire parent remains open.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a row and suppress \(n\). By \(\texttt{lem:equality-degeneracy-and-second-variation}\),
\[\sigma^2=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q_{0,P}(\cdot\mid x)=Q_{1,P}(\cdot\mid x)\quad\text{for }P_X\text{-almost every }x.\tag{1}\]
At equality that lemma also gives \(\theta=0\), \(\psi=0\) almost surely, and the stipulated zero-denominator first-order pivot is identically zero. Its distribution function is \(1\{t\ge0\}\), so
\[\sup_t|1\{t\ge0\}-\Phi(t)|=\frac12.\tag{2}\]
For every smooth equality path in that lemma,
\[\theta(P_t)=t^2\mathcal E+o(t^2),\qquad \mathcal E=\int q(x,y)\lVert\nabla_yw_x(y)\rVert_2^2d(P_X\otimes\ell_{\mathcal Y})(x,y),\tag{3}\]
and hence the second directional derivative is \(2\mathcal E\).

For a centered Gaussian quadratic calibration, \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quadratic-spectral-criterion}\) gives \(L^2\) existence exactly when \(\sum_j\lambda_j^2<\infty\), and for a family \(\Lambda\) it gives the exact truncation identity
\[\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}E\!\left[\left\{\sum_{j>J}\lambda_j(Z_j^2-1)\right\}^2\right]=2\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sum_{j>J}\lambda_j^2.\tag{4}\]
Thus uniform finite-rank approximation is equivalent to a uniformly vanishing square-summable tail. If the parent contains the equality witness in \(\texttt{lem:conditional-hessian-spectral-obstruction}\), that lemma supplies an infinite-dimensional closed score subspace \(\mathcal E\), \(\lambda_\circ>0\), and a bounded self-adjoint Hessian representative \(H^\circ\) satisfying
\[\Pi_{\mathcal E}H^\circ|_{\mathcal E}=\lambda_\circ I_{\mathcal E}.\tag{5}\]
For an orthonormal sequence \((e_j)\) in \(\mathcal E\), the rank-\(J\) compressed centered Gaussian form is
\[\mathcal Q_J^\circ=\lambda_\circ\sum_{j=1}^J(Z_j^2-1).\tag{6}\]
The spectral lemma shows that (6) has no weakly convergent subsequence. Hence the finite-dimensional compressions are not tight. In particular, the unregularized full-tangent spectral/Gaussian construction in \(\texttt{def:degeneracy-handle}\) fails a necessary tightness condition at an admissible parent law and therefore cannot be uniformly tight over \(\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vu}}\). Equation (4) also proves that a positive theorem along that route must remove the identity compression by covariate-direction regularization, obtain uniformly vanishing Hilbert--Schmidt tails, and estimate the regularized Hessian sufficiently well to reproduce the associated quadratic law. This obstruction is confined to that route.

Finally, on any positive-variance collection satisfying \(\texttt{lem:vanishing-variance-studentization-scale-aware}\) and the three displayed relative-rate conditions, put
\[a_n=\sigma_nr_{S,n}+r_{S,n}^2+\frac{\sigma_n+r_{S,n}}{\sqrt n}.\]
Then
\[\frac{a_n}{\sigma_n^2}=\frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}+\left(\frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}\right)^2+\frac1{\sqrt n\sigma_n}+\frac{r_{S,n}/\sigma_n}{\sqrt n\sigma_n}\longrightarrow0.\tag{7}\]
The cited lemma therefore yields uniform first-order studentized normality, ratio-consistent studentization, positivity with probability tending to one, and uniform Wald coverage. Combining (1)--(7) proves every asserted positive and negative clause and leaves only the stated full-parent adaptive problem unresolved.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem combines exact first-order degeneracy, the weighted-Neumann second variation, the Gaussian-series criterion, and an admissible non-Hilbert--Schmidt Hessian witness to rule out the unregularized full-tangent spectral route. \textit{Gap.} The result is route-specific and does not establish impossibility of slower regularized or nonspectral confidence procedures. \textit{Consumer.} It explains why the positive-variance Wald result cannot be extended to equality by an unregularized spectral plug-in.

\begin{lemma}[lem:cross-fit-remainder-variance-scale-aware]\label{lem:cross-fit-remainder-variance-scale-aware}
Let \(\mathscr C\subseteq\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vu}}\) be predetermined and suppose that the density and propensity learner-rate premises used by \(\texttt{lem:conditional-transport-stability-rates}\) and \(\texttt{lem:assignment-score-rates}\) hold uniformly over \(\mathscr C\). The exact conditional fold bias is
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \mathcal B_{k,n}=-E_{P_{X,n}}\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(Q,\widehat Q)+E_{P_{X,n}}\sum_a\left(\frac{\pi_{a,n}}{\bar\pi_{a,-k,n}}-1\right)\int\widehat\phi_{a,-k,n}\,d(Q_{a,P_n}-\widehat Q_{a,-k,n}).\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
The first term is nonpositive and \(O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}^2)\), and the second is \(O_{P_n}(r_{\pi,n}r_{q,n})\). Uniformly over \(\mathbf P\in\mathscr C\),
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \widehat\theta_n-\theta_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_n(O_{i,n})+O_{P_n}\!\left(r_{q,n}^2+r_{\pi,n}r_{q,n}+n^{-1/2}r_{S,n}\right),\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
and the scale-aware empirical-variance bound is
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \widehat\sigma_n^2-\sigma_n^2=O_{P_n}\!\left(\sigma_n r_{S,n}+r_{S,n}^2+\frac{\sigma_n+r_{S,n}}{\sqrt n}\right).\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(m_{k,n}=|I_{k,n}|\), \(w_{k,n}=m_{k,n}/n\), let \(\mathbb P_{k,n}\) be the fold empirical measure, and define
\[\Delta_{k,n}(o)=\widehat S_{-k,n}(o)-\{\theta_n+\psi_n(o)\}.\]
Conditional on the training complement, iterated expectation gives
\[P_n\widehat S_{-k,n}=E_{P_{X,n}}\!\left[\widehat V_{-k,n}+\sum_a\frac{\pi_{a,n}}{\bar\pi_{a,-k,n}}\int\widehat\phi_{a,-k,n}\,dQ_{a,P_n}\right].\tag{1}\]
Use \(\int\widehat\phi_a,d\widehat Q_a=0\), add and subtract \(\widehat Q_a\), and write \(\pi_a/\bar\pi_a=1+(\pi_a/\bar\pi_a-1)\). The signed remainder identity in \(\texttt{lem:conditional-transport-stability-rates}\) and the product bound in \(\texttt{lem:assignment-score-rates}\) then yield exactly the displayed fold-bias identity, with orders \(O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}^2)\) and \(O_{P_n}(r_{\pi,n}r_{q,n})\).

The estimator admits the exact decomposition
\[\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n=\frac1n\sum_i\psi_n(O_{i,n})+\sum_kw_{k,n}P_n\Delta_{k,n}+\sum_kw_{k,n}(\mathbb P_{k,n}-P_n)\Delta_{k,n}.\tag{2}\]
Conditionally on training, the last term has foldwise second moment at most \(P_n\Delta_{k,n}^2/m_{k,n}\). The score-rate conclusion \(\max_k\lVert\Delta_{k,n}\rVert_2=O_{P_n}(r_{S,n})\), balanced folds, fixed \(K\), and conditional Chebyshev therefore make that term \(O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}r_{S,n})\). Combining with the bias orders proves the estimator expansion.

Write \(e_n=\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n\). Empirical centering gives
\[\widehat\sigma_n^2=\sum_kw_{k,n}\mathbb P_{k,n}(\psi_n+\Delta_{k,n})^2-e_n^2.\tag{3}\]
The uniform envelope \(|\psi_n|\le M_\psi\) implies \(\operatorname{Var}(\psi_n^2)\le M_\psi^2\sigma_n^2\), whence
\[\sum_kw_{k,n}\mathbb P_{k,n}\psi_n^2-\sigma_n^2=O_{P_n}(\sigma_n/\sqrt n).\tag{4}\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(|P_n(\psi_n\Delta_{k,n})|=O_{P_n}(\sigma_nr_{S,n})\), while conditional Chebyshev gives an empirical fluctuation \(O_{P_n}(r_{S,n}/\sqrt n)\). Conditional Markov gives \(\sum_kw_{k,n}\mathbb P_{k,n}\Delta_{k,n}^2=O_{P_n}(r_{S,n}^2)\). Finally, (2) implies
\[e_n=O_{P_n}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_n}{\sqrt n}+r_{S,n}^2+\frac{r_{S,n}}{\sqrt n}\right),\quad e_n^2=O_{P_n}\!\left(r_{S,n}^2+\frac{\sigma_n+r_{S,n}}{\sqrt n}\right).\tag{5}\]
Expanding (3) and applying (4)--(5) proves the stated scale-aware variance bound uniformly.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:vanishing-variance-studentization-scale-aware]\label{lem:vanishing-variance-studentization-scale-aware}
Consider any collection of i.i.d. triangular sequences in the variance-unrestricted parent class with \(\sigma_n>0\), \(E[\psi_n]=0\), and \(|\psi_n|\le M_\psi\). Suppose, uniformly over that collection,
\[
\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_n(O_{i,n})+O_{P_n}(\delta_n),
\]
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \widehat\sigma_n^2-\sigma_n^2=O_{P_n}\!\left(\sigma_n r_{S,n}+r_{S,n}^2+\frac{\sigma_n+r_{S,n}}{\sqrt n}\right),\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
where
\[
\frac{\sqrt n\,\delta_n}{\sigma_n}\to0,\qquad \frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}\to0,\qquad \sqrt n\,\sigma_n\to\infty
\]
uniformly. Then the studentized statistic converges uniformly to \(N(0,1)\) in Kolmogorov distance, \(\widehat\sigma_n/\sigma_n\to1\) uniformly in probability, \(P_n(\widehat\sigma_n>0)\to1\) uniformly, and the associated Wald intervals have uniform asymptotic coverage \(1-\alpha\). For the frozen one-step decomposition one may take \(\delta_n=r_{q,n}^2+r_{\pi,n}r_{q,n}+n^{-1/2}(r_{\pi,n}+2r_{q,n})\) and \(r_{S,n}=r_{\pi,n}+2r_{q,n}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Define \(Z_n=(\sigma_n\sqrt n)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_n(O_{i,n})\). For fixed \(t\), Taylor expansion of the complex exponential, together with \(E\psi_n=0\), \(E\psi_n^2=\sigma_n^2\), and \(|\psi_n|^3\le M_\psi\psi_n^2\), gives
\[E\exp\!\left(\frac{it\psi_n}{\sigma_n\sqrt n}\right)=1-\frac{t^2}{2n}+\rho_n(t),\qquad |\rho_n(t)|\le\frac{|t|^3M_\psi}{6\sigma_nn^{3/2}}.\tag{1}\]
Since \(\sqrt n\sigma_n\to\infty\) uniformly, rowwise independence implies \(E[e^{itZ_n}]\to e^{-t^2/2}\) uniformly. A subsequence argument and continuity of \(\Phi\) upgrade this to uniform Kolmogorov convergence.

Put
\[a_n=\sigma_nr_{S,n}+r_{S,n}^2+\frac{\sigma_n+r_{S,n}}{\sqrt n}.\]
The scale assumptions imply
\[\frac{a_n}{\sigma_n^2}=\frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}+\left(\frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}\right)^2+\frac1{\sqrt n\sigma_n}+\frac{r_{S,n}/\sigma_n}{\sqrt n\sigma_n}\longrightarrow0.\tag{2}\]
Hence the variance expansion gives \(\widehat\sigma_n^2/\sigma_n^2\to1\) uniformly in probability. Nonnegativity and \(\sigma_n>0\) then give \(\widehat\sigma_n/\sigma_n\to1\) and \(P_n(\widehat\sigma_n>0)\to1\) uniformly. The estimator expansion and \(\sqrt n\delta_n/\sigma_n\to0\) give
\[\frac{\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)}{\widehat\sigma_n}=\frac{Z_n+o_{P_n}(1)}{\widehat\sigma_n/\sigma_n}.\tag{3}\]
Uniform Slutsky, again justified by subsequences, proves uniform standard-normal convergence. The zero-denominator convention affects only an event of uniformly vanishing probability. Evaluating (3) at \(\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\) proves uniform Wald coverage. The last displayed choices of \(\delta_n\) and \(r_{S,n}\) are exactly those supplied by \(\texttt{lem:cross-fit-remainder-variance-scale-aware}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:observed-gradient-minimal]\label{thm:observed-gradient-minimal}
Fix a row law \(P_n\). If a causal extension satisfies consistency, conditional ignorability, and arm positivity \(P_n\{\pi_{a,n}(X_n)>0\}=1\) for \(a=0,1\), then \(Q_{a,P_n}(\cdot\mid x)=\mu_{a,P_n}(\cdot\mid x)\) for \(P_{X,n}\)-almost every \(x\) and every \(a\). Consequently, \(\theta_n\) is the sharp conditional-coupling lower endpoint on \(E[\|Y_n(1)-Y_n(0)\|_2^2]\). Separately, at every observed law satisfying the analytic properties \(\mathsf U(P_n)\), \(\mathsf D(P_n)\), and \(\mathsf G(P_n)\), \(\psi_n\) is the canonical gradient of \(\theta_n\) in the nonparametric observed-data model and \(\sigma_n^2\) is its semiparametric efficiency bound. Membership in \(\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) together with \(\texttt{ass:regular-support-geometry}\) is sufficient for these three analytic properties.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a row and suppress \(n\). On \(\{A=a\}\), consistency gives \(1\{Y\in B\}=1\{Y(a)\in B\}\). Hence
\[Q_{a,P}(B\mid x)=P\{Y(a)\in B\mid A=a,X=x\}.\tag{1}\]
Ignorability changes the right side to \(P\{Y(a)\in B\mid X=x\}=\mu_{a,P}(B\mid x)\), and positivity makes (1) valid for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). Applying this equality to a countable determining class and then a monotone-class argument yields equality of the kernels.

Every compatible causal extension therefore has conditional potential-outcome marginals \(Q_0,Q_1\). By \(\texttt{lem:conditional-coupling-sharpness}\),
\[\inf E\lVert Y(1)-Y(0)\rVert_2^2=\int W_2^2(Q_0(\cdot\mid x),Q_1(\cdot\mid x))dP_X(x)=\theta_n,\tag{2}\]
and the measurable optimal-coupling construction in that lemma attains (2) in a compatible causal extension. Thus the endpoint is sharp.

If \(\mathsf U(P_n)\), \(\mathsf D(P_n)\), and \(\mathsf G(P_n)\) hold, \(\texttt{thm:abstract-observed-canonical-gradient}\) directly identifies \(\psi_n\) as the canonical gradient and \(\sigma_n^2\) as its squared norm. Finally, if \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) and the regular-support geometry holds, \(\texttt{lem:regular-analytic-gradient-conditions}\) gives \(\mathsf U(P_n)\) and \(\mathsf G(P_n)\), and \(\texttt{lem:integrated-arm-path-differentiation}\) then gives \(\mathsf D(P_n)\). This proves the advertised primitive sufficiency.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Consistency, conditional ignorability, and positivity identify the conditional marginal laws, measurable optimal coupling makes the lower endpoint sharp, and the analytic derivative properties yield the observed-data canonical gradient. \textit{Gap.} This theorem identifies and differentiates the lower endpoint; it does not identify the full joint law of the two potential outcomes. \textit{Consumer.} It separates the causal endpoint from the observed-law influence function used by every subsequent inference result.

\begin{theorem}[thm:regular-efficient-inference-scale-aware]\label{thm:regular-efficient-inference-scale-aware}
Theorem (conditional observational DML for the sharp conditional-optimal-transport endpoint with positive vanishing variance). Let \(\mathscr C\subseteq\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vu}}\) be any predetermined collection such that \(\sigma_n>0\) for every row law under consideration. Use the predetermined normalized coupled learners in \(\texttt{def:coupled-fold-learners}\) and suppose their uniform conditional-density rate premise holds over \(\mathscr C\). Use a predetermined propensity learner satisfying the generic uniform \(L^2(P_{X,n})\) rate premise in \(\texttt{def:propensity-rate-regimes}\), for deterministic \(r_{\pi,n}\to0\). Put
\[
\delta_n=r_{q,n}^2+r_{\pi,n}r_{q,n}+n^{-1/2}(r_{\pi,n}+2r_{q,n}),\qquad r_{S,n}=r_{\pi,n}+2r_{q,n}.
\]
Suppose, uniformly over \(\mathscr C\),
\[
\frac{\sqrt n\,\delta_n}{\sigma_n}\to0,\qquad \frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}\to0,\qquad \sqrt n\,\sigma_n\to\infty.
\]
Then the exact fold bias is
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \mathcal B_{k,n}=-E_{P_{X,n}}\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}(Q,\widehat Q)+E_{P_{X,n}}\sum_a\left(\frac{\pi_{a,n}}{\bar\pi_{a,-k,n}}-1\right)\int\widehat\phi_{a,-k,n}\,d(Q_{a,P_n}-\widehat Q_{a,-k,n}),\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
where \(\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OT}}\ge0\), and its two terms are respectively \(O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}^2)\) and \(O_{P_n}(r_{\pi,n}r_{q,n})\). Moreover,
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \widehat\theta_n-\theta_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_n(O_{i,n})+R_n,\qquad R_n=O_{P_n}(\delta_n)=o_{P_n}(\sigma_n/\sqrt n),\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
\[
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\par\noindent \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\resizebox{0.98\linewidth}{!}{\(\displaystyle \displaystyle \widehat\sigma_n^2-\sigma_n^2=O_{P_n}\!\left(\sigma_n r_{S,n}+r_{S,n}^2+\frac{\sigma_n+r_{S,n}}{\sqrt n}\right)=o_{P_n}(\sigma_n^2).\)}}\par\noindent}}
\]
The studentized statistic converges uniformly to \(N(0,1)\) in Kolmogorov distance, \(\widehat\sigma_n/\sigma_n\to1\) uniformly, \(P_n(\widehat\sigma_n>0)\to1\) uniformly, and the two-sided Wald interval has uniform asymptotic coverage \(1-\alpha\). The influence function is the observed-data canonical gradient \(\psi_n\), so the leading variance attains the semiparametric efficiency bound. Unlike the unconditional one- or two-marginal-sample result summarized in \(\texttt{lem:manole-unconditional-smooth-wasserstein-efficiency}\), this observational-sample theorem includes the covariate-composition and inverse-propensity terms in \(\psi_n\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathscr C\). The density and propensity premises permit application of \(\texttt{lem:conditional-transport-stability-rates}\), \(\texttt{lem:assignment-score-rates}\), and then \(\texttt{lem:cross-fit-remainder-variance-scale-aware}\). These lemmas give the exact displayed fold-bias identity; its transport remainder is nonpositive and \(O_{P_n}(r_{q,n}^2)\), while its assignment term is \(O_{P_n}(r_{\pi,n}r_{q,n})\). They also give
\[\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n=\frac1n\sum_i\psi_n(O_{i,n})+R_n,\qquad R_n=O_{P_n}(\delta_n),\tag{1}\]
\[\widehat\sigma_n^2-\sigma_n^2=O_{P_n}\!\left(\sigma_nr_{S,n}+r_{S,n}^2+\frac{\sigma_n+r_{S,n}}{\sqrt n}\right).\tag{2}\]
The first relative-rate assumption turns (1) into \(R_n=o_{P_n}(\sigma_n/\sqrt n)\). Dividing the deterministic envelope in (2) by \(\sigma_n^2\) gives
\[\frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}+\left(\frac{r_{S,n}}{\sigma_n}\right)^2+\frac1{\sqrt n\sigma_n}+\frac{r_{S,n}/\sigma_n}{\sqrt n\sigma_n}\to0,\tag{3}\]
by the other two relative-rate assumptions. Hence (2) is \(o_{P_n}(\sigma_n^2)\) uniformly.

The canonical gradients are centered and uniformly bounded by \(\texttt{lem:uniform-canonical-gradient-bound}\). All hypotheses of \(\texttt{lem:vanishing-variance-studentization-scale-aware}\) therefore hold. It yields uniform Kolmogorov convergence of the studentized statistic to \(N(0,1)\), uniform ratio consistency of \(\widehat\sigma_n\), positivity with probability tending uniformly to one, and Wald coverage \(1-\alpha\). Finally, \(\texttt{thm:observed-gradient-minimal}\) identifies \(\psi_n\) as the canonical gradient and \(\sigma_n^2\) as the efficiency bound, so (1) attains that bound. The comparison with unconditional marginal sampling follows from the already-stated \(\texttt{lem:manole-unconditional-smooth-wasserstein-efficiency}\); the additional composition and inverse-propensity terms are explicit in \(\psi_n\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Lin--Gao--Blanchet--Glynn already establish smooth-density wavelet conditional-optimal-transport stability and randomized marginal-sample asymptotic normality. The present theorem adds three distinct observational conclusions: the covariate-composition plus inverse-propensity canonical gradient under ignorability and overlap; the semiparametric information lower bound together with an attaining cross-fitted one-step estimator; and uniform studentized normality and Wald coverage on admissible positive collections with \(\sigma_n\to0\) under the displayed scale-aware conditions. \textit{Gap.} The theorem does not cover exact conditional equality, where the first-order influence function vanishes, and it does not claim that its sufficient nuisance-rate or vanishing-variance boundaries are minimax necessary. \textit{Consumer.} It supports efficiency-attaining, propensity-corrected Wald inference for the sharp 401(k) SIPP endpoint when ignorability, overlap, transport regularity, learner rates, and the positive-variance scale conditions are credible; Ji--Lei--Spector's dual-bound AIPW procedure remains the distinct observational comparator.

\begin{proposition}[prop:holder-propensity-efficient-inference-phase-boundary]\label{prop:holder-propensity-efficient-inference-phase-boundary}
Put \(a_q=s/(2s+D)\) and \(a_\pi=t_\pi/(2t_\pi+d_X)\). Over \(\mathscr P^{\mathrm{vs,H}}_{\sigma_\star,t_\pi,L_\pi}\), the finite-net learners satisfy the hypotheses of \(\texttt{thm:regular-efficient-inference-scale-aware}\) whenever \(s>D/2\) and \(a_\pi+a_q>1/2\), equivalently \(t_\pi>Dd_X/(4s)\). More generally, on any predetermined positive-variance collection with these learner rates and constants \(0<c_\sigma\le C_\sigma<\infty\) such that \(c_\sigma n^{-\kappa}\le\sigma_n\le C_\sigma n^{-\kappa}\) uniformly for some \(\kappa\ge0\), the scale-aware theorem applies whenever
\[
\kappa<\min\left\{2a_q-\frac12,\ a_\pi+a_q-\frac12,\ a_q,\ a_\pi,\ \frac12\right\}.
\]
If the propensity is known, the \(a_\pi\)-dependent terms are absent, so the boundary is \(\kappa<\min\{2a_q-1/2,a_q,1/2\}\). If a correctly specified fixed-dimensional propensity learner has uniform rate \(n^{-1/2}\), the same boundary results after setting \(a_\pi=1/2\). These sufficient thresholds are specific to the present \(L^2\)-conditional-density and first-order remainder route; no minimax necessity is claimed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\texttt{lem:finite-net-propensity-rate}\) and \(\texttt{def:foldwise-rate-route}\), \(r_{\pi,n}=n^{-a_\pi}\), \(r_{q,n}=n^{-a_q}\), and \(r_{S,n}=n^{-a_\pi}+2n^{-a_q}\). If \(\sigma_n\ge\sigma_\star\), the nonautomatic conditions in the scale-aware theorem are \(\sqrt n r_{q,n}^2\to0\) and \(\sqrt n r_{\pi,n}r_{q,n}\to0\). These are \(2a_q>1/2\) and \(a_\pi+a_q>1/2\). The first is equivalent to \(s>D/2\), while direct cross multiplication gives
\[\frac{t_\pi}{2t_\pi+d_X}+\frac{s}{2s+D}>\frac12\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 4st_\pi>Dd_X\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t_\pi>\frac{Dd_X}{4s}.\tag{1}\]
Now suppose \(\sigma_n\asymp n^{-\kappa}\). The four components of \(\sqrt n\delta_n/\sigma_n\) have orders
\[n^{\kappa+1/2-2a_q},\quad n^{\kappa+1/2-a_\pi-a_q},\quad n^{\kappa-a_\pi},\quad n^{\kappa-a_q}.\tag{2}\]
Thus they vanish under \(\kappa<2a_q-1/2\), \(\kappa<a_\pi+a_q-1/2\), \(\kappa<a_\pi\), and \(\kappa<a_q\). The condition \(r_{S,n}/\sigma_n\to0\) repeats the last two, and \(\sqrt n\sigma_n\to\infty\) adds \(\kappa<1/2\). Their conjunction is the displayed minimum. If propensity is known, delete the \(a_\pi\)-dependent terms. If its uniform rate is \(n^{-1/2}\), set \(a_\pi=1/2\). These substitutions prove every stated boundary and make no necessity claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition substitutes the proved finite-net density and propensity rates into the scale-aware theorem and solves the resulting exponent inequalities. \textit{Gap.} The boundary is sufficient for this learner and remainder route and is not claimed minimax necessary. \textit{Consumer.} It gives concrete nuisance-smoothness and near-degeneracy planning benchmarks.

\begin{theorem}[thm:observational-efficiency-lower-bound]\label{thm:observational-efficiency-lower-bound}
At every fixed positive-variance observed law satisfying \(\mathsf U(P_n)\), \(\mathsf D(P_n)\), and \(\mathsf G(P_n)\), every regular asymptotically linear estimator of \(\theta_n\) has asymptotic variance at least \(\sigma_n^2\). Along every triangular sequence satisfying the hypotheses of \(\texttt{thm:regular-efficient-inference-scale-aware}\), the proposed cross-fitted one-step estimator has influence function \(\psi_n\) and attains this bound. Hence the efficiency statement is two-sided: \(\sigma_n^2\) is both an information lower bound and the achieved leading variance.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a fixed law satisfying \(\mathsf U(P_n)\), \(\mathsf D(P_n)\), and \(\mathsf G(P_n)\), \(\texttt{thm:observed-gradient-minimal}\) identifies \(\psi_n\) as the canonical gradient and gives \(\lVert\psi_n\rVert_{L^2(P_n)}^2=\sigma_n^2\). By \(\texttt{lem:canonical-gradient-information-inequality}\), every regular asymptotically linear estimator therefore has asymptotic variance at least \(\sigma_n^2\).

Along a triangular sequence satisfying \(\texttt{thm:regular-efficient-inference-scale-aware}\), that theorem gives
\[\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_n(O_{i,n})+o_{P_n}(\sigma_n/\sqrt n).\]
Thus the proposed estimator has influence function \(\psi_n\) and leading variance \(\sigma_n^2/n\), or normalized asymptotic variance \(\sigma_n^2\). It attains the information lower bound, proving both directions of the efficiency statement.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The canonical-gradient information inequality matches the leading variance achieved by the cross-fitted estimator, making the efficiency statement two-sided. \textit{Gap.} The information bound is first-order and does not calibrate inference at equality. \textit{Consumer.} It certifies that no regular asymptotically linear estimator improves the first-order variance at a fixed positive-variance regular law.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal target is the sharp lower endpoint \(\theta_n\), not the generally unidentified full moment \(E[\lVert Y_n(1)-Y_n(0)\rVert_2^2]\). Its observed-data canonical gradient has a covariate-composition term and one inverse-propensity centered transport derivative for the realized arm. This is the observational ingredient beyond randomized marginal-sample smooth COT inference. On a predetermined positive-variance collection satisfying the learner premises and the three relative-rate conditions, \(\widehat\theta_n\) is asymptotically linear with this influence function, attains the information bound, \(\widehat\sigma_n/\sigma_n\to1\), and \([\widehat\theta_n\mathbin\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\widehat\sigma_n/\sqrt n]\) has uniform limiting coverage \(1-\alpha\), including admissible sequences with \(\sigma_n\to0\). In a 401(k) SIPP analysis, these conclusions require credible ignorability and overlap, transport regularity and nuisance learning sufficient for the displayed rates, and positive row variances; exact conditional equality requires a different calibration.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The Gaussian theorem covers positive variance only. Its vanishing-variance extension requires \(\sqrt n\,\delta_n/\sigma_n\to0\), \(r_{S,n}/\sigma_n\to0\), and \(\sqrt n\,\sigma_n\to\infty\); it does not include exact equality. At equality, \(\theta_n=0\), \(\psi_n=0\), and the first-order pivot is nonnormal. The second variation is a weighted Neumann energy, and at the stated nonatomic equality laws the full-tangent Hessian has an infinite-dimensional positive identity compression. This rules out uniformly tight unregularized Gaussian spectral calibration over the full parent, but it does not rule out slower covariate-regularized, derivative-aware, subsampling, or nonspectral procedures. Constructing and validating one procedure that adapts honestly through equality remains open.

\section{Honest scope}

Sharp causal identification uses consistency, conditional ignorability, arm positivity, and measurable conditional-coupling attainment. The canonical-gradient claim instead uses a unique measurable normalized dual derivative, conditional-transport pathwise differentiability, and square integrability; H\"older density and all-feasible-pairs curvature conditions enter only the explicit nuisance-rate route. Uniform Gaussian inference holds on predetermined positive-variance collections satisfying the density and propensity learner premises and \(\sqrt n\,\delta_n/\sigma_n\to0\), \(r_{S,n}/\sigma_n\to0\), and \(\sqrt n\,\sigma_n\to\infty\). The sufficient thresholds \(s>D/2\) and \(t_\pi>Dd_X/(4s)\) are not claimed minimax necessary. No claim is made under hidden confounding, overlap failure, unsupported transport irregularity, or exact equality, and the degeneracy result remains a route-specific obstruction rather than a general impossibility theorem.
