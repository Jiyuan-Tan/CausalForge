\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}transport\_\allowbreak{}boundary\_\allowbreak{}overlap\_\allowbreak{}calibration\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A randomized trial may contain progressively fewer patients near a clinically important covariate boundary even while the target population retains positive density there. Under the stated identification assumptions, the causal target equals the observed-law functional \(\tau=\int_0^1\{\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)\}f_T(x)\,dx\). The proved minimax lower source phase is \(n^{-1/2}\) for \(a<1\), \(\sqrt{\log n/n}\) at \(a=1\), and \(n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}\) for \(a>1\), in addition to \(m^{-1/2}\). The same order is achieved by a population boundary-corrected oracle local-polynomial bias--standard-deviation benchmark, not by a proved data-measurable estimator. The executable capped calibrated-transport plus boundary-spliced estimator has an exact realized-covariate linear representation, and its finite-sample fixed-class interval uses the computable jet-LP radius \(\overline B_X\) plus \(R_S+R_T\). No held-out weight-localization rate, empirical frontier attainment, frontier-order expected length, or executable self-similar adaptive interval is claimed.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Regular transport theory relies on square-integrable Riesz weights, while existing weak-overlap results do not deliver this two-sample Hölder boundary lower phase diagram, its critical logarithm, a boundary-corrected oracle benchmark, or an observable finite-sample execution certificate for the capped transport construction.

\paragraph{Niche.} Polynomial trial depletion \(f_S(x)=x^ag_S(x)\) with target density bounded away from zero makes the transported causal mean regular below \(a=1\), critical at \(a=1\), and boundary-dominated above it, while the independent target covariate sample contributes its own parametric floor.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves the one-sided minimax lower phase diagram, pairs it with a boundary-corrected oracle local-polynomial linear-weight benchmark, constructs an executable capped RiCOT plus boundary regression estimator with an exact realized-design linear/Hoeffding certificate, gives a finite jet-LP bias radius for fixed-class coverage, and proves a two-smoothness nonadaptation obstruction. It does not claim that the executable estimator attains the minimax frontier.

\section{Related work}

Dahabreh et al. provide the randomized-trial consistency, conditional randomization, and conditional-mean transportability framework used to interpret the observed-law integral causally. Burman et al. are the calibrated optimal-transport and RiCOT cardiomyopathy application anchor; their target-outcome estimand and bounded-overlap theory motivate the construction but do not supply the present polynomial-boundary lower theorem or held-out capped-weight localization. Donoho's optimal-recovery perspective and the minimax-linear work of Armstrong and Kolesár, Imbens and Wager, and Hirshberg, Maleki, and Zubizarreta motivate deterministic smoothness-class bias certificates. Armstrong and Kolesár's 2021 finite-sample optimal ATE result is non-nested: it conditions on realized treatment and covariates in a one-sample unconfoundedness model, whereas this paper studies an independent two-sample transported mean with polynomial source depletion. Weak-overlap results of Ma and Wang and Heiler and Kazak motivate boundary regularization but do not yield the present critical two-sample phase diagram. The fixed-class interval here follows from the exact conditional linear representation, a finite Hölder jet-LP envelope, and bounded-sum Hoeffding concentration.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Canonical left-to-right standard normalized degree-\(q\) B-spline basis \(\mathbf b_{h,J}=(N_1,\ldots,N_{J+q})^\top\) of \(\mathcal B_{h,J}\); the basis forms a partition of unity, but no individual coordinate is designated as the constant function.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widetilde\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathbf b\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}\{h,\allowbreak{}J\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{equivalent nonconstant spline-\allowbreak{}coordinate map}; \([h,1]\to\mathbb R^{J+q-1}\); \texttt{nonredundant calibration coordinates}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The vector \((N_2,\ldots,N_{J+q})^\top\) obtained from the standard normalized basis \(\mathbf b_{h,J}=(N_1,\ldots,N_{J+q})^\top\); together with the separate constant coordinate it spans \(\mathcal B_{h,J}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat P\textasciicircum{}S\_\allowbreak{}h} --- \texttt{empirical subprobability measure}; finite measures on \([h,1]\); \texttt{transport source measure}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For fold \(k\), the training-complement interior source measure \(\widehat P^{S,(-k)}_h=|\mathcal T^S_k|^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal T^S_k}\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}\delta_{X_i}\); the fold superscript is suppressed when context fixes \(k\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat P\textasciicircum{}T\_\allowbreak{}h} --- \texttt{empirical subprobability measure}; finite measures on \([h,1]\); \texttt{transport target measure}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For fold \(k\), the training-complement interior target measure \(\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h=|\mathcal T^T_k|^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal T^T_k}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h\}\delta_{X_j^T}\); the fold superscript is suppressed when context fixes \(k\).

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} On a positive-interior-mass branch, the unique exact-feasibility or penalized semi-unbalanced entropic optimizer constructed in \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}weights}\); on the zero-interior-mass branch, the zero finite measure is a deterministic fallback and is not asserted to couple a nonzero target marginal.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta\_\allowbreak{}\{h,\allowbreak{}J\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{calibration multiplier or fallback vector}; \(\mathbb R^{J+q-1}\); \texttt{branch-\allowbreak{}qualified calibration potential}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} On a positive-interior-mass exact-feasibility branch, the selected multiplier for the \(J+q-1\) explicit nonconstant spline-calibration equations; on a positive-interior-mass penalized branch, the corresponding quadratic-penalty gradient multiplier; on the zero-interior-mass branch, the deterministic zero vector.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}psi\_\allowbreak{}\{h,\allowbreak{}J\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{target-\allowbreak{}marginal multiplier or fallback vector}; \texttt{real vectors indexed by interior target training observations}; \texttt{branch-\allowbreak{}qualified target-\allowbreak{}marginal potential}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{On a positive-\allowbreak{}interior-\allowbreak{}mass optimization branch,\allowbreak{} the selected target-\allowbreak{}marginal KKT multiplier,\allowbreak{} jointly minimum-\allowbreak{}norm with the calibration multiplier where applicable;\allowbreak{} on the zero-\allowbreak{}interior-\allowbreak{}mass branch,\allowbreak{} the deterministic zero vector.}
  \item \texttt{J\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{positive integer sequence}; \(\mathbb N^\mathbb N\); \texttt{regression sieve knot-\allowbreak{}interval count}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Deterministic interior regression knot-interval count \(J_{\mu,n}=\lceil n^{1/(2s+1)}\rceil\); the associated degree-\(q\) standard spline basis has \(J_{\mu,n}+q\) coefficients.

  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}beta\_\allowbreak{}\{k,\allowbreak{}h,\allowbreak{}J\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{interior regression coefficient vector}; \(\mathbb R^{J_{\mu,n}+q}\); \texttt{interior regression coefficient}

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{On a positive-\allowbreak{}interior-\allowbreak{}mass optimization branch,\allowbreak{} the exponential continuation of the selected primal-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}dual solution that agrees with the first-\allowbreak{}marginal density at source training atoms;\allowbreak{} on the zero-\allowbreak{}interior-\allowbreak{}mass branch it is the identically zero deterministic fallback.}

It is measurable and bounded, but it need not inherit the raw plan's training-atom spline-calibration equations.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}tau\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{sp\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}\{h,\allowbreak{}J\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{},\allowbreak{}J\_\allowbreak{}\{r,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{real-\allowbreak{}valued estimator}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{proposed estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Boundary-spliced Riesz-calibrated transport estimator indexed by separate regression and calibration knot-interval counts \(J_{\mu,n}\) and \(J_{r,n}\). Its full regression coefficient dimension is \(J_{\mu,n}+q\), its full calibration spline-space dimension is \(J_{r,n}+q\), and its explicit nonconstant calibration vector has dimension \(J_{r,n}+q-1\) because the target-marginal mass equation supplies the constant coordinate.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat H\_\allowbreak{}\{k,\allowbreak{}h,\allowbreak{}J\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{positive-\allowbreak{}definite matrix}; \(\mathbb R^{(J_{\mu,n}+q)\times(J_{\mu,n}+q)}\); \texttt{interior linear-\allowbreak{}smoother Gram matrix}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Observable fold-trained interior ridge Gram matrix for the full \(J_{\mu,n}+q\)-coordinate standard spline basis in the linear-coefficient construction.
  \item \texttt{L\_\allowbreak{}\{i,\allowbreak{}k,\allowbreak{}h,\allowbreak{}J\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{observable linear-\allowbreak{}smoother coefficient}; \([0,1]\to\mathbb R\); \texttt{regression linear coefficient}

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Coefficient of \(Z_i\) in the exact covariate-conditional linear expansion of the unchanged spliced estimator.
  \item \texttt{B\_\allowbreak{}X(h,\allowbreak{}J\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{},\allowbreak{}J\_\allowbreak{}\{r,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{})} --- \texttt{nonnegative observable statistic}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{design-\allowbreak{}discrepancy certificate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Worst-case realized source--target design discrepancy over the bounded radius-\(2M\) Hölder class.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=Y^S(A)\) almost surely under \(F^S_{n,m}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (potential-outcome consistency, \cite{DahabrehRobertsonStuartEtAl2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:trial-randomization]\label{ass:trial-randomization}
\((Y^S(0),Y^S(1))\perp A\mid X\) under \(F^S_{n,m}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (conditional treatment randomization, \cite{DahabrehRobertsonStuartEtAl2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:known-propensity]\label{ass:known-propensity}
\(e\) is fixed and known by the source-trial randomization protocol.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (known randomized-trial propensity, \cite{BurmanGamaloGhosalKundu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-positivity]\label{ass:treatment-positivity}
\(\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\varepsilon\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (trial-treatment positivity, \cite{BurmanGamaloGhosalKundu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:mean-transportability]\label{ass:mean-transportability}
\(\mathbb E_{F^T_{n,m}}[Y^T(d)\mid X^T=x]=\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}[Y^S(d)\mid X=x]=\mu_d(x)\) for every \(d\in\{0,1\}\) and \(P_T\)-almost every \(x\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (conditional mean transportability, \cite{DahabrehRobertsonStuartEtAl2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:source-boundary-factor]\label{ass:source-boundary-factor}
\(c_{S,-}\le g_S(x)\le c_{S,+}\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition permits polynomial depletion of trial enrollment at a clinically meaningful covariate boundary. Beta-type enrollment densities and smooth exponential tilts of \(x^a\) satisfy it, so it describes a concrete weak-overlap mechanism rather than an estimator-dependent rate restriction.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:target-boundary-factor]\label{ass:target-boundary-factor}
\(c_{T,-}\le g_T(x)\le c_{T,+}\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (target density bounded away from zero and infinity, \cite{Tsybakov2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:source-factor-smoothness]\label{ass:source-factor-smoothness}
\(g_S\in\mathcal H^t(M)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder density regularity, \cite{Tsybakov2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:target-factor-smoothness]\label{ass:target-factor-smoothness}
\(g_T\in\mathcal H^t(M)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder density regularity, \cite{Tsybakov2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:control-response-smoothness]\label{ass:control-response-smoothness}
\(\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(M)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder regression regularity, \cite{Tsybakov2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treated-response-smoothness]\label{ass:treated-response-smoothness}
\(\mu_1\in\mathcal H^s(M)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder regression regularity, \cite{Tsybakov2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:residual-nondegeneracy}
\(\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)\ge M^{-1}\) for every \(d\in\{0,1\}\) and \(P_S\)-almost every \(x\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nondegenerate regression noise, \cite{Tsybakov2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sample-balance]\label{ass:sample-balance}
\(m/n\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (proportional two-sample asymptotics, \cite{BurmanGamaloGhosalKundu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:model-class]\label{def:model-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Polynomial-\allowbreak{}boundary transport class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)=\bigl\{\mathbb P_{n,m}=P_S^{\otimes n}\otimes P_T^{\otimes m}:\exists(F^S_{n,m},F^T_{n,m})\text{ with }P_S=(\pi_S)_\#F^S_{n,m},\ P_T=(\pi_T)_\#F^T_{n,m},\text{ and every listed member-property assumption holds at the fixed indices }(n,m,a,s,t)\bigr\}.\] The finite-\((n,m)\) class contains no condition on how \(m/n\) behaves along a sequence.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}trial-\allowbreak{}randomization}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}known-\allowbreak{}propensity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}treatment-\allowbreak{}positivity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}mean-\allowbreak{}transportability}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}source-\allowbreak{}boundary-\allowbreak{}factor}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}target-\allowbreak{}boundary-\allowbreak{}factor}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}source-\allowbreak{}factor-\allowbreak{}smoothness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}target-\allowbreak{}factor-\allowbreak{}smoothness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}control-\allowbreak{}response-\allowbreak{}smoothness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}treated-\allowbreak{}response-\allowbreak{}smoothness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}residual-\allowbreak{}nondegeneracy}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:minimax-risk]\label{def:minimax-risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Two-\allowbreak{}sample minimax root risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)=\inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_{\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)}\Bigl\{\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{n,m}}[(\widehat\tau-\tau)^2]\Bigr\}^{1/2},\] where the infimum ranges over all estimators measurable with respect to \(\mathcal D_{n,m}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}model-\allowbreak{}class}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:frontier-rate]\label{def:frontier-rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Boundary-\allowbreak{}overlap frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)=m^{-1/2}+\begin{cases}n^{-1/2},&a<1,\\ \sqrt{\log n/n},&a=1,\\ n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)},&a>1.\end{cases}\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:boundary-bump]\label{def:boundary-bump}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Boundary-\allowbreak{}bump witness}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[b_h(x)=h^sK(x/h)\mathbf 1\{0\le x\le h\}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{K}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:regression-fit]\label{def:regression-fit}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Executable cross-\allowbreak{}fitted boundary regression}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each \(k\in\{1,\ldots,K_0\}\), use the source indices in \(\mathcal T^S_k\) and set
\[
\widehat G_{k,h}=\frac1{|\mathcal T^S_k|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal T^S_k}\mathbf 1\{X_i\le2h\}\mathbf R_h(X_i)\mathbf R_h(X_i)^\top+n^{-2}I_{p+1},
\qquad
\widehat\alpha_{k,h}=\widehat G_{k,h}^{-1}\frac1{|\mathcal T^S_k|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal T^S_k}\mathbf 1\{X_i\le2h\}\mathbf R_h(X_i)Z_i.
\]
For the interior spline, \(J_{\mu,n}\) is the number of knot intervals and
\[
d_{\mu,n}=J_{\mu,n}+q.
\]
Let
\[
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)
=\bigl(N_{1,h,J_{\mu,n}}(x),\ldots,N_{d_{\mu,n},h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)\bigr)^\top
\in\mathbb R^{d_{\mu,n}}
\]
be the full standard normalized degree-\(q\) B-spline basis for the endpoint-repeated equally spaced knots. Using the same training indices with \(X_i\ge h\), let \(\widehat\beta_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}\) be the unique minimizer over \(\mathbb R^{d_{\mu,n}}\) of
\[
\frac1{|\mathcal T^S_k|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal T^S_k}\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}
\{Z_i-\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_i)^\top\beta\}^2+n^{-2}\|\beta\|_2^2.
\]
Define the untruncated fit
\[
\widehat\mu^{(-k)}_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbf R_h(x)^\top\widehat\alpha_{k,h},&0\le x<h,\\
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)^\top\widehat\beta_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}},&h\le x\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
Empty local or interior samples return the unique ridge solution. The positive ridges and ordered standard bases make every fold fit unique. No clipping map or kink derivative is part of the construction.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal D\_\allowbreak{}\{n,\allowbreak{}m\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{J\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{}}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:ricot-weights]\label{def:ricot-weights}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Executable interior RiCOT weights and certificate role}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each fold \(k\), form \(\widehat P^{S,(-k)}_h\) and \(\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h\) from the training complements. Put
\[
d_{r,n}=J_{r,n}+q,
\qquad
\mathbf b_{h,J_{r,n}}=(N_1,\ldots,N_{d_{r,n}})^\top,
\]
where \((N_1,\ldots,N_{d_{r,n}})\) is the standard left-to-right normalized degree-\(q\) B-spline basis for the \(J_{r,n}\) endpoint-repeated equally spaced knot intervals on \([h,1]\). Define the equivalent calibration coordinates
\[
\boldsymbol\phi_{h,J_{r,n}}(x)=\bigl(1,N_2(x),\ldots,N_{d_{r,n}}(x)\bigr)^\top,
\qquad
\widetilde{\boldsymbol\phi}_{h,J_{r,n}}(x)=\bigl(N_2(x),\ldots,N_{d_{r,n}}(x)\bigr)^\top
\in\mathbb R^{d_{r,n}-1}.
\]
The partition-of-unity identity gives
\[
N_1(x)=1-\sum_{\ell=2}^{d_{r,n}}N_\ell(x),
\]
so \(\boldsymbol\phi_{h,J_{r,n}}\) and \(\mathbf b_{h,J_{r,n}}\) span the same spline space.

If either interior training measure has zero total mass, define \(\widehat\Pi^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}=0\), set the calibration vector in \(\mathbb R^{d_{r,n}-1}\) and every target-marginal multiplier to zero, and set both the raw continuation and the capped downstream weight to zero on \([h,1]\). This branch precedes all optimization below.

Suppose now that both interior training measures have positive total mass. Let \(\mathcal F^\circ_{h,J_{r,n},k}\) be the finite plans \(\Pi\) that have strictly positive density relative to \(\widehat P^{S,(-k)}_h\otimes\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h\), satisfy \(\Pi_2=\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h\), and satisfy
\[
\int\widetilde{\boldsymbol\phi}_{h,J_{r,n}}(x)\,d\Pi_1(x)
=\int\widetilde{\boldsymbol\phi}_{h,J_{r,n}}(z)\,d\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h(z).
\]
Because \(\Pi_2=\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h\) implies
\[
\Pi_1([h,1])=\Pi_2([h,1])=\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h([h,1]),
\]
the separate target-mass constraint supplies the constant coordinate. Together with the preceding \(d_{r,n}-1\) equations and the identity for \(N_1\), it is equivalent to calibration of the full space \(\mathcal B_{h,J_{r,n}}\).

If \(\mathcal F^\circ_{h,J_{r,n},k}\ne\varnothing\), define \(\widehat\Pi^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}\) as the unique minimizer over its closure of
\[
\int c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,z)\,d\Pi
+\eta_0\operatorname{KL}(\Pi\,\|\,\widehat P^{S,(-k)}_h\otimes\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h)
+\lambda_0\operatorname{KL}(\Pi_1\,\|\,\widehat P^{S,(-k)}_h).
\]
Otherwise minimize the same strictly convex criterion subject to \(\Pi_2=\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h\), plus
\[
\xi_n\left\|
\int\widetilde{\boldsymbol\phi}_{h,J_{r,n}}(x)\,d\Pi_1(x)
-\int\widetilde{\boldsymbol\phi}_{h,J_{r,n}}(z)\,d\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h(z)
\right\|_2^2.
\]
Generalized Kullback--Leibler divergence is used for finite measures. In the exact-feasibility branch, let
\[
\widehat\theta^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}\in\mathbb R^{d_{r,n}-1}
\]
and \(\widehat\psi^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n},j}\) be the KKT multipliers for the nonconstant calibration and target-marginal equations; among all joint dual solutions choose the pair of smallest Euclidean norm, with lexicographic tie-breaking. In the penalized branch, set
\[
\widehat\delta^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}=
\int\widetilde{\boldsymbol\phi}_{h,J_{r,n}}(x)\,d\widehat\Pi^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n},1}(x)
-\int\widetilde{\boldsymbol\phi}_{h,J_{r,n}}(z)\,d\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_h(z),
\qquad
\widehat\theta^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}=2\xi_n\widehat\delta^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}
\in\mathbb R^{d_{r,n}-1},
\]
and let \(\widehat\psi^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n},j}\) be a KKT multiplier for the target-marginal equations; among compatible target-marginal multipliers choose the one of smallest Euclidean norm, with lexicographic tie-breaking. For every \(x\in[h,1]\), define the raw KKT continuation
\[
\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}(x)=
\left[
\frac1{|\mathcal T^T_k|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal T^T_k}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h\}
\exp\left\{-\frac{c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,X_j^T)
+\widehat\theta_{h,J_{r,n}}^{(-k)\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol\phi}_{h,J_{r,n}}(x)
+\widehat\psi^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n},j}}{\eta_0}\right\}
\right]^{\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)}
\]
and the capped downstream weight
\[
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}(x)
=\min\left\{\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}(x),\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}h^{-a}\right\}.
\]
Only \(\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}\) is the KKT continuation of \(d\widehat\Pi^{(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n},1}/d\widehat P^{S,(-k)}_h\), and only it agrees with that density at source training atoms. The downstream weight \(\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}\) is its pointwise projection onto \([0,(c_{T,+}/c_{S,-})h^{-a}]\). It is positive, measurable, and bounded at held-out points, but after projection it need not satisfy the raw plan's exact spline-calibration equations. The cap does not truncate the oracle ratio on \([h,1]\), because \(r(x)=x^{-a}g_T(x)/g_S(x)\le(c_{T,+}/c_{S,-})h^{-a}\). The deterministic schedules are \(\eta_n=\eta_0\), \(\lambda_n=\lambda_0\), \(\xi_n=n^2\), and \(J=J_{r,n}\); the boundary-splice bandwidth remains \(h=h_n\) and is not used as the calibration knot-interval count.

Both the raw continuation and the capped downstream weight are measurable functions of the observed covariates. In the execution certificate the capped downstream weight enters only through the observable coefficients \(\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\). The cap still does not inherit the raw plan's exact spline-calibration equations, and no held-out localization, coefficient-norm rate, leverage bound, or frontier attainment is claimed.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal D\_\allowbreak{}\{n,\allowbreak{}m\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{J\_\allowbreak{}\{r,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{}}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:ricot-linear-coefficients]\label{def:ricot-linear-coefficients}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Observable RiCOT linear coefficients}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each fold \(k\), bandwidth \(h\in(0,1)\), and regression knot-interval count \(J_{\mu,n}\), put \(d_{\mu,n}=J_{\mu,n}+q\) and define the observable interior ridge Gram matrix
\[
\widehat H_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}
=\frac1{|\mathcal T^S_k|}\sum_{u\in\mathcal T^S_k}\mathbf 1\{X_u\ge h\}
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_u)\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_u)^\top+n^{-2}I_{d_{\mu,n}}
\in\mathbb R^{d_{\mu,n}\times d_{\mu,n}}.
\]
For \(q_0\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), set
\[
L_{q_0,k,h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)=\frac{\mathbf 1\{q_0\in\mathcal T^S_k\}}{|\mathcal T^S_k|}
\begin{cases}
\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\le2h\}\mathbf R_h(x)^\top\widehat G_{k,h}^{-1}\mathbf R_h(X_{q_0}),&0\le x<h,\\
\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\ge h\}\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)^\top\widehat H_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}^{-1}\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_{q_0}),&h\le x\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
For integers \(n,m\ge2\), every training complement in this display is nonempty. Finally define the observable coefficient of \(Z_{q_0}\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{q_0}(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})={}&
\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mL_{q_0,k_T(j),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_j^T)
+\frac1n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(q_0))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_{q_0})\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\ge h\}\\
&-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}
L_{q_0,k_S(i),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_i).
\end{aligned}
\]
The regression matrices have dimension \(d_{\mu,n}=J_{\mu,n}+q\). The weight construction uses \(d_{r,n}=J_{r,n}+q\) standard B-splines and \(d_{r,n}-1\) nonconstant calibration coordinates. All matrices are invertible because of the positive ridges, and every displayed quantity is a measurable function of the observed source and target covariates. This construction only names the coefficients; the exact estimator identity is proved in \(\mathrm{thm:ricot\text{-}attainment}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}regression-\allowbreak{}fit}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}ricot-\allowbreak{}weights}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:spliced-estimator]\label{def:spliced-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Riesz-\allowbreak{}calibrated boundary-\allowbreak{}spliced estimator and linear carrier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Using the fold maps \(k_S\) and \(k_T\) and the unique training-complement fits from \(\mathrm{def:regression\text{-}fit}\) and \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}weights}\), define
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}=
\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\widehat\mu_{h,J_{\mu,n}}^{(-k_T(j))}(X_j^T)
+\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}\{Z_i-\widehat\mu_{h,J_{\mu,n}}^{(-k_S(i))}(X_i)\}.
\]
Every displayed evaluation uses a fit trained without that observation's deterministic fold. The capped downstream weight is evaluated as a function at held-out \(X_i\), not as an empirical-support lookup; only the raw \(\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT}}\) has KKT-continuation and training-atom density status. If an interior training measure is empty, its weight contribution is zero by \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}weights}\). The indices \(J_{\mu,n}\) and \(J_{r,n}\) are respectively regression and calibration knot-interval counts, not coefficient dimensions. The full regression coefficient dimension is \(J_{\mu,n}+q\), the full calibration spline-space dimension is \(J_{r,n}+q\), and the explicit nonconstant calibration vector has dimension \(J_{r,n}+q-1\) because the target-marginal mass equation supplies the constant coordinate. Only \(h\) is the boundary-splice bandwidth.

After the covariates are fixed, the foldwise ridge fits are linear in the source pseudo-outcomes. With the observable coefficients defined in \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\), algebraic expansion of the unchanged display gives
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}=\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})Z_i.
\]
This equality is proved in \(\mathrm{thm:ricot\text{-}attainment}\); it records the estimator's certificate carrier without modifying its folds, fits, weights, or formula.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}regression-\allowbreak{}fit}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}ricot-\allowbreak{}weights}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}ricot-\allowbreak{}linear-\allowbreak{}coefficients}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:bias-envelope]\label{def:bias-envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite certified Hölder jet-\allowbreak{}LP design discrepancy}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For this certificate use the following H\"older convention. Put \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\). For \(L>0\),
\[
\mathcal H^s(L)=\left\{v\in C^p([0,1]):
\max_{0\le r\le p}\|v^{(r)}\|_\infty\le L,
\quad
[v^{(p)}]_{\beta}:=\sup_{x\ne y}\frac{|v^{(p)}(x)-v^{(p)}(y)|}{|x-y|^\beta}\le L
\right\}.
\]
For integers \(n,m\ge2\) and \(h\in(0,1)\), let \(z_1<\cdots<z_N\) be the distinct sites among \(X_1,\ldots,X_n,X_1^T,\ldots,X_m^T\), and set
\[
c_\ell=\sum_{i:X_i=z_\ell}\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})-
\frac1m\#\{j:X_j^T=z_\ell\}.
\]
Retain the abstract design discrepancy
\[
B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})=
\sup_{\substack{v\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\\ \|v\|_\infty\le1}}
\left|\sum_{\ell=1}^Nc_\ell v(z_\ell)\right|.
\]
For each \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), let \(V_\sigma\) be the optimal value of the finite linear program
\[
\operatorname{maximize}_{(u_{\ell,r})}\quad
\sigma\sum_{\ell=1}^Nc_\ell u_{\ell,0}
\]
over \(N(p+1)\) real variables \(u_{\ell,r}\), \(1\le\ell\le N\), \(0\le r\le p\), subject to
\[
|u_{\ell,0}|\le1,
\qquad
|u_{\ell,r}|\le2M\quad(1\le r\le p),
\]
and, for every \(1\le\ell,k\le N\) and \(0\le r\le p\),
\[
\left|u_{\ell,r}-\sum_{q=r}^p
u_{k,q}\frac{(z_\ell-z_k)^{q-r}}{(q-r)!}\right|
\le
2M\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s-r+1)}|z_\ell-z_k|^{s-r}.
\]
Each absolute-value restriction means its two one-sided linear inequalities. Define the certified jet-LP radius
\[
\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})=\max\{V_{-1},V_1\}.
\]
The two programs have \(N(p+1)\) variables and \(O\{N^2(p+1)\}\) linear inequalities. Every admissible H\"older trace is feasible, so
\[
B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\le\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}).
\]
For \(0<s\le1\) the reduction is exact by H\"older--McShane extension. For \(s>1\) it is a certified conservative jet relaxation, not an asserted exact finite Whitney characterization. Both optimal values are finite because of the box constraints. No stochastic-order or frontier-order claim is part of this definition.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}ricot-\allowbreak{}linear-\allowbreak{}coefficients}.
\end{definition}

R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})

No empirical variance estimator, covariance estimator, studentization, leverage condition, Gaussian approximation, or multiplier approximation is part of this construction.

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:bias-aware-interval]\label{def:bias-aware-interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite-\allowbreak{}sample executable RiCOT certificate interval}

\begin{lemma}[lem:riesz-threshold]\label{lem:riesz-threshold}
For every \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) and \(h\downarrow0\), \[\left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2=\int_h^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\asymp\begin{cases}1,&a<1,\\ \log(1/h),&a=1,\\ h^{1-a},&a>1.\end{cases}\] Hence the target expectation functional has a source \(L^2(P_S)\) Riesz representer if and only if \(a<1\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\). By the definitions \(f_S(x)=x^ag_S(x)\), \(f_T(x)=g_T(x)\), and \(r(x)=f_T(x)/f_S(x)\), and because \(f_S(x)>0\) for \(x\in(0,1]\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2
&=\int_h^1r(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_h^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\\
&=\int_h^1x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)^2}{g_S(x)}\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The two boundary-factor assumptions and \(c_{S,-}>0\) imply the pointwise comparison
\[
\frac{c_{T,-}^2}{c_{S,+}}x^{-a}
\le x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)^2}{g_S(x)}
\le\frac{c_{T,+}^2}{c_{S,-}}x^{-a},
\qquad 0<x\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
All divisions in (1)--(2) are therefore valid. Direct integration gives
\[
\int_h^1x^{-a}\,dx=
\begin{cases}
\{1-h^{1-a}\}/(1-a),&a<1,\\
\log(1/h),&a=1,\\
\{h^{1-a}-1\}/(a-1),&a>1.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(a<1\), the first expression in (3) is bounded above and bounded away from zero for every sufficiently small \(h\). For fixed \(a>1\), the ratio of the third expression to \(h^{1-a}\) converges to \(1/(a-1)\). Combining (1)--(3) proves
\[
\left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2
\asymp
\begin{cases}
1,&a<1,\\
\log(1/h),&a=1,\\
h^{1-a},&a>1.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
The comparison constants depend only on the fixed exponent and boundary-factor constants, and are uniform over the model class.

It remains to verify the representer assertion. On \(L^2(P_S)\), initially define the target-expectation functional on bounded functions by
\[
T(v)=\int_0^1v(x)f_T(x)\,dx
=\int_0^1v(x)r(x)f_S(x)\,dx.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(a<1\), (2)--(3) with \(h\downarrow0\) show that \(r\in L^2(P_S)\). The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality then gives \(|T(v)|\le\|r\|_{L^2(P_S)}\|v\|_{L^2(P_S)}\), so \(r\) is the source Riesz representer.

Conversely, suppose that an element \(q\in L^2(P_S)\) represented \(T\). For every \(\delta>0\) and every Borel set \(B\subseteq[\delta,1]\), the indicator \(\mathbf 1_B\) lies in \(L^2(P_S)\), and (5) would imply
\[
\int_Bq(x)f_S(x)\,dx=\int_Bf_T(x)\,dx
=\int_Br(x)f_S(x)\,dx.
\tag{6}
\]
Hence \(q=r\), \(P_S\)-almost everywhere on \([\delta,1]\). Letting \(\delta\) range over the positive rationals gives \(q=r\), \(P_S\)-almost everywhere on \((0,1]\). When \(a\ge1\), (2)--(3) show that \(\int_0^1r(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx=\infty\), contradicting \(q\in L^2(P_S)\). The point \(x=0\) is Lebesgue-null and creates no additional support case. Thus a source \(L^2(P_S)\) representer exists exactly when \(a<1\). \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This norm is the precise regularity threshold behind the regular, critical, and nonregular regimes. \textit{Gap.} The transported control-regression component in Burman et al.'s distinct target-outcome estimand uses a density-ratio representer under bounded overlap, while Khan and Tamer link large inverse weights to irregularity without computing this polynomial-boundary threshold for the present covariate-only target functional. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma tells analysts of the RICOT cardiomyopathy transport study whether an ordinary influence-function variance is finite before fitting a transport estimator.

\begin{lemma}[lem:boundary-bump]\label{lem:boundary-bump}
Suppose \(t>0\), \(M\ge a+1\), and \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\). There are constants \(c,C>0\) such that, for every sufficiently small \(h\), the perturbations generated by \(b_h\) yield two observed-data laws \(\mathbb P_{+,h},\mathbb P_{-,h}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) whose source marginals satisfy \[\operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,n},P_{-,h}^{S,n})\le Cnh^{2s+a+1},\qquad |\tau(\mathbb P_{+,h})-\tau(\mathbb P_{-,h})|\ge ch^{s+1}.\] A separate smooth target-density perturbation yields two full-data laws whose observed target projections have divergence bounded by a constant and target separation at least \(cm^{-1/2}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix arbitrary \(t>0\). We construct the full-data laws explicitly and verify every support, normalization, positivity, smoothness, and variance condition in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).

Put
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1,
\qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
Both functions integrate to one. Their nonsingular factors are \(g_S(x)=a+1\) and \(g_T(x)=1\). The declared parameter ranges give
\[
c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
\qquad
c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}.
\tag{2}
\]
The factors are constant, so they belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every fixed \(t>0\); the source-factor radius is valid because \(M\ge a+1\). Thus (1)--(2) verify density support, normalization, the two boundary-factor inequalities, and density-factor smoothness without either of the deleted estimator-side smoothness inequalities.

Fix the known randomized propensity \(e\), which satisfies \(\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\varepsilon\). Conditional on \(X=x\), draw \(A\) as Bernoulli with success probability \(e(x)\), independently of the source potential outcomes. Given a sign \(v\in\{-1,1\}\), take the source potential outcomes to be conditionally Bernoulli with means
\[
\mu_{0,v}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,v}(x)=\frac12+v\delta b_h(x),
\tag{3}
\]
and impose consistency by setting \(Y=Y^S(A)\). Give the target potential outcomes conditionally Bernoulli laws with the same two means.

Let \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\). Since \(K\) is a smooth compactly supported bump, dilation gives, for \(0\le q\le p\),
\[
\lVert D^q b_h\rVert_\infty\le C_Kh^{s-q},
\qquad
[D^p b_h]_{\beta}\le C_K,
\tag{4}
\]
uniformly over \(0<h\le1\). Choose a fixed \(\delta>0\) small enough that (3) takes values in \([0,1]\), both responses lie in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), and
\[
\frac14-\delta^2\lVert K\rVert_\infty^2\ge M^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
This is possible because \(M>4\). Equation (5) verifies residual nondegeneracy, and the construction verifies consistency, conditional randomization, known propensity, treatment positivity, and conditional mean transportability. Consequently both induced observed laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) for every fixed \(t>0\).

For \(|u|\le\delta\lVert K\rVert_\infty<1/2\), direct Bernoulli algebra and compactness of that interval give
\[
\operatorname{kl}\!\left(\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)\right)
\le C_0u^2.
\tag{6}
\]
The two source laws differ only in the treated conditional outcome. Additivity of relative entropy for product measures, \(e(x)\le1\), (1), (3), and the substitution \(x=hu\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,\otimes n},P_{-,h}^{S,\otimes n})
&\le C_0n\delta^2\int_0^hb_h(x)^2(a+1)x^a\,dx\\
&=C_0(a+1)\delta^2nh^{2s+a+1}
  \int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du\\
&\le Cnh^{2s+a+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero,
\[
k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0.
\tag{8}
\]
The target covariate law is identical under the two signs, and hence
\[
|\tau(\mathbb P_{+,h})-\tau(\mathbb P_{-,h})|
=2\delta\int_0^hb_h(x)\,dx
=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) prove the source witness, including its boundary support \([0,h]\). The multiplier \(\delta\) can be reduced further, so the constant divergence used in a later two-point reduction can be made arbitrarily small.

For the target-sample witness, retain the source density in (1), put \(\ell(x)=x-1/2\), and choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\). Use the common source regressions
\[
\mu_0(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x),
\tag{10}
\]
with the same randomized Bernoulli full-data construction. A linear polynomial belongs to \(\mathcal H^s(M)\) for every fixed \(s>0\); reducing \(\delta_0\) verifies the response-radius, outcome-support, and variance conditions.

Keep the entire source law fixed and define
\[
f_{T,\pm}(x)=1\pm u_m\ell(x),
\qquad
u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\tag{11}
\]
Because \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), both functions in (11) integrate to one. Strict target-density slack, positivity of \(m\), and smoothness of \(\ell\) permit one fixed \(\delta_1>0\) such that, for every \(m\ge1\), both densities are positive, lie between the target-factor bounds, and belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\). Choose target potential outcomes with the common conditional means in (10), which preserves mean transportability and gives two admissible full-data laws with the same observed source marginal.

The lower target density is uniformly bounded away from zero. The inequality \(\log z\le z-1\) gives \(\operatorname{KL}(p,q)\le\int(p-q)^2/q\) for positive densities \(p,q\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{KL}(P_{T,+}^{\otimes m},P_{T,-}^{\otimes m})
&\le m\int_0^1
\frac{\{f_{T,+}(x)-f_{T,-}(x)\}^2}{f_{T,-}(x)}\,dx\\
&\le C_1m u_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
\le C_2\delta_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Finally, \(\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx=1/12\), so
\[
|\tau_+-\tau_-|
=2\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
=\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{6}m^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
The constants in (7), (9), (12), and (13) depend only on the fixed class constants, not on \(n,m,h\). This proves both assertions. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit witness turns polynomial boundary depletion into the source divergence and target separation used by the minimax and confidence-length converses. \textit{Gap.} Zhang et al. impose a uniformly bounded likelihood ratio and use a different observation model; their construction does not give the polynomial-boundary exponent or independent target-sample floor. \textit{Consumer.} The witness isolates source-boundary and target-sample testing obstructions that apply to every weighting method, without implying that the proposed RiCOT estimator attains the lower rate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:minimax-lower]\label{thm:minimax-lower}
For fixed \(a,s,t,M,\varepsilon,c_{S,-},c_{S,+},c_{T,-},c_{T,+}\) with \(t>0\), \(M\ge a+1\), and \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\), there is \(c>0\) such that \[\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\}.\] At \(a=1\), the source term is \(c\sqrt{\log n/n}\); it is obtained by a multiscale mixture of boundary bumps and has no hidden polynomial loss.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
We prove a two-point testing inequality first.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two laws in the observed experiment, let \(\tau_v=\tau(Q_v)\), and put \(\Delta=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\).  For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), classify the observation by whichever of \(\tau_0,\tau_1\) is closer to \(T\), breaking a tie deterministically.  A classification error under \(Q_v\) implies \(|T-\tau_v|\geq \Delta/2\).  The sum of the two error probabilities of any test is at least \(1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  Consequently,
\[
 \max_{v\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_v}(T-\tau_v)^2
 \geq \frac{\Delta^2}{8}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.
 \tag{14}
\]
For completeness, the total-variation bound needed below is also elementary.  If \(\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)=\infty\), there is nothing to show.  Otherwise, apply the log-sum inequality to a Hahn set for \(Q_0-Q_1\).  Writing \(p=Q_0(H)\) and \(q=Q_1(H)\), this gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}
 \geq 2(p-q)^2.
 \tag{15}
\]
The last inequality follows directly because, with \(q\) fixed, the difference between its middle and right sides vanishes with zero first derivative at \(p=q\), while its second derivative is \(1/\{p(1-p)\}-4\geq0\).  Since \(|p-q|=\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \leq \sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)/2}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Thus a pair having divergence at most a sufficiently small absolute constant and parameter separation \(\Delta\) forces root mean-squared risk at least a fixed multiple of \(\Delta\).

We next give the source-sample pairs.  In every regime set
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad
 f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\leq x\leq1.
 \tag{17}
\]
These are normalized densities on the declared support \(\mathcal X=[0,1]\).  Their nonsingular factors are \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\).  By the declared ranges of \(c_{S,-},c_{S,+}\), by the strict inequalities \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\), and by \(M\geq a+1\), they satisfy the two boundary-factor bounds and belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every \(t>0\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), draw \(A\) from the known Bernoulli randomization with success probability \(e(x)\), independently of the potential outcomes.  Conditional on \(X=x\), take the potential outcomes to be Bernoulli with the response means specified below, and set \(Y=Y^S(A)\).  Give the target potential outcomes Bernoulli laws with the same conditional means.  This construction enforces consistency, conditional randomization, known propensity, and conditional mean transportability.  The assumed bound \(\varepsilon\leq e(x)\leq1-\varepsilon\) supplies treatment positivity.  All perturbation multipliers below are chosen sufficiently small that the response means lie in \([0,1]\), their \(\mathcal H^s(M)\) norms are at most \(M\), and their Bernoulli variances are at least \(M^{-1}\); this is possible because \(M>4\).  Hence every displayed pair below consists of members of \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\).  No inverse or rank condition is used.

For \(a<1\), choose a nonnegative nonzero \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty((1/4,3/4))\) and define
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}\varphi(x).
 \tag{18}
\]
The support of \(\varphi\) stays a positive distance from both boundaries, so \(f_S\) is bounded above and away from zero there.  For \(|u|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, direct Bernoulli algebra gives
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
 \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)\right)\leq C_0u^2.
 \tag{19}
\]
The two target covariate laws are identical.  Additivity of relative entropy for the \(n\) independent source observations, followed by (19), yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{+,n},Q_{-,n})
 \leq C_1\delta^2 n\,n^{-1}\int_0^1\varphi(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx
 \leq C_2\delta^2.
 \tag{20}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1\varphi(x)\,dx
 \geq c_0n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{21}
\]
After reducing the fixed \(\delta>0\), equations (14)--(16) imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\geq c_1n^{-1/2},
 \qquad a<1.
 \tag{22}
\]

For \(a>1\), invoke \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\) with
\[
 h=c_hn^{-1/(2s+a+1)},
 \tag{23}
\]
where the fixed \(c_h>0\) is smaller than the lemma's boundary threshold.  Its source divergence is bounded by
\[
 Cnh^{2s+a+1}=Cc_h^{2s+a+1},
 \tag{24}
\]
whereas its parameter separation is at least
\[
 c h^{s+1}
 =c c_h^{s+1}n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
 \tag{25}
\]
Choose \(c_h\) so that (24) is a sufficiently small absolute constant.  Equations (14)--(16) and (25) then give
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \geq c_2n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)},
 \qquad a>1.
 \tag{26}
\]
The boundary support condition \([0,h]\subseteq[0,1]\) holds because \(0<h<1\).

It remains to obtain the exact critical logarithm when \(a=1\).  Choose a nonnegative nonzero \(\psi\in C_c^\infty((5/4,7/4))\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), let
\[
 J_n=\left\lfloor\frac{\log_2 n}{2(s+1)}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad h_j=2^{-j}\quad(1\leq j\leq J_n),
 \qquad A_n=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{nJ_n}},
 \tag{27}
\]
and form the sign-aligned multiscale perturbation
\[
 u_n(x)=A_n\sum_{j=1}^{J_n}h_j^{-1}\psi(x/h_j).
 \tag{28}
\]
Its \(j\)-th summand is supported on the boundary shell \(((5/4)h_j,(7/4)h_j)\).  Since \(h_{j+1}=h_j/2\), these shells are pairwise disjoint, separated by gaps, and contained in \([0,1]\) because \(h_1=1/2\).

Write \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\).  Dilation gives, for \(0\leq r\leq p\),
\[
 \left\|D^r\{A_nh_j^{-1}\psi(\cdot/h_j)\}\right\|_\infty
 \leq C_\psi A_nh_j^{-(r+1)},
 \qquad
 \left[D^p\{A_nh_j^{-1}\psi(\cdot/h_j)\}\right]_\beta
 \leq C_\psi A_nh_j^{-(s+1)}.
 \tag{29}
\]
Every summand and all its derivatives vanish at its support endpoints.  For two points in the same shell, (29) controls the H\"older quotient.  For points in different shells, insertion of the intervening zero endpoint and the fixed gap-to-shell ratio gives the same bound, up to a constant depending only on \(\psi\) and \(s\).  Because the supports are disjoint, the supremum norms are also controlled by the largest summand.  Therefore
\[
 \|u_n\|_{\mathcal H^s}
 \leq C_\psi A_n h_{J_n}^{-(s+1)}
 \leq \frac{C_\psi\delta}{\sqrt{J_n}},
 \tag{30}
\]
where the final inequality uses \(2^{J_n(s+1)}\leq n^{1/2}\).  Thus, after choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small, the response functions
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm u_n(x)
 \tag{31}
\]
satisfy the outcome support, H\"older-radius, and residual-variance conditions uniformly in \(n\).

At \(a=1\), equation (17) is \(f_S(x)=2x\).  Disjointness of the shells and the substitution \(x=h_ju\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 n\int_0^1u_n(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx
 &=2nA_n^2\sum_{j=1}^{J_n}
   \int h_j^{-2}\psi(x/h_j)^2x\,dx\\
 &=2nA_n^2J_n\int_{5/4}^{7/4}\psi(u)^2u\,du
 \leq C_\psi\delta^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{32}
\]
Using the Bernoulli inequality (19), the KL divergence of the two product-source laws is at most \(C\delta^2\).  Each shell has the same target integral, so
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 &=2A_nJ_n\int_{5/4}^{7/4}\psi(u)\,du\\
 &\geq c_3\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}},
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{33}
\]
because \(J_n\asymp\log n\).  Reduce \(\delta\) once more and apply (14)--(16).  This proves
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(1,s,t)\geq c_4\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}
 \tag{34}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  The finitely many remaining positive \(n\) are covered, after decreasing \(c_4\), by the interior perturbation (18).  Equations (27)--(33) exhibit the critical witness explicitly: it is the equal two-point testing mixture on the positive and negative versions of a sign-aligned superposition of \(J_n\asymp\log n\) disjoint boundary shells.  Hence the logarithmic factor has no hidden polynomial loss.

Combining (22), (26), and (34) with \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rate}\) gives a constant \(c_S>0\), depending only on the fixed class parameters, such that
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\geq c_S\rho_n(a,s)
 \tag{35}
\]
for all positive \(n,m\).

Finally we prove the independent target-sample obstruction, including its positivity and boundary conditions.  Keep the source law fixed, put \(\ell(x)=x-1/2\), and use common response functions
\[
 \mu_0(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x),
 \tag{36}
\]
where \(\delta_0>0\) is fixed and sufficiently small for outcome support, H\"older smoothness, and residual nondegeneracy.  Define two target densities by
\[
 f_{T,\pm}(x)=1\pm u_m\ell(x),
 \qquad
 u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
 \tag{37}
\]
Because \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), both densities integrate to one.  The strict slack \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\), together with the boundedness and smoothness of \(\ell\), permits a fixed \(\delta_1>0\) such that (37) is positive, lies between the target-factor bounds, and belongs to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every \(m\geq1\).  Choose target potential outcomes with the conditional means in (36); then mean transportability holds and the observed source distributions coincide.

The lower density in (37) is uniformly bounded away from zero.  From \(\log z\leq z-1\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{T,+}^{\otimes m},P_{T,-}^{\otimes m})
 &\leq m\int_0^1
   \frac{\{f_{T,+}(x)-f_{T,-}(x)\}^2}{f_{T,-}(x)}\,dx\\
 &\leq C_3m u_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 \leq C_4\delta_1^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{38}
\]
Since \(\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx=1/12\), the target separation is
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 =\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{6}m^{-1/2}.
 \tag{39}
\]
Take \(\delta_1\) small enough that the divergence in (38) is a sufficiently small absolute constant.  Equations (14)--(16) then imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\geq c_Tm^{-1/2}
 \tag{40}
\]
uniformly in \(n\).

Both (35) and (40) hold for the same minimax risk.  Since \(\max\{u,v\}\geq(u+v)/2\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \geq \frac12\min\{c_S,c_T\}
 \{\rho_n(a,s)+m^{-1/2}\}.
\]
This is the claimed lower phase diagram. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The converse certifies that the three source regimes, including the critical logarithm, are structural features of the experiment rather than artifacts of a proposed estimator. \textit{Gap.} Irregular inverse-weight limit theory does not derive the present two-sample H\"older boundary modulus, and fixed-design ATE optimal recovery does not contain its independent target-sample experiment. \textit{Consumer.} Trial planners can use the lower rate to understand the precision cost of weak recruitment near a clinically important covariate boundary.

\begin{theorem}[thm:ricot-attainment]\label{thm:ricot-attainment}
Fix integers \(n,m\ge2\), a bandwidth \(h\in(0,1)\), and \(\gamma\in(0,1/2)\). For every \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\), the unchanged spliced estimator and the observable coefficients in \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\) satisfy the exact identity
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}=\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})Z_i
\]
and the exact error decomposition
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}-\tau
={}&\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\\
&+\left\{\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\mu(X_i)-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)\right\}\\
&+\left\{\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The middle term is bounded in absolute value by
\[
B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\le\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}),
\]
where \(\overline B_X\) is the finite certified jet-LP radius in \(\mathrm{def:bias\text{-}envelope}\). Consequently,
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{\left|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}-\tau\right|
\le \overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})+R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})+R_T(m)\right\}\ge1-\gamma.
\]
For any realized covariate design with \(\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\le b\) and \(\sum_i\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})^2\le g^2\), the displayed certificate radius is at most
\[
b+\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)}\,g+\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)/m}.
\]
This is a finite-sample execution certificate. It makes no stochastic-order, localization, leverage, minimax-attainment, or expected-length claim for \(B_X\), \(\overline B_X\), or the coefficient norm.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Let
\[
\mathcal C=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_n,X_1^T,\ldots,X_m^T)
\]
be the sigma-field generated by all observed covariates.  We first prove the exact
linear identity.  By \(\mathrm{def:regression\text{-}fit}\), the positive ridge in
the boundary Gram matrix gives
\[
\widehat\alpha_{k,h}
=\widehat G_{k,h}^{-1}\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_k^S|}
\sum_{q_0\in\mathcal T_k^S}\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\le 2h\}
\mathbf R_h(X_{q_0})Z_{q_0}.
\tag{1}
\]
The first-order condition for the strictly convex interior ridge criterion likewise
gives
\[
\widehat\beta_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}
=\widehat H_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}^{-1}\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_k^S|}
\sum_{q_0\in\mathcal T_k^S}\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\ge h\}
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_{q_0})Z_{q_0}.
\tag{2}
\]
Both inverses exist because the defining matrices contain the positive ridge
\(n^{-2}I\).  Substituting (1)--(2) into the two branches of the spliced regression
fit and applying \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\) yields,
for every fold \(k\) and every \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\widehat\mu^{(-k)}_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)
=\sum_{q_0=1}^nL_{q_0,k,h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)Z_{q_0}.
\tag{3}
\]
Insert (3) into every fitted-regression occurrence in
\(\mathrm{def:spliced\text{-}estimator}\) and collect the coefficient of a fixed
\(Z_{q_0}\).  That coefficient is
\[
\begin{aligned}
{}&\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m
L_{q_0,k_T(j),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_j^T)
+\frac1n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(q_0))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_{q_0})
\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\ge h\}\\
&\quad-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)
\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}L_{q_0,k_S(i),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_i),
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
which is exactly \(\Gamma_{q_0}(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\).  Hence
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}
=\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})Z_i.
\tag{5}
\]
The ridge matrices, the branch-qualified transport programs, the selected
multipliers, the KKT continuation, the cap, and the empty-interior fallback are all
functions only of the observed covariates.  Their measurability is part of the
corresponding constructions.  Thus every coefficient \(\Gamma_i\) in (5) is
\(\mathcal C\)-measurable.

We next compute the conditional mean of the pseudo-outcome.  By
\(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(\mathrm{ass:trial\text{-}randomization}\), and
\(\mathrm{ass:known\text{-}propensity}\), for \(P_S\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P_S}(Z\mid X=x)
&=\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}(AY\mid X=x)}{e(x)}
-\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}\{(1-A)Y\mid X=x\}}{1-e(x)}\\
&=\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(1)\mid X=x\}
-\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(0)\mid X=x\}\\
&=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)=\mu(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The divisions in (6) are valid because
\(\mathrm{ass:treatment\text{-}positivity}\) gives
\(\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\varepsilon\).  Starting from (5), add and subtract
\(\sum_i\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)\) and
\(m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)\).  The definition
\(\tau=\mathbb E_{P_T}\{\mu(X^T)\}\) then gives the claimed exact decomposition.

It remains to bound its three terms.  By
\(\mathrm{ass:control\text{-}response\text{-}smoothness}\),
\(\mathrm{ass:treated\text{-}response\text{-}smoothness}\), and subadditivity of
the H\"older norm,
\[
\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M).
\]
Since \(0\le\mu_d\le1\), also \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\).  Therefore \(\mu\) is
admissible in the supremum defining \(B_X\), and
\[
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)
-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)\right|
\le B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})
\le\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}),
\tag{7}
\]
where the second inequality is
\(\mathrm{lem:jet\text{-}lp\text{-}envelope}\).

The product experiment in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) makes the source
observations conditionally independent given \(\mathcal C\).  Equation (6) centers
their pseudo-outcomes conditionally.  Moreover, \(0\le Y\le1\) and treatment
positivity imply
\[
-\varepsilon^{-1}\le Z_i\le\varepsilon^{-1}.
\]
Conditional on \(\mathcal C\), the summand
\(\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\) consequently has range length at most
\(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\).  The conditional form of
\(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\), with
\(\delta=\gamma/2\), gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right|
>R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\,\middle|\,\mathcal C\right\}
\le\frac\gamma2
\quad\text{almost surely},
\tag{8}
\]
because the sum of squared conditional range lengths is
\(4\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\).  Integrating (8) preserves the same
unconditional probability bound.

Independently, the target observations are identically distributed,
\(\mathbb E_{P_T}\mu(X^T)=\tau\), and \(\mu(X^T)\in[-1,1]\).  Thus the centered
summands \(m^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\) have range length \(2/m\).  A second
application of \(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\), again with
\(\delta=\gamma/2\), yields
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|>R_T(m)
\right\}\le\frac\gamma2,
\tag{9}
\]

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-frontier]\label{thm:sharp-frontier}
For fixed \(a,s,t,M\) with \(M\ge a+1\) and \(t>0\), along every sequence with \(m/n\to\kappa\), there is \(c>0\) such that
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\left\{m^{-1/2}+\begin{cases}
n^{-1/2},&a<1,\\
\sqrt{\log n/n},&a=1,\\
n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)},&a>1.
\end{cases}\right\}.
\]
This lower phase diagram requires no strict slack in the target-density bounds. Moreover, writing \(P=(P_S,P_T)\) for the pair of one-observation laws associated with \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\), uniformly over \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\),
\[
|\mathfrak b_{h_n}(\mu;P)|\le b_{h_n}^{\circ}(P)\le C h_n^{s+1}
\]
and
\[
m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}
+n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}
\asymp m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s).
\]
For every fixed \(a>0\), the deterministic allowance is class-sharp, although the actual bias may vanish for special laws. Thus the proved minimax lower frontier matches the boundary-corrected oracle allowance and linear-weight benchmark. The benchmark is a population oracle calculation; no data-measurable minimax upper bound or frontier-attainment conclusion is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\mathrm{lem:source\text{-}only\text{-}frontier\text{-}lower}\), for the fixed model constants there exist \(c_0>0\) and \(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\) and every \(m\),
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_0\rho_n(a,s).
\tag{1}
\]
No strict target-density slack is used in (1): the subexperiments proving that lemma have the common target density \(f_T=1\), so \(g_T=1\), and the declared ranges \(c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+}\) permit either or both target-density bounds to be attained.

By \(\mathrm{ass:sample\text{-}balance}\), \(m/n\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\). Therefore there are constants \(c_\kappa,C_\kappa>0\) and \(n_1\) such that
\[
c_\kappa n\le m\le C_\kappa n,
\qquad n\ge n_1.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular,
\[
m^{-1/2}\le c_\kappa^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(a<1\), \(\rho_n(a,s)=n^{-1/2}\). For \(a=1\),
\[
\rho_n(1,s)=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\ge n^{-1/2}
\tag{4}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). For \(a>1\), the conditions \(a>1\) and \(s>0\) give
\[
\frac{s+1}{2s+a+1}<\frac12,
\qquad
\rho_n(a,s)=n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}\ge n^{-1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (3)--(5) imply that, for some finite constant \(C_0\),
\[
m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\le C_0\rho_n(a,s)
\tag{6}
\]
eventually along the sequence. Combining (1) and (6) yields
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
\ge \frac{c_0}{C_0}\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Expanding the three branches of \(\rho_n(a,s)\) in (7) proves the displayed lower phase diagram.

It remains to verify the benchmark assertions. The corrected
\(\mathrm{lem:oracle\text{-}truncation\text{-}envelope}\) gives, uniformly over the model class,
\[
|\mathfrak b_{h_n}(\mu;P)|\le b_{h_n}^{\circ}(P)\le C h_n^{s+1}
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}
+n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}
\asymp m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s).
\tag{9}
\]
That lemma also proves that the deterministic allowance in (8) is class-sharp for every fixed \(a>0\), while giving the special equal-density case at \(a=0\) in which the actual bias and its allowance both vanish. Thus neither (8) nor (9) is a per-law lower equivalence for actual bias.

Finally, \(G_{h_n}^{\circ}(P)\), \(q_{h_n}^{\circ}(P)\), and hence \(\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\) depend on the population law. The benchmark is therefore an oracle population calculation, not a map measurable with respect to \(\mathcal D_{n,m}\). Equations (7)--(9) establish a one-sided minimax lower phase diagram and a boundary-corrected oracle linear-weight benchmark, but no data-driven minimax upper bound or frontier-attainment conclusion. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem states the maximal proved one-sided minimax lower phase diagram and pairs it with the boundary-corrected population local-polynomial benchmark. \textit{Gap.} The oracle benchmark is not a data-measurable minimax upper bound, and Armstrong--Kolesár's one-sample fixed-design ATE optimal-recovery theorem is non-nested with this two-sample polynomial-depletion experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The result supplies a precision impossibility and a population oracle benchmark for transported cardiomyopathy effects near a depleted trial boundary.

\begin{theorem}[thm:nonadaptation]\label{thm:nonadaptation}
Fix \(a>1\), \(t>0\), and \(0<s_1<s_2\), and suppose \(M\ge a+1\) and \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\). Any confidence interval with asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\gamma\) uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t)\cup\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\) has, at some law in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\), expected length at least \(c\{m^{-1/2}+n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\mathcal C_{n,m}\) be an interval with the asserted uniform asymptotic coverage. Thus there is a deterministic sequence \(r_{n,m}\to0\) along the indexing sequence such that, for all sufficiently large indices, every law in the displayed union has coverage at least \(1-\gamma-r_{n,m}\).

We first derive the interval version of the two-point inequality. Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) have parameter values separated by \(\Delta>0\). An interval containing both values has length at least \(\Delta\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
&\ge\Delta Q_0\{\tau(Q_0),\tau(Q_1)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}\\
&\ge\Delta\bigl[Q_0\{\tau(Q_0)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}
+Q_1\{\tau(Q_1)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}
-1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\bigr]\\
&\ge\Delta\{1-2\gamma-2r_{n,m}-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
The second inequality uses \(Q_0(B)\ge Q_1(B)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\). The log-sum and binary-entropy calculation (15)--(16) gives
\[
\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\le\sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)/2}.
\tag{36}
\]
Hence a sufficiently small constant relative entropy makes the final factor in (35) bounded below by a positive constant because \(\gamma<1/2\).

We construct the two comparisons around one smoother reference law. Put
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1,
\qquad
\ell(x)=x-\frac12.
\tag{37}
\]
Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\). Use randomized Bernoulli potential outcomes with
\[
\mu_0(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x),
\tag{38}
\]
make treatment conditionally independent of the potential outcomes given \(X\), impose consistency, and give target potential outcomes the same conditional means. The constant density factors in (37) belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every fixed \(t>0\). Since a linear function is in every fixed-order H\"older class, reducing \(\delta_0\) puts (38) strictly inside both radius-\(M\) response balls, keeps the means in \([0,1]\), and makes the Bernoulli residual variances at least \(M^{-1}\). Denote the resulting observed law by \(Q_0\). Then
\[
Q_0\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)
\cap\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t).
\tag{39}
\]

Let
\[
h=n^{-1/(2s_1+a+1)},
\qquad
b_{h,s_1}(x)=h^{s_1}K(x/h)\mathbf 1\{0\le x\le h\}.
\tag{40}
\]
Construct \(Q_1\) by changing only the treated source and target conditional mean to
\[

\tag{41}
\]
The scaling calculation (4), now at order \(s_1\), and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\) put \(Q_1\) in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t)\). \[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
\le Cn\delta^2h^{2s_1+a+1}

\tag{42}
\]
whereas
\[
\Delta_S:=|\tau(Q_1)-\tau(Q_0)|
=\delta h^{s_1+1}\int_0^1K(u)\,du
\ge c_Sn^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\tag{43}
\]
Reduce \(\delta\) until (36) makes \(\operatorname{TV}(Q_1,Q_0)\) smaller than a fixed number strictly below \(1-2\gamma\). Equations (35), (42), and (43) then imply
\[

f_{T,2}(x)=1+u_m\ell(x),
\qquad
u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.

\]
The equality \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), strict target-density slack, and polynomial smoothness make (45) admissible for every fixed \(t>0\) after choosing a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta_1>0\). The source laws under \(Q_2\) and \(Q_0\) are identical, and the reference target density is one, so

\operatorname{KL}(Q_2,Q_0)
\le m u_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx

\tag{48}
\]

\]
Thus honest adaptation across the two H\"older classes forces the rough-class boundary rate at a smoother law. No numerical lower bound on \(t\), product-smoothness condition, or sample-balance assumption was used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:bounded-sum-hoeffding]\label{lem:bounded-sum-hoeffding}
Let \(W_1,\ldots,W_N\) be independent real random variables with \(\mathbb E W_i=0\) and \(W_i\in[\alpha_i,\beta_i]\) almost surely. For every \(\delta\in(0,1)\),
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{\left|\sum_{i=1}^N W_i\right|>
\sqrt{\frac12\log(2/\delta)\sum_{i=1}^N(\beta_i-\alpha_i)^2}\right\}\le\delta.
\]
The same statement holds conditionally on a sigma-field when conditional independence, conditional centering, and the displayed conditional bounds hold almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
First consider a centered random variable \(W\in[\alpha,\beta]\) and put \(d=\beta-\alpha\). Convexity of the exponential gives
\[
e^{\lambda W}\le\frac{\beta-W}{d}e^{\lambda\alpha}+\frac{W-\alpha}{d}e^{\lambda\beta}.
\]
Taking expectations and using \(\mathbb EW=0\), the logarithm of the right-hand side is the cumulant generating function of a two-point variable with range \(\lambda d\), mean zero, value zero and first derivative zero at \(\lambda=0\). Its second derivative is a Bernoulli variance and is at most \(d^2/4\). Twice integrating this second-derivative bound yields
\[
\mathbb E e^{\lambda W}\le e^{\lambda^2d^2/8}.
\tag{1}
\]
For independent \(W_i\), (1) and multiplication of moment generating functions give, with \(D=\sum_i(\beta_i-\alpha_i)^2\),
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{\sum_iW_i\ge x\right\}
\le\inf_{\lambda>0}\exp\{-\lambda x+\lambda^2D/8\}
=\exp\{-2x^2/D\}.
\]
Applying the same argument to \(-W_i\) and adding the two tail probabilities gives \(2e^{-2x^2/D}\). Substitution of \(x^2=D\log(2/\delta)/2\) proves the assertion. The conditional version repeats the same moment-generating-function calculation with conditional expectations; all inequalities hold almost surely before the final expectation is taken. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:source-only-frontier-lower]\label{lem:source-only-frontier-lower}
For fixed \(a,s,t,M,\varepsilon,c_{S,-},c_{S,+},c_{T,-},c_{T,+}\) with \(t>0\) and \(M\ge a+1\), there is \(c>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(m\),
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\rho_n(a,s).
\]
No strict inequality in the target-density bounds is required. At \(a=1\), the source lower bound is \(c\sqrt{\log n/n}\) and is generated by a multiscale sign mixture whose target covariate law is common to all mixture components.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
We construct explicit subexperiments in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) for every fixed \(t>0\). First we record the testing inequality used below. Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) have parameter values \(\tau_0,\tau_1\), and write \(d=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\). Classifying an observation according to the nearer parameter value shows that, for every estimator \(\widehat\tau\),
\[
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}
       [ (\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2 ]
 \ge \frac{d^2}{8}
       \{1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, a classification error under \(Q_u\) forces \(|\widehat\tau-\tau_u|\ge d/2\), and the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\). We shall also use
\[
 \lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}^2
 \le \frac12\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1).
\tag{2}
\]
For completeness, if a Hahn set for \(Q_0-Q_1\) has probabilities \(p,q\), the log-sum inequality bounds the relative entropy below by the binary relative entropy. For fixed \(q\), the latter minus \(2(p-q)^2\) vanishes with zero first derivative at \(p=q\) and has second derivative \(1/\{p(1-p)\}-4\ge0\), which proves (2). Thus constant relative entropy and nonvanishing parameter separation imply the corresponding root-risk lower bound.

In every regime set
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{3}
\]
These are densities and have factors \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\). By the declared ranges of the density constants and \(M\ge a+1\),
\[
 c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
 \qquad c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+}.
\tag{4}
\]
Both factors are constant and hence belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every \(t>0\). This is the point at which the two removed nuisance-rate conditions are seen to be unnecessary. It also shows that the construction is admissible when either target-density bound is attained.

Given \(X=x\), draw \(A\) with the fixed known probability \(e(x)\). Conditional on \(X\), take the two source potential outcomes to be independent Bernoulli variables with the arm-specific means specified below and independent of \(A\); take target potential outcomes with the same conditional means; and set \(Y=Y^S(A)\). These choices verify \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(\mathrm{ass:trial\text{-}randomization}\), \(\mathrm{ass:known\text{-}propensity}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}transportability}\). The fixed propensity obeys \(\mathrm{ass:treatment\text{-}positivity}\). Because \(M>4\), put
\[
 \Delta_M=\sqrt{\frac14-\frac1M}>0.
\]
Choose every perturbation constant below so that each conditional mean differs from \(1/2\) by at most \(\Delta_M/2\). Then the outcomes lie in \([0,1]\), and in either arm
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)
 \ge \frac14-\frac{\Delta_M^2}{4}
 >\frac1M,
\tag{5}
\]
so \(\mathrm{ass:residual\text{-}nondegeneracy}\) holds. The control regression is constant, and the calculations below place every treated regression in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\). The target covariate distribution in (3) is common to all alternatives, so the \(m\) target observations contribute zero divergence for every \(m\).

\emph{The nonregular regime \(a>1\).}
For \(h\in(0,1)\) and fixed sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), use \(b_h\) from \(\mathrm{def:boundary\text{-}bump}\) and set
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\). Dilation gives, for \(0\le v\le p\),
\[
 \lVert D^v b_h\rVert_\infty\le C h^{s-v},
 \qquad [D^p b_h]_{\beta}\le C,
\tag{7}
\]
where \(C\) depends only on the fixed bump \(K\). Reducing \(\delta\) therefore places both regressions in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), uniformly in \(h\).

For \(|u|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, direct calculation and Taylor's theorem give
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
  \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)
 \right)
 =2u\log\frac{1+2u}{1-2u}
 \le C_0u^2.
\tag{8}
\]
The control-arm laws agree, and the treated-arm contribution is multiplied by \(e(x)\le1\). Additivity of relative entropy, (3), and (8) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,\otimes n},P_{-,h}^{S,\otimes n})
 &\le Cn\int_0^h b_h(x)^2x^a\,dx\\
 &=Cnh^{2s+a+1}\int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero,
\[
 k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0,
 \qquad
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{10}
\]
Choose \(h=c_hn^{-1/(2s+a+1)}\), with \(c_h>0\) sufficiently small that (9) is bounded by a small constant. Equations (1), (2), and (10) yield
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge c n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\tag{11}
\]

\emph{The regular regime \(a<1\).}
Choose a nonnegative, nonzero \(C^\infty\) function \(\varphi\) supported in \([1/4,3/4]\), and set
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}\varphi(x).
\tag{12}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the range, variance, and H\"older restrictions hold. On the support of \(\varphi\), the source density is bounded above and below by positive constants. Thus (8) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_+^{S,\otimes n},P_-^{S,\otimes n})
 \le C\delta^2,
\tag{13}
\]
while the uniform target density gives
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1\varphi(x)\,dx
 \ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{14}
\]
After reducing \(\delta\), (1), (2), (13), and (14) prove
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{15}
\]

\emph{The critical regime \(a=1\).}
Let \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), fix an integer \(j_0\ge1\), and choose \(L=L_n\) so that
\[
 \ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}},
 \qquad L-j_0+1\asymp\log n.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(j_0\le j\le L\), define
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\},
\tag{17}
\]
and, for \(\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{L-j_0+1}\), set
\[
 \mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+
 \sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x).
\tag{18}
\]
The shell interiors are disjoint. Equation (16) implies
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}\le C\ell_j^s
 \qquad(j_0\le j\le L).
\tag{19}
\]
Consequently, for \(0\le v\le p\),
\[
 \lVert D^v\phi_j\rVert_\infty
 \le C\delta n^{-1/2}\ell_j^{-(v+1)}
 \le C\delta\ell_j^{s-v},
 \qquad
 [D^p\phi_j]_{\beta}
 \le C\delta n^{-1/2}\ell_j^{-(s+1)}
 \le C\delta.
\tag{20}
\]
The zero extension of the smooth bump and all its derivatives vanish at shell endpoints. The local bounds in (20), applied through a common endpoint for adjacent shells and with \(|x-y|\) comparable to the larger shell scale for nonadjacent shells, show that the sum in (18) has global order-\(s\) H\"older norm at most \(C'\delta\), independently of \(L\). Taking \(\delta\) small therefore verifies both response-smoothness conditions and (5).

The contribution of coordinate \(j\) to the target parameter is
\[
 d_j:=\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)\,dx
 =\frac{\delta k_1}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(\omega^{(j)}\) flips only coordinate \(j\), then \(f_S(x)=2x\), so (8) yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{S,\otimes n},
                    P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{S,\otimes n})
 \le Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
\tag{22}
\]
uniformly in \(j,n\), and the other signs.

Place the uniform product prior \(\Pi\) on \(\omega\). Conditional on all covariates and treatment assignments, each source likelihood contribution involves at most one shell coordinate. The likelihood and posterior therefore factor across coordinates; the target observations are sign-free. Since conditional expectation minimizes squared loss and \(\tau_\omega=\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j\omega_j\),
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}
 \mathbb E_\Pi[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
   \mathbb E_\Pi[\operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})].
\tag{23}
\]
Writing \(q_j=\mathbb E_\Pi[\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}]\), one has
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|.
\tag{24}
\]
Let \(\overline P_{j,+}\) and \(\overline P_{j,-}\) be the mixture laws conditional on \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\). Bayes' formula gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi|q_j|
 =\lVert\overline P_{j,+}-\overline P_{j,-}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\tag{25}
\]
Pairing every sign vector with its \(j\)-flip and applying the pointwise log-sum inequality followed by (22) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\overline P_{j,+},\overline P_{j,-})
 \le 2^{-(L-j_0)}
 \sum_{\omega:\,\omega_j=1}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega,P_{\omega^{(j)}})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{26}
\]
The target factors cancel because their law is common. Equations (2) and (24)--(26), with sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\), imply
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi[\operatorname{Var}
   (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})]
 \ge c_1>0
\tag{27}
\]
uniformly in \(j\). Therefore (16), (21), and (23) yield
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 \ge c_1\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
 \ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\tag{28}
\]
Taking square roots proves the critical bound without hidden polynomial loss.

Equations (11), (15), and (28), together with \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rate}\), prove that for every fixed \(t>0\), all sufficiently large \(n\), and every \(m\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\rho_n(a,s).
\]
Every constructed observed law belongs to the broadened \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), and every construction uses the boundary-attaining density \(f_T=1\). This proves the no-slack assertion. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The source-only converse supplies all three overlap regimes for every fixed \(t>0\), including the critical multiscale logarithm without target-density slack. \textit{Gap.} A single boundary bump gives the nonregular branch but cannot yield the critical logarithm; the multiscale sign mixture closes that gap. \textit{Consumer.} It is the maximal source-side lower-bound input for the lower phase-diagram theorems.

\begin{lemma}[lem:jet-lp-envelope]\label{lem:jet-lp-envelope}
Under the convention and notation of \(\mathrm{def:bias\text{-}envelope}\), every trace \(u_{\ell,r}=v^{(r)}(z_\ell)\) generated by \(v\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\) with \(\|v\|_\infty\le1\) is feasible for both signed jet linear programs. Consequently,
\[
B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\le\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}).
\]
The programs have \(N(p+1)\) variables and \(O\{N^2(p+1)\}\) linear inequalities. If \(0<s\le1\), every feasible zeroth-order jet has an admissible H\"older--McShane extension, so equality holds. For \(s>1\), only the displayed certified upper bound is claimed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(v\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\) with \(\|v\|_\infty\le1\), and put \(u_{\ell,r}=v^{(r)}(z_\ell)\). The box restrictions follow immediately from the defining derivative bounds. For \(r=p\), the H\"older condition gives
\[
|u_{\ell,p}-u_{k,p}|\le2M|z_\ell-z_k|^\beta
=2M\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s-p+1)}|z_\ell-z_k|^{s-p}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(0\le r<p\), repeated use of the fundamental theorem of calculus gives the Taylor formula with integral remainder
\[
v^{(r)}(x)-\sum_{q=r}^p v^{(q)}(y)\frac{(x-y)^{q-r}}{(q-r)!}
=\frac1{(p-r-1)!}\int_y^x(x-z)^{p-r-1}\{v^{(p)}(z)-v^{(p)}(y)\}\,dz,
\tag{2}
\]
with the oriented-integral interpretation when \(x<y\). Bounding the integrand by \(2M|z-y|^\beta\) and evaluating the beta integral yields
\[
\left|v^{(r)}(x)-\sum_{q=r}^p v^{(q)}(y)\frac{(x-y)^{q-r}}{(q-r)!}\right|
\le2M\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(p-r+\beta+1)}|x-y|^{p-r+\beta}.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(p+\beta=s\), (3) is precisely the jet constraint in the definition. Equations (1)--(3) prove feasibility for every admissible trace.

For such a trace,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i v(X_i)-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mv(X_j^T)
=\sum_{\ell=1}^Nc_\ell u_{\ell,0}.
\tag{4}
\]
Taking the supremum of the two signs in (4) over admissible functions, and then enlarging from admissible traces to the jet polytope, proves \(B_X\le\overline B_X\). There are \(N(p+1)\) variables. Splitting each absolute-value restriction into two one-sided inequalities gives \(O\{N^2(p+1)\}\) inequalities, and the box constraints make both objective values finite.

It remains to verify the exact low-smoothness statement. If \(0<s\le1\), then \(p=0\), \(\beta=s\), and feasibility is exactly
\[
|u_{\ell,0}|\le1,
\qquad
|u_{\ell,0}-u_{k,0}|\le2M|z_\ell-z_k|^s.
\tag{5}
\]
The function \(d_s(x,y)=|x-y|^s\) is a metric on \([0,1]\). The McShane formula
\[
\widetilde v(x)=\inf_{1\le\ell\le N}\{u_{\ell,0}+2M d_s(x,z_\ell)\}
\tag{6}
\]
extends the finite trace and has H\"older seminorm at most \(2M\). Projection of \(\widetilde v\) onto \([-1,1]\) preserves both its values at the sites and its H\"older constant. Thus every feasible jet in (5) is the trace of an admissible function. The relaxation is therefore exact for \(0<s\le1\). No extension assertion is made for the higher-order jet polytope. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:oracle-truncation-envelope]\label{lem:oracle-truncation-envelope}
For fixed \(a,s,t,M\) with \(M\ge a+1\) and \(t>0\), let \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\), write \(P=(P_S,P_T)\) for its pair of one-observation laws, and let \(h\in(0,1)\). The matrix \(G_h^{\circ}(P)\) is positive definite, and for every polynomial \(\ell\) of degree at most \(p\),
\[
\int_0^h w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)\ell(x)f_S(x)\,dx
=\int_0^h\ell(x)f_T(x)\,dx.
\]
Uniformly over the model class,
\[
\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{\top}\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)\asymp h^{1-a},
\qquad
|\mathfrak b_h(\mu;P)|\le b_h^{\circ}(P)\le C h^{s+1},
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\omega_h^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2
&=\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{\top}\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)
+\int_h^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\\
&\asymp
\begin{cases}
1,&a<1,\\
\log(1/h),&a=1,\\
h^{1-a},&a>1.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]
For every fixed \(a>0\), the allowance is class-sharp in the sense that, for an admissible member law and all sufficiently small \(h\), \(b_h^{\circ}(P)\ge c h^{s+1}\). At \(a=0\), if \(f_S=f_T\) on \([0,h]\), both \(\mathfrak b_h(\mu;P)\) and \(b_h^{\circ}(P)\) may vanish. Consequently, for the deterministic regime bandwidth \(h_n\),
\[
m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}+n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}
\asymp m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s).
\]
This is a population boundary-local-polynomial linear-weight benchmark. It is not a data-measurable estimator and does not establish a minimax upper bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a member law \(P\) and \(h\in(0,1)\). Put
\[
\mathbf R_1(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^p)^\top,
\qquad
A_a=\int_0^1\mathbf R_1(u)\mathbf R_1(u)^\top u^a\,du.
\tag{1}
\]
For every nonzero \(z\in\mathbb R^{p+1}\), the polynomial
\(u\mapsto z^\top\mathbf R_1(u)\) is nonzero except at finitely many points. Since
\(u^a>0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(u\in(0,1)\),
\[
z^\top A_a z=\int_0^1\{z^\top\mathbf R_1(u)\}^2u^a\,du>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \(A_a\) is positive definite. The change of variables \(x=hu\), the identity
\(f_S(x)=x^ag_S(x)\), and \(\mathrm{ass:source\text{-}boundary\text{-}factor}\) give the Loewner bounds
\[
c_{S,-}h^{a+1}A_a
\preceq G_h^{\circ}(P)
\preceq c_{S,+}h^{a+1}A_a.
\tag{3}
\]
All eigenvalues of \(G_h^{\circ}(P)\) are therefore positive, so its inverse in the oracle definition exists. This also records explicitly the rank and support condition used below: every nonzero polynomial coordinate has positive squared mass on the positive-length interval \((0,h)\).

Let \(\ell(x)=c^\top\mathbf R_h(x)\) be an arbitrary polynomial of degree at most \(p\). By the definitions of \(G_h^{\circ}(P)\), \(q_h^{\circ}(P)\), and \(w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(\cdot;P)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^h w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)\ell(x)f_S(x)\,dx
&=\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}
  \left\{\int_0^h\mathbf R_h(x)\mathbf R_h(x)^\top f_S(x)\,dx\right\}c\\
&=\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top c
=\int_0^h\ell(x)f_T(x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
This proves exact polynomial reproduction.

We next establish the size of the boundary weight. The target-factor bounds in \(\mathrm{ass:target\text{-}boundary\text{-}factor}\) imply
\[
\|q_h^{\circ}(P)\|_2\le C_1h,
\qquad
e_0^\top q_h^{\circ}(P)=\int_0^hf_T(x)\,dx\ge c_{T,-}h,
\tag{5}
\]
where \(e_0=(1,0,\ldots,0)^\top\). The lower eigenvalue bound in (3) and the first inequality in (5) yield
\[
\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)\le C_2h^{1-a}.
\tag{6}
\]
Conversely, Cauchy--Schwarz in the inner product induced by \(G_h^{\circ}(P)\) gives
\[
\{e_0^\top q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^2
\le\{e_0^\top G_h^{\circ}(P)e_0\}
\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P).
\tag{7}
\]
Because
\[
e_0^\top G_h^{\circ}(P)e_0=\int_0^hf_S(x)\,dx
\le \frac{c_{S,+}}{a+1}h^{a+1},
\tag{8}
\]
equations (5), (7), and (8) imply the matching lower bound
\[
\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)\ge C_3h^{1-a}.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus the first asserted equivalence holds uniformly over the class. All divisions in (8) are valid because \(a\ge0\).

Direct multiplication and the definition of \(G_h^{\circ}(P)\) show that
\[
\int_0^h\{w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)\}^2f_S(x)\,dx
=\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P).
\tag{10}
\]
Using (8)--(10) and ordinary Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\int_0^h|w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)|f_S(x)\,dx
\le
\left[\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)\right]^{1/2}
\left\{\int_0^hf_S(x)\,dx\right\}^{1/2}
\le C_4h.
\tag{11}
\]
Also \(\int_0^hf_T(x)\,dx\le c_{T,+}h\).

Let \(v\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\) with \(\|v\|_\infty\le1\), and let
\[
T_pv(x)=\sum_{r=0}^p\frac{v^{(r)}(0)}{r!}x^r.
\tag{12}
\]
The stated H\"older convention and Taylor's formula with a \(\beta\)-H\"older \(p\)-th derivative give
\[
|v(x)-T_pv(x)|\le C_{s,M}x^s,
\qquad 0\le x\le h.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(p=0\), (13) is exactly the defining \(s\)-H\"older inequality; for \(p\ge1\), it follows by integrating the order-\(p\) remainder, so it also covers the boundary case \(\beta=1\). Since \(T_pv\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(p\), reproduction (4) cancels its contribution. Equations (11)--(13) therefore imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathfrak b_h(v;P)|
&=\left|\int_0^h\{w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)f_S(x)-f_T(x)\}\{v(x)-T_pv(x)\}\,dx\right|\\
&\le C_{s,M}h^s
\left\{\int_0^h|w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)|f_S(x)\,dx+\int_0^hf_T(x)\,dx\right\}
\le C_5h^{s+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Taking the defining supremum over \(v\) proves \(b_h^{\circ}(P)\le C_5h^{s+1}\). The assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:control\text{-}response\text{-}smoothness}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:treated\text{-}response\text{-}smoothness}\) give \(\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\), and the outcome range gives \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\). Moreover, the definition of \(\omega_h^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\) and \(r=f_T/f_S\) yields the exact identity
\[
\int_0^1\omega_h^{\circ}(x;P)\mu(x)f_S(x)\,dx-
\int_0^1\mu(x)f_T(x)\,dx=\mathfrak b_h(\mu;P).
\tag{15}
\]
Hence the actual oracle bias satisfies \(|\mathfrak b_h(\mu;P)|\le b_h^{\circ}(P)\le C_5h^{s+1}\).

We now prove the asserted class sharpness for \(a>0\). Use the admissible density pair
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad f_T(x)=1.
\tag{16}
\]
Its factors are \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\), which obey the factor bounds by their declared ranges and belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) because \(M\ge a+1\). Complete this density pair to a member \(P\) of \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) by taking both source potential outcomes, conditionally on \(X\), to be independent Bernoulli variables of mean \(1/2\), independent of the randomized treatment, and taking target potential outcomes with the same conditional means. Consistency supplies the observed outcome. The response functions are constant and their conditional variances are \(1/4>M^{-1}\) because \(M>4\); hence all identification, positivity, smoothness, boundedness, and residual-nondegeneracy member conditions hold. This verifies membership rather than assuming it.

Under (16), rescaling shows that
\[
w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(hu;P)=h^{-a}L_a(u),
\qquad
L_a(u)=\sum_{j=0}^p\lambda_ju^j,
\tag{17}
\]
where reproduction is equivalent to
\[
(a+1)\sum_{j=0}^p\frac{\lambda_j}{a+k+j+1}=\frac1{k+1},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,p.
\tag{18}
\]
Take \(v(x)=\delta x^s\), where the fixed \(\delta>0\) is small enough that
\(v\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\) and \(\|v\|_\infty\le1\). Equations (16)--(18) give
\[
\mathfrak b_h(v;P)=\delta h^{s+1}\Delta_{a,s},
\qquad
\Delta_{a,s}=(a+1)\sum_{j=0}^p\frac{\lambda_j}{a+s+j+1}-\frac1{s+1}.
\tag{19}
\]
We claim \(\Delta_{a,s}\ne0\) whenever \(a>0\). If it were zero, the equations in (18) would also hold for \(k=s\). Hence the last column would be a linear combination of the first \(p+1\) columns in the matrix with rows indexed by \(x_i\in\{0,1,\ldots,p,s\}\) and columns indexed by
\[
y_j\in\{a+1,a+2,\ldots,a+p+1,1\},
\]
whose entries, up to nonzero column scalings, are \((x_i+y_j)^{-1}\). But the algebraic Cauchy determinant identity
\[
\det\left(\frac1{x_i+y_j}\right)_{i,j=0}^{p+1}
=
\frac{\prod_{i<k}(x_k-x_i)\prod_{j<\ell}(y_\ell-y_j)}
{\prod_{i,j}(x_i+y_j)}
\tag{20}
\]
is nonzero: \(s\in(p,p+1]\) makes the row nodes distinct, \(a>0\) makes the column nodes distinct, and all denominators are positive. This contradiction proves the claim. Therefore (19) implies
\[
b_h^{\circ}(P)\ge\delta|\Delta_{a,s}|h^{s+1},
\tag{21}
\]
which proves class sharpness for every \(a>0\). The endpoint qualification is necessary: if \(a=0\) and \(f_S=f_T\) on \([0,h]\), then \(q_h^{\circ}(P)=G_h^{\circ}(P)e_0\), so \(w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)=1\) on \([0,h)\) and every bias in the defining supremum is zero.

It remains to combine the boundary and interior pieces. Their supports are disjoint up to the Lebesgue-null point \(h\), so (10) and the definition of \(r\) give the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\omega_h^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2
&=\int_0^h\{w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)\}^2f_S(x)\,dx
  +\int_h^1r(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx\\
&=\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)
  +\int_h^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
The first term has order \(h^{1-a}\) by (6)--(9), while
\(\mathrm{lem:riesz\text{-}threshold}\) gives orders \(1\), \(\log(1/h)\), and
\(h^{1-a}\) for the second term when \(a<1\), \(a=1\), and \(a>1\), respectively. Thus
\[
\|\omega_h^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2\asymp
\begin{cases}
1,&a<1,\\
\log(1/h),&a=1,\\
h^{1-a},&a>1.
\end{cases}
\tag{23}
\]

Finally substitute the three branches of \(h_n\). If \(a<1\), then
\[
h_n^{s+1}=n^{-1/2},
\qquad n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}\asymp n^{-1/2}.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(a=1\), then
\[
h_n^{s+1}=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}},
\qquad
\log(1/h_n)=\frac{\log n-\log\log n}{2(s+1)}\asymp\log n,
\tag{25}
\]
and hence the source standard-deviation term is also of order
\(\sqrt{\log n/n}\). If \(a>1\), put \(D=2s+a+1\). Then
\[
h_n^{s+1}=n^{-(s+1)/D},
\tag{26}
\]
and (23) gives
\[
n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}
\asymp n^{-1/2}h_n^{(1-a)/2}
=n^{-1/2+(a-1)/(2D)}
=n^{-(s+1)/D}.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (14)--(15) and (24)--(27), together with
\(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rate}\), prove the actual-bias upper bound and the displayed deterministic allowance. Every inverse, division, and limiting comparison used above is justified by (2)--(3), the positive boundary-factor constants, and \(h>0\). The construction uses the law itself and therefore is oracle; none of these calculations defines a data-measurable estimator. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The corrected oracle carrier separates law-specific actual bias from its explicit class allowance, proves polynomial reproduction at the boundary, and derives the full boundary-corrected weight norm. \textit{Gap.} Raw truncation leaves an order-\(h\) boundary term; the corrected boundary polynomial leaves an order-\(h^{s+1}\) allowance, but the resulting object remains a population oracle. \textit{Consumer.} The sharp-frontier theorem uses this benchmark to compare the lower phase diagram with a bias--standard-deviation balance without treating it as a measurable estimator.

\section{Interpretation}

For a RiCOT cardiomyopathy transport analysis, the lower phase diagram quantifies how sparse trial enrollment near a clinically important boundary limits precision even when the target population contains patients there. The oracle benchmark shows the bias--standard-deviation order available after population boundary correction but is not an implementable estimator. The implemented interval is a realized-design diagnostic: analysts can report the computed jet-LP discrepancy and coefficient norm and identify which component makes the radius large. A short realized radius is certified for that dataset, but the theory does not say that typical radii have frontier order.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The critical source lower bound at \(a=1\) requires the multiscale sign-mixture witness; one boundary bump gives only parametric order. The corrected oracle benchmark reproduces boundary polynomials and removes the order-\(h\) raw-truncation bias, but it depends on the population densities and is not a data-measurable minimax upper bound. For the executable estimator, no theorem controls the held-out capped RiCOT continuation, \(\overline B_X\), or \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\) at frontier order. The jet LP is exact for \(0<s\le1\) and a conservative outer relaxation for \(s>1\). Executable self-similar adaptation remains a research question, with no self-similarity class or positive adaptive theorem retained in the formal core.

\section{Honest scope}

The proved and proposed fixed-class results concern a one-dimensional boundary, known randomized-treatment propensity bounded away from zero and one, bounded outcomes, fixed response-Hölder radii and density-factor bounds, and independent source and target samples. The rate theorem is a one-sided minimax lower phase diagram paired with a boundary-corrected population oracle benchmark, not a data-measurable same-class upper bound. The capped calibrated-transport construction is executable, and its interval uses a finite observable jet-LP bias radius plus bounded-outcome concentration radii. No stochastic held-out localization, leverage bound, Gaussian approximation, expected-length rate, empirical frontier attainment, or unrestricted adaptation claim is made.
