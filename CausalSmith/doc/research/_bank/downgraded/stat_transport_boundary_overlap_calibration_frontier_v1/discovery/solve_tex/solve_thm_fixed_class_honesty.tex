%%% FIELD proof-ricot-attainment
\textbf{Proof.}
Let
\[
\mathcal C=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_n,X_1^T,\ldots,X_m^T)
\]
be the sigma-field generated by all observed covariates.  We first prove the exact
linear identity.  By \(\mathrm{def:regression\text{-}fit}\), the positive ridge in
the boundary Gram matrix gives
\[
\widehat\alpha_{k,h}
=\widehat G_{k,h}^{-1}\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_k^S|}
\sum_{q_0\in\mathcal T_k^S}\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\le 2h\}
\mathbf R_h(X_{q_0})Z_{q_0}.
\tag{1}
\]
The first-order condition for the strictly convex interior ridge criterion likewise
gives
\[
\widehat\beta_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}
=\widehat H_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}^{-1}\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_k^S|}
\sum_{q_0\in\mathcal T_k^S}\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\ge h\}
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_{q_0})Z_{q_0}.
\tag{2}
\]
Both inverses exist because the defining matrices contain the positive ridge
\(n^{-2}I\).  Substituting (1)--(2) into the two branches of the spliced regression
fit and applying \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\) yields,
for every fold \(k\) and every \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\widehat\mu^{(-k)}_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)
=\sum_{q_0=1}^nL_{q_0,k,h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)Z_{q_0}.
\tag{3}
\]
Insert (3) into every fitted-regression occurrence in
\(\mathrm{def:spliced\text{-}estimator}\) and collect the coefficient of a fixed
\(Z_{q_0}\).  That coefficient is
\[
\begin{aligned}
{}&\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m
L_{q_0,k_T(j),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_j^T)
+\frac1n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(q_0))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_{q_0})
\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\ge h\}\\
&\quad-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)
\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}L_{q_0,k_S(i),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_i),
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
which is exactly \(\Gamma_{q_0}(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\).  Hence
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}
=\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})Z_i.
\tag{5}
\]
The ridge matrices, the branch-qualified transport programs, the selected
multipliers, the KKT continuation, the cap, and the empty-interior fallback are all
functions only of the observed covariates.  Their measurability is part of the
corresponding constructions.  Thus every coefficient \(\Gamma_i\) in (5) is
\(\mathcal C\)-measurable.

We next compute the conditional mean of the pseudo-outcome.  By
\(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(\mathrm{ass:trial\text{-}randomization}\), and
\(\mathrm{ass:known\text{-}propensity}\), for \(P_S\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P_S}(Z\mid X=x)
&=\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}(AY\mid X=x)}{e(x)}
-\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}\{(1-A)Y\mid X=x\}}{1-e(x)}\\
&=\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(1)\mid X=x\}
-\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(0)\mid X=x\}\\
&=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)=\mu(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The divisions in (6) are valid because
\(\mathrm{ass:treatment\text{-}positivity}\) gives
\(\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\varepsilon\).  Starting from (5), add and subtract
\(\sum_i\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)\) and
\(m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)\).  The definition
\(\tau=\mathbb E_{P_T}\{\mu(X^T)\}\) then gives the claimed exact decomposition.

It remains to bound its three terms.  By
\(\mathrm{ass:control\text{-}response\text{-}smoothness}\),
\(\mathrm{ass:treated\text{-}response\text{-}smoothness}\), and subadditivity of
the H\"older norm,
\[
\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M).
\]
Since \(0\le\mu_d\le1\), also \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\).  Therefore \(\mu\) is
admissible in the supremum defining \(B_X\), and
\[
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)
-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)\right|
\le B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})
\le\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}),
\tag{7}
\]
where the second inequality is
\(\mathrm{lem:jet\text{-}lp\text{-}envelope}\).

The product experiment in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) makes the source
observations conditionally independent given \(\mathcal C\).  Equation (6) centers
their pseudo-outcomes conditionally.  Moreover, \(0\le Y\le1\) and treatment
positivity imply
\[
-\varepsilon^{-1}\le Z_i\le\varepsilon^{-1}.
\]
Conditional on \(\mathcal C\), the summand
\(\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\) consequently has range length at most
\(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\).  The conditional form of
\(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\), with
\(\delta=\gamma/2\), gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right|
>R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\,\middle|\,\mathcal C\right\}
\le\frac\gamma2
\quad\text{almost surely},
\tag{8}
\]
because the sum of squared conditional range lengths is
\(4\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\).  Integrating (8) preserves the same
unconditional probability bound.

Independently, the target observations are identically distributed,
\(\mathbb E_{P_T}\mu(X^T)=\tau\), and \(\mu(X^T)\in[-1,1]\).  Thus the centered
summands \(m^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\) have range length \(2/m\).  A second
application of \(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\), again with
\(\delta=\gamma/2\), yields
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|>R_T(m)
\right\}\le\frac\gamma2,
\tag{9}
\]
because the sum of squared ranges is \(4/m\).

Combining the exact decomposition with (7)--(9) and applying the union bound gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}-\tau\right|
\le\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})
+R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})+R_T(m)
\right\}\ge1-\gamma.
\tag{10}
\]
Finally, if the realized covariates satisfy \(\overline B_X\le b\) and
\(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\le g^2\), the definitions of the two radii give
\[
\overline B_X+R_S+R_T
\le b+\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)}\,g
+\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)/m}.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (5), (7), (10), and (11) prove every assertion.  The argument is exact
algebra plus a fixed-class deterministic envelope and bounded-sum concentration;
it supplies none of the stochastic-order, localization, leverage,
minimax-attainment, or expected-length conclusions excluded in the statement.
\(\square\)

%%% FIELD proof-fixed-class-honesty
\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\mathrm{def:bias\text{-}aware\text{-}interval}\), the event
\(\tau\in\mathcal I_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}\) is exactly
\[
\left|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}-\tau\right|
\le\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})
+R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})+R_T(m).
\tag{1}
\]
For each \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\),
\(\mathrm{thm:ricot\text{-}attainment}\) assigns probability at least
\(1-\gamma\) to (1).  The constants and the H\"older class in this assertion are
fixed uniformly across the displayed model class.  Taking the infimum over its
members therefore gives
\[
\inf_{\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)}
\mathbb P_{n,m}\{\tau\in\mathcal I_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}\}
\ge1-\gamma.
\tag{2}
\]

The interval is symmetric about
\(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}\), and its half-length is
\(\overline B_X+R_S+R_T\).  Its realized length is consequently exactly
\[
2\{\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})
+R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})+R_T(m)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
If the realized covariate design satisfies \(\overline B_X\le b\) and
\(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\le g^2\), then
\(\mathrm{def:foldwise\text{-}inference\text{-}statistics}\), as incorporated in
the interval construction, gives
\[
R_S\le\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)}\,g,
\qquad
R_T(m)=\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)/m}.
\tag{4}
\]
Substitution of (4) into (3) proves the claimed realized-design length bound.

Finally, \(\overline B_X\) is, by the construction in
\(\mathrm{def:bias\text{-}envelope}\), the maximum of two finite signed jet linear
programs.  If \(N\) is the number of distinct observed covariate sites, each program
has one variable \(u_{\ell,r}\) for every
\(1\le\ell\le N\) and \(0\le r\le p\), hence \(N(p+1)\) variables.  The pairwise
jet restrictions contribute \(O\{N^2(p+1)\}\) one-sided linear inequalities after
each absolute-value constraint is split into its two signs; the box restrictions
do not change this order.  This verifies the stated finite computation.  No
averaging or stochastic-order control of \(\overline B_X\) or
\(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\) was used, so the proof entails none of the expected-length,
Gaussian-approximation, multiplier-approximation, variance-consistency, leverage,
or frontier-attainment claims expressly excluded by the theorem.
\(\square\)
