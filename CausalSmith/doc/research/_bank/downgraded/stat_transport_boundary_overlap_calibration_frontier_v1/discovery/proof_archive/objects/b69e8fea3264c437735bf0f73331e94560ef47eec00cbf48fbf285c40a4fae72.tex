\textbf{Proof.}
Because \(f_T=q_0g_S\) on \([h,1]\), the left side equals
\[
\langle q_0,\Pi_J\mu-\mu\rangle_{g_S}.
\]
The projection residual \(\Pi_J\mu-\mu\) is orthogonal to every element of \(\mathcal B_{h,J}\), in particular to \(\Pi_Jq_0\). Hence subtracting that term gives the displayed identity exactly. The factor assumptions and their positive lower bounds imply, by the quotient rule for Hölder functions, that \(q_0\in\mathcal H^t(C)\), with \(C\) uniform over the model class. The normalized B-spline approximation lemma supplies spline approximants \(q_J,\mu_J\in\mathcal B_{h,J}\) satisfying
\[
\|q_0-q_J\|_\infty\le CJ^{-t},\qquad
\|\mu-\mu_J\|_\infty\le CJ^{-s}.
\]
Best approximation of an orthogonal projection and boundedness of \(g_S\) give the same orders in \(L^2(g_S)\). Cauchy--Schwarz in the exact identity therefore yields
\[
\left|\langle q_0-\Pi_Jq_0,\Pi_J\mu-\mu\rangle_{g_S}\right|
\le CJ^{-t}J^{-s}=CJ^{-(s+t)}.
\]
This proves both assertions. \(\square\)
