\textbf{Proof.}
We first record the elementary testing inequality used below. If probability laws \(Q_0,Q_1\) have parameter values separated by \(d>0\), the decision rule that selects the parameter value nearest an arbitrary estimator \(\widehat\tau\) makes an error only on an event where the squared estimation loss is at least \(d^2/4\). Since the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\),
\[
\max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}(\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2
\ge \frac{d^2}{8}\{1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\]
For completeness, the scalar log-sum inequality applied to an event attaining total variation reduces the comparison to Bernoulli laws; the second-derivative bound for binary relative entropy gives
\[
\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}^2\le\frac12\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Thus a pair with sufficiently small constant Kullback--Leibler divergence yields root risk bounded below by a constant multiple of its parameter separation.

Use throughout
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1.
\]
Then \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\). The declared parameter ranges imply
\[
c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
\qquad
c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+},
\]
and \(M\ge a+1\) gives the source-factor Hölder bound. Hence no strict target-density slack is needed. Let treatment follow the fixed known propensity \(e\). Conditional on \(X\), construct Bernoulli potential outcomes independent of treatment with the conditional means specified below, and give the target potential outcomes the same conditional means. A sufficiently small fixed perturbation constant keeps all means in \([0,1]\), preserves both armwise Hölder bounds, and ensures
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)\ge M^{-1}.
\]
Consistency, conditional randomization, mean transportability, treatment positivity, the density conditions, and all remaining member properties of \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) then hold. The target covariate law is the same under every source alternative below, so target observations contribute zero divergence.

Suppose first that \(a>1\). For \(h\in(0,1)\), put
\[
\mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\]
Smooth scaling of the fixed bump makes these regressions admissible for a sufficiently small \(\delta>0\). The Bernoulli relative-entropy inequality
\[
\operatorname{kl}\!\left(\operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12+u\right),
\operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12-u\right)\right)\le C_0u^2,
\qquad |u|\le\delta,
\]
together with \(e(x)\le1\), gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,n},P_{-,h}^{S,n})
\le Cn\int_0^h b_h(x)^2x^a\,dx
\le Cnh^{2s+a+1}.
\]
Because \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero, \(k_1=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0\), and
\[
|\tau_+-\tau_-|
=2\delta h^{s+1}k_1.
\]
Taking \(h=c_0n^{-1/(2s+a+1)}\), with a fixed \(c_0>0\) small enough that the product divergence is sufficiently small, the testing inequality yields
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
\ge c n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\]

If \(a<1\), choose a nonnegative nonzero \(C^\infty\) function \(q\) supported in \([1/4,3/4]\), and set
\[
\mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}q(x).
\]
On the support of \(q\), the source density is bounded above, so
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_+^{S,n},P_-^{S,n})\le C\delta^2.
\]
At the same time,
\[
|\tau_+-\tau_-|
=2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1q(x)\,dx
\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\]
Choosing \(\delta\) small and applying the testing inequality proves the regular source lower bound.

It remains to treat \(a=1\). Let \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), fix \(j_0\), and choose \(L\) so that
\[
\ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}},
\qquad
L-j_0+1\asymp\log n.
\]
For \(j=j_0,\ldots,L\), define
\[
\phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
\mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\},
\]
and, for \(\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{L-j_0+1}\), set
\[
\mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+\sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x).
\]
The choice of \(L\) implies
\[
(\sqrt n\,\ell_j)^{-1}\le C\ell_j^s.
\]
Smooth scaling, disjoint dyadic interiors, and the vanishing of the compactly supported smooth bump at shell endpoints give a common \(\mathcal H^s(M)\) bound. A sufficiently small \(\delta\) therefore makes every sign alternative a member of the model.

The contribution of coordinate \(j\) to the target is
\[
d_j=\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)\,dx
=\frac{\delta k_1}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Since \(f_S(x)=2x\), flipping only \(\omega_j\) changes the source experiment by
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{S,n},P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{S,n})
\le Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
\le C\delta^2,
\]
uniformly in \(j\) and the other signs.

Place the uniform product prior on \(\omega\). Conditional on the data, the likelihood and posterior factor over the shells because each observation belongs to at most one shell. Since conditional expectation minimizes squared error,
\[
\inf_{\widehat\tau}\mathbb E_\Pi(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2
\ge
\mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}(\tau_\omega\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
=\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2\,
\mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}).
\]
If \(q_j=\mathbb E_\Pi(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})\), then
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})=1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|.
\]
Under the prior predictive law, direct substitution of the two mixture densities shows that \(\mathbb E_\Pi|q_j|\) equals the total-variation distance between the mixture experiments obtained by fixing \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\). Joint convexity of relative entropy, which is the log-sum inequality integrated over the sample space, bounds their divergence by the average neighboring divergence and hence by \(C\delta^2\). The preceding total-variation inequality and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\) give
\[
\mathbb E_\Pi(1-|q_j|)\ge c_1>0
\]
uniformly in \(j\). Consequently,
\[
\inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
\mathbb E_\omega(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2
\ge c_1\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
\ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\]
Taking square roots proves the critical lower bound without polynomial loss. The three cases prove the lemma. \(\square\)
