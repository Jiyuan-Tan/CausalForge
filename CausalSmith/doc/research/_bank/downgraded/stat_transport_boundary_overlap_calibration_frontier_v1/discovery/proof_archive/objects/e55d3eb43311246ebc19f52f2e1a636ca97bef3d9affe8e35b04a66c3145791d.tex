Burman et al. (2026) provide the optimization and cardiomyopathy application anchor, not a nested theorem or estimand comparator. Their observed target unit includes an outcome, and their target parameter differs from the covariate-only transported treatment effect studied here. Their calibrated-transport analysis uses a bounded two-sided density ratio, smooth log-tilt approximation, a conditioned calibration design, quantitative interiority, bounded plan-to-source ratios, a bounded target dual, and empirical-offset convergence. The resulting statistical experiments and estimands are incomparable: neither model class contains the other, and the present positive-depletion regime lies outside bounded overlap. Their transport geometry therefore informs the unchanged RiCOT construction without supplying empirical localization or inference for this theorem. Khan and Tamer (2010) establish the general link between large inverse weights and nonparametric behavior but do not derive the present two-sample boundary phase. Zhang et al. (2025) work with a uniformly bounded likelihood-ratio class and a different observation and smoothness model, so their minimax result is informative rather than nested. Donoho (1994) supplies the modulus and optimal-recovery blueprint. Ma and Wang (2020) motivate local-polynomial repair of trimming bias. Armstrong and Kolesár (2020) motivate bias-aware fixed-class inference, although the interval here uses a direct bounded-outcome Hoeffding certificate and makes no optimal-length claim. Cai and Low (2004) provide the ordered-modulus language behind the nonadaptation result, while Bull (2012) motivates the explicit self-similarity restriction for a future executable adaptive procedure.
