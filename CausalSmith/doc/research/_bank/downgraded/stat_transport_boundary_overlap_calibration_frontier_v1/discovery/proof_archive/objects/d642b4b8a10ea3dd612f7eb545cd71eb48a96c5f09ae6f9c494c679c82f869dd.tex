Let \(B_h=(c_{T,+}/c_{S,-})h^{-a}\), \(u=\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h,J_{r,n}}\), and \(w=\min\{u,B_h\}\). The boundary-factor assumptions imply \(0<r(x)\le B_h\) on \([h,1]\). Projection onto \([0,B_h]\) is nonexpansive relative to every point in that interval, so
\[
0\le w(x)\le B_h,
\qquad
|w(x)-r(x)|\le |u(x)-r(x)|.
\]
For every held-out function \(v\), the capped and raw empirical discrepancies satisfy the exact identity
\[
\Delta_w(v)=\Delta_u(v)+\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{u(X_i)-B_h\}_+v(X_i)\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}.
\]
Thus raw training-atom calibration does not pass through the cap. For any probability measure \(Q\) and square-integrable \(v\),
\[
\left|\int\{w(x)-r(x)\}v(x)\,dQ(x)\right|
\le \|u-r\|_{L^2(Q)}\|v\|_{L^2(Q)}.
\]
The cap alone gives only
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw(X_i)^2\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}\le B_h^2,
\]
not the oracle-order norm or maximum-leverage conclusion. The zero-mass branch is deterministic; on either positive-mass optimization branch strict convexity gives a unique primal plan, and the stated minimum-norm conventions totalize the selected dual variables. These facts establish measurability and the cap-compatible deterministic inequalities, but not a quantitative held-out localization rate.
