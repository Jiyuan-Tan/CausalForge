\textbf{Proof.}
We use the following elementary two-point reduction.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two observed-data laws and let their parameter separation be \(\Delta=|\tau(Q_1)-\tau(Q_0)|\).  For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), set
\(E=\{|T-\tau(Q_0)|<\Delta/2\}\).  On \(E^c\) the squared error under \(Q_0\) is at least \(\Delta^2/4\), while on \(E\) the squared error relative to \(\tau(Q_1)\) is at least \(\Delta^2/4\).  Therefore
\[
 \max_{v\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_v}
   [\{T-\tau(Q_v)\}^2]
 \ge \frac{\Delta^2}{8}
 \{Q_0(E^c)+Q_1(E)\}
 \ge \frac{\Delta^2}{8}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.
\]
The log-sum inequality applied to \(E\) and \(E^c\), followed by the elementary binary inequality
\(p\log(p/q)+(1-p)\log\{(1-p)/(1-q)\}\ge2(p-q)^2\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \le \sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)/2}.
\]
Thus a pair with separation \(\Delta\) and sufficiently small constant Kullback--Leibler divergence forces root mean-squared risk at least a fixed positive multiple of \(\Delta\).

We now construct the source pairs in all three regimes.  Throughout, use the admissible baseline densities
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,\qquad f_T(x)=1,
\]
and the Bernoulli full-data construction from \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\).  The target sample law is common within each source pair.

If \(a<1\), choose a nonnegative nonzero
\(\phi\in C_c^\infty((1/2,1))\) and put
\(u_n(x)=\delta n^{-1/2}\phi(x)\).  Use treated regressions
\(1/2\pm u_n\) and the common control regression \(1/2\).  For sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\), the same support, H\"older, positivity, and variance checks as in the boundary-bump construction give two model members.  Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{+,n}^S,Q_{-,n}^S)
 \le Cn\int_0^1u_n(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
 \qquad
 |\tau(Q_{+,n})-\tau(Q_{-,n})|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1\phi(x)\,dx.
\]
After reducing \(\delta\), the two-point reduction gives the source lower bound \(cn^{-1/2}\).

If \(a>1\), apply \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\) at
\[
 h=c_0n^{-1/(2s+a+1)},
\]
where \(c_0>0\) is fixed and sufficiently small.  Its source divergence is bounded by
\(Cc_0^{2s+a+1}\), which can be made smaller than a fixed testing constant, while its separation is at least
\[
 cc_0^{s+1}n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\]
The two-point reduction gives the asserted nonregular source rate.

It remains to establish the critical logarithm without a polynomial loss.  Let
\(\psi\in C_c^\infty((5/4,7/4))\) be nonnegative and nonzero.  For sufficiently large \(n\), define
\[
 J_n=\left\lfloor\frac{\log_2 n}{2(s+1)}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad h_j=2^{-j},\quad 1\le j\le J_n,
 \qquad A_n=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{nJ_n}},
\]
and form the multiscale boundary perturbation
\[
 u_n(x)=A_n\sum_{j=1}^{J_n}h_j^{-1}\psi(x/h_j).
\]
The summands have disjoint supports contained in dyadic boundary shells.  Standard H\"older scaling, including the gaps between adjacent supports, gives
\[
 \lVert u_n\rVert_{\mathcal H^s}
 \le C_\psi A_nh_{J_n}^{-(s+1)}
 \le \frac{C_\psi\delta}{\sqrt{J_n}}.
\]
Thus \(1/2\pm u_n\) are admissible treated regressions for all large \(n\) after choosing \(\delta\) small.  At \(a=1\), \(f_S(x)=2x\).  Disjointness and the change of variables \(x=h_ju\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 n\int_0^1u_n(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx
 &=2nA_n^2\sum_{j=1}^{J_n}
   \int h_j^{-2}\psi(x/h_j)^2x\,dx\\
 &=2nA_n^2J_n\int_{5/4}^{7/4}\psi(u)^2u\,du
 \le C_\psi\delta^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Bernoulli divergence inequality used in
\(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\) therefore bounds the product-source divergence by \(C\delta^2\).  On the other hand, every shell contributes the same target integral:
\[
 |\tau(Q_{+,n})-\tau(Q_{-,n})|
 =2\int_0^1u_n(x)\,dx
 =2A_nJ_n\int_{5/4}^{7/4}\psi(u)\,du
 \ge c\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
\]
Choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small and applying the two-point reduction proves the critical source lower bound.  This is a two-sign multiscale mixture of boundary-shell bumps; the number of active scales is \(J_n\asymp\log n\), so the gain is exactly \(\sqrt{\log n}\) and no polynomial factor is introduced.

For the independent target floor, use the target-density pair from
\(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\).  Its source law is common, its separation is at least \(c_Tm^{-1/2}\), and its observed target-sample divergence can be made smaller than the same testing constant by reducing the fixed multiplier \(\delta_1\) in that construction.  Hence
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_T'm^{-1/2}.
\]
The three source constructions likewise give
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_S\rho_n(a,s).
\]
Both conclusions hold uniformly in the other sample size.  Since
\(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge c\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\}.
\]
The construction was written for sufficiently large \(n\).  For the finitely many remaining \(n\), the fixed interior two-point perturbation has strictly positive separation; decreasing \(c\) extends the displayed inequality to all indices for which the model is considered. \(\square\)
