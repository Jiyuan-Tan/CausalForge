\textbf{Proof.}
We first record a testing inequality.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two observed-data laws, write \(\tau_v=\tau(Q_v)\), and put \(\Delta=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\).  Given an arbitrary estimator \(T\), classify the data by the nearer of \(\tau_0\) and \(\tau_1\).  A classification error under \(Q_v\) implies \(|T-\tau_v|\ge\Delta/2\).  Since the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\),
\[
\max_{v\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_v}[(T-\tau_v)^2]
\ge \frac{\Delta^2}{8}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.
\tag{14}
\]
For a Hahn set of \(Q_0-Q_1\), the log-sum inequality reduces relative entropy to binary relative entropy.  The elementary inequality
\[
p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}\ge2(p-q)^2
\tag{15}
\]
follows because the difference between the left side and \(2(p-q)^2\), as a function of \(p\), vanishes with zero derivative at \(p=q\) and has second derivative \(1/\{p(1-p)\}-4\ge0\).  Thus
\[
\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
\le\sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)/2}.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (14)--(16) show that a pair with a sufficiently small constant divergence forces root mean-squared risk of order \(\Delta\).

In every source construction below take
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1.
\tag{17}
\]
The factors \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\) obey all density bounds and belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every fixed \(t>0\).  Use the randomized Bernoulli full-data construction from the preceding lemma.  All perturbation constants are fixed sufficiently small to keep the conditional means in \([0,1]\), the response functions in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), and the conditional variances above \(M^{-1}\).  The target covariate law is common within each source pair, so the target sample contributes no divergence.

\emph{Regular regime \(a<1\).}
Choose a nonnegative nonzero function \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty((1/4,3/4))\), and set
\[
\mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}\varphi(x).
\tag{18}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), these are admissible response functions.  On the support of \(\varphi\), the source density in (17) is bounded above and below by positive constants.  The Bernoulli bound (6) therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_{+,n},Q_{-,n})
\le C\delta^2,
\qquad
|\tau_+-\tau_-|
=2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1\varphi(x)\,dx
\ge c_0n^{-1/2}.
\tag{19}
\]
After reducing \(\delta\), (14)--(16) imply
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_1n^{-1/2}.
\tag{20}
\]

\emph{Nonregular regime \(a>1\).}
Apply the boundary-bump lemma at
\[
h=c_hn^{-1/(2s+a+1)},
\tag{21}
\]
where \(c_h>0\) is fixed and sufficiently small.  Its source divergence is at most \(Cc_h^{2s+a+1}\), while its parameter separation is at least
\[
c'c_h^{s+1}n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (14)--(16) give
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
\ge c_2n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\tag{23}
\]

\emph{Critical regime \(a=1\).}
Choose a nonnegative, nonzero function \(\psi\in C_c^\infty((5/4,7/4))\).  For sufficiently large \(n\), let
\[
J_n=\left\lfloor\frac{\log_2 n}{2(s+1)}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
h_j=2^{-j},\quad 1\le j\le J_n,
\qquad
A_n=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{nJ_n}},
\tag{24}
\]
and define the multiscale perturbation
\[
u_n(x)=A_n\sum_{j=1}^{J_n}h_j^{-1}\psi(x/h_j).
\tag{25}
\]
The supports in (25) are pairwise disjoint boundary shells separated by gaps.  Put \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\).  On shell \(j\), differentiation and H\"older scaling give
\[
\lVert D^q\{A_nh_j^{-1}\psi(\cdot/h_j)\}\rVert_\infty
\le C_\psi A_nh_j^{-(q+1)},\quad 0\le q\le p,
\tag{26}
\]
and
\[
[D^p\{A_nh_j^{-1}\psi(\cdot/h_j)\}]_\beta
\le C_\psi A_nh_j^{-(s+1)}.
\tag{27}
\]
The smooth pieces vanish to every order at their support endpoints.  Hence they paste through the intervening zero regions, and cross-shell differences are bounded by the same largest-scale quantity as in (27).  Since \(2^{J_n(s+1)}\le n^{1/2}\),
\[
\lVert u_n\rVert_{\mathcal H^s}
\le C_\psi A_nh_{J_n}^{-(s+1)}
\le \frac{C_\psi\delta}{\sqrt{J_n}}.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus the regressions
\[
\mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm u_n(x)
\tag{29}
\]
belong to the model after choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small.

At \(a=1\), the source density is \(f_S(x)=2x\).  Disjointness, followed by \(x=h_ju\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
n\int_0^1u_n(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx
&=2nA_n^2\sum_{j=1}^{J_n}
\int h_j^{-2}\psi(x/h_j)^2x\,dx\\
&=2nA_n^2J_n\int_{5/4}^{7/4}\psi(u)^2u\,du
\le C_\psi\delta^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
The Bernoulli divergence bound therefore makes the product-source divergence at most \(C\delta^2\).  Every shell has the same target integral, so
\[
|\tau_+-\tau_-|
=2A_nJ_n\int_{5/4}^{7/4}\psi(u)\,du
\ge c_3\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}},
\tag{31}
\]
because \(J_n\asymp\log n\).  Reducing \(\delta\) and using (14)--(16) proves
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(1,s,t)
\ge c_4\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
\tag{32}
\]
The construction uses \(J_n\) disjoint boundary shells, so (30)--(31) exhibit the exact logarithmic gain with no polynomial loss.

Equations (20), (23), and (32), together with the definition of \(\rho_n(a,s)\), give
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_S\rho_n(a,s)
\tag{33}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly in \(m\).  The target-density pair in the boundary-bump lemma has a common source law, constant divergence after \(m\) target observations, and separation at least \(c_Tm^{-1/2}\).  Equations (14)--(16) therefore also give
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_T'm^{-1/2}
\tag{34}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\), uniformly in \(n\).  Finally, \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\).  Combining (33) and (34), and then taking the infimum over all estimators in the definition of \(\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\), proves
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
\ge c\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\}.
\]
No step used a numerical lower bound on \(t\) or a product-smoothness inequality. \(\square\)
