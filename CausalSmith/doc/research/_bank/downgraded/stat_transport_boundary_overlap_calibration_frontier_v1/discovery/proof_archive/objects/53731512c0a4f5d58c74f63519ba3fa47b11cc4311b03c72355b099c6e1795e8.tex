Burman et al. (2026) are the optimization and application anchor, not a nested estimand comparator. Their observed target unit includes an outcome, Proposition 2.1 identifies a distinct target-outcome estimand, and Theorem 3.1 gives its efficient influence function. Their population theory imposes bounded target-to-trial density ratios, while their calibrated-weight analysis uses quantitative interiority, bounded plan-to-source ratios, and convergence of an empirical transport offset. Those results inform the semi-unbalanced entropic construction and the transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy consumer, but do not supply polynomial-boundary held-out stability. Khan and Tamer (2010) establish the general link between arbitrarily large inverse weights and slower-than-parametric estimation. Zhang et al. (2025) analyze a uniformly bounded likelihood-ratio class and a different moment-estimation experiment. Donoho (1994) supplies the modulus-of-continuity blueprint used for the source-side converse and truncated-oracle comparison; no data-driven minimax upper theorem is attributed to it here. Ma and Wang (2020) show how local polynomial correction can repair trimming bias under weak overlap. Armstrong and Kolesár (2020) motivate folded-normal bias-aware critical values, while Armstrong and Kolesár (2018) explain the additional correction needed after bandwidth inspection. Cai and Low (2004) provide ordered-modulus tools for nonadaptation, and Bull (2012) supplies the self-similarity mechanism relevant to a future executable adaptive construction.
