\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\epsilon_i=Z_i-\mu(X_i)\).  Conditional on all source and target covariates, replace every \(Z_i\) in the linear regression fit by \(\mu(X_i)\); call the resulting oracle fit \(\widehat\mu^\circ\).  Linearity gives
\[
 \widehat\mu(x)=\widehat\mu^\circ(x)+\sum_{i\in\mathcal T^S_1}L_{i,h}(x)\epsilon_i.
\tag{14}
\]
Substitution of (14) into the two displayed empirical averages defining \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}\) shows, term for term, that the coefficient of \(\epsilon_i\) is exactly \(\Gamma_{i,h}\).  Thus
\[
 \widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}-\tau
 =\sum_i\Gamma_{i,h}\epsilon_i
 +(P^T_{\mathcal V_1}-P_T)\mu+R_{n,m},
\tag{15}
\]
where \(P^T_{\mathcal V_1}\) and \(P^S_{\mathcal V_1}\) denote validation empirical means and the realized remainder is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{n,m}={}&(P^T_{\mathcal V_1}-P_T)
 \{(\widehat\mu^\circ-\mu)\mathbf1_{[0,h)}\}
 +P_T\{(\widehat\mu^\circ-\mu)\mathbf1_{[0,h)}\}\\
&+(P^T_{\mathcal V_1}-P_T)
 \{(\widehat\mu^\circ-\mu)\mathbf1_{[h,1]}\}\\
&+(P_T-P_S\widehat r^{\mathrm{DS}})
 \{(\widehat\mu^\circ-\mu)\mathbf1_{[h,1]}\}\\
&+(P_S-P^S_{\mathcal V_1})
 \{\widehat r^{\mathrm{DS}}(\widehat\mu^\circ-\mu)\mathbf1_{[h,1]}\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
This is the requested full source--target empirical decomposition; no empirical average in (16) has been replaced by its population value.

For the boundary population term, the order-\(p\) local-polynomial remainder for each response and \(f_T\le c_{T,+}\) give
\[
 \left|P_T\{(\widehat\mu^\circ-\mu)\mathbf1_{[0,h)}\}\right|
 \le 2C_{\mathrm{LP}}Mc_{T,+}h^{s+1}+C\lambda_nJ_{\mu,n}.
\tag{17}
\]
On the interior, \(f_T=q_0g_S\) and \(f_S=x^ag_S\), while \(\widehat r^{\mathrm{DS}}=x^{-a}\widehat q^{\mathrm{DS}}\).  Consequently the fourth line of (16) is exactly
\[
 \int_h^1(q_0-\widehat q^{\mathrm{DS}})
 (\widehat\mu^\circ-\mu)g_S,dx.
\tag{18}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz, the localized-series bounds, and the ridge resolvent bound make (18) at most
\[
 C\{\delta_{s,h}\delta_{t,h}+\lambda_n(J_{\mu,n}+J_{r,n})\}
\tag{19}
\]
in root mean square.  Conditional on the training covariates, the first and third lines of (16) are centered target validation averages and the fifth is a centered source validation average.  Their conditional second moments are bounded respectively by
\[
 C\frac{\delta_{s,h}^2}{m},
 \qquad
 C\frac{\delta_{s,h}^2}{m},
 \qquad
 C\frac{V_a(h)\delta_{s,h}^2}{n},
\tag{20}
\]
where local support, (2), and (11) give the last bound.  Adding a factor \(\log(en)\) makes these inequalities simultaneous with probability \(1-o(1)\).  Equations (17)--(20) are precisely the terms in \(\widehat B\), proving the remainder assertion.

It remains to verify the complete coefficient bounds.  The validation and training source sets are disjoint.  Hence there can be no algebraic cancellation between their squared coefficients, and (11)--(12) give the lower bound
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_{i,h}^2
 \ge\frac1{(N^S_1)^2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal V^S_1}
 \mathbf1(X_i\ge h)\{\widehat r^{\mathrm{DS}}(X_i)\}^2
 \ge_P c\frac{V_a(h)}n.
\tag{21}
\]
For the upper bound, the Gram inequalities defining \(\mathcal E_{n,m}(h)\), local support, and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \sum_{i\in\mathcal T^S_1}
 \left\{P^T_{\mathcal V_1}L_{i,h}
 -P^S_{\mathcal V_1}(\widehat r^{\mathrm{DS}}L_{i,h})\right\}^2
 \le_P C\frac{V_a(h)}n.
\tag{22}
\]
The same calculation with the largest basis value gives a maximum-to-sum ratio bounded by the right side of (13), plus \(CJ_{\mu,n}/N_h\); both tend to zero under the stability caps.  Combining (21)--(22) proves the squared-norm, leverage, and noncancellation claims. \(\square\)
