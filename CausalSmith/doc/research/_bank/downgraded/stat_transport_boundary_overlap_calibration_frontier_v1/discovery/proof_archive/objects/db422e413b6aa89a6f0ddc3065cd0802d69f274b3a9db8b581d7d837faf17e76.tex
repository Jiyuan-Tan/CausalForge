\textbf{Exact decomposition and counterexample to expected length.}
For arbitrary frozen weights \(w_i=\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}\), direct addition and subtraction gives the exact interior identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{int}}-\int_h^1\mu(x)f_T(x)\,dx
={}&\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\\
&+\left\{\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h\}-\int_h^1\mu(x)f_T(x)\,dx\right\}\\
&+C_{n,m}(h)-\Delta_{h,J_{r,n}}(\mu).
\end{aligned}
\]
The two response-smoothness assumptions imply \(\mu\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\), and hence
\[
 |\Delta_{h,J_{r,n}}(\mu)|\le2M\sup_{v\in\mathcal H^s(1)}|\Delta_{h,J_{r,n}}(v)|.
\]
Thus the displayed envelope includes the exact held-out interior discrepancy. Also,
\[
 \int_0^1\epsilon^{-1/(2s)}\,d\epsilon<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s>1/2,
\]
which verifies the announced one-dimensional Hölder entropy threshold.

Nevertheless, the frozen expected-length assertion is false. Use the ordinary-overlap member law and the event \(E_n\) constructed in the preceding counterexample. The constant function \(v=1\) belongs to \(\mathcal H^1(1)\), every target observation is interior, and all fitted weights are nonnegative. Therefore, on \(E_n\),
\[
 |\Delta_{h_n,J}(1)|
 =\left|1-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h_n,J}(X_i)\right|
 \ge \frac1n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-1)}_{h_n,J}(X_{i_\star})-1,
\]
and consequently
\[
 \widehat B_{h_n,J_{\mu,n},J}
 \ge2M\left\{\frac1n\exp(c_3n^2/J)-1\right\}.
\]
For \(u\ge0\), the folded-normal definition gives \(\operatorname{cv}_{1-\gamma/2}(u)\ge u\): if \(c<u\), choosing the allowed shift \(b=u\) gives \(P\{|N(u,1)|\le c\}<1/2<1-\gamma/2\). Hence both the positive-standard-error branch and the zero-standard-error branch of the interval have length at least \(2\widehat B\). It follows that
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{n,m}}[\operatorname{length}(\mathcal I_{h_n,J_{\mu,n},J})]
 \ge \frac{C}{n}\exp\{c_3n^2/J-O(n\log J)\}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
This contradicts the claimed \(O(n^{-1/2})\) maximal expected length for \(a=0\). After the proposed cap removes this counterexample, coverage still requires a uniform asymptotic linear expansion, consistency of the four-term variance statistic, a Lindeberg bound for the capped triangular-array summands, and conditional multiplier approximation of the centered calibration--boundary process. Those steps are not consequences of the oracle norm and entropy integral alone.
