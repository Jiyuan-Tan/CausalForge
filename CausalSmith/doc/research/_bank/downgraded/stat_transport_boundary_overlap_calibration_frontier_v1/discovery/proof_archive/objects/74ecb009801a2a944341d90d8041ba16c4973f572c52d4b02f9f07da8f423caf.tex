\textbf{Proof.}
By the source-only frontier lower bound, there is a constant \(c_0>0\), independent of \(n\) and \(m\), such that for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_0\rho_n(a,s).
\]
This application is valid under the stated restrictions \(M\ge a+1\), \(t>1/2\), and
\[
\frac{s}{2s+1}+\frac{t}{2t+1}>\frac12.
\]
In particular, that lower-bound lemma uses target density \(f_T=1\), which satisfies the allowed weak inequalities \(c_{T,-}\le 1\le c_{T,+}\).  Thus the lower bound does not require either target-density inequality to have strict slack.

By the sample-balance assumption \(m/n\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\), there are constants \(c_\kappa,C_\kappa>0\) and an integer \(n_0\) such that
\[
c_\kappa n\le m\le C_\kappa n
\]
whenever \(n\ge n_0\).  Consequently,
\[
m^{-1/2}\le c_\kappa^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}.
\]
We now compare \(n^{-1/2}\) with the three branches of \(\rho_n(a,s)\).  If \(a<1\), then \(\rho_n(a,s)=n^{-1/2}\).  If \(a=1\), then, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\rho_n(1,s)=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\ge n^{-1/2}.
\]
If \(a>1\), positivity of \(s\) and the strict inequality \(a>1\) give
\[
\frac{s+1}{2s+a+1}<\frac12,
\]
and therefore
\[
\rho_n(a,s)=n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}\ge n^{-1/2}.
\]
Thus, in every regime, there is a constant \(C_1<\infty\) such that
\[
m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\le C_1\rho_n(a,s)
\]
eventually.  Combining this inequality with the source-only lower bound yields
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
\ge \frac{c_0}{C_1}\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\},
\]
which is the first display after expanding the definition of \(\rho_n(a,s)\).

Finally, the oracle truncation-envelope lemma gives, uniformly over \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\),
\[
m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}+n^{-1/2}
\left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h_n\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}
\asymp m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s).
\]
This is an equality of deterministic orders for the exact truncated representer and its boundary approximation term.  It supplies no measurable data-driven estimator, so the conclusion is precisely the asserted lower frontier and matching oracle benchmark, without a minimax upper-bound claim. \(\square\)
