\textbf{Proof.}
First let \(W\) be centered and supported on \([\alpha,\beta]\), with \(d=\beta-\alpha>0\). Centering implies \(\alpha\le0\le\beta\). Convexity of the exponential gives, for every \(w\in[\alpha,\beta]\),
\[
e^{\lambda w}\le\frac{\beta-w}{d}e^{\lambda\alpha}+\frac{w-\alpha}{d}e^{\lambda\beta}.
\]
Put \(p=-\alpha/d\) and \(z=\lambda d\). Taking expectations and using \(\mathbb E W=0\) yields
\[
\mathbb E e^{\lambda W}\le(1-p)e^{-pz}+pe^{(1-p)z}.
\]
Let \(\phi(z)\) be the logarithm of the right side. Direct differentiation gives \(\phi(0)=\phi'(0)=0\) and \(\phi''(z)=q_z(1-q_z)\le1/4\), where \(q_z\in[0,1]\) is the exponentially tilted Bernoulli probability. Taylor's formula with integral remainder therefore gives \(\phi(z)\le z^2/8\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E e^{\lambda W}\le\exp\{\lambda^2d^2/8\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The conclusion is immediate when \(d=0\).

For independent \(W_i\), (1) and factorization give, with \(D=\sum_i(\beta_i-\alpha_i)^2\),
\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(\lambda\sum_iW_i\right)\le\exp(\lambda^2D/8).
\]
Exponential Markov inequality then implies
\[
P\!\left(\sum_iW_i>u\right)\le\exp(-\lambda u+\lambda^2D/8).
\]
For \(D>0\), minimizing at \(\lambda=4u/D\) gives \(\exp(-2u^2/D)\). Applying the same argument to \(-W_i\) and taking the union of the two tails proves the display. If \(D=0\), every centered \(W_i\) is zero almost surely. \(\square\)
