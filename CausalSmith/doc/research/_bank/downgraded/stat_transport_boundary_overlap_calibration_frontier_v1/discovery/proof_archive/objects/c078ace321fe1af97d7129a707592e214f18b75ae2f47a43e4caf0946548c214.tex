\textbf{Proof.}
First consider a centered random variable \(W\in[\alpha,\beta]\) and put \(d=\beta-\alpha\). Convexity of the exponential gives
\[
e^{\lambda W}\le\frac{\beta-W}{d}e^{\lambda\alpha}+\frac{W-\alpha}{d}e^{\lambda\beta}.
\]
Taking expectations and using \(\mathbb EW=0\), the logarithm of the right-hand side is the cumulant generating function of a two-point variable with range \(\lambda d\), mean zero, value zero and first derivative zero at \(\lambda=0\). Its second derivative is a Bernoulli variance and is at most \(d^2/4\). Twice integrating this second-derivative bound yields
\[
\mathbb E e^{\lambda W}\le e^{\lambda^2d^2/8}.
\tag{1}
\]
For independent \(W_i\), (1) and multiplication of moment generating functions give, with \(D=\sum_i(\beta_i-\alpha_i)^2\),
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{\sum_iW_i\ge x\right\}
\le\inf_{\lambda>0}\exp\{-\lambda x+\lambda^2D/8\}
=\exp\{-2x^2/D\}.
\]
Applying the same argument to \(-W_i\) and adding the two tail probabilities gives \(2e^{-2x^2/D}\). Substitution of \(x^2=D\log(2/\delta)/2\) proves the assertion. The conditional version repeats the same moment-generating-function calculation with conditional expectations; all inequalities hold almost surely before the final expectation is taken. \(\square\)
