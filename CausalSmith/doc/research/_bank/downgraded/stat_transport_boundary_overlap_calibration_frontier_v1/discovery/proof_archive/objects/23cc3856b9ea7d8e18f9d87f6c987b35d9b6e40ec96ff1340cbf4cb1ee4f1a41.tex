\textbf{Lower phase diagram and boundary-corrected oracle benchmark.}
For fixed \(a,s,t,M\) with \(M\ge a+1\) and \(t>0\), along every sequence with \(m/n\to\kappa\), there is \(c>0\) such that
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\left\{m^{-1/2}+\begin{cases}
n^{-1/2},&a<1,\\
\sqrt{\log n/n},&a=1,\\
n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)},&a>1.
\end{cases}\right\}.
\]
This one-sided minimax lower phase diagram requires no strict slack in the target-density bounds. For the oracle boundary-local-polynomial linear weight, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\),
\[
|\mathfrak b_{h_n}(\mu;P)|\le b_{h_n}^{\circ}(P)\le C h_n^{s+1}
\]
and
\[
m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}
+n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}
\asymp m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s).
\]
The allowance \(h_n^{s+1}\) is class-sharp for every fixed \(a>0\), but the actual oracle bias can vanish for special laws, including the equal-density case at \(a=0\). The benchmark is not a data-measurable estimator, a minimax upper bound, or a claim of frontier attainment.
