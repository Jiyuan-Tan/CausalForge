\textbf{Proof.}
On \(\mathcal E_{n,m}(h_n)\), apply the exact decomposition lemma.  Conditional residual centering, the upper coefficient-norm bound, bounded conditional variance, and sample balance give
\[
 \left\|\sum_i\Gamma_{i,h_n}\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right\|_2
 \le Cn^{-1/2}V_a(h_n)^{1/2},
\tag{29}
\]
while bounded \(\mu\) gives
\[
 \left\|(N^T_1)^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal V^T_1}
 \{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\right\|_2\le Cm^{-1/2}.
\tag{30}
\]
The remainder lemma bounds the remaining root mean square by the five terms displayed after \(m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\) in the proposed statement.  On \(\mathcal E_{n,m}(h_n)^c\), the estimator is zero and \(|\tau|\le1\); the square root of the failure probability is smaller than every polynomial rate by the localized-series lemma.  Minkowski's inequality, (29)--(30), and the oracle truncation envelope prove the upper bound.  The empirical norm and leverage conclusions are equations (12)--(13) of the localization lemma.

It remains to identify the matching subregime.  When \(s>1/2\) and \(t>1/2\), all stability caps are asymptotically inactive.  Up to powers of \(\log(en)\),
\[
 \delta_{u,h_n}\asymp N_{h_n}^{-u/(2u+1)}.
\tag{31}
\]
For \(a<1\), \(N_{h_n}\asymp n\), and the product is \(o(n^{-1/2})\) because
\[
 \frac{s}{2s+1}+\frac{t}{2t+1}>\frac12.
\tag{32}
\]
For \(a=1\), \(N_{h_n}\asymp n/\log n\), so the strict polynomial inequality in (32) dominates every logarithmic factor and the product is \(o\{\sqrt{\log n/n}\}\).  For \(a>1\), writing \(D=2s+a+1\),
\[
 N_{h_n}=\frac{n}{V_a(h_n)}\asymp nh_n^{a-1}
 =n^{2(s+1)/D}.
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (31)--(33) make the product of order
\[
 n^{-\{2(s+1)/D\}\{s/(2s+1)+t/(2t+1)\}}
 =o\{n^{-(s+1)/D}\}.
\tag{34}
\]
The ridge and empirical remainder terms are smaller by the same inequalities.  Thus the upper bound matches the oracle frontier when \(s>1/2\).  If \(s\le1/2\), the dimension cap may bind, so only the explicit displayed upper-minus-lower bracket has been proved; no converse for that gap is claimed. \(\square\)
