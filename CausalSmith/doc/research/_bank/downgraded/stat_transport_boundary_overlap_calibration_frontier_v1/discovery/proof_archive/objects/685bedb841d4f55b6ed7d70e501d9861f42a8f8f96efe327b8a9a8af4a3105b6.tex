\textbf{Proof.}
We first record an elementary testing inequality.  If two laws \(Q_0,Q_1\) have parameter values separated by \(d>0\), deciding which parameter is closer to an arbitrary estimator shows
\[
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}(\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2
 \ge \frac{d^2}{8}\{1-\|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\]
Indeed, on a wrong nearest-parameter decision the squared error is at least \(d^2/4\), and the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\).  The elementary Pinsker inequality \(\|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^2\le\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)/2\) follows by integrating the scalar convex inequality underlying relative entropy.  Thus any pair with sufficiently small constant Kullback--Leibler divergence gives root risk bounded below by a constant times \(d\).  All perturbation amplitudes below are multiplied by a sufficiently small fixed constant so that this condition holds.

For \(a>1\), take \(h=n^{-1/(2s+a+1)}\) in the source pair from the boundary-bump lemma.  Its divergence is bounded because \(nh^{2s+a+1}=1\), while its separation is
\[
 h^{s+1}=n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}=\rho_n(a,s).
\]
For \(a<1\), use the same Bernoulli regression construction with a fixed smooth bump supported inside \([1/4,3/4]\), now of amplitude \(n^{-1/2}\).  The source density is bounded above and below on this fixed interval, so the product divergence is bounded and the target integral changes by a constant times \(n^{-1/2}=\rho_n(a,s)\).

It remains to prove the critical logarithm.  Set \(a=1\).  Choose dyadic lengths \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), with disjoint shells \([\ell_j,2\ell_j]\), for \(j=j_0,\ldots,L\), where \(j_0\) is fixed and \(\ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}\).  Hence \(L-j_0+1\asymp\log n\).  For independent signs \(\omega_j\in\{-1,1\}\), define
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\},
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+\sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x),
 \quad \mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12.
\]
Because \((\sqrt n\,\ell_j)^{-1}\le C\ell_j^s\) for every retained shell, smooth scaling and disjoint support put all these regressions in the same \(\mathcal H^s(M)\) ball when \(\delta\) is small.  The Bernoulli outcomes stay in the required range and retain the variance lower bound.  With \(f_T=1\), every sign contributes the same amount
\[
 d_j=\int\phi_j(x)\,dx
 =\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n}\int_0^1K(u)\,du
 =\frac{d_0}{\sqrt n}
\]
to \(\tau\).  Flipping only sign \(j\) has source divergence bounded by
\[
 Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
\]
uniformly in \(j\).

Put the uniform product prior on the signs.  Conditional on the data, its posterior factorizes over \(j\), because the shells are disjoint and the likelihood groups observations by shell.  Therefore, for every estimator, its integrated squared risk is at least the posterior variance of \(\tau_\omega\):
\[
 \mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(\tau_\omega\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^L d_j^2\,
   \mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}).
\]
If \(q_j=\mathbb E(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})\), then \(1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|\).  Averaging the latter quantity equals one minus the total-variation distance between the two mixture experiments obtained by fixing \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\).  Convexity of relative entropy and the preceding one-coordinate divergence bound make that total-variation distance at most a fixed number below one.  Consequently
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2
 \ge c\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
 \ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\]
Taking square roots proves the source term \(\sqrt{\log n/n}\) without polynomial loss.

Finally, the target-density pair in the boundary-bump lemma gives the independent floor \(cm^{-1/2}\).  The minimax root risk is at least the maximum of the source and target lower bounds, and \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\).  This yields the stated two-sample sum in all three regimes. \(\square\)
