\textbf{Proof.}
Reveal first the target validation observations one at a time, next all remaining source and target covariates, and finally the source treatment--outcome pairs one at a time.  The target increments
\[
 (N^T_1)^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}
\]
are martingale differences.  Once all covariates have been revealed, every \(\Gamma_{i,h_n}\) is measurable and
\[
 \mathbb E\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\mid X_i\}=0
\tag{23}
\]
by consistency, randomization, the known propensity, and the definition of \(Z\).  Hence the later source increments \(\Gamma_{i,h_n}\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\) are also martingale differences.  This ordering is the point at which target-dependent coefficients are handled: the target observations are not treated as independent centered summands after conditioning them fixed.

Bounded outcomes and treatment positivity give \(|Z_i-\mu(X_i)|\le C_\varepsilon\).  Residual nondegeneracy and treatment positivity give
\[
 0<c\le\operatorname{Var}(Z_i\mid X_i)\le C<\infty.
\tag{24}
\]
By the coefficient lemma and sample balance, the predictable quadratic variation is comparable in probability to
\[
 n^{-1}V_a(h_n)+m^{-1}.
\tag{25}
\]
Its ratio to its expectation converges to one: the validation direct term follows by scalar Bernstein, and the training-smoother term follows from the empirical Gram convergence and the finite-band spline quadratic-form bound used in (22).  The target sample variance obeys the ordinary bounded law of large numbers.  Thus the normalized predictable quadratic variation converges to one uniformly.

The largest normalized source increment is bounded by the square root of the coefficient leverage ratio, times a fixed constant, and therefore tends to zero.  The largest normalized target increment is bounded by \(C/m\) divided by the square root of (25), which also tends to zero.  Consequently, for every \(\eta>0\), the written-out conditional Lindeberg sum satisfies
\[
 \sum_r\mathbb E\left[
 D_{n,r}^2\mathbf1\{|D_{n,r}|>\eta\}
 \mid\mathcal F_{n,r-1}\right]\longrightarrow_P0,
\tag{26}
\]
where \((D_{n,r})\) is the sequence of normalized increments just constructed.  The cited martingale triangular-array central limit theorem applied to (25)--(26) gives \(L_{n,m}/\{\operatorname{Var}(L_{n,m})\}^{1/2}\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).  Every bound is uniform in the declared constants; a subsequence argument and continuity of the normal distribution upgrade this to uniform Kolmogorov distance \(o(1)\).

For sandwich consistency, write \(v(x)=\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid X=x)\).  Conditional independence gives the source variance \(\sum_i\Gamma_{i,h_n}^2v(X_i)\).  The leave-one-out residual obeys
\[
 \widehat\epsilon_i^2-\epsilon_i^2
 =-2\epsilon_i\{\widehat\mu^{(-i)}(X_i)-\mu(X_i)\}
 +\{\widehat\mu^{(-i)}(X_i)-\mu(X_i)\}^2.
\tag{27}
\]
Conditional centering of the first term, the localized regression bound for the second, and maximum leverage imply
\[
 \sum_i\Gamma_{i,h_n}^2\{\widehat\epsilon_i^2-v(X_i)\}
 =o_P\{n^{-1}V_a(h_n)\}.
\tag{28}
\]
The target sample variance based on \(\widehat\mu(X_j^T)\) differs from that based on \(\mu(X_j^T)\) by \(o_P(m^{-1})\), by Cauchy--Schwarz and the target part of \(\delta_{s,h_n}\).  Equations (25), (28), and source--target independence prove variance consistency, including all smoother covariance because the full \(\Gamma_i\) is used.

Finally, the exact decomposition lemma and its simultaneous Bernstein bounds give \(\mathbb P\{|R_{n,m}|\le\widehat B\}=1-o(1)\).  This proves the lemma without any multiplier approximation. \(\square\)
