\textbf{Proof.}
Assume \(a=0\). By the definitions of the density factors, for every \(x\in(0,1]\),
\[
 f_S(x)=x^a g_S(x)=g_S(x),
 \qquad
 f_T(x)=g_T(x),
 \qquad
 r(x)=x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}.
\]
The source-boundary-factor assumption gives
\[
 c_{S,-}\le g_S(x)\le c_{S,+}
 \qquad (0\le x\le1),
\]
and the target-boundary-factor assumption gives
\[
 c_{T,-}\le g_T(x)\le c_{T,+}
 \qquad (0\le x\le1).
\]
In particular, \(g_S(x)\ge c_{S,-}>0\) throughout the closed interval, so every division below is valid, including at the boundary point. Combining the lower bound on \(g_T\) with the upper bound on \(g_S\), and combining the upper bound on \(g_T\) with the lower bound on \(g_S\), yields
\[
 \frac{c_{T,-}}{c_{S,+}}
 \le \frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}
 \le \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}},
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]
The symbol \(r\) is defined on \((0,1]\), rather than at zero. On precisely that domain the preceding defining identity gives \(r(x)=g_T(x)/g_S(x)\), which proves the asserted, well-typed ratio statement.

Because \(a=0<1\), the first branch in the definition of the regime bandwidth applies and gives
\[
 h_n=n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}.
\]
Since \(s>0\), the exponent \(s+1\) is positive, and direct exponent arithmetic gives the exact equality
\[
 h_n^{s+1}
 =\left(n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}\right)^{s+1}
 =n^{-1/2}.
\]

Finally, apply the already-established Riesz-threshold lemma with \(a=0\) and \(h=h_n\). Since \(h_n\downarrow0\), its regular-overlap branch gives
\[
 \int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\asymp1.
\]
The comparison constants depend only on the fixed positive source and target boundary-factor constants. Consequently
\[
 \int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx=O(1)
\]
uniformly over the model class as \(n\to\infty\). The displayed restrictions involving \(s\) and \(t\) are compatible with this specialization but are not needed for these deterministic identities. None of the proved displays contains an empirical series-weight norm, a realized residual sum, or the risk of an estimator; therefore no such statistical conclusion follows from this proposition alone. \(\square\)
