\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\mathrm{lem:source\text{-}only\text{-}frontier\text{-}lower}\), for the fixed model constants there exist \(c_0>0\) and \(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\) and every \(m\),
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_0\rho_n(a,s).
\tag{1}
\]
No strict target-density slack is used in (1): the subexperiments proving that lemma have the common target density \(f_T=1\), so \(g_T=1\), and the declared ranges \(c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+}\) permit either or both target-density bounds to be attained.

By \(\mathrm{ass:sample\text{-}balance}\), \(m/n\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\). Therefore there are constants \(c_\kappa,C_\kappa>0\) and \(n_1\) such that
\[
c_\kappa n\le m\le C_\kappa n,
\qquad n\ge n_1.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular,
\[
m^{-1/2}\le c_\kappa^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(a<1\), \(\rho_n(a,s)=n^{-1/2}\). For \(a=1\),
\[
\rho_n(1,s)=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\ge n^{-1/2}
\tag{4}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). For \(a>1\), the conditions \(a>1\) and \(s>0\) give
\[
\frac{s+1}{2s+a+1}<\frac12,
\qquad
\rho_n(a,s)=n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}\ge n^{-1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (3)--(5) imply that, for some finite constant \(C_0\),
\[
m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\le C_0\rho_n(a,s)
\tag{6}
\]
eventually along the sequence. Combining (1) and (6) yields
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
\ge \frac{c_0}{C_0}\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Expanding the three branches of \(\rho_n(a,s)\) in (7) proves the displayed lower phase diagram.

It remains to verify the benchmark assertions. The corrected
\(\mathrm{lem:oracle\text{-}truncation\text{-}envelope}\) gives, uniformly over the model class,
\[
|\mathfrak b_{h_n}(\mu;P)|\le b_{h_n}^{\circ}(P)\le C h_n^{s+1}
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}
+n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}
\asymp m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s).
\tag{9}
\]
That lemma also proves that the deterministic allowance in (8) is class-sharp for every fixed \(a>0\), while giving the special equal-density case at \(a=0\) in which the actual bias and its allowance both vanish. Thus neither (8) nor (9) is a per-law lower equivalence for actual bias.

Finally, \(G_{h_n}^{\circ}(P)\), \(q_{h_n}^{\circ}(P)\), and hence \(\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\) depend on the population law. The benchmark is therefore an oracle population calculation, not a map measurable with respect to \(\mathcal D_{n,m}\). Equations (7)--(9) establish a one-sided minimax lower phase diagram and a boundary-corrected oracle linear-weight benchmark, but no data-driven minimax upper bound or frontier-attainment conclusion. \(\square\)
