\textbf{Proof.}
Assume \(a=0\).  The density-factor definitions give, for every \(x\in(0,1]\),
\[
 f_S(x)=x^a g_S(x)=g_S(x),
 \qquad
 f_T(x)=g_T(x),
 \qquad
 r(x)=x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}.
\]
The source- and target-boundary-factor assumptions state, respectively,
\[
 c_{S,-}\le g_S(x)\le c_{S,+}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 c_{T,-}\le g_T(x)\le c_{T,+},
 \qquad x\in[0,1].
\]
In particular, \(g_S(x)\ge c_{S,-}>0\), so the displayed division is valid on the declared domain of \(r\).  Dividing the lower target bound by the upper source bound and the upper target bound by the lower source bound yields
\[
 \frac{c_{T,-}}{c_{S,+}}
 \le r(x)=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}
 \le \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}},
 \qquad 0<x\le1.
\]

Because \(a=0<1\), the first branch of the definition of \(h_n\) applies.  Hence
\[
 h_n=n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}.
\]
The model fixes \(s>0\), so \(s+1>0\), and exponent arithmetic gives
\[
 h_n^{s+1}
 =\left(n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}\right)^{s+1}
 =n^{-1/2}.
\]
By the Riesz-threshold lemma in its \(a<1\) branch, as \(n\to\infty\),
\[
 \int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\asymp1,
\]
because \(h_n\downarrow0\).  In particular,
\[
 \int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx=O(1),
\]
with a constant depending only on the fixed source and target boundary-factor constants.

It remains to prove positivity of the actual capped downstream weight.  Fix a fold \(k\) for which both interior training measures have positive total mass.  This is one of the two positive-mass optimization branches in the definition of the executable RiCOT weights, so it is not the deterministic zero fallback.  Positive mass of \(\widehat P^{T,(-k)}_{h_n}\) implies that at least one target training index \(j\in\mathcal T^T_k\) satisfies \(X_j^T\ge h_n\).  For each \(x\in[h_n,1]\), every exponential summand in the raw KKT continuation is strictly positive, and at least one such summand is present.  Consequently,
\[
 \frac1{|\mathcal T^T_k|}
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal T^T_k}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h_n\}
 \exp\!\left\{-\frac{c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,X_j^T)
 +\widehat\theta_{h_n,J_{r,n}}^{(-k)\top}
 \widetilde{\mathbf b}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)
 +\widehat\psi^{(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n},j}}{\eta_0}\right\}>0.
\]
Here all quantities in the exponent are finite by the finite-dimensional branch construction, \(\eta_0>0\), and \(|\mathcal T^T_k|>0\) because its empirical interior measure has positive mass.  Since \(\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)>0\), the raw continuation therefore obeys
\[
 \widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)>0,
 \qquad x\in[h_n,1].
\]
The cap in the same definition is also strictly positive.  Indeed, at \(a=0\),
\[
 \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}h_n^{-a}
 =\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}>0.
\]
Taking the minimum of these two strictly positive numbers proves
\[
 \widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)
 =\min\!\left\{
 \widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x),
 \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}
 \right\}>0,
 \qquad x\in[h_n,1].
\]
This argument uses neither the numerical restrictions on \(s\) and \(t\) nor any stochastic norm or residual bound; those restrictions remain part of the proposition's stated regime.  The conclusions proved here are deterministic identities and branchwise positivity only.  They imply no empirical norm bound, no nonzero realized residual sum, and no root-risk bound. \(\square\)
