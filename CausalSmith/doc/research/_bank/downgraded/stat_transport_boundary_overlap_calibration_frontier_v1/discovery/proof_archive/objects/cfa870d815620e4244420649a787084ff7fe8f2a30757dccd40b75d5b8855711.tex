\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(n,m\ge2\), \(h\in(0,1)\), and a law in the model class. The positive ridges in \(\mathrm{def:regression\text{-}fit}\) make \(\widehat G_{k,h}\) and \(\widehat Q_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}\) invertible. The displayed formula for \(\widehat\alpha_{k,h}\), and the first-order equation for the unique interior ridge minimizer, give respectively
\[
\mathbf R_h(x)^\top\widehat\alpha_{k,h}
=\sum_{\ell=1}^nL_{k\ell}^{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)Z_\ell,
\qquad 0\le x<h,
\]
and
\[
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)^\top\widehat\beta_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}
=\sum_{\ell=1}^nL_{k\ell}^{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)Z_\ell,
\qquad h\le x\le1.
\]
Therefore, on both pieces of the splice,
\[
\widehat\mu_{h,J_{\mu,n}}^{(-k)}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^nL_{k\ell}^{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)Z_\ell.
\tag{1}
\]
Substitute (1) into the unchanged estimator in \(\mathrm{def:spliced\text{-}estimator}\). The target plug-in term contributes
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^n\left\{\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mL_{k_T(j),\ell}^{h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_j^T)\right\}Z_\ell.
\]
The direct interior residual term contributes the second summand in the definition of \(\Gamma_\ell\), and expansion of its fitted-regression subtraction contributes the third summand. Collecting the coefficient of each \(Z_\ell\) proves
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}
=\sum_{\ell=1}^n\Gamma_\ell(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})Z_\ell.
\tag{2}
\]
This is a purely algebraic identity and uses no calibration equation for the capped downstream weight.

By the definition of the known randomization probability and the two observed outcome regressions,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P_S}(Z\mid X=x)
&=\frac{P_S(A=1\mid X=x)}{e(x)}\mathbb E_{P_S}(Y\mid A=1,X=x)\\
&\quad-\frac{P_S(A=0\mid X=x)}{1-e(x)}\mathbb E_{P_S}(Y\mid A=0,X=x)\\
&=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)=\mu(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Treatment positivity justifies both divisions. Since \(0\le Y\le1\) and \(\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\varepsilon\), conditional on \(X=x\), the support of \(Z\) is contained in \([-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon^{-1}]\).

Add and subtract \(\sum_i\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)\) and \(m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)\) in (2). This gives the stated three-term decomposition exactly. The two Hölder assumptions and the triangle inequality for the Hölder norm give
\[
\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M),
\qquad \|\mu\|_\infty\le1.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus the absolute value of the middle term is at most \(B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\), deterministically for every realized covariate design.

All coefficients \(\Gamma_i\) are measurable with respect to the source and target covariates: the regression smoother matrices use covariates only, and the selected capped RiCOT weights in \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}weights}\) use covariates only. Conditional on these covariates, the source observations remain independent and (3) centers their pseudo-outcomes. The conditional support interval of \(\Gamma_i Z_i\) has length at most \(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\). Hoeffding's inequality therefore yields, for every \(u>0\),
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left(
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right|>u
\,\middle|\,X_1,\ldots,X_n,X_1^T,\ldots,X_m^T
\right)
\le2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\varepsilon^2u^2}{2\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i^2}\right\},
\tag{5}
\]
with the probability equal to zero when \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2=0\). Taking
\[
u=\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i^2}
\]
makes the right side of (5) equal to \(\gamma/2\).

Independently of (5), the target variables \(\mu(X_j^T)\) are i.i.d., have mean \(\tau\), and lie in \([-1,1]\). A second application of Hoeffding's inequality gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left{
\left|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|
>\sqrt{\frac{2\log(4/\gamma)}m}
\right}\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{6}
\]
The union bound applied to (4)--(6) proves the probability statement.

Finally, the Hölder class intersected with the unit sup-norm ball is separable in the uniform norm, and the displayed evaluation functional is continuous in that norm. Hence its supremum equals the supremum over a fixed countable dense subset, so \(B_X\) is measurable and observable. If a realized design satisfies \(B_X\le b\) and \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\le g^2\), the displayed radius is at most
\[
b+\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)}g+\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)/m}.
\]
This is the asserted realized-design implication and makes no stochastic order claim for \(b\) or \(g\). \(\square\)
