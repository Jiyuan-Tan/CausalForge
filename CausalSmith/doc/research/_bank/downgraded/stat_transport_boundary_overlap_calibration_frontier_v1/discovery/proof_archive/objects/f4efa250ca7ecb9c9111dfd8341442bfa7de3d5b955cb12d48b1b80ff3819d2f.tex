\textbf{Proof.}
When \(a=0\), the definitions give \(f_S=g_S\), \(f_T=g_T\), and \(r=g_T/g_S\). The two boundary-factor assumptions therefore imply, for every \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\frac{c_{T,-}}{c_{S,+}}\le r(x)\le\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}.
\]
The definition of the regime bandwidth gives
\[
h_n=n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}},
\qquad
h_n^{s+1}=n^{-1/2}.
\]
The already-established Riesz-threshold lemma, specialized to \(a=0<1\), gives uniformly
\[
\int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx=O(1).
\]
It remains only to check the pointwise positivity assertion. If both interior training measures of a fold are nonzero, every term retained by the indicator in the displayed dual-extension formula is a positive exponential, the sum contains at least one such term, and the exponent \(\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)\) is strictly positive. Hence
\[
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)>0
\qquad\text{for every }x\in[h_n,1].
\]
This proves exactly the deterministic assertions. Pointwise positivity neither bounds the empirical squared norm nor prevents cancellation in the realized weighted residual sum, so no risk conclusion follows from this proposition. \(\square\)
