\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(b_J=\mathbf b_{h,J}\).  Since \(f_S(x)=x^ag_S(x)\), the population matrix shared by the weighted regression and ratio fits is
\[
 G_J=\mathbb E_{P_S}\{\mathbf 1(X\ge h)X^{-a}b_J(X)b_J(X)^\top\}
 =\int_h^1b_J(x)b_J(x)^\top g_S(x)\,dx.
\tag{1}
\]
The normalized-spline stability lemma and \(c_{S,-}\le g_S\le c_{S,+}\) give
\[
 cJ^{-1}\|v\|_2^2\le v^\top G_Jv\le CJ^{-1}\|v\|_2^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Every normalized degree-\(q\) B-spline is bounded by one, and at most \(q+1\) coordinates are nonzero at a point.  A matrix summand in the empirical version of (1) therefore has norm at most \(Ch^{-a}\).  If its support meets a knot cell \(I\), its matrix second moment is bounded by
\[
 C\int_Ix^{-2a}f_S(x)\,dx
 =C\int_Ix^{-a}g_S(x)\,dx
 \le C W_a(h,J).
\tag{3}
\]
For the leftmost cell and \(0<a<1\), if \(hJ\to0\), direct integration gives
\[
 W_a(h,J)\asymp\int_h^{h+C/J}x^{-a}\,dx\asymp J^{a-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
This is the boundary-cell variance calculation omitted by the rejected argument.

Because the Gram matrix is banded with a number of nonzero diagonals depending only on \(q\), applying the matrix Bernstein inequality to each overlapping block and taking a union bound gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{\|\widehat G_J-G_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}>c/(2J)\}
 \le CJ\exp\!\left[-c\min\left\{
 \frac{n}{J^2W_a(h,J)},\frac{nh^a}{J}
 \right\}\right].
\tag{5}
\]
The first exponent is the variance term from (3); the second is the envelope term.  Since \(W_a(h,J)\le V_a(h)\), the two stability caps in \(\mathsf J_u(h)\) make both exponents in (5) at least \(c\log^3(en)\).  For the boundary local-polynomial Gram, bounded summands and
\[
 \mathbb E\{\mathbf 1(X\le2h)\mathbf R_h(X)\mathbf R_h(X)^\top\}
 =h^{a+1}\int_0^2u^ag_S(hu)\mathbf R_1(u)\mathbf R_1(u)^\top\,du
\tag{6}
\]
give lower and upper eigenvalues comparable to \(h^{a+1}\), and Bernstein gives failure probability \(C\exp(-cnh^{a+1})\).  At every declared \(h_n\), \(nh_n^{a+1}\) is a positive power of \(n\), up to logarithms.  Fixed-block size deviations are exponentially unlikely.  Equations (5)--(6), for \(J_{\mu,n}\) and \(J_{r,n}\), prove
\[
 \sup_{\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)}
 \mathbb P_{n,m}\{\mathcal E_{n,m}(h_n)^c\}
 \le C\exp\{-c\log^3(en)\}.
\tag{7}
\]

The factor bounds and the Hölder algebra for a positive denominator imply \(q_0=g_T/g_S\in\mathcal H^t(C_q)\), where \(C_q\) is a computable function of \(t,M,c_{S,-},c_{S,+},c_{T,+}\).  Let \(q_{0,J}\) be its spline approximant.  Then
\[
 \|q_0-q_{0,J}\|_\infty\le CJ^{-t}.
\tag{8}
\]
The source normal-equation perturbation has squared induced \(L^2(g_S)\) size at most \(CJ V_a(h)\log(en)/n\), because summing the coordinate variances in (3) over the locally supported basis gives \(CV_a(h)/n\) before multiplication by the inverse Gram.  The target moment has corresponding size \(CJ\log(em)/m\).  Equations (2), (5), and (8), followed by the ridge resolvent identity, therefore yield
\[
 \|\widehat q^{\mathrm{DS}}-q_0\|_{L^2(g_S;[h,1])}
 \le C\left\{J^{-t}+\sqrt{\frac{JV_a(h)\log(en)}n}
 +\sqrt{\frac{J\log(em)}m}+\lambda_nJ\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Projection onto \([q_-,q_+]\) cannot increase pointwise error because \(q_0\) lies in this interval.  The identical score calculation for weighted least-squares regression, using \(\mathbb E(Z\mid X)=\mu(X)\) and bounded \(Z\), gives
\[
 \|\widehat\mu-\mu\|_{L^2(g_S;[h,1])}
 \le C\{\delta_{s,h}+\lambda_nJ_{\mu,n}\}.
\tag{10}
\]

Finally, on every branch
\[
 q_-x^{-a}\le\widehat r^{\mathrm{DS}}_{h,J}(x)\le q_+x^{-a}.
\tag{11}
\]
Conditional on the training blocks, scalar Bernstein on the independent source validation block and the boundary-factor bounds give
\[
 \frac1{N^S_1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal V^S_1}
 \widehat r^{\mathrm{DS}}_{h,J}(X_i)^2\mathbf1(X_i\ge h)=O_P\{V_a(h)\},
\tag{12}
\]
and, using the lower inequality in (11), the denominator is bounded below by \(cN^S_1V_a(h)\) with probability tending to one.  The largest squared term is at most \(q_+^2h^{-2a}\).  Hence the leverage ratio is bounded by
\[
 O_P\!\left(\frac{h^{-2a}}{nV_a(h)}\right)
 =O_P\!\left[
 \frac{h^{-2a}}n\mathbf1\{a<1\}
 +\frac1{nh^2\log(1/h)}\mathbf1\{a=1\}
 +\frac1{nh^{a+1}}\mathbf1\{a>1\}
 \right],
\tag{13}
\]
which tends to zero at \(h=h_n\).  This proves all claims. \(\square\)
