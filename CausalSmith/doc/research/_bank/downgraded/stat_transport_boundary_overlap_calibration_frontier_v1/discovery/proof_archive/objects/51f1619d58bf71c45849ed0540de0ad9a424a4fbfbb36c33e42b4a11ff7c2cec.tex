\textbf{Proof.}
For a univariate \(C^{p,s-p}\) Hölder ball, Taylor's theorem implies every pairwise jet inequality imposed by the program. Thus every admissible function produces a feasible vector and the program is an upper bound. Conversely, on an interval of mesh diameter \(\delta\), two functions with the same mesh jets can change the linear functional by at most
\[
2M\{1+\sum_i|\Gamma_i|\}\delta^s.
\]
Piecewise Taylor extension of a feasible one-dimensional jet attains the limiting constraints. Hence the displayed refinement correction is valid, decreases to zero, and at the stopping mesh is at most \(n^{-2}\). This proves both computability and
\[
B_X\le\overline B_X\le B_X+n^{-2}.
\tag{4}
\]

For the expectation, decompose the functional defining \(B_X\) at the boundary polynomial and the interior \(L^2(g_S)\) projection, exactly as in the localization proof, now uniformly over \(f\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\). Boundary Taylor remainders contribute \(Ch^{s+1}\), and the projection-product identity contributes \(CJ^{-(s+t)}\). The target empirical process indexed by \(\mathcal H^s(2M)\) contributes \(Cm^{-1/2}\). For the weighted source process, conditional symmetrization and the coefficient bound give scale \(Cn^{-1/2}V_a(h)^{1/2}\). Its entropy integral is finite because, in one dimension,
\[
\int_0^1\sqrt{\log N(\epsilon,\mathcal H^s(2M),\|\cdot\|_\infty)}\,d\epsilon
\le C\int_0^1\epsilon^{-1/(2s)}\,d\epsilon<\infty
\]
when \(s>1/2\). The Gram perturbation terms obey the same bound by the resolvent calculation in the localization lemma. Taking expectations, using (4), and adding the mesh tolerance proves the result. \(\square\)
