\textbf{Proof.}
Equip the observed-data space of \(\mathcal D_{n,m}\) with its product Borel \(\sigma\)-field.  An interval-valued statistic must, at a minimum, be a single-valued measurable map
\[
T_{n,m}:\mathcal D_{n,m}\longmapsto [L_{n,m}(\mathcal D_{n,m}),U_{n,m}(\mathcal D_{n,m})],
\qquad L_{n,m}\le U_{n,m},
\]
where the two endpoints are specified functions of the observations.

Inspect the complete construction in \(\mathrm{def:adaptive\text{-}handle}\).  It gives only the following instructions: use adjacent-scale local-polynomial differences, estimate \(a\) from empirical shell masses, combine those quantities with calibration residuals, and select one member of a dyadic family of intervals \(\mathcal I_{h,J}\).  It gives no formula mapping the data to an estimate of \(a\) or of the boundary regularity, no numerical thresholds for any adjacent-scale comparison, no rule mapping the resulting diagnostics to a dyadic \(h\), and no convention selecting one candidate when two or more candidates satisfy an intended comparison.  In particular, the construction supplies neither endpoint functions \(L_{n,m},U_{n,m}\) nor an equivalent measurable index map
\[
\widehat\lambda_{n,m}:\mathcal D_{n,m}\longmapsto
\{(h,\lceil h^{-1}\rceil):h\text{ is an admissible dyadic scale}\}
\]
for which \(T_{n,m}(\mathcal D_{n,m})=\mathcal I_{\widehat\lambda_{n,m}}\).

This is a failure of definition, not a probabilistic remainder.  The boundary self-similarity restriction constrains member laws and cannot choose a value of \(\widehat\lambda_{n,m}\) for a realized data set.  Hence the frozen text is compatible with distinct completions, for example completions that choose different dyadic candidates whenever more than one passes an unspecified comparison, and it singles out none of them.  Therefore it does not define a single interval-valued statistic.

Because no such random interval has been defined, neither
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\{\tau\in T_{n,m}(\mathcal D_{n,m})\}
\qquad\text{nor}\qquad
\mathbb E_{n,m}\!\left[U_{n,m}(\mathcal D_{n,m})-L_{n,m}(\mathcal D_{n,m})\right]
\]
is an evaluable quantity attached to \(\mathrm{def:adaptive\text{-}handle}\).  Consequently no adaptive coverage or expected-length guarantee follows from that construction.  Specifying measurable diagnostics, their thresholds, a single-valued selection map, and deterministic tie breaking is logically prior to applying self-similarity in an adaptation proof.  This proves the claim. \(\square\)
