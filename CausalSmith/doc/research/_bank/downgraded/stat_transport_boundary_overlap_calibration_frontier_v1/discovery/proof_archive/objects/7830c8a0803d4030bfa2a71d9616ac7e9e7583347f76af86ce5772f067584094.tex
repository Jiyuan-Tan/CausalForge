\textbf{Proof.}
The coverage hypothesis means that there is a deterministic sequence \(r_{n,m}\downarrow0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n,m\), every law in the displayed union has coverage at least \(1-\gamma-r_{n,m}\).

We first derive the interval version of the two-point inequality.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) have parameter values separated by \(\Delta>0\).  An interval containing both values has length at least \(\Delta\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
&\ge\Delta Q_0\{\tau(Q_0),\tau(Q_1)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}\\
&\ge\Delta\bigl[Q_0\{\tau(Q_0)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}
+Q_1\{\tau(Q_1)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}
-1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\bigr]\\
&\ge\Delta\{1-2\gamma-2r_{n,m}-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
The second inequality uses \(Q_0(B)\ge Q_1(B)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  By (16), a sufficiently small constant relative entropy makes the final factor in (35) bounded below by a positive constant because \(\gamma<1/2\).

We construct the two comparisons around one smoother reference law.  Put
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1,
\qquad
\ell(x)=x-\frac12.
\tag{36}
\]
Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\).  Use randomized Bernoulli potential outcomes with
\[
\mu_0(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x),
\tag{37}
\]
make treatment conditionally independent of the potential outcomes given \(X\), impose consistency, and give target potential outcomes the same conditional means.  The constant density factors in (36) belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every fixed \(t>0\).  Since a linear function is in every fixed-order H\"older class, reducing \(\delta_0\) puts (37) strictly inside both radius-\(M\) response balls, keeps the means in \([0,1]\), and makes the Bernoulli residual variances at least \(M^{-1}\).  Denote the resulting observed law by \(Q_0\).  Then
\[
Q_0\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)
\cap\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t).
\tag{38}
\]

Let
\[
h=n^{-1/(2s_1+a+1)},
\qquad
b_{h,s_1}(x)=h^{s_1}K(x/h)\mathbf 1\{0\le x\le h\}.
\tag{39}
\]
Construct \(Q_1\) by changing only the treated source and target conditional mean to
\[
\mu_{1,1}(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x)+\delta b_{h,s_1}(x).
\tag{40}
\]
The scaling calculation (4), now at order \(s_1\), and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\) put \(Q_1\) in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t)\).  The baseline Bernoulli probabilities in (37) stay uniformly separated from zero and one.  Taylor's theorem for Bernoulli relative entropy, followed by (36) and (39), therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
\le Cn\delta^2h^{2s_1+a+1}
=C\delta^2,
\tag{41}
\]
whereas
\[
\Delta_S:=|\tau(Q_1)-\tau(Q_0)|
=\delta h^{s_1+1}\int_0^1K(u)\,du
\ge c_Sn^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\tag{42}
\]
Reduce \(\delta\) until (16) makes \(\operatorname{TV}(Q_1,Q_0)\) smaller than a fixed number strictly below \(1-2\gamma\).  Equations (35), (41), and (42) then imply
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
\ge c_1n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\tag{43}
\]

For the target comparison, retain the complete source law and the responses (37), and change only the target density to
\[
f_{T,2}(x)=1+u_m\ell(x),
\qquad
u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\tag{44}
\]
The equality \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), strict target-density slack, and polynomial smoothness make (44) admissible for every fixed \(t>0\) after choosing a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta_1>0\).  Let \(Q_2\) be the resulting law, with target potential outcomes chosen to preserve mean transportability.  Then \(Q_2\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\).  The source laws under \(Q_2\) and \(Q_0\) are identical, and the reference target density is one, so
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_2,Q_0)
\le mu_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
\le C\delta_1^2,
\tag{45}
\]
while
\[
\Delta_T:=|\tau(Q_2)-\tau(Q_0)|
=\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
=\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{12}m^{-1/2}.
\tag{46}
\]
Reduce \(\delta_1\) until \(\operatorname{TV}(Q_2,Q_0)<1-2\gamma\) by (16).  Applying (35) to \(Q_0,Q_2\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
\ge c_2m^{-1/2}.
\tag{47}
\]
Both (43) and (47) hold at the same smoother law \(Q_0\).  Since \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\), they imply
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
\ge c\left\{m^{-1/2}+n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}\right\}.
\]
Thus honest adaptation across the two H\"older classes forces the rough-class boundary rate at a smoother law.  No numerical lower bound on \(t\) and no product-smoothness condition was used. \(\square\)
