\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\mathcal C=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_n,X_1^T,\ldots,X_m^T)\) be the sigma-field generated by the realized source and target covariates.  We first establish the exact linear identity.  The positive ridge in each boundary Gram matrix gives the normal-equation identity
\[
\widehat\alpha_{k,h}
=\widehat G_{k,h}^{-1}\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_k^S|}
\sum_{q_0\in\mathcal T_k^S}\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\le 2h\}\mathbf R_h(X_{q_0})Z_{q_0}.
\]
Likewise, differentiating the strictly convex interior ridge criterion gives
\[
\widehat\beta_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}
=\widehat H_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}^{-1}\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_k^S|}
\sum_{q_0\in\mathcal T_k^S}\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\ge h\}
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_{q_0})Z_{q_0}.
\]
The inverses exist because both matrices contain a positive multiple of the identity.  Substitution into the two branches of the spliced regression fit, followed by the definition of \(L_{q_0,k,h,J_{\mu,n}}\), yields, for every \(k\) and \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\widehat\mu^{(-k)}_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)
=\sum_{q_0=1}^nL_{q_0,k,h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)Z_{q_0}.
\tag{1}
\]
Now insert (1) into all fitted-regression occurrences in the estimator of \(\mathrm{def:spliced\text{-}estimator}\) and collect the coefficient of a fixed \(Z_{q_0}\).  That coefficient is
\[
\begin{aligned}
{}&\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mL_{q_0,k_T(j),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_j^T)
+\frac1n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(q_0))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_{q_0})
\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\ge h\}\\
&\quad-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)
\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}L_{q_0,k_S(i),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_i),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is exactly \(\Gamma_{q_0}(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\) by \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\).  Therefore
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}
=\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})Z_i.
\tag{2}
\]
Every ridge matrix and every branch-qualified transport program used in these coefficients depends only on the observed covariates; the empty-interior fallback is deterministic, and the specified optimizer, multiplier selection, continuation, and cap are measurable.  Hence each \(\Gamma_i\) is \(\mathcal C\)-measurable.

We next identify the conditional mean of the pseudo-outcome.  By consistency, conditional trial randomization, and the known propensity, for \(P_S\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P_S}(Z\mid X=x)
&=\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}(AY\mid X=x)}{e(x)}
-\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}\{(1-A)Y\mid X=x\}}{1-e(x)}\\
&=\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(1)\mid X=x\}
-\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(0)\mid X=x\}\\
&=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)=\mu(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The two divisions are valid because treatment positivity gives \(e(x)\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\).  Adding and subtracting \(\sum_i\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)\) and \(m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)\) in (2), and using the definition \(\tau=\mathbb E_{P_T}\mu(X^T)\), proves the displayed exact error decomposition.

It remains to prove the finite-sample probability bound.  By subadditivity of the Hoelder norm and the two response-smoothness assumptions,
\[
\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M).
\]
Because \(0\le\mu_d\le1\), also \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\).  Thus \(\mu\) is feasible in the supremum defining \(B_X\), and consequently
\[
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)\right|
\le B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}).
\tag{4}
\]

The product experiment in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) makes the source observations independent, and therefore makes \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\) conditionally independent given \(\mathcal C\).  Equation (3) gives their conditional means.  Since \(0\le Y\le1\) and \(e(X)\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\),
\[
-\varepsilon^{-1}\le Z_i\le\varepsilon^{-1}.
\]
Consequently, conditional on \(\mathcal C\), the centered summand \(\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\) has range length at most \(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\).  The conditional form of \(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\), applied with \(\delta=\gamma/2\), gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right|>R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})
\,\middle|\,\mathcal C\right\}\le\frac\gamma2
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the squared-range sum in that application is \(4\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\), which gives exactly the stated \(R_S\).  Taking expectations preserves (5).

Independently, the target observations are i.i.d., \(\mathbb E_{P_T}\mu(X^T)=\tau\), and \(\mu(X^T)\in[-1,1]\).  The variables \(m^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\) are centered and have range length \(2/m\).  A second application of the same Hoeffding lemma with \(\delta=\gamma/2\) therefore gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|>R_T(m)
\right\}\le\frac\gamma2,
\tag{6}
\]
because the squared-range sum is \(4/m\).

Combine the exact decomposition with (4)--(6) and use the union bound.  The result is
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}-\tau\right|
\le B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})+R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})+R_T(m)
\right\}\ge1-\gamma.
\]
Finally, on a realized design with \(B_X\le b\) and \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\le g^2\), the definitions of the two radii imply
\[
B_X+R_S+R_T
\le b+\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)}\,g
+\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)/m}.
\]
This argument uses only exact algebra, the fixed-class Hoelder envelope, and bounded-sum concentration; it establishes none of the stochastic-order or minimax-attainment properties excluded by the statement. \(\square\)
