\textbf{Proof.}
We construct full-data extensions and verify membership in the corrected model before computing the two separations. Put
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1,
\qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
These are probability densities on the declared support. Their nonsingular factors are \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\). The declared ranges \(c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+}\) and \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\) give all density-factor bounds, while \(M\ge a+1\) gives the required radius bound. Both factors are constant, so they belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every fixed \(t>0\). Thus neither \(t>1/2\) nor a product-smoothness inequality is needed for this construction.

Use the fixed known propensity \(e\), draw \(A\mid X=x\) as \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(e(x))\), and make \(A\) conditionally independent of the potential outcomes given \(X\). Conditional on \(X=x\), take Bernoulli potential outcomes with means
\[
\mu_{0,+}(x)=\mu_{0,-}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\tag{2}
\]
Give the target potential outcomes the same conditional means. By the scaling identity in \(\mathrm{def:boundary\text{-}bump}\), the family \(b_h(x)=h^sK(x/h)\mathbf 1\{x\le h\}\) has uniformly bounded \(\mathcal H^s\)-norm for \(0<h\le1\). Choose the fixed number \(\delta>0\) small enough that (2) takes values in \([1/4,3/4]\), both response functions belong to \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), and
\[
\frac14-\delta^2\ge M^{-1}.
\tag{3}
\]
Such a choice exists because \(M>4\). Set \(Y=Y^S(A)\). This verifies consistency, conditional trial randomization, the known-propensity design, treatment positivity, mean transportability, response smoothness, bounded support, and residual nondegeneracy. Thus both induced observed laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\). All density normalizations, support conditions, and boundary-factor bounds have now been checked explicitly; no density ratio is evaluated at \(x=0\).

For \(|u|\le\delta\le1/4\), the elementary Bernoulli bound
\[
\operatorname{kl}\!\left(\operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
\operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)\right)
\le \frac{4u^2}{1/4-u^2}
\le C_0u^2
\tag{4}
\]
follows from \(\operatorname{kl}(p,q)\le(p-q)^2/\{q(1-q)\}\). The source alternatives differ only in the treated conditional outcome distribution. Tensorization, (4), and \(e(x)\le1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,n},P_{-,h}^{S,n})
&\le C_0n\delta^2\int_0^h b_h(x)^2(a+1)x^a\,dx\\
&=C_0(a+1)\delta^2nh^{2s+a+1}
  \int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du\\
&\le Cnh^{2s+a+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The target covariate law is common to these alternatives. Since \(K\ge0\) is nonzero, \(k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0\), and hence
\[
\left|\tau(\mathbb P_{+,h})-\tau(\mathbb P_{-,h})\right|
=2\delta\int_0^hb_h(x)\,dx
=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{6}
\]
This proves the source part. The multiplier \(\delta\) may be chosen arbitrarily smaller while preserving every membership check; consequently the constant on the right of (5) may, when needed, be made arbitrarily small.

For the independent target-sample witness, retain the source density in (1), let \(\ell(x)=x-1/2\), and choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\). Keep the entire source law common, with Bernoulli potential-outcome means
\[
\mu_0(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x),
\tag{7}
\]
and put
\[
f_{T,\pm}(x)=1\pm u_m\ell(x),
\qquad
u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Because \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), (8) gives two densities. The strict slack \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\) permits a fixed \(\delta_1>0\) for which both densities obey the target bounds for every \(m\ge1\). The function \(\ell\) is smooth, so reducing \(\delta_0,\delta_1\) if necessary verifies the order-\(t\) density-factor condition for every fixed \(t>0\), the order-\(s\) response conditions, the outcome-support condition, and (3). Assign target potential outcomes the conditional means in (7). This supplies admissible full-data extensions and verifies mean transportability.

Since \(f_{T,-}\) is bounded away from zero, the inequality \(\operatorname{KL}(p,q)\le\int(p-q)^2/q\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{KL}(P_{T,+}^{\otimes m},P_{T,-}^{\otimes m})
&\le m\int_0^1
 \frac{\{f_{T,+}(x)-f_{T,-}(x)\}^2}{f_{T,-}(x)}\,dx\\
&\le C_1mu_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
\le C_2\delta_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Finally, \(\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx=1/12\), so
\[
|\tau_+-\tau_-|
=2\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
=\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{6}m^{-1/2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (9)--(10) establish the target part, including its full-data extension. \(\square\)
