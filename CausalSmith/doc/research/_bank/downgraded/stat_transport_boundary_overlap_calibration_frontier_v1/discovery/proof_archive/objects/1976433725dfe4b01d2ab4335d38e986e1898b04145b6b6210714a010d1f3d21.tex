\textbf{Proof.}
We construct explicit subexperiments contained in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\).  Their source and target density factors are constant, so the construction satisfies every declared density smoothness condition for every \(t>0\); in particular, the two displayed restrictions involving \(s\) and \(t\) are not needed by the testing calculation itself.

We first record the elementary testing inequality used in all three regimes.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two observed-data laws with parameter values \(\tau_0,\tau_1\), and put \(d=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\).  Given any estimator \(\widehat\tau\), define a test by choosing the nearer of \(\tau_0\) and \(\tau_1\).  A testing error under \(Q_u\) implies \(|\widehat\tau-\tau_u|\ge d/2\).  Since the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\),
\[
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}
       [ (\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2]
 \ge \frac{d^2}{8}
       \{1-\|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, data processing of relative entropy by the indicator of a measurable set, followed by the binary calculation below, gives Pinsker's inequality.  If the set has probabilities \(p\) and \(q\), then
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \ge p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}.
\]
For fixed \(q\), subtracting \(2(p-q)^2\) from the right-hand side yields a function which vanishes with zero first derivative at \(p=q\) and has second derivative
\[
 \frac1{p(1-p)}-4\ge0.
\]
Taking the supremum over measurable sets therefore gives
\[
 \|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^2
 \le \frac12\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus a pair with sufficiently small constant Kullback--Leibler divergence has root mean-squared risk bounded below by a constant multiple of its parameter separation.

For every regime, take
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{3}
\]
These functions integrate to one, and their nonsingular factors are \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\).  The parameter ranges imply
\[
 c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
 \qquad c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+}.
\tag{4}
\]
Both factors are constant and hence belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\), because \(M\ge a+1\).  In particular, (3)--(4) use no strict inequality in either target-density bound.

Draw \(A\) according to the fixed known propensity \(e(X)\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), let the two source potential outcomes be independent Bernoulli variables with the arm-specific means specified below, independently of \(A\); give the target potential outcomes the same conditional means.  Finally set \(Y=Y^S(A)\).  These choices satisfy \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(\mathrm{ass:trial\text{-}randomization}\), \(\mathrm{ass:known\text{-}propensity}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}transportability}\).  Because \(M>4\),
\[
 \Delta_M:=\sqrt{\frac14-\frac1M}>0.
\]
All perturbation constants below are chosen small enough that each conditional mean differs from \(1/2\) by at most \(\Delta_M/2\).  The outcomes then lie in \([0,1]\), and, for either treatment arm,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)
 \ge \frac14-\frac{\Delta_M^2}{4}
 >\frac1M.
\tag{5}
\]
The control regression is constant, and the scaling arguments below place every treated regression in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\).  Thus all remaining member conditions hold.  The target covariate law in (3) is common to all alternatives, so the \(m\) target observations contribute zero divergence for every \(m\).

\emph{The nonregular case \(a>1\).}
For \(h\in(0,1)\) and a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), let
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\tag{6}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:boundary\text{-}bump}\), dilation multiplies a derivative of order \(v\le\lfloor s\rfloor\) by \(h^{s-v}\) and preserves the order-\(s\) H\"older seminorm up to the fixed norm of \(K\).  Reducing \(\delta\) if necessary therefore puts both regressions in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), uniformly in \(h\).

For \(|u|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, direct calculation and Taylor's theorem give
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12+u\right),
  \operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12-u\right)
 \right)
 =2u\log\frac{1+2u}{1-2u}
 \le C_0u^2.
\tag{7}
\]
The control-arm conditional laws agree, while the treated-arm contribution is multiplied by \(e(x)\le1\).  Additivity of relative entropy for product laws, (3), and (7) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,\otimes n},P_{-,h}^{S,\otimes n})
 &\le Cn\int_0^h b_h(x)^2x^a\,dx\\
 &=Cnh^{2s+a+1}\int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Because \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero,
\[
 k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0,
 \qquad
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Choose \(h=c_0n^{-1/(2s+a+1)}\), where \(c_0>0\) is small enough that (8) is bounded by a sufficiently small constant.  Equations (1)--(2) and (9) imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge c n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\tag{10}
\]

\emph{The regular case \(a<1\).}
Choose a nonnegative, nonzero \(C^\infty\) function \(q\) supported in \([1/4,3/4]\), and set
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}q(x).
\tag{11}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), (11) satisfies the same range, variance, and H\"older conditions.  On the support of \(q\), the source density is bounded above and below by positive constants.  Therefore (7) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_+^{S,\otimes n},P_-^{S,\otimes n})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{12}
\]
Under the uniform target density,
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1q(x)\,dx
 \ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
After reducing \(\delta\), equations (1)--(2), (12), and (13) prove
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{14}
\]

\emph{The critical case \(a=1\).}
Put \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), fix an integer \(j_0\ge1\), and choose \(L=L_n\) so that
\[
 \ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}},
 \qquad
 L-j_0+1\asymp\log n.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(j_0\le j\le L\), define
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\}.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{L-j_0+1}\), let
\[
 \mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+
 \sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x).
\tag{17}
\]
The shell interiors are disjoint.  Moreover, (15) implies
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 \le C\ell_j^s,
 \qquad j_0\le j\le L.
\tag{18}
\]
The conventional smooth-bump extension of \(K\) is flat at the endpoints of its support.  Hence each shell piece in (16), extended by zero, has order-\(s\) H\"older norm at most \(C\delta\) by (18).  The dyadic shell separation and endpoint flatness bound the cross-shell H\"older quotients by the same constant.  Thus the sum in (17) has H\"older norm at most \(C'\delta\), independently of \(L\).  Choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small makes every sign alternative a member of the model and enforces (5).

The contribution of coordinate \(j\) to the target parameter is
\[
 d_j:=\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)\,dx
 =\frac{\delta k_1}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(\omega^{(j)}\) is obtained by flipping only coordinate \(j\), then \(f_S(x)=2x\), and (7) implies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{S,\otimes n},
                    P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{S,\otimes n})
 \le Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
\tag{20}
\]
uniformly in \(j\), \(n\), and the other signs.

Place the uniform product prior \(\Pi\) on \(\omega\).  The covariate likelihood is sign-free, and every observed outcome involves at most one shell coordinate.  The likelihood, prior, and therefore the posterior factor across coordinates.  Since conditional expectation minimizes squared loss and \(\tau_\omega=\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j\omega_j\),
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}
 \mathbb E_\Pi[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 =
 \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
       (\tau_\omega\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
   \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
       (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}).
\tag{21}
\]
Write \(q_j=\mathbb E_\Pi(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|.
\tag{22}
\]
Let \(\overline P_{j,+}\) and \(\overline P_{j,-}\) be the mixture experiments conditional on \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\), respectively.  Bayes' formula gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi|q_j|
 =\|\overline P_{j,+}-\overline P_{j,-}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\tag{23}
\]
Pair every sign vector with its \(j\)-flip.  Convexity of relative entropy, equivalently the pointwise log-sum inequality applied to the paired mixture densities, and (20) give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\overline P_{j,+},\overline P_{j,-})
 \le 2^{-(L-j_0)}
 \sum_{\omega:\,\omega_j=1}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega,P_{\omega^{(j)}})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{24}
\]
The target factors cancel in (24) because their law is common.  Equations (2), (22)--(24), and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
   (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 \ge c_1>0
\tag{25}
\]
uniformly in \(j\).  Since the supremum risk dominates the Bayes risk, (15), (19), (21), and (25) give
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 \ge c_1\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
 \ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\tag{26}
\]
Taking square roots proves the critical lower bound \(c\sqrt{\log n/n}\) without a hidden polynomial loss.

The three bounds (10), (14), and (26), together with \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rate}\), prove that, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(m\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\rho_n(a,s).
\]
Every subexperiment used the boundary-attaining target density \(f_T=1\), so no strict target-density slack is required. \(\square\)
