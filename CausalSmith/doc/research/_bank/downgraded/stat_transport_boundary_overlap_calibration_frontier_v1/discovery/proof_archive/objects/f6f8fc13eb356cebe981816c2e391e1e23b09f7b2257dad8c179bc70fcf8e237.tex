\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\mathrm{lem:source\text{-}only\text{-}frontier\text{-}lower}\), for the fixed model constants there exist \(c_0>0\) and \(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\) and every \(m\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_0\rho_n(a,s).
\tag{1}
\]
No strict target-density slack is used in (1): the subexperiments proving that lemma have the common target density \(f_T=1\), so \(g_T=1\), and the declared ranges \(c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+}\) permit either or both target-density bounds to be attained.

By \(\mathrm{ass:sample\text{-}balance}\), \(m/n\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\).  Therefore there are constants \(c_\kappa,C_\kappa>0\) and \(n_1\) such that
\[
 c_\kappa n\le m\le C_\kappa n,
 \qquad n\ge n_1.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular,
\[
 m^{-1/2}\le c_\kappa^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(a<1\), \(\rho_n(a,s)=n^{-1/2}\).  For \(a=1\),
\[
 \rho_n(1,s)=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\ge n^{-1/2}
\tag{4}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  For \(a>1\), the inequality \(a>1\) and the condition \(s>0\) give
\[
 \frac{s+1}{2s+a+1}<\frac12,
 \qquad
 \rho_n(a,s)=n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}\ge n^{-1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (3)--(5) imply that, for some finite constant \(C_0\),
\[
 m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\le C_0\rho_n(a,s)
\tag{6}
\]
eventually along the sequence.  Combining (1) and (6) yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge \frac{c_0}{C_0}\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Expanding the three branches of \(\rho_n(a,s)\) in (7) gives exactly
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\left\{m^{-1/2}+
 \begin{cases}
 n^{-1/2},&a<1,\\
 \sqrt{\log n/n},&a=1,\\
 n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)},&a>1.
 \end{cases}\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]

It remains to verify the benchmark assertion.  Applying \(\mathrm{lem:oracle\text{-}truncation\text{-}envelope}\) uniformly over \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) gives
\[
 m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}+n^{-1/2}
 \left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h_n\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}
 \asymp m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s).
\tag{9}
\]
The norm in (9) is finite because it is truncated at the positive bandwidth \(h_n\); its order follows from the positive source and target boundary-factor bounds.  Thus (8) and (9) establish the lower frontier and the matching deterministic truncated-oracle bias--standard-deviation envelope.  The right-hand side of (9) is a population-law calculation involving \(r=f_T/f_S\); neither (9) nor the lower-bound argument constructs a map measurable with respect to \(\mathcal D_{n,m}\).  Hence no data-driven minimax upper bound or frontier-attainment assertion follows. \(\square\)
