\textbf{Proof.}
Fix arbitrary \(t>0\). We construct the full-data laws explicitly and verify every support, normalization, positivity, smoothness, and variance condition in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).

Put
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1,
\qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
Both functions integrate to one. Their nonsingular factors are \(g_S(x)=a+1\) and \(g_T(x)=1\). The declared parameter ranges give
\[
c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
\qquad
c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}.
\tag{2}
\]
The factors are constant, so they belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every fixed \(t>0\); the source-factor radius is valid because \(M\ge a+1\). Thus (1)--(2) verify density support, normalization, the two boundary-factor inequalities, and density-factor smoothness without either of the deleted estimator-side smoothness inequalities.

Fix the known randomized propensity \(e\), which satisfies \(\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\varepsilon\). Conditional on \(X=x\), draw \(A\) as Bernoulli with success probability \(e(x)\), independently of the source potential outcomes. Given a sign \(v\in\{-1,1\}\), take the source potential outcomes to be conditionally Bernoulli with means
\[
\mu_{0,v}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,v}(x)=\frac12+v\delta b_h(x),
\tag{3}
\]
and impose consistency by setting \(Y=Y^S(A)\). Give the target potential outcomes conditionally Bernoulli laws with the same two means.

Let \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\). Since \(K\) is a smooth compactly supported bump, dilation gives, for \(0\le q\le p\),
\[
\lVert D^q b_h\rVert_\infty\le C_Kh^{s-q},
\qquad
[D^p b_h]_{\beta}\le C_K,
\tag{4}
\]
uniformly over \(0<h\le1\). Choose a fixed \(\delta>0\) small enough that (3) takes values in \([0,1]\), both responses lie in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), and
\[
\frac14-\delta^2\lVert K\rVert_\infty^2\ge M^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
This is possible because \(M>4\). Equation (5) verifies residual nondegeneracy, and the construction verifies consistency, conditional randomization, known propensity, treatment positivity, and conditional mean transportability. Consequently both induced observed laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) for every fixed \(t>0\).

For \(|u|\le\delta\lVert K\rVert_\infty<1/2\), direct Bernoulli algebra and compactness of that interval give
\[
\operatorname{kl}\!\left(\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)\right)
\le C_0u^2.
\tag{6}
\]
The two source laws differ only in the treated conditional outcome. Additivity of relative entropy for product measures, \(e(x)\le1\), (1), (3), and the substitution \(x=hu\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,\otimes n},P_{-,h}^{S,\otimes n})
&\le C_0n\delta^2\int_0^hb_h(x)^2(a+1)x^a\,dx\\
&=C_0(a+1)\delta^2nh^{2s+a+1}
  \int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du\\
&\le Cnh^{2s+a+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero,
\[
k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0.
\tag{8}
\]
The target covariate law is identical under the two signs, and hence
\[
|\tau(\mathbb P_{+,h})-\tau(\mathbb P_{-,h})|
=2\delta\int_0^hb_h(x)\,dx
=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) prove the source witness, including its boundary support \([0,h]\). The multiplier \(\delta\) can be reduced further, so the constant divergence used in a later two-point reduction can be made arbitrarily small.

For the target-sample witness, retain the source density in (1), put \(\ell(x)=x-1/2\), and choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\). Use the common source regressions
\[
\mu_0(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x),
\tag{10}
\]
with the same randomized Bernoulli full-data construction. A linear polynomial belongs to \(\mathcal H^s(M)\) for every fixed \(s>0\); reducing \(\delta_0\) verifies the response-radius, outcome-support, and variance conditions.

Keep the entire source law fixed and define
\[
f_{T,\pm}(x)=1\pm u_m\ell(x),
\qquad
u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\tag{11}
\]
Because \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), both functions in (11) integrate to one. Strict target-density slack, positivity of \(m\), and smoothness of \(\ell\) permit one fixed \(\delta_1>0\) such that, for every \(m\ge1\), both densities are positive, lie between the target-factor bounds, and belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\). Choose target potential outcomes with the common conditional means in (10), which preserves mean transportability and gives two admissible full-data laws with the same observed source marginal.

The lower target density is uniformly bounded away from zero. The inequality \(\log z\le z-1\) gives \(\operatorname{KL}(p,q)\le\int(p-q)^2/q\) for positive densities \(p,q\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{KL}(P_{T,+}^{\otimes m},P_{T,-}^{\otimes m})
&\le m\int_0^1
\frac{\{f_{T,+}(x)-f_{T,-}(x)\}^2}{f_{T,-}(x)}\,dx\\
&\le C_1m u_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
\le C_2\delta_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Finally, \(\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx=1/12\), so
\[
|\tau_+-\tau_-|
=2\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
=\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{6}m^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
The constants in (7), (9), (12), and (13) depend only on the fixed class constants, not on \(n,m,h\). This proves both assertions. \(\square\)
