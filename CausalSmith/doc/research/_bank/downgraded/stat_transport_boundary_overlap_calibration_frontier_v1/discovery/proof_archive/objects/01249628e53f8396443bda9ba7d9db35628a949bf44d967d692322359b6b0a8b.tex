\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\mathcal C_{n,m}\) be an interval with the asserted uniform asymptotic coverage.  Thus, along the sequence under consideration,
\[
 \inf_{Q\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t)\cup
                   \mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)}
 Q\{\tau(Q)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}
 \ge1-\gamma-o(1).
\tag{1}
\]
We first derive the interval form of the two-point inequality.  If \(Q_0,Q_1\) have parameter separation
\(\Delta=|\tau(Q_1)-\tau(Q_0)|\), an interval containing both parameter values has length at least \(\Delta\).  Moreover, total variation gives
\(Q_0(B)\ge Q_1(B)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  Consequently, (1) implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
 &\ge \Delta\,Q_0\{\tau(Q_0),\tau(Q_1)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}\\
 &\ge \Delta\bigl[
 Q_0\{\tau(Q_0)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}
 +Q_1\{\tau(Q_1)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}
 -1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \bigr]\\
 &\ge \Delta\{1-2\gamma-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)-o(1)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

We construct both comparisons around a single smoother reference law.  Put
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad \ell(x)=x-\frac12,
\tag{3}
\]
and choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\).  Let the source potential outcomes be conditionally Bernoulli with
\[
 \mu_0(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x),
\tag{4}
\]
independently of treatment conditional on \(X\), use the fixed known propensity, and set \(Y=Y^S(A)\).  Give the target potential outcomes the same conditional means.  The factors \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\) satisfy their support, normalization, boundary, and order-\(t\) H\"older conditions for every fixed \(t>0\).  Since \(\ell\) is smooth of every order and \(M>4\), reducing \(\delta_0\) puts both responses strictly inside the radius-\(M\) balls for \(s_1\) and \(s_2\), keeps their values in \([0,1]\), and makes every conditional Bernoulli variance at least \(M^{-1}\).  Hence the resulting observed law \(Q_0\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\) and also satisfies all order-\(s_1\) response restrictions.

For the rough source alternative, take
\[
 h=n^{-1/(2s_1+a+1)},
 \qquad
 b_{h,s_1}(x)=h^{s_1}K(x/h)\mathbf 1\{0\le x\le h\},
\tag{5}
\]
and change only the treated response to
\[
 \mu_{1,1}(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x)+\delta b_{h,s_1}(x).
\tag{6}
\]
The scaling calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\) and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\) put the resulting law \(Q_1\) in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t)\), while preserving outcome support and residual nondegeneracy.  The source Bernoulli probabilities under \(Q_0\) are uniformly separated from zero and one.  The same direct Bernoulli relative-entropy calculation therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 \le Cn\delta^2h^{2s_1+a+1}
 =C\delta^2,
\tag{7}
\]
and the target law in (3) gives
\[
 \Delta_S:=|\tau(Q_1)-\tau(Q_0)|
 =\delta h^{s_1+1}\int_0^1K(u)\,du
 \ge c_Sn^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\tag{8}
\]
The elementary log-sum argument gives
\(\operatorname{TV}(Q_1,Q_0)\le\sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)/2}\).  Reduce \(\delta\) so that this total variation is at most a fixed
\(\eta_S<1-2\gamma\).  Equations (2), (7), and (8) then imply, for all sufficiently large indices,
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
 \ge c_1n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\tag{9}
\]

For the target-sample comparison, keep the source law and both response functions in (3)--(4) fixed, and change only the target density to
\[
 f_{T,2}(x)=1+u_m\ell(x),
 \qquad u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\tag{10}
\]
The identity \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), the strict slack
\(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\), and the smoothness of \(\ell\) make (10) a positive normalized member of the order-\(t\) target-density class for a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta_1>0\).  Choose target potential outcomes to preserve conditional-mean transportability, and denote the resulting law by \(Q_2\).  It belongs to the smoother class.  Its source law is identical to that of \(Q_0\), and the reference target density is one, so
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_2,Q_0)
 \le m u_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 \le C\delta_1^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \Delta_T:=|\tau(Q_2)-\tau(Q_0)|
 =\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 =\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{12}m^{-1/2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Reduce \(\delta_1\) so that
\(\operatorname{TV}(Q_2,Q_0)\le\eta_T<1-2\gamma\).  Applying (2) to \(Q_0,Q_2\) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
 \ge c_2m^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Both (9) and (13) hold at the same law
\(Q_0\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\).  Since
\(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
 \ge c\left\{m^{-1/2}
 +n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus uniform honesty over the two fixed H\"older classes forces the rough-class boundary length at a smoother law, for every fixed density smoothness \(t>0\). \(\square\)
