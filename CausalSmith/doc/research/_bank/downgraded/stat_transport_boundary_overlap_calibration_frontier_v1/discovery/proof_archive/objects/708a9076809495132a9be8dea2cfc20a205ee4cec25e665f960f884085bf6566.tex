\textbf{Proof.}
We construct explicit subexperiments contained in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\).  Their source and target density factors are constant.  Consequently, they satisfy the declared factor-smoothness assumptions for every fixed \(t>0\); neither \(t>1/2\) nor \(s/(2s+1)+t/(2t+1)>1/2\) enters the testing argument.

We first derive the testing inequality used below.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two observed-data laws with parameter values \(\tau_0,\tau_1\), and write \(d=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\).  Given an arbitrary estimator \(\widehat\tau\), choose the parameter value nearest to \(\widehat\tau\) to obtain a test.  A testing error under \(Q_u\) implies \(|\widehat\tau-\tau_u|\ge d/2\).  The sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\), whence
\[
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}
       [ (\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2]
 \ge \frac{d^2}{8}
       \{1-\|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, data processing of relative entropy by the indicator of a measurable set reduces Pinsker's inequality to the binary calculation
\[
 p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}\ge 2(p-q)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, for fixed \(q\), the difference between the left side of (2) and \(2(p-q)^2\) vanishes with zero first derivative at \(p=q\), and its second derivative is
\[
 \frac1{p(1-p)}-4\ge0.
\]
Taking the supremum over measurable sets gives
\[
 \|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^2
 \le \frac12\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus (1) yields a root-mean-squared risk lower bound of constant order in the parameter separation whenever the Kullback--Leibler divergence is bounded by a sufficiently small constant.

For all three regimes, take
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{4}
\]
These are probability densities and have nonsingular factors \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\).  The declared parameter ranges give
\[
 c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
 \qquad c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+}.
\tag{5}
\]
Hence (4)--(5) satisfy \(\mathrm{ass:source\text{-}boundary\text{-}factor}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:target\text{-}boundary\text{-}factor}\), including when either target bound is attained.  Both factors are constant.  Their order-\(t\) H\"older seminorms vanish and their suprema are at most \(M\), because \(M\ge a+1\).  Thus \(\mathrm{ass:source\text{-}factor\text{-}smoothness}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:target\text{-}factor\text{-}smoothness}\) hold for every \(t>0\).

Draw \(A\) according to the fixed known propensity \(e(X)\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), let the two source potential outcomes be independent Bernoulli variables with the arm-specific means specified below, independently of \(A\); give the target potential outcomes the same conditional means.  Finally set \(Y=Y^S(A)\).  These choices satisfy \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(\mathrm{ass:trial\text{-}randomization}\), \(\mathrm{ass:known\text{-}propensity}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}transportability}\).  Since the fixed radius satisfies \(M>4\), define
\[
 \Delta_M=\sqrt{\frac14-\frac1M}>0.
\]
All perturbation constants below are chosen so that each conditional mean differs from \(1/2\) by at most \(\Delta_M/2\).  The outcomes then lie in \([0,1]\), and in either treatment arm
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)
 \ge \frac14-\frac{\Delta_M^2}{4}
 >\frac1M.
\tag{6}
\]
This verifies \(\mathrm{ass:residual\text{-}nondegeneracy}\).  The control regression is constant, and the scaling arguments below verify both response-smoothness assumptions.  The target covariate law in (4) is common to every alternative, so the \(m\) target observations contribute zero divergence for every \(m\).

\emph{The nonregular case \(a>1\).}
For \(h\in(0,1)\) and a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), put
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\tag{7}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:boundary\text{-}bump}\), dilation multiplies a derivative of order \(v\le\lfloor s\rfloor\) by \(h^{s-v}\) and preserves the order-\(s\) H\"older seminorm up to the fixed norm of \(K\).  Reducing \(\delta\) if necessary therefore puts both regressions in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), uniformly in \(h\), and also enforces the range restriction used in (6).

For \(|u|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, direct calculation and Taylor's theorem give
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12+u\right),
  \operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12-u\right)
 \right)
 =2u\log\frac{1+2u}{1-2u}
 \le C_0u^2.
\tag{8}
\]
The control-arm laws agree, and the treated-arm contribution is multiplied by \(e(x)\le1\), which follows from \(\mathrm{ass:treatment\text{-}positivity}\).  The chain rule and product additivity for relative entropy, followed by (4) and (8), yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,\otimes n},P_{-,h}^{S,\otimes n})
 &\le Cn\int_0^h b_h(x)^2x^a\,dx\\
 &=Cnh^{2s+a+1}\int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(K\) is nonnegative, continuous, and nonzero,
\[
 k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0,
 \qquad
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{10}
\]
Choose \(h=c_0n^{-1/(2s+a+1)}\), with \(c_0>0\) small enough that (9) is bounded by a sufficiently small constant.  Equations (1), (3), and (10) then imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge c n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\tag{11}
\]

\emph{The regular case \(a<1\).}
Choose a nonnegative, nonzero \(C^\infty\) function \(q\) supported in \([1/4,3/4]\), and set
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}q(x).
\tag{12}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), (12) satisfies the range, variance, and order-\(s\) H\"older conditions.  On the support of \(q\), the source density in (4) is bounded above and below by positive constants.  Therefore (8) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_+^{S,\otimes n},P_-^{S,\otimes n})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Under the uniform target density,
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1q(x)\,dx
 \ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{14}
\]
After reducing \(\delta\), equations (1), (3), (13), and (14) prove
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{15}
\]

\emph{The critical case \(a=1\).}
Let \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), fix an integer \(j_0\ge1\), and choose \(L=L_n\) so that
\[
 \ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}},
 \qquad
 L-j_0+1\asymp\log n.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(j_0\le j\le L\), define
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\}.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{L-j_0+1}\), let
\[
 \mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+
 \sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x).
\tag{18}
\]
The shell interiors are disjoint.  From (16), uniformly for \(j_0\le j\le L\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}\le C\ell_j^s.
\tag{19}
\]
The smooth bump \(K\), extended by zero off its support, is flat at the support endpoints.  Scaling (17), using (19), shows that each zero-extended shell piece has order-\(s\) H\"older norm at most \(C\delta\).  On one shell the usual scaling inequality applies; across distinct dyadic shells, the distance to their common or separating endpoint and endpoint flatness give the same bound for the H\"older quotient.  Hence the sum in (18) has H\"older norm at most \(C'\delta\), uniformly in \(L\).  Choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small makes every sign alternative satisfy both response-smoothness assumptions, the range condition, and (6).

The contribution of coordinate \(j\) to the target parameter is
\[
 d_j:=\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)\,dx
 =\frac{\delta k_1}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(\omega^{(j)}\) flips only coordinate \(j\), then \(f_S(x)=2x\), and (8) implies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{S,\otimes n},
                    P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{S,\otimes n})
 \le Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
\tag{21}
\]
uniformly in \(j\), \(n\), and the other signs.

Place the uniform product prior \(\Pi\) on \(\omega\).  Conditional on the observed covariates, each outcome belongs to at most one shell, so the likelihood, prior, and posterior factor across sign coordinates.  Since conditional expectation minimizes squared loss and \(\tau_\omega=\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j\omega_j\), posterior independence gives
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}
 \mathbb E_\Pi[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 =
 \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
       (\tau_\omega\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
   \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
       (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}).
\tag{22}
\]
Write \(q_j=\mathbb E_\Pi(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})\).  Since \(\omega_j^2=1\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|.
\tag{23}
\]
Let \(\overline P_{j,+}\) and \(\overline P_{j,-}\) be the mixture experiments conditional on \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\), respectively.  Bayes' formula yields
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi|q_j|
 =\|\overline P_{j,+}-\overline P_{j,-}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\tag{24}
\]
Pair every sign vector with its \(j\)-flip.  The pointwise log-sum inequality for the paired mixture densities and (21) give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\overline P_{j,+},\overline P_{j,-})
 \le 2^{-(L-j_0)}
 \sum_{\omega:\,\omega_j=1}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega,P_{\omega^{(j)}})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{25}
\]
The target likelihood cancels in (25) because its law is common to every sign vector.  By (3), (23)--(25), and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\),
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
   (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 \ge c_1>0
\tag{26}
\]
uniformly in \(j\).  The supremum risk dominates the Bayes risk.  Thus (16), (20), (22), and (26) give
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 \ge c_1\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
 \ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\tag{27}
\]
Taking square roots proves the critical lower bound \(c\sqrt{\log n/n}\) without a hidden polynomial loss.

The three bounds (11), (15), and (27), together with \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rate}\), prove that, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(m\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\rho_n(a,s).
\]
Every subexperiment used the boundary-attaining target density \(f_T=1\), so no strict target-density slack is required. \(\square\)
