\textbf{Proof.}
Let
\[
L_{n,m}=\sum_i\Gamma_{i,h_n}\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}
+\frac1m\sum_j\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\},
\qquad
\sigma_{n,m}^2=\operatorname{Var}(L_{n,m}).
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:direct\text{-}series\text{-}sandwich\text{-}normality}\), uniformly over the model class,
\[
\sup_z\left|\mathbb P\{L_{n,m}/\sigma_{n,m}\le z\}-\Phi(z)\right|=o(1),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathrm{se}}^2/\sigma_{n,m}^2\to_P1,
\]
and the estimator equals \(\tau+L_{n,m}+R_{n,m}\) with \(|R_{n,m}|\le\widehat B\) except on an event of probability \(o(1)\).

For a deterministic \(u\ge0\), the definition of \(\operatorname{cv}_{1-\gamma/2}(u)\) gives
\[
\inf_{|b|\le u}\mathbb P\{|N(b,1)|\le\operatorname{cv}_{1-\gamma/2}(u)\}\ge1-\gamma/2.
\]
Slutsky's theorem, uniformly valid because of the Kolmogorov and variance-consistency conclusions, therefore yields coverage at least \(1-\gamma/2-o(1)\) on the good branch.  This is at least \(1-\gamma-o(1)\).  On the complementary branch the interval is \([-1,1]\), which contains \(\tau\) by definition of the estimand.  The asserted uniform coverage follows.

For length, if \(z_{1-\gamma/4}\) denotes the corresponding standard-normal quantile, the triangle inequality implies
\[
\operatorname{cv}_{1-\gamma/2}(u)\le u+z_{1-\gamma/4}.
\]
Thus on the good branch the interval length is at most
\[
2\widehat B+2z_{1-\gamma/4}\widehat{\mathrm{se}}.
\]
The bias definition, sandwich consistency, the coefficient norm bound, and sample balance give its expectation bounded by
\[
C\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)+\delta_{s,h_n}\delta_{t,h_n}
+\lambda_n(J_{\mu,n}+J_{r,n})\}.
\]
The fallback interval has length two, while its probability is exponentially small, so it does not change this order.  The claimed standard-error consistency and normal approximation were already supplied by the same lemma, and the matching-subregime statement follows from \(\mathrm{thm:ricot\text{-}attainment}\). \(\square\)
