The deterministic coverage reduction is valid after the finite zero-standard-error correction. If uniformly
\[
\widehat\tau-\tau=b_P+L_{n,m}+r_{n,m},\qquad |b_P|\le\widehat B+\widehat E,qquad r_{n,m}=o_P(\sigma_{n,m}),
\]
if \(L_{n,m}/\sigma_{n,m}\) is asymptotically standard normal, and if \(\widehat{\mathrm{se}}/\sigma_{n,m}\to1\), then the folded-normal critical-value definition gives conditional noncoverage at most \(\gamma/2+o(1)\); combining this with an envelope failure probability at most \(\gamma/2+o(1)\) yields coverage \(1-\gamma-o(1)\). For \(s>1/2\), the entropy bound
\[
\log N(\epsilon,\mathcal H^s(1),L^2(Q))\le C\epsilon^{-1/s}
\]
has finite entropy integral. What remains unproved is uniform covariance convergence and oracle replacement for the fold-varying calibrated weights, the weighted triangular-array Lindeberg condition, consistency of all four variance terms, and the uniform-integrability bound needed for expected length.
