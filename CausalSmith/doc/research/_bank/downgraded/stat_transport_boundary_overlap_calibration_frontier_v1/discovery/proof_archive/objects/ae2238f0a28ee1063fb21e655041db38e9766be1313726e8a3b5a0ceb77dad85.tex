\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\mathscr X\) be the sigma-field generated by all source and target covariates. By the positive ridge terms in \(\mathrm{def:regression\text{-}fit}\), each foldwise regression coefficient is a unique linear function of the training pseudo-outcomes. The matrices and smoother coefficients in \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\) are exactly the corresponding normal-equation coefficients. Hence, for every fold \(k\) and every \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\widehat\mu^{(-k)}_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)
=\sum_{q=1}^n L_{q,k,h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)Z_q.
\tag{1}
\]
Substitute (1) into the target plug-in part and the fitted-regression part of \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}\). The coefficient of a fixed \(Z_q\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mL_{q,k_T(j),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_j^T)
+\frac1n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(q))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_q)\mathbf 1\{X_q\ge h\}\\
&\qquad-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}
L_{q,k_S(i),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_i),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is \(\Gamma_q(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\) by definition. This proves the first identity. All factors in this coefficient depend only on the covariates: the RiCOT primal and dual programs use only source and target covariates, and the ridge Gram matrices do likewise. Thus every \(\Gamma_q\) is \(\mathscr X\)-measurable.

Consistency, conditional trial randomization, and the known propensity imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P_S}(Z\mid X=x)
&=\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}\{AY\mid X=x\}}{e(x)}
-\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}\{(1-A)Y\mid X=x\}}{1-e(x)}\\
&=\mathbb E\{Y^S(1)\mid X=x\}-\mathbb E\{Y^S(0)\mid X=x\}
=\mu(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Adding and subtracting \(\sum_i\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)\) and \(m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)\) in the first identity gives the displayed exact decomposition.

Because \(\mu_0,\mu_1\in\mathcal H^s(M)\), their difference belongs to \(\mathcal H^s(2M)\). Since both response functions take values in \([0,1]\), \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\). The definition of \(B_X\) therefore gives the deterministic inequality
\[
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)\right|\le B_X.
\tag{3}
\]

Treatment positivity and \(0\le Y\le1\) give \(-\varepsilon^{-1}\le Z_i\le\varepsilon^{-1}\). Conditional on \(\mathscr X\), the source variables are independent, (2) centers them, and the interval containing \(\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\) has length at most \(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\). Applying \(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\) conditionally, with \(\delta=\gamma/2\), yields
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right|>R_S
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X\right\}\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{4}
\]
The target variables are independent, \(\mathbb E_{P_T}\mu(X^T)=\tau\), and \(\mu(X^T)\in[-1,1]\). A second application of the same lemma to \(m^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\) gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|>R_T(m)
\right\}\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{5}
\]
Taking expectations in (4), combining (3)--(5) with the exact decomposition, and using the union bound proves the probability assertion. Finally, substituting \(B_X\le b\) and \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\le g^2\) into the definitions of \(R_S\) and \(R_T\) proves the realized-design implication. No bound on either observable diagnostic was used. \(\square\)
