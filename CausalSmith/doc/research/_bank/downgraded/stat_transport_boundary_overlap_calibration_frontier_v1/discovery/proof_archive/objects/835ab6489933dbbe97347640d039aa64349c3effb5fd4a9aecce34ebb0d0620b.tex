In a randomized trial whose covariate density depletes as \(x^a\) at a clinically important boundary while the target density stays positive, the causal target is identified as \(\tau=\int_0^1\{\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)\}f_T(x)\,dx\). For every density-factor smoothness \(t>0\), the proved source minimax lower phase is \(n^{-1/2}\) when \(a<1\), \(\sqrt{\log n/n}\) when \(a=1\), and \(n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}\) when \(a>1\), with an independent \(m^{-1/2}\) target-sample floor where the target-density bounds have strict slack. The deterministic truncated-oracle bias--standard-deviation envelope has the same algebraic phase, but no data-driven minimax upper bound is claimed. The unchanged boundary-spliced capped-RiCOT estimator has an exact conditional linear representation and a finite-sample Hoeffding execution certificate, yielding fixed-class honest intervals of realized radius \(B_X+R_S+R_T\); empirical capped-weight localization, frontier-order expected length, and executable positive adaptation remain open. Boundary-bump and multiscale witnesses prove the converse, and a two-class lower bound rules out honest smoothness adaptation without an explicit restriction such as boundary self-similarity.
