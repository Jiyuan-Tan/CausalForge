\textbf{Proof.}
Assume \(a=0\). By the definitions \(f_S(x)=x^a g_S(x)\), \(f_T(x)=g_T(x)\), and \(r(x)=x^{-a}g_T(x)/g_S(x)\),
\[
f_S(x)=g_S(x),\qquad f_T(x)=g_T(x),\qquad r(x)=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}
\]
for every \(x\in[0,1]\). The source- and target-boundary-factor assumptions give
\[
c_{S,-}\le g_S(x)\le c_{S,+},
\qquad
c_{T,-}\le g_T(x)\le c_{T,+}.
\]
In particular, \(g_S(x)>0\). Dividing the target inequalities by the corresponding positive source bounds therefore yields
\[
\frac{c_{T,-}}{c_{S,+}}
\le \frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}
\le \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}},
\qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]

Because \(a=0<1\), the definition of the regime bandwidth gives
\[
h_n=n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}.
\]
Since \(s>0\), exponentiation is valid and gives the exact identity
\[
h_n^{s+1}=\left(n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}\right)^{s+1}=n^{-1/2}.
\]
The already-established Riesz-threshold lemma, specialized to \(a=0<1\) and \(h=h_n\), gives
\[
\int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\asymp1,
\]
and hence the asserted \(O(1)\) bound. Its comparison constants depend only on the fixed boundary-factor constants, so this conclusion is uniform over the model class. The displayed restrictions \(t>1/2\) and \(s/(2s+1)+t/(2t+1)>1/2\) are compatible with this specialization; no further use of them is needed for these deterministic identities.

Fix a fold \(k\) for which both interior training measures are nonzero. Write the raw KKT continuation in \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}weights}\) as
\[
\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)
=
\left[
\frac1{|\mathcal T^T_k|}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal T^T_k}
\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h_n\}
\exp\!\left\{-\frac{c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,X_j^T)
+\widehat\theta_{h_n,J_{r,n}}^{(-k)\top}
\widetilde{\mathbf b}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)
+\widehat\psi^{(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n},j}}
{\eta_0}\right\}
\right]^{\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)}.
\]
Nonzero target interior mass means that at least one indicator in this sum equals one. Every included exponential is finite and strictly positive, the denominator \(|\mathcal T^T_k|\) is positive, and \(\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)>0\). Consequently
\[
\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)>0
\qquad\text{for every }x\in[h_n,1].
\]
Only this raw continuation agrees with the fitted first-marginal density at source training atoms. The downstream RiCOT weight in the proposition is instead its capped projection. Since \(a=0\), the deterministic cap is the strictly positive constant
\[
\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}h_n^{-a}=\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}>0.
\]
Thus the definition of the capped weight implies
\[
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)
=\min\!\left\{
\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x),
\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}
\right\}>0,
\qquad x\in[h_n,1].
\]
This is a pointwise deterministic assertion. It supplies neither an empirical squared-norm bound nor a lower bound on an observed residual sum, which may exhibit cancellation, and therefore it does not imply a root-mean-squared risk bound. \(\square\)
