\textbf{Proof.}
We first record a self-contained two-point inequality. Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two observed-data laws, let \(\theta_j=\tau(Q_j)\), and put \(\Delta=|\theta_1-\theta_0|\). For any estimator \(\widehat\tau\), the event \(E=\{|\widehat\tau-\theta_0|<\Delta/2\}\) gives
\[
\max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}(\widehat\tau-\theta_j)^2
\ge \frac{\Delta^2}{8}
 \{Q_0(E^c)+Q_1(E)\}
\ge \frac{\Delta^2}{8}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.
\tag{11}
\]
For completeness, if \(\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)\le\zeta<\infty\), write \(q_0,q_1\) for densities with respect to a common dominating measure and \(A_{01}=\int\sqrt{q_0q_1}\). Jensen's inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz give, respectively,
\[
A_{01}=\mathbb E_{Q_0}\sqrt{q_1/q_0}\ge e^{-\zeta/2},
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
\le\sqrt{1-A_{01}^2}
\le\sqrt{1-e^{-\zeta}}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus a fixed sufficiently small Kullback--Leibler bound and a separation \(\Delta\) imply a root-mean-squared minimax lower bound \(c\Delta\). All pairs below have a common target law or a common source law, so the displayed divergence is also the divergence of the complete two-sample experiment.

We next prove the source lower bound in each overlap regime. Throughout, use the admissible baseline \(f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a\), \(f_T(x)=1\), Bernoulli potential outcomes, and the full-data extension checked in \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\). Because its nonsingular density factors are constant, this baseline belongs to the model for every fixed \(t>0\).

If \(a<1\), take
\[
\mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
For a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), these laws are in the model for every \(n\). The calculation in (4), now integrated against the probability density \(f_S\), gives source divergence at most \(C\delta^2\), while their target separation is \(2\delta n^{-1/2}\). Reducing \(\delta\) and applying (11)--(12) proves
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c n^{-1/2},
\qquad a<1.
\tag{14}
\]

Suppose \(a=1\). This is the critical multiscale step. For all sufficiently large \(n\), set
\[
L_n=\left\lfloor\frac{\log_2 n}{4(s+1)}\right\rfloor,
\qquad x_j=2^{-j},
\qquad q_n=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{nL_n}},
\tag{15}
\]
and define the sign-aligned multiscale boundary perturbation
\[
v_n(x)=q_n\sum_{j=1}^{L_n}x_j^{-1}K(x/x_j)
=q_n\sum_{j=1}^{L_n}x_j^{-(s+1)}b_{x_j}(x).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus (16) is explicitly a multiscale mixture of the boundary bumps from \(\mathrm{def:boundary\text{-}bump}\). Hölder scaling and a geometric sum imply
\[
\|v_n\|_{\mathcal H^s}
\le Cq_n\sum_{j=1}^{L_n}x_j^{-(s+1)}
\le Cq_nx_{L_n}^{-(s+1)}
\le C\delta\frac{n^{-1/4}}{\sqrt{L_n}},
\tag{17}
\]
because \(2^{L_n(s+1)}\le n^{1/4}\). Hence \(\mu_{1,\pm}=1/2\pm v_n\) and \(\mu_{0,\pm}=1/2\) obey the support, Hölder, and variance conditions after a fixed reduction of \(\delta\).

It remains to compute the information and separation. For \(x\in(x_{k+1},x_k]\), only indices \(j\le k\) can contribute and
\[
\sum_{j\le k}x_j^{-1}\le 2^{k+1}\le2x^{-1}.
\]
For \(0\le x\le x_{L_n}\), the full sum is at most \(2x_{L_n}^{-1}\). Since \(K\le1\) and \(f_S(x)=2x\), these bounds give
\[
\int_0^1v_n(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx
\le Cq_n^2\left\{1+\int_{x_{L_n}}^1\frac{dx}{x}\right\}
\le Cq_n^2L_n.
\tag{18}
\]
The Bernoulli calculation (4) and (18) show that the \(n\)-sample source divergence is at most \(Cnq_n^2L_n=C\delta^2\). On the other hand, nonnegativity of \(K\) and a change of variables yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\tau_+-\tau_-|
&=2\int_0^1v_n(x)\,dx\\
&=2q_n\sum_{j=1}^{L_n}x_j^{-1}
 \int_0^{x_j}K(x/x_j)\,dx\\
&=2\delta\left(\int_0^1K(u)\,du\right)
 \sqrt{\frac{L_n}{n}}
\ge c\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
After reducing \(\delta\), (11)--(12) prove the critical lower bound for all sufficiently large \(n\). For the finitely many remaining \(n\), the parametric construction (13) applies, and \(\sqrt{\log n/n}\) is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(n^{-1/2}\); decreasing the final constant covers those indices as well. This proves the claimed logarithm without a polynomial loss.

If \(a>1\), choose
\[
h=d\,n^{-1/(2s+a+1)},
\tag{20}
\]
where \(d>0\) is fixed small enough that \(h\) lies in the range of \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\) for every relevant \(n\). The source pair in that lemma can be scaled so that
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_+,Q_-)
\le Cnh^{2s+a+1}=Cd^{2s+a+1}
\tag{21}
\]
is as small as required in (12), whereas
\[
|\tau(Q_+)-\tau(Q_-)|
\ge ch^{s+1}
=cd^{s+1}n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (11)--(12) prove the \(a>1\) branch. Combining (14), (19), and (22) gives, for every regime,
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_S\rho_n(a,s).
\tag{23}
\]

Finally use the target-density pair in \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\). Its source law is common, its target-product divergence is bounded by \(C\delta_1^2\), and its parameter separation is \(c\delta_1m^{-1/2}\). Choose the freely scalable \(\delta_1\) sufficiently small and apply (11)--(12) to obtain
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_Tm^{-1/2}.
\tag{24}
\]
The same minimax risk appears in (23) and (24). Therefore \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\) gives
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
\ge c\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\}.
\]
The critical construction (15)--(19) is the announced multiscale boundary-bump mixture, and all three source constructions lie in the corrected class for arbitrary fixed \(t>0\). \(\square\)
