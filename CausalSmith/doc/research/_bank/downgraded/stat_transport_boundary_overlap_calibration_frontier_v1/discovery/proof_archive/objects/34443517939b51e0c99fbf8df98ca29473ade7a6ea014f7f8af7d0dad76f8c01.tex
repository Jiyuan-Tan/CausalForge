\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\). By the definitions \(f_S(x)=x^ag_S(x)\), \(f_T(x)=g_T(x)\), and \(r(x)=f_T(x)/f_S(x)\), and because \(f_S(x)>0\) for \(x\in(0,1]\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2
&=\int_h^1r(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_h^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\\
&=\int_h^1x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)^2}{g_S(x)}\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The two boundary-factor assumptions and \(c_{S,-}>0\) imply the pointwise comparison
\[
\frac{c_{T,-}^2}{c_{S,+}}x^{-a}
\le x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)^2}{g_S(x)}
\le\frac{c_{T,+}^2}{c_{S,-}}x^{-a},
\qquad 0<x\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
All divisions in (1)--(2) are therefore valid. Direct integration gives
\[
\int_h^1x^{-a}\,dx=
\begin{cases}
\{1-h^{1-a}\}/(1-a),&a<1,\\
\log(1/h),&a=1,\\
\{h^{1-a}-1\}/(a-1),&a>1.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(a<1\), the first expression in (3) is bounded above and bounded away from zero for every sufficiently small \(h\). For fixed \(a>1\), the ratio of the third expression to \(h^{1-a}\) converges to \(1/(a-1)\). Combining (1)--(3) proves
\[
\left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2
\asymp
\begin{cases}
1,&a<1,\\
\log(1/h),&a=1,\\
h^{1-a},&a>1.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
The comparison constants depend only on the fixed exponent and boundary-factor constants, and are uniform over the model class.

It remains to verify the representer assertion. On \(L^2(P_S)\), initially define the target-expectation functional on bounded functions by
\[
T(v)=\int_0^1v(x)f_T(x)\,dx
=\int_0^1v(x)r(x)f_S(x)\,dx.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(a<1\), (2)--(3) with \(h\downarrow0\) show that \(r\in L^2(P_S)\). The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality then gives \(|T(v)|\le\|r\|_{L^2(P_S)}\|v\|_{L^2(P_S)}\), so \(r\) is the source Riesz representer.

Conversely, suppose that an element \(q\in L^2(P_S)\) represented \(T\). For every \(\delta>0\) and every Borel set \(B\subseteq[\delta,1]\), the indicator \(\mathbf 1_B\) lies in \(L^2(P_S)\), and (5) would imply
\[
\int_Bq(x)f_S(x)\,dx=\int_Bf_T(x)\,dx
=\int_Br(x)f_S(x)\,dx.
\tag{6}
\]
Hence \(q=r\), \(P_S\)-almost everywhere on \([\delta,1]\). Letting \(\delta\) range over the positive rationals gives \(q=r\), \(P_S\)-almost everywhere on \((0,1]\). When \(a\ge1\), (2)--(3) show that \(\int_0^1r(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx=\infty\), contradicting \(q\in L^2(P_S)\). The point \(x=0\) is Lebesgue-null and creates no additional support case. Thus a source \(L^2(P_S)\) representer exists exactly when \(a<1\). \(\square\)
