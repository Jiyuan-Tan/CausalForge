\textbf{Proof.}
Assume \(a=0\). By the definitions of the density factors, for every \(x\in(0,1]\),
\[
f_S(x)=x^a g_S(x)=g_S(x),
\qquad
f_T(x)=g_T(x),
\qquad
r(x)=x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}.
\]
The source-boundary-factor assumption and the target-boundary-factor assumption give
\[
c_{S,-}\le g_S(x)\le c_{S,+}
\quad\text{and}\quad
c_{T,-}\le g_T(x)\le c_{T,+},
\qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]
In particular, \(g_S(x)\ge c_{S,-}>0\), so division by \(g_S(x)\) is valid. Dividing the lower target bound by the upper source bound, and the upper target bound by the lower source bound, yields
\[
\frac{c_{T,-}}{c_{S,+}}
\le r(x)=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}
\le\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}},
\qquad 0<x\le1.
\]

Because \(a=0<1\), the first branch in the definition of \(h_n\) applies:
\[
h_n=n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}.
\]
The model fixes \(s>0\), hence \(s+1>0\), and therefore
\[
h_n^{s+1}
=\left(n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}\right)^{s+1}
=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Moreover, \(h_n\downarrow0\). The \(a<1\) branch of the Riesz-threshold lemma consequently gives
\[
\int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\asymp1,
\]
and hence
\[
\int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx=O(1).
\]
The comparison constant depends only on the fixed source and target boundary-factor constants. The two displayed restrictions involving \(s\) and \(t\) are compatible with, but not needed for, these deterministic calculations. No empirical Gram matrix, leverage, estimator risk, or interval coverage has been bounded in this argument. \(\square\)
