\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\mathrm{lem:source\text{-}only\text{-}frontier\text{-}lower}\), there is a constant \(c_0>0\), independent of \(n\) and \(m\), such that for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c_0\rho_n(a,s).
\tag{1}
\]
The subexperiments proving that lemma have \(f_T=1\).  The declared parameter ranges give \(c_{T,-}\le 1\le c_{T,+}\), so those subexperiments remain admissible when either target-density bound is attained.  Thus (1) requires no strict slack in the target-density bounds.

By \(\mathrm{ass:sample\text{-}balance}\), \(m/n\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\).  Consequently, there are constants \(c_\kappa,C_\kappa>0\) and an integer \(n_0\) such that
\[
 c_\kappa n\le m\le C_\kappa n
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(n\ge n_0\).  In particular,
\[
 m^{-1/2}\le c_\kappa^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}.
\tag{3}
\]
In every overlap regime, \(n^{-1/2}\) is bounded above by a fixed multiple of \(\rho_n(a,s)\).  Indeed,
\[
 \rho_n(a,s)=n^{-1/2}\qquad\text{if }a<1,
\tag{4}
\]
while, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \rho_n(1,s)=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\ge n^{-1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(a>1\), then
\[
 \frac{s+1}{2s+a+1}<\frac12,
\]
and hence
\[
 \rho_n(a,s)=n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}\ge n^{-1/2}.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (3)--(6) imply that, eventually,
\[
 m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\le C_1\rho_n(a,s)
\tag{7}
\]
for a finite constant \(C_1\) depending only on the fixed parameters and \(\kappa\).  Combining (1) and (7) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge \frac{c_0}{C_1}\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Substitution of the three branches in \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rate}\) proves the displayed lower phase diagram.

The already established \(\mathrm{lem:oracle\text{-}truncation\text{-}envelope}\) gives, uniformly over \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\),
\[
 m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}
 +n^{-1/2}\left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h_n\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}
 \asymp m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s).
\tag{9}
\]
This is an order identity for a deterministic boundary-approximation term and the exact truncated source representer.  It does not construct an estimator measurable with respect to \(\mathcal D_{n,m}\), so (9) is not a minimax upper bound.  Equations (8)--(9) prove exactly the lower phase diagram and its truncated-oracle benchmark. \(\square\)
