\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\mathscr X\) be the sigma-field generated by all observed source and target covariates. The regression fits in \(\mathrm{def:regression\text{-}fit}\) are ridge least-squares fits. Substituting their normal-equation solutions into the two branches of the fitted regression gives, exactly,
\[
\widehat\mu^{(-k)}_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)
=\sum_{i=1}^nL_{ki}(x;h,J_{\mu,n})Z_i.
\tag{1}
\]
Every RiCOT weight is a function only of the source and target covariates and the fixed tuning constants. Hence the quantities \(L_{ki}\), \(w_i\), and \(\Gamma_i\) in \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\) are \(\mathscr X\)-measurable. Substitute (1) into the unchanged estimator:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}
={}&\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{i=1}^nL_{k_T(j)i}(X_j^T;h,J_{\mu,n})Z_i
+\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw_i(h,J_{r,n})Z_i\\
&-\frac1n\sum_{\ell=1}^nw_\ell(h,J_{r,n})
\sum_{i=1}^nL_{k_S(\ell)i}(X_\ell;h,J_{\mu,n})Z_i\\
=&\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})Z_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
This is algebra only and does not use a calibration equation for the capped weight.

Consistency, conditional trial randomization, and the known propensity imply
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{n,m}}(Z_i\mid X_i=x)
=\mathbb E(Y_i\mid A_i=1,X_i=x)-\mathbb E(Y_i\mid A_i=0,X_i=x)
=\mu(x).
\tag{3}
\]
Treatment positivity and \(0\le Y\le1\) also give
\[
-\varepsilon^{-1}\le Z_i\le\varepsilon^{-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
Add and subtract \(\sum_i\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)\) and \(m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)\) in (2). This gives the displayed three-term decomposition. The two outcome-regression smoothness assumptions and the triangle inequality for the Hölder norm give \(\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\); since both regressions take values in \([0,1]\), \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\). The definition of \(B_X\) therefore bounds the middle term deterministically.

Conditional on \(\mathscr X\), the source variables are independent, the variables \(Z_i-\mu(X_i)\) are centered by (3), and (4) shows that the conditional range length of \(\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\) is at most \(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\). If \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2>0\), \(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\) with
\[
x=\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i^2}
\]
gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right|>x
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X\right\}\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{5}
\]
If the coefficient norm is zero, the source sum is identically zero and (5) still holds.

Independently, the target observations \(\mu(X_j^T)\) are iid, take values in \([-1,1]\), and have expectation \(\tau\). Applying the same lemma to \(m^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\), whose range length is \(2/m\), gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|>
\sqrt{\frac{2\log(4/\gamma)}m}
\right\}\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{6}
\]
Although (6) is unconditional, (5) may be integrated over \(\mathscr X\); the union bound then makes the joint failure probability at most \(\gamma\). Combining the decomposition, the deterministic \(B_X\) bound, (5), and (6) proves the probability display. The final sentence records exactly what that display does and does not bound. \(\square\)
