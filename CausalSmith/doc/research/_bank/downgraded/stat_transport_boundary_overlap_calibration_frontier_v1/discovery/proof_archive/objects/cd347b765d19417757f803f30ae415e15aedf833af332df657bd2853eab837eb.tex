\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(P_0\) be the interior baseline used in the boundary-bump proof: \(f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a\), \(f_T(x)=1\), and both armwise regressions constant.  Then \(P_0\) belongs to the smoother class \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\).  Put
\[
 h=n^{-1/(2s_1+a+1)}
\]
and form an alternative \(P_1\) by adding a sufficiently small positive multiple of \(b_h\), of smoothness \(s_1\), to the treated regression.  The boundary-bump calculation gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_1^{S,n},P_0^{S,n})\le C\delta^2,
 \qquad
 |\tau(P_1)-\tau(P_0)|\ge c\,n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\]
Choose the fixed amplitude multiplier \(\delta\) so small that the total-variation distance is less than \((1-2\gamma)/4\).  This does not change the rate.

Let \(C_{n,m}\) be any interval satisfying the asserted uniform asymptotic coverage.  Under \(P_0\), the event \(\{\tau(P_0)\in C_{n,m}\}\) has probability at least \(1-\gamma-o(1)\).  By coverage under \(P_1\) and the total-variation inequality, under \(P_0\) the event \(\{\tau(P_1)\in C_{n,m}\}\) has probability at least \(1-\gamma-o(1)-\|P_1-P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\).  Hence their intersection has probability bounded below by a strictly positive constant.  On that intersection the interval length is at least \(|\tau(P_1)-\tau(P_0)|\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}|C_{n,m}|
 \ge c_1 n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\]

Use the same \(P_0\) and the smooth target-density alternative from the boundary-bump proof, with amplitude chosen so its total-variation distance is also less than \((1-2\gamma)/4\).  That alternative lies in both smoothness classes, leaves the source experiment unchanged, and separates \(\tau\) by \(cm^{-1/2}\).  Repeating the preceding intersection argument at the same baseline \(P_0\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}|C_{n,m}|\ge c_2m^{-1/2}.
\]
Since the same smoother law \(P_0\) supports both conclusions and \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\), the asserted sum lower bound follows. \(\square\)
