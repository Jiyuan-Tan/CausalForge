\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \((n,m,h,J)\) and one fold. The conventions in the corrected construction first map an empty training complement to the zero empirical measure, aggregate tied atoms, and order the remaining distinct atoms. Each of these operations is Borel measurable on the finite product sample space. On the zero-mass branch every selected object is identically zero and hence measurable.

Consider a nonzero branch. Partition the data space by the active interior index sets, the equality pattern among empirical atoms, and the ranks of the resulting finite calibration matrices. There are finitely many active-set and equality patterns and finitely many ranks, and every resulting stratum is Borel. On each stratum a plan is a vector in a fixed finite-dimensional compact simplex. Its marginal and calibration constraints have Borel coefficients, and the generalized Kullback--Leibler objective is lower semicontinuous in the plan. Because \(\eta_0>0\) and every reference mass on the ordered product support is positive, the plan-level entropy is strictly convex. Therefore the minimizer is unique in either branch. The exact positive-feasibility event is Borel: it is the countable union over \(L\ge1\) of the compact feasibility events on which every density coordinate is at least \(L^{-1}\). The unique minimizer on each branch is Borel: on successive rational grids, minimize the objective plus \(L\) times the distance to the branch's closed linear-constraint set, break ties lexicographically, and let \(L\to\infty\). Compactness and uniqueness force these Borel approximants to converge to the selected minimizer.

The penalized branch contains the strictly positive feasible product plan obtained by rescaling \(S\otimes T\) to have second marginal \(T\); strict positivity in the exact branch is part of the branch event. If a minimizing plan placed zero mass on a positive-reference cell, moving an infinitesimal amount toward a strictly positive feasible plan would have entropy directional derivative \(-\infty\), contradicting optimality. Hence the optimizer is strictly positive on every product-support cell. Put \(q=d\widehat\Pi/d(S\otimes T)\) and \(w=d\widehat\Pi_1/dS\). With the stated multiplier sign convention, exact-branch stationarity gives
\[
c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,z)+\eta_0\log q(x,z)+\lambda_0\log w(x)
+\widehat\theta^\top\beta(x)+\widehat\psi_z=0.
\]
In the penalized branch, the derivative of \(\xi_n\|\delta\|_2^2\) is \(2\xi_n\delta^\top\beta(x)\), so the same equation holds with \(\widehat\theta=2\xi_n\delta\). Solving for \(q\) and integrating with respect to \(T\) yields
\[
w(x)=\left[\int\exp\left\{-\frac{c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,z)+\widehat\theta^\top\beta(x)+\widehat\psi_z}{\eta_0}\right\}\,dT(z)\right]^{\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)}.
\]
This is exactly \(\widetilde r\), proving that the raw continuation, and only the raw continuation, equals the first-marginal density at training atoms. Conditional on the unique primal plan, the KKT solutions form a nonempty closed affine set. On every fixed-rank stratum its minimum-norm element is the Moore--Penrose solution \(A^+b\), which is Borel in the entries of \((A,b)\). Exponentiation, finite summation, the fixed positive power, and the minimum with \(C_h\) preserve measurability. This proves measurability of the plan, selected duals, raw continuation, and capped downstream fold map without a stability assumption.

The density-factor bounds give, for \(x\ge h\),
\[
r(x)=x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}
\le \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}h^{-a}=C_h.
\]
Projection of a real number onto \([0,C_h]\) cannot increase its distance from any point of that interval. Because \(\widetilde r\ge0\), this gives
\[
|\widehat r-r|\le|\widetilde r-r|,
\qquad
d_{\mathrm{cap}}=(\widetilde r-C_h)_+\le|\widetilde r-r|.
\]
The raw first-marginal identity and the definition of \(\delta\) give
\[
\int\beta\widetilde r\,dS-\int\beta\,dT=\delta.
\]
Since \(\widehat r=\widetilde r-d_{\mathrm{cap}}\), subtraction proves the displayed training calibration identity. Finally, substituting the same equality into the definition of the held-out residual gives
\[
\Delta_{h,J_{r,n}}(v)-\Delta^{\mathrm{raw}}_{h,J_{r,n}}(v)
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n d_{\mathrm{cap}}^{(-k_S(i))}(X_i)v(X_i)\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}.
\]
The pointwise bound on \(d_{\mathrm{cap}}\) and the triangle inequality give the last inequality. \(\square\)
