\textbf{Proof.}
Conditional on the source covariates and treatment assignments, the variables \(\epsilon_i=Z_i-\mu(X_i)\) are independent and centered by trial randomization and consistency.  They are uniformly bounded by a constant depending only on \(\varepsilon\), and their conditional variances are bounded below by a positive class constant by residual nondegeneracy and treatment positivity.  Thus the Berry--Esseen numerator for the source term is bounded by
\[
C\sum_i|\Gamma_{i,h_n}|^3
\le C\max_i|\Gamma_{i,h_n}|\sum_i\Gamma_{i,h_n}^2.
\]
After division by \(\{\sum_i\Gamma_{i,h_n}^2\}^{3/2}\), the result is \(o_P(1)\) by the leverage conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem:direct\text{-}series\text{-}linearization}\).  The target variables \(m^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\) are independent, centered, and bounded; their maximum leverage is \(O(m^{-1})\).  Source and target samples are independent.  Conditioning first on the covariate-trained coefficients, applying \(\mathrm{lem:berry\text{-}esseen\text{-}independent\text{-}array}\), and then removing the conditioning gives the asserted uniform Kolmogorov convergence.  The perturbation of the coefficients caused by replacing population covariate moments with fold moments is \(o_P\{n^{-1/2}V_a(h_n)^{1/2}\}\) by the good-Gram bounds and the same Bernstein calculation used in the localized-series lemma, so it does not alter the limit.

For variance estimation, cross-fitting makes \(\widehat\epsilon_i\) independent of \(Z_i\) conditional on its training complement.  Expanding
\[
\widehat\epsilon_i^2-\epsilon_i^2
=-2\epsilon_i\{\widehat\mu^{(-k_S(i))}(X_i)-\mu(X_i)\}
+\{\widehat\mu^{(-k_S(i))}(X_i)-\mu(X_i)\}^2
\]
and applying conditional Bernstein, the localized regression rate, and \(\max_i\Gamma_i^2/\sum_i\Gamma_i^2=o_P(1)\) shows
\[
\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\widehat\epsilon_i^2
-\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\mathbb E(\epsilon_i^2\mid X_i)=o_P\left(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\right).
\]
The target sample variance is consistent because the spliced regression converges in \(L^2(P_T)\); on \([0,h_n]\) this follows from the boundary Gram bound and \(nh_n^{a+1}\to\infty\), and on \([h_n,1]\) from the localized series rate.  These two independent variance pieces give sandwich consistency.

Finally, the proof of the linearization lemma bounds the conditional bias by a fixed constant times exactly the three terms used in \(\widehat B\).  Choosing \(C_{\mathrm{class}}\) as the displayed maximum makes \(|R_{n,m}|\le\widehat B\) on the good event apart from a probability tending uniformly to zero.  The complement of the good event is exponentially small. \(\square\)
