\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(p=dQ_1/d\nu\) and \(q=dQ_0/d\nu\) for \(\nu=Q_0+Q_1\), and let \(A=\{p\ge q\}\). The log-sum inequality, obtained by applying Jensen's inequality separately on \(A\) and \(A^c\), gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
\ge \operatorname{kl}\{Q_1(A),Q_0(A)\}.
\]
For fixed \(v\in(0,1)\), the binary divergence as a function of \(u\) has value and first derivative zero at \(u=v\) and second derivative \(1/\{u(1-u)\}\ge4\). Taylor's integral formula therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{kl}(u,v)\ge2(u-v)^2.
\]
The boundary cases follow by continuity. Since \(Q_1(A)-Q_0(A)=\operatorname{TV}(Q_1,Q_0)\),
\[
\operatorname{TV}(Q_1,Q_0)
\le\sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)/2}.
\]

Now put \(E=\{|T-\theta_0|<\Delta/2\}\). On \(E^c\), the squared error under \(Q_0\) is at least \(\Delta^2/4\); on \(E\), the triangle inequality makes the squared error under \(Q_1\) at least \(\Delta^2/4\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}(T-\theta_0)^2+\mathbb E_{Q_1}(T-\theta_1)^2
\ge\frac{\Delta^2}{4}\{Q_0(E^c)+Q_1(E)\}
\ge\frac{\Delta^2}{4}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_1,Q_0)\}.
\]
The larger of the two mean squared errors is at least half their sum. Substitute the total-variation bound and take square roots. \(\square\)
