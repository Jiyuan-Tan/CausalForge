\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\mathcal C=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_n,X_1^T,\ldots,X_m^T)\).  By the positive ridge terms in \(\mathrm{def:regression\text{-}fit}\), every foldwise regression coefficient is unique and is linear in the source pseudo-outcomes in its training complement.  The boundary normal equations give
\[
\mathbf R_h(x)^\top\widehat\alpha_{k,h}
=\sum_{q=1}^n
\frac{\mathbf 1\{q\in\mathcal T_k^S\}}{|\mathcal T_k^S|}
\mathbf 1\{X_q\le 2h\}
\mathbf R_h(x)^\top\widehat G_{k,h}^{-1}\mathbf R_h(X_q)Z_q
\qquad(0\le x<h).
\]
Likewise, differentiating the strictly convex interior ridge criterion and using the definition of \(\widehat H_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}\) gives
\[
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)^\top\widehat\beta_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}
=\sum_{q=1}^n
\frac{\mathbf 1\{q\in\mathcal T_k^S\}}{|\mathcal T_k^S|}
\mathbf 1\{X_q\ge h\}
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)^\top
\widehat H_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}^{-1}
\mathbf b_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_q)Z_q
\qquad(h\le x\le1).
\]
Both formulas remain valid when the corresponding local subsample is empty, because then the right-hand side and the ridge solution are zero.  Comparing the two displays with \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\) yields, for every fold \(k\) and every \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\widehat\mu^{(-k)}_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)
=\sum_{q=1}^n L_{q,k,h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)Z_q.
\tag{1}
\]

Substitute (1) into the target plug-in term and the fitted-regression term in \(\mathrm{def:spliced\text{-}estimator}\).  The coefficient of a fixed \(Z_q\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
{}&\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mL_{q,k_T(j),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_j^T)
+\frac1n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(q))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_q)
  \mathbf 1\{X_q\ge h\}\\
&\quad-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)
\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}L_{q,k_S(i),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\), (2) is \(\Gamma_q(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\).  This proves
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}
=\sum_{q=1}^n\Gamma_q(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})Z_q.
\tag{3}
\]
The ridge matrices and the RiCOT primal--dual programs depend only on the observed covariates.  Hence every coefficient in (3) is \(\mathcal C\)-measurable, including on the deterministic zero-interior-mass branch of \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}weights}\).

We next establish the conditional mean of the pseudo-outcome.  Consistency, conditional trial randomization, and the definition of the known randomization propensity imply, for \(P_S\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P_S}(Z\mid X=x)
&=\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}(AY\mid X=x)}{e(x)}
-\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}\{(1-A)Y\mid X=x\}}{1-e(x)}\\
&=\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(1)\mid X=x\}
-\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(0)\mid X=x\}\\
&=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)=\mu(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
The divisions in (4) are valid because treatment positivity gives \(e(x)\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\).  Adding and subtracting \(\sum_i\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)\) and \(m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)\) in (3), and using the defining identity \(\tau=\mathbb E_{P_T}\{\mu(X^T)\}\), proves the stated exact error decomposition.

By subadditivity of the H\"older norm, the two response-smoothness assumptions give
\[
\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M).
\]
Because \(0\le\mu_d\le1\), also \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\).  Therefore \(\mu\) is feasible in the supremum defining \(B_X\), and hence
\[
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)
-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)\right|
\le B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}).
\tag{5}
\]

Treatment positivity and \(0\le Y\le1\) imply \(-\varepsilon^{-1}\le Z_i\le\varepsilon^{-1}\).  The product sampling law implies that, conditionally on \(\mathcal C\), the source pseudo-outcomes are independent; (4) gives their conditional means.  Thus the conditionally centered summand
\[
W_i=\Gamma_i(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}
\]
has conditional range length at most \(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\).  The conditional version of \(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\), with \(\delta=\gamma/2\), gives almost surely
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right|>R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})
\,\middle|\,\mathcal C\right\}
\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, the lemma's threshold is
\[
\sqrt{\frac12\log(4/\gamma)\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{4\Gamma_i^2}{\varepsilon^2}}
=R_S(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}).
\]

Independence and identical distribution of the target sample, the equality \(\mathbb E_{P_T}\mu(X^T)=\tau\), and \(\mu(X^T)\in[-1,1]\) allow a second application of \(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\) to the independent variables \(m^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\).  Their range lengths are \(2/m\), so, again with \(\delta=\gamma/2\),
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left\{
\left|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|>R_T(m)
\right\}
\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{7}
\]
Taking expectations in (6), combining (5)--(7) with the exact decomposition, and applying the union bound prove the advertised probability bound.

Finally, on a realized covariate design satisfying \(B_X\le b\) and \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\le g^2\), the definitions of the two radii give
\[
B_X+R_S+R_T
\le b+\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)}\,g
+\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)/m}.
\]
No smoothness or localization property of either density factor was used.  In particular, the proof is unchanged when the two quarantined density-rate conditions are removed from \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), and it makes none of the rate or attainment claims excluded in the theorem statement. \(\square\)
