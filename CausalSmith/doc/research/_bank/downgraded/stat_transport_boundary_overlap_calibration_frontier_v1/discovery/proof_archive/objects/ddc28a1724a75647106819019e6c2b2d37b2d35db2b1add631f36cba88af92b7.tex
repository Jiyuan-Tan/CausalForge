A randomized trial may contain progressively fewer patients near a clinically important covariate boundary even while the target population retains positive density there. The causal target is identified as the observed-law integral \(\tau=\int_0^1\{\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)\}f_T(x)\,dx\). The source-side minimax lower rate is \(n^{-1/2}\) for \(a<1\), \(\sqrt{\log n/n}\) at \(a=1\), and \(n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}\) for \(a>1\). A separate \(m^{-1/2}\) witness is available when the target-density bounds have strict slack, while under \(m/n\to\kappa\) the source lower bound alone controls the displayed two-sample order without that slack. A truncated-oracle bias--variance calculation matches the three regimes. The squared-distance Riesz-calibrated transport estimator and boundary local-polynomial splice are explicit, but their held-out optimizer stability and empirical risk attainment remain open. The corrected fixed-class interval is also explicit and now includes the observable fold-varying discrepancy \(|C_{n,m}(h)|\); its coverage and expected-length guarantees remain open pending the weight, Gaussian, multiplier, and moment bounds.
