\textbf{Proof.}
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:direct\text{-}series\text{-}linearization}\) at \(h=h_n\).  By independence of the two samples, residual centering, and the coefficient norm bound,
\[
\left\{\mathbb E\left(\sum_i\Gamma_{i,h_n}\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\le Cn^{-1/2}V_a(h_n)^{1/2},
\]
whereas
\[
\left\{\mathbb E\left[\frac1m\sum_j\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\right]^2\right\}^{1/2}
\le m^{-1/2}
\]
because \(|\mu|\le1\).  Minkowski's inequality and the remainder bound give
\[
\|\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}-\tau\|_2
\le C\{m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}+n^{-1/2}V_a(h_n)^{1/2}
+\delta_{s,h_n}\delta_{t,h_n}+\lambda_n(J_{\mu,n}+J_{r,n})\}.
\]
The exponentially rare fallback contributes at most its square-root probability times a fixed moment bound; this is smaller than every displayed polynomial term.  By \(\mathrm{lem:oracle\text{-}truncation\text{-}envelope}\), the first three terms equal \(m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\) up to constants.  The probability, empirical norm, and leverage assertions are exactly those of \(\mathrm{lem:localized\text{-}direct\text{-}series\text{-}control}\).

It remains to identify when the product is absorbed.  Put
\[
\alpha=\frac{s}{2s+1}+\frac{t}{2t+1}.
\]
For \(a<1\), \(N_{h_n}=n\), so \(\delta_{s,h_n}\delta_{t,h_n}=O\{(\log n/n)^\alpha\}=o(n^{-1/2})\) because \(\alpha>1/2\).  For \(a\ge1\), the dominant product has polynomial order
\[
\left(\frac{\log n}{N_{h_n}}\right)^\alpha.
\]
At \(a=1\), comparison with \(\sqrt{\log n/n}\) is favorable exactly when \((2s+1)\alpha\ge s+1\), equivalently \(t(2s-1)\ge1\).  For \(a>1\), equality leaves a logarithmic factor, so strict inequality, equivalently \(t(2s-1)>1\), is required.  Since \(\lambda_n(J_{\mu,n}+J_{r,n})=o(n^{-1})\), the ridge term is always negligible.  This proves the proposed theorem, including the honest unmatched regimes. \(\square\)
