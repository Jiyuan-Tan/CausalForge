\textbf{Proof.}
We construct a rough alternative and a target-density alternative around the same smoother law. Put
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1,
\qquad
\ell(x)=x-\frac12.
\]
Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\), and take Bernoulli potential outcomes with
\[
\mu_0(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x).
\]
Use the fixed known propensity and make treatment conditionally independent of the potential outcomes. Constant density factors satisfy \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\) for every \(t>0\). Since \(\ell\) is infinitely smooth, reducing \(\delta_0\) puts both regressions strictly inside the radius-\(M\) balls for orders \(s_1\) and \(s_2\), keeps the means in \([0,1]\), and preserves the residual-variance lower bound. Let \(Q_0\) be the resulting observed-data law. It belongs to \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\), and its response functions also satisfy the order-\(s_1\) restrictions.

Set
\[
h=n^{-1/(2s_1+a+1)},
\qquad
b_{h,s_1}(x)=h^{s_1}K(x/h)\mathbf 1\{0\le x\le h\}.
\]
Construct \(Q_1\) by replacing the treated regression by
\[
\mu_{1,1}(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x)+\delta b_{h,s_1}(x)
\]
and leaving every other component unchanged. Smooth bump scaling and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\) place \(Q_1\) in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t)\). Because the baseline Bernoulli probabilities are uniformly separated from zero and one, the same calculation as in \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
\le Cn\delta^2h^{2s_1+a+1}
=C\delta^2,
\]
whereas
\[
\Delta_S:=|\tau(Q_1)-\tau(Q_0)|
=\delta h^{s_1+1}\int_0^1K(u)\,du
\ge c_Sn^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\]
Choose \(\delta\) so small that, by the total-variation inequality in \(\mathrm{lem:two\text{-}point\text{-}testing\text{-}risk}\),
\[
\operatorname{TV}(Q_1,Q_0)\le\eta_S<1-2\gamma.
\]

Let \(\mathcal C_{n,m}\) be any interval satisfying the asserted uniform asymptotic coverage. Uniformity gives
\[
Q_0\{\tau(Q_0)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}\ge1-\gamma-o(1),
\qquad
Q_1\{\tau(Q_1)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}\}\ge1-\gamma-o(1).
\]
Applying \(\mathrm{lem:two\text{-}point\text{-}interval\text{-}length}\) with reference law \(Q_0\) yields, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
\ge c_1n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\]

For the second alternative, retain exactly the same source law and response functions and replace only the target density by
\[
f_{T,2}(x)=1+u_m\ell(x),
\qquad
u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\]
The strict target-density slack and the infinite smoothness of \(\ell\) make this an admissible order-\(t\) density for every \(t>0\) when \(\delta_1>0\) is fixed and sufficiently small. Denote the resulting law by \(Q_2\). It belongs to both response-smoothness classes. Directly,
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_2,Q_0)
\le Cm u_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
\le C\delta_1^2,
\]
and
\[
\Delta_T:=|\tau(Q_2)-\tau(Q_0)|
=\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
=\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{12}m^{-1/2}.
\]
Reduce \(\delta_1\) so that \(\operatorname{TV}(Q_2,Q_0)\le\eta_T<1-2\gamma\). Uniform coverage and the interval-length lemma, again under the identical smoother reference law \(Q_0\), give
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
\ge c_2m^{-1/2}.
\]
The two lower bounds hold at the same \(Q_0\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\). Therefore \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\) proves
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
\ge c\left\{m^{-1/2}+n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}\right\}.
\]
No nuisance-estimation smoothness threshold appears in the construction. \(\square\)
