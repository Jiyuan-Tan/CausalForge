\textbf{Proof.}
Fix arbitrary \(t>0\).  We construct the full-data laws explicitly and verify every support, normalization, positivity, smoothness, and variance condition in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).

For the source-boundary witness, set
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad
 f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
Both functions integrate to one.  Their nonsingular factors are
\(g_S(x)=a+1\) and \(g_T(x)=1\).  The declared parameter ranges and the hypotheses give
\[
 c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
 \qquad
 c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}.
\tag{2}
\]
The factors are constant, so they belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every fixed \(t>0\); the source-factor radius is valid because \(M\ge a+1\).  Thus (1) verifies density support, normalization, the two boundary-factor inequalities, and density-factor smoothness without either of the deleted estimator-side smoothness inequalities.

Keep the fixed known randomization probability \(e\), which satisfies
\(\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\varepsilon\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), let \(A\) be Bernoulli with success probability \(e(x)\), independently of the two source potential outcomes, and let those potential outcomes be Bernoulli with means
\[
 \mu_{0,+}(x)=\mu_{0,-}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\tag{3}
\]
Set \(Y=Y^S(A)\), and give the target potential outcomes the same conditional means.  This construction verifies consistency, conditional treatment randomization, the known propensity, and conditional-mean transportability.

Write \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\).  Dilation of the fixed smooth bump gives, for \(0\le v\le p\),
\[
 \lVert D^v b_h\rVert_\infty\le C_Kh^{s-v},
 \qquad
 [D^p b_h]_{\beta}\le C_K.
\tag{4}
\]
Hence \(\sup_{0<h\le1}\lVert b_h\rVert_{\mathcal H^s}\le C_K'\).  Since \(M>4\), choose a fixed \(\delta>0\), independent of \(h,n,m\), sufficiently small that the two treated regressions in (3) lie in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), take values in \([0,1]\), and obey
\[
 \frac14-\delta^2\lVert K\rVert_\infty^2\ge M^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
The control response is constant and belongs to the same ball.  Bernoulli variance is \(p(1-p)\), so (5) verifies residual nondegeneracy in both arms.  Consequently the induced observed two-sample laws \(\mathbb P_{+,h}\) and \(\mathbb P_{-,h}\) belong to \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\).

For \(|u|\le\delta\lVert K\rVert_\infty<1/2\), direct Bernoulli algebra yields
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(
   \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
   \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)
 \right)
 =2u\log\frac{1+2u}{1-2u}
 \le C_0u^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Only the treated conditional-outcome law changes, and its contribution is multiplied by \(e(x)\le1\).  Additivity of relative entropy for independent products, (1), (3), and (6) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,n},P_{-,h}^{S,n})
 &\le C_0n\delta^2\int_0^hb_h(x)^2(a+1)x^a\,dx\\
 &=C_0(a+1)\delta^2nh^{2s+a+1}
   \int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du\\
 &\le Cnh^{2s+a+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero,
\(k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0\).  The target covariate distribution is common to the two alternatives, and therefore
\[
 \left|\tau(\mathbb P_{+,h})-\tau(\mathbb P_{-,h})\right|
 =2\delta\int_0^hb_h(x)\,dx
 =2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (7) and (8) prove the source part with constants independent of \(n,m,h\).

For the independent target-sample witness, retain the source density in (1), put \(\ell(x)=x-1/2\), and use the common source responses
\[
 \mu_0(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x).
\tag{9}
\]
The function \(\ell\) is smooth of every order.  A sufficiently small fixed \(\delta_0>0\) therefore makes (9) satisfy the response-radius, outcome-support, and residual-variance conditions for the fixed \(s>0\).  Construct source and target potential outcomes as above, so all identification assumptions continue to hold.  Keep this entire source law fixed and define
\[
 f_{T,\pm}(x)=1\pm u_m\ell(x),
 \qquad
 u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), both functions in (10) integrate to one.  The strict slack \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\), positivity of \(m\), and smoothness of \(\ell\) permit one fixed \(\delta_1>0\) such that, for every \(m\ge1\), both densities are positive, lie between the target-factor bounds, and belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\).  Thus the corresponding full-data laws are members of the model class, their observed source projections agree, and their observed target projections have the densities in (10).

The lower target density is bounded away from zero.  The elementary inequality \(\log v\le v-1\) implies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(p,q)
 \le\int\frac{(p-q)^2}{q}
\]
for two densities \(p,q\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{T,+}^{\otimes m},P_{T,-}^{\otimes m})
 &\le m\int_0^1
 \frac{\{f_{T,+}(x)-f_{T,-}(x)\}^2}{f_{T,-}(x)}\,dx\\
 &\le C_1mu_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 \le C_2\delta_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Finally, \(\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx=1/12\), so (9)--(10) give
\[
 \left|\tau_+-\tau_-\right|
 =2\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 =\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{6}m^{-1/2}.
\tag{12}
\]
The divergence constant in (11) can be made arbitrarily small by reducing \(\delta_1\).  This proves the target part. \(\square\)
