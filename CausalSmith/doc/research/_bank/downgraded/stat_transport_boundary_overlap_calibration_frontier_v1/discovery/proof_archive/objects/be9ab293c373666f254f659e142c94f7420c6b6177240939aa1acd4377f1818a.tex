\textbf{Proof.}
We prove the assertion by explicit subexperiments contained in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\).  The source and target density factors in all of them are constant, so their membership does not require any strict slack in the density bounds.

We first record the elementary testing inequality used in each regime.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two laws with parameter values \(\tau_0,\tau_1\), and put \(d=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\).  Given an estimator \(\widehat\tau\), classify an observation by the nearer parameter value.  An error under \(Q_u\) implies \(|\widehat\tau-\tau_u|\ge d/2\).  Since the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\),
\[
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}
       [ (\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2 ]
 \ge \frac{d^2}{8}
       \{1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, let a Hahn set for \(Q_0-Q_1\) have probabilities \(p\) and \(q\) under \(Q_0\) and \(Q_1\).  The log-sum inequality applied to this set and its complement gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \ge p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}.
\]
For fixed \(q\), the right-hand side minus \(2(p-q)^2\) vanishes with zero first derivative at \(p=q\), and its second derivative is
\[
 \frac{1}{p(1-p)}-4\ge0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}^2
 \le \frac12\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus a pair with sufficiently small constant Kullback--Leibler divergence yields root mean-squared risk bounded below by a constant multiple of its parameter separation.

For every regime, take
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{3}
\]
Both are densities, with \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\).  The declared parameter ranges and \(M\ge a+1\) imply
\[
 c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
 \qquad c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+},
\tag{4}
\]
and put both constant factors in \(\mathcal H^t(M)\).  In particular, this construction remains admissible when either target-density bound is attained.

Draw \(A\) with the fixed known propensity \(e(X)\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), take the two source potential outcomes to be independent Bernoulli variables having the arm-specific means specified below and independent of \(A\); take the target potential outcomes to have the same conditional means, and set \(Y=Y^S(A)\).  This verifies \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(\mathrm{ass:trial\text{-}randomization}\), \(\mathrm{ass:known\text{-}propensity}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:mean\text{-}transportability}\).  The fixed propensity satisfies \(\mathrm{ass:treatment\text{-}positivity}\).  Since \(M>4\), define
\[
 \Delta_M=\sqrt{\frac14-\frac1M}>0.
\]
All perturbation constants below are chosen sufficiently small that every conditional mean differs from \(1/2\) by at most \(\Delta_M/2\).  Hence the outcomes lie in \([0,1]\), and, for either arm,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)
 \ge \frac14-\frac{\Delta_M^2}{4}
 >\frac1M.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{ass:residual\text{-}nondegeneracy}\) holds.  The control regression is constant, and the scaling calculations below put every treated regression in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\).  The target covariate law in (3) is common to all alternatives, so the \(m\) target observations contribute zero divergence for every \(m\).

\emph{The nonregular case \(a>1\).}
For \(h\in(0,1)\) and a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), let
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\tag{6}
\]
Put \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\).  For \(0\le v\le p\), dilation gives \(\lVert D^v b_h\rVert_\infty\le C h^{s-v}\), while \([D^p b_h]_{\beta}\le C\), with \(C\) depending only on the fixed bump \(K\).  Reducing \(\delta\) if necessary therefore puts both regressions in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), uniformly in \(h\).

For \(|u|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, direct calculation and Taylor's theorem give
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
  \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)
 \right)
 =2u\log\frac{1+2u}{1-2u}
 \le C_0u^2.
\tag{7}
\]
The control-arm conditional laws agree, while the treated-arm contribution is multiplied by \(e(x)\le1\).  Additivity of relative entropy for product laws, (3), and (7) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,\otimes n},P_{-,h}^{S,\otimes n})
 &\le Cn\int_0^h b_h(x)^2x^a\,dx\\
 &=Cnh^{2s+a+1}\int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Because \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero,
\[
 k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0,
 \qquad
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Choose \(h=c_hn^{-1/(2s+a+1)}\), with \(c_h>0\) small enough that (8) is bounded by a sufficiently small constant.  Equations (1), (2), and (9) imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge c n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\tag{10}
\]

\emph{The regular case \(a<1\).}
Choose a nonnegative, nonzero \(C^\infty\) function \(\varphi\) supported in \([1/4,3/4]\), and set
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}\varphi(x).
\tag{11}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), these regressions satisfy the range, variance, and H\"older restrictions.  On the support of \(\varphi\), the source density is bounded above and below by positive constants.  Hence (7) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_+^{S,\otimes n},P_-^{S,\otimes n})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{12}
\]
Under the uniform target density,
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1\varphi(x)\,dx
 \ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
After reducing \(\delta\), (1), (2), (12), and (13) prove
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{14}
\]

\emph{The critical case \(a=1\).}
Put \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), fix an integer \(j_0\ge1\), and choose \(L=L_n\) so that
\[
 \ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}},
 \qquad
 L-j_0+1\asymp\log n.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(j_0\le j\le L\), define
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\}.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{L-j_0+1}\), let
\[
 \mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+
 \sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x).
\tag{17}
\]
The shell interiors are disjoint.  Equation (15) implies, uniformly for \(j_0\le j\le L\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}\le C\ell_j^s.
\tag{18}
\]
More explicitly, for \(0\le v\le p\),
\[
 \lVert D^v\phi_j\rVert_\infty
 \le C\delta n^{-1/2}\ell_j^{-(v+1)}
 \le C\delta\ell_j^{s-v},
 \qquad
 [D^p\phi_j]_{\beta}
 \le C\delta n^{-1/2}\ell_j^{-(s+1)}
 \le C\delta.
\tag{19}
\]
Because the zero extension of the smooth compactly supported bump and all its derivatives vanish at shell endpoints, the disjoint pieces paste across their common endpoints with global order-\(s\) H\"older norm at most \(C'\delta\), independently of \(L\).  Taking \(\delta\) small therefore makes every alternative in (17) satisfy both response-smoothness assumptions and (5).

The contribution of coordinate \(j\) to the target parameter is
\[
 d_j:=\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)\,dx
 =\frac{\delta k_1}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(\omega^{(j)}\) is obtained by flipping only coordinate \(j\), then \(f_S(x)=2x\), and (7) implies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{S,\otimes n},
                    P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{S,\otimes n})
 \le Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
\tag{21}
\]
uniformly in \(j,n\), and the other signs.

Place the uniform product prior \(\Pi\) on \(\omega\).  Conditional on all observed covariates, each source likelihood contribution involves at most one shell coordinate.  Because the target law is sign-free, the full likelihood and hence the posterior factor across coordinates.  Conditional expectation minimizes squared loss, and \(\tau_\omega=\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j\omega_j\); therefore
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}
 \mathbb E_\Pi[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 =\mathbb E_\Pi[\operatorname{Var}(\tau_\omega\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})]
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
   \mathbb E_\Pi[\operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})].
\tag{22}
\]
Write \(q_j=\mathbb E_\Pi[\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}]\).  Since \(\omega_j\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|.
\tag{23}
\]
Let \(\overline P_{j,+}\) and \(\overline P_{j,-}\) be the mixture experiments conditional on \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\), respectively.  Bayes' formula gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi[|q_j|]
 =\lVert\overline P_{j,+}-\overline P_{j,-}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\tag{24}
\]
Pair every sign vector with its \(j\)-flip.  The pointwise log-sum inequality for the paired densities, followed by (21), gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\overline P_{j,+},\overline P_{j,-})
 \le 2^{-(L-j_0)}
 \sum_{\omega:\,\omega_j=1}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega,P_{\omega^{(j)}})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{25}
\]
The target factors cancel in (25) because their law is common.  Equations (2) and (23)--(25), with a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\), yield
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi[\operatorname{Var}
   (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})]
 \ge c_1>0
\tag{26}
\]
uniformly in \(j\).  Consequently, (15), (20), and (22) give
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 \ge c_1\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
 \ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\tag{27}
\]
Taking square roots proves the critical lower bound \(c\sqrt{\log n/n}\) without a hidden polynomial loss.

The bounds (10), (14), and (27), together with \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rate}\), prove that, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(m\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\rho_n(a,s).
\]
All constructed full-data laws satisfy every member-property in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), and every construction uses the boundary-attaining target density \(f_T=1\).  This proves the no-slack assertion as well. \(\square\)
