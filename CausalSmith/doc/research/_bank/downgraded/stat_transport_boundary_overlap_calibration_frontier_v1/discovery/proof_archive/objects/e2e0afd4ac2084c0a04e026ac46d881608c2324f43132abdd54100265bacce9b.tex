\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\mathcal C\) be the sigma-field generated by all source and target covariates. The positive ridge terms in \(\mathrm{def:regression\text{-}fit}\) make every foldwise regression coefficient a unique linear function of the source pseudo-outcomes in its training complement. The matrices and smoother coefficients in \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\) are exactly the coefficients obtained from the corresponding normal equations. Therefore, for every fold \(k\), every \(x\in[0,1]\), and \(d_{\mu,n}=J_{\mu,n}+q\),
\[
\widehat\mu^{(-k)}_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)
=\sum_{q_0=1}^n L_{q_0,k,h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)Z_{q_0}.
\tag{1}
\]
The folds are well defined because \(n,m\ge2\); the positive ridges make all displayed inverses exist even when a local subsample is empty. Substitution of (1) into the target plug-in term and the fitted-regression term of \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}_{h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}\) shows that the coefficient of a fixed \(Z_{q_0}\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
{}&\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mL_{q_0,k_T(j),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_j^T)
+\frac1n\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(q_0))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_{q_0})\mathbf 1\{X_{q_0}\ge h\}\\
&\quad-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}
L_{q_0,k_S(i),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Expression (2) is \(\Gamma_{q_0}(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\) by \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}linear\text{-}coefficients}\), proving the first identity. Every factor in (2) is \(\mathcal C\)-measurable: the ridge Gram matrices and branch-qualified RiCOT primal--dual programs depend only on the observed covariates, the empty-interior fallback is deterministic, the optimizers and tie-broken multipliers are selected uniquely, and the pointwise cap is measurable. Hence every \(\Gamma_{q_0}\) is \(\mathcal C\)-measurable.

By consistency, conditional trial randomization, and the definition of the known randomization propensity, for \(P_S\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P_S}(Z\mid X=x)
&=\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}(AY\mid X=x)}{e(x)}
-\frac{\mathbb E_{P_S}\{(1-A)Y\mid X=x\}}{1-e(x)}\\
&=\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(1)\mid X=x\}
-\mathbb E_{F^S_{n,m}}\{Y^S(0)\mid X=x\}\\
&=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)=\mu(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The divisions in (3) are valid because treatment positivity gives \(e(x)\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\). Adding and subtracting \(\sum_i\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)\) and \(m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)\) in the first identity gives the displayed exact error decomposition.

We next prove that the finite jet programs are valid certificates. Put \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\), as in \(\mathrm{def:bias\text{-}envelope}\). Let \(v\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\) with \(\|v\|_\infty\le1\), and at each distinct site set
\[
u_{\ell,r}=v^{(r)}(z_\ell),\qquad 0\le r\le p.
\tag{4}
\]
The magnitude constraints in the jet programs follow immediately from the stated H\"older convention. For two sites \(z_k,z_\ell\), Taylor's identity applied to \(v^{(r)}\), with the \(\beta\)-H\"older bound on \(v^{(p)}\), gives
\[
\left|v^{(r)}(z_\ell)-\sum_{q=r}^p
\frac{v^{(q)}(z_k)}{(q-r)!}(z_\ell-z_k)^{q-r}\right|
\le 2M\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s-r+1)}
|z_\ell-z_k|^{s-r}.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(r=p\), (5) is exactly the defining H\"older inequality. For \(r<p\), its constant follows by integrating the remainder kernel:
\[
\frac1{(p-r-1)!}\int_0^d(d-u)^{p-r-1}u^\beta\,du
=\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s-r+1)}d^{s-r},
\qquad d=|z_\ell-z_k|.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus every H\"older trace is feasible for both sign programs.

The coefficients \(c_\ell\) in \(\mathrm{def:bias\text{-}envelope}\) collect all repeated observations at a common site, so
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i v(X_i)-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m v(X_j^T)
=\sum_{\ell=1}^N c_\ell v(z_\ell).
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (4)--(7) imply, first for the positive sign and then for the negative sign,
\[
B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\le
\overline B_X(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}).
\tag{8}
\]
The programs have the explicitly indexed variables \(u_{\ell,r}\), hence \(N(p+1)\) variables. The box constraints contribute \(O(Np)\) inequalities and the ordered pairs \((\ell,k)\) and derivative indices contribute \(O(N^2p)\) inequalities after each absolute-value constraint is split into two linear inequalities. This proves the computational certificate claim.

If \(0<s\le1\), then \(p=0\), and feasibility is precisely
\[
|u_{\ell,0}|\le1,
\qquad |u_{\ell,0}-u_{k,0}|\le2M|z_\ell-z_k|^s.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(d_s(x,y)=|x-y|^s\) is a metric on \([0,1]\), define directly
\[
V_0(x)=\min_{1\le\ell\le N}\{u_{\ell,0}+2M d_s(x,z_\ell)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The triangle inequality shows that \(|V_0(x)-V_0(y)|\le2M d_s(x,y)\). Moreover, the term \(\ell=k\) gives \(V_0(z_k)\le u_{k,0}\), while (9) gives \(u_{\ell,0}+2M d_s(z_k,z_\ell)\ge u_{k,0}\) for every \(\ell\), so \(V_0(z_k)=u_{k,0}\). Pointwise projection of \(V_0\) onto \([-1,1]\) is nonexpansive, preserves these site values, and preserves the H\"older constant. Hence every feasible objective value comes from a function in \(\mathcal H^s(2M)\) with sup norm at most one. Combining this reverse inequality with (8) proves \(B_X=\overline B_X\) for \(0<s\le1\). For \(s>1\), (8) remains valid, but the pairwise Taylor inequalities are used only as necessary trace conditions, so no exact Whitney-extension claim is made.

The response-smoothness assumptions and subadditivity of the H\"older norm imply \(\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\). Since \(0\le\mu_d\le1\), also \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\). Taking \(v=\mu\) in (7)--(8) yields the displayed two-step deterministic bias bound.

Treatment positivity and \(0\le Y\le1\) imply \(-\varepsilon^{-1}\le Z_i\le\varepsilon^{-1}\). Conditional on \(\mathcal C\), the source pseudo-outcomes are independent and centered as in (3). The conditional range length of \(\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\) is at most \(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\). Applying \(\mathrm{lem:bounded\text{-}sum\text{-}hoeffding}\) conditionally with \(\delta=\gamma/2\) gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left(
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right|>R_S
\,\middle|\,\mathcal C\right)\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{11}
\]
Independence of the target sample, the identity \(\mathbb E_{P_T}\mu(X^T)=\tau\), and the support bound \(\mu(X^T)\in[-1,1]\) permit a second application of the same lemma to the independent variables \(m^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\), whose range lengths are \(2/m\). Again taking \(\delta=\gamma/2\),
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left(
\left|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|>R_T(m)
\right)\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{12}
\]
Taking expectations in (11), combining (8), (11), and (12) with the exact decomposition, and applying the union bound prove the coverage probability. Finally, \(\overline B_X\le b\) and \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\le g^2\), together with the definitions of \(R_S\) and \(R_T\), give the asserted realized-design radius. No density-smoothness condition, held-out density-ratio localization, leverage bound, or stochastic rate for the observable diagnostics enters the argument. \(\square\)
