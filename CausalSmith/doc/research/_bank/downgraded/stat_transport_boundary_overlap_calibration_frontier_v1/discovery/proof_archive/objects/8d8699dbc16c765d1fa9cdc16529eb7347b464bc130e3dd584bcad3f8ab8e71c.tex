\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\mathrm{lem:riesz\text{-}threshold}\), uniformly over the model class,
\[
 \left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}
 \asymp
 \begin{cases}
 1,&a<1,\\
 \sqrt{\log(1/h)},&a=1,\\
 h^{(1-a)/2},&a>1.
 \end{cases}
\]
If \(a<1\), then \(h_n=n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}\), so \(h_n^{s+1}=n^{-1/2}\), and the oracle stochastic term is also of order \(n^{-1/2}\).  If \(a=1\), then
\[
 h_n^{s+1}=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}},
 \qquad
 \log(1/h_n)=\frac{\log n-\log\log n}{2(s+1)}\asymp\log n,
\]
and hence \(n^{-1/2}\sqrt{\log(1/h_n)}\asymp\sqrt{\log n/n}\).  If \(a>1\), writing \(D=2s+a+1\) gives
\[
 h_n^{s+1}=n^{-(s+1)/D},
\]
and
\[
 n^{-1/2}h_n^{(1-a)/2}
 =n^{-1/2+(a-1)/(2D)}
 =n^{-(s+1)/D}.
\]
The three cases are exactly \(\rho_n(a,s)\); adding the independent term \(m^{-1/2}\) proves the claim. \(\square\)
