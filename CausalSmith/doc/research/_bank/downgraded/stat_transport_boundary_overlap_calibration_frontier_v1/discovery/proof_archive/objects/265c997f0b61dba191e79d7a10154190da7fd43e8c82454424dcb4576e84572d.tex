\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a member law \(P\) and \(h\in(0,1)\). Put
\[
\mathbf R_1(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^p)^\top,
\qquad
A_a=\int_0^1\mathbf R_1(u)\mathbf R_1(u)^\top u^a\,du.
\tag{1}
\]
For every nonzero \(z\in\mathbb R^{p+1}\), the polynomial
\(u\mapsto z^\top\mathbf R_1(u)\) is nonzero except at finitely many points. Since
\(u^a>0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(u\in(0,1)\),
\[
z^\top A_a z=\int_0^1\{z^\top\mathbf R_1(u)\}^2u^a\,du>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \(A_a\) is positive definite. The change of variables \(x=hu\), the identity
\(f_S(x)=x^ag_S(x)\), and \(\mathrm{ass:source\text{-}boundary\text{-}factor}\) give the Loewner bounds
\[
c_{S,-}h^{a+1}A_a
\preceq G_h^{\circ}(P)
\preceq c_{S,+}h^{a+1}A_a.
\tag{3}
\]
All eigenvalues of \(G_h^{\circ}(P)\) are therefore positive, so its inverse in the oracle definition exists. This also records explicitly the rank and support condition used below: every nonzero polynomial coordinate has positive squared mass on the positive-length interval \((0,h)\).

Let \(\ell(x)=c^\top\mathbf R_h(x)\) be an arbitrary polynomial of degree at most \(p\). By the definitions of \(G_h^{\circ}(P)\), \(q_h^{\circ}(P)\), and \(w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(\cdot;P)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^h w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)\ell(x)f_S(x)\,dx
&=\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}
  \left\{\int_0^h\mathbf R_h(x)\mathbf R_h(x)^\top f_S(x)\,dx\right\}c\\
&=\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top c
=\int_0^h\ell(x)f_T(x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
This proves exact polynomial reproduction.

We next establish the size of the boundary weight. The target-factor bounds in \(\mathrm{ass:target\text{-}boundary\text{-}factor}\) imply
\[
\|q_h^{\circ}(P)\|_2\le C_1h,
\qquad
e_0^\top q_h^{\circ}(P)=\int_0^hf_T(x)\,dx\ge c_{T,-}h,
\tag{5}
\]
where \(e_0=(1,0,\ldots,0)^\top\). The lower eigenvalue bound in (3) and the first inequality in (5) yield
\[
\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)\le C_2h^{1-a}.
\tag{6}
\]
Conversely, Cauchy--Schwarz in the inner product induced by \(G_h^{\circ}(P)\) gives
\[
\{e_0^\top q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^2
\le\{e_0^\top G_h^{\circ}(P)e_0\}
\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P).
\tag{7}
\]
Because
\[
e_0^\top G_h^{\circ}(P)e_0=\int_0^hf_S(x)\,dx
\le \frac{c_{S,+}}{a+1}h^{a+1},
\tag{8}
\]
equations (5), (7), and (8) imply the matching lower bound
\[
\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)\ge C_3h^{1-a}.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus the first asserted equivalence holds uniformly over the class. All divisions in (8) are valid because \(a\ge0\).

Direct multiplication and the definition of \(G_h^{\circ}(P)\) show that
\[
\int_0^h\{w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)\}^2f_S(x)\,dx
=\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P).
\tag{10}
\]
Using (8)--(10) and ordinary Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\int_0^h|w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)|f_S(x)\,dx
\le
\left[\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)\right]^{1/2}
\left\{\int_0^hf_S(x)\,dx\right\}^{1/2}
\le C_4h.
\tag{11}
\]
Also \(\int_0^hf_T(x)\,dx\le c_{T,+}h\).

Let \(v\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\) with \(\|v\|_\infty\le1\), and let
\[
T_pv(x)=\sum_{r=0}^p\frac{v^{(r)}(0)}{r!}x^r.
\tag{12}
\]
The stated H\"older convention and Taylor's formula with a \(\beta\)-H\"older \(p\)-th derivative give
\[
|v(x)-T_pv(x)|\le C_{s,M}x^s,
\qquad 0\le x\le h.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(p=0\), (13) is exactly the defining \(s\)-H\"older inequality; for \(p\ge1\), it follows by integrating the order-\(p\) remainder, so it also covers the boundary case \(\beta=1\). Since \(T_pv\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(p\), reproduction (4) cancels its contribution. Equations (11)--(13) therefore imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathfrak b_h(v;P)|
&=\left|\int_0^h\{w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)f_S(x)-f_T(x)\}\{v(x)-T_pv(x)\}\,dx\right|\\
&\le C_{s,M}h^s
\left\{\int_0^h|w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)|f_S(x)\,dx+\int_0^hf_T(x)\,dx\right\}
\le C_5h^{s+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Taking the defining supremum over \(v\) proves \(b_h^{\circ}(P)\le C_5h^{s+1}\). The assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:control\text{-}response\text{-}smoothness}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:treated\text{-}response\text{-}smoothness}\) give \(\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\), and the outcome range gives \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\). Moreover, the definition of \(\omega_h^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\) and \(r=f_T/f_S\) yields the exact identity
\[
\int_0^1\omega_h^{\circ}(x;P)\mu(x)f_S(x)\,dx-
\int_0^1\mu(x)f_T(x)\,dx=\mathfrak b_h(\mu;P).
\tag{15}
\]
Hence the actual oracle bias satisfies \(|\mathfrak b_h(\mu;P)|\le b_h^{\circ}(P)\le C_5h^{s+1}\).

We now prove the asserted class sharpness for \(a>0\). Use the admissible density pair
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad f_T(x)=1.
\tag{16}
\]
Its factors are \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\), which obey the factor bounds by their declared ranges and belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) because \(M\ge a+1\). Complete this density pair to a member \(P\) of \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) by taking both source potential outcomes, conditionally on \(X\), to be independent Bernoulli variables of mean \(1/2\), independent of the randomized treatment, and taking target potential outcomes with the same conditional means. Consistency supplies the observed outcome. The response functions are constant and their conditional variances are \(1/4>M^{-1}\) because \(M>4\); hence all identification, positivity, smoothness, boundedness, and residual-nondegeneracy member conditions hold. This verifies membership rather than assuming it.

Under (16), rescaling shows that
\[
w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(hu;P)=h^{-a}L_a(u),
\qquad
L_a(u)=\sum_{j=0}^p\lambda_ju^j,
\tag{17}
\]
where reproduction is equivalent to
\[
(a+1)\sum_{j=0}^p\frac{\lambda_j}{a+k+j+1}=\frac1{k+1},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,p.
\tag{18}
\]
Take \(v(x)=\delta x^s\), where the fixed \(\delta>0\) is small enough that
\(v\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\) and \(\|v\|_\infty\le1\). Equations (16)--(18) give
\[
\mathfrak b_h(v;P)=\delta h^{s+1}\Delta_{a,s},
\qquad
\Delta_{a,s}=(a+1)\sum_{j=0}^p\frac{\lambda_j}{a+s+j+1}-\frac1{s+1}.
\tag{19}
\]
We claim \(\Delta_{a,s}\ne0\) whenever \(a>0\). If it were zero, the equations in (18) would also hold for \(k=s\). Hence the last column would be a linear combination of the first \(p+1\) columns in the matrix with rows indexed by \(x_i\in\{0,1,\ldots,p,s\}\) and columns indexed by
\[
y_j\in\{a+1,a+2,\ldots,a+p+1,1\},
\]
whose entries, up to nonzero column scalings, are \((x_i+y_j)^{-1}\). But the algebraic Cauchy determinant identity
\[
\det\left(\frac1{x_i+y_j}\right)_{i,j=0}^{p+1}
=
\frac{\prod_{i<k}(x_k-x_i)\prod_{j<\ell}(y_\ell-y_j)}
{\prod_{i,j}(x_i+y_j)}
\tag{20}
\]
is nonzero: \(s\in(p,p+1]\) makes the row nodes distinct, \(a>0\) makes the column nodes distinct, and all denominators are positive. This contradiction proves the claim. Therefore (19) implies
\[
b_h^{\circ}(P)\ge\delta|\Delta_{a,s}|h^{s+1},
\tag{21}
\]
which proves class sharpness for every \(a>0\). The endpoint qualification is necessary: if \(a=0\) and \(f_S=f_T\) on \([0,h]\), then \(q_h^{\circ}(P)=G_h^{\circ}(P)e_0\), so \(w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)=1\) on \([0,h)\) and every bias in the defining supremum is zero.

It remains to combine the boundary and interior pieces. Their supports are disjoint up to the Lebesgue-null point \(h\), so (10) and the definition of \(r\) give the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\omega_h^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2
&=\int_0^h\{w_h^{\mathrm{BLP}}(x;P)\}^2f_S(x)\,dx
  +\int_h^1r(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx\\
&=\{q_h^{\circ}(P)\}^\top\{G_h^{\circ}(P)\}^{-1}q_h^{\circ}(P)
  +\int_h^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
The first term has order \(h^{1-a}\) by (6)--(9), while
\(\mathrm{lem:riesz\text{-}threshold}\) gives orders \(1\), \(\log(1/h)\), and
\(h^{1-a}\) for the second term when \(a<1\), \(a=1\), and \(a>1\), respectively. Thus
\[
\|\omega_h^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2\asymp
\begin{cases}
1,&a<1,\\
\log(1/h),&a=1,\\
h^{1-a},&a>1.
\end{cases}
\tag{23}
\]

Finally substitute the three branches of \(h_n\). If \(a<1\), then
\[
h_n^{s+1}=n^{-1/2},
\qquad n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}\asymp n^{-1/2}.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(a=1\), then
\[
h_n^{s+1}=\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}},
\qquad
\log(1/h_n)=\frac{\log n-\log\log n}{2(s+1)}\asymp\log n,
\tag{25}
\]
and hence the source standard-deviation term is also of order
\(\sqrt{\log n/n}\). If \(a>1\), put \(D=2s+a+1\). Then
\[
h_n^{s+1}=n^{-(s+1)/D},
\tag{26}
\]
and (23) gives
\[
n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}
\asymp n^{-1/2}h_n^{(1-a)/2}
=n^{-1/2+(a-1)/(2D)}
=n^{-(s+1)/D}.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (14)--(15) and (24)--(27), together with
\(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rate}\), prove the actual-bias upper bound and the displayed deterministic allowance. Every inverse, division, and limiting comparison used above is justified by (2)--(3), the positive boundary-factor constants, and \(h>0\). The construction uses the law itself and therefore is oracle; none of these calculations defines a data-measurable estimator. \(\square\)
