There is an exact algebraic decomposition that exposes both the valid deterministic reduction and the defect in the frozen bias envelope. Write
\[
w_i=\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k_S(i))}_{h,J_{r,n}}(X_i)\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}
\]
and define the observable fold-varying discrepancy
\[
C_{n,m}(h)=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\widehat\mu_{h,J_{\mu,n}}^{(-k_T(j))}(X_j^T)\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h\}
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\widehat\mu_{h,J_{\mu,n}}^{(-k_S(i))}(X_i).
\]
For the interior part of the estimator, adding and subtracting the same empirical averages of \(\mu\) yields the identity
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{int}}-\int_h^1\mu(x)f_T(x)\,dx
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}
+\left\{\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h\}-\int_h^1\mu(x)f_T(x)\,dx\right\}
+C_{n,m}(h)-\Delta_{h,J_{r,n}}(\mu).
\]
Because the two armwise Hölder conditions imply \(\mu\in\mathcal H^s(2M)\),
\[
|\Delta_{h,J_{r,n}}(\mu)|\le2M\sup_{v\in\mathcal H^s(1)}|\Delta_{h,J_{r,n}}(v)|.
\]
The frozen envelope contains the right-hand side but omits \(|C_{n,m}(h)|\). The proposed correction adds precisely this computable term. It cannot be discarded by exact training-sample calibration because the regression and calibration dimensions differ and every displayed evaluation is on a held-out fold.

After a valid bias bound is available, the decision-theoretic coverage reduction is deterministic. If uniformly
\[
\widehat\tau-\tau=b_P+L_{n,m}+r_{n,m},\qquad |b_P|\le B_{n,m},\qquad r_{n,m}=o_P(\sigma_{n,m}),
\]
if \(L_{n,m}/\sigma_{n,m}\) has Kolmogorov distance \(o(1)\) from \(N(0,1)\), and if \(\widehat{\mathrm{se}}/\sigma_{n,m}\to1\), then the definition of \(\operatorname{cv}_{1-\gamma/2}\) bounds noncoverage on the bias event by \(\gamma/2+o(1)\). An envelope event of probability at least \(1-\gamma/2-o(1)\) therefore gives coverage at least \(1-\gamma-o(1)\). Moreover, for \(s>1/2\), the one-dimensional Hölder entropy bound implies
\[
\int_0^1\sqrt{\log N(\epsilon,\mathcal H^s(1),L^2(Q))}\,d\epsilon
\le C\int_0^1\epsilon^{-1/(2s)}\,d\epsilon<\infty.
\]
What remains is to derive the uniform linear expansion, variance consistency, weighted Lindeberg condition, multiplier approximation, and uniform integrability from the primitive estimator construction.
