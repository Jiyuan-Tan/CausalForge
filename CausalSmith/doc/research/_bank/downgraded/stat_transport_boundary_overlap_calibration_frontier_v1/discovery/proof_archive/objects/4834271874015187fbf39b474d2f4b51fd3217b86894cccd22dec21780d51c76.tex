\textbf{Proof.}
We first check that the displayed restriction on \(M\) is exactly the normalization-compatible one for the submodel used below.  If \(f_S(x)=x^ag_S(x)\) is a density and the Hölder norm controls the supremum norm, then
\[
 1=\int_0^1x^ag_S(x)\,dx
 \le \frac{\lVert g_S\rVert_\infty}{a+1}
 \le \frac{M}{a+1}.
\]
Thus nonemptiness requires \(M\ge a+1\) for any radius-\(M\) source factor, and equality permits the constant factor used next.

Put
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,\qquad f_T(x)=1,
\]
so that \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\).  The bounds on \(c_{S,-},c_{S,+},c_{T,-},c_{T,+}\), together with \(M\ge a+1\), put these factors in all density-factor conditions, including at equality.  Retain the known propensity \(e\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), take \(Y^S(0)\) and \(Y^S(1)\) to be Bernoulli variables, conditionally independent of \(A\), with
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\]
Give the target potential outcomes the same conditional means.  A smooth compactly supported bump satisfies
\[
 \sup_{0<h\le1}\lVert h^sK(\cdot/h)\mathbf 1_{[0,h]}\rVert_{\mathcal H^s}<\infty.
\]
Choose the fixed \(\delta>0\) small enough that both response functions lie in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\) and \([0,1]\).  Since \(M>4\), it may also be chosen so that
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)
 \ge \frac14-\delta^2\ge M^{-1}.
\]
Consistency, conditional randomization, mean transportability, treatment positivity, and every remaining member property then hold by construction.  Both full-data laws therefore induce members of \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\).

For \(|u|\le\delta<1/2\), the elementary inequality
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(\operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12+u\right),
 \operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12-u\right)\right)
 \le C_0u^2
\]
follows from \(\operatorname{kl}(p,q)\le(p-q)^2/\{q(1-q)\}\).  The two source laws differ only in the treated response.  Tensorization and \(e(x)\le1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,n},P_{-,h}^{S,n})
 &\le C_0n\delta^2\int_0^hb_h(x)^2(a+1)x^a\,dx\\
 &=C_0(a+1)\delta^2nh^{2s+a+1}
   \int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du
 \le Cnh^{2s+a+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(K\ge0\) is nonzero, \(k_1=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0\).  The observed target covariate law is common to the two experiments, and hence
\[
 |\tau(\mathbb P_{+,h})-\tau(\mathbb P_{-,h})|
 =2\delta\int_0^hb_h(x)\,dx
 =2\delta k_1h^{s+1}\ge ch^{s+1}.
\]

For the independent target-sample witness, use the same density factors but a common nonconstant source contrast.  Let \(\ell(x)=x-1/2\), choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\), and set
\[
 \mu_0(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x).
\]
These smooth armwise regressions satisfy the response and nondegeneracy conditions when \(\delta_0\) is small.  Keep the entire source law fixed and define
\[
 f_{T,\pm}(x)=1\pm u_m\ell(x),
 \qquad
 u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\]
Since \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), both functions integrate to one.  The strict slack \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\) permits a fixed \(\delta_1>0\) for which both densities are positive, obey the prescribed bounds, and belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\).  With \(f_{T,-}\) bounded away from zero,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{T,+}^{\otimes m},P_{T,-}^{\otimes m})
 &\le m\int_0^1\frac{\{f_{T,+}(x)-f_{T,-}(x)\}^2}{f_{T,-}(x)}\,dx\\
 &\le C_1mu_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 \le C_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Assign target potential outcomes the displayed common conditional means.  This supplies full-data extensions satisfying mean transportability, while the observed target projections differ only through \(f_{T,\pm}\).  Finally,
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 =\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{6}m^{-1/2}.
\]
This proves both asserted witnesses. \(\square\)
