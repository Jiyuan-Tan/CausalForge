\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a member of the model and suppose \(a=0\).  By the definitions of the density factors, for every \(x\in(0,1]\),
\[
f_S(x)=x^a g_S(x)=g_S(x),
\qquad
f_T(x)=g_T(x),
\qquad
r(x)=x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}.
\]
The source-boundary-factor and target-boundary-factor assumptions give
\[
c_{S,-}\le g_S(x)\le c_{S,+}
\quad\text{and}\quad
c_{T,-}\le g_T(x)\le c_{T,+},
\qquad x\in[0,1].
\]
Because \(c_{S,-}>0\), division by \(g_S(x)\) is valid.  Hence, for every \(x\in(0,1]\),
\[
\frac{c_{T,-}}{c_{S,+}}
\le r(x)=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}
\le\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}.
\]

Since \(a=0<1\), the first branch in the definition of the bandwidth applies and gives
\[
h_n=n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}.
\]
The symbol-table restriction \(s>0\) implies \(s+1>0\), and direct exponentiation therefore yields
\[
h_n^{s+1}
=\left(n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}\right)^{s+1}
=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Moreover, \(h_n\) tends to zero as \(n\) tends to infinity.  The \(a<1\) branch of the Riesz-threshold lemma then gives
\[
\int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\asymp 1,
\]
uniformly over the model class, and in particular
\[
\int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx=O(1).
\]

It remains to prove the positivity assertion.  Fix a fold \(k\) for which both interior training measures have positive total mass.  This excludes the zero-mass fallback in the executable RiCOT-weight definition.  Positive target interior mass means
\[
\frac{1}{|\mathcal T^T_k|}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal T^T_k}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h_n\}>0.
\]
Consequently \(|\mathcal T^T_k|>0\), and there is at least one index \(j_0\in\mathcal T^T_k\) such that \(X_{j_0}^T\ge h_n\).  On either positive-mass optimization branch, the selected multipliers \(\widehat\theta^{(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}\) and \(\widehat\psi^{(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n},j}\) are finite real vectors or coordinates.  For any \(x\in[h_n,1]\), the squared transport cost is finite, the normalized B-spline coordinate vector is finite, and \(\eta_0>0\).  Thus every exponential appearing in the raw continuation is strictly positive; in particular, the term indexed by \(j_0\) is strictly positive.  Therefore
\[
\frac1{|\mathcal T^T_k|}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal T^T_k}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h_n\}
\exp\!\left\{-\frac{
c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,X_j^T)
+\widehat\theta_{h_n,J_{r,n}}^{(-k)\top}
\widetilde{\mathbf b}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)
+\widehat\psi^{(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n},j}}
{\eta_0}\right\}>0.
\]
Since \(\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)>0\), raising this quantity to that power preserves strict positivity.  Hence
\[
\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)>0,
\qquad x\in[h_n,1].
\]
At \(a=0\), the cap in the downstream RiCOT weight is
\[
\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}h_n^{-a}
=\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}>0.
\]
The minimum of the strictly positive raw continuation and this strictly positive cap is strictly positive.  By the definition of the capped downstream weight,
\[
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)>0,
\qquad x\in[h_n,1].
\]

The restrictions \(t>1/2\) and \(s/(2s+1)+t/(2t+1)>1/2\) are not needed for these deterministic calculations.  The argument bounds neither an empirical norm nor a realized residual sum and supplies no stochastic risk inequality, so none of those stronger conclusions follows from this proposition alone. \(\square\)
