\textbf{Proof.}

First we verify the causal and statistical signals. By the definition of the randomized-trial propensity and the treatment positivity assumption,
\[
\mathbb E_{P_S}\!\left(\frac{AY}{e(X)}\,\middle|\,X=x\right)=\mu_1(x),
\qquad
\mathbb E_{P_S}\!\left(\frac{(1-A)Y}{1-e(X)}\,\middle|\,X=x\right)=\mu_0(x).
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E_{P_S}(Z\mid X=x)=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)=\mu(x).
\tag{1}
\]
Consistency, trial randomization, and conditional mean transportability give the separate identifying equality between the observed-law functional \(\tau=\mathbb E_{P_T}\{\mu(X)\}\) and the target average treatment effect. The concentration argument below concerns this identified functional and does not redefine the causal target.

Next, the positive ridges in \(\mathrm{def:regression\text{-}fit}\) make both regression coefficients linear in the training pseudo-outcomes. Substitution of their normal-equation formulas gives
\[
\widehat\mu^{(-k)}_{h,J_{\mu,n}}(x)
=\sum_{\ell=1}^nL_{k,h,J_{\mu,n}}(x,\ell)Z_\ell.
\tag{2}
\]
The RiCOT weights use only source and target covariates, and the deterministic branch and tie-breaking rules make them measurable functions of those covariates. Substitute (2) into the two terms of \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}\). The coefficient of \(Z_\ell\) is
\[
\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mL_{k_T(j),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_j^T,\ell)
+\frac{w_\ell(h,J_{r,n})}{n}
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw_i(h,J_{r,n})L_{k_S(i),h,J_{\mu,n}}(X_i,\ell),
\]
which is exactly \(\Gamma_\ell(h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\). This proves the linear representation on every positive-mass or fallback branch.

Add and subtract \(\sum_i\Gamma_i\mu(X_i)\) and \(m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)\). This is an algebraic identity and gives the displayed three-term decomposition. The two Hölder assumptions and subadditivity of the Hölder norm imply
\[
\mu=\mu_1-\mu_0\in\mathcal H^s(2M).
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(0\le\mu_d\le1\), also \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le1\). Thus \(\mu\) is feasible in the supremum defining \(B_X\), and the middle term is bounded by \(B_X\) for every realized design.

It remains to control the two centered terms. Let \(\mathcal F_X\) be the sigma-field generated by all source and target covariates. Under the product sampling law, the variables \(Z_i\) are conditionally independent given \(\mathcal F_X\). Since \(0\le Y\le1\) and \(\varepsilon\le e(X)\le1-\varepsilon\),
\[
-\varepsilon^{-1}\le Z_i\le\varepsilon^{-1}.
\]
Conditional on \(\mathcal F_X\), the centered summand \(\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\) therefore has a bounding interval of length at most \(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\). Applying \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}hoeffding}\) and (1) yields
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left(
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}\right|>
\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\log(4/\gamma)\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i^2}
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_X\right)\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{4}
\]
Here and below the arguments \((h,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n})\) of \(\Gamma_i\) are suppressed.

Independently, the target variables \(\mu(X_j^T)\) are i.i.d. in \([-1,1]\) with mean \(\tau\). Applying the same lemma to \(m^{-1}\{\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\}\), whose bounding interval has length \(2/m\), gives
\[
\mathbb P_{n,m}\!\left(
\left|\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|>
\sqrt{\frac{2\log(4/\gamma)}m}\right)\le\frac\gamma2.
\tag{5}
\]
Integrating (4) over \(\mathcal F_X\), combining (4) and (5) by the union bound, and using the deterministic \(B_X\) bound proves that the displayed interval covers \(\tau\) with probability at least \(1-\gamma\), uniformly over the class.

Finally, if the realized inequalities \(B_X\le b\) and \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\le g^2\) hold, direct substitution into the interval definition gives the asserted half-width. No stochastic order for either observable diagnostic was used, so the proof establishes no empirical frontier-attainment or expected-length-rate conclusion. \(\square\)
