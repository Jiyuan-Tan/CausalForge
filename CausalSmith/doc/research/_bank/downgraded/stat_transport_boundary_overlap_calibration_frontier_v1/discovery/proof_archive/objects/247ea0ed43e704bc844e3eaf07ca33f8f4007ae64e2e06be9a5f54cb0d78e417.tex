\textbf{Proof.}
We first derive the interval inequality used below. Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) have parameter values \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), and let \(\mathcal C\) be any random interval. If
\[
Q_0\{\theta_0\in\mathcal C\}\ge1-\gamma-r,
\qquad
Q_1\{\theta_1\in\mathcal C\}\ge1-\gamma-r,
\tag{25}
\]
then transfer of the second event from \(Q_1\) to \(Q_0\) and the union bound show
\[
Q_0\{\theta_0,\theta_1\in\mathcal C\}
\ge1-2\gamma-2r-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\tag{26}
\]
On this event the interval length is at least \(|\theta_1-\theta_0|\). Consequently
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C|
\ge |\theta_1-\theta_0|
 \{1-2\gamma-2r-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.
\tag{27}
\]
The affinity calculation (12) in the preceding proof shows directly that a sufficiently small fixed Kullback--Leibler bound makes the total variation in (27) smaller than any prescribed number in \((0,1)\).

We now construct all alternatives around one smoother reference law. Put
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1,
\qquad
\ell(x)=x-\frac12.
\tag{28}
\]
Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\), and take Bernoulli potential outcomes with
\[
\mu_0(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x).
\tag{29}
\]
Use the fixed known propensity, conditional randomization, consistency, and target potential outcomes with the same conditional means. The factor functions \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\) satisfy the support, normalization, positivity, overlap, and order-\(t\) Hölder conditions for every fixed \(t>0\). Because \(\ell\) is smooth, reducing \(\delta_0\) puts (29) strictly inside both the order-\(s_1\) and order-\(s_2\) radius-\(M\) response balls and preserves the residual-variance lower bound. Denote the induced two-sample law by \(Q_0\). In particular,
\[
Q_0\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)
\quad\text{and}\quad
Q_0\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t).
\tag{30}
\]

For the rough alternative, set
\[
h=d\,n^{-1/(2s_1+a+1)},
\qquad
b_{h,s_1}(x)=h^{s_1}K(x/h)\mathbf 1\{0\le x\le h\},
\tag{31}
\]
where \(d>0\) will be chosen small, and replace only the treated response by
\[
\mu_{1,1}(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x)+\delta b_{h,s_1}(x).
\tag{32}
\]
Hölder scaling and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\) put the resulting law \(Q_1\) in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t)\); all other member properties remain those checked in (28)--(30). The Bernoulli probabilities in (29) are uniformly separated from zero and one, so the calculation in (5), with \(s=s_1\), yields
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
\le Cn\delta^2h^{2s_1+a+1}
=C\delta^2d^{2s_1+a+1},
\tag{33}
\]
while
\[
\Delta_S:=|\tau(Q_1)-\tau(Q_0)|
=\delta h^{s_1+1}\int_0^1K(u)\,du
\ge c_Sn^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\tag{34}
\]
Choose \(d\) and \(\delta\) so that the total variation implied by (12) is some \(\eta_S<1-2\gamma\).

Let \(\mathcal C_{n,m}\) have the asserted uniform asymptotic coverage. Along any indexing sequence on which that assertion is made, uniformity supplies a deterministic \(r_{n,m}\to0\) for which (25) holds with \(Q_0,Q_1\) and \(r=r_{n,m}\). Equations (27), (33), and (34) then give, at the smoother law \(Q_0\),
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
\ge c_1n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}
\tag{35}
\]
for all sufficiently large indices.

For the target-sample alternative, keep the source law and response functions in (28)--(29) unchanged and replace only the target density by
\[
f_{T,2}(x)=1+u_m\ell(x),
\qquad
u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\tag{36}
\]
The strict target-density slack, smoothness of \(\ell\), and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta_1>0\) make the resulting law \(Q_2\) a member of both response-smoothness classes for every fixed \(t>0\). The target calculation in (9), now comparing (36) with the uniform density, gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_2,Q_0)\le C\delta_1^2,
\qquad
\Delta_T:=|\tau(Q_2)-\tau(Q_0)|
=\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
=\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{12}m^{-1/2}.
\tag{37}
\]
Reduce \(\delta_1\) so that the associated total variation is \(\eta_T<1-2\gamma\). Uniform coverage and (27), again under the identical smoother reference law \(Q_0\), imply
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|\ge c_2m^{-1/2}.
\tag{38}
\]
Both (35) and (38) hold at the same law \(Q_0\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\). Using \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\) proves
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|\mathcal C_{n,m}|
\ge c\left\{m^{-1/2}+n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}\right\}.
\]
Every perturbation remains on the common support \([0,1]\), every density is normalized and positive, and neither a sample-balance condition nor either removed density-smoothness threshold is used. \(\square\)
