\textbf{Proof.}
We prove a two-point testing inequality first.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two laws in the observed experiment, let \(\tau_v=\tau(Q_v)\), and put \(\Delta=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\).  For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), classify the observation by whichever of \(\tau_0,\tau_1\) is closer to \(T\), breaking a tie deterministically.  A classification error under \(Q_v\) implies \(|T-\tau_v|\geq \Delta/2\).  The sum of the two error probabilities of any test is at least \(1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  Consequently,
\[
 \max_{v\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_v}(T-\tau_v)^2
 \geq \frac{\Delta^2}{8}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.
 \tag{14}
\]
For completeness, the total-variation bound needed below is also elementary.  If \(\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)=\infty\), there is nothing to show.  Otherwise, apply the log-sum inequality to a Hahn set for \(Q_0-Q_1\).  Writing \(p=Q_0(H)\) and \(q=Q_1(H)\), this gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}
 \geq 2(p-q)^2.
 \tag{15}
\]
The last inequality follows directly because, with \(q\) fixed, the difference between its middle and right sides vanishes with zero first derivative at \(p=q\), while its second derivative is \(1/\{p(1-p)\}-4\geq0\).  Since \(|p-q|=\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \leq \sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)/2}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Thus a pair having divergence at most a sufficiently small absolute constant and parameter separation \(\Delta\) forces root mean-squared risk at least a fixed multiple of \(\Delta\).

We next give the source-sample pairs.  In every regime set
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad
 f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\leq x\leq1.
 \tag{17}
\]
These are normalized densities on the declared support \(\mathcal X=[0,1]\).  Their nonsingular factors are \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\).  By the declared ranges of \(c_{S,-},c_{S,+}\), by the strict inequalities \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\), and by \(M\geq a+1\), they satisfy the two boundary-factor bounds and belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every \(t>0\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), draw \(A\) from the known Bernoulli randomization with success probability \(e(x)\), independently of the potential outcomes.  Conditional on \(X=x\), take the potential outcomes to be Bernoulli with the response means specified below, and set \(Y=Y^S(A)\).  Give the target potential outcomes Bernoulli laws with the same conditional means.  This construction enforces consistency, conditional randomization, known propensity, and conditional mean transportability.  The assumed bound \(\varepsilon\leq e(x)\leq1-\varepsilon\) supplies treatment positivity.  All perturbation multipliers below are chosen sufficiently small that the response means lie in \([0,1]\), their \(\mathcal H^s(M)\) norms are at most \(M\), and their Bernoulli variances are at least \(M^{-1}\); this is possible because \(M>4\).  Hence every displayed pair below consists of members of \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\).  No inverse or rank condition is used.

For \(a<1\), choose a nonnegative nonzero \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty((1/4,3/4))\) and define
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}\varphi(x).
 \tag{18}
\]
The support of \(\varphi\) stays a positive distance from both boundaries, so \(f_S\) is bounded above and away from zero there.  For \(|u|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, direct Bernoulli algebra gives
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
 \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)\right)\leq C_0u^2.
 \tag{19}
\]
The two target covariate laws are identical.  Additivity of relative entropy for the \(n\) independent source observations, followed by (19), yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_{+,n},Q_{-,n})
 \leq C_1\delta^2 n\,n^{-1}\int_0^1\varphi(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx
 \leq C_2\delta^2.
 \tag{20}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1\varphi(x)\,dx
 \geq c_0n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{21}
\]
After reducing the fixed \(\delta>0\), equations (14)--(16) imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\geq c_1n^{-1/2},
 \qquad a<1.
 \tag{22}
\]

For \(a>1\), invoke \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\) with
\[
 h=c_hn^{-1/(2s+a+1)},
 \tag{23}
\]
where the fixed \(c_h>0\) is smaller than the lemma's boundary threshold.  Its source divergence is bounded by
\[
 Cnh^{2s+a+1}=Cc_h^{2s+a+1},
 \tag{24}
\]
whereas its parameter separation is at least
\[
 c h^{s+1}
 =c c_h^{s+1}n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
 \tag{25}
\]
Choose \(c_h\) so that (24) is a sufficiently small absolute constant.  Equations (14)--(16) and (25) then give
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \geq c_2n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)},
 \qquad a>1.
 \tag{26}
\]
The boundary support condition \([0,h]\subseteq[0,1]\) holds because \(0<h<1\).

It remains to obtain the exact critical logarithm when \(a=1\).  Choose a nonnegative nonzero \(\psi\in C_c^\infty((5/4,7/4))\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), let
\[
 J_n=\left\lfloor\frac{\log_2 n}{2(s+1)}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad h_j=2^{-j}\quad(1\leq j\leq J_n),
 \qquad A_n=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{nJ_n}},
 \tag{27}
\]
and form the sign-aligned multiscale perturbation
\[
 u_n(x)=A_n\sum_{j=1}^{J_n}h_j^{-1}\psi(x/h_j).
 \tag{28}
\]
Its \(j\)-th summand is supported on the boundary shell \(((5/4)h_j,(7/4)h_j)\).  Since \(h_{j+1}=h_j/2\), these shells are pairwise disjoint, separated by gaps, and contained in \([0,1]\) because \(h_1=1/2\).

Write \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\).  Dilation gives, for \(0\leq r\leq p\),
\[
 \left\|D^r\{A_nh_j^{-1}\psi(\cdot/h_j)\}\right\|_\infty
 \leq C_\psi A_nh_j^{-(r+1)},
 \qquad
 \left[D^p\{A_nh_j^{-1}\psi(\cdot/h_j)\}\right]_\beta
 \leq C_\psi A_nh_j^{-(s+1)}.
 \tag{29}
\]
Every summand and all its derivatives vanish at its support endpoints.  For two points in the same shell, (29) controls the H\"older quotient.  For points in different shells, insertion of the intervening zero endpoint and the fixed gap-to-shell ratio gives the same bound, up to a constant depending only on \(\psi\) and \(s\).  Because the supports are disjoint, the supremum norms are also controlled by the largest summand.  Therefore
\[
 \|u_n\|_{\mathcal H^s}
 \leq C_\psi A_n h_{J_n}^{-(s+1)}
 \leq \frac{C_\psi\delta}{\sqrt{J_n}},
 \tag{30}
\]
where the final inequality uses \(2^{J_n(s+1)}\leq n^{1/2}\).  Thus, after choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small, the response functions
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm u_n(x)
 \tag{31}
\]
satisfy the outcome support, H\"older-radius, and residual-variance conditions uniformly in \(n\).

At \(a=1\), equation (17) is \(f_S(x)=2x\).  Disjointness of the shells and the substitution \(x=h_ju\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 n\int_0^1u_n(x)^2f_S(x)\,dx
 &=2nA_n^2\sum_{j=1}^{J_n}
   \int h_j^{-2}\psi(x/h_j)^2x\,dx\\
 &=2nA_n^2J_n\int_{5/4}^{7/4}\psi(u)^2u\,du
 \leq C_\psi\delta^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{32}
\]
Using the Bernoulli inequality (19), the KL divergence of the two product-source laws is at most \(C\delta^2\).  Each shell has the same target integral, so
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 &=2A_nJ_n\int_{5/4}^{7/4}\psi(u)\,du\\
 &\geq c_3\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}},
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{33}
\]
because \(J_n\asymp\log n\).  Reduce \(\delta\) once more and apply (14)--(16).  This proves
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(1,s,t)\geq c_4\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}
 \tag{34}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  The finitely many remaining positive \(n\) are covered, after decreasing \(c_4\), by the interior perturbation (18).  Equations (27)--(33) exhibit the critical witness explicitly: it is the equal two-point testing mixture on the positive and negative versions of a sign-aligned superposition of \(J_n\asymp\log n\) disjoint boundary shells.  Hence the logarithmic factor has no hidden polynomial loss.

Combining (22), (26), and (34) with \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}rate}\) gives a constant \(c_S>0\), depending only on the fixed class parameters, such that
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\geq c_S\rho_n(a,s)
 \tag{35}
\]
for all positive \(n,m\).

Finally we prove the independent target-sample obstruction, including its positivity and boundary conditions.  Keep the source law fixed, put \(\ell(x)=x-1/2\), and use common response functions
\[
 \mu_0(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x),
 \tag{36}
\]
where \(\delta_0>0\) is fixed and sufficiently small for outcome support, H\"older smoothness, and residual nondegeneracy.  Define two target densities by
\[
 f_{T,\pm}(x)=1\pm u_m\ell(x),
 \qquad
 u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
 \tag{37}
\]
Because \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), both densities integrate to one.  The strict slack \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\), together with the boundedness and smoothness of \(\ell\), permits a fixed \(\delta_1>0\) such that (37) is positive, lies between the target-factor bounds, and belongs to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every \(m\geq1\).  Choose target potential outcomes with the conditional means in (36); then mean transportability holds and the observed source distributions coincide.

The lower density in (37) is uniformly bounded away from zero.  From \(\log z\leq z-1\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{T,+}^{\otimes m},P_{T,-}^{\otimes m})
 &\leq m\int_0^1
   \frac{\{f_{T,+}(x)-f_{T,-}(x)\}^2}{f_{T,-}(x)}\,dx\\
 &\leq C_3m u_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 \leq C_4\delta_1^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{38}
\]
Since \(\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx=1/12\), the target separation is
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 =\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{6}m^{-1/2}.
 \tag{39}
\]
Take \(\delta_1\) small enough that the divergence in (38) is a sufficiently small absolute constant.  Equations (14)--(16) then imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\geq c_Tm^{-1/2}
 \tag{40}
\]
uniformly in \(n\).

Both (35) and (40) hold for the same minimax risk.  Since \(\max\{u,v\}\geq(u+v)/2\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \geq \frac12\min\{c_S,c_T\}
 \{\rho_n(a,s)+m^{-1/2}\}.
\]
This is the claimed lower phase diagram. \(\square\)
