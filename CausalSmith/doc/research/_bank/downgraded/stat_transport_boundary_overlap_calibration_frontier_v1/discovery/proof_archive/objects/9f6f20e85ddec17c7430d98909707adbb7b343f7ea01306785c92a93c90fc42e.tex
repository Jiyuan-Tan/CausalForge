\textbf{Proof.}
We give explicit full-data extensions and check every class condition. First note the normalization boundary. If \(f_S(x)=x^ag_S(x)\) is a density and the Hölder norm dominates the supremum norm, then
\[
1=\int_0^1x^ag_S(x)\,dx
\le \frac{\lVert g_S\rVert_\infty}{a+1}
\le \frac{M}{a+1}.
\]
Thus \(M\ge a+1\) is necessary for nonemptiness and permits the constant factor used below.

Set
\[
f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
\qquad
f_T(x)=1.
\]
Then \(g_S(x)=a+1\) and \(g_T(x)=1\). The declared ranges of \(c_{S,-},c_{S,+},c_{T,-},c_{T,+}\), together with \(M\ge a+1\), verify the source and target factor bounds. Constant functions belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every \(t>0\), so no lower threshold on \(t\) is used.

Retain the fixed known propensity \(e\). Conditional on \(X=x\), choose Bernoulli potential outcomes, conditionally independent of \(A\), with means
\[
\mu_{0,+}(x)=\mu_{0,-}(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\]
Give the target potential outcomes the same conditional means. By the scaling in \(\mathrm{def:boundary\text{-}bump}\) and the defining norm bound for \(K\),
\[
\sup_{0<h\le1}\lVert b_h\rVert_{\mathcal H^s}<\infty.
\]
Choose a fixed \(\delta>0\), independent of \(h,n,m\), small enough that both armwise response functions lie in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\) and take values in \([0,1]\). Since \(M>4\), the same choice may be made so that
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)
\ge \frac14-\delta^2
\ge M^{-1}
\]
for both arms. Consistency is imposed by setting \(Y=Y^S(A)\); conditional randomization holds by construction; and the target conditional means give mean transportability. Hence both full-data constructions induce members of the broadened \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\).

For \(|u|\le\delta<1/2\),
\[
\operatorname{kl}\!\left(\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)\right)
\le \frac{(2u)^2}{(1/2-u)(1/2+u)}
\le C_0u^2.
\]
The source laws differ only in the treated conditional outcome distribution. Tensorization, treatment positivity, and the elementary upper bound \(e(x)\le1\) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,n},P_{-,h}^{S,n})
&\le C_0n\delta^2\int_0^hb_h(x)^2(a+1)x^a\,dx\\
&=C_0(a+1)\delta^2nh^{2s+a+1}
\int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du\\
&\le Cnh^{2s+a+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(K\ge0\) is nonzero, \(k_1=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0\). The observed target covariate law is common to the alternatives, while
\[
\left|\tau(\mathbb P_{+,h})-\tau(\mathbb P_{-,h})\right|
=2\delta\int_0^hb_h(x)\,dx
=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\]
This proves the source witness.

For the independent target-sample witness, keep \(f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a\), let \(\ell(x)=x-1/2\), and choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\). Use common source regressions
\[
\mu_0(x)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x).
\]
The function \(\ell\) is infinitely smooth, so these regressions satisfy the response and nondegeneracy conditions for every \(s>0\) after reducing \(\delta_0\) if necessary. Keep the entire source law fixed and put
\[
f_{T,\pm}(x)=1\pm u_m\ell(x),
\qquad
u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\]
Since \(\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=0\), these are densities. The strict slack \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\) permits a fixed \(\delta_1>0\) such that, for every \(m\ge1\), both densities are positive and obey the prescribed target bounds. Because \(\ell\) is infinitely smooth and \(M>4\), they also belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every fixed \(t>0\). With \(f_{T,-}\) bounded away from zero,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{KL}(P_{T,+}^{\otimes m},P_{T,-}^{\otimes m})
&\le m\int_0^1
\frac{\{f_{T,+}(x)-f_{T,-}(x)\}^2}{f_{T,-}(x)}\,dx\\
&\le C_1mu_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
\le C_2\delta_1^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Assign the target potential outcomes the displayed common conditional means. This gives admissible full-data extensions and preserves mean transportability. Finally,
\[
\left|\tau_+-\tau_-\right|
=2\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
=\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{6}m^{-1/2}.
\]
Both constants may be reduced by choosing \(\delta_1\) smaller, which will be used in the two-point argument. \(\square\)
