\textbf{Proof.}
On the good branch, equations (5)--(6) give the exact decomposition
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{pl}}_{h,J}-\tau
=\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J)\{Z_i-\mu(X_i)\}
+\left\{\sum_{i=1}^n\Gamma_i(h,J)\mu(X_i)-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)\right\}
+\left\{\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(\mu_0,\mu_1\in\mathcal H^s(M)\), their difference belongs to \(\mathcal H^s(2M)\). Therefore the middle absolute value in (11) is at most \(B_X(h,J)\le\overline B_X(h,J)\), deterministically conditional on the covariates.

Conditional on all covariates, each centered source term in (11) is independent and has interval length at most \(2|\Gamma_i|/\varepsilon\). Hoeffding's inequality therefore gives
\[
\mathbb P\left\{\left|\sum_i\Gamma_i(Z_i-\mu(X_i))\right|>R_S(h,J)\middle|X_1,\ldots,X_n,X_1^T,\ldots,X_m^T\right\}\le\gamma/2.
\tag{12}
\]
Independently, \(-1\le\mu(X_j^T)\le1\), so
\[
\mathbb P\left\{\left|m^{-1}\sum_j\mu(X_j^T)-\tau\right|>R_T(m)\right\}\le\gamma/2.
\tag{13}
\]
The union bound, (11)--(13), and the extra nonnegative numerical tolerance \(n^{-2}\) prove coverage at least \(1-\gamma\) on the good branch. On its complement the reported interval is \([-1,1]\), which contains \(\tau\). This proves the finite-sample uniform coverage assertion without a normal approximation.

For length, on the good branch the radius is
\[
\overline B_X(h_n,J_n)+R_S(h_n,J_n)+R_T(m)+n^{-2}.
\]
The coefficient certificate gives
\[
R_S(h_n,J_n)\le Cn^{-1/2}V_a(h_n)^{1/2},
\qquad R_T(m)\le Cm^{-1/2}.
\tag{14}
\]
The optimal-recovery lemma and \(J_n^{-(s+t)}\le\rho_n(a,s)\) give
\[
\sup_P E_P\{\overline B_X(h_n,J_n)\mathbf1(\mathcal E_{n,m})\}
\le C\{m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}+\rho_n(a,s)+n^{-1/2}V_a(h_n)^{1/2}\}.
\tag{15}
\]
The fallback interval has length two, and the localization lemma makes its expected contribution at most \(C\exp\{-c\log^2(en)\}\). Finally, the oracle-truncation envelope identifies the two \(h_n\)-dependent terms in (14)--(15) with \(\rho_n(a,s)\). Multiplying the radius by two proves the expected-length bound. \(\square\)
