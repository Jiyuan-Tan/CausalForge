\textbf{Proof.}
We prove the asserted bound by explicit subexperiments contained in the model.  The constructions below in fact work for every \(t>0\); neither \(t>1/2\) nor \(s/(2s+1)+t/(2t+1)>1/2\) enters the testing calculation.

We first derive the testing inequality that will be used.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two laws with parameter values \(\tau_0,\tau_1\), and put \(d=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\).  For any estimator \(\widehat\tau\), choosing the nearer of \(\tau_0\) and \(\tau_1\) defines a test whose error under \(Q_u\) implies \(|\widehat\tau-\tau_u|\ge d/2\).  The sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\), whence
\[
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}
       (\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2
 \ge \frac{d^2}{8}
       \{1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\]
We also record an elementary divergence bound.  If \(A\) realizes total variation, \(p=Q_0(A)\), and \(q=Q_1(A)\), the log-sum inequality gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)\ge
 p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}.
\]
For fixed \(q\), subtracting \(2(p-q)^2\) from the right-hand side produces a function that vanishes with zero first derivative at \(p=q\) and whose second derivative is
\[
 \frac1{p(1-p)}-4\ge0.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}^2
 =(p-q)^2\le\frac12\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Consequently, a pair with sufficiently small constant Kullback--Leibler divergence yields root mean-squared risk at least a constant multiple of its parameter separation.

For every regime, take
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]
These are densities, and their nonsingular factors are \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\).  The parameter ranges give
\[
 c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
 \qquad
 c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+}.
\]
Both factors are constant, hence belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every \(t>0\), because \(M\ge a+1\).  Thus the construction uses no strict slack in either target-density inequality.

Let treatment be drawn according to the fixed known propensity \(e\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), take the two potential outcomes to be independent Bernoulli variables with the arm-specific means specified below, independently of \(A\); take target potential outcomes with the same conditional means.  Set the observed outcome equal to \(Y^S(A)\).  This enforces consistency, conditional randomization, and conditional mean transportability.  Since \(M>4\),
\[
 \Delta_M:=\sqrt{\frac14-\frac1M}>0.
\]
All perturbation constants below are chosen small enough that every conditional mean differs from \(1/2\) by at most \(\Delta_M/2\).  Hence all outcomes lie in \([0,1]\) and
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)
 \ge \frac14-\frac{\Delta_M^2}{4}
 >\frac1M.
\]
The control regression is constant, and the scaling arguments given below put every treated regression in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\).  All model-class conditions are therefore satisfied.  The target covariate law is uniform and identical under all alternatives, so the \(m\) target observations contribute zero divergence for every \(m\).

\emph{The nonregular case \(a>1\).}
For \(h\in(0,1)\) and a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), define
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\]
By the definition \(b_h(x)=h^sK(x/h)\mathbf 1\{0\le x\le h\}\), dilation multiplies every derivative of order \(v\le\lfloor s\rfloor\) by \(h^{s-v}\) and preserves the order-\(s\) Hölder seminorm up to the fixed norm of \(K\).  Thus, after reducing \(\delta\) if necessary, both regressions lie in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), uniformly in \(h\).

For \(|u|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, direct evaluation and Taylor's theorem give
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12+u\right),
  \operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12-u\right)
 \right)
 =2u\log\frac{1+2u}{1-2u}
 \le C_0u^2.
\]
The control-arm conditional laws agree, while the treated-arm contribution is multiplied by \(e(x)\le1\).  Product additivity of relative entropy and \(f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a\) therefore yield
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,\otimes n},P_{-,h}^{S,\otimes n})
 \le Cn\int_0^h b_h(x)^2x^a\,dx
 =Cnh^{2s+a+1}\int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du.
\]
Because \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero,
\[
 k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0,
 \qquad
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\]
Choose \(h=c_0n^{-1/(2s+a+1)}\), where \(c_0>0\) is small enough that the displayed divergence is bounded by a fixed sufficiently small constant.  The testing inequality gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge c n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\]

\emph{The regular case \(a<1\).}
Choose a nonnegative, nonzero \(C^\infty\) function \(q\) supported in \([1/4,3/4]\), and set
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}q(x).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), these regressions satisfy the same range, variance, and Hölder conditions.  On the support of \(q\), the source density is bounded, so the preceding Bernoulli calculation gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_+^{S,\otimes n},P_-^{S,\otimes n})
 \le C\delta^2.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1q(x)\,dx
 \ge c n^{-1/2}.
\]
After choosing \(\delta\) small, the testing inequality proves
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\]

\emph{The critical case \(a=1\).}
Put \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), fix an integer \(j_0\), and select \(L=L_n\) so that
\[
 \ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}},
 \qquad
 L-j_0+1\asymp\log n.
\]
For \(j_0\le j\le L\), define the shell perturbation
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\}.
\]
For \(\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{L-j_0+1}\), let
\[
 \mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+
 \sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x).
\]
The shell interiors are disjoint.  Moreover,
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 \le C\ell_j^s,
 \qquad j_0\le j\le L,
\]
because \(\ell_j\ge\ell_L\).  After dilation to a shell of width \(\ell_j\), the order-\(s\) Hölder norm of \(\phi_j\) is thus at most \(C\delta\).  Since a smooth compactly supported bump and all its derivatives vanish at shell endpoints, these disjoint-shell pieces paste into a function with global order-\(s\) Hölder norm at most \(C'\delta\), independently of \(L\).  Choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small makes every sign alternative a member of the model.

The contribution of coordinate \(j\) to the target parameter is
\[
 d_j:=\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)\,dx
 =\frac{\delta k_1}{\sqrt n}.
\]
If \(\omega^{(j)}\) is obtained by flipping only coordinate \(j\), then \(f_S(x)=2x\) and the Bernoulli bound imply
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{S,\otimes n},
                    P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{S,\otimes n})
 \le Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
\]
uniformly in \(j\), \(n\), and the other signs.

Place the uniform product prior \(\Pi\) on \(\omega\).  The covariate likelihood is sign-free, and each observed outcome involves at most one shell coordinate.  Hence the likelihood, and therefore the posterior, factorizes across coordinates.  Since conditional expectation minimizes squared loss and
\(\tau_\omega=\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j\omega_j\),
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}
 \mathbb E_\Pi(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2
 \ge
 \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
       (\tau_\omega\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
   \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
       (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}).
\]
Write \(q_j=\mathbb E_\Pi(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|.
\]
Let \(\overline P_{j,+}\) and \(\overline P_{j,-}\) be the mixture experiments conditional on \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\), respectively.  Direct substitution into Bayes' formula gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi|q_j|
 =\lVert\overline P_{j,+}-\overline P_{j,-}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Pairing every sign vector with its \(j\)-flip and applying the log-sum inequality gives joint convexity in the explicit form
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\overline P_{j,+},\overline P_{j,-})
 \le 2^{-(L-j_0)}
 \sum_{\omega:\,\omega_j=1}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega,P_{\omega^{(j)}})
 \le C\delta^2.
\]
The target factors cancel because their law is common.  The derived total-variation inequality and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\) therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
   (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 \ge c_1>0
\]
uniformly in \(j\).  Consequently,
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2
 \ge c_1\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
 \ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\]
Taking square roots proves the critical lower bound \(c\sqrt{\log n/n}\) without a hidden polynomial loss.

Combining the three regimes with the definition of \(\rho_n(a,s)\) proves, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(m\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\rho_n(a,s).
\]
Every subexperiment used the boundary-attaining target density \(f_T=1\), so the conclusion requires no strict target-density slack. \(\square\)
