\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(L_{n,m}\) be the complete linear term in the proposed statement and let \(R_{n,m}=\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}-\tau-L_{n,m}\).  The martingale-normality lemma gives, uniformly over the class,
\[
 \sup_z\left|\mathbb P\left\{
 \frac{L_{n,m}}{\{\operatorname{Var}(L_{n,m})\}^{1/2}}\le z\right\}-\Phi(z)\right|=o(1),
 \qquad
 \frac{\widehat{\mathrm{se}}^2}{\operatorname{Var}(L_{n,m})}\longrightarrow_P1,
\tag{35}
\]
and \(\mathbb P\{|R_{n,m}|\le\widehat B\}=1-o(1)\).  At zero bias, the definition of the critical-value map gives
\[
 P\{|N(0,1)|\le\operatorname{cv}_{1-\gamma/2}(0)\}
 \ge1-\gamma/2.
\tag{36}
\]
Uniform Slutsky applied to (35) makes \(|L_{n,m}|\le\operatorname{cv}_{1-\gamma/2}(0)\widehat{\mathrm{se}}\) with probability at least \(1-\gamma/2-o(1)\).  Intersecting with \(|R_{n,m}|\le\widehat B\) and using the triangle inequality makes the coverage probability at least \(1-\gamma/2-o(1)\), and hence at least \(1-\gamma-o(1)\).  This argument permits arbitrary dependence between the bounded remainder and the linear term.  On the complementary branch the interval is \([-1,1]\), which contains \(\tau\).  This proves honest coverage, including the zero-standard-error boundary case.

For expected length, the good-branch length is exactly \(2\widehat B+2\operatorname{cv}_{1-\gamma/2}(0)\widehat{\mathrm{se}}\).  The coefficient norm, variance consistency, and bounded residuals imply
\[
 \sup_P\mathbb E_P(\widehat{\mathrm{se}})
 \le C\{m^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2}V_a(h_n)^{1/2}\}.
\tag{37}
\]
Because this theorem assumes \(s>1/2\), equations (31)--(34) in the attainment proof and the strict product-smoothness relation give
\[
 \widehat B_{h_n,J_{\mu,n},J_{r,n}}
 =o\{m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\}+O(h_n^{s+1}).
\tag{38}
\]
The oracle truncation envelope identifies the right side of (37)--(38) with \(m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\).  The fallback length is two and its probability is \(O[\exp\{-c\log^3(en)\}]\), so it is negligible in expectation.  Equations (37)--(38) prove the length bound.  The remaining normality and variance statements are exactly (35). \(\square\)
