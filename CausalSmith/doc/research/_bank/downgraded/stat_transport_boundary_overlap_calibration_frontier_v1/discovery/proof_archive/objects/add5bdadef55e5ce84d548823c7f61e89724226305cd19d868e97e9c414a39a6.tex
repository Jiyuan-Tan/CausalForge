\textbf{Proof.}
Fix arbitrary \(s>0\) and \(t>0\), fix a law \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(0,s,t)\), and set \(a=0\). By the definitions of the density factors and the Riesz weight, for every \(x\in(0,1]\),
\[
f_S(x)=x^0g_S(x)=g_S(x),\qquad
f_T(x)=g_T(x),\qquad
r(x)=x^0\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}.
\tag{1}
\]
The source- and target-boundary-factor assumptions give
\[
c_{S,-}\le g_S(x)\le c_{S,+},
\qquad
c_{T,-}\le g_T(x)\le c_{T,+},
\qquad x\in[0,1].
\tag{2}
\]
Because \(c_{S,-}>0\), division by \(g_S(x)\) is valid. Combining the lower numerator bound with the upper denominator bound, and conversely, yields
\[
\frac{c_{T,-}}{c_{S,+}}
\le r(x)=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}
\le \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}},
\qquad 0<x\le1.
\tag{3}
\]

Since \(a=0<1\), the first branch in the definition of \(h_n\) gives
\[
h_n=n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}.
\tag{4}
\]
The assumption \(s>0\) implies \(s+1>0\), so ordinary exponent algebra gives
\[
h_n^{s+1}
=\left(n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}\right)^{s+1}
=n^{-1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover, \(h_n\downarrow0\). Applying \(\mathrm{lem:riesz\text{-}threshold}\) along this sequence in its \(a<1\) case gives
\[
\int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\asymp1,
\tag{6}
\]
and therefore the displayed integral is \(O(1)\) as \(n\to\infty\). Its comparison constant is uniform over the model class and depends only on the fixed boundary-factor constants.

It remains to establish the branch-qualified positivity assertion under the corrected spline-coordinate construction. Fix a fold \(k\) for which both interior training measures have positive total mass, and define
\[
I^T_{k,n}=\{j\in\mathcal T^T_k:X_j^T\ge h_n\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Positive target interior mass implies \(I^T_{k,n}\ne\varnothing\) and \(|\mathcal T^T_k|>0\). Positive source and target interior masses exclude the zero-mass fallback in \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}weights}\), so either its exact-feasibility optimization branch or its penalized optimization branch applies.

Write \(d=J_{r,n}+q\) for the number of standard normalized B-splines and \(\widetilde\phi=(N_2,\ldots,N_d)\) for the corrected nonconstant calibration coordinates. Every \(N_\ell\) is finite on the compact interval \([h_n,1]\), hence \(\widetilde\phi(x)\in\mathbb R^{d-1}\) for every \(x\) in that interval. On either positive-mass branch, the selected calibration multiplier is a finite vector in \(\mathbb R^{d-1}\), and every selected target-marginal multiplier is a finite real number. Choose any \(j_\star\in I^T_{k,n}\). For every \(x\in[h_n,1]\), the squared transport cost is finite and \(\eta_0>0\); consequently,
\[
\exp\!\left[-\frac{
c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,X_{j_\star}^T)
+\widehat\theta_{h_n,J_{r,n}}^{(-k)\top}\widetilde\phi(x)
+\widehat\psi^{(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n},j_\star}}
{\eta_0}\right]>0.
\tag{8}
\]
This is one strictly positive summand in the bracket defining the raw KKT continuation; all other summands are nonnegative. The bracket is thus strictly positive. Since \(\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)>0\),
\[
\widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)>0,
\qquad x\in[h_n,1].
\tag{9}
\]
At \(a=0\), the deterministic downstream cap is
\[
\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}h_n^{-a}
=\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:ricot\text{-}weights}\), the capped downstream weight is the minimum of the strictly positive raw KKT continuation in (9) and the strictly positive cap in (10). Therefore
\[
\widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)>0,
\qquad x\in[h_n,1].
\tag{11}
\]
The use of \(d-1\) nonconstant coordinates affects only the finite inner product in (8), not the positivity argument. All conclusions above are deterministic conditional on the realized positive-interior-mass branch. No empirical norm bound, nonzero realized residual sum, coefficient-norm control, localization rate, or root-risk bound follows from them. \(\square\)
