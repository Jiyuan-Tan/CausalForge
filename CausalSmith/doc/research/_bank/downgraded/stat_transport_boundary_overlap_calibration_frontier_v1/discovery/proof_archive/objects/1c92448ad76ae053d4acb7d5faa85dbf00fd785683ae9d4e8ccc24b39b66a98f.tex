\textbf{Proof.}
We prove the assertion by explicit subexperiments contained in the model. The constructions work for every \(t>0\): their source and target density factors are constant, so neither a density-ratio sieve threshold nor a nuisance-product relation enters the argument.

We first derive the testing inequality used below. Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two laws with parameter values \(\tau_0,\tau_1\), and write \(d=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\). Given any estimator \(\widehat\tau\), assign the observation to the nearer parameter value. An error under \(Q_u\) implies \(|\widehat\tau-\tau_u|\ge d/2\). The sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\); hence
\[
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}
       (\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2
 \ge \frac{d^2}{8}
       \{1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, data processing of relative entropy by a measurable set with probabilities \(p\) and \(q\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)\ge
 p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}.
\]
For fixed \(q\), subtracting \(2(p-q)^2\) from the right-hand side gives a function that vanishes with zero first derivative at \(p=q\) and whose second derivative is
\[
 \frac1{p(1-p)}-4\ge0.
\]
Taking a total-variation maximizing set, with the infinite-relative-entropy case being immediate, therefore gives Pinsker's inequality
\[
 \lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}^2
 \le\frac12\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1).
\tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) show that a pair having sufficiently small constant Kullback--Leibler divergence gives root mean-squared risk at least a constant multiple of its parameter separation.

For every regime, take
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{3}
\]
Both functions integrate to one. Their nonsingular factors are \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\). The declared parameter ranges give
\[
 c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
 \qquad
 c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+}.
\tag{4}
\]
Both factors are constant and therefore belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every \(t>0\), because \(M\ge a+1\). In particular, (3)--(4) use no strict slack in either target-density inequality.

Draw treatment with the fixed known propensity \(e\). Conditional on \(X=x\), take the two source potential outcomes to be independent Bernoulli variables with the arm-specific means specified below, independently of \(A\); give the target potential outcomes the same conditional means. Set \(Y=Y^S(A)\). These choices give consistency, conditional treatment randomization, the known propensity, and conditional mean transportability. Treatment positivity supplies \(\varepsilon\le e(x)\le1-\varepsilon\). Since \(M>4\),
\[
 \Delta_M:=\sqrt{\frac14-\frac1M}>0.
\]
Choose every perturbation constant below small enough that each conditional mean differs from \(1/2\) by at most \(\Delta_M/2\). The outcomes then lie in \([0,1]\), and for either arm
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)
 \ge \frac14-\frac{\Delta_M^2}{4}
 >\frac1M.
\tag{5}
\]
The control regression is constant, and the scaling calculations below put every treated regression in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\). Thus all remaining model conditions hold. The target covariate law in (3) is common to all alternatives, so the \(m\) target observations contribute zero divergence for every \(m\).

\emph{The nonregular case \(a>1\).}
For \(h\in(0,1)\) and a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), let
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\tag{6}
\]
By \(b_h(x)=h^sK(x/h)\mathbf 1\{0\le x\le h\}\), dilation multiplies a derivative of order \(v\le\lfloor s\rfloor\) by \(h^{s-v}\) and preserves the order-\(s\) H\"older seminorm up to the fixed norm of \(K\). Reducing \(\delta\) if needed therefore puts both regressions in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), uniformly in \(h\).

For \(|u|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, direct calculation and Taylor's theorem give
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12+u\right),
  \operatorname{Bern}\left(\frac12-u\right)
 \right)
 =2u\log\frac{1+2u}{1-2u}
 \le C_0u^2.
\tag{7}
\]
The control-arm conditional laws agree, while the treated-arm contribution is multiplied by \(e(x)\le1\). Additivity of relative entropy for product laws, (3), and (7) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,\otimes n},P_{-,h}^{S,\otimes n})
 &\le Cn\int_0^h b_h(x)^2x^a\,dx\\
 &=Cnh^{2s+a+1}\int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Because \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero,
\[
 k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0,
 \qquad
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Choose \(h=c_0n^{-1/(2s+a+1)}\), with \(c_0>0\) small enough that (8) is bounded by a sufficiently small constant. Equations (1)--(2) and (9) imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge c n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\tag{10}
\]

\emph{The regular case \(a<1\).}
Choose a nonnegative, nonzero \(C^\infty\) function \(q\) supported in \([1/4,3/4]\), and set
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}q(x).
\tag{11}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), (11) satisfies the same range, variance, and H\"older conditions. On the support of \(q\), the source density is bounded above and below by positive constants. Therefore (7) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_+^{S,\otimes n},P_-^{S,\otimes n})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{12}
\]
In contrast, under the uniform target density,
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1q(x)\,dx
 \ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
After reducing \(\delta\), equations (1)--(2), (12), and (13) prove
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{14}
\]

\emph{The critical case \(a=1\).}
Put \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), fix an integer \(j_0\ge1\), and choose \(L=L_n\) so that
\[
 \ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}},
 \qquad
 L-j_0+1\asymp\log n.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(j_0\le j\le L\), define
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\}.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{L-j_0+1}\), let
\[
 \mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+
 \sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x).
\tag{17}
\]
The shell interiors are disjoint. Moreover, (15) implies
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 \le C\ell_j^s,
 \qquad j_0\le j\le L.
\tag{18}
\]
After dilation to a shell of width \(\ell_j\), (18) bounds the order-\(s\) H\"older norm of \(\phi_j\) by \(C\delta\). Since a smooth bump supported on \([0,1]\), together with all its derivatives, vanishes at the endpoints after extension by zero, the disjoint shell pieces paste into a function with global order-\(s\) H\"older norm at most \(C'\delta\), independently of \(L\). Choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small makes every sign alternative in (17) a member of the model and enforces (5).

The contribution of coordinate \(j\) to the target parameter is
\[
 d_j:=\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)\,dx
 =\frac{\delta k_1}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(\omega^{(j)}\) is obtained by flipping only coordinate \(j\), then \(f_S(x)=2x\), and (7) implies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{S,\otimes n},
                    P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{S,\otimes n})
 \le Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
\tag{20}
\]
uniformly in \(j,n\), and the other signs.

Place the uniform product prior \(\Pi\) on \(\omega\). The covariate likelihood is sign-free, and an observed outcome involves at most one shell coordinate. Hence the likelihood and posterior factor across coordinates. Since conditional expectation minimizes squared loss and \(\tau_\omega=\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j\omega_j\),
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}
 \mathbb E_\Pi(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2
 =
 \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
       (\tau_\omega\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
   \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
       (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}).
\tag{21}
\]
Write \(q_j=\mathbb E_\Pi(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|.
\tag{22}
\]
Let \(\overline P_{j,+}\) and \(\overline P_{j,-}\) be the mixture experiments conditional on \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\), respectively. Bayes' formula gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi|q_j|
 =\lVert\overline P_{j,+}-\overline P_{j,-}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\tag{23}
\]
Pairing every sign vector with its \(j\)-flip and applying the pointwise log-sum inequality to the paired densities gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\overline P_{j,+},\overline P_{j,-})
 \le 2^{-(L-j_0)}
 \sum_{\omega:\,\omega_j=1}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega,P_{\omega^{(j)}})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{24}
\]
The target factors cancel in (24) because their law is common. Equations (2), (20), (22)--(24), and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi\operatorname{Var}
   (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 \ge c_1>0
\tag{25}
\]
uniformly in \(j\). Consequently, (15), (19), and (21) give
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2
 \ge c_1\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
 \ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\tag{26}
\]
Taking square roots proves the critical lower bound \(c\sqrt{\log n/n}\) without a hidden polynomial loss.

The three bounds (10), (14), and (26), together with the definition of \(\rho_n(a,s)\), prove that for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(m\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge c\rho_n(a,s).
\]
Every subexperiment used the boundary-attaining target density \(f_T=1\), so no strict target-density slack is required. \(\square\)
