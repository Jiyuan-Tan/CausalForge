\textbf{Proof.}
By the definitions of \(r\), \(f_S\), \(f_T\), \(g_S\), and \(g_T\), for every \(h\in(0,1)\),
\[
 \left\|r\mathbf 1\{X\ge h\}\right\|_{L^2(P_S)}^2
 =\int_h^1 r(x)^2 f_S(x)\,dx
 =\int_h^1 \frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx
 =\int_h^1 x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)^2}{g_S(x)}\,dx.
\]
The source- and target-boundary-factor assumptions give, pointwise on \([0,1]\),
\[
 \frac{c_{T,-}^2}{c_{S,+}}x^{-a}
 \le x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)^2}{g_S(x)}
 \le \frac{c_{T,+}^2}{c_{S,-}}x^{-a}.
\]
The constants in this comparison are finite and strictly positive.  Direct integration gives
\[
 \int_h^1x^{-a}\,dx=
 \begin{cases}
 (1-h^{1-a})/(1-a),&a<1,\\
 \log(1/h),&a=1,\\
 (h^{1-a}-1)/(a-1),&a>1.
 \end{cases}
\]
This proves the displayed three-regime equivalence, uniformly over members of the class.

It remains to justify the representer assertion.  On \(L^2(P_S)\), the source component of the target-expectation functional is the linear map
\[
 L(v)=\int_0^1v(x)f_T(x)\,dx=\mathbb E_{P_S}\{r(X)v(X)\}.
\]
If \(a<1\), the preceding calculation with \(h\downarrow0\) shows that \(r\in L^2(P_S)\), so Cauchy--Schwarz makes \(L\) continuous and \(r\) is its representer.  Conversely, if \(L\) had an \(L^2(P_S)\) representer \(q\), then for every bounded measurable \(v\),
\[
 \int_0^1v(x)q(x)f_S(x)\,dx=\int_0^1v(x)f_T(x)\,dx.
\]
Because \(f_S(x)>0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\in(0,1]\), uniqueness of Radon--Nikodym derivatives gives \(q=r\), \(P_S\)-almost surely.  For \(a\ge1\), the same integral calculation shows \(r\notin L^2(P_S)\), a contradiction.  Thus a source \(L^2(P_S)\) representer exists if and only if \(a<1\). \(\square\)
