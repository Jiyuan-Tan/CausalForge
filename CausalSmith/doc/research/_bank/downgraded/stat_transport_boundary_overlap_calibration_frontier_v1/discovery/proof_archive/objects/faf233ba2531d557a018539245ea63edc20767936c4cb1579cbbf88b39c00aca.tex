For fixed \(a,s,t,M\) with \(M\ge a+1\) and \(t>0\), along every sequence with \(m/n\to\kappa\), there is \(c>0\) such that
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\left\{m^{-1/2}+\begin{cases}
n^{-1/2},&a<1,\\
\sqrt{\log n/n},&a=1,\\
n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)},&a>1.
\end{cases}\right\}.
\]
This lower phase diagram requires no strict slack in the target-density bounds. Moreover, writing \(P=(P_S,P_T)\) for the pair of one-observation laws associated with \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\), uniformly over \(\mathbb P_{n,m}\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\),
\[
|\mathfrak b_{h_n}(\mu;P)|\le b_{h_n}^{\circ}(P)\le C h_n^{s+1}
\]
and
\[
m^{-1/2}+h_n^{s+1}
+n^{-1/2}\|\omega_{h_n}^{\circ}(\cdot;P)\|_{L^2(P_S)}
\asymp m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s).
\]
For every fixed \(a>0\), the deterministic allowance is class-sharp, although the actual bias may vanish for special laws. Thus the proved minimax lower frontier matches the boundary-corrected oracle allowance and linear-weight benchmark. The benchmark is a population oracle calculation; no data-measurable minimax upper bound or frontier-attainment conclusion is asserted.
