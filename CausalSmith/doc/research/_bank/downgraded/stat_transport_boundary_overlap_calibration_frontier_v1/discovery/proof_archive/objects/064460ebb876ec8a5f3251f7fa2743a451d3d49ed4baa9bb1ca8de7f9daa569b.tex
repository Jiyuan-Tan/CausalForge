\textbf{Proof.}
We construct both testing alternatives around one common nonconstant smooth baseline.  Let
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad
 f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad
 \ell(x)=x-\frac12.
\]
Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta_0>0\), and take Bernoulli potential outcomes with
\[
 \mu_0(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_1(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x).
\]
Use the known propensity and make treatment conditionally independent of the potential outcomes.  Because \(\ell\) is smooth, small \(\delta_0\) places both regressions strictly inside the radius-\(M\) balls for orders \(s_1\) and \(s_2\), keeps their values in \([0,1]\), and preserves the variance lower bound.  The condition \(M\ge a+1\) admits the constant source factor \(g_S=a+1\), and the remaining density conditions hold for \(g_T=1\).  Denote the resulting observed law by \(P_0\).  It belongs to \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\), and in fact its regressions belong to both smoothness classes.

Set
\[
 h=n^{-1/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\]
Form \(P_1^S\) by replacing the treated regression by
\[
 \mu_{1,1}(x)=\frac12+\delta_0\ell(x)+\delta b_h(x),
\]
where \(\delta>0\) is a sufficiently small fixed constant; leave every other component unchanged.  Smooth scaling of \(b_h\) puts \(P_1^S\) in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_1,t)\).  The same Bernoulli calculation as in \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\), now relative to the common nonconstant baseline whose probabilities stay uniformly away from zero and one, gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}((P_1^S)^{\otimes n},(P_0^S)^{\otimes n})
 \le Cn\delta^2h^{2s_1+a+1}
 =C\delta^2,
\]
and
\[
 |\tau(P_1^S)-\tau(P_0)|
 =\delta h^{s_1+1}\int_0^1K(u)\,du
 \ge c n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\]
Choose \(\delta\) so that Pinsker's inequality makes the total-variation distance at most a fixed \(\eta<1-2\gamma\).

Let \(C_{n,m}\) be any interval with the asserted uniform asymptotic coverage.  Under \(P_0\),
\[
 P_0\{\tau(P_0)\in C_{n,m}\}\ge1-\gamma-o(1).
\]
Coverage under \(P_1^S\) and the total-variation inequality imply
\[
 P_0\{\tau(P_1^S)\in C_{n,m}\}
 \ge1-\gamma-\eta-o(1).
\]
The intersection of these two events has \(P_0\)-probability at least \(1-2\gamma-\eta-o(1)\), a positive constant.  On that intersection the interval length is at least the source separation.  Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}|C_{n,m}|
 \ge c_1n^{-(s_1+1)/(2s_1+a+1)}.
\]

For the second alternative, retain exactly the same nonconstant source law and armwise regressions, and replace only the target density by
\[
 f_{T,1}(x)=1+u_m\ell(x),
 \qquad
 u_m=\delta_1m^{-1/2}.
\]
The strict inequalities \(c_{T,-}<1<c_{T,+}\) allow a fixed small \(\delta_1>0\) for which this is an admissible \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) density.  This alternative belongs to both smoothness classes.  Relative to the same baseline \(P_0\),
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{T,1}^{\otimes m},P_{T,0}^{\otimes m})
 \le Cmu_m^2\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 \le C\delta_1^2,
\]
whereas the nonconstant contrast gives the explicit separation
\[
 |\tau(P_{T,1})-\tau(P_0)|
 =\delta_0u_m\int_0^1\ell(x)^2\,dx
 =\frac{\delta_0\delta_1}{12}m^{-1/2}.
\]
Choose \(\delta_1\) so that the total-variation distance is also smaller than \(1-2\gamma\).  Repeating the same coverage-intersection argument, still at the identical smoother law \(P_0\), yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}|C_{n,m}|\ge c_2m^{-1/2}.
\]
Since both inequalities hold at the same \(P_0\in\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s_2,t)\) and \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\), the claimed sum lower bound follows. \(\square\)
