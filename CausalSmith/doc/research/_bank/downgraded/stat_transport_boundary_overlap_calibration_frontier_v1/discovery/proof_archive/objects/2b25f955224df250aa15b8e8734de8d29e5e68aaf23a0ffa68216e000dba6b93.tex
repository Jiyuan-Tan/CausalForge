\textbf{Proof.}
First consider a centered variable \(W\in[A,B]\), put \(d=B-A\), and suppose \(d>0\). Convexity of the exponential gives, with \(Y=(W-A)/d\in[0,1]\),
\[
e^{uY}\le1+Y(e^u-1).
\]
Writing \(q=\mathbb E(Y)=-A/d\), taking expectations, and centering yields
\[
\mathbb E e^{\lambda W}
\le e^{-uq}\{1-q+qe^u\},\qquad u=\lambda d.
\]
For \(\phi_q(u)=\log(1-q+qe^u)-qu\), direct differentiation gives \(\phi_q(0)=\phi_q'(0)=0\) and
\[
0\le\phi_q''(u)=\frac{q(1-q)e^u}{(1-q+qe^u)^2}\le\frac14.
\]
Taylor's formula with integral remainder therefore implies \(\phi_q(u)\le u^2/8\), and hence
\[
\mathbb E e^{\lambda W}\le\exp\{\lambda^2(B-A)^2/8\}.
\]
The same inequality is trivial when \(d=0\). Independence and exponential Markov's inequality now give, for every \(\lambda>0\),
\[
\mathbb P\left\{\sum_iW_i\ge x\right\}
\le\exp\left\{-\lambda x+\frac{\lambda^2}{8}\sum_i(B_i-A_i)^2\right\}.
\]
When the sum of squared ranges is positive, minimizing the right-hand side at \(\lambda=4x/\sum_i(B_i-A_i)^2\) gives \(\exp\{-2x^2/\sum_i(B_i-A_i)^2\}\). Applying the same argument to \(-W_i\) and using the union bound proves the display. If every range is zero, centeredness makes every \(W_i=0\) almost surely, which proves the stated convention. \(\square\)
