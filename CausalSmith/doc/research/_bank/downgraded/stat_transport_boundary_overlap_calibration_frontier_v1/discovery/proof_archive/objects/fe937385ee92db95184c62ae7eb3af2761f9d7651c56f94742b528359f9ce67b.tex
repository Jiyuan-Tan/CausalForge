After replacing the zero-standard-error convention by its finite continuous extension and restricting the global Hölder-process assertion to \(s>1/2\), the deterministic coverage reduction is valid. On an event where the absolute regularization bias is at most \(\widehat B_{h,J}+\widehat E_{h,J}\) and the centered term is asymptotically standard normal, the definition of \(\operatorname{cv}_{1-\gamma/2}\) gives conditional noncoverage at most \(\gamma/2+o(1)\); a union bound with a calibration-envelope failure event of probability at most \(\gamma/2+o(1)\) yields coverage \(1-\gamma-o(1)\). The missing step is a uniform proof of those stochastic events, variance consistency, and expected-length control for the empirical optimizer.
