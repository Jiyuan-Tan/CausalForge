\textbf{Proof.}
Assume \(a=0\).  For every \(x\in(0,1]\), the definitions of the density
factors and the Riesz weight give
\[
 f_S(x)=x^a g_S(x)=g_S(x),\qquad
 f_T(x)=g_T(x),\qquad
 r(x)=x^{-a}\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}=\frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}.
\]
The source- and target-boundary-factor assumptions imply
\[
 c_{S,-}\le g_S(x)\le c_{S,+},
 \qquad
 c_{T,-}\le g_T(x)\le c_{T,+},
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]
Because \(c_{S,-}>0\), division by \(g_S(x)\) is valid.  Consequently,
\[
 \frac{c_{T,-}}{c_{S,+}}
 \le \frac{g_T(x)}{g_S(x)}
 \le \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}},
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]
Strictly speaking, the initially defined density ratio is needed only on
\((0,1]\).  Since \(g_S\) and \(g_T\) have positive continuous extensions
to zero and \(g_S(0)\ge c_{S,-}\), the quotient \(g_T/g_S\) supplies its
continuous version at \(x=0\); the preceding display uses this version.

The \(a<1\) branch of the definition of \(h_n\) applies and yields
\[
 h_n=n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}.
\]
Since \(s>0\), one has \(s+1>0\), and direct exponent arithmetic gives
\[
 h_n^{s+1}
 =\left(n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}\right)^{s+1}
 =n^{-1/2}.
\]
Moreover, \(h_n\downarrow0\).  Applying
\(\text{lem:riesz-threshold}\) in its \(a<1\) regime therefore gives
\[
 \int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx\asymp1,
\]
and in particular
\[
 \int_{h_n}^1\frac{f_T(x)^2}{f_S(x)}\,dx=O(1).
\]
The constants are uniform over the model class because the comparison in
\(\text{lem:riesz-threshold}\) depends only on the fixed positive boundary
constants.

It remains to prove the positive-branch assertion.  Fix a fold \(k\) for
which both interior training measures have positive total mass, and put
\(h=h_n\) and \(J=J_{r,n}\).  This is exactly the positive-interior-mass
branch of \(\text{def:ricot-weights}\), whether the exact-feasibility or
penalized subbranch is selected.  Positive mass of the target interior
measure means that there is at least one \(j\in\mathcal T^T_k\) with
\(X_j^T\ge h\); it also implies \(|\mathcal T^T_k|>0\).  For a fixed
\(x\in[h,1]\), every finite term
\[
 \exp\!\left\{-\frac{
 c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,X_j^T)
 +\widehat\theta_{h,J}^{(-k)\top}
   \widetilde{\mathbf b}_{h,J}(x)
 +\widehat\psi_{h,J,j}^{(-k)}}{\eta_0}\right\}
\]
is strictly positive.  The cost and spline coordinates are finite on their
compact domains, the selected multipliers are real finite-dimensional
vectors, and \(\eta_0>0\).  At least one indicator in the defining target
sum equals one, so
\[
 \frac1{|\mathcal T^T_k|}
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal T^T_k}\mathbf 1\{X_j^T\ge h\}
 \exp\!\left\{-\frac{
 c_{\mathrm{OT}}(x,X_j^T)
 +\widehat\theta_{h,J}^{(-k)\top}
   \widetilde{\mathbf b}_{h,J}(x)
 +\widehat\psi_{h,J,j}^{(-k)}}{\eta_0}\right\}>0.
\]
Because \(\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)>0\), raising this sum to that power
shows
\[
 \widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h,J}(x)>0.
\]
The cap is also strictly positive:
\[
 \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}h^{-a}
 =\frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}>0,
\]
where the equality uses \(a=0\).  Hence the pointwise projection specified
in \(\text{def:ricot-weights}\) satisfies
\[
 \widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x)
 =\min\!\left\{
 \widetilde r^{\mathrm{KKT},(-k)}_{h_n,J_{r,n}}(x),
 \frac{c_{T,+}}{c_{S,-}}h_n^{-a}
 \right\}>0,
 \qquad x\in[h_n,1].
\]
This proves positivity of the capped downstream continuation; it does not
transfer the raw plan's calibration equations to that continuation.  The
conditions \(t>1/2\) and
\(s/(2s+1)+t/(2t+1)>1/2\) are compatible with, but not needed for, these
deterministic conclusions.  No empirical norm, realized residual sum, or
risk bound has been invoked or established. \(\square\)
