\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(J_u=\mathsf J_u(h)\), first with \(u=t\).  Since \(f_S(x)=x^ag_S(x)\), the population matrix and vector underlying the direct ratio fit are
\[
G_J=\mathbb E_{P_S}\!\left[\mathbf 1\{X\ge h\}X^{-a}\mathbf b_{h,J}(X)\mathbf b_{h,J}(X)^\top\right]
=\int_h^1\mathbf b_{h,J}(x)\mathbf b_{h,J}(x)^\top g_S(x)\,dx,
\]
\[
g_J=\mathbb E_{P_T}\!\left[\mathbf 1\{X^T\ge h\}\mathbf b_{h,J}(X^T)\right]
=\int_h^1\mathbf b_{h,J}(x)g_T(x)\,dx.
\]
The boundary-factor assumptions and \(\mathrm{lem:normalized\text{-}bspline\text{-}stability}\) give
\[
cJ^{-1}\|v\|_2^2\le v^\top G_Jv\le CJ^{-1}\|v\|_2^2.
\]
The weighted summand is bounded by \(Ch^{-a}\), has only a fixed number of nonzero spline coordinates, and its variance in a boundary knot cell is bounded by \(CJ^{-1}h^{-a}\).  Matrix Bernstein, followed by a union bound over the fixed folds and the two dimensions, therefore gives
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\|\widehat G_J-G_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}>c/(2J)\right)
\le 2J\exp\{-cN_h/J\}.
\]
The same scalar calculation for cell counts and target empirical coordinates yields
\[
\mathbb P\{\mathcal E_{n,m}(h)^c\}
\le C\exp\left[-c\min\left\{N_h/J_s,N_h/J_t,m/J_s,m/J_t,nh^{a+1}\right\}/\log(en)\right].
\]
At \(h=h_n\), each entry inside the minimum diverges because \(J_u\asymp\{N_h/\log(en)\}^{1/(2u+1)}\), \(m\asymp n\), and \(nh_n^{a+1}\to\infty\).

Let \(q_J\) be the spline approximant supplied by the cited spline lemma.  Since \(q=g_T/g_S\in\mathcal H^t(C)\) by the factor smoothness and the positive lower bound on \(g_S\),
\[
\|q-q_J\|_\infty\le CJ_t^{-t}.
\]
On the good event the inverse Gram norm is at most \(CJ_t\).  The centered source and target vectors are, by the same Bernstein calculation, of orders \(\sqrt{J_t\log(en)/N_h}\) and \(\sqrt{J_t\log(em)/m}\) in the induced \(L^2(g_S)\) norm.  The ridge perturbation is at most \(C\lambda_nJ_t\).  Projection onto \([q_-,q_+]\) is nonexpansive because \(q(x)\in[q_-,q_+]\).  Consequently
\[
\|\widehat q^{\mathrm{DS}}-q\|_{L^2(g_S;[h,1])}
\le C\{\delta_{t,h}+\lambda_nJ_t\}
\]
on the good event.  Replacing \(q_J\) by the order-\(s\) spline approximant to \(\mu\), and using boundedness of \(Z\), gives the identical regression inequality with \(t,J_t,q\) replaced by \(s,J_s,\mu\).

For every branch, including the midpoint fallback,
\[
q_-x^{-a}\le\widehat r^{\mathrm{DS}}(x)\le q_+x^{-a}.
\]
Therefore scalar Bernstein and the boundary-factor bounds imply
\[
\frac1n\sum_i\widehat r^{\mathrm{DS}}(X_i)^2\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge h\}
=O_P\left(1+\int_h^1x^{-a}\,dx\right)=O_P\{V_a(h)\}.
\]
The largest squared summand is at most \(q_+^2h^{-2a}\), whereas the sum is bounded below with probability tending to one by a constant times \(n\int_h^1x^{-a}\,dx\).  Hence the leverage ratio is bounded in probability by
\[
C\begin{cases}
h^{-2a}/n,&a<1,\\
\{nh^2\log(1/h)\}^{-1},&a=1,\\
\{nh^{a+1}\}^{-1},&a>1,
\end{cases}
\]
which tends to zero at the three declared choices of \(h_n\).  This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
