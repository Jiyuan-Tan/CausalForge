\textbf{Proof.}
We construct subexperiments in \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\).  The construction uses constant nonsingular density factors, so it belongs to the class for every fixed \(t>0\).

We first derive the testing inequality used below.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two laws with parameter values \(\tau_0,\tau_1\), and put \(d=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\).  Classify an observation according to the parameter value nearest to an arbitrary estimator \(\widehat\tau\).  A classification error under \(Q_u\) implies \(|\widehat\tau-\tau_u|\ge d/2\).  Since the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\),
\[
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}
       [ (\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2 ]
 \ge \frac{d^2}{8}
       \{1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, let a Hahn set for \(Q_0-Q_1\) have probabilities \(p\) and \(q\) under \(Q_0\) and \(Q_1\).  The log-sum inequality on this set and its complement yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \ge p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}.
\]
For fixed \(q\), the right-hand side minus \(2(p-q)^2\) vanishes with zero first derivative at \(p=q\), and its second derivative is
\[
 \frac{1}{p(1-p)}-4\ge0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}^2
 \le \frac12\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus a pair having sufficiently small constant Kullback--Leibler divergence gives a root-mean-squared risk lower bound of the order of its parameter separation.

For every regime take
\[
 f_S(x)=(a+1)x^a,
 \qquad f_T(x)=1,
 \qquad 0\le x\le1.
\tag{3}
\]
These are densities and their nonsingular factors are \(g_S=a+1\) and \(g_T=1\).  The declared ranges of the density constants and \(M\ge a+1\) give
\[
 c_{S,-}\le a+1\le c_{S,+},
 \qquad c_{T,-}\le1\le c_{T,+}.
\tag{4}
\]
The constant factors belong to \(\mathcal H^t(M)\) for every \(t>0\).  Hence the density-factor assumptions hold, including when either target-density bound is attained.

Given \(X\), draw \(A\) with the fixed known propensity \(e(X)\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), take the two source potential outcomes to be independent Bernoulli variables with the arm-specific means specified below and independent of \(A\).  Take the target potential outcomes to have the same conditional means, and set \(Y=Y^S(A)\).  This verifies consistency, conditional trial randomization, the known-propensity condition, and conditional mean transportability.  Treatment positivity holds by the fixed design.  Because \(M>4\), define
\[
 \Delta_M=\sqrt{\frac14-\frac1M}>0.
\]
Choose every perturbation constant below sufficiently small that each conditional mean differs from \(1/2\) by at most \(\Delta_M/2\).  Then the outcomes lie in \([0,1]\), and, for either arm,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)
 \ge \frac14-\frac{\Delta_M^2}{4}
 >\frac1M.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus residual nondegeneracy holds.  The control regression is constant, and the scaling calculations below put every treated regression in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\).  The target covariate law in (3) is common to all alternatives, so the \(m\) target observations contribute no divergence for any \(m\).

\emph{The nonregular case \(a>1\).}
For \(h\in(0,1)\) and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\), set
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta b_h(x).
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(p=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and \(\beta=s-p\in(0,1]\).  Dilation of the fixed bump gives, for \(0\le v\le p\),
\[
 \lVert D^v b_h\rVert_\infty\le C h^{s-v},
 \qquad [D^p b_h]_{\beta}\le C,
\tag{7}
\]
where \(C\) depends only on \(K\).  Reducing \(\delta\) if necessary therefore puts both regressions in \(\mathcal H^s(M)\), uniformly in \(h\).

For \(|u|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, direct calculation and Taylor's theorem give
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12+u\right),
  \operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac12-u\right)
 \right)
 =2u\log\frac{1+2u}{1-2u}
 \le C_0u^2.
\tag{8}
\]
The control-arm conditional laws agree, while the treated-arm divergence is multiplied by \(e(x)\le1\).  Additivity of relative entropy, (3), and (8) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,h}^{S,\otimes n},P_{-,h}^{S,\otimes n})
 &\le Cn\int_0^h b_h(x)^2x^a\,dx\\
 &=Cnh^{2s+a+1}\int_0^1K(u)^2u^a\,du.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(K\) is nonnegative and nonzero,
\[
 k_1:=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0,
 \qquad
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|=2\delta k_1h^{s+1}.
\tag{10}
\]
Choose \(h=c_hn^{-1/(2s+a+1)}\), with \(c_h>0\) small enough that (9) is bounded by a sufficiently small constant.  Equations (1), (2), and (10) imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)
 \ge c n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}.
\tag{11}
\]

\emph{The regular case \(a<1\).}
Choose a nonnegative, nonzero \(C^\infty\) function \(\varphi\) supported in \([1/4,3/4]\), and set
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm\delta n^{-1/2}\varphi(x).
\tag{12}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), these regressions satisfy the range, variance, and H\"older restrictions.  On the support of \(\varphi\), the source density is bounded above and below by positive constants.  Hence (8) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_+^{S,\otimes n},P_-^{S,\otimes n})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Under the uniform target density,
\[
 |\tau_+-\tau_-|
 =2\delta n^{-1/2}\int_0^1\varphi(x)\,dx
 \ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{14}
\]
After reducing \(\delta\), (1), (2), (13), and (14) prove
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{15}
\]

\emph{The critical case \(a=1\).}
Put \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), fix an integer \(j_0\ge1\), and choose \(L=L_n\) so that
\[
 \ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}},
 \qquad L-j_0+1\asymp\log n.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(j_0\le j\le L\), define
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\}.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{L-j_0+1}\), let
\[
 \mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+
 \sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x).
\tag{18}
\]
The shell interiors are disjoint.  Equation (16) implies, uniformly over \(j_0\le j\le L\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}\le C\ell_j^s.
\tag{19}
\]
More explicitly, for \(0\le v\le p\),
\[
 \lVert D^v\phi_j\rVert_\infty
 \le C\delta n^{-1/2}\ell_j^{-(v+1)}
 \le C\delta\ell_j^{s-v},
 \qquad
 [D^p\phi_j]_{\beta}
 \le C\delta n^{-1/2}\ell_j^{-(s+1)}
 \le C\delta.
\tag{20}
\]
The zero extension of the smooth compactly supported bump and all its derivatives vanish at shell endpoints.  The disjoint pieces therefore paste across their endpoints with global order-\(s\) H\"older norm at most \(C'\delta\), independently of \(L\).  Taking \(\delta\) small makes every alternative in (18) satisfy both response-smoothness assumptions and (5).

The contribution of coordinate \(j\) to the target parameter is
\[
 d_j:=\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)\,dx
 =\frac{\delta k_1}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(\omega^{(j)}\) is obtained by flipping only coordinate \(j\), then \(f_S(x)=2x\), and (8) implies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{S,\otimes n},
                    P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{S,\otimes n})
 \le Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
\tag{22}
\]
uniformly in \(j,n\), and the other signs.

Place the uniform product prior \(\Pi\) on \(\omega\).  Conditional on all observed covariates, each source likelihood contribution involves at most one shell coordinate.  The target law is sign-free.  Hence the likelihood and posterior factor across coordinates.  Conditional expectation minimizes squared loss and \(\tau_\omega=\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j\omega_j\), so
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}
 \mathbb E_\Pi[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 =\mathbb E_\Pi[\operatorname{Var}(\tau_\omega\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})]
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
   \mathbb E_\Pi[\operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})].
\tag{23}
\]
Write \(q_j=\mathbb E_\Pi[\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}]\).  Since \(\omega_j\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|.
\tag{24}
\]
Let \(\overline P_{j,+}\) and \(\overline P_{j,-}\) be the mixture laws conditional on \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\).  Bayes' formula gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi[|q_j|]
 =\lVert\overline P_{j,+}-\overline P_{j,-}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\tag{25}
\]
Pair every sign vector with its \(j\)-flip.  Joint convexity of relative entropy, which follows pointwise from the log-sum inequality, and (22) give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\overline P_{j,+},\overline P_{j,-})
 \le 2^{-(L-j_0)}
 \sum_{\omega:\,\omega_j=1}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega,P_{\omega^{(j)}})
 \le C\delta^2.
\tag{26}
\]
The target factors cancel because their law is common.  Equations (2) and (24)--(26), with a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\), yield
\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi[\operatorname{Var}
   (\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})]
 \ge c_1>0
\tag{27}
\]
uniformly in \(j\).  Consequently, (16), (21), and (23) imply
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2]
 \ge c_1\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
 \ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\tag{28}
\]
Taking square roots proves the critical lower bound \(c\sqrt{\log n/n}\) without a hidden polynomial loss.

Equations (11), (15), and (28), together with the definition of \(\rho_n(a,s)\), prove that, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(m\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,m}(a,s,t)\ge c\rho_n(a,s).
\]
Every constructed law satisfies every post-repair member property of \(\mathcal M_{n,m}(a,s,t)\) for arbitrary fixed \(t>0\), and each uses the boundary-attaining target density \(f_T=1\).  This also proves the no-slack assertion. \(\square\)
