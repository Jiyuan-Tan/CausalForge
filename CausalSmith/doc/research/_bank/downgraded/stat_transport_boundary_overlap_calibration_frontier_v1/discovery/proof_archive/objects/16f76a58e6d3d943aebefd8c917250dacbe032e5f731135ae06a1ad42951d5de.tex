\textbf{Proof.}
We use an elementary two-point testing inequality.  If two laws \(Q_0,Q_1\) have parameter values separated by \(d>0\), the rule that selects the parameter value nearest an arbitrary estimator \(\widehat\tau\) satisfies
\[
 \max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_u}(\widehat\tau-\tau_u)^2
 \ge \frac{d^2}{8}\{1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\]
Indeed, on an erroneous nearest-parameter decision the squared loss is at least \(d^2/4\), and the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\).  The scalar inequality for relative entropy gives Pinsker's bound
\[
 \lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}^2
 \le \frac12\operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Thus a pair with sufficiently small constant Kullback--Leibler divergence gives root risk at least a constant times its parameter separation.  In every construction below the fixed perturbation multiplier is chosen small enough to make this total-variation bound strictly smaller than one.

Suppose first that \(a>1\).  In \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\), set
\[
 h=n^{-1/(2s+a+1)}.
\]
Then \(nh^{2s+a+1}=1\), whereas
\[
 h^{s+1}=n^{-(s+1)/(2s+a+1)}=\rho_n(a,s).
\]
The two-point inequality proves the asserted source lower bound in the nonregular regime.

If \(a<1\), retain the same admissible baseline densities and place a fixed \(C^\infty\) regression bump inside \([1/4,3/4]\), with height \(\delta n^{-1/2}\).  On this fixed interval \(f_S\) is bounded above and away from zero.  The product Kullback--Leibler divergence is therefore bounded by
\[
 Cn(\delta n^{-1/2})^2\le C\delta^2,
\]
while integration against \(f_T=1\) changes \(\tau\) by a positive constant times \(n^{-1/2}=\rho_n(a,s)\).  This proves the regular source lower bound.

It remains to obtain the logarithm at \(a=1\).  Let \(\ell_j=2^{-j}\), and use the disjoint shells \([\ell_j,2\ell_j]\) for \(j=j_0,\ldots,L\), where \(j_0\) is fixed and
\[
 \ell_L\asymp n^{-1/\{2(s+1)\}}.
\]
Consequently \(L-j_0+1\asymp\log n\).  For independent signs \(\omega_j\in\{-1,1\}\), set
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\,\ell_j}
 K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{\ell_j}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{\ell_j\le x\le2\ell_j\},
\]
and define
\[
 \mu_{1,\omega}(x)=\frac12+\sum_{j=j_0}^L\omega_j\phi_j(x),
 \qquad
 \mu_{0,\omega}(x)=\frac12.
\]
For every retained shell,
\[
 (\sqrt n\,\ell_j)^{-1}\le C\ell_j^s.
\]
Smooth scaling, disjoint support, and the vanishing of a compactly supported smooth bump at the shell endpoints therefore give a common \(\mathcal H^s(M)\) bound.  For small fixed \(\delta\), all response means remain in \([0,1]\) and all Bernoulli variances remain at least \(M^{-1}\).  Thus every sign vector indexes a law in the model class.

Writing \(k_1=\int_0^1K(u)\,du>0\), the contribution of sign \(j\) to the target is
\[
 d_j=\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)\,dx
 =\frac{\delta k_1}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Because \(f_S(x)=2x\), flipping only \(\omega_j\) changes the source experiment by Kullback--Leibler divergence at most
\[
 Cn\int_{\ell_j}^{2\ell_j}\phi_j(x)^2x\,dx
 \le C\delta^2,
\]
uniformly in \(j\).

Put the uniform product prior on the signs.  Conditional on the data, its posterior factorizes over \(j\), since each observation belongs to at most one shell and the likelihood is a product of one-coordinate factors.  Hence the integrated squared risk of every estimator is at least
\[
 \mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(\tau_\omega\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2\,
   \mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m}).
\]
If \(q_j=\mathbb E(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})\), then
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\omega_j\mid\mathcal D_{n,m})=1-q_j^2\ge1-|q_j|.
\]
Under the prior predictive distribution, \(\mathbb E|q_j|\) is the total-variation distance between the two mixture experiments obtained by fixing \(\omega_j=1\) and \(\omega_j=-1\).  Convexity of relative entropy bounds the Kullback--Leibler divergence between these mixtures by the average of the one-coordinate divergences, hence by \(C\delta^2\).  Pinsker's inequality and a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta\) yield \(\mathbb E(1-|q_j|)\ge c_0>0\), uniformly in \(j\).  It follows that
\[
 \inf_{\widehat\tau}\sup_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega(\widehat\tau-\tau_\omega)^2
 \ge c_0\sum_{j=j_0}^Ld_j^2
 \ge c\frac{\log n}{n}.
\]
Taking square roots proves \(\rho_n(1,s)=\sqrt{\log n/n}\) with no polynomial loss.

Finally, the target-density pair in \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}bump}\) has bounded target-product divergence and separation \(cm^{-1/2}\), so it supplies an independent target-sample lower bound.  The minimax root risk is at least each of the source and target lower bounds, and
\[
 \max\{u,v\}\ge\frac{u+v}{2}.
\]
Combining the bounds proves the displayed two-sample sum in all three regimes. \(\square\)
