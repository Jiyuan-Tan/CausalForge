\textbf{Counterexample to the frozen mean-square assertion.}
Set \(a=0\), \(s=t=1\), \(f_S=f_T=1\), and \(e=1/2\). Let both source potential outcomes be independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(1/2\), independently of \(X\), and use the same conditional means in the target population. Then \(\mu_0=\mu_1=1/2\), \(\mu=0\), \(\tau=0\), and \(\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=d,X=x)=1/4>M^{-1}\). Thus this fixed law belongs to every displayed finite-sample class for these parameters, and it satisfies \(a<2t(2s+1)\).

Write \(J=J_{r,n}\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), choose two disjoint interior intervals \(U_n\) and \(V_n\), away from \([0,2h_n]\), each lying in a fixed number of separated degree-one spline supports and having length \(J^{-4}\). Fix one source index \(i_\star\in\mathcal V^S_1\). Let \(E_n\) be the covariate event on which \(X_{i_\star}\in U_n\), all other source observations lie in \(V_n\), and all target observations lie in \(U_n\). Independence and the uniform densities give
\[
 \mathbb P_{n,m}(E_n)=|U_n|^{m+1}|V_n|^{n-1}
 =\exp\{-O((n+m)\log J)\}.
\]
For fold \(1\), the source training measure is supported on \(V_n\), whereas the target training measure is supported on \(U_n\). Since the degree-one spline space contains the function \(x\), exact calibration would imply equality of the two first moments, which is impossible for separated intervals. Hence the penalized branch is selected.

Put \(\beta(x)=\widetilde{\mathbf b}_{h_n,J}(x)\), let \(Q_n=\widehat\Pi^{(-1)}_{h_n,J,1}\), let \(T_n=\widehat P^{T,(-1)}_{h_n}\), and write \(\delta_n=\int\beta\,dQ_n-\int\beta\,dT_n\). The two measures have mass one. Because \(x\) belongs to the degree-one normalized spline space, there are coefficients \(d_{0,J}\) and \(d_J\) such that
\[
 x=d_{0,J}+d_J^\top\beta(x),
 \qquad \|d_J\|_2\le C\sqrt J.
\]
Separation of \(U_n\) and \(V_n\) therefore gives
\[
 d_J^\top\delta_n=\int x\,dQ_n-\int x\,dT_n\le-c_0,
 \qquad \|\delta_n\|_2^2\ge c_1J^{-1}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \int\delta_n^\top\beta,dT_n-int\delta_n^\top\beta,dQ_n=-\|\delta_n\|_2^2\le-c_1J^{-1}.
\]
Normalized degree-one splines are Lipschitz of order \(J\); the choice \(|U_n|=|V_n|=J^{-4}\) turns the preceding averaged inequality into the pointwise bound
\[
 \delta_n^\top\{\beta(x_U)-\beta(x_V)\}\le-c_2J^{-1},
 \qquad x_U\in U_n,\quad x_V\in V_n.
\]
The fallback definition sets \(\widehat\theta=2n^2\delta_n\). If \(S_n(x)\) denotes the exponential average inside the uncapped continuation, the target-marginal multipliers cancel term by term in the ratio and \(0\le c_{\mathrm{OT}}\le1\), so
\[
 \frac{S_n(x_U)}{S_n(x_V)}
 \ge \exp\left\{\frac{2c_2n^2/J-1}{\eta_0}\right\}.
\]
The raw continuation is \(S_n(x)^{\eta_0/(\eta_0+\lambda_0)}\). Its average over the fold-one source training atoms equals \(Q_n([h_n,1])=1\), hence some \(x_V\in V_n\) has raw weight at least one. Consequently,
\[
 \widehat r^{\mathrm{RICOT},(-1)}_{h_n,J}(X_{i_\star})
 \ge \exp\left\{c_3n^2/J\right\}
 \quad\hbox{on }E_n
\]
for the frozen, uncapped definition.

Conditionally on all covariates, the estimator is linear in the independent pseudo-outcomes. The direct coefficient of \(Z_{i_\star}\) is the last displayed weight divided by \(n\). The fold-one regression is zero on \(U_n\), because its training B-spline rows are supported on the separated interval \(V_n\). Every other occurrence of \(Z_{i_\star}\) is through a ridge regression operator whose norm is polynomial in \(n\) and \(J\); locality eliminates its source-residual occurrences on \(V_n\). Thus, for large \(n\), the full coefficient \(c_{i_\star,n}\) satisfies
\[
 |c_{i_\star,n}|\ge \frac1{2n}\exp\{c_3n^2/J\}.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Var}(Z_{i_\star}\mid X_{i_\star})=2\). The conditional-variance identity and \(m/n\to\kappa\) now yield
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{n,m}}[(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{sp}}-\tau)^2]
 \ge 2\mathbb P_{n,m}(E_n)\inf_{E_n}c_{i_\star,n}^2
 \ge \frac1{2n^2}\exp\{2c_3n^2/J-O(n\log J)\}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Since \(J\asymp(n/\log n)^{1/3}\), the positive exponent \(n^2/J\) dominates \(n\log J\). This contradicts the asserted \(O(n^{-1})\) mean-square bound even at ordinary overlap. It does not contradict an \(O_P\) weight statement, because \(\mathbb P_{n,m}(E_n)\to0\). The oracle calculation and the typical-cell calculation from the previous round remain valid: with
\[
 V_h=1+\mathbf 1\{a=1\}\log(1/h)+\mathbf 1\{a>1\}h^{1-a},
\]
the oracle envelope is \(m^{-1/2}+h^{s+1}+n^{-1/2}V_h^{1/2}\), and it equals \(m^{-1/2}+\rho_n(a,s)\) at \(h=h_n\). Also, when \(h_nJ_{r,n}\to\infty\), the smallest-support source occupancy is of order
\[
 \frac{nh_n^a}{J_{r,n}}
 \asymp n^{(2s+1)/(2s+a+1)-1/(2t+1)}(\log n)^{1/(2t+1)},
\]
which diverges polynomially under \(a<2t(2s+1)\). That typical occupancy cannot supply the uniform integrability needed for root mean-square risk. The proposed oracle cap removes precisely this rare-event exponential tail but does not itself prove the remaining empirical localization bounds.
