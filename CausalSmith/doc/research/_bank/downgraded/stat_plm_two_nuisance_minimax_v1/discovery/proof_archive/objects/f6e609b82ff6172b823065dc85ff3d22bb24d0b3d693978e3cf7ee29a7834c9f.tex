Fix a sequence in \(\mathcal R_n^{\rm asym}\). By definition, \(s_{\pi,n}=o(s_{\mu,n})\), so \(s_{\pi,n}\le s_{\mu,n}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Consequently,
\[
\min\{s_{\mu,n}^2,s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n}\}=s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n}.
\]
The condition \(n^{-1/2}=o(s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n})\) therefore gives
\[
\frac{\mathsf U_n}{s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n}}
=\frac{n^{-1/2}}{s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n}}+1\longrightarrow1.
\]
In particular, \(\mathsf U_n\asymp s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n}\). For the lower display, direct division yields
\[
\frac{\mathsf L_n}{s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n}}
=\frac{n^{-1/2}}{s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n}}
+s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n}
+\frac{s_{\pi,n}}{s_{\mu,n}}.
\]
The three terms tend to zero respectively by \(n^{-1/2}=o(s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n})\), by \(s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n}\to0\), and by \(s_{\pi,n}=o(s_{\mu,n})\). Hence \(\mathsf L_n=o(s_{\mu,n}s_{\pi,n})\), as claimed.
