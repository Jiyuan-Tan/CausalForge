\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\psi=\psi_\eta\).  Centering and the exact Luxemburg convention give
\[
 E_P\eta=0,
 \qquad E_P\exp(\eta^2/\psi^2)\le2. \tag{1}
\]
If \(|t|\psi\le1\), introduce an independent copy \(\eta'\).  Jensen's inequality, symmetry of \(\eta-\eta'\), \(\cosh x\le e^{x^2/2}\), and Lyapunov's inequality give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_Pe^{t\eta}
 &\le E_Pe^{t(\eta-\eta')}
 =E_P\cosh\{t(\eta-\eta')\}\\
 &\le E_Pe^{t^2(\eta-\eta')^2/2}
 \le \{E_Pe^{t^2\eta^2}\}^2
 \le 2^{2t^2\psi^2}
 \le e^{4\psi^2t^2}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
If \(|t|\psi>1\), the inequality \(t\eta\le \eta^2/(2\psi^2)+t^2\psi^2/2\) and (1) give the same, looser, last bound.  Therefore, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 |M(z)|\le E_Pe^{\operatorname{Re}(z)\eta}
 \le \exp(4\psi^2|z|^2). \tag{3}
\]
The right side is locally bounded.  Expanding the exponential and using (1) to dominate every compact subseries shows that expectation and differentiation may be interchanged; hence \(M\) is entire.

We first localize a zero.  Suppose, to the contrary, that \(M\) is zero-free on \(|z|\le R_{\mathrm z}\).  For \(R<R_{\mathrm z}\), let \(f=\log M\) be the analytic logarithm normalized by \(f(0)=0\).  Then \(f'(0)=E_P\eta=0\), and (3) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Re}f(z)\le4\psi^2R^2,
 \qquad |z|\le R. \tag{4}
\]
For completeness, the coefficient inequality needed below follows directly from Fourier coefficients.  If \(H(z)=\sum_{j\ge0}a_jz^j\) has nonnegative real part on \(|z|\le R\), then, for \(j\ge1\),
\[
 \frac{a_jR^j}{2}
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\operatorname{Re}H(Re^{it})e^{-ijt}\,dt,
\]
so \(|a_j|R^j\le2\operatorname{Re}H(0)\).  Apply this identity to \(H=4\psi^2R^2-f\).  Since \(H(0)=4\psi^2R^2\),
\[
 |f^{(k)}(0)|\le8k!\psi^2R^{2-k}. \tag{5}
\]
Letting \(R\uparrow R_{\mathrm z}\) and using the displayed definition of \(R_{\mathrm z}\) yields
\[
 |\kappa_k(\eta)|=|f^{(k)}(0)|
 \le \frac{\delta}{2^{k+1}}<\delta, \tag{6}
\]
contradicting cumulant separation.  Thus \(M\) has at least one zero in \(|z|\le R_{\mathrm z}\).

Put \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}=R_++1\) and \(R_{\mathrm{out}}=R_++2\).  Factoring the finitely many zeros inside a circle and integrating \(\log|M|\) around that circle gives the elementary zero-count identity
\[
 \sum_{|a|<R_{\mathrm{out}}}\log\frac{R_{\mathrm{out}}}{|a|}
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|M(R_{\mathrm{out}}e^{it})|\,dt-log|M(0)|. \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, each factor \(z-a\) contributes \(\log R_{\mathrm{out}}\) to the circular mean when \(|a|<R_{\mathrm{out}}\), while a zero-free factor has the same circular mean as its value at zero.  Formula (7) also follows at a boundary zero by a radial limit.  If \(N\) counts zeros in \(|z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), including multiplicities, then (3), (7), and \(\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 N\log(R_{\mathrm{out}}/R_{\mathrm{fac}})
 &\le4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{out}}^2,\\
 N&\le4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{out}}^3
 \le4U_\psi^2(U_R+2)^3=N_{\mathrm{cert}}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]

Let \(s=2^{-m_\circ}\).  The grid contains more than \(N_{\mathrm{cert}}\) radii.  For any zero \(a\), at most one grid radius lies within \(s/3\) of \(|a|\).  Hence (8) supplies a grid circle \(C_\star\) such that
\[
 \bigl||z|-|a|\bigr|\ge s/3
 \quad\text{for every }z\in C_\star
 \text{ and every zero }a\text{ with }|a|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}. \tag{9}
\]
Zeros outside that disk are still farther from \(C_\star\).  The circle strictly encloses the zero found in (6) and contains no boundary zero.  Local factorization of \(M\) and direct integration of \((z-a)^{-1}\) show
\[
 m_\star=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_\star}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz
 =\#\{\text{zeros inside }C_\star\}, \tag{10}
\]
with multiplicity.  Thus \(m_\star\in\mathbb N\setminus\{0\}\).

It remains to verify the numerical boundary modulus.  List the zeros \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\) in \(|z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), and put
\[
 Q(z)=\prod_{\ell=1}^N
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\ell)}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline a_\ell z},
 \qquad g(z)=\frac{M(z)}{Q(z)}. \tag{11}
\]
Then \(|Q|=1\) on \(|z|=R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), \(g\) is analytic and zero-free in that disk, and \(|g(0)|\ge1\).  Let
\[
 B=4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2,
 \qquad u(z)=B-\log|g(z)|.
\]
The boundary bound (3) and the maximum principle give \(u\ge0\).  The Poisson representation of the positive harmonic function \(u\), together with
\[
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}-|z|}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|}
 \le \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-|z|^2}{|R_{\mathrm{fac}}e^{it}-z|^2}
 \le\frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}-|z|},
\]
gives \(u(z)\le\{(R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|)/(R_{\mathrm{fac}}-|z|)\}u(0)\).  Since \(u(0)\le B\), for \(|z|\le R_+\),
\[
 \log|g(z)|
 \ge-8U_RU_\psi^2(U_R+1)^2=-e_\circ,
 \qquad |g(z)|\ge e^{-e_\circ}\ge2^{-2e_\circ}. \tag{12}
\]
For \(z\in C_\star\), (9) and (11) give, factor by factor,
\[
 \left|\frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\ell)}
 {R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline a_\ell z}\right|
 \ge\frac{s}{6R_{\mathrm{fac}}}
 \ge2^{-(m_\circ+d_\circ^{\mathrm{den}})}, \tag{13}
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\le U_R+1\) and \(6(U_R+1)\le2^{3(2U_R+1)}\).  Combining (8), (12), and (13) yields
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_\star}|M(z)|
 \ge2^{-2e_\circ-N_{\mathrm{cert}}(m_\circ+d_\circ^{\mathrm{den}})}
 =\rho_M>0. \tag{14}
\]

Finally, (3) gives \(\sup_{C_\star}|M|\le A_M\).  Applying the power-series coefficient identity on the unit circle centered at \(z\) gives \(|M'(z)|\le A_M\) whenever \(|z|\le R_0\).  Therefore (14), \(\operatorname{len}(C_\star)\le2\pi R_0\), and the definition of \(\Lambda_0\) imply
\[
 \operatorname{len}(C_\star)\sup_{z\in C_\star}
 \left\{1+|M(z)|+|M'(z)|+\left|\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\right|\right\}
 \le\Lambda_0. \tag{15}
\]
All grid sizes and constants in (8)--(15) are deterministic functions only of \((k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\).  This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

The calculation in (1)--(3) of \(\text{lem:noise-only-contour-bank}\), using only centering and the exact Luxemburg premise, gives

\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\), \(g\in\mathcal G\), and bounded \(g'\), and define
\[
 \Phi(d)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{v\in[0,1]}
 E_P\!\left[|g'(X,V)-g(X,V)-d(V)|\mid V=v\right]. \tag{1}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality gives, for any \(d,d'\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\),
\[
 |\Phi(d)-\Phi(d')|\le\|d-d'\|_\infty. \tag{2}
\]
The displacement class is uniformly bounded by \(2C_g\) and satisfies
\[
 |d(u)-d(v)|\le L_D|u-v|^{\gamma_D}. \tag{3}
\]
To verify compactness without importing an extra premise, take any sequence in the class, extract a diagonal subsequence converging on the dyadic rationals, and use (3) on a finite dyadic mesh to show that this subsequence is uniformly Cauchy.  Its uniform limit remains bounded and satisfies (3), so it belongs to \(\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\).  Thus the class is compact in the uniform norm.  A minimizing sequence for \(\Phi\), compactness, and (2) supply \(d_\star\) with
\[
 \Phi(d_\star)=\inf_{d\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}}\Phi(d)=d_P(g,g'). \tag{4}
\]

The zero function belongs to the displacement class.  Hence
\[
 d_P(g,g_0)\le d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g,g_0). \tag{5}
\]
The asserted inclusion follows by applying (5) to every \(g\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}(P,\varepsilon_0)\), and taking the infimum over \(g\in\mathcal G\) gives
\[
 a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le a_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{dir}}(P). \tag{6}
\]
If \(a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}<\varepsilon_0\), choose
\(t=(\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}})/2\).  The definition of an infimum supplies \(g\in\mathcal G\) such that
\[
 d_P(g,g_0)<a_{\mathcal G}(P)+t<\varepsilon_0. \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\) is nonempty.

For the strictness witness, suppose \(g_0=0\), \(\varepsilon_0<a\le C_g\), and \(g_a(x,v)=-a\) belongs to \(\mathcal G\).  The constant displacement \(d_a(v)=a\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\), and
\[
 g_0(X,V)-g_a(X,V)-d_a(V)=0.
\]
Consequently \(d_P(g_a,g_0)=0\), whereas
\[
 d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g_a,g_0)
 =\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{v}E_P[a\mid V=v]=a>\varepsilon_0. \tag{8}
\]
This proves both membership in \(\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\) and nonmembership in the direct ball. \(\square\)

\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\text{lem:modifier-shift-attainment-and-inclusion}\), admissibility of \(\bar g_n\) supplies an attained \(d\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\) such that, with \(R_n=D_n-d(V)\),
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{u\in[0,1]}E_P(|R_n|\mid V=u)\le\varepsilon_0. \tag{1}
\]
The range restrictions imply \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), while the displacement class gives \(|d|\le2C_g\).  Therefore, for \(|z|\le R_0\),
\[
 \left|e^{z\{D_n-d(v)\}}-1\right|
 \le R_0e^{4C_gR_0}|D_n-d(v)|=A_D|D_n-d(v)|. \tag{2}
\]
If \(h<\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal I,\{0,1\})\), kernel support, normalization, nonnegativity, and the density bounds give
\[
 c_V\le\mu_{v,h}=E_Pw_{v,h}\le C_V. \tag{3}
\]
On the support of \(w_{v,h}\), the displacement condition gives \(|d(V)-d(v)|\le L_Dh^{\gamma_D}\).  Since
\(D_n-d(v)=R_n+d(V)-d(v)\), conditioning (2) on \(V\), applying (1), and integrating against the nonnegative weight yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|e^{-zd(v)}E_P[w_{v,h}e^{zD_n}]-\mu_{v,h}\right|
 &\le A_DE_P[w_{v,h}|D_n-d(v)|]\\
 &\le A_D\{L_Dh^{\gamma_D}+\varepsilon_0\}\mu_{v,h}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
The revised error-radius assumption gives \(0<\varepsilon_0\le\varepsilon_\star=(8A_D)^{-1}\).  For sufficiently small \(h\), also \(A_DL_Dh^{\gamma_D}\le1/8\).  Thus the last member of (4) is at most \(\mu_{v,h}/4\).

For each \(z\) in the closed bank disk, the straight segment from \(e^{-zd(v)}A_{v,h}(z)\) to the positive number \(\mu_{v,h}\) stays at distance at least \(3\mu_{v,h}/4\) from zero.  This gives a zero-free homotopy to a constant.  Multiplication by the zero-free entire function \(e^{zd(v)}\) creates neither zeros nor winding.  Consequently \(A_{v,h}\) is zero-free on \(|z|\le R_0\), has winding zero on every bank circle, and, by (3)--(4),
\[
 |A_{v,h}(z)|
 \ge e^{-2C_gR_0}\frac34\mu_{v,h}
 \ge\frac34c_Ve^{-2C_gR_0}. \tag{5}
\]
Finally, \(\eta\perp(X,V)\) and \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\) imply the exact factorization
\[
 F_{v,h}(z)=M(z)A_{v,h}(z). \tag{6}
\]
On the contour supplied by \(\text{lem:noise-only-contour-bank}\), (5)--(6) give
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_\star}|F_{v,h}(z)|
 \ge\frac34\rho_Mc_Ve^{-2C_gR_0}
 =\frac32\rho_F. \tag{7}
\]
Every step is localized in \(v\); no assertion about an unlocalized contaminated transform was used. \(\square\)
%%% FIELD proof-selector-consistency
\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(F_v(z)=F_{v,h_n}(z)\).  The range restrictions and treatment sub-Gaussianity imply, for every fixed integer \(s\ge1\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 E_P[(1+|Z_n|)^se^{sR_0|Z_n|}]<\infty. \tag{1}
\]
For an integer \(q\ge2\), expansion of the \(q\)-th moment of a centered iid sum, grouping index tuples in which every index occurs at least twice, gives
\[
 E_P|(P_{n_1}-P)f|^q
 \le C_q\left\{(Pf^2/n_1)^{q/2}+P|f|^q/n_1^{q-1}\right\}. \tag{2}
\]
Apply (2) to the real and imaginary parts of
\(h_n^{-1}K((V-v)/h_n)e^{zZ_n}\).  Truncate at \(|\eta|\le A\sqrt{\log n}\), discretize \(v\in\mathcal I\) and each contour arclength with polynomially fine meshes, and use the Lipschitz property of \(K\), the smooth contour parametrizations, and (1) to control the oscillations between mesh points.  Since the bank is finite and \(n_1h_n\) diverges at a polynomial rate, choosing \(q\) arbitrarily large gives, for every fixed \(D>0\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}P(\mathcal U_n^c)=O(n^{-D}), \tag{3}
\]
where
\[
 \mathcal U_n=left\{
 \max_j\sup_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}
 |\widehat F_{1j,v}(z)-F_v(z)|\le\rho_F/4
 \right\}. \tag{4}
\]
The identical argument with the extra factor \(Z_n\) controls the empirical \(z\)-derivative at \(v_{\mathrm{ref}}\).

The certified population contour satisfies
\(\inf_{v,z\in C_\star}|F_v(z)|\ge3\rho_F/2\) by conditional-\(L^1\) contour robustness.  Hence on \(\mathcal U_n\) it passes the empirical threshold \(5\rho_F/4\).  Moreover,
\[
 F_{v_{\mathrm{ref}}}(z)=M(z)A_{v_{\mathrm{ref}},h_n}(z), \tag{5}
\]
and the second factor is zero-free with winding zero throughout the bank disk.  Thus the population winding in (5) equals the nonzero treatment-noise winding.  The straight-line homotopy between the population and empirical boundary curves stays nonzero by (4), so the empirical logarithmic-derivative integral has that same nonzero integer value.  At least one contour therefore passes the selector.

Conversely, suppose \(j=\widehat j_n\) on \(\mathcal U_n\).  The empirical threshold and (4) imply
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}|F_v(z)|\ge\rho_F. \tag{6}
\]
The same nonzero homotopy, followed by (5) and the zero winding of its code factor, gives
\[
 \widehat m_j
 =\operatorname{wind}(F_{v_{\mathrm{ref}}},C_j)
 =\operatorname{wind}(M,C_j)=m_j\ne0. \tag{7}
\]
The complement probability in (3) tends to zero uniformly, and (6)--(7) are exactly the four asserted selector conclusions. \(\square\)
%%% FIELD proof-localized-contour-bias
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a pair, a selected good circle \(C\), \(v\in\mathcal I\), and a sufficiently small \(h\).  Put \(w=w_{v,h}\) and define the entire functions
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A(z)&=E_P[we^{zD_n}],\\
 A_\tau(z)&=E_P[w\tau_0(V)e^{zD_n}],\\
 Q(z)&=E_P[wq_0(X,V)e^{zD_n}].
 \end{aligned} \tag{1}
\]
Conditional-\(L^1\) contour robustness makes \(A\) zero-free in the bank disk, and independence gives \(F_{v,h}=MA\).  Since \(we^{zZ_n}\) is measurable with respect to \((X,V,T)\), the conditional mean restriction implies
\(E_P[w\xi e^{zZ_n}]=0\).  Substituting
\(Y=q_0(X,V)+\tau_0(V)\eta+\xi\) and differentiating \(M\) therefore give
\[
 G_{v,h}(z)=M(z)Q(z)+M'(z)A_\tau(z),
 \qquad
 \frac{G_{v,h}(z)}{F_{v,h}(z)}
 =\frac{Q(z)}{A(z)}+\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\frac{A_\tau(z)}{A(z)}. \tag{2}
\]
The ratio \(Q/A\) is analytic inside \(C\), so its contour integral is zero.  The selected winding
\(m(C)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint_C M'/M\,dz\) is a nonzero integer.  Hence
\[
 \Theta^C_{v,h}-\tau_0(v)
 =\operatorname{Re}\left\{
 \frac1{2\pi i m(C)}\oint_C\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}
 \left(\frac{A_\tau(z)}{A(z)}-\tau_0(v)\right)dz
 \right\}. \tag{3}
\]
This proves analytic cancellation of the arbitrary bounded baseline.  It also proves exact recovery whenever the treatment slope is constant.

Kernel support, the H\"older restriction, the density upper bound, and \(|D_n|\le2C_g\) give
\[
 |A_\tau(z)-\tau_0(v)A(z)|
 \le C_Ve^{2C_gR_0}L_\tau h^\beta. \tag{4}
\]
Robustness and the elementary upper bound on \(A\) give
\[
 |A(z)|\ge\frac34c_Ve^{-2C_gR_0},
 \qquad |A(z)|\le C_Ve^{2C_gR_0}. \tag{5}
\]
The selected population denominator is at least \(\rho_F\).  Since \(F=MA\), (5) implies
\[
 \inf_{z\in C}|M(z)|
 \ge\frac{\rho_F}{C_Ve^{2C_gR_0}}. \tag{6}
\]
By \(\text{lem:all-bank-transform-derivative-envelope}\),
\(\operatorname{len}(C)\sup_C|M'|\le\Lambda_0\).  Combining (3)--(6), using \(|m(C)|\ge1\), and enlarging the numerical factor \(4/3\) to \(4\), yields
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}|\Theta^C_{v,h}-\tau_0(v)|
 \le \frac{4C_V^2}{c_V\rho_F}e^{6C_gR_0}\Lambda_0
 L_\tau h^\beta
 =B_{\mathrm{bias}}L_\tau h^\beta. \tag{7}
\]
The only regularity of the code used above was the declared H\"older displacement witness needed for (5); no smoothness of \(q_0\), \(g_0\), or \(\bar g_n\) was invoked. \(\square\)
%%% FIELD proof-uniform-bahadur-multiplier
\textbf{Proof.}
All constants below are uniform over \((P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\); they may depend on the fixed constants in the law class, the finite contour bank, the kernel, and \(\beta\).  Condition on the pilot.  On the event supplied by \(\text{lem:selector-consistency}\), the selected index \(j=\widehat j_n\) is fixed for the independent inference half and
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}|F_{v,h_n}(z)|\ge\rho_F,
 \qquad \widehat m_j=m_j\ne0. \tag{1}
\]
The identities \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\) and \(Y=q_0(X,V)+\tau_0(V)\eta+\xi\), together with the range restrictions, give
\[
 |D_n|\le2C_g,
 \qquad |Z_n|\le|\eta|+2C_g,
 \qquad |Y|\le C_q+C_\tau|\eta|+|\xi|. \tag{2}
\]
The treatment and conditional outcome sub-Gaussian assumptions therefore imply, for every fixed \(s<\infty\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 E_P\!\left[(1+|Y|+|Z_n|)^s e^{sR_0|Z_n|}\right]<\infty. \tag{3}
\]

We first establish the ratio expansion.  Put
\[
 d_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
For \(H_n=A\sqrt{\log n}\), truncate on \(|\eta|+|\xi|\le H_n\).  Bernstein's inequality on polynomial grids in \(v\) and contour arclength, followed by the Lipschitz oscillation bounds for \(K\), \(z\mapsto e^{zZ_n}\), and \(z\mapsto Ye^{zZ_n}\), gives
\[
 \max_{j\le|\mathfrak C_0|}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}
 \left\{|widehat F_{2j,v}(z)-F_{v,h_n}(z)|
 +|widehat G_{2j,v}(z)-G_{v,h_n}(z)|\right\}=O_P(d_n). \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, on the truncation set the envelope is
\(h_n^{-1}e^{O(H_n)}(1+H_n)=h_n^{-1}n^{o(1)}\), while the variance is \(O(h_n^{-1})\).  The grid cardinality is polynomial because \(h_n^{-1}\) is polynomial.  The conditional sub-Gaussian tail bounds in (3) show, after increasing the fixed \(A\), that the probability that any of the \(n\) observations is truncated is \(O(n^{-D})\) for any prescribed fixed \(D\).  Thus (4) holds for the untruncated transforms, and the same calculation makes every fixed-modulus transform failure probability \(O(n^{-D})\).  In particular, (1) and (4) imply the inference-denominator success test with this probability.

Write \(\Delta_F=\widehat F-F\) and \(\Delta_G=\widehat G-G\).  On the denominator event,
\[
 \frac{G+\Delta_G}{F+\Delta_F}-\frac GF
 =\frac{F\Delta_G-G\Delta_F}{F^2}+R,
 \qquad |R|\le C\{|Delta_F\Delta_G|+|\Delta_F|^2\}. \tag{5}
\]
Contour integration of the linear term is exactly
\(n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i)\).  Its expectation is zero because the definitions of \(F\) and \(G\) make the expectation of the pointwise numerator \(YF-G\) vanish.  Since the bank has uniformly bounded contour length, (4)--(5) give
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|
 \widehat\tau_n(v)-\Theta^j_{v,h_n}
 -n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i)
 \right|
 =O_P(d_n^2)
 =o_P\!\left(\frac{(n_2h_n)^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right), \tag{6}
\]
because \(n_2h_n/(\log n)^3\to\infty\).

We next verify nondegenerate studentization.  Let \(b_{v,j}(O)\) be the real coefficient of \(Y\), and hence of \(\xi\), in the score.  It vanishes unless \(|V-v|\le h_n\).  The factorization \(F=MA\), independence of \(\eta\) from \((X,V)\), and the winding identity yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[\eta b_{v,j}(O)]
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}
 \frac{E_P[\eta h_n^{-1}K((V-v)/h_n)e^{zZ_n}]}{F_{v,h_n}(z)}\,dz\right\}\\
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz\right\}=1. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
Independence, the density upper bound, and treatment sub-Gaussianity give
\(E_P[\eta^2\mathbf 1\{|V-v|\le h_n\}]\le Ch_n\).  Cauchy--Schwarz in (7) gives \(E_Pb_{v,j}^2\ge c/h_n\).  Conditional on \((X,V,T)\), the score equals \(b_{v,j}\xi\) plus a measurable term.  Conditional mean zero removes the cross term, and conditional variance nondegeneracy supplies the lower bound below.  Conversely, the score formula, (1)--(3), and
\(E_P[h_n^{-2}K^2((V-v)/h_n)]\le C/h_n\) supply the upper bound.  Hence, with
\[
 s_{v,j}^2=h_n\operatorname{Var}_P\{\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\},
\]
one has
\[
 0<c\le s_{v,j}^2\le C<\infty
 \quad\text{uniformly in }v,j,P,\bar g_n. \tag{8}
\]

It remains to verify, rather than assume, the corrected Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Kato interface.  Include both signs and define
\[
 f_{v,j}^{\pm}(O)=\pm
 \frac{\sqrt{h_n}\{\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)-E_P\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\}}{s_{v,j}}. \tag{9}
\]
Truncate (9) on \(|\eta|+|\xi|\le H_n\), center it, and renormalize its variance to one.  By (3) and (8), this changes its means, variances, empirical supremum, and Gaussian covariance semimetric by \(O(n^{-D})\), uniformly, for arbitrarily large fixed \(D\).  On the truncation set, (1)--(3) imply the deterministic envelope
\[
 b_n\le Ch_n^{-1/2}(1+H_n)e^{CH_n}
 =h_n^{-1/2}n^{o(1)}. \tag{10}
\]
The class is pointwise measurable because \(v\mapsto f_{v,j}^{\pm}(O)\) is continuous and rational grids are dense.  For any finitely supported probability measure \(Q\), the Lipschitz property of \(K\), differentiation of the population coefficients in the ratio score, and (1) give a Lipschitz modulus in \(v\) bounded by \(b_nn^C\).  A one-dimensional grid of mesh proportional to \(\epsilon n^{-C}\), together with the two signs and the finite bank, therefore gives fixed \(v_0<\infty\) and \(a_n\le n^C\) such that
\[
 \sup_QN\{\mathcal F_n,L^2(Q),b_n\epsilon\}
 \le(a_n/\epsilon)^{v_0},
 \qquad 0<\epsilon\le1. \tag{11}
\]
This is the \(\operatorname{VC}(b_n,a_n,v_0)\)-type covering condition in the cited theorem; no assertion that the untruncated score is bounded is used.

Take \(\sigma_n=1\) and
\[
 K_n=Av_0\{\log n\vee\log(a_nb_n)\}=O(\log n).
\]

\[
 \frac{b_n^2K_n}{n_2}
 \le\frac{n^{o(1)}K_n}{n_2h_n}\longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]
Thus the load-bearing multiplier condition \(b_n^2K_n\le n_2\sigma_n^2\) holds for every sufficiently large \(n\).

For the empirical coupling choose \(\gamma_n=n^{-c_0}\), where
\(0<c_0<\beta/(2\beta+1)\).  Because
\(b_n=n^{1/(4\beta+2)+o(1)}\), each of the three cited empirical coupling radii is \(n^{-c+o(1)}\) for some \(c>0\), and its failure probability is
\[
 O(\gamma_n+\log n/n)=o(1). \tag{13}
\]
For the multiplier coupling put
\[
 q_n=\frac{b_n^2K_n^3}{n_2},
 \qquad \delta_n^{\mathrm{mult}}=q_n^{1/8}.
\]
Then \(q_n=n^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)+o(1)}\).  Outside a sample set of probability at most \(3/n_2\), the cited multiplier theorem gives coupling radius
\[
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{mult}}
 \le (K_n/n_2)^{1/2}+q_n^{1/4}+q_n^{1/8}
 =n^{-c+o(1)} \tag{14}
\]
and conditional coupling failure probability at most
\[
 A''\{q_n^{1/4}/q_n^{1/8}+1/n_2\}
 =A''\{q_n^{1/8}+1/n_2\}=o(1). \tag{15}
\]
Thus (13), (15), and the \(3/n_2\) exceptional-sample probability verify that all coupling failure probabilities vanish uniformly.

Let \(B_{n,P,j}\) be the centered covariance-matched Gaussian process for (9).  It is separable, has pointwise variance one, and has almost surely finite supremum by (11).  A direct dyadic chaining argument gives
\[
 E\sup_{v,\pm}|B_{n,P,j}(v,\pm)|\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, take successively finer minimal nets in the covariance semimetric.  At level \(\ell\), (11) bounds the number of increments by \((Ca_nb_n2^\ell)^{v_0}\); the Gaussian moment-generating identity gives
\(E\max_{r\le N}|G_r|\le s\sqrt{2\log(2N)}\) for centered Gaussian variables of standard deviation at most \(s\).  Summing
\(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{\log(Ca_nb_n2^\ell)}\) proves (16).  The same chaining construction couples the negligible covariance-semimetric truncation error to a negligible Gaussian-supremum error.

The corrected Gaussian anti-concentration inequality and (13)--(16) now give
\[
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{emp}}\{E\sup|B_{n,P,j}|+1\}\to0,
 \qquad
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{mult}}\{E\sup|B_{n,P,j}|+1\}\to0, \tag{17}
\]
uniformly.  Thus both the empirical and conditional multiplier absolute-supremum laws have uniformly vanishing Kolmogorov distance from the same Gaussian law.  This verifies the required quantitative condition \(\epsilon_n\sqrt{\log n}\to0\), rather than inferring distributional equivalence from radius convergence alone.

We finally connect the population score calculation to the implemented plug-in score.  The transform bound (4), the ratio identity (5), (1), and (3) give
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}
 \frac{\|\sqrt{h_n}(\widehat\psi_{v,j}-\psi^j_{v,h_n})\|_{P,2}}{s_{v,j}}
 =O_P(d_n). \tag{18}
\]
The square classes generated by (11) satisfy the same polynomial covering bound with envelope \(4b_n^2\).  Bernstein's inequality on the same nets, (12), and (18) yield
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|\frac{\widehat s_n(v)}{s_{v,j}}-1\right|=O_P(d_n)=o_P(1). \tag{19}
\]
The conditional Gaussian chaining calculation used for (16) turns (18) and (19) into a multiplier-process discrepancy \(O_P(d_n\sqrt{\log n})\).  After multiplication by the anti-concentration factor in (16), this is \(O_P(d_n\log n)=o_P(1)\).  Moreover, multiplying the remainder in (6) by \(\sqrt{n_2h_n}/s_{v,j}\) gives \(o_P(1/\sqrt{\log n})\), again negligible on the anti-concentration scale.  Equations (17)--(19) prove the asserted conditional multiplier validity, ratio consistency, and bounds away from zero and infinity.

Choose the truncation and grid constants so that every pilot-transform, inference-transform, square-class, and plug-in comparison failure is \(O(n^{-D})\), where \(D>\beta/(2\beta+1)+2\).  Intersecting the pilot-measurable conditions defines \(\mathcal E_{1n}\), and intersecting the inference and comparison conditions defines \(\mathcal E_{2n}\).  Then
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 P(\mathcal E_{1n}^c\cup\mathcal E_{2n}^c)=o(r_n), \tag{20}
\]
and both actual fallback events are contained in this union.

For completeness, the expected critical-value assertion requires no additional tail theorem.  Conditional Markov inequality bounds the conditional \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile by \(\alpha^{-1}\) times the conditional expected multiplier supremum.  On the comparison event, (16), (18), and (19) make this expectation times \(\sup_v\widehat s_n(v)\) at most \(C\sqrt{\log n}\).  On its complement but within the actual denominator-success event, the denominator is bounded below by \(\rho_F/2\); (3), the deterministic floor \(\widehat s_n\ge n^{-1}\), and finiteness of the bank give polynomial moments of the product.  H\"older's inequality and the arbitrarily large \(D\) in (20) make that complementary expectation negligible.  Hence
\[
 E_P\!\left[\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\widehat s_n(v);
 \text{ both actual success events}\right]\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (1), (6), and (17)--(21) establish every assertion of the lemma. \(\square\)

\textbf{Proof.}
Fix an arbitrary \(\mathcal A\in\mathfrak H_\alpha\).  Let \(P_0^n,P_1^n,\ldots,P_m^n\) be the experiments supplied by \(\text{lem:many-bump-subexperiment}\), all with the exact external code \(\bar g_n=0\), and write \(\tau_0,\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_m\) for their slope curves.  There is a constant \(c_s>0\) such that
\[
 \min_{j\ne\ell}\|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty
 \ge s_n:=c_sr_n, \qquad
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)\le c_0\log m,
 \quad j=1,\ldots,m. \tag{1}
\]
The fixed bump amplitude in that lemma may be chosen so that \(c_0<(1-\alpha)/4\).

We record the required many-hypothesis testing inequality directly.  Let \(J\) be uniform on \(\{0,ldots,m\}\), and conditionally on \(J=j\), draw the data from \(P_j^n\).  If \(\overline P=(m+1)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^mP_j^n\), the elementary relative-entropy identity
\[
 \frac1{m+1}\sum_{j=0}^m\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 =\frac1{m+1}\sum_{j=0}^m\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,\overline P)
 +\operatorname{KL}(\overline P,P_0^n) \tag{2}
\]
shows that the mutual information \(I(J;O_1^n)\) is at most \(c_0\log m\).  For any measurable, possibly randomized classifier \(\widehat J\), put \(E=\mathbf 1\{\widehat J\ne J\}\) and \(p_e=P(E=1)\).  Conditional on \(E=0\), \(J\) is determined by \(\widehat J\); conditional on \(E=1\), at most \(m\) values remain.  Hence
\[
 H(J\mid O_1^n)
 \le H(E)+p_e\log m
 \le\log2+p_e\log m. \tag{3}
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log(m+1)\), (2)--(3) yield
\[
 \frac1{m+1}\sum_{j=0}^mP_j^n(\widehat J\ne j)
 \ge p_{*,n}:=\frac{\log(m+1)-c_0\log m-\log2}{\log m}. \tag{4}
\]
In particular, \(\liminf_np_{*,n}\ge1-c_0>\alpha\).  This proves the testing inequality from the entropy identities rather than assuming a testing conclusion.

For a realized band, let
\[
 \Delta_n=\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\{U_n(v)-L_n(v)\},
\]
and define \(\widehat J\) to be the smallest index \(j\) for which \(\tau_j(v)\in[L_n(v),U_n(v)]\) for every \(v\in\mathcal I\), with value zero if no such index exists.  This is measurable because the candidate family is finite and its curves are continuous.  By (1), on the event that the band covers \(\tau_j\) and \(\Delta_n<s_n\), no other candidate curve can lie wholly in the band.  Therefore
\[
 P_j^n(\widehat J\ne j)
 \le P_j^n\{\tau_j\notin\mathcal A_n\}
 +P_j^n(\Delta_n\ge s_n). \tag{5}
\]

Uniform asymptotic honesty over \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\), and membership of every pair in (1), imply that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \max_{0\le j\le m}P_j^n\{\tau_j\notin\mathcal A_n\}
 \le\alpha+\varepsilon. \tag{6}
\]
Averaging (5), using (4)--(6), and applying
\(P_j^n(\Delta_n\ge s_n)\le E_{P_j^n}\Delta_n/s_n\), gives
\[
 \frac1{m+1}\sum_{j=0}^mE_{P_j^n}\Delta_n
 \ge s_n\{p_{*,n}-\alpha-\varepsilon\}. \tag{7}
\]
Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) smaller than \((1-c_0-\alpha)/2\).  The braces in (7) are then bounded below by a positive constant for all large \(n\).  Since all of these exact-code pairs lie in \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\),
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}r_n^{-1}
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)\ge c>0. \tag{8}
\]
The argument used no symmetry, fixed-width property, or contour construction for \(\mathcal A\).

Finally, \(\text{thm:honest-spectral-band}\) places \(\mathcal B\) in \(\mathfrak H_\alpha\) and gives the matching upper bound \(Cr_n\) on the identical pair class.  Combining this upper bound with (8) proves minimax expected sup-width order \(r_n\). \(\square\)
