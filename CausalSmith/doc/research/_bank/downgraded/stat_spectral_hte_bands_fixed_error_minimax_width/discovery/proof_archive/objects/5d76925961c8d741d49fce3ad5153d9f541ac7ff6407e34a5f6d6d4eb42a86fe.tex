\textbf{Proof of the proposed statement.}
Throughout, condition on the pilot.  On the event \(\mathcal E_{1n}\) constructed in the selector proof, \(j=\widehat j_n\) is fixed, its population denominators obey the displayed moduli, and
\[
 \widehat m_j=\operatorname{wind}(H,C_j)
 =\operatorname{wind}(M,C_j)=m_j\ne0. \tag{1}
\]
The qualification by \(\mathcal E_{1n}\) is necessary: the estimator uses \(\widehat m_j\), whereas its population center and score use \(m_j\).

We first collect uniform moment and entropy bounds.  From the range assumptions and the structural equation,
\[
 |Y|\le C_q+C_\tau|\eta|+|\xi|. \tag{2}
\]
For each fixed \(s<\infty\), treatment sub-Gaussianity and conditional outcome sub-Gaussianity therefore give
\[
 \sup_Q E_Q\left[(1+|Y|+|Z_n|)^s
 e^{sR_0|Z_n|}\right]<\infty. \tag{3}
\]
Translation of the fixed bounded Lipschitz kernel is a one-dimensional Lipschitz class, and the bank contains finitely many piecewise smooth contours.  After truncating \(|\eta|+|\xi|\) at \(A\sqrt{\log n}\), the resulting envelopes are \(n^{o(1)}h_n^{-1}\), and polynomial grids in \(v\) and contour arclength cover every class used below.  The complement of the truncation event is \(O(n^{-D})\) for arbitrarily large fixed \(D\).  Applying the iid moment inequality (4) of the selector proof on those grids and controlling oscillations yields
\[
 \sup_{v,z}\{|\widehat F_{2j,v}(z)-F_{v,h_n}(z)|
              +|\widehat G_{2j,v}(z)-G_{v,h_n}(z)|\}
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}}\right) \tag{4}
\]
uniformly in \(Q\) and the finitely many possible \(j\).  The same proof gives polynomially small probability for every fixed-modulus failure.

Let \(\Delta_F=\widehat F_{2j,v}-F_{v,h_n}\) and \(\Delta_G=\widehat G_{2j,v}-G_{v,h_n}\).  On the inference-denominator event, the exact algebraic identity
\[
 \frac{G+\Delta_G}{F+\Delta_F}-\frac GF
 =\frac{F\Delta_G-G\Delta_F}{F^2}+R,
 \qquad
 |R|\le C\{|\Delta_F\Delta_G|+|\Delta_F|^2\}, \tag{5}
\]
holds uniformly on the selected contour.  Contour integration of the linear term in (5), followed by (1), is exactly
\[
 n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i), \tag{6}
\]
whose expectation is zero.  Equations (4)--(5) give a uniform remainder
\[
 O_P\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}\right)
 =o_P\!\left(\frac{(n_2h_n)^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right), \tag{7}
\]
because
\[
 \frac{n_2h_n}{(\log n)^3}\longrightarrow\infty \tag{8}
\]
for every fixed \(\beta>0\).  This proves the Bahadur expansion.

We next verify nondegenerate studentization rather than assuming it.  Let the coefficient of \(Y\), and hence of \(\xi\), in the score be
\[
 b_{v,j}(O)=\operatorname{Re}\left\{
 \frac{w_{v,h_n}}{2\pi i m_j}
 \oint_{C_j}\frac{e^{zZ_n}}{F_{v,h_n}(z)}\,dz\right\}. \tag{9}
\]
It vanishes unless \(|V-v|\le h_n\).  Differentiating the population contour functional along the constant-slope path \(Y\mapsto Y+t\eta\), or directly using independence, gives
\[
 E_Q[\eta b_{v,j}(O)]
 =\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi i m_j}
 \oint_{C_j}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz\right\}=1. \tag{10}
\]
Since \(\eta\perp V\), \(E\eta^2\le C\psi_\eta^2\), and \(P(|V-v|\le h_n)\le2C_Vh_n\), Cauchy--Schwarz in (10) implies
\[
 E_Qb_{v,j}^2\ge\frac{c}{h_n}. \tag{11}
\]
The remaining part of the score is measurable with respect to \((X,V,T)\), while \(E(\xi\mid X,V,T)=0\).  Conditional variance nondegeneracy and (11) thus show
\[
 h_n\operatorname{Var}_Q(\psi^j_{v,h_n})
 \ge h_n\underline\sigma_\xi^2E_Qb_{v,j}^2\ge c. \tag{12}
\]
Conversely, (3), the denominator and contour envelopes, and \(Ew_{v,h_n}^2\le C/h_n\) give
\[
 \sup_{Q,v,j}h_nE_Q[(\psi^j_{v,h_n})^2]\le C. \tag{13}
\]

For completeness, apply the cited Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Kato coupling to the symmetric class consisting of plus and minus the centered, studentized functions \(\sqrt{h_n}\psi^j_{v,h_n}/s_j(v)\).  On the truncation event its envelope is \(b_n=h_n^{-1/2}n^{o(1)}\), its variance is bounded by one, and its entropy logarithm is \(O(\log n)\).  Since \(n_2h_n\) is polynomially divergent, every empirical-process and conditional-multiplier coupling error displayed in that cited result tends to zero uniformly.  Its anti-concentration conclusion and the \(o(1/\sqrt{\log n})\) normalized remainder in (7) transfer conditional multiplier quantiles to the studentized absolute supremum.  Repeating the grid moment argument for squared scores, and using (12)--(13), gives
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|\frac{\widehat s_n(v)}{s_j(v)}-1\right|=o_P(1),
 \qquad 0<c\le\inf_{v}s_j(v)\le\sup_{v}s_j(v)\le C. \tag{14}
\]
Replacing population scores by plug-in scores changes their normalized empirical \(L^2\) distance by \(o_P(1/\sqrt{\log n})\), by (4), so the same coupling applies to the implemented multiplier process.

Let \(\mathcal E_{2n}\) be the intersection of inference-denominator success, the bounds (4), and the variance and plug-in comparison events just used.  The truncation and arbitrarily high moment orders in (3)--(4) give
\[
 \sup_Q P_Q(\mathcal E_{1n}^c\cup\mathcal E_{2n}^c)=o(r_n). \tag{15}
\]
In particular, each of the two actual fallback probabilities is \(o(r_n)\).  Finally, conditional Gaussian maximal inequalities for the same logarithmic-entropy class give
\[
 E_e\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|n_2^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in I_2}
 e_i\sqrt{h_n}\{\widehat\psi_i(v)-\overline{\widehat\psi}(v)\}/\widehat s_n(v)\right|
 \le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{16}
\]
For fixed \(\alpha\), Gaussian concentration bounds its conditional \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile by the right side of (16) plus a fixed constant.  Equations (3), (13)--(16), including integration over the truncation complement, therefore yield
\[
 E_P\left[\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\widehat s_n(v);
 \text{both actual success events}\right]\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{17}
\]
Equations (7), (12)--(17) prove every assertion of the proposed statement. \(\square\)
