\textbf{Proof.}
All constants below are uniform over \((P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\); they may depend on the fixed constants in the law class, the finite contour bank, the kernel, and \(\beta\). Condition on the pilot. On the event supplied by \(\text{lem:selector-consistency}\), the selected index \(j=\widehat j_n\) is fixed for the independent inference half and
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}|F_{v,h_n}(z)|\ge\rho_F,
 \qquad \widehat m_j=m_j\ne0. \tag{1}
\]
The identity \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\), the range restrictions, and the structural equation give
\[
 |D_n|\le2C_g,qquad
 |Z_n|\le|\eta|+2C_g,qquad
 |Y|\le C_q+C_\tau|\eta|+|\xi|. \tag{2}
\]
The treatment and conditional outcome sub-Gaussian assumptions therefore imply, for every fixed \(s<\infty\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 E_P\!left[(1+|Y|+|Z_n|)^s e^{sR_0|Z_n|}\right]<\infty. \tag{3}
\]

We first establish the ratio expansion. Put
\[
 d_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
For \(H_n=A\sqrt{\log n}\), truncate on \(|\eta|+|\xi|\le H_n\). Bernstein's inequality on polynomial grids in \(v\) and contour arclength, followed by the Lipschitz oscillation bounds for \(K\), \(z\mapsto e^{zZ_n}\), and \(z\mapsto Ye^{zZ_n}\), gives
\[
 \max_{j\le|\mathfrak C_0|}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}
 \left\{|\widehat F_{2j,v}(z)-F_{v,h_n}(z)|
 +|\widehat G_{2j,v}(z)-G_{v,h_n}(z)|\right\}=O_P(d_n). \tag{4}
\]
Here the grid cardinality is polynomial because \(h_n^{-1}\) is polynomial, while the truncated envelope is \(h_n^{-1}\exp(O(H_n))(1+H_n)=h_n^{-1}n^{o(1)}\). By (3), the probability that any of the \(n\) observations is truncated is at most \(C_Dn^{-D}\) after increasing \(A\), for any prescribed \(D\). The same calculation therefore proves (4) for the untruncated transforms and makes every fixed-modulus failure probability \(O(n^{-D})\). In particular, (1) and (4) imply the inference-denominator success test with that probability.

Write \(\Delta_F=\widehat F-F\) and \(\Delta_G=\widehat G-G\). On the denominator event,
\[
 \frac{G+\Delta_G}{F+\Delta_F}-\frac GF
 =\frac{F\Delta_G-G\Delta_F}{F^2}+R,
 \qquad |R|\le C\{|\Delta_F\Delta_G|+|\Delta_F|^2\}. \tag{5}
\]
Contour integration of the linear term is exactly \(n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i)\), and its expectation is zero because the definitions of \(F\) and \(G\) make the expectation of the numerator \(YF-G\) vanish pointwise on the contour. Since the bank has bounded contour length, (4)--(5) give
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|widehat\tau_n(v)-\Theta^j_{v,h_n}
 -n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i)\right|
 =O_P(d_n^2)
 =o_P\!\left(\frac{(n_2h_n)^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right), \tag{6}
\]
because \(n_2h_n/(\log n)^3\to\infty\).

We next verify nondegenerate studentization. Let \(b_{v,j}(O)\) be the real coefficient of \(Y\), and hence of \(\xi\), in the score. The score formula shows that \(b_{v,j}=0\) unless \(|V-v|\le h_n\). The factorization \(F=MA\), independence of \(\eta\) from \((X,V)\), and the winding identity yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[\eta b_{v,j}(O)]
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}
 \frac{E_P[\eta h_n^{-1}K((V-v)/h_n)e^{zZ_n}]}{F_{v,h_n}(z)}\,dz\right\}\\
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz\right\}=1. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
Independence, the upper density bound, and treatment sub-Gaussianity give \(E_P[\eta^2\mathbf 1\{|V-v|\le h_n\}]\le Ch_n\). Cauchy--Schwarz in (7) gives \(E_Pb_{v,j}^2\ge c/h_n\). Conditional on \((X,V,T)\), the score equals \(b_{v,j}\xi\) plus a measurable term. Conditional mean zero removes the cross term, and conditional variance nondegeneracy gives the lower bound below. Conversely, the score formula, (1)--(3), and \(E[h_n^{-2}K^2((V-v)/h_n)]\le C/h_n\) give the upper bound. Thus, with
\[
 s_{v,j}^2=h_n\operatorname{Var}_P\{\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\},
\]
we have
\[
 0<c\le s_{v,j}^2\le C<\infty \quad
 \text{uniformly in }v,j,P,\bar g_n. \tag{8}
\]

It remains to verify, rather than assume, the corrected Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Kato interface. Include both signs and define
\[
 f_{v,j}^{\pm}(O)=\pm\frac{\sqrt{h_n}\{\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)-E_P\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\}}{s_{v,j}}. \tag{9}
\]
Truncate (9) on \(|\eta|+|\xi|\le H_n\), center it, and renormalize its variance to one. By (3) and (8), this changes its means, variances, empirical supremum, and Gaussian covariance semimetric by \(O(n^{-D})\), uniformly, for arbitrarily large fixed \(D\). On the truncation set, (1)--(3) imply the deterministic envelope
\[
 b_n\le C h_n^{-1/2}(1+H_n)e^{C H_n}
 =h_n^{-1/2}n^{o(1)}. \tag{10}
\]
The class is pointwise measurable because \(v\mapsto f_{v,j}^{\pm}(O)\) is continuous and rational grids are dense. For any finitely supported probability measure \(Q\), the Lipschitz property of \(K\), differentiation of the population coefficients in the ratio score, and the denominator bound (1) give a Lipschitz modulus in \(v\) bounded by \(b_n n^C\). A one-dimensional grid of mesh \(\epsilon n^{-C}\), together with the two signs and the finite bank, therefore gives fixed \(v_0<\infty\) and \(a_n\le n^C\) such that
\[
 \sup_Q N\{\mathcal F_n,L^2(Q),b_n\epsilon\}
 \le(a_n/\epsilon)^{v_0},qquad 0<\epsilon\le1. \tag{11}
\]
This is exactly the \(\operatorname{VC}(b_n,a_n,v_0)\)-type covering condition used by the cited theorem; no assertion that the untruncated score is bounded is being made.

Take \(\sigma_n=1\) and
\[
 K_n=A v_0\{\log n\vee\log(a_nb_n)\}=O(\log n).
\]
Since \(h_n=(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}c_h\), (10) gives
\[
 \frac{b_n^2K_n}{n_2}le\frac{n^{o(1)}K_n}{n_2h_n}\longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]
Thus the load-bearing multiplier size condition \(b_n^2K_n\le n_2\sigma_n^2\) holds for all sufficiently large \(n\). This explicitly verifies the condition missing from the former citation interface.

For the empirical coupling, choose \(\gamma_n=n^{-c_0}\) with \(0<c_0<\beta/(2\beta+1)\). Because \(b_n=n^{1/(4\beta+2)+o(1)}\), each of the three cited empirical coupling radii is \(n^{-c+o(1)}\) for some \(c>0\), and its failure probability is
\[
 O(\gamma_n+\log n/n)=o(1). \tag{13}
\]
For the multiplier coupling put
\[
 q_n=\frac{b_n^2K_n^3}{n_2},qquad \delta_n^{\mathrm{mult}}=q_n^{1/8}.
\]
Then \(q_n=n^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)+o(1)}\), and the cited multiplier theorem gives, outside a sample set of probability at most \(3/n_2\), coupling radius
\[
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{mult}}
 \le (K_n/n_2)^{1/2}+q_n^{1/4}+q_n^{1/8}=n^{-c+o(1)} \tag{14}
\]
and conditional coupling failure probability at most
\[
 A''\{q_n^{1/4}/q_n^{1/8}+1/n_2\}
 =A''\{q_n^{1/8}+1/n_2\}=o(1). \tag{15}
\]
Equations (13) and (15), together with the \(3/n_2\) exceptional-sample probability, verify that all coupling failure probabilities vanish uniformly, not merely that the radii vanish.

Let \(B_{n,P,j}\) be the centered covariance-matched Gaussian process for (9). It is separable, has pointwise variance one, and has almost surely finite supremum by (11). A direct dyadic chaining argument gives
\[
 E\sup_{v,\pm}|B_{n,P,j}(v,\pm)|\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, take successively finer minimal nets in the covariance semimetric. At level \(\ell\), (11) bounds the number of increments by \((Ca_nb_n2^\ell)^{v_0}\); the Gaussian moment-generating function gives \(E\max_{r\le N}|G_r|\le s\sqrt{2\log(2N)}\) for centered Gaussian variables of standard deviation at most \(s\). Summing \(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{\log(Ca_nb_n2^\ell)}\) proves (16). The corrected Gaussian anti-concentration inequality and (13)--(16) now imply
\[
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{emp}}\{E\sup|B_{n,P,j}|+1\}\to0,qquad
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{mult}}\{E\sup|B_{n,P,j}|+1\}\to0, \tag{17}
\]
uniformly. Thus both the empirical and conditional multiplier absolute-supremum laws have uniformly vanishing Kolmogorov distance from the same Gaussian law. This verifies the required quantitative condition \(\epsilon_n\sqrt{\log n}\to0\), rather than inferring distributional equivalence from radius convergence alone.

We finally connect the population score calculation to the implemented plug-in score. The transform bound (4), the ratio identity (5), (1), and (3) give
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}
 \frac{\|\sqrt{h_n}(\widehat\psi_{v,j}-\psi^j_{v,h_n})\|_{P,2}}{s_{v,j}}
 =O_P(d_n). \tag{18}
\]
The square classes generated by (11) satisfy the same polynomial covering bound with envelope \(4b_n^2\). Bernstein's inequality on the same nets, (12), and (18) yield
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|\frac{\widehat s_n(v)}{s_{v,j}}-1\right|=o_P(1). \tag{19}
\]
The conditional Gaussian chaining calculation used for (16) turns (18) into a multiplier-process discrepancy \(O_P(d_n\sqrt{\log n})=o_P(1)\). Moreover, multiplying the remainder in (6) by \(\sqrt{n_2h_n}/s_{v,j}\) gives \(o_P(1/\sqrt{\log n})\). Both discrepancies therefore obey the anti-concentration scale in (17). Equations (17)--(19) prove the asserted conditional multiplier validity, ratio consistency, and bounds away from zero and infinity.

Choose the truncation and grid constants so that every pilot transform, inference transform, square-class, and plug-in comparison failure is \(O(n^{-D})\) with \(D>\beta/(2\beta+1)+2\). Intersecting the pilot-measurable conditions defines \(\mathcal E_{1n}\), and intersecting the inference and comparison conditions defines \(\mathcal E_{2n}\). Then
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 P(\mathcal E_{1n}^c\cup\mathcal E_{2n}^c)=o(r_n), \tag{20}
\]
and both actual fallback events are contained in this union.

For completeness, the expected critical-value assertion does not require an unmentioned tail theorem. Conditional Markov inequality bounds the fixed \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile by \(\alpha^{-1}\) times the conditional expected multiplier supremum. On the comparison event, the chaining bound (16), (18), and (19) make this expectation times \(\sup_v\widehat s_n(v)\) at most \(C\sqrt{\log n}\). On its complement but within the actual denominator-success event, the denominator is bounded below by \(\rho_F/2\); (3), the deterministic floor \(\widehat s_n\ge n^{-1}\), and the finite bank give polynomial moments of the product. H\"older's inequality and the arbitrarily large \(D\) in (20) make that complementary expectation negligible. Hence
\[
 E_P\!\left[\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\widehat s_n(v);
 \text{ both actual success events}\right]\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (1), (6), and (17)--(21) establish every assertion of the lemma. \(\square\)
