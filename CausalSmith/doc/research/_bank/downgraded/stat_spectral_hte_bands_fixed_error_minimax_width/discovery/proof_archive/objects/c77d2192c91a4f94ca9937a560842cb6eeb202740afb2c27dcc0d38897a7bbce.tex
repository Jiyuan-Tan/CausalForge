\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\mathscr M\) for the collection of transforms \(M(z)=E[e^{z\eta}]\) in the statement.  Centering gives \(M(0)=1\) and \(M'(0)=0\).  The definition of the sub-Gaussian norm and Young's inequality give constants \(A_0,A_1<\infty\), depending only on \(\psi_\eta\), such that
\[
 |M(z)|\le A_0\exp(A_1|z|^2),\qquad z\in\mathbb C. \tag{1}
\]
The same calculation with \(|\eta|^r e^{|z||\eta|}\), for each fixed \(r\), bounds \(M^{(r)}\) uniformly on every compact disk.  Hence every sequence in \(\mathscr M\) has, by repeated Arzel\`a--Ascoli extraction and a diagonal argument, a subsequence converging uniformly on compact subsets of \(\mathbb C\), together with every fixed derivative.  Let \(\overline{\mathscr M}\) be this compact closure in the compact-open topology.  Since every member equals one at zero, no member of the closure is identically zero.  On a common sufficiently small disk about zero the transforms are bounded away from zero, so the analytic logarithms normalized by \(\log M(0)=0\) converge there.  Consequently
\[
 \left|\left.\frac{d^k}{dz^k}\log M(z)\right|_{z=0}\right|\ge\delta,
 \qquad M\in\overline{\mathscr M}. \tag{2}
\]

We next prove that the zeros are uniformly localized.  If this were false, there would be \(M_r\in\overline{\mathscr M}\) having no zero in \(\{|z|\le r\}\).  Pass to a compact-open limit \(M_\infty\).  If \(M_\infty\) had a zero, a sufficiently small circle around that zero would be zero-free on its boundary, and uniform convergence plus the argument principle applied to the straight-line homotopy between \(M_r\) and \(M_\infty\) would force \(M_r\) to have the same positive number of zeros inside for all large \(r\), a contradiction.  Thus \(M_\infty\) is zero-free on \(\mathbb C\).

Because \(\mathbb C\) is simply connected, \(M_\infty=e^g\) for an entire function \(g\) with \(g(0)=0\).  By (1), \(\operatorname{Re}g(z)\le C(1+|z|^2)\).  For completeness, applying the maximum-modulus principle to the M\"obius transform of \(g-g(0)\) yields, for \(0<r<R\),
\[
 \max_{|z|\le r}|g(z)-g(0)|
 \le \frac{2r}{R-r}
 \left\{\max_{|z|\le R}\operatorname{Re}g(z)-\operatorname{Re}g(0)\right\}. \tag{3}
\]
Taking \(r=R/2\) in (3) and then using Cauchy's coefficient formula shows that every Taylor coefficient of \(g\) of degree larger than two is zero.  Hence \(g(z)=a_1z+a_2z^2\).  The identity \(M_\infty'(0)=0\) gives \(a_1=0\), so every logarithmic derivative of order \(k\ge3\) vanishes.  This contradicts (2).  There is therefore a finite \(R_{\mathrm{loc}}=R_{\mathrm{loc}}(k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\) such that every transform in \(\overline{\mathscr M}\) has a zero in \(|z|<R_{\mathrm{loc}}\).

Fix \(M\in\overline{\mathscr M}\).  Its zeros are isolated, \(M(0)=1\), and it has a zero in the preceding disk.  A translated dyadic Jordan polygon can therefore be chosen so that its interior contains at least one zero and its boundary \(C(M)\) contains none.  Put
\[
 b(M)=\inf_{z\in C(M)}|M(z)|>0,
 \qquad
 m(M)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C(M)}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz\in\mathbb N.
\]
If \(\widetilde M\) is uniformly within \(b(M)/2\) of \(M\) on \(C(M)\), the straight-line homotopy is zero-free on the boundary.  Its winding is constant, so \(m(\widetilde M)=m(M)\ne0\), and \(\inf_{C(M)}|\widetilde M|\ge b(M)/2\).  These neighborhoods cover the compact set \(\overline{\mathscr M}\); choose a finite subcover with contours \(\mathfrak C_0=\{C_1,\ldots,C_J\}\), and set
\[
 R_0=\max_{1\le j\le J}\sup_{z\in C_j}|z|,
 \qquad
 \rho_M=\frac12\min_{1\le j\le J}b(M_j)>0. \tag{4}
\]
The uniform bounds following (1), the finiteness of the bank, and the boundary lower bound in (4) give a finite constant
\[
 \Lambda_0\ge
 \max_{1\le j\le J}\operatorname{len}(C_j)
 \sup_{\substack{\widetilde M\in\mathscr M:\ C_j\text{ is certified for }\widetilde M\\ z\in C_j}}
 \left\{1+|\widetilde M(z)|+|\widetilde M'(z)|+left|\frac{\widetilde M'(z)}{\widetilde M(z)}\right|\right\}. \tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) give exactly the asserted radius, modulus, winding, and envelope.  The compact class and the deterministic enumeration of translated dyadic polygons use only \((k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\); neither the law itself, the external code, nor \(n\) enters the bank.  This proves uniformity and the claimed parameter dependence. \(\square\)
