\textbf{Proof.}
Because \(\mathcal I\) is a compact subinterval of \((0,1)\), for all small \(h\) one can place \(m\asymp h^{-1}\) translated bump supports disjointly inside \(\mathcal I\).  Write
\[
 \tau_j(v)=\gamma h^\beta\varphi((v-v_j)/h),
 \quad j=1,\ldots,m,
 \qquad \tau_0(v)=0. \tag{1}
\]
Scaling gives
\[
 [\tau_j]_\beta\le\gamma[\varphi]_\beta,
 \qquad \|\tau_j\|_\infty=\gamma h^\beta. \tag{2}
\]
Choose the fixed \(\gamma\) small enough for the H\"older-radius restriction and, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the range restriction.  Proposition \(\text{prop:exact-code-reduction}\) verifies every other member condition.  In particular, with \(g_0=\bar g_n=0\), the displacement and its conditional remainder are both zero, so each exact-code pair belongs to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\).

At a maximizer of the \(j\)-th bump, every other bump is zero.  Therefore disjoint support gives
\[
 \min_{j\ne\ell}\|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty
 \ge\gamma h^\beta\ge c r_n. \tag{3}
\]
Under the exact-code reduction,
\[
 Y/T=\tau_j(V)+\epsilon,
 \qquad V\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1],
 \qquad \epsilon\sim N(0,\sigma_k^2). \tag{4}
\]
The treatment coordinate has the same law under every hypothesis and is ancillary.  Direct calculation of the Gaussian likelihood ratio therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 =\frac{n}{2\sigma_k^2}\int_0^1\tau_j(v)^2\,dv
 =\frac{\gamma^2\|\varphi\|_2^2}{2\sigma_k^2}nh^{2\beta+1}. \tag{5}
\]
For \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), one has
\(nh^{2\beta+1}\asymp\log n\) and \(\log m\asymp\log n\).  Decreasing the fixed \(\gamma\) makes (5) at most \(c_0\log m\) for any prescribed sufficiently small \(c_0>0\).  This proves membership, separation, and divergence simultaneously. \(\square\)
