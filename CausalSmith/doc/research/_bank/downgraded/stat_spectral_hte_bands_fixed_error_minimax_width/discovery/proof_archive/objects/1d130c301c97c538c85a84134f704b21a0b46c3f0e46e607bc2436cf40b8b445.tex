\textbf{Proof.}
Because \(\delta_n\downarrow0\) and the fixed-separation class satisfies \(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta\), there is \(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\),
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n. \tag{1}
\]
Every member condition other than cumulant separation and every external-code condition is identical on the two sides of (1); the remaining implication is \(\delta\ge\delta_n\). Hence a band sequence honest over \(\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n\) is, by restriction, a member of \(\mathfrak H_\alpha\). Applying \(\text{thm:minimax-all-band-width}\) gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}r_n^{-1}
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n}
 W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)
 \ge
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}r_n^{-1}
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)
 \ge c. \tag{2}
\]
This lower bound is therefore uniform over every measurable band honest for the weak-separation enlargement.

For the upper side, define \(\mathcal A_n(v)=[-C_\tau,C_\tau]\) for every sample and every \(v\in\mathcal I\). The slope-range condition is retained in \(\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n\), so
\[
 P\{\tau_0(v)\in\mathcal A_n(v)\text{ for every }v\in\mathcal I\}=1,
 \qquad
 W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)=2C_\tau. \tag{3}
\]
Thus the deterministic band is uniformly honest and proves the displayed achievable constant-width side of the bracket.

If \(\delta_n\equiv\delta\), the enlarged class is the fixed-separation class. Then \(\text{thm:honest-spectral-band}\) supplies an \(O(r_n)\) band and \(\text{thm:minimax-all-band-width}\) supplies the matching \(\Omega(r_n)\) all-band lower bound, so the bracket closes at order \(r_n\). When \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), (2)--(3) establish only the stated interval between the fixed-class H\"older lower order and constant width. Neither inequality contains a nontrivial dependence on \(\delta_n\); consequently these bounds alone do not determine a shrinking-width threshold, a sharp \(\delta_n\)-dependent rate, or adaptation across weak and Gaussian regimes. \(\square\)
