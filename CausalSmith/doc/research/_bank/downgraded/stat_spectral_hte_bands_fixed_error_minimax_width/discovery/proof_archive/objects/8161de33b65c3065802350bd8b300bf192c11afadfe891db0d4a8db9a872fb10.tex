\textbf{Proof.}
Choose a nonzero compactly supported function \(\varphi\) on \([-1,1]\) with \(\|\varphi\|_\infty=1\) and finite H\"older seminorm \([\varphi]_\beta\).  Because \(\mathcal I\) is a compact subinterval of \((0,1)\), for all sufficiently small \(h\) it contains \(m\asymp h^{-1}\) centers whose radius-\(h\) neighborhoods are pairwise disjoint.  For
\[
 \tau_j(v)=\gamma h^\beta\varphi((v-v_j)/h),\qquad j=1,\ldots,m,
\]
the scaling identity gives \([\tau_j]_\beta\le\gamma[\varphi]_\beta\) and \(\|\tau_j\|_\infty=\gamma h^\beta\).  Choose \(\gamma>0\) fixed and small enough that \(\gamma[\varphi]_\beta\le L_\tau\), the range restriction holds, and the divergence constant below has the required size.  The exact-code reduction then proves membership of every law and pair in the two stated classes.

Disjoint support and \(\|\varphi\|_\infty=1\) imply, for distinct \(j,\ell\in\{0,ldots,m\}\),
\[
 \|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty\ge\gamma h^\beta. \tag{1}
\]
With \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), the right side of (1) is at least \(c r_n\).

By the exact regression reduction, \(T\) has the same ancillary distribution under every hypothesis and, conditional on \(V\), \(Y/T\sim N(\tau_j(V),\sigma_k^2)\).  Therefore the \(n\)-sample divergence from the zero curve is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 =\frac{n}{2\sigma_k^2}\int_0^1\tau_j(v)^2\,dv
 =\frac{\gamma^2\|\varphi\|_2^2}{2\sigma_k^2}nh^{2\beta+1}. \tag{2}
\]
Now \(nh^{2\beta+1}\asymp\log n\), whereas \(m\asymp h^{-1}\) implies \(\log m\sim(2\beta+1)^{-1}\log n\).  Taking \(\gamma\) still smaller if necessary makes (2) at most \(c_0\log m\) for any prescribed sufficiently small \(c_0>0\).  This proves membership, separation, and the asserted divergence bound. \(\square\)
