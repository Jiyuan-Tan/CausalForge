\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\mathscr M\) be the collection of transforms \(M(z)=E[e^{z\eta}]\) in the statement.  Centering gives \(M(0)=1\) and \(M'(0)=0\).  The sub-Gaussian bound and Young's inequality imply constants \(A_0,A_1<\infty\), depending only on \(\psi_\eta\), such that
\[
 |M(z)|\le A_0\exp(A_1|z|^2),\qquad z\in\mathbb C. \tag{1}
\]
The same calculation applied to \(|\eta|^r e^{|z||\eta|}\) bounds \(M^{(r)}\) uniformly on each compact disk, for every fixed \(r\).  Repeated Arzel\`a--Ascoli extraction and a diagonal argument therefore make the compact-open closure \(\overline{\mathscr M}\) compact, with convergence of every fixed derivative.

There is a common disk about zero on which every member of \(\overline{\mathscr M}\) is zero-free.  Indeed, the uniform second-derivative bound and \(M(0)=1,M'(0)=0\) give \(\sup_{M\in\overline{\mathscr M}}|M(z)-1|\le C|z|^2\).  On a sufficiently small disk this is at most \(1/2\).  The analytic logarithms normalized by \(\log M(0)=0\) consequently converge there with their derivatives.  Thus compact-open closure preserves the separation:
\[
 \left|\left.\frac{d^k}{dz^k}\log M(z)\right|_{z=0}\right|\ge\delta,
 \qquad M\in\overline{\mathscr M}. \tag{2}
\]

We next show that zeros are uniformly localized.  Otherwise, for every integer \(r\) one could choose \(M_r\in\overline{\mathscr M}\) having no zero in \(\{|z|\le r\}\).  Pass to a compact-open limit \(M_\infty\).  If \(M_\infty\) had a zero, choose a small circle around it containing no boundary zero.  Uniform convergence and the argument principle, applied to the straight-line homotopy, would force \(M_r\) to have a zero inside that circle for all large \(r\), a contradiction.  Hence \(M_\infty\) is zero-free on \(\mathbb C\).

Because \(\mathbb C\) is simply connected, \(M_\infty=e^g\) for an entire \(g\) with \(g(0)=0\).  Equation (1) yields \(\operatorname{Re}g(z)\le C(1+|z|^2)\).  The Borel--Carath\'eodory inequality gives, for \(0<r<R\),
\[
 \max_{|z|\le r}|g(z)-g(0)|
 \le \frac{2r}{R-r}
 \left\{\max_{|z|\le R}\operatorname{Re}g(z)-\operatorname{Re}g(0)\right\}. \tag{3}
\]
Taking \(r=R/2\), then applying Cauchy's coefficient formula and letting \(R\to\infty\), shows that every Taylor coefficient of \(g\) above degree two is zero.  Thus \(g(z)=a_1z+a_2z^2\).  Since \(M_\infty'(0)=0\), \(a_1=0\), and every logarithmic derivative of order \(k\ge3\) vanishes.  This contradicts (2).  Hence a finite \(R_{\mathrm{loc}}=R_{\mathrm{loc}}(k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\) contains a zero of every transform in the closure.

Fix \(M\in\overline{\mathscr M}\).  Its zeros are isolated, \(M(0)=1\), and at least one zero lies in the preceding disk.  Choose a counterclockwise translated-dyadic Jordan polygon \(C(M)\) whose interior contains a zero and whose boundary contains none.  Put
\[
 b(M)=\inf_{z\in C(M)}|M(z)|>0,
 \qquad
 m(M)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C(M)}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz\in\mathbb N. \tag{4}
\]
If \(\widetilde M\) is within \(b(M)/2\) of \(M\) on \(C(M)\), the straight-line homotopy is boundary-zero-free.  It follows that \(m(\widetilde M)=m(M)\ne0\) and \(\inf_{C(M)}|\widetilde M|\ge b(M)/2\).  These neighborhoods cover the compact set \(\overline{\mathscr M}\).  Select a finite subcover with contours \(\mathfrak C_0=\{C_1,\ldots,C_J\}\), and define
\[
 R_0=\max_{1\le j\le J}\sup_{z\in C_j}|z|,
 \qquad
 \rho_M=\frac12\min_{1\le j\le J}b(M_j)>0. \tag{5}
\]

For later selected-contour use, the envelope can be chosen uniformly over more than the single certifying contour.  Compact-disk derivative bounds give
\[
 A_R=\sup_{\widetilde M\in\overline{\mathscr M},\ |z|\le R_0}
 \{1+|\widetilde M(z)|+|\widetilde M'(z)|\}<\infty.
\]
Set
\[
 \Lambda_0=
 \max_{1\le j\le J}\operatorname{len}(C_j)
 \left\{A_R+\frac{5A_R}{2\rho_M}\right\}. \tag{6}
\]
Then every bank contour satisfying \(\inf_{C_j}|\widetilde M|\ge2\rho_M/5\) obeys
\[
 \operatorname{len}(C_j)\sup_{z\in C_j}
 \left\{1+|\widetilde M(z)|+|\widetilde M'(z)|+
 \left|\frac{\widetilde M'(z)}{\widetilde M(z)}\right|\right\}
 \le\Lambda_0. \tag{7}
\]
In particular, the contour certifying each \(M\in\mathscr M\) has boundary modulus at least \(\rho_M\), nonzero winding, and satisfies (7).  The compact class, the deterministic polygon enumeration, and (5)--(6) use only \((k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\).  No supplied code, code-error radius, law index, or sample size enters the construction.  This proves the lemma. \(\square\)
