\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\), \(g\in\mathcal G\), and bounded \(g'\), and define
\[
 \Phi(d)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{v\in[0,1]}
 E_P\!\left[\left|g'(X,V)-g(X,V)-d(V)\right|\mid V=v\right],
 \qquad d\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}.
\]
For \(d,d'\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\), the reverse triangle inequality and conditional Jensen inequality give, for almost every \(v\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left|E_P\!\left[|g'(X,V)-g(X,V)-d(V)|\mid V=v\right]
       -E_P\!\left[|g'(X,V)-g(X,V)-d'(V)|\mid V=v\right]\right|\\
 &\hspace{35mm}\le |d(v)-d'(v)|.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking essential suprema and then interchanging \(d,d'\) yields
\[
 |\Phi(d)-\Phi(d')|\le \|d-d'\|_\infty. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(\Phi\) is continuous for the uniform norm.

The class \(\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\) is uniformly bounded by \(2C_g\) and equicontinuous because every member satisfies
\[
 |d(u)-d(v)|\le L_D|u-v|^{\gamma_D},
 \qquad u,v\in[0,1]. \tag{2}
\]
To make compactness explicit, let \((d_r)_{r\ge1}\) be any sequence in this class. Successive Bolzano--Weierstrass extractions on the countable set of dyadic rationals, followed by diagonalization, give a subsequence that converges at every dyadic rational. Given \(\epsilon>0\), choose a finite dyadic mesh whose spacing \(s\) satisfies \(2L_Ds^{\gamma_D}<\epsilon/2\). Convergence at the finitely many mesh points then shows that this subsequence is uniformly Cauchy on \([0,1]\). Its uniform limit \(d_\infty\) is bounded by \(2C_g\), and passage to the limit in (2) shows that \(d_\infty\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\). Thus \(\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\) is sequentially compact in the uniform norm, hence compact because the uniform norm is metrizable.

Now take a minimizing sequence for \(\Phi\). Compactness supplies a uniformly convergent subsequence with limit \(d_\star\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\), and (1) gives
\[
 \Phi(d_\star)=\inf_{d\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}}\Phi(d)=d_P(g,g'). \tag{3}
\]
This proves attainment.

If \(g\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}(P,\varepsilon_0)\), then the zero function belongs to \(\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\), so the defining infimum for the shifted metric gives
\[
 d_P(g,g_0)
 \le \Phi(0)
 =d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g,g_0)
 \le\varepsilon_0. \tag{4}
\]
Hence \(g\in\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\), proving
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}(P,\varepsilon_0)\subseteq\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\). Taking the infimum of the first inequality in (4) over \(g\in\mathcal G\) also gives
\[
 a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le a_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{dir}}(P). \tag{5}
\]

Suppose next that \(a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}<\varepsilon_0\). Set
\(t=(\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}})/2>0\). By the defining property of the infimum, there is \(g\in\mathcal G\) such that
\[
 d_P(g,g_0)<a_{\mathcal G}(P)+t
 \le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}+t
 <\varepsilon_0. \tag{6}
\]
Therefore \(g\in\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\), so this set is nonempty.

Finally, assume \(g_0=0\), \(\varepsilon_0<a\le C_g\), and \(g_a(x,v)=-a\) belongs to \(\mathcal G\). The constant function \(d_a(v)=a\) satisfies \(\|d_a\|_\infty=a\le C_g\le2C_g\) and has H\"older seminorm zero, so \(d_a\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\). Moreover,
\[
 g_0(X,V)-g_a(X,V)-d_a(V)=0,
\]
and consequently
\[
 d_P(g_a,g_0)=0\le\varepsilon_0. \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(g_a\in\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\). On the other hand,
\[
 d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g_a,g_0)
 =\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{v\in[0,1]}E_P[a\mid V=v]
 =a>\varepsilon_0, \tag{8}
\]
so \(g_a\notin\Gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}(P,\varepsilon_0)\). Combining (7) and (8) proves the asserted strict-set witness. \(\square\)
