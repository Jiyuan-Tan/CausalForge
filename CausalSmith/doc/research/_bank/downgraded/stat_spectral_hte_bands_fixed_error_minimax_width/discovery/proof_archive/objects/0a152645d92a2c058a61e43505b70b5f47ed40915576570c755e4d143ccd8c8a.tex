\textbf{Proof.}
All constants below are uniform over \((P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\); they may depend on the fixed constants in the law class, the finite contour bank, the kernel, and \(\beta\).  Condition on the pilot.  On the event supplied by \(\text{lem:selector-consistency}\), the selected index \(j=\widehat j_n\) is fixed for the independent inference half and
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}|F_{v,h_n}(z)|\ge\rho_F,
 \qquad \widehat m_j=m_j\ne0. \tag{1}
\]
The identities \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\) and \(Y=q_0(X,V)+\tau_0(V)\eta+\xi\), together with the range restrictions, give
\[
 |D_n|\le2C_g,
 \qquad |Z_n|\le|\eta|+2C_g,
 \qquad |Y|\le C_q+C_\tau|\eta|+|\xi|. \tag{2}
\]
The treatment and conditional outcome sub-Gaussian assumptions therefore imply, for every fixed \(s<\infty\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 E_P\!\left[(1+|Y|+|Z_n|)^s e^{sR_0|Z_n|}\right]<\infty. \tag{3}
\]

We first establish the ratio expansion.  Put
\[
 d_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
For \(H_n=A\sqrt{\log n}\), truncate on \(|\eta|+|\xi|\le H_n\).  Bernstein's inequality on polynomial grids in \(v\) and contour arclength, followed by the Lipschitz oscillation bounds for \(K\), \(z\mapsto e^{zZ_n}\), and \(z\mapsto Ye^{zZ_n}\), gives
\[
 \max_{j\le|\mathfrak C_0|}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}
 \left\{|\widehat F_{2j,v}(z)-F_{v,h_n}(z)|
 +|\widehat G_{2j,v}(z)-G_{v,h_n}(z)|\right\}=O_P(d_n). \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, on the truncation set the envelope is
\(h_n^{-1}e^{O(H_n)}(1+H_n)=h_n^{-1}n^{o(1)}\), while the variance is \(O(h_n^{-1})\).  The grid cardinality is polynomial because \(h_n^{-1}\) is polynomial.  The conditional sub-Gaussian tail bounds in (3) show, after increasing the fixed \(A\), that the probability that any of the \(n\) observations is truncated is \(O(n^{-D})\) for any prescribed fixed \(D\).  Thus (4) holds for the untruncated transforms, and the same calculation makes every fixed-modulus transform failure probability \(O(n^{-D})\).  In particular, (1) and (4) imply the inference-denominator success test with this probability.

Write \(\Delta_F=\widehat F-F\) and \(\Delta_G=\widehat G-G\).  On the denominator event,
\[
 \frac{G+\Delta_G}{F+\Delta_F}-\frac GF
 =\frac{F\Delta_G-G\Delta_F}{F^2}+R,
 \qquad |R|\le C\{|\Delta_F\Delta_G|+|\Delta_F|^2\}. \tag{5}
\]
Contour integration of the linear term is exactly
\(n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i)\).  Its expectation is zero because the definitions of \(F\) and \(G\) make the expectation of the pointwise numerator \(YF-G\) vanish.  Since the bank has uniformly bounded contour length, (4)--(5) give
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|
 \widehat\tau_n(v)-\Theta^j_{v,h_n}
 -n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i)
 \right|
 =O_P(d_n^2)
 =o_P\!\left(\frac{(n_2h_n)^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right), \tag{6}
\]
because \(n_2h_n/(\log n)^3\to\infty\).

We next verify nondegenerate studentization.  Let \(b_{v,j}(O)\) be the real coefficient of \(Y\), and hence of \(\xi\), in the score.  It vanishes unless \(|V-v|\le h_n\).  The factorization \(F=MA\), independence of \(\eta\) from \((X,V)\), and the winding identity yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[\eta b_{v,j}(O)]
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}
 \frac{E_P[\eta h_n^{-1}K((V-v)/h_n)e^{zZ_n}]}{F_{v,h_n}(z)}\,dz\right\}\\
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz\right\}=1.
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
Independence, the density upper bound, and treatment sub-Gaussianity give
\(E_P[\eta^2\mathbf 1\{|V-v|\le h_n\}]\le Ch_n\).  Cauchy--Schwarz in (7) gives \(E_Pb_{v,j}^2\ge c/h_n\).  Conditional on \((X,V,T)\), the score equals \(b_{v,j}\xi\) plus a measurable term.  Conditional mean zero removes the cross term, and conditional variance nondegeneracy supplies the lower bound below.  Conversely, the score formula, (1)--(3), and
\(E_P[h_n^{-2}K^2((V-v)/h_n)]\le C/h_n\) supply the upper bound.  Hence, with
\[
 s_{v,j}^2=h_n\operatorname{Var}_P\{\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\},
\]
one has
\[
 0<c\le s_{v,j}^2\le C<\infty
 \quad\text{uniformly in }v,j,P,\bar g_n. \tag{8}
\]

It remains to verify, rather than assume, the corrected Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Kato interface.  Include both signs and define
\[
 f_{v,j}^{\pm}(O)=\pm
 \frac{\sqrt{h_n}\{\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)-E_P\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\}}{s_{v,j}}. \tag{9}
\]
Truncate (9) on \(|\eta|+|\xi|\le H_n\), center it, and renormalize its variance to one.  By (3) and (8), this changes its means, variances, empirical supremum, and Gaussian covariance semimetric by \(O(n^{-D})\), uniformly, for arbitrarily large fixed \(D\).  On the truncation set, (1)--(3) imply the deterministic envelope
\[
 b_n\le Ch_n^{-1/2}(1+H_n)e^{CH_n}
 =h_n^{-1/2}n^{o(1)}. \tag{10}
\]
The class is pointwise measurable because \(v\mapsto f_{v,j}^{\pm}(O)\) is continuous and rational grids are dense.  For any finitely supported probability measure \(Q\), the Lipschitz property of \(K\), differentiation of the population coefficients in the ratio score, and (1) give a Lipschitz modulus in \(v\) bounded by \(b_nn^C\).  A one-dimensional grid of mesh proportional to \(\epsilon n^{-C}\), together with the two signs and the finite bank, therefore gives fixed \(v_0<\infty\) and \(a_n\le n^C\) such that
\[
 \sup_QN\{\mathcal F_n,L^2(Q),b_n\epsilon\}
 \le(a_n/\epsilon)^{v_0},
 \qquad 0<\epsilon\le1. \tag{11}
\]
This is the \(\operatorname{VC}(b_n,a_n,v_0)\)-type covering condition in the cited theorem; no assertion that the untruncated score is bounded is used.

Take \(\sigma_n=1\) and
\[
 K_n=Av_0\{\log n\vee\log(a_nb_n)\}=O(\log n).
\]
Since \(h_n=c_h(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), (10) gives
\[
 \frac{b_n^2K_n}{n_2}
 \le\frac{n^{o(1)}K_n}{n_2h_n}\longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]
Thus the load-bearing multiplier condition \(b_n^2K_n\le n_2\sigma_n^2\) holds for every sufficiently large \(n\).

For the empirical coupling choose \(\gamma_n=n^{-c_0}\), where
\(0<c_0<\beta/(2\beta+1)\).  Because
\(b_n=n^{1/(4\beta+2)+o(1)}\), each of the three cited empirical coupling radii is \(n^{-c+o(1)}\) for some \(c>0\), and its failure probability is
\[
 O(\gamma_n+\log n/n)=o(1). \tag{13}
\]
For the multiplier coupling put
\[
 q_n=\frac{b_n^2K_n^3}{n_2},
 \qquad \delta_n^{\mathrm{mult}}=q_n^{1/8}.
\]
Then \(q_n=n^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)+o(1)}\).  Outside a sample set of probability at most \(3/n_2\), the cited multiplier theorem gives coupling radius
\[
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{mult}}
 \le (K_n/n_2)^{1/2}+q_n^{1/4}+q_n^{1/8}
 =n^{-c+o(1)} \tag{14}
\]
and conditional coupling failure probability at most
\[
 A''\{q_n^{1/4}/q_n^{1/8}+1/n_2\}
 =A''\{q_n^{1/8}+1/n_2\}=o(1). \tag{15}
\]
Thus (13), (15), and the \(3/n_2\) exceptional-sample probability verify that all coupling failure probabilities vanish uniformly.

Let \(B_{n,P,j}\) be the centered covariance-matched Gaussian process for (9).  It is separable, has pointwise variance one, and has almost surely finite supremum by (11).  A direct dyadic chaining argument gives
\[
 E\sup_{v,\pm}|B_{n,P,j}(v,\pm)|\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, take successively finer minimal nets in the covariance semimetric.  At level \(\ell\), (11) bounds the number of increments by \((Ca_nb_n2^\ell)^{v_0}\); the Gaussian moment-generating identity gives
\(E\max_{r\le N}|G_r|\le s\sqrt{2\log(2N)}\) for centered Gaussian variables of standard deviation at most \(s\).  Summing
\(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{\log(Ca_nb_n2^\ell)}\) proves (16).  The same chaining construction couples the negligible covariance-semimetric truncation error to a negligible Gaussian-supremum error.

The corrected Gaussian anti-concentration inequality and (13)--(16) now give
\[
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{emp}}\{E\sup|B_{n,P,j}|+1\}\to0,
 \qquad
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{mult}}\{E\sup|B_{n,P,j}|+1\}\to0, \tag{17}
\]
uniformly.  Thus both the empirical and conditional multiplier absolute-supremum laws have uniformly vanishing Kolmogorov distance from the same Gaussian law.  This verifies the required quantitative condition \(\epsilon_n\sqrt{\log n}\to0\), rather than inferring distributional equivalence from radius convergence alone.

We finally connect the population score calculation to the implemented plug-in score.  The transform bound (4), the ratio identity (5), (1), and (3) give
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}
 \frac{\|\sqrt{h_n}(\widehat\psi_{v,j}-\psi^j_{v,h_n})\|_{P,2}}{s_{v,j}}
 =O_P(d_n). \tag{18}
\]
The square classes generated by (11) satisfy the same polynomial covering bound with envelope \(4b_n^2\).  Bernstein's inequality on the same nets, (12), and (18) yield
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|\frac{\widehat s_n(v)}{s_{v,j}}-1\right|=O_P(d_n)=o_P(1). \tag{19}
\]
The conditional Gaussian chaining calculation used for (16) turns (18) and (19) into a multiplier-process discrepancy \(O_P(d_n\sqrt{\log n})\).  After multiplication by the anti-concentration factor in (16), this is \(O_P(d_n\log n)=o_P(1)\).  Moreover, multiplying the remainder in (6) by \(\sqrt{n_2h_n}/s_{v,j}\) gives \(o_P(1/\sqrt{\log n})\), again negligible on the anti-concentration scale.  Equations (17)--(19) prove the asserted conditional multiplier validity, ratio consistency, and bounds away from zero and infinity.

Choose the truncation and grid constants so that every pilot-transform, inference-transform, square-class, and plug-in comparison failure is \(O(n^{-D})\), where \(D>\beta/(2\beta+1)+2\).  Intersecting the pilot-measurable conditions defines \(\mathcal E_{1n}\), and intersecting the inference and comparison conditions defines \(\mathcal E_{2n}\).  Then
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 P(\mathcal E_{1n}^c\cup\mathcal E_{2n}^c)=o(r_n), \tag{20}
\]
and both actual fallback events are contained in this union.

For completeness, the expected critical-value assertion requires no additional tail theorem.  Conditional Markov inequality bounds the conditional \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile by \(\alpha^{-1}\) times the conditional expected multiplier supremum.  On the comparison event, (16), (18), and (19) make this expectation times \(\sup_v\widehat s_n(v)\) at most \(C\sqrt{\log n}\).  On its complement but within the actual denominator-success event, the denominator is bounded below by \(\rho_F/2\); (3), the deterministic floor \(\widehat s_n\ge n^{-1}\), and finiteness of the bank give polynomial moments of the product.  H\"older's inequality and the arbitrarily large \(D\) in (20) make that complementary expectation negligible.  Hence
\[
 E_P\!\left[\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\widehat s_n(v);
 \text{ both actual success events}\right]\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (1), (6), and (17)--(21) establish every assertion of the lemma. \(\square\)
