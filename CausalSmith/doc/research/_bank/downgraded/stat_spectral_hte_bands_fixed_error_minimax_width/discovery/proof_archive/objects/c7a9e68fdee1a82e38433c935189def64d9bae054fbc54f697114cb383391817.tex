\textbf{Proof.}
Fix an honest band sequence \(\mathcal A\), and use the \(M_n=m_n+1\) hypotheses of the many-bump lemma.  Let
\[
 d_n=\min_{j\ne\ell}\|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty\ge c_dr_n,
 \qquad
 D_n(\mathcal A)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\{U_n(v)-L_n(v)\}.
\]
Write \(P_j^n\) for the sample law under curve \(\tau_j\) and exact code zero, and let \(\mathcal C_{j,n}\) be the event that the entire curve \(\tau_j\) lies in the band.  Uniform honesty gives
\[
 \kappa_n:=\min_{0\le j\le m_n}P_j^n(\mathcal C_{j,n})
 \ge1-\alpha-o(1). \tag{1}
\]

Define a decoder \(\widehat J\) to be the unique index whose full curve is contained in the band, with an arbitrary value if there is no unique such index.  If \(\mathcal C_{j,n}\) occurs and \(D_n(\mathcal A)<d_n\), no second curve can be contained, because two contained curves separated by \(d_n\) at some point force the pointwise interval width to be at least \(d_n\).  Thus Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \frac1{M_n}\sum_{j=0}^{m_n}P_j^n(\widehat J=j)
 \ge \kappa_n-\frac{1}{M_nd_n}\sum_{j=0}^{m_n}E_jD_n(\mathcal A). \tag{2}
\]

For completeness, we derive the needed testing inequality.  Let \(J\) be uniform on the hypotheses and draw the data from \(P_J^n\).  If \(\overline P=M_n^{-1}\sum_jP_j^n\), the information identity and nonnegativity of divergence give
\[
 I(J;O_1^n)=\frac1{M_n}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,\overline P)
 \le\frac1{M_n}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 \le c_0\log m_n. \tag{3}
\]
If \(p_e=P(\widehat J\ne J)\), conditioning first on the error indicator gives
\[
 H(J\mid O_1^n)\le\log2+p_e\log M_n.
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log M_n\), this and (3) imply
\[
 \frac1{M_n}\sum_jP_j^n(\widehat J=j)
 \le\frac{c_0\log m_n+\log2}{\log M_n}. \tag{4}
\]
Combining (1), (2), and (4), and choosing the bump amplitude so that \(c_0<(1-\alpha)/2\), yields
\[
 \max_{0\le j\le m_n}E_jD_n(\mathcal A)
 \ge d_n\left\{1-\alpha-c_0-o(1)\right\}
 \ge c r_n. \tag{5}
\]
Every exact-code pair used in (5) belongs to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\).  Hence (5) is bounded above by the supremum risk in the theorem, and taking the lower limit proves the all-band lower bound.  The declared dependency \(\text{thm:honest-spectral-band}\) supplies the uniform \(Cr_n\) reverse inequality for the particular spectral band.  Combining the two directions gives minimax order \(r_n\).  No symmetry, fixed-width, or contour restriction entered (2)--(5). \(\square\)
