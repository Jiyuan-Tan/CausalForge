\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(c_{\mathrm{sg}}>0\) be a universal constant such that centering and the Luxemburg sub-Gaussian bound imply
\[
 M(0)=1,\qquad M'(0)=0,\qquad
 |M(z)|\le E_Pe^{|\operatorname{Re}z|\,|\eta|}
 \le \exp\{c_{\mathrm{sg}}\psi_\eta^2|z|^2\},
 \quad z\in\mathbb C. \tag{1}
\]
The same integrable envelope on every compact disk permits differentiation under the expectation, so \(M\) is entire.

We first localize a zero uniformly. If \(M\) had no zero on the disk \(\{|z|<R\}\), it would possess there an analytic logarithm \(G\), normalized by \(G(0)=0\). On that disk, (1) gives \(\operatorname{Re}G(z)\le c_{\mathrm{sg}}\psi_\eta^2R^2\). Applying the elementary Borel--Carath\'eodory estimate on the disks of radii \(R\) and \(R/2\), followed by Cauchy's coefficient estimate on radii \(R/2\) and \(R/4\), gives a constant \(A_k<\infty\), depending only on \(k\), for which
\[
 |G^{(k)}(0)|\le A_k\psi_\eta^2R^{2-k}. \tag{2}
\]
Choose
\[
 R_{\mathrm z}=1+\left(\frac{2A_k\psi_\eta^2}{\delta}\right)^{1/(k-2)}. \tag{3}
\]
Since \(G^{(k)}(0)=\kappa_k(\eta)\), (2) with \(R=R_{\mathrm z}\) would contradict \(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta\). Thus every admissible transform has at least one zero in \(\{|z|<R_{\mathrm z}\}\).

Put \(R_{\mathrm f}=R_{\mathrm z}+1\) and \(R_{\mathrm o}=R_{\mathrm z}+2\). Jensen's identity applied between these radii and (1) bounds the number \(N(M)\) of zeros in \(\{|z|<R_{\mathrm f}\}\), counted with multiplicity, by
\[
 N(M)\log(R_{\mathrm o}/R_{\mathrm f})
 \le c_{\mathrm{sg}}\psi_\eta^2R_{\mathrm o}^2. \tag{4}
\]
Let \(N_0\) be the ceiling of the right side of (4) divided by \(\log(R_{\mathrm o}/R_{\mathrm f})\). It depends only on \((k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\).

Choose a dyadic mesh \(d=2^{-s}\) small enough that the translated grid
\[
 \mathcal R=\{R_{\mathrm z}+1/4+\ell d:
 0\le \ell\le \lfloor(2d)^{-1}\rfloor\} \tag{5}
\]
contains more than \(N_0\) radii. The bank \(\mathfrak C_0\) consists of the circles \(C_r=\{|z|=r\}\) for \(r\in\mathcal R\), and \(R_0=\max\mathcal R\). A zero modulus can be within \(d/3\) of at most one grid radius. Therefore some \(C_\star=C_{r_\star}\) satisfies
\[
 \bigl||a|-r_\star\bigr|\ge d/3
 \quad\text{for every zero }a\text{ with }|a|<R_{\mathrm f}. \tag{6}
\]
The zero localized in (3) lies inside \(C_\star\), while (6) excludes boundary zeros. The argument principle consequently gives
\[
 m_\star=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint_{C_\star}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz
 \in\mathbb N,\qquad m_\star\ge1. \tag{7}
\]

It remains to obtain a law-uniform boundary modulus. List the zeros in \(|z|<R_{\mathrm f}\) as \(a_1,\ldots,a_{N(M)}\) and use the disk factorization
\[
 M(z)=Q(z)g(z),\qquad
 Q(z)=\prod_{\nu=1}^{N(M)}
 \frac{R_{\mathrm f}(z-a_\nu)}
 {R_{\mathrm f}^2-\overline{a_\nu}z}. \tag{8}
\]
The function \(g\) is zero-free on the disk. On \(|z|=R_{\mathrm f}\), \(|Q(z)|=1\), so (1) bounds \(|g|\) from above there; also \(|g(0)|=|Q(0)|^{-1}\ge1\). Harnack's inequality, applied after subtracting \(\log|g|\) from its boundary upper bound, yields a constant \(A_0<\infty\), depending only on \((k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\), such that
\[
 \inf_{|z|\le R_0}|g(z)|\ge e^{-A_0}. \tag{9}
\]
For \(z\in C_\star\), (6) gives for every factor in (8)
\[
 \left|\frac{R_{\mathrm f}(z-a_\nu)}
 {R_{\mathrm f}^2-\overline{a_\nu}z}\right|
 \ge \frac{d}{6R_{\mathrm f}}. \tag{10}
\]
Equations (8)--(10) and \(N(M)\le N_0\) imply
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_\star}|M(z)|
 \ge e^{-A_0}\left(\frac{d}{6R_{\mathrm f}}\right)^{N_0}
 =:\rho_M>0. \tag{11}
\]

Finally, (1) and Cauchy's derivative estimate on the unit enlargement of \(\{|z|\le R_0\}\) give deterministic bounds \(A_M,A'_M<\infty\) for \(\sup_{|z|\le R_0}|M(z)|\) and \(\sup_{|z|\le R_0}|M'(z)|\). Enlarge the output \(\Lambda_0\), if necessary, so that it bounds the displayed contour expression on \(C_\star\). We may, and do, take the same \(\Lambda_0\) to bound that expression on every bank circle satisfying \(\inf_C|M|\ge2\rho_M/5\), because the bank is finite and
\[
 \operatorname{len}(C)\sup_C|M'/M|
 \le 2\pi R_0 A'_M\frac{5}{2\rho_M}. \tag{12}
\]
This strengthened bookkeeping property is used only for a contour that passes the pilot modulus test. The grid, \(R_0\), \(\rho_M\), and \(\Lambda_0\) depend only on \((k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\), which proves the lemma. \(\square\)
