\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(\mathcal A\in\mathfrak H_\alpha\), and take the \(M_n=m_n+1\) hypotheses supplied by \(\text{lem:many-bump-subexperiment}\).  Write
\[
 d_n=\min_{j\ne\ell}\|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty\ge c_dr_n,
 \qquad
 D_n(\mathcal A)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\{U_n(v)-L_n(v)\}. \tag{1}
\]
Let \(P_j^n\) be the sample law under \(\tau_j\) and exact code zero, and let \(\mathcal C_{j,n}\) be the event that the entire curve \(\tau_j\) lies in the band.  Uniform asymptotic honesty and membership of every testing pair give
\[
 \kappa_n:=\min_{0\le j\le m_n}P_j^n(\mathcal C_{j,n})
 \ge1-\alpha-o(1). \tag{2}
\]

We first convert the band into a measurable multiple-hypothesis decoder.  The compactly supported H\"older bump is continuous and attains its unit sup-norm, so fix \(u_\star\) in its support with \(|\varphi(u_\star)|=1\), and put \(x_j=v_j+hu_\star\).  At the finite set \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_{m_n}\}\), every two candidate curves differ by at least \(d_n\) in one coordinate because their supports are disjoint.  Let \(\widehat J\) be the unique index whose finite vector \((\tau_j(x_1),\ldots,\tau_j(x_{m_n}))\) is contained coordinatewise in the corresponding band intervals, with an arbitrary fixed value if uniqueness fails.  This is measurable.  Full-curve containment implies finite-vector containment, and on \(\mathcal C_{j,n}\cap\{D_n(\mathcal A)<d_n\}\) no second candidate vector can be contained.  Markov's inequality consequently gives
\[
 \frac1{M_n}\sum_{j=0}^{m_n}P_j^n(\widehat J=j)
 \ge \kappa_n-
 \frac1{M_nd_n}\sum_{j=0}^{m_n}E_jD_n(\mathcal A). \tag{3}
\]

For completeness, we derive the required testing inequality.  Let \(J\) be uniform on the \(M_n\) hypotheses and, conditionally on \(J=j\), draw the observations from \(P_j^n\).  With \(\overline P=M_n^{-1}\sum_jP_j^n\), the information-centroid identity and nonnegativity of relative entropy imply
\[
 I(J;O_1^n)
 =\frac1{M_n}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,\overline P)
 \le\frac1{M_n}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 \le c_0\log m_n. \tag{4}
\]
If \(p_e=P(\widehat J\ne J)\), conditioning first on the error indicator yields
\[
 H(J\mid O_1^n)\le\log2+p_e\log M_n.
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log M_n\), equation (4) entails
\[
 \frac1{M_n}\sum_jP_j^n(\widehat J=j)
 \le\frac{c_0\log m_n+\log2}{\log M_n}. \tag{5}
\]
Combining (2), (3), and (5), and choosing the fixed bump amplitude in the many-bump construction so that \(c_0<(1-\alpha)/2\), gives
\[
 \max_{0\le j\le m_n}E_jD_n(\mathcal A)
 \ge d_n\{1-\alpha-c_0-o(1)\}
 \ge cr_n. \tag{6}
\]
Every pair in (6) belongs to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\).  Thus the left side of (6) is bounded above by
\(\sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)\).  Division by \(r_n\) and passage to the lower limit prove the all-band lower bound.

The honest-spectral-band theorem supplies a particular honest band with upper risk \(Cr_n\) on the same pair class.  No symmetry, fixed-width, or contour restriction entered (1)--(6), so the matching conclusion applies to every measurable honest band. \(\square\)
