\textbf{Proof.}
Condition on the pilot and work first on the selector event of \(\text{lem:selector-consistency}\).  Then \(j=\widehat j_n\) is fixed and
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}|F_{v,h_n}(z)|\ge\rho_F,
 \qquad \widehat m_j=m_j\ne0. \tag{1}
\]
The structural equation and range restrictions imply
\[
 |Y|\le C_q+C_\tau|\eta|+|\xi|,
 \qquad |Z_n|\le|\eta|+2C_g. \tag{2}
\]
The two conditional sub-Gaussian premises therefore imply, for each fixed \(s<\infty\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 E_P[(1+|Y|+|Z_n|)^se^{sR_0|Z_n|}]<\infty. \tag{3}
\]
The moment expansion, truncation, and polynomial-grid argument used in the selector proof now applies on the inference half to both localized transforms.  Uniformly in the pair class,
\[
 \sup_{v,z}\left\{
 |\widehat F_{2j,v}(z)-F_{v,h_n}(z)|
 +|\widehat G_{2j,v}(z)-G_{v,h_n}(z)|
 \right\}
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}}\right), \tag{4}
\]
and any fixed-modulus failure in (4) has probability \(O(n^{-D})\) for arbitrarily large fixed \(D\).  In particular, (1) and (4) imply the inference-denominator test for all sufficiently large \(n\) outside such an event.

Let \(\Delta_F=\widehat F-F\) and \(\Delta_G=\widehat G-G\).  On the denominator event, the algebraic ratio identity is
\[
 \frac{G+\Delta_G}{F+\Delta_F}-\frac GF
 =\frac{F\Delta_G-G\Delta_F}{F^2}+R,
 \qquad
 |R|\le C\{|\Delta_F\Delta_G|+|\Delta_F|^2\}. \tag{5}
\]
Contour integration of the linear member in (5) is exactly
\(n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i)\); its expectation is zero by the definition of \(F\) and \(G\).  Equations (4)--(5) make the integrated remainder
\[
 O_P\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}\right)
 =o_P\!\left((n_2h_n)^{-1/2}/\sqrt{\log n}\right), \tag{6}
\]
because \(n_2h_n/(\log n)^3\to\infty\).  This proves the uniform Bahadur expansion.

We next verify studentization from the primitive variance premise.  Let \(b_{v,j}(O)\) be the real coefficient multiplying \(Y\), and therefore \(\xi\), in \(\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\).  It vanishes unless \(|V-v|\le h_n\).  The factorization \(F=MA\), independence, and the winding identity give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[\eta b_{v,j}(O)]
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{
 \frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}
 \frac{E_P[\eta w_{v,h_n}e^{zZ_n}]}{F_{v,h_n}(z)}\,dz
 \right\}\\
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{
 \frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz
 \right\}=1. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
Treatment sub-Gaussianity, independence of \(\eta\) and \(V\), and the design-density upper bound give
\[
 E_P[\eta^2\mathbf 1\{|V-v|\le h_n\}]\le Ch_n.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz in (7) therefore implies \(E_Pb_{v,j}^2\ge c/h_n\).  Conditional on \((X,V,T)\), write the score as \(b_{v,j}\xi\) plus a measurable term.  Conditional mean zero eliminates the cross covariance, and conditional variance nondegeneracy supplies the lower bound below.  The score formula, (1), (2), and (3) supply the upper bound:
\[
 0<c\le h_n\operatorname{Var}_P(\psi^j_{v,h_n})
 \le h_nE_P[(\psi^j_{v,h_n})^2]\le C. \tag{8}
\]

Consider plus and minus the centered functions
\[
 f_{v,j}(O)=\frac{\sqrt{h_n}\{\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)-E_P\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\}}
 {\{h_n\operatorname{Var}_P(\psi^j_{v,h_n})\}^{1/2}}. \tag{9}
\]
After truncation at \(|\eta|+|\xi|\le A\sqrt{\log n}\), translations of the bounded Lipschitz kernel and the one-dimensional smooth contour parametrizations have polynomial covering numbers; this follows directly by discretizing their parameters and using their Lipschitz moduli.  Their variance is at most one by construction, and the truncated envelope is
\[
 b_n=h_n^{-1/2}n^{o(1)},
 \qquad
 \frac{b_n^2\log^7n}{n_2}
 \le\frac{n^{o(1)}\log^7n}{n_2h_n}\longrightarrow0. \tag{10}
\]
Thus \(\text{lem:cck-empirical-multiplier-coupling}\) applies conditionally on the pilot.  It couples both the empirical absolute supremum and the centered Gaussian multiplier absolute supremum to the same covariance-matched Gaussian absolute supremum with vanishing error.  Its anti-concentration conclusion, together with (6), transfers the conditional multiplier quantile to the studentized leading process, uniformly over the pair class.

Applying the same moment-grid calculation to squared scores, and using (8), gives uniform ratio consistency of \(\widehat s_n\).  The ratio identity (5) and (4) also give normalized empirical \(L^2\) distance \(o_P(1/\sqrt{\log n})\) between plug-in and population scores.  Hence the implemented multiplier process has the same uniform approximation, and \(\widehat s_n\) is bounded above and away from zero in outer probability.

Choose the moment order in (3)--(4) so large that the pilot selector failure, the inference denominator failure, and the score-comparison failure all have probability \(o(r_n)\).  Their complements define measurable \(\mathcal E_{1n}\) and \(\mathcal E_{2n}\) with the sigma-field properties in the statement and
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 P(\mathcal E_{1n}^c\cup\mathcal E_{2n}^c)=o(r_n). \tag{11}
\]
Both actual fallback events are contained in this union.

Finally, dyadic parameter nets for (9), followed by the elementary bound
\(E\max_{1\le\ell\le N}|G_\ell|\le C\sqrt{\log(2N)}\) obtained by integrating the Gaussian union bound, give conditional expected supremum at most \(C\sqrt{\log n}\).  The same exponential bound places its fixed \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile within a constant of that expectation.  Ratio consistency controls \(\sup_v\widehat s_n(v)\) on the comparison event; H\"older's inequality and the arbitrarily high moments in (3) control its complement.  Therefore
\[
 E_P\!\left[
 \widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\widehat s_n(v);
 \text{both actual success events}\right]
 \le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{12}
\]
Equations (1), (6), and (8)--(12) establish every assertion. \(\square\)
