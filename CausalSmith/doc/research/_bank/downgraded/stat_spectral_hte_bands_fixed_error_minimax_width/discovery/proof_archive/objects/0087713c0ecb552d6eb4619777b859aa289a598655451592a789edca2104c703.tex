\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a pair \(Q=(P,\bar g_n)\) and write
\[
 H(z)=E_Pe^{zZ_n},\qquad
 H_n(z)=n_1^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_1}e^{zZ_{n,i}},
\]
and
\[
 F_{v}(z)=E_P[w_{v,h_n}e^{zZ_n}],\qquad
 F_{1n,v}(z)=n_1^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_1}w_{i,v}e^{zZ_{n,i}}. \tag{1}
\]
We first establish the uniform pilot law of large numbers needed below.  Since \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), for the finite union \(\mathcal T=\bigcup_jC_j\),
\[
 U=(1+|\eta|+2C_g)\exp\{R_0(|\eta|+2C_g)\}
\]
has uniformly bounded moments of every fixed order.  Moreover,
\[
 |e^{zZ_n}-e^{z'Z_n}|\le |z-z'|U. \tag{2}
\]
Boundedness and Lipschitz continuity of \(K\) similarly give, for every fixed \(q\ge2\),
\[
 \sup_{v,z}E_P|w_{v,h_n}e^{zZ_n}|^q\le C_qh_n^{1-q}, \tag{3}
\]
and deterministic Lipschitz moduli of order \(h_n^{-2}e^{R_0|Z_n|}\) in \(v\) and \(h_n^{-1}U\) in \(z\).

Truncate on \(\max_{i\in I_1}|\eta_i|\le A\sqrt{\log n}\).  By the sub-Gaussian tail bound, \(A\) can be chosen so that the complement has probability \(O(n^{-D})\) for any prescribed \(D\).  On the truncation event, polynomial grids in \(z\) along the finitely many piecewise smooth contours and in \(v\in\mathcal I\) control the oscillations in (2)--(3).  At every grid point, the iid sum inequality
\[
 E_P|(P_{n_1}-P)f|^q
 \le C_q\left\{\left(\frac{Pf^2}{n_1}\right)^{q/2}
       +\frac{P|f|^q}{n_1^{q-1}}\right\} \tag{4}
\]
applies.  Because \(n_1h_n\) grows as a positive power of \(n\), up to logarithms, a union bound followed by arbitrarily large fixed \(q\) proves, for each \(D>0\),
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}P_Q(\mathcal E_n^c)=O(n^{-D}), \tag{5}
\]
where
\[
 \mathcal E_n=
 \left\{\max_j\sup_{z\in C_j}|H_n(z)-H(z)|\le\rho_M/8,\ 
 \max_j\sup_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}|F_{1n,v}(z)-F_v(z)|
 \le\rho_Mc_V/8\right\}. \tag{6}
\]

For the population-certified contour \(C_\star\), the direct perturbation calculation in the conditional-robustness lemma gives
\[
 \inf_{C_\star}|H|\ge3\rho_M/4,
 \qquad
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_\star}|F_v(z)|\ge3\rho_Mc_V/4. \tag{7}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n\), both empirical moduli in (7) are at least their test thresholds \(5\rho_M/8\) and \(5\rho_Mc_V/8\).  In addition, the straight-line homotopy between \(H_n\) and \(H\) is zero-free on \(C_\star\), so their windings agree and are nonzero.  Here
\[
 H(z)=M(z)B(z),\qquad B(z)=E_Pe^{zD_n},qquad
 \sup_{|z|\le R_0}|B(z)-1|\le1/4, \tag{8}
\]
so \(B\) is zero-free inside every bank contour and has winding zero.  Thus the winding of \(H\) equals that of \(M\).  The certified contour passes all three pilot tests; hence \(\widehat j_n\ne\varnothing\).

Conversely, if a contour is selected on \(\mathcal E_n\), its two empirical moduli exceed the \(5/8\) thresholds.  Equation (6) then forces its population moduli to be at least \(\rho_M/2\) and \(\rho_Mc_V/2\), respectively.  The same homotopy shows
\[
 \widehat m_{\widehat j_n}
 =\operatorname{wind}(H,C_{\widehat j_n})
 =\operatorname{wind}(M,C_{\widehat j_n})\ne0. \tag{9}
\]
Equation (5), with any fixed \(D>0\), proves the asserted uniform convergence; it also records the stronger polynomially small selector failure used later. \(\square\)
