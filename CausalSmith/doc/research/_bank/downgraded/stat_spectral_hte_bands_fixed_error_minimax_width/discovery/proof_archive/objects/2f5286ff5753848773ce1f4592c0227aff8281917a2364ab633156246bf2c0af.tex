\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\), \(g\in\mathcal G\), and bounded \(g'\), and define
\[
 \Phi(d)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{v\in[0,1]}
 E_P\!\left[|g'(X,V)-g(X,V)-d(V)|\mid V=v\right]. \tag{1}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality gives, for any \(d,d'\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\),
\[
 |\Phi(d)-\Phi(d')|\le\|d-d'\|_\infty. \tag{2}
\]
The displacement class is uniformly bounded by \(2C_g\) and satisfies
\[
 |d(u)-d(v)|\le L_D|u-v|^{\gamma_D}. \tag{3}
\]
To verify compactness without importing an extra premise, take any sequence in the class, extract a diagonal subsequence converging on the dyadic rationals, and use (3) on a finite dyadic mesh to show that this subsequence is uniformly Cauchy.  Its uniform limit remains bounded and satisfies (3), so it belongs to \(\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\).  Thus the class is compact in the uniform norm.  A minimizing sequence for \(\Phi\), compactness, and (2) supply \(d_\star\) with
\[
 \Phi(d_\star)=\inf_{d\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}}\Phi(d)=d_P(g,g'). \tag{4}
\]

The zero function belongs to the displacement class.  Hence
\[
 d_P(g,g_0)\le d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g,g_0). \tag{5}
\]
The asserted inclusion follows by applying (5) to every \(g\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}(P,\varepsilon_0)\), and taking the infimum over \(g\in\mathcal G\) gives
\[
 a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le a_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{dir}}(P). \tag{6}
\]
If \(a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}<\varepsilon_0\), choose
\(t=(\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}})/2\).  The definition of an infimum supplies \(g\in\mathcal G\) such that
\[
 d_P(g,g_0)<a_{\mathcal G}(P)+t<\varepsilon_0. \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\) is nonempty.

For the strictness witness, suppose \(g_0=0\), \(\varepsilon_0<a\le C_g\), and \(g_a(x,v)=-a\) belongs to \(\mathcal G\).  The constant displacement \(d_a(v)=a\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\), and
\[
 g_0(X,V)-g_a(X,V)-d_a(V)=0.
\]
Consequently \(d_P(g_a,g_0)=0\), whereas
\[
 d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g_a,g_0)
 =\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{v}E_P[a\mid V=v]=a>\varepsilon_0. \tag{8}
\]
This proves both membership in \(\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\) and nonmembership in the direct ball. \(\square\)
