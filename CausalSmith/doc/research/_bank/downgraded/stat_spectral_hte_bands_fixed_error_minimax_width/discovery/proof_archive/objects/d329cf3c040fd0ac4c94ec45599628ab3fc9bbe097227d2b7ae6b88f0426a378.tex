\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(\mathcal A\in\mathfrak H_\alpha\) and the \(M_n=m_n+1\) exact-code hypotheses in the many-bump lemma. Put
\[
 d_n=\min_{j\ne\ell}\|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty\ge c_dr_n,\qquad
 D_n(\mathcal A)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\{U_n(v)-L_n(v)\}. \tag{1}
\]
Let \(P_j^n\) be the sample law and \(\mathcal C_{j,n}\) the event that the whole curve \(\tau_j\) is contained in the band. Uniform honesty implies
\[
 \kappa_n:=\min_{0\le j\le m_n}P_j^n(\mathcal C_{j,n})
 \ge1-\alpha-o(1). \tag{2}
\]

For each bump choose a point at which its absolute value is one after rescaling, and collect these locations into a finite evaluation vector. Any two candidate vectors differ by at least \(d_n\) in some coordinate. Decode by selecting the unique candidate vector contained coordinatewise in the reported intervals, with a fixed default if uniqueness fails. On \(\mathcal C_{j,n}\cap\{D_n(\mathcal A)<d_n\}\), the decoder is correct. Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \frac1{M_n}\sum_{j=0}^{m_n}P_j^n(\widehat J=j)
 \ge\kappa_n-\frac1{M_nd_n}
 \sum_{j=0}^{m_n}E_jD_n(\mathcal A). \tag{3}
\]

Let \(J\) be uniform on the hypotheses and conditionally draw from \(P_J^n\). For \(\overline P=M_n^{-1}\sum_jP_j^n\), the information-centroid identity gives
\[
 I(J;O_1^n)
 =\frac1{M_n}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,\overline P)
 \le\frac1{M_n}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 \le c_0\log m_n. \tag{4}
\]
If \(p_e=P(\widehat J\ne J)\), then
\[
 H(J\mid O_1^n)\le\log2+p_e\log M_n. \tag{5}
\]
Together with \(H(J)=\log M_n\), this implies
\[
 \frac1{M_n}\sum_jP_j^n(\widehat J=j)
 \le\frac{c_0\log m_n+\log2}{\log M_n}. \tag{6}
\]
Choose the bump amplitude so that \(c_0<(1-\alpha)/2\). Combining (2), (3), and (6) yields
\[
 \max_{0\le j\le m_n}E_jD_n(\mathcal A)
 \ge d_n\{1-\alpha-c_0-o(1)\}\ge cr_n. \tag{7}
\]
The supremum over \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) dominates the maximum in (7). Dividing by \(r_n\) and taking the lower limit proves the converse. The honest-spectral-band theorem supplies a band with expected sup-width at most \(Cr_n\) over the same class, establishing the minimax order. \(\square\)
