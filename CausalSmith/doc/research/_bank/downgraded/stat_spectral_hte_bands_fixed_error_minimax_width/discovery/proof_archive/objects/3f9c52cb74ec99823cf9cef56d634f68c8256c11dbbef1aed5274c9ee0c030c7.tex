\textbf{Proof.}
Choose the fixed bump \(\varphi\) in the construction and let \([\varphi]_\beta<\infty\) denote its H\"older seminorm. Since \(\mathcal I\Subset(0,1)\), for all sufficiently small \(h\) one can place \(m\asymp h^{-1}\) disjoint radius-\(h\) supports inside \(\mathcal I\). Define
\[
 \tau_j(v)=\gamma h^\beta\varphi((v-v_j)/h),
 \quad j=1,\ldots,m,\qquad \tau_0(v)=0. \tag{1}
\]
Scaling gives \([\tau_j]_\beta\le\gamma[\varphi]_\beta\) and \(\|\tau_j\|_\infty=\gamma h^\beta\). Taking fixed \(\gamma>0\) sufficiently small, and then \(n\) sufficiently large, verifies the H\"older radius and uniform range conditions. The exact-code proposition verifies all other law and pair conditions, with \(\bar g_n=g_0=0\).

Disjoint support and \(\|\varphi\|_\infty=1\) imply for every distinct \(j,\ell\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}\),
\[
 \|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty\ge\gamma h^\beta. \tag{2}
\]
At \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), the right side is at least \(cr_n\).

Under the exact-code reduction, \(T\) is ancillary and
\[
 Y/T=\tau_j(V)+\epsilon,\qquad
 V\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1],\qquad
 \epsilon\sim N(0,\sigma_k^2). \tag{3}
\]
Thus the product-law divergence from the zero curve is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 =\frac{n}{2\sigma_k^2}\int_0^1\tau_j(v)^2\,dv
 =\frac{\gamma^2\|\varphi\|_2^2}{2\sigma_k^2}
 nh^{2\beta+1}. \tag{4}
\]
Now \(nh^{2\beta+1}\asymp\log n\), while \(m\asymp h^{-1}\) implies \(\log m\asymp\log n\). Reducing \(\gamma\), if necessary, makes (4) at most \(c_0\log m\) for any prescribed sufficiently small \(c_0>0\). \(\square\)
