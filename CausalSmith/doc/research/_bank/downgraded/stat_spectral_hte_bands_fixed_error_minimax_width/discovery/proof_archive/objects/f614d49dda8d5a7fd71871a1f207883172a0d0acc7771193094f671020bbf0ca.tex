\textbf{Proof.}
Choose a nonzero compactly supported \(\beta\)-Hölder function
\(\varphi\) on \([-1,1]\) with \(\|\varphi\|_\infty=1\).
Because \(\mathcal I\Subset(0,1)\), for all sufficiently small \(h\) it contains \(m\asymp h^{-1}\) centers with disjoint radius-\(h\) neighborhoods.  Put
\[
 \tau_j(v)=\gamma h^\beta\varphi((v-v_j)/h),
 \quad j=1,\ldots,m,\qquad \tau_0(v)=0. \tag{1}
\]
Scaling gives
\([\tau_j]_\beta\le\gamma[\varphi]_\beta\) and
\(\|\tau_j\|_\infty=\gamma h^\beta\).  Choose \(\gamma>0\) small enough for the Hölder and range restrictions and for the information bound below.  The exact-code proposition verifies all remaining primitive conditions.  In particular \(g_0=\bar g_n=0\), so \(d=0\) and remainder zero certify membership in the enlarged code ball.

Disjoint supports give, for distinct \(j,\ell\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\),
\[
 \|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty\ge\gamma h^\beta. \tag{2}
\]
At \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), this is at least \(cr_n\).

Under the reduction, \(T\) is ancillary and
\[
 Y/T=\tau_j(V)+\epsilon,\qquad
 \epsilon\sim N(0,\sigma_k^2). \tag{3}
\]
Consequently the product-law divergence from the zero curve is
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 =\frac{n}{2\sigma_k^2}\int_0^1\tau_j(v)^2\,dv
 =\frac{\gamma^2\|\varphi\|_2^2}{2\sigma_k^2}
 nh^{2\beta+1}. \tag{4}
\]
Now \(nh^{2\beta+1}\asymp\log n\) and
\(m\asymp h^{-1}\) gives \(\log m\sim(2\beta+1)^{-1}\log n\).
Decreasing the fixed \(\gamma\) makes (4) at most \(c_0\log m\) for any prescribed sufficiently small \(c_0>0\). \(\square\)
