\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(\mathcal A\in\mathfrak H_\alpha\), and take the \(M_n=m_n+1\) hypotheses supplied by \(\text{lem:many-bump-subexperiment}\).  Write
\[
 d_n=\min_{j\ne\ell}\|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty\ge c_dr_n,
 \qquad
 D_n(\mathcal A)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\{U_n(v)-L_n(v)\}. \tag{1}
\]
Let \(P_j^n\) be the sample law under \(\tau_j\) and exact code zero, and let \(\mathcal C_{j,n}\) be the event that the entire curve \(\tau_j\) lies in the band.  Since all these pairs belong to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\), uniform asymptotic honesty gives
\[
 \kappa_n:=\min_{0\le j\le m_n}P_j^n(\mathcal C_{j,n})
 \ge1-\alpha-o(1). \tag{2}
\]

For each unordered pair \(j\ne\ell\), choose a deterministic point \(t_{j\ell}\in\mathcal I\) at which \(|\tau_j-\tau_\ell|\) attains its supremum; such a point exists because the finitely many H\"older curves are continuous on the compact interval.  Define a measurable decoder \(\widehat J\) to be the unique index whose values at all finitely many points \(t_{j\ell}\) lie in the corresponding band intervals, using an arbitrary fixed index if uniqueness fails.  If \(\mathcal C_{j,n}\) occurs and \(D_n(\mathcal A)<d_n\), no second candidate curve can be contained at all these points.  Markov's inequality consequently yields
\[
 \frac1{M_n}\sum_{j=0}^{m_n}P_j^n(\widehat J=j)
 \ge \kappa_n-
 \frac1{M_nd_n}\sum_{j=0}^{m_n}E_jD_n(\mathcal A). \tag{3}
\]

We now derive the testing bound.  Let \(J\) be uniform on the \(M_n\) hypotheses and, conditionally on \(J=j\), draw the observations from \(P_j^n\).  For \(\overline P=M_n^{-1}\sum_jP_j^n\), the information-centroid identity and nonnegativity of Kullback--Leibler divergence give
\[
 I(J;O_1^n)=\frac1{M_n}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,\overline P)
 \le\frac1{M_n}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 \le c_0\log m_n. \tag{4}
\]
If \(p_e=P(\widehat J\ne J)\), conditioning first on the error indicator and then using that at most \(M_n-1\) wrong labels remain gives
\[
 H(J\mid O_1^n)\le\log2+p_e\log M_n.
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log M_n\), equation (4) implies
\[
 \frac1{M_n}\sum_jP_j^n(\widehat J=j)
 \le\frac{c_0\log m_n+\log2}{\log M_n}. \tag{5}
\]
Combining (2), (3), and (5), and choosing the fixed bump amplitude in \(\text{lem:many-bump-subexperiment}\) so that \(c_0<(1-\alpha)/2\), gives
\[
 \max_{0\le j\le m_n}E_jD_n(\mathcal A)
 \ge d_n\{1-\alpha-c_0-o(1)\}
 \ge c r_n. \tag{6}
\]
Every pair used in (6) belongs to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\), so its left-hand side is bounded above by the supremum risk in the theorem.  Taking the lower limit proves the all-band lower bound.  The theorem \(\text{thm:honest-spectral-band}\) supplies the matching \(Cr_n\) upper bound for the particular spectral band on the same pair class.  No symmetry, fixed-width, or contour restriction entered (1)--(6). \(\square\)
