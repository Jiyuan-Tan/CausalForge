\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}spectral\_\allowbreak{}hte\_\allowbreak{}bands}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} fixed\_\allowbreak{}error\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}width\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} This note extends the accepted scalar fixed-error contour substrate in \texttt{stat\_sa\_plm\_cumulant\_converse\_nongaussian\_spectral\_annihilation}: its noise-only finite bank is inherited, while the new setting is a localized Hölder treatment-slope curve with simultaneous bands. Under fixed order-\(k\) non-Gaussian treatment-noise separation, any frozen code whose conditional \(L^1\) remainder is at most \(\varepsilon_0\) after removal of an admissible Hölder modifier shift can remain persistently imperfect, and localized contour cancellation removes the arbitrary bounded baseline outcome regression without estimating it. The resulting split-sample multiplier band is uniformly simultaneously honest with expected supremum width \((\log n/n)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}\); a many-location asymmetric-Bernoulli exact-code subexperiment gives the matching lower bound over all measurable honest bands. The contribution is this modifier-localized honest-band and all-band-width extension, not the generic Hölder rate or a replacement scalar \(n^{-1}\) MSE theory.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The accepted \texttt{stat\_sa\_plm\_cumulant\_converse\_nongaussian\_spectral\_annihilation} note supplies the inherited finite contour bank and scalar fixed-error \(n^{-1}\) MSE/converse substrate, but scalar squared-error analysis does not furnish modifier localization, simultaneous honesty, or a many-location Hölder expected-supremum-width converse. Published uniform bands for heterogeneous or continuous-treatment causal functions instead rely on estimated pseudo-outcomes, outcome regressions, or balancing weights.

\paragraph{Niche.} Independent additive non-Gaussian treatment noise supplies the inherited transform winding and law-independent finite bank. A bounded Hölder modifier-only code displacement has zero winding after localization, while only its conditional direct remainder must be small; spectral cancellation then leaves Hölder variation of the causal treatment slope as the explicit localization bias. This is a new inference setting, not a claim to originate the generic Hölder rate.

\paragraph{Fill.} Using the inherited bank as substrate, the paper derives attainment and strict enlargement of the modifier-shift code certificate, a uniform localized denominator modulus, reference-localized selector consistency, analytic baseline cancellation, an explicit Hölder-bias bound, a selected-contour Bahadur and multiplier theorem, a total honest band at the canonical Hölder width, and a matching many-bump converse over every measurable honest band.

\section{Related work}

The accepted \texttt{stat\_sa\_plm\_cumulant\_converse\_nongaussian\_spectral\_annihilation} note is the closest predecessor and inherited substrate: it derives a noise-only finite contour bank and scalar fixed-error contour/MSE theory, including a matched \(n^{-1}\) scalar minimax converse. This note does not re-claim that bank or scalar rate. Its extension is to a modifier-indexed Hölder curve with a modifier-shift conditional-remainder certificate, localized denominator and bias control, pilot-selected-contour multiplier calibration, simultaneous honesty, and an all-measurable-band expected sup-width converse. The predecessor's scalar squared-error converse has no many-location alternatives or band-width functional and therefore cannot provide the \((\log n/n)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}\) Hölder sup-width lower bound here. Lee, Okui, and Whang (2017, Theorem 2 and Sections 2--4) obtain a doubly robust uniform conditional-treatment-effect band from estimated propensity and outcome regressions; their smoothing and inferential architecture informs this paper, but their pseudo-response does not address a persistently imperfect frozen treatment code. Ai, Huang, and Zhang (2026, Sections 3--5 and Theorems 1--3) establish uniform Bahadur representations, bootstrap bands, and data-driven bias control for general continuous-treatment causal functions using estimated minimum-variance balancing weights. There is no dominance claim in either direction: their model covers broad dose-response and loss-defined functionals without additive independent non-Gaussian residuals, whereas this paper studies a varying-coefficient slope under additive independent noise, fixed cumulant separation, localized contour cancellation, no outcome learner, and a fixed-radius supplied-code certificate. Fan and Zhang (2000, Theorem 2) give the classical varying-coefficient maximum-deviation benchmark. Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato (2014, Appendix A, Theorems 5.1 and 5.2, and Corollary 2.1) supply the empirical-process coupling, conditional multiplier coupling, and Gaussian anti-concentration interface after the new contour-ratio linearization. Low (1997) and Armstrong and Kolesár (2020) inform the testing-to-width and explicit-bias logic. Kennedy, Balakrishnan, Robins, and Wasserman (2024) settle Hölder heterogeneous-effect estimation rates but not honest expected supremum width under fixed external-code error. Jin, Mackey, and Syrgkanis (2025) inform the separated non-Gaussian structure-agnostic regime, but not a uniform treatment-slope band with the modifier-shift conditional-remainder certificate used here. Chen and Christensen (2018) and Babii (2020) inform the weak-separation question through inverse-problem conditioning rather than serving as dominance targets.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(B_{\mathrm{bias}}=4C_V^2\exp(6C_gR_0)\Lambda_0/(c_V\rho_F)\).
  \item \texttt{Band} --- \texttt{random band}; \texttt{total bias-\allowbreak{}aware spectral confidence band}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} On the selector and inference-denominator success events, \(\mathcal B_n(v)=[\widehat\tau_n(v)\pm\{\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\widehat s_n(v)/\sqrt{n_2h_n}+B_{\mathrm{bias}}L_\tau h_n^\beta\}]\); on either failure event it is \([-C_\tau,C_\tau]\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent and identically distributed sampling, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2014}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-treatment-noise]\label{ass:independent-treatment-noise}
\(\eta\perp(X,V)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent additive treatment noise, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-mean-restriction]\label{ass:outcome-mean-restriction}
\(E_P[\xi\mid X,V,T]=0\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (varying-coefficient partially linear conditional-mean restriction, \cite{FanZhang2000}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:modifier-density]\label{ass:modifier-density}
\(c_V\le f_V(v)\le C_V\) for every \(v\in[0,1]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (random-design density bounded above and away from zero, \cite{LeeOkuiWhang2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-slope]\label{ass:holder-slope}
\(\sup_{u\ne v}|\tau_0(u)-\tau_0(v)|/|u-v|^\beta\le L_\tau\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder ball of known exponent and radius, \cite{Low1997}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:slope-range]\label{ass:slope-range}
\(\|\tau_0\|_\infty\le C_\tau\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded varying coefficient, \cite{FanZhang2000}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-regression-range]\label{ass:treatment-regression-range}
\(\|g_0\|_\infty\le C_g\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded treatment regression, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-regression-range]\label{ass:outcome-regression-range}
\(\|q_0\|_\infty\le C_q\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded baseline outcome regression, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-subgaussian]\label{ass:treatment-subgaussian}
With the exact Luxemburg convention
\[
 \lVert W\rVert_{\psi_2}
 =\inf\{u>0:E_P[\exp(W^2/u^2)]\le2\},
\]
the treatment noise satisfies \(\lVert\eta\rVert_{\psi_2}\le\psi_{\eta}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform sub-Gaussian treatment noise under the Luxemburg convention, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-subgaussian]\label{ass:outcome-subgaussian}
\(\|\xi\|_{\psi_2\mid X,V,T}\le\psi_\xi\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform conditional sub-Gaussian outcome noise, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-variance]\label{ass:outcome-variance}
\(E_P[\xi^2\mid X,V,T]\ge\underline\sigma_\xi^2\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform conditional variance nondegeneracy, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2014}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cumulant-separation]\label{ass:cumulant-separation}
\(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed non-Gaussian cumulant separation, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:law-candidate-approximation]\label{ass:law-candidate-approximation}
\(a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This law-level condition is procedure-independent and now measures the conditional remainder after removing a bounded Hölder modifier displacement. It includes direct approximation through the zero displacement and permits exact smooth modifier shifts when the fixed candidate class contains the corresponding code.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:error-radius-ordering]\label{ass:error-radius-ordering}
\(0\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}<\varepsilon_0\le\varepsilon_\star\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is a transparent compatibility range between law-level approximability and robust spectral conditioning; it permits a fixed strictly positive code-error radius whenever the displayed primitive constants are fixed.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pair-law-membership]\label{ass:pair-law-membership}
\(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{law}}(\beta,L_\tau,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This member property keeps the primitive probability law separate from every supplied treatment code.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:admissible-external-code]\label{ass:admissible-external-code}
\(\bar g_n\in\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The essential-supremum conditional remainder requirement allows a persistent bounded Hölder modifier-only displacement and controls only the residual conditional direct error. The band theorem is conditional on receiving such a fixed external input and does not claim to learn it from the inference sample.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:asymptotic-band-honesty]\label{ass:asymptotic-band-honesty}
\(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}P\{\tau_0(v)\in\mathcal A_n(v;\bar g_n)\text{ for every }v\in\mathcal I\}\ge1-\alpha\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform asymptotic honesty for confidence bands, \cite{Low1997}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hard-subclass-compatibility]\label{ass:hard-subclass-compatibility}
\(\mathfrak S_k\ne\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This ordinary nonemptiness condition only requires the fixed tail and variance constants to admit a bounded asymmetric two-point treatment residual and a Gaussian regression noise scale. It holds on an open set of primitive constants and is stated explicitly so the converse never silently assumes an unavailable hard experiment.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:noise-only-contour-bank]\label{lem:noise-only-contour-bank}
Uniformly over every centered treatment-noise law satisfying \(\|\eta\|_{\psi_2}\le\psi_\eta\) and \(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta\), the construction \(\text{def:noise-only-bank}\) returns a finite bank \(\mathfrak C_0\), \(R_0<\infty\), \(\rho_M>0\), and \(\Lambda_0<\infty\) such that some \(C_\star\in\mathfrak C_0\) has nonzero winding \(m_\star\), \(\inf_{z\in C_\star}|M(z)|\ge\rho_M\), and \(\operatorname{len}(C_\star)\sup_{z\in C_\star}\{1+|M(z)|+|M'(z)|+|M'(z)/M(z)|\}\le\Lambda_0\). The bank and all four outputs depend only on \((k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\psi=\psi_\eta\).  Centering and the exact Luxemburg convention give
\[
 E_P\eta=0,
 \qquad E_P\exp(\eta^2/\psi^2)\le2. \tag{1}
\]
If \(|t|\psi\le1\), introduce an independent copy \(\eta'\).  Jensen's inequality, symmetry of \(\eta-\eta'\), \(\cosh x\le e^{x^2/2}\), and Lyapunov's inequality give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_Pe^{t\eta}
 &\le E_Pe^{t(\eta-\eta')}
 =E_P\cosh\{t(\eta-\eta')\}\\
 &\le E_Pe^{t^2(\eta-\eta')^2/2}
 \le \{E_Pe^{t^2\eta^2}\}^2
 \le 2^{2t^2\psi^2}
 \le e^{4\psi^2t^2}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
If \(|t|\psi>1\), the inequality \(t\eta\le \eta^2/(2\psi^2)+t^2\psi^2/2\) and (1) give the same, looser, last bound.  Therefore, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 |M(z)|\le E_Pe^{\operatorname{Re}(z)\eta}
 \le \exp(4\psi^2|z|^2). \tag{3}
\]
The right side is locally bounded.  Expanding the exponential and using (1) to dominate every compact subseries shows that expectation and differentiation may be interchanged; hence \(M\) is entire.

We first localize a zero.  Suppose, to the contrary, that \(M\) is zero-free on \(|z|\le R_{\mathrm z}\).  For \(R<R_{\mathrm z}\), let \(f=\log M\) be the analytic logarithm normalized by \(f(0)=0\).  Then \(f'(0)=E_P\eta=0\), and (3) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Re}f(z)\le4\psi^2R^2,
 \qquad |z|\le R. \tag{4}
\]
For completeness, the coefficient inequality needed below follows directly from Fourier coefficients.  If \(H(z)=\sum_{j\ge0}a_jz^j\) has nonnegative real part on \(|z|\le R\), then, for \(j\ge1\),
\[
 \frac{a_jR^j}{2}
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\operatorname{Re}H(Re^{it})e^{-ijt}\,dt,
\]
so \(|a_j|R^j\le2\operatorname{Re}H(0)\).  Apply this identity to \(H=4\psi^2R^2-f\).  Since \(H(0)=4\psi^2R^2\),
\[
 |f^{(k)}(0)|\le8k!\psi^2R^{2-k}. \tag{5}
\]
Letting \(R\uparrow R_{\mathrm z}\) and using the displayed definition of \(R_{\mathrm z}\) yields
\[
 |\kappa_k(\eta)|=|f^{(k)}(0)|
 \le \frac{\delta}{2^{k+1}}<\delta, \tag{6}
\]
contradicting cumulant separation.  Thus \(M\) has at least one zero in \(|z|\le R_{\mathrm z}\).

Put \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}=R_++1\) and \(R_{\mathrm{out}}=R_++2\).  Factoring the finitely many zeros inside a circle and integrating \(\log|M|\) around that circle gives the elementary zero-count identity
\[
 \sum_{|a|<R_{\mathrm{out}}}\log\frac{R_{\mathrm{out}}}{|a|}
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|M(R_{\mathrm{out}}e^{it})|\,dt-log|M(0)|. \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, each factor \(z-a\) contributes \(\log R_{\mathrm{out}}\) to the circular mean when \(|a|<R_{\mathrm{out}}\), while a zero-free factor has the same circular mean as its value at zero.  Formula (7) also follows at a boundary zero by a radial limit.  If \(N\) counts zeros in \(|z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), including multiplicities, then (3), (7), and \(\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 N\log(R_{\mathrm{out}}/R_{\mathrm{fac}})
 &\le4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{out}}^2,\\
 N&\le4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{out}}^3
 \le4U_\psi^2(U_R+2)^3=N_{\mathrm{cert}}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]

Let \(s=2^{-m_\circ}\).  The grid contains more than \(N_{\mathrm{cert}}\) radii.  For any zero \(a\), at most one grid radius lies within \(s/3\) of \(|a|\).  Hence (8) supplies a grid circle \(C_\star\) such that
\[
 \bigl||z|-|a|\bigr|\ge s/3
 \quad\text{for every }z\in C_\star
 \text{ and every zero }a\text{ with }|a|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}. \tag{9}
\]
Zeros outside that disk are still farther from \(C_\star\).  The circle strictly encloses the zero found in (6) and contains no boundary zero.  Local factorization of \(M\) and direct integration of \((z-a)^{-1}\) show
\[
 m_\star=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_\star}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz
 =\#\{\text{zeros inside }C_\star\}, \tag{10}
\]
with multiplicity.  Thus \(m_\star\in\mathbb N\setminus\{0\}\).

It remains to verify the numerical boundary modulus.  List the zeros \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\) in \(|z|<R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), and put
\[
 Q(z)=\prod_{\ell=1}^N
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\ell)}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline a_\ell z},
 \qquad g(z)=\frac{M(z)}{Q(z)}. \tag{11}
\]
Then \(|Q|=1\) on \(|z|=R_{\mathrm{fac}}\), \(g\) is analytic and zero-free in that disk, and \(|g(0)|\ge1\).  Let
\[
 B=4\psi^2R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2,
 \qquad u(z)=B-\log|g(z)|.
\]
The boundary bound (3) and the maximum principle give \(u\ge0\).  The Poisson representation of the positive harmonic function \(u\), together with
\[
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}-|z|}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|}
 \le \frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-|z|^2}{|R_{\mathrm{fac}}e^{it}-z|^2}
 \le\frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|}{R_{\mathrm{fac}}-|z|},
\]
gives \(u(z)\le\{(R_{\mathrm{fac}}+|z|)/(R_{\mathrm{fac}}-|z|)\}u(0)\).  Since \(u(0)\le B\), for \(|z|\le R_+\),
\[
 \log|g(z)|
 \ge-8U_RU_\psi^2(U_R+1)^2=-e_\circ,
 \qquad |g(z)|\ge e^{-e_\circ}\ge2^{-2e_\circ}. \tag{12}
\]
For \(z\in C_\star\), (9) and (11) give, factor by factor,
\[
 \left|\frac{R_{\mathrm{fac}}(z-a_\ell)}
 {R_{\mathrm{fac}}^2-\overline a_\ell z}\right|
 \ge\frac{s}{6R_{\mathrm{fac}}}
 \ge2^{-(m_\circ+d_\circ^{\mathrm{den}})}, \tag{13}
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(R_{\mathrm{fac}}\le U_R+1\) and \(6(U_R+1)\le2^{3(2U_R+1)}\).  Combining (8), (12), and (13) yields
\[
 \inf_{z\in C_\star}|M(z)|
 \ge2^{-2e_\circ-N_{\mathrm{cert}}(m_\circ+d_\circ^{\mathrm{den}})}
 =\rho_M>0. \tag{14}
\]

Finally, (3) gives \(\sup_{C_\star}|M|\le A_M\).  Applying the power-series coefficient identity on the unit circle centered at \(z\) gives \(|M'(z)|\le A_M\) whenever \(|z|\le R_0\).  Therefore (14), \(\operatorname{len}(C_\star)\le2\pi R_0\), and the definition of \(\Lambda_0\) imply
\[
 \operatorname{len}(C_\star)\sup_{z\in C_\star}
 \left\{1+|M(z)|+|M'(z)|+\left|\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\right|\right\}
 \le\Lambda_0. \tag{15}
\]
All grid sizes and constants in (8)--(15) are deterministic functions only of \((k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\).  This proves the lemma. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma produces a finite contour bank and quantitative transform moduli solely from \((k,\delta,\psi_\eta)\), so the admissible code-error radius is chosen afterward without circularity. \textit{Gap.} Separated finite-order non-Gaussian estimation and a scalar contour identity do not provide the localized band's required noise-only quantitative ordering. \textit{Consumer.} All denominator, selector, bias, and multiplier arguments use these bank outputs.

\begin{lemma}[lem:conditional-l1-contour-robustness]\label{lem:conditional-l1-contour-robustness}
For every \((P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\), let \(d\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\) attain the infimum in the modifier-shift certificate for \(\bar g_n\), put \(D_n=g_0(X,V)-\bar g_n(X,V)\), \(w_{v,h}=h^{-1}K((V-v)/h)\), and \(\mu_{v,h}=E_Pw_{v,h}\). With \(A_D=R_0e^{4C_gR_0}\), for every \(|z|\le R_0\), \(v\in\mathcal I\), and sufficiently small \(h>0\),
\[
 \left|e^{-zd(v)}E_P[w_{v,h}e^{zD_n}]-\mu_{v,h}\right|
 \le A_D\{L_Dh^{\gamma_D}+\varepsilon_0\}\mu_{v,h}
 \le\mu_{v,h}/4.
\]
Consequently \(A_{v,h}(z)=E_P[w_{v,h}e^{zD_n}]\) is zero-free on \(|z|\le R_0\), has winding zero on every bank circle, and satisfies
\[
 |A_{v,h}(z)|\ge\frac34c_Ve^{-2C_gR_0}.
\]
On the noise-certified contour \(C_\star\),
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_\star}|F_{v,h}(z)|
 \ge\frac34\rho_Mc_Ve^{-2C_gR_0}=\frac32\rho_F.
\]
These are derived consequences of the modifier-shift conditional-\(L^1\) certificate and \(\varepsilon_0\le\varepsilon_\star\); no global contaminated-transform modulus, zero-freeness, or safe contour is assumed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\text{lem:modifier-shift-attainment-and-inclusion}\), admissibility of \(\bar g_n\) supplies an attained \(d\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\) such that, with \(R_n=D_n-d(V)\),
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{u\in[0,1]}E_P(|R_n|\mid V=u)\le\varepsilon_0. \tag{1}
\]
The range restrictions imply \(|D_n|\le2C_g\), while the displacement class gives \(|d|\le2C_g\).  Therefore, for \(|z|\le R_0\),
\[
 \left|e^{z\{D_n-d(v)\}}-1\right|
 \le R_0e^{4C_gR_0}|D_n-d(v)|=A_D|D_n-d(v)|. \tag{2}
\]
If \(h<\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal I,\{0,1\})\), kernel support, normalization, nonnegativity, and the density bounds give
\[
 c_V\le\mu_{v,h}=E_Pw_{v,h}\le C_V. \tag{3}
\]
On the support of \(w_{v,h}\), the displacement condition gives \(|d(V)-d(v)|\le L_Dh^{\gamma_D}\).  Since
\(D_n-d(v)=R_n+d(V)-d(v)\), conditioning (2) on \(V\), applying (1), and integrating against the nonnegative weight yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|e^{-zd(v)}E_P[w_{v,h}e^{zD_n}]-\mu_{v,h}\right|
 &\le A_DE_P[w_{v,h}|D_n-d(v)|]\\
 &\le A_D\{L_Dh^{\gamma_D}+\varepsilon_0\}\mu_{v,h}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
The revised error-radius assumption gives \(0<\varepsilon_0\le\varepsilon_\star=(8A_D)^{-1}\).  For sufficiently small \(h\), also \(A_DL_Dh^{\gamma_D}\le1/8\).  Thus the last member of (4) is at most \(\mu_{v,h}/4\).

For each \(z\) in the closed bank disk, the straight segment from \(e^{-zd(v)}A_{v,h}(z)\) to the positive number \(\mu_{v,h}\) stays at distance at least \(3\mu_{v,h}/4\) from zero.  This gives a zero-free homotopy to a constant.  Multiplication by the zero-free entire function \(e^{zd(v)}\) creates neither zeros nor winding.  Consequently \(A_{v,h}\) is zero-free on \(|z|\le R_0\), has winding zero on every bank circle, and, by (3)--(4),
\[
 |A_{v,h}(z)|
 \ge e^{-2C_gR_0}\frac34\mu_{v,h}
 \ge\frac34c_Ve^{-2C_gR_0}. \tag{5}
\]
Finally, \(\eta\perp(X,V)\) and \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\) imply the exact factorization
\[
 F_{v,h}(z)=M(z)A_{v,h}(z). \tag{6}
\]
On the contour supplied by \(\text{lem:noise-only-contour-bank}\), (5)--(6) give
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_\star}|F_{v,h}(z)|
 \ge\frac34\rho_Mc_Ve^{-2C_gR_0}
 =\frac32\rho_F. \tag{7}
\]
Every step is localized in \(v\); no assertion about an unlocalized contaminated transform was used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The shift-factorization inequality turns fixed residual error and a Hölder modifier displacement into a uniform localized denominator modulus and a winding-zero code factor. \textit{Gap.} Neither an unconditional error criterion nor a global contaminated transform protects every modifier-localized denominator. \textit{Consumer.} Selection, contour bias, and inference all use the resulting \(\rho_F\) modulus.

\begin{lemma}[lem:selector-consistency]\label{lem:selector-consistency}
For the reference-localized pilot selector in \(\text{def:spectral-band}\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 P\left\{\widehat j_n=\varnothing\text{ or }
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_{v,h_n}(z)|<\rho_F
 \text{ or }\widehat m_{\widehat j_n}\ne m_{\widehat j_n}
 \text{ or }m_{\widehat j_n}=0\right\}\longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(m_j=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint_{C_j}M'(z)/M(z)\,dz\). The winding equality is derived from \(F_{v_{\mathrm{ref}},h_n}=MA_{v_{\mathrm{ref}},h_n}\) and the zero-free localized code factor; no global contaminated-transform claim is made.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(F_v(z)=F_{v,h_n}(z)\).  The range restrictions and treatment sub-Gaussianity imply, for every fixed integer \(s\ge1\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 E_P[(1+|Z_n|)^se^{sR_0|Z_n|}]<\infty. \tag{1}
\]
For an integer \(q\ge2\), expansion of the \(q\)-th moment of a centered iid sum, grouping index tuples in which every index occurs at least twice, gives
\[
 E_P|(P_{n_1}-P)f|^q
 \le C_q\left\{(Pf^2/n_1)^{q/2}+P|f|^q/n_1^{q-1}\right\}. \tag{2}
\]
Apply (2) to the real and imaginary parts of
\(h_n^{-1}K((V-v)/h_n)e^{zZ_n}\).  Truncate at \(|\eta|\le A\sqrt{\log n}\), discretize \(v\in\mathcal I\) and each contour arclength with polynomially fine meshes, and use the Lipschitz property of \(K\), the smooth contour parametrizations, and (1) to control the oscillations between mesh points.  Since the bank is finite and \(n_1h_n\) diverges at a polynomial rate, choosing \(q\) arbitrarily large gives, for every fixed \(D>0\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}P(\mathcal U_n^c)=O(n^{-D}), \tag{3}
\]
where
\[
 \mathcal U_n=left\{
 \max_j\sup_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}
 |\widehat F_{1j,v}(z)-F_v(z)|\le\rho_F/4
 \right\}. \tag{4}
\]
The identical argument with the extra factor \(Z_n\) controls the empirical \(z\)-derivative at \(v_{\mathrm{ref}}\).

The certified population contour satisfies
\(\inf_{v,z\in C_\star}|F_v(z)|\ge3\rho_F/2\) by conditional-\(L^1\) contour robustness.  Hence on \(\mathcal U_n\) it passes the empirical threshold \(5\rho_F/4\).  Moreover,
\[
 F_{v_{\mathrm{ref}}}(z)=M(z)A_{v_{\mathrm{ref}},h_n}(z), \tag{5}
\]
and the second factor is zero-free with winding zero throughout the bank disk.  Thus the population winding in (5) equals the nonzero treatment-noise winding.  The straight-line homotopy between the population and empirical boundary curves stays nonzero by (4), so the empirical logarithmic-derivative integral has that same nonzero integer value.  At least one contour therefore passes the selector.

Conversely, suppose \(j=\widehat j_n\) on \(\mathcal U_n\).  The empirical threshold and (4) imply
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}|F_v(z)|\ge\rho_F. \tag{6}
\]
The same nonzero homotopy, followed by (5) and the zero winding of its code factor, gives
\[
 \widehat m_j
 =\operatorname{wind}(F_{v_{\mathrm{ref}}},C_j)
 =\operatorname{wind}(M,C_j)=m_j\ne0. \tag{7}
\]
The complement probability in (3) tends to zero uniformly, and (6)--(7) are exactly the four asserted selector conclusions. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma makes the population contour certificate operational through a reference-localized pilot winding test and a uniform localized boundary test. \textit{Gap.} Generic band coupling does not control a data-selected complex contour or separate its winding from modifier displacement. \textit{Consumer.} The independent inference half conditions on the same contour, winding, and denominator modulus used by the estimator.

\begin{lemma}[lem:localized-contour-bias]\label{lem:localized-contour-bias}
For every \((P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\), the selected good contour is denominator-safe for all \(v\in\mathcal I\) and all sufficiently small \(h\). The baseline outcome term is analytic and integrates to zero, a constant treatment slope is recovered exactly despite \(D_n\), and
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}|\Theta_{v,h}-\tau_0(v)|
 \le B_{\mathrm{bias}}L_\tau h^\beta,
 \qquad
 B_{\mathrm{bias}}=\frac{4C_V^2}{c_V\rho_F}
 e^{6C_gR_0}\Lambda_0.
\]
No smoothness of \(q_0\), \(g_0\), or \(\bar g_n\) is used beyond the explicitly declared \(\gamma_D\)-H\"older modifier-only displacement witness in the external-code certificate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a pair, a selected good circle \(C\), \(v\in\mathcal I\), and a sufficiently small \(h\).  Put \(w=w_{v,h}\) and define the entire functions
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A(z)&=E_P[we^{zD_n}],\\
 A_\tau(z)&=E_P[w\tau_0(V)e^{zD_n}],\\
 Q(z)&=E_P[wq_0(X,V)e^{zD_n}].
 \end{aligned} \tag{1}
\]
Conditional-\(L^1\) contour robustness makes \(A\) zero-free in the bank disk, and independence gives \(F_{v,h}=MA\).  Since \(we^{zZ_n}\) is measurable with respect to \((X,V,T)\), the conditional mean restriction implies
\(E_P[w\xi e^{zZ_n}]=0\).  Substituting
\(Y=q_0(X,V)+\tau_0(V)\eta+\xi\) and differentiating \(M\) therefore give
\[
 G_{v,h}(z)=M(z)Q(z)+M'(z)A_\tau(z),
 \qquad
 \frac{G_{v,h}(z)}{F_{v,h}(z)}
 =\frac{Q(z)}{A(z)}+\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\frac{A_\tau(z)}{A(z)}. \tag{2}
\]
The ratio \(Q/A\) is analytic inside \(C\), so its contour integral is zero.  The selected winding
\(m(C)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint_C M'/M\,dz\) is a nonzero integer.  Hence
\[
 \Theta^C_{v,h}-\tau_0(v)
 =\operatorname{Re}\left\{
 \frac1{2\pi i m(C)}\oint_C\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}
 \left(\frac{A_\tau(z)}{A(z)}-\tau_0(v)\right)dz
 \right\}. \tag{3}
\]
This proves analytic cancellation of the arbitrary bounded baseline.  It also proves exact recovery whenever the treatment slope is constant.

Kernel support, the H\"older restriction, the density upper bound, and \(|D_n|\le2C_g\) give
\[
 |A_\tau(z)-\tau_0(v)A(z)|
 \le C_Ve^{2C_gR_0}L_\tau h^\beta. \tag{4}
\]
Robustness and the elementary upper bound on \(A\) give
\[
 |A(z)|\ge\frac34c_Ve^{-2C_gR_0},
 \qquad |A(z)|\le C_Ve^{2C_gR_0}. \tag{5}
\]
The selected population denominator is at least \(\rho_F\).  Since \(F=MA\), (5) implies
\[
 \inf_{z\in C}|M(z)|
 \ge\frac{\rho_F}{C_Ve^{2C_gR_0}}. \tag{6}
\]
By \(\text{lem:all-bank-transform-derivative-envelope}\),
\(\operatorname{len}(C)\sup_C|M'|\le\Lambda_0\).  Combining (3)--(6), using \(|m(C)|\ge1\), and enlarging the numerical factor \(4/3\) to \(4\), yields
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}|\Theta^C_{v,h}-\tau_0(v)|
 \le \frac{4C_V^2}{c_V\rho_F}e^{6C_gR_0}\Lambda_0
 L_\tau h^\beta
 =B_{\mathrm{bias}}L_\tau h^\beta. \tag{7}
\]
The only regularity of the code used above was the declared H\"older displacement witness needed for (5); no smoothness of \(q_0\), \(g_0\), or \(\bar g_n\) was invoked. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Analytic cancellation removes the bounded baseline outcome regression, constant slopes are recovered exactly, and the only remaining deterministic error is an explicit Hölder localization bias. \textit{Gap.} Classical varying-coefficient smoothing does not supply a modifier-shift-robust contour-bias envelope. \textit{Consumer.} The explicit bias allowance yields target-curve coverage without undersmoothing or an outcome learner.

\begin{lemma}[lem:uniform-bahadur-multiplier]\label{lem:uniform-bahadur-multiplier}
There exist measurable events \(\mathcal E_{1n}\) in the pilot sigma-field and \(\mathcal E_{2n}\) in the full sample sigma-field such that
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 P(\mathcal E_{1n}^c\cup\mathcal E_{2n}^c)=o(r_n).
\]
On \(\mathcal E_{1n}\), the reference-localized selector is nonempty,
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_{\widehat j_n}}|F_{v,h_n}(z)|\ge\rho_F,
 \qquad
 \widehat m_{\widehat j_n}=m_{\widehat j_n}\ne0,
\]
where the equality is to the treatment-noise winding and is derived rather than assumed. The event \(\mathcal E_{2n}\) implies inference-denominator success. On \(\mathcal E_{1n}\cap\mathcal E_{2n}\), conditionally on the pilot and uniformly over \((P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\) and \(v\in\mathcal I\),
\[
 \widehat\tau_n(v)-\Theta^{\widehat j_n}_{v,h_n}
 =n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^{\widehat j_n}_{v,h_n}(O_i)
 +o_P\!\left((n_2h_n)^{-1/2}/\sqrt{\log n}\right).
\]
In outer probability over the pilot, the studentized absolute supremum of the leading process and its conditional Gaussian multiplier analogue have uniformly vanishing Kolmogorov distance from the same covariance-matched Gaussian absolute supremum; \(\widehat s_n\) is uniformly ratio-consistent and bounded above and away from zero. The two actual fallback probabilities are \(o(r_n)\) uniformly and
\[
 E_P\!\left[\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\widehat s_n(v)\,;\text{ both actual success events}\right]\le C\sqrt{\log n}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
All constants below are uniform over \((P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\); they may depend on the fixed constants in the law class, the finite contour bank, the kernel, and \(\beta\).  Condition on the pilot.  On the event supplied by \(\text{lem:selector-consistency}\), the selected index \(j=\widehat j_n\) is fixed for the independent inference half and
\[
 \inf_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}|F_{v,h_n}(z)|\ge\rho_F,
 \qquad \widehat m_j=m_j\ne0. \tag{1}
\]
The identities \(Z_n=\eta+D_n\) and \(Y=q_0(X,V)+\tau_0(V)\eta+\xi\), together with the range restrictions, give
\[
 |D_n|\le2C_g,
 \qquad |Z_n|\le|\eta|+2C_g,
 \qquad |Y|\le C_q+C_\tau|\eta|+|\xi|. \tag{2}
\]
The treatment and conditional outcome sub-Gaussian assumptions therefore imply, for every fixed \(s<\infty\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 E_P\!\left[(1+|Y|+|Z_n|)^s e^{sR_0|Z_n|}\right]<\infty. \tag{3}
\]

We first establish the ratio expansion.  Put
\[
 d_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
For \(H_n=A\sqrt{\log n}\), truncate on \(|\eta|+|\xi|\le H_n\).  Bernstein's inequality on polynomial grids in \(v\) and contour arclength, followed by the Lipschitz oscillation bounds for \(K\), \(z\mapsto e^{zZ_n}\), and \(z\mapsto Ye^{zZ_n}\), gives
\[
 \max_{j\le|\mathfrak C_0|}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I,z\in C_j}
 \left\{|widehat F_{2j,v}(z)-F_{v,h_n}(z)|
 +|widehat G_{2j,v}(z)-G_{v,h_n}(z)|\right\}=O_P(d_n). \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, on the truncation set the envelope is
\(h_n^{-1}e^{O(H_n)}(1+H_n)=h_n^{-1}n^{o(1)}\), while the variance is \(O(h_n^{-1})\).  The grid cardinality is polynomial because \(h_n^{-1}\) is polynomial.  The conditional sub-Gaussian tail bounds in (3) show, after increasing the fixed \(A\), that the probability that any of the \(n\) observations is truncated is \(O(n^{-D})\) for any prescribed fixed \(D\).  Thus (4) holds for the untruncated transforms, and the same calculation makes every fixed-modulus transform failure probability \(O(n^{-D})\).  In particular, (1) and (4) imply the inference-denominator success test with this probability.

Write \(\Delta_F=\widehat F-F\) and \(\Delta_G=\widehat G-G\).  On the denominator event,
\[
 \frac{G+\Delta_G}{F+\Delta_F}-\frac GF
 =\frac{F\Delta_G-G\Delta_F}{F^2}+R,
 \qquad |R|\le C\{|Delta_F\Delta_G|+|\Delta_F|^2\}. \tag{5}
\]
Contour integration of the linear term is exactly
\(n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i)\).  Its expectation is zero because the definitions of \(F\) and \(G\) make the expectation of the pointwise numerator \(YF-G\) vanish.  Since the bank has uniformly bounded contour length, (4)--(5) give
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|
 \widehat\tau_n(v)-\Theta^j_{v,h_n}
 -n_2^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_2}\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O_i)
 \right|
 =O_P(d_n^2)
 =o_P\!\left(\frac{(n_2h_n)^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right), \tag{6}
\]
because \(n_2h_n/(\log n)^3\to\infty\).

We next verify nondegenerate studentization.  Let \(b_{v,j}(O)\) be the real coefficient of \(Y\), and hence of \(\xi\), in the score.  It vanishes unless \(|V-v|\le h_n\).  The factorization \(F=MA\), independence of \(\eta\) from \((X,V)\), and the winding identity yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[\eta b_{v,j}(O)]
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}
 \frac{E_P[\eta h_n^{-1}K((V-v)/h_n)e^{zZ_n}]}{F_{v,h_n}(z)}\,dz\right\}\\
 &=\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac1{2\pi i m_j}\oint_{C_j}\frac{M'(z)}{M(z)}\,dz\right\}=1. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
Independence, the density upper bound, and treatment sub-Gaussianity give
\(E_P[\eta^2\mathbf 1\{|V-v|\le h_n\}]\le Ch_n\).  Cauchy--Schwarz in (7) gives \(E_Pb_{v,j}^2\ge c/h_n\).  Conditional on \((X,V,T)\), the score equals \(b_{v,j}\xi\) plus a measurable term.  Conditional mean zero removes the cross term, and conditional variance nondegeneracy supplies the lower bound below.  Conversely, the score formula, (1)--(3), and
\(E_P[h_n^{-2}K^2((V-v)/h_n)]\le C/h_n\) supply the upper bound.  Hence, with
\[
 s_{v,j}^2=h_n\operatorname{Var}_P\{\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\},
\]
one has
\[
 0<c\le s_{v,j}^2\le C<\infty
 \quad\text{uniformly in }v,j,P,\bar g_n. \tag{8}
\]

It remains to verify, rather than assume, the corrected Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Kato interface.  Include both signs and define
\[
 f_{v,j}^{\pm}(O)=\pm
 \frac{\sqrt{h_n}\{\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)-E_P\psi^j_{v,h_n}(O)\}}{s_{v,j}}. \tag{9}
\]
Truncate (9) on \(|\eta|+|\xi|\le H_n\), center it, and renormalize its variance to one.  By (3) and (8), this changes its means, variances, empirical supremum, and Gaussian covariance semimetric by \(O(n^{-D})\), uniformly, for arbitrarily large fixed \(D\).  On the truncation set, (1)--(3) imply the deterministic envelope
\[
 b_n\le Ch_n^{-1/2}(1+H_n)e^{CH_n}
 =h_n^{-1/2}n^{o(1)}. \tag{10}
\]
The class is pointwise measurable because \(v\mapsto f_{v,j}^{\pm}(O)\) is continuous and rational grids are dense.  For any finitely supported probability measure \(Q\), the Lipschitz property of \(K\), differentiation of the population coefficients in the ratio score, and (1) give a Lipschitz modulus in \(v\) bounded by \(b_nn^C\).  A one-dimensional grid of mesh proportional to \(\epsilon n^{-C}\), together with the two signs and the finite bank, therefore gives fixed \(v_0<\infty\) and \(a_n\le n^C\) such that
\[
 \sup_QN\{\mathcal F_n,L^2(Q),b_n\epsilon\}
 \le(a_n/\epsilon)^{v_0},
 \qquad 0<\epsilon\le1. \tag{11}
\]
This is the \(\operatorname{VC}(b_n,a_n,v_0)\)-type covering condition in the cited theorem; no assertion that the untruncated score is bounded is used.

Take \(\sigma_n=1\) and
\[
 K_n=Av_0\{\log n\vee\log(a_nb_n)\}=O(\log n).
\]
Since \(h_n=c_h(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), (10) gives
\[
 \frac{b_n^2K_n}{n_2}
 \le\frac{n^{o(1)}K_n}{n_2h_n}\longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]
Thus the load-bearing multiplier condition \(b_n^2K_n\le n_2\sigma_n^2\) holds for every sufficiently large \(n\).

\(0<c_0<\beta/(2\beta+1)\).  Because
\(b_n=n^{1/(4\beta+2)+o(1)}\), each of the three cited empirical coupling radii is \(n^{-c+o(1)}\) for some \(c>0\), and its failure probability is
\[
 O(\gamma_n+\log n/n)=o(1). \tag{13}
\]
For the multiplier coupling put
\[
 q_n=\frac{b_n^2K_n^3}{n_2},
 \qquad \delta_n^{\mathrm{mult}}=q_n^{1/8}.
\]
Then \(q_n=n^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)+o(1)}\).  Outside a sample set of probability at most \(3/n_2\), the cited multiplier theorem gives coupling radius
\[
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{mult}}
 \le (K_n/n_2)^{1/2}+q_n^{1/4}+q_n^{1/8}

\]
and conditional coupling failure probability at most
\[
 A''\{q_n^{1/4}/q_n^{1/8}+1/n_2\}
 =A''\{q_n^{1/8}+1/n_2\}=o(1). \tag{15}
\]
Thus (13), (15), and the \(3/n_2\) exceptional-sample probability verify that all coupling failure probabilities vanish uniformly.

Let \(B_{n,P,j}\) be the centered covariance-matched Gaussian process for (9).  It is separable, has pointwise variance one, and has almost surely finite supremum by (11).  A direct dyadic chaining argument gives
\[
 E\sup_{v,\pm}|B_{n,P,j}(v,\pm)|\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, take successively finer minimal nets in the covariance semimetric.  At level \(\ell\), (11) bounds the number of increments by \((Ca_nb_n2^\ell)^{v_0}\); the Gaussian moment-generating identity gives
\(E\max_{r\le N}|G_r|\le s\sqrt{2\log(2N)}\) for centered Gaussian variables of standard deviation at most \(s\).  Summing
\(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{\log(Ca_nb_n2^\ell)}\) proves (16).  The same chaining construction couples the negligible covariance-semimetric truncation error to a negligible Gaussian-supremum error.

The corrected Gaussian anti-concentration inequality and (13)--(16) now give
\[
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{emp}}\{E\sup|B_{n,P,j}|+1\}\to0,
 \qquad
 \epsilon_n^{\mathrm{mult}}\{E\sup|B_{n,P,j}|+1\}\to0, \tag{17}
\]
uniformly.  Thus both the empirical and conditional multiplier absolute-supremum laws have uniformly vanishing Kolmogorov distance from the same Gaussian law.  This verifies the required quantitative condition \(\epsilon_n\sqrt{\log n}\to0\), rather than inferring distributional equivalence from radius convergence alone.

\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}
 \frac{\|\sqrt{h_n}(\widehat\psi_{v,j}-\psi^j_{v,h_n})\|_{P,2}}{s_{v,j}}
 =O_P(d_n). \tag{18}
\]
The square classes generated by (11) satisfy the same polynomial covering bound with envelope \(4b_n^2\).  Bernstein's inequality on the same nets, (12), and (18) yield
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\left|\frac{\widehat s_n(v)}{s_{v,j}}-1\right|=O_P(d_n)=o_P(1). \tag{19}
\]
The conditional Gaussian chaining calculation used for (16) turns (18) and (19) into a multiplier-process discrepancy \(O_P(d_n\sqrt{\log n})\).  After multiplication by the anti-concentration factor in (16), this is \(O_P(d_n\log n)=o_P(1)\).  Moreover, multiplying the remainder in (6) by \(\sqrt{n_2h_n}/s_{v,j}\) gives \(o_P(1/\sqrt{\log n})\), again negligible on the anti-concentration scale.  Equations (17)--(19) prove the asserted conditional multiplier validity, ratio consistency, and bounds away from zero and infinity.

Choose the truncation and grid constants so that every pilot-transform, inference-transform, square-class, and plug-in comparison failure is \(O(n^{-D})\), where \(D>\beta/(2\beta+1)+2\).  Intersecting the pilot-measurable conditions defines \(\mathcal E_{1n}\), and intersecting the inference and comparison conditions defines \(\mathcal E_{2n}\).  Then
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 P(\mathcal E_{1n}^c\cup\mathcal E_{2n}^c)=o(r_n), \tag{20}
\]
and both actual fallback events are contained in this union.

For completeness, the expected critical-value assertion requires no additional tail theorem.  E_P\!\left[\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\widehat s_n(v);
 \text{ both actual success events}\right]\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (1), (6), and (17)--(21) establish every assertion of the lemma. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-spectral-band]\label{thm:honest-spectral-band}
For \(h_n=c_h(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), every fixed \(\varepsilon_0\in(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}},\varepsilon_\star]\), and all sufficiently large \(n\), the corrected total band in \(\text{def:spectral-band}\) satisfies

\]
and
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}W_n(\mathcal B,P,\bar g_n)\le Cr_n.
\]
The procedure uses the fixed admissible external code \(\bar g_n\), never estimates \(q_0\), permits \(\varepsilon_0\) to remain strictly positive, and makes no assertion that the code is learned from the inference sample.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(\varepsilon_0\in(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}},\varepsilon_\star]\).  All probability statements below are uniform over \((P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\).  On \(\mathcal E_{1n}\cap\mathcal E_{2n}\), \(\text{lem:uniform-bahadur-multiplier}\) gives a uniform Bahadur expansion whose studentized remainder is \(o_P(1/\sqrt{\log n})\), a uniformly ratio-consistent standard error, and empirical and conditional multiplier absolute-supremum laws coupled to the same unit-variance Gaussian absolute supremum.  The empirical and conditional multiplier coupling failure probabilities vanish uniformly, and each coupling radius multiplied by the Gaussian expected supremum plus one converges to zero.  The anti-concentration conclusion in \(\text{lem:cck-empirical-multiplier-coupling}\) therefore transfers the conditional multiplier quantile to the studentized leading empirical process.  Consequently,
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 P\left\{
 |\widehat\tau_n(v)-\Theta_{v,h_n}|
 \le\frac{\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\widehat s_n(v)}{\sqrt{n_2h_n}}
 \text{ for every }v\in\mathcal I
 \right\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{1}
\]
The center, score, standard error, and multiplier process in (1) use the same pilot-selected contour and the same nonzero treatment-noise winding.  By \(\text{lem:localized-contour-bias}\),
\[
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal I}|\Theta_{v,h_n}-\tau_0(v)|
 \le B_{\mathrm{bias}}L_\tau h_n^\beta. \tag{2}
\]
The triangle inequality combines (1) and (2) to give simultaneous coverage on the two success events.  On either actual failure event, \(\text{def:spectral-band}\) returns \([-C_\tau,C_\tau]\), which contains the entire target curve by the slope-range restriction.  The two failure probabilities are \(o(r_n)=o(1)\), uniformly, so
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 P\{\tau_0(v)\in\mathcal B_n(v)\text{ for every }v\in\mathcal I\}
 \ge1-\alpha. \tag{3}
\]

On success, the supremum width is at most
\[
 \frac{2\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\widehat s_n(v)}
 {\sqrt{n_2h_n}}
 +2B_{\mathrm{bias}}L_\tau h_n^\beta. \tag{4}
\]
On failure it is exactly \(2C_\tau\).  The critical-value moment bound and the uniform \(o(r_n)\) fallback probability from \(\text{lem:uniform-bahadur-multiplier}\) therefore imply, for every sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 W_n(\mathcal B,P,\bar g_n)
 \le C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}}
 +2B_{\mathrm{bias}}L_\tau h_n^\beta+o(r_n). \tag{5}
\]
For \(h_n=c_h(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\) and \(n_2\asymp n\), direct exponent arithmetic gives
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n_2h_n}}
 \asymp
 \left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)},
 \qquad
 h_n^\beta
 \asymp
 \left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}
 =r_n. \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) prove the asserted expected-width upper bound after enlarging \(C\).

Finally, the estimator and band are the total measurable sample maps in \(\text{def:spectral-band}\).  They use the fixed admissible external treatment code and never estimate \(q_0\).  The condition \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}<\varepsilon_0\le\varepsilon_\star\) supplies a nonempty admissible-code set and the contour-robustness bound while allowing the fixed code error to remain strictly positive.  Thus the argument applies to every fixed \(\varepsilon_0\) in the stated interval.  Equations (3), (5), and (6) prove the theorem. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:exact-code-reduction]\label{prop:exact-code-reduction}
For every fixed \(k\ge3\), the construction \(\text{def:asymmetric-exact-code-subexperiment}\) belongs to the pair class when \(\tau_0\) satisfies both the displayed H\"older seminorm restriction and the displayed uniform range restriction. Its treatment noise \(\eta=a_k(B-p_k)\) is centered, bounded, sub-Gaussian, nonzero almost surely, and has nonzero order-\(k\) cumulant. Moreover \(\xi=\eta\epsilon\) satisfies the conditional tail and variance restrictions, \(T=\eta\ne0\) almost surely, and \(Y/T=\tau_0(V)+\epsilon\). Hence the observed experiment is ordinary random-design Gaussian nonparametric regression for \(\tau_0\), plus ancillary \(T\), for both odd and even \(k\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Choose \((p_k,a_k,\sigma_k)\in\mathfrak S_k\).  Since \(p_k\in(1/4,3/4)\),
\[
 \eta=a_k(B-p_k)\in\{-a_kp_k,a_k(1-p_k)\}
\]
is centered, bounded, independent of \((X,V)\), and nonzero almost surely.  Compatibility gives \(\|\eta\|_{\psi_2}\le\psi_\eta\), and direct differentiation of its log moment-generating function gives
\[
 \kappa_k(\eta)=a_k^k\left.\partial_t^k\log(1-p_k+p_ke^t)\right|_{t=0},
 \qquad |\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta. \tag{1}
\]
This works for odd and even \(k\), because the selected asymmetric Bernoulli parameter avoids the finite zero set of the displayed cumulant polynomial.

Here \(V\) is uniform, so its density is one and compatibility states \(c_V\le1\le C_V\).  The choices \(g_0=q_0=\bar g_n=0\) obey the range restrictions.  Since \(0\in\mathcal G\), both the law-level approximation error and the supplied-code error equal zero, so the candidate and pair-membership requirements hold.  The assumed H\"older and range restrictions on \(\tau_0\) supply the remaining curve conditions.

Conditional on \((X,V,T)\), \(T=\eta\) is fixed and \(\epsilon\) remains centered Gaussian.  Thus
\[
 E_P(\xi\mid X,V,T)=0,
 \quad
 \|\xi\|_{\psi_2\mid X,V,T}
 =|\eta|\sigma_k\|N(0,1)\|_{\psi_2}
 \le\psi_\xi, \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 E_P(\xi^2\mid X,V,T)=\eta^2\sigma_k^2
 \ge a_k^2\sigma_k^2\min\{p_k,1-p_k\}^2
 \ge\underline\sigma_\xi^2. \tag{3}
\]
All support, tail, separation, and nondegeneracy conditions of the primitive law class have now been checked.  Finally, \(T=\eta\ne0\) almost surely and
\[
 \frac{Y}{T}=\tau_0(V)+\epsilon. \tag{4}
\]
Thus the observed experiment is ordinary uniform-design Gaussian nonparametric regression for \(\tau_0\), together with ancillary \(T\). \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The asymmetric two-point treatment residual gives a nonzero order-\(k\) cumulant and a valid Gaussian-regression reduction for every \(k\ge3\), including odd orders. \textit{Gap.} A symmetric Rademacher residual cannot certify odd-order cumulant separation. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition embeds the standard Gaussian Hölder experiment used by the all-band converse.

\begin{lemma}[lem:many-bump-subexperiment]\label{lem:many-bump-subexperiment}
For sufficiently small fixed \(\gamma>0\), every law in \(\text{def:many-bump-family}\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{law}}\), and every pair with \(\bar g_n=0\) belongs to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\). The \(m+1\) laws have pairwise sup-norm separation at least \(c r_n\), and each active-bump law has Kullback--Leibler divergence from the zero-bump law at most \(c_0\log m\) for a sufficiently small \(c_0>0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Because \(\mathcal I\) is a compact subinterval of \((0,1)\), for all small \(h\) one can place \(m\asymp h^{-1}\) translated bump supports disjointly inside \(\mathcal I\).  Write
\[
 \tau_j(v)=\gamma h^\beta\varphi((v-v_j)/h),
 \quad j=1,\ldots,m,
 \qquad \tau_0(v)=0. \tag{1}
\]
Scaling gives
\[
 [\tau_j]_\beta\le\gamma[\varphi]_\beta,
 \qquad \|\tau_j\|_\infty=\gamma h^\beta. \tag{2}
\]
Choose the fixed \(\gamma\) small enough for the H\"older-radius restriction and, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the range restriction.  Proposition \(\text{prop:exact-code-reduction}\) verifies every other member condition.  In particular, with \(g_0=\bar g_n=0\), the displacement and its conditional remainder are both zero, so each exact-code pair belongs to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\).

At a maximizer of the \(j\)-th bump, every other bump is zero.  Therefore disjoint support gives
\[
 \min_{j\ne\ell}\|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty
 \ge\gamma h^\beta\ge c r_n. \tag{3}
\]
Under the exact-code reduction,
\[
 Y/T=\tau_j(V)+\epsilon,
 \qquad V\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1],
 \qquad \epsilon\sim N(0,\sigma_k^2). \tag{4}
\]
The treatment coordinate has the same law under every hypothesis and is ancillary.  Direct calculation of the Gaussian likelihood ratio therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 =\frac{n}{2\sigma_k^2}\int_0^1\tau_j(v)^2\,dv
 =\frac{\gamma^2\|\varphi\|_2^2}{2\sigma_k^2}nh^{2\beta+1}. \tag{5}
\]
For \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), one has
\(nh^{2\beta+1}\asymp\log n\) and \(\log m\asymp\log n\).  Decreasing the fixed \(\gamma\) makes (5) at most \(c_0\log m\) for any prescribed sufficiently small \(c_0>0\).  This proves membership, separation, and divergence simultaneously. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit disjoint-location bump family supplies same-class sup-norm separation and logarithmic Kullback--Leibler control. \textit{Gap.} A generic testing-to-length theorem does not construct the required location family inside this causal law--code experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The family is the least-favorable input to the all-measurable-band width lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[thm:minimax-all-band-width]\label{thm:minimax-all-band-width}
There exists \(c>0\), depending only on the displayed class constants and \(\alpha\), such that every \(\mathcal A\in\mathfrak H_\alpha\) satisfies \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}r_n^{-1}\sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)\ge c\). Therefore the bias-aware spectral band has minimax expected sup-width order \(r_n=(\log n/n)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}\) over the same fixed positive code-error pair class. The lower bound ranges over every measurable band, not only contour-based, symmetric, or fixed-width bands.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix an arbitrary \(\mathcal A\in\mathfrak H_\alpha\).  Let \(P_0^n,P_1^n,\ldots,P_m^n\) be the experiments supplied by \(\text{lem:many-bump-subexperiment}\), all with the exact external code \(\bar g_n=0\), and write \(\tau_0,\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_m\) for their slope curves.  There is a constant \(c_s>0\) such that
\[
 \min_{j\ne\ell}\|\tau_j-\tau_\ell\|_\infty
 \ge s_n:=c_sr_n, \qquad
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)\le c_0\log m,
 \quad j=1,\ldots,m. \tag{1}
\]
The fixed bump amplitude in that lemma may be chosen so that \(c_0<(1-\alpha)/4\).

We record the required many-hypothesis testing inequality directly.  Let \(J\) be uniform on \(\{0,ldots,m\}\), and conditionally on \(J=j\), draw the data from \(P_j^n\).  If \(\overline P=(m+1)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^mP_j^n\), the elementary relative-entropy identity
\[
 \frac1{m+1}\sum_{j=0}^m\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,P_0^n)
 =\frac1{m+1}\sum_{j=0}^m\operatorname{KL}(P_j^n,\overline P)
 +\operatorname{KL}(\overline P,P_0^n) \tag{2}
\]
shows that the mutual information \(I(J;O_1^n)\) is at most \(c_0\log m\).  For any measurable, possibly randomized classifier \(\widehat J\), put \(E=\mathbf 1\{\widehat J\ne J\}\) and \(p_e=P(E=1)\).  Conditional on \(E=0\), \(J\) is determined by \(\widehat J\); conditional on \(E=1\), at most \(m\) values remain.  Hence
\[
 H(J\mid O_1^n)
 \le H(E)+p_e\log m
 \le\log2+p_e\log m. \tag{3}
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log(m+1)\), (2)--(3) yield
\[
 \frac1{m+1}\sum_{j=0}^mP_j^n(\widehat J\ne j)
 \ge p_{*,n}:=\frac{\log(m+1)-c_0\log m-\log2}{\log m}. \tag{4}
\]
In particular, \(\liminf_np_{*,n}\ge1-c_0>\alpha\).  This proves the testing inequality from the entropy identities rather than assuming a testing conclusion.

For a realized band, let
\[
 \Delta_n=\sup_{v\in\mathcal I}\{U_n(v)-L_n(v)\},
\]
and define \(\widehat J\) to be the smallest index \(j\) for which \(\tau_j(v)\in[L_n(v),U_n(v)]\) for every \(v\in\mathcal I\), with value zero if no such index exists.  This is measurable because the candidate family is finite and its curves are continuous.  By (1), on the event that the band covers \(\tau_j\) and \(\Delta_n<s_n\), no other candidate curve can lie wholly in the band.  Therefore
\[
 P_j^n(\widehat J\ne j)
 \le P_j^n\{\tau_j\notin\mathcal A_n\}
 +P_j^n(\Delta_n\ge s_n). \tag{5}
\]

Uniform asymptotic honesty over \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\), and membership of every pair in (1), imply that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \max_{0\le j\le m}P_j^n\{\tau_j\notin\mathcal A_n\}
 \le\alpha+\varepsilon. \tag{6}
\]
Averaging (5), using (4)--(6), and applying
\(P_j^n(\Delta_n\ge s_n)\le E_{P_j^n}\Delta_n/s_n\), gives
\[
 \frac1{m+1}\sum_{j=0}^mE_{P_j^n}\Delta_n
 \ge s_n\{p_{*,n}-\alpha-\varepsilon\}. \tag{7}
\]
Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) smaller than \((1-c_0-\alpha)/2\).  The braces in (7) are then bounded below by a positive constant for all large \(n\).  Since all of these exact-code pairs lie in \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\),
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}r_n^{-1}
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)\ge c>0. \tag{8}
\]
The argument used no symmetry, fixed-width property, or contour construction for \(\mathcal A\).

Finally, \(\text{thm:honest-spectral-band}\) places \(\mathcal B\) in \(\mathfrak H_\alpha\) and gives the matching upper bound \(Cr_n\) on the identical pair class.  Combining this upper bound with (8) proves minimax expected sup-width order \(r_n\). \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem proves that the simultaneous logarithmic price is information-limited in this Hölder curve-and-band experiment and matches the spectral band's upper rate. \textit{Gap.} The accepted scalar contour predecessor's \(n^{-1}\) minimax converse concerns one scalar estimand and squared-error risk; it cannot yield a many-location Hölder sup-width lower bound for simultaneous bands. Classical regression-band results likewise do not directly establish expected supremum-width optimality over this law--external-code pair class. \textit{Consumer.} The reported width is minimax optimal even against nonspectral, asymmetric, and variable-width measurable bands; the novelty is this localized honest-band and all-band-width extension, not the generic Hölder rate itself.

\begin{lemma}[lem:cck-empirical-multiplier-coupling]\label{lem:cck-empirical-multiplier-coupling}

 K_n=Av\{\log n\vee\log(ab/\sigma)\}\ge1.

\[
 P\left\{|W_n-W^0|>
 \frac{bK_n}{(\gamma n)^{1/2}}+
 \frac{(b\sigma)^{1/2}K_n^{3/4}}{\gamma^{1/2}n^{1/4}}+
 \frac{b^{1/3}\sigma^{2/3}K_n^{2/3}}{\gamma^{1/3}n^{1/6}}
 \right\}
 \le A'\left(\gamma+\frac{\log n}{n}\right).
\]
For independent standard Gaussian multipliers, let \(\widetilde W_n(x_1^n)\) be the conditional supremum of the centered multiplier process. If, in addition,
\[
 b^2K_n\le n\sigma^2,
\]
and
\[
 \psi_n=\left(\frac{\sigma^2K_n}{n}\right)^{1/2}
 +\left(\frac{b^2\sigma^2K_n^3}{n}\right)^{1/4},
 \qquad
 \gamma_n(\delta)=\delta^{-1}
 \left(\frac{b^2\sigma^2K_n^3}{n}\right)^{1/4}+\frac1n,
\]
then for every \(\delta>0\) there is a measurable set \(S_{n,0}\) with \(P(X_1^n\in S_{n,0})\ge1-3/n\) such that, for every \(x_1^n\in S_{n,0}\), a coupling to the same law \(W^0\) satisfies
\[
 P\{|\widetilde W_n(x_1^n)-W^0|>\psi_n+\delta\}
 \le A''\gamma_n(\delta).
\]
Moreover, if \(X=(X_t)_{t\in T}\) is a centered separable Gaussian process with \(E[X_t^2]=1\) for every \(t\) and \(\sup_t|X_t|<\infty\) almost surely, then
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}P\left(\left|\sup_t|X_t|-x\right|\le\epsilon\right)
 \le4\epsilon\left\{E\sup_t|X_t|+1\right\}.
\]
Consequently, in a triangular application, distributional equivalence requires both that the empirical and conditional-multiplier coupling failure probabilities vanish uniformly and that the common coupling radius \(\epsilon_n\) satisfy \(\epsilon_n\{E\sup_t|B_n(t)|+1\}\to0\). In particular, when \(E\sup_t|B_n(t)|=O(\sqrt{\log n})\), the quantitative condition \(\epsilon_n\sqrt{\log n}\to0\) is sufficient; convergence of the radii without these probability and anti-concentration conditions is not sufficient.
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The cited interface records the exact empirical coupling, conditional multiplier coupling, envelope condition, failure probabilities, and Gaussian anti-concentration inequality used in the triangular score-process application. \textit{Gap.} A qualitative appeal to multiplier validity would omit the load-bearing envelope condition and the requirement that coupling radii vanish on the Gaussian anti-concentration scale. \textit{Consumer.} The selected-contour Bahadur lemma verifies this interface for truncated and studentized localized influence scores.

\begin{lemma}[lem:modifier-shift-attainment-and-inclusion]\label{lem:modifier-shift-attainment-and-inclusion}
For fixed \(P\), \(g\in\mathcal G\), and bounded \(g'\), the infimum over \(d\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\) in \(d_P(g,g')\) is attained. If
\[
 d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g,g')=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{v\in[0,1]}
 E_P[|g'(X,V)-g(X,V)|\mid V=v],
\]
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}(P,\varepsilon_0)
 =\{g\in\mathcal G:d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g,g_0)\le\varepsilon_0\},
 \qquad
 a_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{dir}}(P)=\inf_{g\in\mathcal G}d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g,g_0),
\]
then \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}(P,\varepsilon_0)\subseteq\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\) and \(a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le a_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{dir}}(P)\). If \(a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}<\varepsilon_0\), then \(\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\ne\varnothing\). Moreover, if \(g_0=0\), \(\varepsilon_0<a\le C_g\), and \(\mathcal G\) contains the constant code \(g_a(x,v)=-a\), then \(g_a\in\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\setminus\Gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}(P,\varepsilon_0)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\), \(g\in\mathcal G\), and bounded \(g'\), and define
\[
 \Phi(d)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{v\in[0,1]}
 E_P\!\left[|g'(X,V)-g(X,V)-d(V)|\mid V=v\right]. \tag{1}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality gives, for any \(d,d'\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\),
\[
 |\Phi(d)-\Phi(d')|\le\|d-d'\|_\infty. \tag{2}
\]
The displacement class is uniformly bounded by \(2C_g\) and satisfies
\[
 |d(u)-d(v)|\le L_D|u-v|^{\gamma_D}. \tag{3}
\]
To verify compactness without importing an extra premise, take any sequence in the class, extract a diagonal subsequence converging on the dyadic rationals, and use (3) on a finite dyadic mesh to show that this subsequence is uniformly Cauchy.  Its uniform limit remains bounded and satisfies (3), so it belongs to \(\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\).  Thus the class is compact in the uniform norm.  A minimizing sequence for \(\Phi\), compactness, and (2) supply \(d_\star\) with
\[
 \Phi(d_\star)=\inf_{d\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}}\Phi(d)=d_P(g,g'). \tag{4}
\]

The zero function belongs to the displacement class.  Hence
\[
 d_P(g,g_0)\le d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g,g_0). \tag{5}
\]
The asserted inclusion follows by applying (5) to every \(g\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{dir}}(P,\varepsilon_0)\), and taking the infimum over \(g\in\mathcal G\) gives
\[
 a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le a_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{dir}}(P). \tag{6}
\]
If \(a_{\mathcal G}(P)\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}<\varepsilon_0\), choose
\(t=(\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}})/2\).  The definition of an infimum supplies \(g\in\mathcal G\) such that
\[
 d_P(g,g_0)<a_{\mathcal G}(P)+t<\varepsilon_0. \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\) is nonempty.

For the strictness witness, suppose \(g_0=0\), \(\varepsilon_0<a\le C_g\), and \(g_a(x,v)=-a\) belongs to \(\mathcal G\).  The constant displacement \(d_a(v)=a\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_{\gamma_D,L_D}\), and
\[
 g_0(X,V)-g_a(X,V)-d_a(V)=0.
\]
Consequently \(d_P(g_a,g_0)=0\), whereas
\[
 d_P^{\mathrm{dir}}(g_a,g_0)
 =\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{v}E_P[a\mid V=v]=a>\varepsilon_0. \tag{8}
\]
This proves both membership in \(\Gamma(P,\varepsilon_0)\) and nonmembership in the direct ball. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Compactness makes the modifier-shift certificate operational, while the inclusion and constant-shift witness show that it genuinely enlarges the direct conditional-error class. \textit{Gap.} A direct conditional-\(L^1\) ball excludes persistent modifier-only displacement even though that displacement is spectrally removable. \textit{Consumer.} The robustness proof selects an attained displacement witness for every admissible external code.

\begin{theorem}[thm:weak-separation-minimax-bracket]\label{thm:weak-separation-minimax-bracket}
Form the natural triangular weak-separation enlargement \(\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n\) by retaining every member condition and every external-code restriction in \(\text{def:spectral-law-class}\) and \(\text{def:experiment-pair-class}\), except that \(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta\) is replaced at sample size \(n\) by \(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta_n\), where \(\delta_n\downarrow0\).  Let \(\widetilde{\mathfrak H}_\alpha\) be the measurable band sequences satisfying the displayed uniform asymptotic honesty condition with \(\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n\) in place of \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\).  Then there is \(c>0\) such that every \(\mathcal A\in\widetilde{\mathfrak H}_\alpha\) satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}r_n^{-1}
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n}
 W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)\ge c.
\]
The deterministic band \([-C_\tau,C_\tau]\) is honest over this enlargement and has expected sup-width \(2C_\tau\) for every \(n\).  Hence the conclusion derivable from the frozen primitive conditions in the vanishing-separation regime is the honest bracket
\[
 c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}
 \quad\text{as an all-band asymptotic lower order},
 \qquad
 2C_\tau\quad\text{as a uniform achievable upper bound}.
\]
At the fixed endpoint \(\delta_n\equiv\delta\), the two established fixed-separation theorems close the bracket at order \(r_n\).  For \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), the sharp dependence on \(\delta_n\), the threshold for shrinking width, and adaptation across the weak and Gaussian regimes remain unresolved by these bounds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Because \(\delta_n\downarrow0\) and the fixed-separation class satisfies \(|\kappa_k(\eta)|\ge\delta\), there is \(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\),
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n. \tag{1}
\]
Every member condition other than cumulant separation and every external-code condition is identical on the two sides of (1); the remaining implication is \(\delta\ge\delta_n\).  Hence a band sequence honest over \(\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n\) is, by restriction, a member of \(\mathfrak H_\alpha\).  Applying \(\text{thm:minimax-all-band-width}\) gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}r_n^{-1}
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n}
 W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)
 \ge
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}r_n^{-1}
 \sup_{(P,\bar g_n)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{spec},n}}
 W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)
 \ge c. \tag{2}
\]
This lower bound is therefore uniform over every measurable band honest for the weak-separation enlargement.

For the upper side, define \(\mathcal A_n(v)=[-C_\tau,C_\tau]\) for every sample and every \(v\in\mathcal I\).  The slope-range condition is retained in \(\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_n\), so
\[
 P\{\tau_0(v)\in\mathcal A_n(v)\text{ for every }v\in\mathcal I\}=1,
 \qquad
 W_n(\mathcal A,P,\bar g_n)=2C_\tau. \tag{3}
\]
Thus the deterministic band is uniformly honest and proves the displayed achievable constant-width side of the bracket.

If \(\delta_n\equiv\delta\), the enlarged class is the fixed-separation class.  Then \(\text{thm:honest-spectral-band}\) supplies an \(O(r_n)\) band and \(\text{thm:minimax-all-band-width}\) supplies the matching \(\Omega(r_n)\) all-band lower bound, so the bracket closes at order \(r_n\).  When \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), (2)--(3) establish only the stated interval between the fixed-class Hölder lower order and constant width.  Neither inequality contains a nontrivial dependence on \(\delta_n\); consequently these bounds alone do not determine a shrinking-width threshold, a sharp \(\delta_n\)-dependent rate, or adaptation across weak and Gaussian regimes. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem records the strongest consequence of the unchanged primitives when cumulant separation vanishes: the fixed-class Hölder lower order persists, while the deterministic range band gives a universal constant upper bound. \textit{Gap.} The current bank and coupling bounds do not yield a sharp stability modulus or shrinking-width threshold as \(\delta_n\downarrow0\). \textit{Consumer.} It separates the closed fixed-separation theorem from the unresolved weak-to-Gaussian transition without overstating a \(\delta_n\)-dependent rate.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal target is the structural treatment-slope curve \(\tau_0(v)\), not the inherited scalar contour functional or its sample analogue. On a selected denominator-safe contour, \(\Theta_{v,h}\) is a computable localized population functional: analytic contour integration cancels the arbitrary bounded baseline outcome regression, exactly recovers every constant slope, and differs from \(\tau_0(v)\) by at most \(B_{\mathrm{bias}}L_\tau h^\beta\). The sample center \(\widehat\tau_n(v)\) estimates \(\Theta_{v,h_n}\); adding the explicit bias allowance converts multiplier coverage for that smoothed functional into simultaneous coverage for the causal curve. Balancing stochastic width and Hölder bias at \(h_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\) yields the expected sup-width rate. The rate is the standard Hölder benchmark; the contribution is its honest, fixed-code, localized spectral realization and matching all-band converse.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The finite bank is inherited scalar spectral substrate, not an independent replacement derivation in this note. The shrinking-width theorem uses fixed positive cumulant separation; its constants can deteriorate through the bank radius and boundary modulus as separation weakens, and the present primitives do not identify a sharp \(\delta_n\)-dependent rate. Over the natural vanishing-separation enlargement, the proved result is only the honest bracket between the Hölder lower order \(r_n\) and the constant-width band \(2C_\tau\). The procedure also presumes an externally fixed code satisfying the modifier-shift conditional-remainder certificate; certification or learning of that code is outside the theorem. Finally, the scalar predecessor's \(n^{-1}\) MSE converse neither proves nor substitutes for the present many-location honest-band lower bound.

\section{Honest scope}

The closed fixed-separation results take \(\beta\in(0,1]\) and its Hölder radius as known, work on an interior one-dimensional modifier interval, assume the displayed density, range, tail, variance, independence, and fixed cumulant conditions, and are uniform over law--code pairs with \(0\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{app}}<\varepsilon_0\le\varepsilon_\star\).  The probability-law class remains distinct from the external-code input, and no auxiliary-sample learning guarantee is claimed.  The exact-code asymmetric Bernoulli subclass embeds ordinary uniform-design Gaussian nonparametric regression for every fixed \(k\ge3\), which makes the all-band lower bound valid over the same pair class.  Unknown-smoothness adaptation, boundary inference, multivariate modifiers, and sharp weak-separation behavior remain outside the closed theorem.
