Prediction and semiparametric information reward different features of a proxy system.  In the witness, the strong late proxy makes \(Y\) highly predictable, even after treatment is included in the predictor, but the weak early proxy makes the selection bridge unstable and drives the observational source-score variance at order \(\epsilon^{-2}\).  The design-value gap therefore compares exactly what the budgeted estimator pays for: experimental variation, observational bridge-residual variation, and source-specific unit costs.  The pilot rule is feasible because the catalogue is finite; the finite-cell corollary shows that estimating the treatment-aware predictive risk accurately does not repair its objective mismatch.
