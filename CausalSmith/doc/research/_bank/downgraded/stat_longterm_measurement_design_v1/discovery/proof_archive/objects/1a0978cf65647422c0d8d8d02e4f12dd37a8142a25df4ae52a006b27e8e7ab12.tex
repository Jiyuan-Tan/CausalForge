Write \(p_d\) and \(v_d\) as in the statement.  By the budget-profile theorem and the nondegenerate-component condition contained in \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\),
\[
 v_d
 =\left(\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}\right)^2
 \in(0,\infty)
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).
 \tag{10}
\]
The family \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty.  Therefore \(\mathcal O_P\) is finite and nonempty, because it contains every diagonal pair \((d,d)\), and every ratio in (1) is finite and strictly positive by (10).  This proves
\(1\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty\).

Suppose first that the implication on the left side of (2) holds.  For every \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), division by \(v_{d'}>0\) gives
\[
 \frac{v_d}{v_{d'}}\le C.
\]
Taking the maximum over \(\mathcal O_P\) yields \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C\).  Conversely, if \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) and \(p_d\le p_{d'}\), then \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), whence
\[
 v_d
 =\frac{v_d}{v_{d'}}v_{d'}
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)v_{d'}
 \le Cv_{d'}.
\]
This proves (2), including sharpness of (1).

Let \(d_{\mathrm p}^*\) attain the minimum of \(v_d\) over the nonempty diagnostic optimizer set, and let \(d_{\mathrm e}^*\) attain the minimum of \(v_d\) over \(\mathcal D\).  Since \(d_{\mathrm p}^*\) minimizes \(p_d\),
\(p_{d_{\mathrm p}^*}\le p_{d_{\mathrm e}^*}\), and hence
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
 =\frac{v_{d_{\mathrm p}^*}}{v_{d_{\mathrm e}^*}}
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P).
 \tag{11}
\]
The reverse lower bound \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\ge1\) follows because the denominator minimizes \(v_d\) over the larger set \(\mathcal D\).  Moreover, for a number \(C\), the selected-design inequality
\[
 \min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}v_d
 \le C\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}v_{d'}
\]
holds if and only if \(C\ge\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\).  This proves (3).  Taking suprema over \(P\in\mathcal Q\) proves both uniform-over-law equivalences.  These arguments also show why no comparison of \(T_d\) is necessary for the pointwise statement: all information relevant to its sharp constant is exhausted by the two finite scalar lists \((p_d:d\in\mathcal D)\) and \((v_d:d\in\mathcal D)\).

We next prove the quadratic-envelope assertion.  From (6)--(7), every \(d\) and every \(x\in\mathcal X_d(P)\) satisfy
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)Q_{R,d}(x)
 \le F_d(x)
 \le\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}(x).
 \tag{12}
\]
For a diagnostic-ordered pair \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), substitute the realized directions from (4) into (12).  Since \(p_d\le p_{d'}\),
\[
 v_d
 \le\Lambda_+(P)Bp_d
 \le\Lambda_+(P)Bp_{d'}
 \le\frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}v_{d'}.
 \tag{13}
\]
Taking the maximum over \(\mathcal O_P\), and then using (11), proves (8).  Conversely, (7) is exactly the assertion that one pair of finite positive constants bounds \(F_d/Q_{R,d}\) from below and above on every chosen cone, so it is necessary and sufficient for a two-sided envelope of this stipulated kind.  It is not necessary for (2), which only evaluates the finitely many realized scalar pairs.

For nonnegative numbers \(u\) and \(w\), direct completion of the square gives
\[
 \frac{u}{\alpha}+\frac{w}{1-\alpha}
 -(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)^2
 =\left(
 \sqrt{\frac{u(1-\alpha)}{\alpha}}
 -\sqrt{\frac{w\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
 \right)^2\ge0.
 \tag{14}
\]
The infimum over \(0<\alpha<1\) is attained at
\(\alpha=\sqrt u/(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)\) when \(u,w>0\), and is obtained as a boundary limit when one is zero.  Apply (14) with
\(u=c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)\) and
\(w=c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)\) to obtain (9).

At a fixed \(\alpha\), the numerator on the right side of (9) is a quadratic form.  In finite dimension, write it as \(x^{\mathsf T}A_{d,\alpha}x\) and write \(Q_{R,d}(x)=x^{\mathsf T}R_dx\), where \(R_d\) is positive definite.  With \(y=R_d^{1/2}x\),
\[
 \sup_{x\ne0}
 \frac{x^{\mathsf T}A_{d,\alpha}x}{x^{\mathsf T}R_dx}
 =\sup_{y\ne0}
 \frac{y^{\mathsf T}R_d^{-1/2}A_{d,\alpha}R_d^{-1/2}y}{y^{\mathsf T}y},
 \tag{15}
\]
which is the largest eigenvalue of
\(R_d^{-1/2}A_{d,\alpha}R_d^{-1/2}\).  But (9) and the elementary inequality
\(\sup_x\inf_\alpha a(x,\alpha)\le\inf_\alpha\sup_x a(x,\alpha)\) yield only
\[
 \sup_{x\ne0}\frac{F_d(x)}{Q_{R,d}(x)}
 \le
 \inf_{0<\alpha<1}
 \lambda_{\max}(A_{d,\alpha},R_d).
 \tag{16}
\]
Thus a largest generalized eigenvalue is a full-space upper envelope, not the sharp selection constant.  Even when equality holds in (16), (13) shows that selection also needs a positive lower envelope for the comparator design.

Finally, strict conservatism can be seen without any limiting argument.  Take two abstract designs with \(\mathcal H_d=\mathbb R^2\), \(Q_{R,d}(x)=x_1^2+x_2^2\), and realized directions \(x_d(P)=(1,0)\).  Choose the cost-weighted source forms so that (5) equals
\[
 F_1(x)=x_1^2+Mx_2^2,
 \qquad
 F_2(x)=x_1^2+x_2^2,
 \qquad M>1;
 \tag{17}
\]
for example, for each source take its cost-weighted quadratic form equal to one quarter of the displayed \(F_d\).  Then (4) gives
\(Bp_1=Bp_2=v_1=v_2=1\).  Hence both designs are diagnostic minimizers,
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)=1,
\]
whereas the largest full-space generalized eigenvalue in the first design is \(M\), and \(\Lambda_+(P)/\Lambda_-(P)=M\).  The same realized law-level scalars are compatible with every \(M>1\); only unused directions change.  Therefore neither the diagnostic ordering nor the realized design values determine a largest generalized eigenvalue, and that eigenvalue is not generally the sharp constant.  The saturation qualifications stated after (9) are indispensable.
