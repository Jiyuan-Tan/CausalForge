The center-law membership, common identification, strict optimizer assignments, cost identities, diagnostic values, variance formulas, and exact ratio are established in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\).  In particular,
\[
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 +\frac{896}{75(10\chi-3)},
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)=\frac{3872}{25}.
\]
Expanding the exact ratio there at fixed \(\chi\) gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 +O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{1}
\]

It remains to prove stability.  In the relative interior of the finite-cell model, all conditional probabilities are rational functions of the cell probabilities with denominators bounded away from zero on a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(P_\epsilon\).  For each design and treatment arm, the outcome-bridge and selection-bridge equations are finite linear systems.  At the center their matrices are nonsingular because both relevant proxy signals are nonzero.  Matrix inversion is continuous on the nonsingular set; hence, after shrinking the neighborhood, both bridge solutions, all source-score variances, both profiled values, and both diagnostic risks vary continuously.

The same shrinking preserves positive cells, treatment and source overlap, both nonzero proxy-completeness determinants, operator bijectivity, and finite strictly positive score variances.  The factorization defining \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bin}}(\chi)\) preserves consistency, experimental randomization, observational latent exchangeability, source invariance, and the sequential conditional independence.  Moreover \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\) throughout this constrained family, and the completeness argument used at the center gives \(\mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E_P(U)\) for both arms.  Thus both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau=0\) throughout the neighborhood.

At \(P_\epsilon\), both optimizer gaps are strict and \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)>0\).  Continuity and finiteness of the two-design family therefore furnish \(r_\epsilon>0\) such that every \(P\) with \(\|P-P_\epsilon\|_{\mathrm{cell}}<r_\epsilon\) remains regular, retains the same unique diagnostic and arm-normalized optimizers, and satisfies
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)>\frac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon).
\]
This proves the neighborhood assertion and the lemma.
