We prove the support-respecting statement.  Uniformity below follows because the design set, treatment set, basis dimensions, and fold count are all finite and fixed.

Fix \((d,a)\), and let \(n\) denote a relevant training-sample size.  Bounded bases, the \((4+\delta)\)-moment bound for \(Y\), source-arm positivity, and the proportional cell-size condition imply, entry by entry,
\[
 \widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a}=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat g^h_{d,a}-g^h_{d,a}=O_P(n^{-1/2}),                  \tag{8}
\]
and the same bounds hold for \(\widehat M^q_{d,a}-M^q_{d,a}\) and \(\widehat g^q_{d,a}-g^q_{d,a}\).  Each coordinate has finite variance, so this follows from Chebyshev's inequality after conditioning on the relevant source-arm cell.  A finite union bound makes (8) simultaneous over designs, arms, and folds.

If \(\|\widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a}\|_{\mathrm{op}}<c_0/2\), then, for every vector \(x\),
\[
 \|\widehat M^h_{d,a}x\|
 \ge \|M^h_{d,a}x\|
      -\|(\widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a})x\|
 \ge \frac{c_0}{2}\|x\|.
\]
Thus \(\widehat M^h_{d,a}\) is nonsingular and its inverse has operator norm at most \(2/c_0\).  The same argument applies to \(\widehat M^q_{d,a}\).  Equation (8) shows that these events hold simultaneously with probability tending to one.  On them, the exact population moment equations give
\[
 \widehat\beta_{d,a}-\beta_{d,a}
 = (\widehat M^h_{d,a})^{-1}
 \left\{(\widehat g^h_{d,a}-g^h_{d,a})
 -(\widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a})\beta_{d,a}\right\},         \tag{9}
\]
and the analogous identity holds for \(\widehat\gamma_{d,a}-\gamma_{d,a}\).  The population coefficient vectors are uniformly bounded: the population inverses have norm at most \(c_0^{-1}\), the outcome right-hand sides are bounded by the moment condition, and the selection right-hand sides are bounded by the basis envelope.  Consequently,
\[
 \max_{d,a,\ell}\left\{
 \|\widehat\beta_{d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\beta_{d,a}\|
 +\|\widehat\gamma_{d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\gamma_{d,a}\|
 \right\}=O_P(n^{-1/2}).                                     \tag{10}
\]
The stipulated zero convention is asymptotically irrelevant because the joint singularity event has probability tending to zero.

We now translate coefficient rates into the four correctly typed bridge rates.  The bounded bases give the pointwise bounds
\[
 |\widehat h_d-h_d|\le C\|\widehat\beta_d-\beta_d\|,
 \qquad
 |\widehat q_d-q_d|\le C\|\widehat\gamma_d-\gamma_d\|.       \tag{11}
\]
Thus the first bound in (11) gives the retained source-defined strong-outcome rate
\[
 \|\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{12}
\]
On observational support, conditional expectation is an \(L_2\)-contraction, and hence
\[
 \|T_d(\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d)\|_{2,O,q,d}
 \le \|\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d\|_{2,O,h,d}
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{13}
\]
The second bound in (11) directly gives the corrected strong-selection rate
\[
 \|\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d\|_{2,O,q,d}
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{14}
\]
Finally, conditional expectation preserves the pointwise envelope in (11), so every source-defined version of \(T_d^\star(\widehat q_d-q_d)\) is bounded in absolute value by \(C\|\widehat\gamma_d-\gamma_d\|\).  It follows under the artificial two-source law that
\[
 \|T_d^\star(\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d)\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{15}
\]
Equations (12)--(15) prove the proposed displays (5)--(6).  In particular, either weak--strong product is \(O_P(B^{-1})=o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Notice that (13)--(14), unlike the rejected claims, never evaluate an observational \(L_d\)-object on unrestricted experimental \(L_d\)-support.

It remains to verify the multiplier and variance conditions.  Bounded bridge bases and (10)--(11) imply, uniformly in \(d,a,\ell\),
\[
 |\Delta h_{B,\ell}|=O_P(B^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 |\Delta q_{B,\ell}|=O_P(B^{-1/2})                            \tag{16}
\]
with the stochastic orders multiplied by fixed deterministic envelopes.  The population selection bridge is bounded because its basis and coefficient are bounded, while the outcome moment assumption makes \(Y-h_d\) square-integrable.  Therefore
\[
 \|\mathbf1\{G=O\}\Delta q_{B,\ell}(Y-h_d)\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 +\|\mathbf1\{G=O\}q_d\Delta h_{B,\ell}\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}),                                               \tag{17}
\]
and
\[
 \|\mathbf1\{G=O\}\Delta q_{B,\ell}\Delta h_{B,\ell}\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 =O_P(B^{-1}).                                                 \tag{18}
\]
Thus the multiplier expression is \(O_P(B^{-1/2})=o_P(1)\).

For either source, let \(\phi_{g,d}\) be the population score and \(\widehat\phi_{g,d}\) its fold-external plug-in version.  Empirical arm probabilities are root-\(n\) consistent and bounded away from zero with probability tending to one.  On that event, the score is locally Lipschitz in the finite coefficient vectors and arm probabilities.  Equations (10)--(11), together with \(E_P(|Y|^{4+\delta}\mid G=O)<\infty\), give
\[
 \|\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d}\|_2=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\phi_{g,d}+\phi_{g,d}\|_2=O_P(1),
 \qquad
 E_P(\phi_{g,d}^4\mid G=g)<\infty.                            \tag{19}
\]
Conditional on the training data for an evaluation fold,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathbb P_n\widehat\phi_{g,d}^2-P\phi_{g,d}^2|
 \le{}&| (\mathbb P_n-P)\phi_{g,d}^2|\\
 &+\mathbb P_n\!\left[
 |\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d}|
 |\widehat\phi_{g,d}+\phi_{g,d}|
 \right].
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{20}
\]
The first term is \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\) by Chebyshev's inequality applied to \(\phi_{g,d}^2\).  Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, (19), and the law of large numbers make the second term \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Fixed-fold averaging and a finite union bound preserve the rate.  Taking \(n\asymp m\) in the full-catalogue pilot and \(n\asymp B\) in the main study yields
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}=O_P(m^{-1/2})              \tag{21}
\]
and
\[
 \max_{g\in\{E,O\}}
 |\widetilde V_{g,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{g,\widehat d_m}|
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{22}
\]

Equations (12)--(18) and (21)--(22) verify, on this fixed-dimensional subclass, the corrected learner-rate conditions and every product, multiplier, pilot-variance, and main-study-variance condition named in Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) and \(\mathrm{thm{:}postselection\text{-}wald}\), with \(r_{V,m}=m^{-1/2}\), \(r_{\widetilde V,B}=B^{-1/2}\), and all four bridge rates of order \(B^{-1/2}\).  Applying those theorems gives the asserted oracle regret, selected-design normal limit, and Wald coverage.  The proof uses the additional fixed-dimensional conditions stated here and therefore makes no claim over all of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).
