Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), \(d\in\mathcal D\), and \(0<\alpha<1\), and put
\[
 x_E=\frac{\alpha B}{c_{E,d}},
 \qquad
 x_O=\frac{(1-\alpha)B}{c_{O,d}}.
\]
The asserted lower bound on \(B\) makes \(x_E,x_O\ge2\).  For every \(x\ge2\),
\[
 0\le \frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x
 =\frac{x-\lfloor x\rfloor}{x\lfloor x\rfloor}
 \le \frac2{x^2},                                           \tag{1}
\]
because \(0\le x-\lfloor x\rfloor<1\) and \(\lfloor x\rfloor\ge x/2\).  Applying (1) to the two source counts gives
\[
 B\left\{\frac{V_{E,d}}{n_{E,B}(d,\alpha)}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_{O,B}(d,\alpha)}\right\}
 =\mathcal V_d(\alpha;P)+\mathfrak r_{d,B}(\alpha;P)
\]
and
\[
 0\le\mathfrak r_{d,B}(\alpha;P)
 \le\frac2B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}c_{E,d}^2}{\alpha^2}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}c_{O,d}^2}{(1-\alpha)^2}\right\}.           \tag{2}
\]
The right-hand side of (2) tends to zero.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}designwise\text{-}gradient}\) identifies the expression before multiplication by \(B\) as the variance of the two-source gradient for \(\theta_d(P)=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}\).  When this functional equals \(\tau\) and the displayed gradient is canonical for \(\tau\), the same expression is the causal local efficiency bound; no such causal interpretation is needed for the floor-count algebra.

Set
\[
 a_d=c_{E,d}V_{E,d},
 \qquad
 b_d=c_{O,d}V_{O,d}.
\]
Positive costs and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) give \(a_d,b_d>0\).  For \(0<\alpha<1\), direct completion of the square yields
\[
 \frac{a_d}{\alpha}+\frac{b_d}{1-\alpha}
 -(\sqrt{a_d}+\sqrt{b_d})^2
 =\frac{\{(1-\alpha)\sqrt{a_d}-\alpha\sqrt{b_d}\}^2}
        {\alpha(1-\alpha)}\ge0.                             \tag{3}
\]
Equality holds at exactly one point,
\[
 \alpha=\frac{\sqrt{a_d}}{\sqrt{a_d}+\sqrt{b_d}}
 =\alpha_d^*(P).
\]
Substitution in (3) proves
\[
 \mathcal V_d^*(P)
 =(\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}})^2,
\]
and completes the proof of the proposed narrowed statement.
