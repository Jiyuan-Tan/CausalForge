Write
\[
 v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P),\qquad
 \widehat v_d=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*,\qquad
 v_* =\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d,
\]
and choose any \(d^*\in D^*(P)\).  By the definition of \(\varepsilon_m\) and the empirical minimizing property of \(\widehat d_m\),
\[
 v_{\widehat d_m}
 \le \widehat v_{\widehat d_m}+\varepsilon_m
 \le \widehat v_{d^*}+\varepsilon_m
 \le v_{d^*}+2\varepsilon_m
 =v_*+2\varepsilon_m.
 \tag{1}
\]
Since \(v_*\) is the population minimum,
\[
 0\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m.
 \tag{2}
\]
If \(\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\), the definition of \(\gamma(P)\) and (2) give
\[
 \gamma(P)\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m.
 \tag{3}
\]
This proves the event inclusion.  If \(D^*(P)=\mathcal D\), its left-hand event is empty and the convention \(\gamma(P)=+\infty\) makes its right-hand event empty.  Otherwise finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) gives \(\gamma(P)>0\).

Set
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}.
\]
The pilot conditions yield \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  By the pilot construction and the budget-profile theorem,
\[
 \widehat v_d=F_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d}),
 \qquad
 v_d=F_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d}),
 \tag{4}
\]
where
\[
 F_d(x,y)=\bigl(\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}\bigr)^2.
\]
Nondegeneracy, positive costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) place the population variance pairs in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\); with probability tending to one the estimated pairs lie in a slightly larger such compact set.  The gradients of the finitely many maps \(F_d\) are uniformly bounded there.  The mean-value theorem gives
\[
 \varepsilon_m
 =\max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat v_d-v_d|
 =O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).
 \tag{5}
\]
Equations (3) and (5) prove the asserted probability limit when a suboptimal design exists, while the all-optimal case was already handled.

It remains to establish the allocation assertions.  Put
\[
 \nu_0=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0
\]
and consider
\[
 \mathcal E_m=\{Z_m<\nu_0/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_0/2\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Its probability tends to one, and on it every estimated variance exceeds \(\underline v_m\).  Hence the truncations are simultaneously inactive.  For
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}},
\]
the same compact-set mean-value argument yields
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=O_P(r_{V,m}).
 \tag{7}
\]

The sharpened second-order allocation-loss lemma, applied under exactly these variance, rate, and truncation conditions, now gives
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
\frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
+\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
\right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
=O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1}).
 \tag{8}
\]
Because \(r_{V,m}=o(1)\) and \(B\to\infty\), (8) is \(o_P(1)\).  The tie rule was used only to choose an empirical minimizer in (1); thus the consistency assertion is membership in all of \(D^*(P)\), rather than convergence to a particular population tie.
