Put \(t=2a-1\), and first consider a center design having early signal \(x\in(0,1)\) and late signal \(y\in(0,1)\). Write \(E=W_x\) and \(L=W_y\). All latent cells are strictly positive. Consistency follows from \(Y(a)=U\) and \(Z(a)=Z\). In source \(E\), treatment is independent of \((U,Z)\); in source \(O\), treatment is independent of \(Z\) conditional on \(U\). The overlap and source-support inequalities are therefore strict. The distribution of \((U,Z)\) is source invariant, and conditional independence of the proxies given \(U\) gives the sequential-outcome restriction.

The proposed outcome bridge obeys
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{L}{y}\mathrel{\Big|}U\right]=U.
\]
Conditional independence of \(E\) and \(L\) given \(U\), including after conditioning on \(T=t,G=O\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E[Y\mid E,T=t,G=O]
 =\mathbb E[U\mid E,T=t,G=O]
 =\mathbb E[L/y\mid E,T=t,G=O].                         \tag{5}
\]
Bayes' rule also gives
\[
 \mathbb E[E\mid L=l,T=t,G=O]
 =x\frac{t/4+yl}{1+tyl/4}.                              \tag{6}
\]
For
\[
 q(e,a)=\frac{1-te/(4x)}{1-1/16}
 =\frac{16}{15}\left(1-\frac{te}{4x}\right),           \tag{7}
\]
substitution of (6) yields
\[
 \mathbb E[q(E,a)\mid L=l,T=t,G=O]
 =\frac1{1+tyl/4}
 =\frac{p(L=l\mid T=t,G=E)}{p(L=l\mid T=t,G=O)}.        \tag{8}
\]
Thus (5) and (8) are the outcome- and selection-bridge equations.

For binary \(U\), conditional expectation given a binary proxy has rank two exactly when its two conditional rows differ. Since \(x,y\ne0\), both completeness conditions hold. Further,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(E,L\mid T=t,G=O)
 =xy-\frac{xt}{4}\frac{yt}{4}=\frac{15}{16}xy\ne0.     \tag{9}
\]
All four conditional cells are positive, so (9) says that the \(2\)-by-\(2\) conditional-expectation matrix from functions of \(L\) to functions of \(E\) has nonzero determinant. Hence the bridge operator is bijective. Finite support supplies every required moment, and the score variances calculated below are finite and positive. This verifies every member condition in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}regular\text{-}law\text{-}class}\), so \(P_{\chi,\epsilon}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

At the experimental center, \(p_{E,a}=1/2\), \(\mathbb E[L\mid A=a,G=E]=0\), and \(\mu_{d,a}=0\). Since \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), both bridge contrasts and \(\tau\) are zero. The experimental score is \(2TL/y\), whence
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}.                                  \tag{10}
\]
For the observational score, (7) gives \(2Tq(E,A)(U-L/y)\). The center moments
\[
 \mathbb E[TE]=\frac{x}{4},\qquad
 \mathbb E[UL]=y,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[TEUL]=\frac{xy}{4}                           \tag{11}
\]
therefore imply, after expanding both squares,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 V_O(x,y)
 &=\frac{1024}{225}\,
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left\{1-\frac{TE}{2x}+\frac1{16x^2}\right\}
 \left\{1+\frac1{y^2}-\frac{2UL}{y}\right\}
 \right]\\
 &=\frac{64}{225}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}\left(14+x^{-2}\right).
                                                               \tag{12}
\end{aligned}
\]
The conditional mean of \(U\) given \((E,L)=(e,l)\) is
\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid E=e,L=l]=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel},
\]
and \(P(E=e,L=l)=(1+xyel)/4\). Averaging the conditional variance yields
\[
 R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.               \tag{13}
\]

For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), take \(x=\epsilon\) and \(y^2=1-3/(10\chi)\). Direct substitution in the primitive cost formulas gives
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{5\chi-1}{2},\qquad
 c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{5\chi+1}{2},
\]
whereas \(c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=2\) and \(c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=3\). Hence
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =\frac{5(\chi-1)}2>0.                                 \tag{14}
\]
Equation (13) and the paired costs give
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac32<3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B), \tag{15}
\]
so \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is uniquely prediction-selected.

Equations (10)--(12) give
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{40\chi}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14),           \tag{16}
\]
and, for \(d_{\mathrm e}\),
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,\qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}.                   \tag{17}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) now yields
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\left(\sqrt{32}+\sqrt{1152/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}.                                     \tag{18}
\]
For the other design,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}
 &=\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3}=:a_{\chi},\\
 c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 &=\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}
   (\epsilon^{-2}+14)=:b_{\chi,\epsilon}.             \tag{19}
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \((5\chi+1)/(10\chi-3)>1/2\),
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \ge b_{\chi,\epsilon}
 >\frac{16}{75}\epsilon^{-2}
 >\frac{3872}{25}                                      \tag{20}
\]
for \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\). Thus \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is uniquely value-optimal. Dividing \((\sqrt{a_{\chi}}+\sqrt{b_{\chi,\epsilon}})^2\) by (18) proves the arm-normalized identity (1) in the statement.

Treatment is balanced in both sources at every center, so \(p_{E,1}=p_{O,1}=1/2\). The IKM-normalized source moments are therefore one quarter of their arm-normalized counterparts, every profiled value is multiplied by one quarter, and the minimizing design and approximation ratio are unchanged. This proves the IKM equality in (1).

Discarding the nonnegative \(a_\chi\) and cross terms in the exact ratio gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \ge \frac{25}{3872}b_{\chi,\epsilon}
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14).     \tag{21}
\]
The identity
\[
 \frac{5\chi+1}{10\chi-3}-\frac12
 =\frac5{2(10\chi-3)}>0                                \tag{22}
\]
proves the strict pointwise inequality in (2).

For fixed \(\epsilon\), let the right side of the exact ratio (1) minus \((\epsilon^{-2}+14)/726\) be \(g_\epsilon(\chi)\). It extends continuously to \(\chi=1\), is strictly positive there and at every finite \(\chi\ge1\) by (21)--(22), and tends to \(+\infty\) as \(\chi\to\infty\) because \(a_\chi\sim10\chi\). Choose \(M\) so that \(g_\epsilon(\chi)\ge1\) for \(\chi\ge M\). Continuity and strict positivity on the compact interval \([1,M]\) give a positive minimum there. Hence \(\inf_{\chi>1}g_\epsilon(\chi)>0\), proving (3).

Multiplying (3) by \(\epsilon^2\) gives
\[
 \liminf_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\epsilon^2
 \inf_{\chi>1}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \ge\frac1{726}.                                      \tag{23}
\]
For the reverse direction take \(\chi_\epsilon=\epsilon^{-2/3}>1\). From (19),
\[
 \epsilon^2a_{\chi_\epsilon}=O(\epsilon^{4/3})\longrightarrow0,
\]
whereas
\[
 \epsilon^2b_{\chi_\epsilon,\epsilon}
 =\frac{32(5\chi_\epsilon+1)}{75(10\chi_\epsilon-3)}
 (1+14\epsilon^2)\longrightarrow\frac{16}{75}.         \tag{24}
\]
Consequently the exact ratio implies
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi_\epsilon,\epsilon})
 =\frac{25}{3872}
 \left(\sqrt{\epsilon^2a_{\chi_\epsilon}}
 +\sqrt{\epsilon^2b_{\chi_\epsilon,\epsilon}}\right)^2
 \longrightarrow \frac{25}{3872}\frac{16}{75}
 =\frac1{726}.                                        \tag{25}
\]
The infimum is no larger than the value at \(\chi_\epsilon\), so (25) and (23) prove the first limit in (4). The center IKM ratio equals the arm-normalized ratio for every \((\chi,\epsilon)\), proving the second limit as well.
