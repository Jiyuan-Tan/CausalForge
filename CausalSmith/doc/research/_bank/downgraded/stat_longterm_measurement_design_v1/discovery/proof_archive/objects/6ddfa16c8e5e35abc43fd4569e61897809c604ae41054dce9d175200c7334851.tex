Fix \(P\) as in the statement.  The proof has four parts.

\emph{1. Interior source sizes and arm counts.}
For every \(d\in\mathcal D\), positivity of the costs and of \(V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\) implies
\[
 0<\alpha_d^*(P)<1.
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le \alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).
 \tag{1}
\]
The pilot-regret theorem gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus, with probability tending to one, \(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\), and both purchased source counts are bounded below by a positive constant times \(B\).  Experimental overlap gives \(p_{E,a}>0\).  Observational latent overlap implies \(p_{O,a}>0\) after averaging over \((U,X)\).  Conditional on the pilot, each arm count is binomial with a success probability bounded away from zero, and every balanced observational fold has size diverging at rate \(B\).  Chebyshev's inequality therefore gives
\[
 P(\mathsf D_B)\longrightarrow1.
 \tag{3}
\]

\emph{2. A deterministic-path asymptotic expansion.}
Consider a deterministic sequence \((d_B,\alpha_B)\) satisfying the displayed condition (1) of the theorem.  Write \(e_B,o_B\) for its source sizes and abbreviate \(h=h_{d_B}\), \(q=q_{d_B}\).  By (1) above and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\),
\[
 e_B\asymp B,\qquad o_B\asymp B,
 \tag{4}
\]
uniformly along such paths.

We first record the elementary arm-ratio expansion used below.  If \(W_1,\ldots,W_N\) are independent observations from one source, \(p_a=P(A=a)>0\), and \(f\) is square integrable, then direct subtraction of the conditional mean and the identity \(N_a/N=p_a+O_P(N^{-1/2})\) give
\[
 \frac{1}{N_a}\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}f(W_i)
 -E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}
 =\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{p_a}
 \bigl[f(W_i)-E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}\bigr]
 +o_P(N^{-1/2}).
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the centered sum on the right is \(O_P(N^{-1/2})\) by its variance, while \(Np_a/N_a-1=O_P(N^{-1/2})\); their product is \(O_P(N^{-1})\).  Conditional on a fold-external random function \(f_N\), the same variance calculation shows that the empirical-minus-population term is \(o_P(N^{-1/2})\) whenever \(\|f_N\|_2=o_P(1)\).  Balanced-fold averaging changes the coefficient \(1/o_B\) by only \(O(B^{-2})\) per observation and hence has an \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) effect.  All these conclusions hold simultaneously over the fixed number of folds.

For a fold \(\ell\), write \(\Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-h\) and \(\Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-q\).  The outcome moment equation and the selection-bridge change-of-measure identity imply, for every square-integrable \(f\) and \(u\),
\[
 E_O\{u(S_{2d_B},S_{1d_B},a,X)(Y-h)\mid A=a\}=0,
 \tag{6}
\]
\[
 E_E\{f(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}
 =E_O\{q f(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Consequently, conditional on the training data, the nuisance drift in the estimator of the arm mean is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &E_E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)
 +E_O\{(q+\Delta q_\ell)(Y-h-\Delta h_\ell)\mid A=a\}\\
 &\hspace{35mm}=-E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{8}
\]
Here the two terms linear in \(\Delta h_\ell\) cancel by (7), and both residual terms linear in \(q\) or \(\Delta q_\ell\) vanish by (6).

Since treatment probabilities are positive, conditional and unconditional source norms differ only by fixed constants.  Using first iterated expectation through \(T_{d_B}\), and then through its adjoint, gives the two bounds
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B}
       \|T_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B},\\
 |E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|T_{d_B}^{\star}\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}
       \|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]
The source weights under \(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) are bounded away from zero by (4), so the deterministic-path conditions (2)--(4) of the theorem and (9) imply that (8) is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed folds and treatment arms.

It remains to control the centered nuisance terms.  The experimental perturbation is \(\Delta h_\ell\), whose conditional empirical fluctuation is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) by (5), the strong-norm rate, and (4).  The observational perturbation is the sum
\[
 \Delta q_\ell(Y-h)-q\Delta h_\ell-\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell.
 \tag{10}
\]
Condition (5) of the theorem makes its \(L_2(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm \(o_P(1)\).  Conditional Chebyshev bounds on each evaluation fold, followed by the finite-fold union bound, make its centered empirical contribution \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  The stipulated training scheme supplies the conditional independence used in these variance bounds: the experimental term uses outcome bridges trained only on observational data, and each observational evaluation fold is excluded from the training data for both bridges.

Applying (5) to the remaining oracle terms, and using balanced-fold equivalence, proves the expansion
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)
 ={}&\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 +o_P(B^{-1/2}).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
This derivation uses the observed bridge contrast as its center; it does not assert equality with \(\tau\).

For completeness, (11) has a normal limit under only the finite second moments already stated in the core.  The two sums are independent, centered, and have variances \(V_{E,d_B}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d_B}/o_B\).  For a centered square-integrable random variable \(X\), dominated convergence applied to
\[
 \frac{e^{iuX}-1-iuX}{u^2}
\]
as \(u\to0\) gives \(E(e^{iuX})=1-u^2E(X^2)/2+o(u^2)\).  Applying this expansion to the characteristic functions of the two independent sums shows that
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B}{s_{0,B}(d_B,\alpha_B)}
 \left\{
 \frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 \right\}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1),
 \tag{12}
\]
where
\[
 s_{0,B}^2(d_B,\alpha_B)
 =B\left(\frac{V_{E,d_B}}{e_B}+\frac{V_{O,d_B}}{o_B}\right).
\]
If \(d_B\) varies, every subsequence has a further subsequence on which it is constant because \(\mathcal D\) is finite; the same limit in (12) on every such subsequence proves (12) for the original sequence.  Nondegeneracy and (4) keep \(s_{0,B}\) bounded above and away from zero.  Equations (11)--(12) therefore give the deterministic-path studentized limit.

\emph{3. Transfer to the pilot-selected path and variance consistency.}
By (2), one can choose a deterministic \(t_B\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\left\{
 |\widehat\alpha_m-\alpha_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)|>t_B
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{13}
\]
The deterministic-path conclusions above are uniform over
\[
 \{(d,\alpha):d\in\mathcal D,
 |\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)|\le t_B\}.
 \tag{14}
\]
To see this, failure of uniformity would select a deterministic violating pair from (14) for infinitely many \(B\), contradicting the hypotheses and the conclusions just proved along every deterministic path.  Independence of the pilot and main study then permits conditioning on the realized pair \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\).  Equations (11)--(14) yield
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)
 -\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
 \tag{15}
\]

The sharpened pilot allocation result, evaluated at the selected design, gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).
 \tag{16}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\), direct subtraction of (12) in the theorem from the oracle expression gives
\[
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 \le \frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|
 +\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.
 \tag{17}
\]
The two coefficients are \(O_P(1)\) by (1)--(2), and the maximum variance error is \(o_P(1)\) by condition (10).  Combining (3), (16), and (17) proves
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).
 \tag{18}
\]
The finite family and nondegeneracy imply
\[
 \min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)>0.
\]
Hence (3), (16), and (18) show that \(P(\widehat s_B^2>0)\to1\), proving
\[
 P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\longrightarrow1
 \tag{19}
\]
and also \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  Equations (15) and (19), together with this ratio convergence, prove
\[
 \mathsf T_B\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1),
 \tag{20}
\]
including the declared zero extension off \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\).

\emph{4. Coverage and the causal specialization.}
On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the event \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\) is exactly
\[
 |\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}.
\]
The complement of \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\) has vanishing probability by (19).  Since the standard normal distribution is continuous at both critical points, (20) gives
\[
 P\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}\longrightarrow1-\eta.
\]
If \(\theta_d(P)=\tau\) for every \(d\in\mathcal D\), then \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)=\tau\) identically.  Replacing the center in (15), (20), and the coverage event by \(\tau\) proves all final assertions of the theorem.
