Write all selected quantities at \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\).  The pilot-oracle-regret theorem and (9) give
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).\tag{18}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and every cost and source variance is positive and finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0\qquad(d\in\mathcal D).             \tag{19}
\]
Thus \(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\) with probability tending to one, and both selected source counts are bounded below by a positive multiple of \(B\).  The totalized conclusion of the pilot-oracle-regret theorem, evaluated at the random member \(\widehat d_m\) of the finite family, gives
\[
s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).                   \tag{20}
\]

We next prove a direct arm-normalized expansion.  Fix deterministic sequences \(d_B\in\mathcal D\) and \(\alpha_B\) satisfying (1).  From every subsequence, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) permits extraction of a further subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant.  Along it,
\[
\frac{e_B}{B}\to\frac{\alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{E,d}},\qquad
\frac{o_B}{B}\to\frac{1-\alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{O,d}},\qquad n_B\asymp B.\tag{21}
\]
Hence both source probabilities under \(P_{B,d,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) are bounded away from zero.

For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), abbreviate
\[
r_{E,a}=h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a},
\quad
r_{O,a}=q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)
\{Y-h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\}.                                    \tag{22}
\]
The definition of \(\mu_{d,a}\) gives \(\mathbb E(r_{E,a}\mid A=a,G=E)=0\), and the observed outcome-bridge equation gives \(\mathbb E(r_{O,a}\mid A=a,G=O)=0\).

We repeatedly use the following elementary identity.  If \((I_i,R_i)_{i=1}^k\) are independent and identically distributed, \(I_i\) is Bernoulli with mean \(p\in(0,1)\), \(\mathbb E(I_iR_i)=0\), and \(\mathbb E(I_iR_i^2)<\infty\), then, on \(\sum_iI_i>0\),
\[
\frac{\sum_iI_iR_i}{\sum_iI_i}
-\frac1k\sum_i\frac{I_iR_i}{p}
=\frac{(p-\overline I_k)\overline{IR}_k}{p\overline I_k}
=O_P(k^{-1}).                                                       \tag{23}
\]
Indeed, both centered sample means in the numerator are \(O_P(k^{-1/2})\), while \(\overline I_k\) is bounded away from zero with probability tending to one.  Apply (23) to the oracle experimental arm mean and separately in each of the fixed \(L\) balanced observational folds.  The difference between the balanced fold weights and \(o_B^{-1}\) is negligible at order \(B^{-1/2}\).  The oracle version \(\widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)\) of (6) therefore satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)-\mu_{d,a}
&=\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}
\frac{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{p_{E,a}}r_{E,a}(O_i)\\
&\quad+\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}
\frac{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{p_{O,a}}r_{O,a}(O_i)
+o_P(B^{-1/2}).
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{24}
\]

For fold \(\ell\), write \(\Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d\) and \(\Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d\).  After the same random-denominator replacement, the conditional population nuisance contribution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=E)\\
&\quad+\mathbb E\{\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_d)-q_d\Delta h_\ell
-\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O\}.
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{25}
\]
The selection-bridge equation and change of measure give
\[
\mathbb E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=E)
=\mathbb E(q_d\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O),                         \tag{26}
\]
whereas measurability of \(\Delta q_\ell\) with respect to \((S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X)\) and the observed outcome-bridge equation give
\[
\mathbb E\{\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_d)\mid A=a,G=O\}=0.                \tag{27}
\]
Thus (25) equals \(-\mathbb E(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)\).  Iterating conditional expectations in either direction and applying Cauchy--Schwarz yields, for a finite constant depending only on the positive arm and source probabilities,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathbb E(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)|
\le C_a\min\{&
\|T_d^\star\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
\|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ},\\
&\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
\|T_d\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}\}.
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{28}
\]
Conditions (2)--(4) make (28) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed folds.

Conditional on the training observations, each evaluation observation is independent of its fitted nuisance functions.  Conditional Chebyshev inequality bounds a centered empirical nuisance average by its \(L_2(P_{B,d,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm divided by the square root of its source or fold size.  Condition (2) controls the experimental \(\Delta h_\ell\) term, while (5) controls the three observational multiplier terms.  These norms are \(o_P(1)\), and every evaluation size is of order \(B\), so every centered nuisance average is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  The nuisance arm averages are \(o_P(1)\); their products with the \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\) arm-proportion errors are also \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Combining these facts with (25)--(28) gives
\[
\widehat\mu_{B,d}(a)-\widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)=o_P(B^{-1/2}).        \tag{29}
\]
Subtracting the expansions for \(a=0\) from those for \(a=1\) yields
\[
\widehat\tau_B(d,\alpha_B)-\theta_d(P)
=\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d}(O_i)
+\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d}(O_i)
+o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                                     \tag{30}
\]

The two leading sums are independent and centered, with variances \(V_{E,d}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d}/o_B\).  Here the needed central-limit calculation follows directly from finite second moments.  If \(R\) is centered with variance \(\sigma^2<\infty\), the identity
\[
e^{ix}=1+ix-\frac{x^2}{2}+x^2r(x),\qquad r(x)\longrightarrow0,
\]
together with a bounded version of \(r\) and dominated convergence gives
\[
\mathbb E e^{itR/\sqrt k}
=1-\frac{t^2\sigma^2}{2k}+o(k^{-1}).
\]
Raising this expression to the \(k\)-th power gives \(\exp(-t^2\sigma^2/2)\).  Apply this calculation separately to the experimental and observational sums.  Their independence multiplies the characteristic functions, while (21) makes their deterministic variance weights converge.  Combining this calculation with (30) gives
\[
\frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)\}}
{[B\{V_{E,d_B}/e_B+V_{O,d_B}/o_B\}]^{1/2}}
\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)                                  \tag{31}
\]
along every deterministic path satisfying (1).  This is a direct arm-normalized expansion; no differently normalized fixed-design result is used.

We lift (31) through the independent pilot.  From (18), choose deterministic \(b_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
P\left\{\max_d|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|>b_m\right\}\to0.\tag{32}
\]
Conditional on any pilot realization in the complementary event, the main-study design and allocation form a deterministic path covered by (31).  This convergence is uniform over those realizations: otherwise one could choose a violating deterministic sequence and then extract a constant-design subsequence, contradicting (31).  Pilot independence permits integration of conditional bounded-Lipschitz distances, while the exceptional event in (32) has vanishing probability.  Hence
\[
\frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)
-\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)                                  \tag{33}
\]
with the stipulated zero-denominator extension of the design--allocation estimator.  The further total extension defining \(\widehat\tau_B\) will be asymptotically equivalent once \(P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\to1\) is established below.

It remains to prove that all denominators and the estimated variance are asymptotically valid.  On the high-probability interior event from (18)--(19), each source count is at least \(cB\) for some \(c>0\).  Experimental overlap gives \(p_{E,a}>0\).  Observational latent overlap implies \(p_{O,a}>0\) after integration over \((U,X)\).  Conditional on the pilot, an experimental arm is empty with probability at most \((1-p_{E,a})^{cB}\), and a balanced observational fold-arm is empty with probability at most \((1-p_{O,a})^{cB/L-O(1)}\).  A finite union bound proves
\[
P(\mathsf D_B)\to1.                                                \tag{34}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
&\le\frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
|\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
&\quad+\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
|\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{35}
\]
The count ratios are \(O_P(1)\), while (10) makes the uniform variance error \(o_P(1)\).  Equations (20) and (35) prove
\[
\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2=o_P(1),\qquad
\widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).              \tag{36}
\]
Nondegeneracy, positive costs, and finiteness imply
\[
0<\min_d\mathcal V_d^*(P)\le\max_d\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty.     \tag{37}
\]
Together, (34)--(37) yield \(P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\to1\) and \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  To replace the denominator directly, let \(Z_B\) denote the statistic in (33) and \(R_B=\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\) on \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\).  Convergence in (33) makes \((Z_B)\) tight.  On \(\{|Z_B|\le M,|R_B-1|\le\delta<1/2\}\),
\[
|Z_B/R_B-Z_B|\le2M\delta.
\]
First choose \(M\) to control the tightness tail and then let \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Because \(R_B\to_P1\) and the complement event has vanishing probability, \(Z_B/R_B-Z_B\to_P0\).  Thus the two statistics have the same weak limit, and the total extension gives
\[
\mathsf T_B\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the event \(\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}\) equals \(\{|\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\}\); the symmetric difference under the total extension is contained in \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}^c\).  Continuity of the standard normal distribution at the two critical values proves coverage \(1-\eta\).  Finally, if \(\theta_d(P)=\tau\) for all \(d\), then \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)=\tau\) for every pilot realization.  Substitution gives the asserted causal-centered limit and coverage.
