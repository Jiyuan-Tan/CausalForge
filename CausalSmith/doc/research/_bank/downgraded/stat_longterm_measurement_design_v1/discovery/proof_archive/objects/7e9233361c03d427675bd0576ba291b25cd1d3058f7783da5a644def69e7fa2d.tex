The cited Carneiro--Lee--Wilhelm result reduces their balanced-treatment population data-collection problem to residual variance divided by sample size. Under the prescribed linear cost, the largest continuous feasible size for subset \(M_d\) is \(B/(c_{E,d}+c_{O,d})\), so direct substitution gives
\[
 \frac{R_d(P)}{B/(c_{E,d}+c_{O,d})}
 =\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}(P;B).                              \tag{1}
\]
Only the support is mapped to \(M_d\); the role assignment remains fixed by the present catalogue.

We first derive the residual variance rather than taking it from the witness definition. At a binary center, \(Y=U\), \(E(U)=E(W_x)=E(W_y)=0\), and conditional independence given \(U\) gives
\[
 E(UW_x)=x,\qquad E(UW_y)=y,\qquad E(W_xW_y)=xy.
\]
The normal equations for the population regression of \(U\) on \((W_x,W_y)\) are therefore
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&xy\\xy&1\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}\beta_1\\\beta_2\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Because \(0<x,y<1\), the determinant \(1-x^2y^2\) is strictly positive. Matrix inversion yields
\[
 \beta_1=\frac{x(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2},
 \qquad
 \beta_2=\frac{y(1-x^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.                       \tag{2}
\]
The orthogonal-projection identity then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sigma^2(x,y)
 &=E(U^2)-(x,y)
 \begin{pmatrix}1&xy\\xy&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}
 \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}\\
 &=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}=R(x,y).                \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the exact CLW-to-catalogue criterion mapping.

We next verify regularity and common identification. The center construction has strictly positive cells, consistency by construction, experimental randomization, observational latent exchangeability and overlap, source-invariant proxy kernels, and conditional independence of the early and late proxies given \(U\). Hence external validity and the sequential-outcome restriction hold. For a binary proxy with nonzero signal, the two-by-two conditional-expectation matrix has nonzero determinant. Since \(\epsilon,y_\chi,1/2\) are nonzero, both completeness and both operator-bijectivity conditions hold for both designs. The displayed finite bridges solve the observed equations, and their scores have finite, strictly positive variances. Thus \(P_{\chi,\epsilon}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\). Since \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), \(\tau=0\). The outcome-bridge equation, completeness, external validity, and experimental randomization give \(\mu_{d,a}=E(U)=0\), so both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau\).

Substitute \(x=\epsilon\) and \(y^2=y_\chi^2=1-3/(10\chi)\) in (3):
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{3}{10\chi}\frac{1-\epsilon^2}
 {1-\epsilon^2(1-3/(10\chi))}.
\]
The denominator of the last fraction exceeds its numerator by \(3\epsilon^2/(10\chi)\), so that fraction is less than one. For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), taking \(x=y=1/2\) gives \(R_{d_{\mathrm e}}=3/5\). This proves the raw-risk chain. The paired costs are \(5\chi\) and \(5\), hence
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac32<3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}.                    \tag{4}
\]
Thus both the raw-risk and CLW continuous selectors choose \(d_{\mathrm p}\).

For the integer version, put \(C_d=c_{E,d}+c_{O,d}\) and \(n_d(B)=\lfloor B/C_d\rfloor\). Whenever \(B\ge2C_d\), the elementary floor inequality
\[
 0\le \frac{B}{\lfloor B/C_d\rfloor}-C_d
 \le\frac{2C_d^2}{B}                                      \tag{5}
\]
follows from \(0\le1/\lfloor z\rfloor-1/z\le2/z^2\) for \(z\ge2\). Multiplying (5) by the finite positive \(R_d\) shows that
\[
 \frac{B R_d}{n_d(B)}\longrightarrow C_dR_d.
\]
The limiting gap in (4) is strict, so the integer criterion has the same unique minimizer for all sufficiently large \(B\). This also states the positivity boundary for its denominators.

For the source scores, the outcome bridge is \(h(W_y,a)=W_y/y\), whence at the balanced experimental center
\[
 V_{E,d}=\frac4{y^2}.
\]
The selection bridge is \(q(W_x,a)=16\{1-TW_x/(4x)\}/15\). Conditional expansion gives
\[
 V_{O,d}=\frac{64(1-y^2)(1+14x^2)}{225x^2y^2}.
\]
Substitution gives the two displayed components for \(d_{\mathrm p}\), while \(d_{\mathrm e}\) has \(V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16\) and \(V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=384/25\). The budget-profile theorem yields
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*=\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot384/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}.
\]
The exact ratio (4) in the statement follows by substituting the components and costs in the profiled formula. Dropping its first nonnegative square-root term gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 \ge\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14)
 >\frac{\epsilon^{-2}+14}{726}.                            \tag{6}
\]
Since this exceeds one, \(d_{\mathrm e}\) uniquely minimizes the bridge profile. At every center both source treatment probabilities equal one half, so each IKM source moment and profiled value is one quarter of its arm-normalized counterpart. The factor cancels from the ratio. Because both raw-risk and CLW selectors choose \(d_{\mathrm p}\), all four ratios in the statement coincide.

Finally, write
\[
 A(\chi)=10\chi+1+\frac3{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 D(\chi)=\frac{16}{75}+\frac{16}{15(10\chi-3)},
 \qquad C_0=\frac{25}{3872}.
\]
The exact ratio satisfies
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho
 =C_0\left[\epsilon^2A(\chi)+D(\chi)(1+14\epsilon^2)
 +2\epsilon\sqrt{1+14\epsilon^2}\sqrt{A(\chi)D(\chi)}\right]. \tag{7}
\]
For \(\chi=k\epsilon^{-2/3}\), locally uniformly on compact subsets of \(k\in(0,\infty)\),
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho
 =\frac1{726}+C_0\epsilon^{2/3}
 \left\{2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac8{75k}\right\}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3}).                                      \tag{8}
\]
The rescaled minimizers remain in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\): the excess in braces diverges at both endpoints. Its derivative is
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{32}{15}}k^{-1/2}-\frac8{75}k^{-2},
\]
which changes sign once, at \(k_\star=\sqrt[3]{18}/15\). Therefore
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)\sim k_\star\epsilon^{-2/3},
 \qquad
 C_0q(k_\star)=\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}.
\]
Equation (8) proves the sharp expansion for the common ratio and completes the proof.
