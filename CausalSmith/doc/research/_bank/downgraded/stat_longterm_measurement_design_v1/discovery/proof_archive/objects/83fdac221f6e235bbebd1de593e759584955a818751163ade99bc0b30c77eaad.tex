By nondegeneracy, every \(V_{E,d}\) and \(V_{O,d}\) is positive and finite.  All costs are positive, and \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty.  The budget-profile theorem therefore gives
\[
 0<\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \le\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty. \tag{1}
\]
The predictive optimizer set is nonempty because it is the argmin of a real-valued criterion on a finite set.  Substitution of the profiled formula into the definition of \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\), followed by (1), proves the first display and shows that the least constant for the actual best-tie rule is precisely the displayed ratio itself.  Thus the requested law-specific finiteness needs no comparison involving \(T_d\).

For the pairwise assertion, let
\[
 C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)=
 \max_{\substack{d,d'\in\mathcal D:\,
 \Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}(P;B)\le\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d'}(P;B)}}
 \frac{\mathcal V_d^*(P)}{\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P)}.
\]
The maximum is over a nonempty finite collection and is finite by (1).  Its definition proves every ordered-pair implication.  Conversely, any constant satisfying all those implications must dominate every ratio in the maximum, so \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) is the least such constant.  Taking a predictive minimizer and an efficiency minimizer as an ordered pair also proves
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P).
\]
The predictive-impossibility theorem gives
\(\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\infty\), so no finite constant is uniform on that region.

Finally, even if one augments \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{comp}}\) with a common Hilbert space and positive quadratic forms \(Q\) and \(R\), a bound
\[
 mR(v)\le Q(v)\le MR(v)
\]
translates predictive minimization into at most the condition number \(M/m\), not \(M\) alone.  A largest generalized eigenvalue can also be strictly larger than the catalogue-restricted sharp factor: take \(R=I_2\), \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\), and a catalogue whose only relevant transported direction is \((0,1)\).  The restricted ratio is \(1\), whereas the largest generalized eigenvalue is \(2\).  Equality therefore requires a relevant ordered catalogue comparison to attain the top generalized eigendirection, together with the corresponding lower normalization.  The frozen handle supplies neither condition, proving the final claim.
