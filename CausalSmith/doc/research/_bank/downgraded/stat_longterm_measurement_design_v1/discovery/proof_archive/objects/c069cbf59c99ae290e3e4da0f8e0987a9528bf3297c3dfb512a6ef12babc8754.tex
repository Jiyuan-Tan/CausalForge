Fix \(P\) and \(B\).  By Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\), positive costs and nondegenerate source moments imply
\[
 0<v_d<\infty
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).                                  \tag{1}
\]
The relation \(\mathcal O_P\) is nonempty because it contains every diagonal pair, and it is finite because \(\mathcal D\) is finite.  Hence the maximum defining \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) exists, is finite by (1), and is at least one by a diagonal pair.

Suppose first that all diagnostic-ordered pairs satisfy \(v_d\le Cv_{d'}\).  Division by the positive \(v_{d'}\), followed by maximization over \(\mathcal O_P\), gives \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C\).  Conversely, if \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\), the definition of the maximum gives
\[
 \frac{v_d}{v_{d'}}\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C
\]
for every \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\).  This proves (2).

Let \(d_s\) minimize \(v_d\) among the diagnostic minimizers, and let \(d_*\) minimize \(v_d\) over all designs.  Since \(d_s\) is diagnostic-optimal,
\[
 p_{d_s}\le p_{d_*},
\]
so \((d_s,d_*)\in\mathcal O_P\).  Therefore
\[
 1\le
 \frac{v_{d_s}}{v_{d_*}}
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P).                                \tag{2}
\]
The middle ratio is exactly both \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)\) and \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) by their definitions.  Moreover, a constant \(C\) validates the actual best-tie inequality if and only if \(v_{d_s}\le Cv_{d_*}\); division by \(v_{d_*}>0\) proves that its smallest value is this ratio.  Taking suprema over a nonempty subclass \(\mathcal Q\) now proves both stated uniform-if-and-only-if claims: a common finite constant exists exactly when the corresponding pointwise sharp constants have finite supremum.  Thus the finite order enumeration is the exact weakest lawwise comparison.  The operators affect the values through the bridge solutions, but no additional operator comparison is required for this finite-law statement.

Consider next a numerical realization of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{comp}}\) satisfying (4)--(6).  Positivity of \(Q_{R,d}\) on the chosen cone makes every ratio in (6) well defined.  Condition (7) is equivalent, by the definitions of the infima and suprema, to the two-sided envelope
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)Q_{R,d}(x)
 \le F_d(x)
 \le\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}(x)                               \tag{3}
\]
for every design and every cone direction.  This explains in the literal sense why (7) is the weakest two-sided envelope on those cones.

Apply (3) to the law-realized directions in (4).  If \(p_d\le p_{d'}\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_d
 &\le\Lambda_+(P)Bp_d
 \le\Lambda_+(P)Bp_{d'}\\
 &\le\frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}v_{d'}.
                                                               \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]
The sharp characterization (2), followed by (2) above, gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)
 \le\frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}.
\]

Finally, for nonnegative numbers \(u= c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)\) and \(w=c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)\), the same square identity used in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) gives
\[
 \inf_{0<\alpha<1}\left\{\frac u\alpha+\frac w{1-\alpha}\right\}
 =(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)^2=F_d(x),                            \tag{5}
\]
including the boundary cases \(u=0\) or \(w=0\) by taking the appropriate one-sided limit.  This proves (9).

At a fixed \(\alpha\), the numerator in (5) is a quadratic form, so in a finite-dimensional full-space representation with positive-definite \(Q_{R,d}\), its largest ratio to \(Q_{R,d}\) is a largest generalized eigenvalue.  Profiling takes the pointwise infimum over \(\alpha\); unless the source quadratic forms have special proportional structure, that infimum is not itself quadratic.  Furthermore, (4) requires both a positive lower envelope and a finite upper envelope across designs, whereas a largest generalized eigenvalue supplies only the latter for one fixed form and one full space.  Equality in the final certificate would additionally require the extrema to be reached by the law-realized directions in the relevant diagnostic order.  A common-space representation, lower-envelope normalization, and this saturation are therefore necessary for a generalized-eigenvalue equality, and none is implied by designwise bijectivity or by diagnostic ordering.
