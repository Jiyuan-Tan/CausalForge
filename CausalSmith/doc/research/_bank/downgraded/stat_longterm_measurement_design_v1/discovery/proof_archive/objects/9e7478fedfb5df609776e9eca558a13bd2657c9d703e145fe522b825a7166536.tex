Put \(t_m=r_{V,m}+\underline v_m\) and
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\left\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\right\}.
\]
The pilot variance-rate assumption gives \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})\).  For \(x,y>0\), define
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}.
\]
For every \(V>0\) and \(x\geq0\),
\[
 |(x\vee\underline v_m)-V|
 \leq |x-V|+\underline v_m.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and the displayed premise of the lemma bounds all costs and population variance components away from zero and infinity, the maps \(A_d\) have uniformly bounded first derivatives on a common compact rectangle containing, with probability tending to one, all pairs
\[
 \bigl(\widehat V_{E,d}\vee\underline v_m,
       \widehat V_{O,d}\vee\underline v_m\bigr)
 \quad\text{and}\quad (V_{E,d},V_{O,d}).
\]
The mean-value theorem and (1) therefore give
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=O_P(t_m).                    \tag{2}
\]

Finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) and positivity of all four components imply that there is a constant \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\leq\alpha_d^*(P)\leq1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).
\]
Since \(t_m=o(1)\), (2) implies
\[
 \Pr\left\{a_0\leq\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\leq1-a_0
       \text{ for every }d\in\mathcal D\right\}\longrightarrow1. \tag{3}
\]

By the budget-profile theorem,
\[
 \mathcal V_d(\alpha;P)
 =\frac{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{\alpha}
  +\frac{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{1-\alpha}.
\]
On \([a_0,1-a_0]\), its second derivative is
\[
 \partial_\alpha^2\mathcal V_d(\alpha;P)
 =\frac{2c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{\alpha^3}
  +\frac{2c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{(1-\alpha)^3},
\]
which is uniformly bounded over the finite family.  Moreover,
\(\partial_\alpha\mathcal V_d(\alpha_d^*(P);P)=0\).  Taylor's theorem, (2), and (3) yield
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 \left|\mathcal V_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m};P)
             -\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|=O_P(t_m^2).                 \tag{4}
\]

For \(x\geq2\), the elementary floor inequality
\[
 0\leq\frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x\leq\frac2{x^2}            \tag{5}
\]
holds.  On the event in (3), both continuous source counts are bounded below by a positive constant times \(B\), uniformly over \(d\).  Thus (5), applied to the two counts, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|
 B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}
 -\mathcal V_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m};P)
 \right|\\
 &\hspace{35mm}=O_P(B^{-1}).
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{6}
\]
The counts are positive with probability tending to one; defining the expression arbitrarily on the vanishing complementary event does not change its stochastic order.  Combining (4) and (6) proves
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
 =O_P\bigl(t_m^2+B^{-1}\bigr).
\]
The absence of a linear allocation term follows from the zero first derivative at \(\alpha_d^*(P)\), proving the final assertion.
