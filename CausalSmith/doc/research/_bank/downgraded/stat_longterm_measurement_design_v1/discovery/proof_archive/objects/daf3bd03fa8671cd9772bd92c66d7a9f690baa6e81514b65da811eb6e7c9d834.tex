Fix \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\).  The binary-witness stability lemma establishes first that \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).  We verify that every defining property of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\) persists on a relative neighborhood inside the constrained finite-cell model.

The consistency equalities, treatment-invariant potential outcomes, external validity, experimental randomization, observational latent exchangeability, and the conditional independences used by the sequential-outcome restriction hold identically throughout the constrained family by the potential-variable specification and factorization in the lemma statement.  Strict positivity of all factor probabilities is an open condition.  On a sufficiently small relative neighborhood of \(P_\epsilon\), it therefore supplies experimental and observational treatment overlap and finite source-density ratios.

It remains to check the rank, bridge, and variance conditions.  For a binary proxy \(W\) and binary \(U\), the conditional-expectation map from functions of \(U\) to functions of \(W\), after conditioning on a treatment arm in source \(O\), is injective exactly when
\[
 P(W=1\mid U=1)-P(W=1\mid U=-1)\ne0,                            \tag{1}
\]
because the positive treatment weights only rescale the two columns of its \(2\times2\) probability matrix.  At \(P_\epsilon\), the differences in (1) for the early and late proxies of \(d_{\mathrm p}\) are \(\epsilon\) and \(3/4\), and those for \(d_{\mathrm e}\) are \(1/2\) and \(1/2\).  All are nonzero.  Their finitely many determinants are continuous functions of the cell probabilities, so both designwise completeness conditions remain valid on a relative neighborhood.

For each treatment arm and each design, the matrix of \(T_d\) from the binary late proxy to the binary early proxy has determinant equal to a nonzero positive scaling factor times the product of the two proxy contrasts in (1) and the conditional variance of \(U\) in that arm.  All cell probabilities are positive, and the two proxy contrasts at \(P_\epsilon\) are nonzero; hence every one of these finitely many determinants is nonzero at \(P_\epsilon\) and remains nonzero nearby.  Thus \(T_d\) is bijective nearby.  In finite dimension its adjoint is then bijective as well, so the observed selection-bridge equation has a unique square-integrable solution.  The outcome bridge exists by the displayed witness formula and, equivalently, by inversion of the same finite conditional-expectation matrix; its coordinates vary continuously while the determinant stays away from zero.

Finally, finite support makes all relevant moments finite.  On the open set where the preceding determinants do not vanish, the bridge coordinates and therefore \(V_{E,d}\) and \(V_{O,d}\) are continuous rational functions of the cell probabilities.  The binary-witness stability lemma and membership of \(P_\epsilon\) in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\) give strict positivity of all four variance components at \(P_\epsilon\).  Their minimum consequently remains positive on a relative neighborhood.  Taking \(r_\epsilon^{\mathrm{reg}}\) smaller than the finitely many positivity, determinant, and variance-margin radii proves the claim.
