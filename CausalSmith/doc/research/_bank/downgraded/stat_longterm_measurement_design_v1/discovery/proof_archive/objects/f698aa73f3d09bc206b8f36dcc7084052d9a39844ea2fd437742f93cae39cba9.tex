Choose a singleton admissible family \(\mathcal D=\{d\}\).  For definiteness, take \(K=3\), let the three catalogue variables have roles one, two, and three under \(d\), set \(c_1=c_2=c_3=1\), \(\kappa=3\), \(b_E=1\), and \(b_O=2\).  These choices satisfy the catalogue-cost and cap restrictions.  Let \(X\) be constant.  In both sources let \(U\) be uniform on \(\{1,2,3\}\), let \(S_2\) be an independent Bernoulli random variable with success probability \(1/2\), and take \(P(G=E)=P(G=O)=1/2\).  In the experimental source let
\[
 P(A=1\mid U=u,G=E)=\frac12,
\]
whereas in the observational source let
\[
 P(A=1\mid U=u,G=O)=e_u,
 \qquad
 (e_1,e_2,e_3)=\left(\frac15,\frac12,\frac45\right).
 \tag{1}
\]
Conditional on \((S_2=s,U=u)\), generate \(S_1\) and \(S_3\) independently as Bernoulli variables having the common success probability \(p_{s,u}\), independently of \((A,G)\), where
\[
 (p_{0,1},p_{0,2},p_{0,3})
 =\left(\frac15,\frac12,\frac45\right),
 \qquad
 (p_{1,1},p_{1,2},p_{1,3})
 =\left(\frac3{10},\frac45,\frac25\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
Use these same variables in both treatment potential catalogues, so that
\[
 Z(a)=(S_1,S_2,S_3),
 \qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Define
\[
 Y(0)=S_3,
 \qquad
 Y(1)=S_3+\delta_{S_2}(U),
 \tag{3}
\]
where
\[
 \bigl(\delta_0(1),\delta_0(2),\delta_0(3)\bigr)
 =\left(4,-\frac{16}{5},1\right),
 \qquad
 \bigl(\delta_1(1),\delta_1(2),\delta_1(3)\bigr)
 =\left(-\frac{16}{5},-\frac8{25},1\right).
 \tag{4}
\]
Finally set \(Z=Z(A)\) and \(Y=Y(A)\).  This specifies a finite-support law.

We first verify the observed outcome bridge.  Direct multiplication using (1)--(4) gives, for each \(s\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \sum_{u=1}^3 e_u\delta_s(u)=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{u=1}^3 e_up_{s,u}\delta_s(u)=0.
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, for \(s=0\) the two sums are respectively
\[
 \frac45-\frac85+\frac45=0,
 \qquad
 \frac4{25}-\frac45+\frac{16}{25}=0,
\]
and for \(s=1\) they are respectively
\[
 -\frac{16}{25}-\frac4{25}+\frac45=0,
 \qquad
 -\frac{24}{125}-\frac{16}{125}+\frac8{25}=0.
\]
For \(A=1\), the first identity in (5) gives the zero numerator of
\(\mathbb E_P[\delta_s(U)\mid S_2=s,A=1,G=O]\), and the second gives the zero numerator after additionally conditioning on \(S_1=1\).  Subtracting the second numerator from the first gives the corresponding result for \(S_1=0\).  All conditioning cells have positive probability.  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\delta_{S_2}(U)\mid S_2,S_1,A=1,G=O]=0.
 \tag{6}
\]
For \(A=0\), equation (3) gives \(Y=S_3\) identically.  It follows from (3) and (6) that
\[
 h_d(s_3,s_2,a)=s_3
 \tag{7}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid S_2,S_1,A,X,G=O]
 =\mathbb E_P[h_d(S_3,S_2,A,X)\mid S_2,S_1,A,X,G=O].
 \tag{8}
\]

We next verify both completeness conditions.  Fix an arm \(a\) and put
\[
 w_{a,u}=e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}.
\]
Every \(w_{a,u}\) is strictly positive.  If a function \(v:\{1,2,3\}\to\mathbb R\) obeys
\[
 \mathbb E_P[v(U)\mid S_1,S_2,A=a,G=O]=0,
 \tag{9}
\]
then, for each \(s\), the equations obtained from \(S_1=1\) and \(S_1=0\), followed by their sum, imply
\[
 \sum_{u=1}^3 w_{a,u}p_{s,u}v(u)=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{u=1}^3 w_{a,u}v(u)=0.
 \tag{10}
\]
The omitted common factor \(1/3\) is positive.  Thus (10), for \(s=0,1\), is the matrix equation
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&1\\
 1/5&1/2&4/5\\
 3/10&4/5&2/5
 \end{pmatrix}
 \operatorname{diag}(w_{a,1},w_{a,2},w_{a,3})
 \begin{pmatrix}v(1)\\v(2)\\v(3)\end{pmatrix}=0.
 \tag{11}
\]
The determinant of the first matrix in (11) is \(-27/100\), and the diagonal matrix is nonsingular.  Therefore \(v=0\).  This proves early-proxy completeness.  Since \(S_3\) has exactly the same conditional Bernoulli kernel as \(S_1\), the identical calculation with \(S_3\) in place of \(S_1\) proves late-proxy completeness.

The observed bridge operator is also bijective.  Conditional on a fixed pair \((S_2=s,A=a)\) in the observational source, its matrix maps a function of the binary \(S_3\) to its two conditional means given the binary \(S_1\).  Write
\[
 q_{s,a,b}=P(S_3=1\mid S_1=b,S_2=s,A=a,G=O),
 \qquad b\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The corresponding two-by-two matrix has rows \((1-q_{s,a,b},q_{s,a,b})\), so its determinant is \(q_{s,a,1}-q_{s,a,0}\).  If \(\omega_{s,a}\) denotes the conditional distribution of \(U\) given \((S_2=s,A=a,G=O)\) and \(m_{s,a}=\mathbb E_{\omega_{s,a}}[p_{s,U}]\), conditional independence of \(S_1\) and \(S_3\) yields
\[
 q_{s,a,1}-q_{s,a,0}
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{\omega_{s,a}}(p_{s,U})}
 {m_{s,a}(1-m_{s,a})}>0.
 \tag{12}
\]
The inequality follows because \(\omega_{s,a}\) assigns positive mass to every \(u\), the three entries in each row of (2) are not all equal, and they lie strictly between zero and one.  Hence every block is nonsingular.  Since the domain and codomain are finite-dimensional spaces of the same dimension, \(T_d\) is bijective.  Equation (8) and bijectivity also show that (7) is the unique square-integrable observed outcome bridge.

The adjoint \(T_d^\star\) is bijective as well.  The source density ratio
\[
 \frac{p_P(S_3,S_2,X\mid A,G=E)}
 {p_P(S_3,S_2,X\mid A,G=O)}
\]
is finite and strictly positive on the finite support.  It therefore has a unique finite, hence square-integrable, inverse image under \(T_d^\star\).  Taking that inverse image as \(q_d\) verifies selection-bridge existence.

It remains to check that the constructed observed law lies in the full causal class.  Consistency holds by construction.  In the observational source, treatment depends only on \(U\), so \((Y(a),Z(a))\) is independent of \(A\) given \(U,X\); all propensities in (1) are strictly between zero and one.  In the experimental source, \(A\) is a fair coin independent of \((Y(a),Z(a),U,X)\).  The joint law of \((S_1,S_2,S_3,U,X)\) is source invariant, which gives external validity.  Both source-support ratios are finite because every latent and treatment cell has positive mass.  Conditional on \((S_2,U,X,G=O)\), the variable \(S_1\) is independent of \(S_3\), and \((Y(a),S_3)\) is a measurable function of \((S_2,U,S_3)\); hence the sequential-outcome restriction holds.  The completeness, bridge, and operator conditions were verified in (8)--(12), and all variables are square-integrable because the support is finite.

Finally, both score variances are finite and strictly positive.  For the experimental component, (7) gives a nonconstant Bernoulli bridge prediction in each randomized arm, so \(V_{E,d}>0\).  For the observational component, the residual is zero in arm zero and is \(\delta_{S_2}(U)\) in arm one.  The selection bridge cannot vanish on every treated \((S_2,S_1)\)-cell, because its image under \(T_d^\star\) is the strictly positive source density ratio.  On any cell where it is nonzero, every conditional latent state has positive probability and every value in (4) is nonzero.  Thus the treated observational score is nonzero with positive probability, so \(V_{O,d}>0\).  Finiteness again follows from finite support.  This verifies the nondegeneracy condition and therefore, by Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}regular\text{-}law\text{-}class}\), establishes
\[
 P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}.
 \tag{13}
\]

It remains to compute the two targets.  Under experimental randomization, (7) and treatment invariance of \(S_3\) imply
\[
 \mu_{d,1}=\mathbb E_P[S_3\mid A=1,G=E]
 =\mathbb E_P[S_3\mid A=0,G=E]=\mu_{d,0},
\]
and hence
\[
 \theta_d(P)=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=0.
 \tag{14}
\]
On the other hand, (3)--(4), the uniform distribution of \(U\), and the fair independent distribution of \(S_2\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \tau
 &=\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid G=O]
 =\mathbb E_P[\delta_{S_2}(U)]\\
 &=\frac16\left[
 \left(4-\frac{16}{5}+1\right)
 +\left(-\frac{16}{5}-\frac8{25}+1\right)
 \right]
 =\frac16\left(\frac95-\frac{63}{25}\right)
 =-\frac3{25}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{15}
\]
Equations (13)--(15) give the required witness.  Since membership in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{id}}\) requires \(\theta_d(P)=\tau\) for every admissible design, this witness is not in that subclass.  Therefore \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\not\subseteq\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{id}}\), and the observed bridge contrast cannot be identified with the long-term average treatment effect throughout \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).
