Put
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}.
\]
The pilot assumptions give \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  Let
\[
 \nu_0=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0.
\]
Since \(\underline v_m\downarrow0\), the event
\[
 \mathcal E_m=\{Z_m<\nu_0/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_0/2\}
 \tag{1}
\]
has probability tending to one.  On \(\mathcal E_m\), simultaneously for all \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(g\in\{E,O\}\),
\[
 \widehat V_{g,d}\ge V_{g,d}-Z_m>\nu_0/2>\underline v_m.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus the truncation in every pilot allocation is inactive on \(\mathcal E_m\).

For each \(d\), define
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}.
\]
The population variance pairs and, on \(\mathcal E_m\), the estimated variance pairs lie in a common compact rectangle contained in \((0,\infty)^2\).  The costs are positive and finite, and \(\mathcal D\) is finite, so the first derivatives of the finitely many maps \(A_d\) are uniformly bounded on that rectangle.  The mean-value theorem and (2) yield
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
 \le C Z_m
\]
on \(\mathcal E_m\), for a finite deterministic \(C\).  Hence
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}).
 \tag{3}
\]

Write
\[
 a_d=c_{E,d}V_{E,d},\qquad b_d=c_{O,d}V_{O,d},\qquad
 F_d(\alpha)=\frac{a_d}{\alpha}+\frac{b_d}{1-\alpha}.
\]
The budget-profile theorem gives
\[
 F_d\{\alpha_d^*(P)\}=\mathcal V_d^*(P),
 \qquad F_d'\{\alpha_d^*(P)\}=0.
 \tag{4}
\]
Finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) and strict positivity of every \(a_d,b_d\) furnish a deterministic \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).
\]
By (3), with probability tending to one all \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\) belong to \([a_0,1-a_0]\).  On this interval,
\[
 F_d''(\alpha)=\frac{2a_d}{\alpha^3}
 +\frac{2b_d}{(1-\alpha)^3}
\]
is bounded uniformly over the finite design family.  Taylor's theorem around \(\alpha_d^*(P)\), (3), and (4) therefore imply
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 \left|F_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m})-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2).
 \tag{5}
\]

For every \(x\ge2\),
\[
 0\le\frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x
 \le\frac2{x^2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
On the preceding interior event, both continuous source counts are bounded below by a positive constant times \(B\), uniformly in \(d\).  For all sufficiently large \(B\), applying (6) to both counts gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\Bigg|&B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}
 -F_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m})\Bigg|
 =O_P(B^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
The same event makes every floor count positive.  Its complement has probability tending to zero, so the stipulated zero-count convention does not alter the stochastic order.  Combining (5) and (7) proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1}),
\end{aligned}
\]
as claimed.  The proof also explains why \(\underline v_m\) disappears from the sharp rate: it is inactive on an event whose probability tends to one.
