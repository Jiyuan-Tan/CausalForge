By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}clw\text{-}population\text{-}design\text{-}rule}\), under its transported complete-randomization and arm-homoskedastic specialization the published population criterion is affine-linear residual variance divided by sample size. Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}clw\text{-}linear\text{-}cost\text{-}selector}\) maps the support only to \(M_d\) and imposes \(c(M_d,n)=nC_d\), so the continuous criterion is \(C_dr_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P)/B\).

We first compute both residuals. Since \(X\) has one-point support, \(X_{\mathrm{nr}}\) is zero-dimensional and \(\xi_d=(1,W_x,W_y)^\top\). Symmetry and conditional independence given \(U\) give
\[
 \mathbb E(U)=\mathbb E(W_x)=\mathbb E(W_y)=0,
 \quad \mathbb E(UW_x)=x,
 \quad \mathbb E(UW_y)=y,
 \quad \mathbb E(W_xW_y)=xy.                              \tag{11}
\]
Therefore the full affine Gram matrix, including its unique intercept, and the outcome cross moment are
\[
 \Gamma_d(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&xy\\0&xy&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det\Gamma_d(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=1-x^2y^2>0,
 \qquad g_d(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=(0,x,y)^\top.              \tag{12}
\]
The intercept coefficient is zero, and inversion of the lower \(2\)-by-\(2\) block gives
\[
 \beta_1=\frac{x(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2},
 \qquad
 \beta_2=\frac{y(1-x^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.                       \tag{13}
\]
The projection identity now yields
\[
 r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =1-(x,y)
 \begin{pmatrix}1&xy\\xy&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}
 \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.                        \tag{14}
\]
For the unrestricted risk, if \(r,s\in\{-1,1\}\), then
\[
 P(W_x=r,W_y=s\mid G=O)=\frac{1+xyrs}{4},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(U\mid W_x=r,W_y=s,G=O)=\frac{xr+ys}{1+xyrs}.
\]
Since \(U^2=1\), the conditional-mean projection gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_d(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 &=1-\frac{1}{2}\left\{
 \frac{(x+y)^2}{1+xy}+\frac{(x-y)^2}{1-xy}\right\}\\
 &=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}
 =r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon}).                 \tag{15}
\end{aligned}
\]
This equality has been proved only at the symmetric centers. Also, \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\) and \(Z(1)=Z(0)\), so the affine residual law is identical in the two arms of the transported randomized comparison. Thus the transported arm-homoskedastic condition holds; finite support gives all moment conditions; and (12) proves full-Gram admissibility.

We next verify regularity rather than assume it. All center latent cells are strictly positive. Consistency is built into the potential variables. In source \(E\), \(T\) is independent of \((U,Z(0),Z(1),Y(0),Y(1))\); in source \(O\), conditioning on \(U\) makes treatment independent of the potential variables, with treatment probabilities in \([3/8,5/8]\). The law of \((U,Z(0),Z(1),X)\) is source-invariant, and every required source-support ratio is finite. Conditional independence of the proxies given \(U\) proves the sequential-outcome restriction. For a design with early signal \(x\ne0\) and late signal \(y\ne0\), the two binary conditional-expectation matrices in the completeness restrictions have nonzero determinants proportional to \(x(1-1/16)\) and \(y(1-1/16)\), respectively. The designwise matrix mapping the late proxy to the early proxy conditional on an observational treatment arm has nonzero determinant proportional to \(xy(1-1/16)\). Hence both completeness conditions and bijectivity of \(T_d\) hold.

The displayed candidates in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}witness}\) solve the bridge equations. Indeed, conditional proxy independence gives
\[
 \mathbb E\{W_y/y\mid W_x,A,G=O\}
 =\mathbb E(U\mid W_x,A,G=O)=\mathbb E(Y\mid W_x,A,G=O).
\]
Writing \(t=2a-1\), direct Bayes calculation gives
\[
 \mathbb E(W_x\mid W_y=l,T=t,G=O)
 =x\frac{t/4+yl}{1+tyl/4}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E\left[\frac{16}{15}\left(1-\frac{tW_x}{4x}\right)
 \mathrel{\Big|}W_y=l,T=t,G=O\right]
 =\frac{1}{1+tyl/4},                                      \tag{16}
\]
which is exactly the experimental-to-observational density ratio for \(W_y\) conditional on the treatment arm. Thus the selection bridge also exists. The explicit variances computed below are finite and strictly positive. We have therefore verified every membership condition in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), as well as membership in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{CLW}}\). Finally, experimental randomization and \(\mathbb E(U)=0\) imply \(\mu_{d,a}=0\) for both arms, while \(Y(1)-Y(0)=0\). Hence both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau=0\).

Substitute \((x,y)=(\epsilon,y_\chi)\) for \(d_{\mathrm p}\) and \((x,y)=(1/2,1/2)\) for \(d_{\mathrm e}\). Equations (14)--(15) give
\[
 r_{d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{lin}}
 =R_{d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{3}{10\chi}
 \frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2(1-3/(10\chi))},
 \qquad
 r_{d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{lin}}=R_{d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{3}{5}. \tag{17}
\]
Since \(C_{d_{\mathrm p}}=5\chi\) and \(C_{d_{\mathrm e}}=5\),
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{3}{2}\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac{3}{2}<3=B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}.       \tag{18}
\]
The inequality is strict because \(0<y_\chi<1\). Equation (17) also gives the raw-risk chain in the statement. Hence both continuous selectors choose \(d_{\mathrm p}\).

For the integer selector, if \(B\ge2C_d\), then
\[
 0\le \frac{B}{\lfloor B/C_d\rfloor}-C_d
 \le\frac{2C_d^2}{B}.                                     \tag{19}
\]
Multiplication by \(r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})\) gives
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}^{\mathrm{int}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 \longrightarrow C_dr_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon}). \tag{20}
\]
The catalogue is finite and the gap in (18) is strict, so a finite \(B_0(P_{\chi,\epsilon})\) preserves the singleton integer selector for every larger budget. The primitive cost formulas also give
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =\frac{5(\chi-1)}2>0.                                    \tag{21}
\]

It remains to compute the source-score profiles. With \(t=2a-1\), \(h_d(W_y,a)=W_y/y\), \(\mu_{d,a}=0\), and \(p_{g,a}=1/2\),
\[
 \phi_{E,d}=\frac{2TW_y}{y},
 \qquad V_{E,d}=\frac{4}{y^2}.                            \tag{22}
\]
For the observational score,
\[
 \phi_{O,d}=2T\frac{16}{15}left(1-\frac{TW_x}{4x}\right)
 \left(U-\frac{W_y}{y}\right).
\]
Conditional on \(U\), the pair \((T,W_x)\) is independent of \(W_y\), and
\[
 \mathbb E\left[\left(1-\frac{TW_x}{4x}\right)^2\mathrel{\Big|}U\right]
 =\frac{1+14x^2}{16x^2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\left[\left(U-\frac{W_y}{y}\right)^2\mathrel{\Big|}U\right]
 =\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 V_{O,d}=\frac{64(1-y^2)(1+14x^2)}{225x^2y^2}.             \tag{23}
\]
At the two designs these identities become
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{40\chi}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14),              \tag{24}
\]
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}.
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) now gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot384/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25},                                        \tag{25}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*}{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*}
 =\frac{25}{3872}
 \left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3}}
 +\sqrt{\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}
 (\epsilon^{-2}+14)}
 \right\}^{2}.                                            \tag{26}
\]
Dropping the first nonnegative square-root term in (26) gives
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*}{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*}
 \ge\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14)
 >\frac{\epsilon^{-2}+14}{726}>1,                         \tag{27}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\). Thus \(D^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}\), and the named and raw-risk ratios both equal (26).

At every center \(p_{E,1}=p_{O,1}=1/2\). Hence
\[
 V_{g,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=\frac{1}{4}V_{g,d},
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^*=\frac{1}{4}\mathcal V_d^*,
 \qquad g\in\{E,O\}.                                    \tag{28}
\]
The common factor preserves the optimizer and cancels from every best-tie ratio. This proves the four ratio equalities and the IKM optimizer claim. For fixed \(\chi\), expansion of (26) gives
\[
 \rho(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 +O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}).                                  \tag{29}
\]

Finally we optimize the exact center ratio. Define the proof intermediates
\[
 A(\chi)=10\chi+1+\frac{3}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 D(\chi)=\frac{16}{75}+\frac{16}{15(10\chi-3)},
 \qquad C_0=\frac{25}{3872}.
\]
Equation (26) is equivalently
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho
 =C_0\left[
 \epsilon^2A(\chi)+D(\chi)(1+14\epsilon^2)
 +2\epsilon\sqrt{1+14\epsilon^2}\sqrt{A(\chi)D(\chi)}
 \right].                                                 \tag{30}
\]
Set \(\chi=k\epsilon^{-2/3}\). Locally uniformly for \(k\) in compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\),
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho
 =\frac{1}{726}+C_0\epsilon^{2/3}
 \left\{2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac{8}{75k}\right\}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3}).                                      \tag{31}
\]
The exact positive terms in (30) show that the rescaled objective is coercive when \(k\downarrow0\) or \(k\uparrow\infty\). Comparison with any fixed positive \(k\) also excludes the boundary \(\chi\downarrow1\) for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\). Thus the infimum is attained, all rescaled minimizers lie in one compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), and the locally uniform expansion applies to them. The bracket in (31) has derivative
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{32}{15}}k^{-1/2}-\frac{8}{75}k^{-2},
\]
which vanishes uniquely at
\[
 k_\star=\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15}.
\]
At this point
\[
 C_0\left\{2\sqrt{\frac{32k_\star}{15}}+\frac{8}{75k_\star}\right\}
 =\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}.
\]
This proves (9)--(10), including the assertion for every minimizing factor.
