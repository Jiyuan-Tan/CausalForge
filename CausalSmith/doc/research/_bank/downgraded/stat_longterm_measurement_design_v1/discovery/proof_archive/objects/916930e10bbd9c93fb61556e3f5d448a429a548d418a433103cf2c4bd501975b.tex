Fix \(P\) as in the theorem.  We first establish a deterministic-design-and-allocation expansion and then transfer it through the independent pilot.

\emph{Interior counts.}  For each \(d\in\mathcal D\), positivity of the costs and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) imply \(0<\alpha_d^*(P)<1\).  Since \(\mathcal D\) is finite, some \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) satisfies
\[
 2a_0\le \alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).                                    \tag{28}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1). \tag{29}
\]
Thus, with probability tending to one, \(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\), and both purchased source counts are bounded below by a positive multiple of \(B\).  Conditional on the pilot, source-arm positivity and the assumed independent and identically distributed sampling imply convergence of every empirical source-arm proportion to its positive population value.  A finite union bound over the experimental arms and the fixed observational fold-arm cells gives
\[
 P(\mathsf D_B)\longrightarrow1.                              \tag{30}
\]

\emph{Deterministic-path expansion.}  Take any deterministic path \((d_B,\alpha_B)\) such that \(d_B\in\mathcal D\) and \(|\alpha_B-\alpha_{d_B}^*(P)|\to0\), and let \(e_B,o_B\) be its floor counts.  By (28) and finiteness of the cost catalogue,
\[
 e_B\asymp B,\qquad o_B\asymp B.                             \tag{31}
\]
Write \(h=h_{d_B}\), \(q=q_{d_B}\), \(\Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-h\), and \(\Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-q\).

We use the following elementary arm-ratio expansion.  If \(W_1,\ldots,W_N\) are independent and identically distributed, \(p_a=P(A=a)>0\), \(N_a=\sum_i\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}\), and \(f\in L_2\), then
\[
 \frac1{N_a}\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}f(W_i)
 -E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}
 =\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{p_a}
 \bigl[f(W_i)-E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}\bigr]+o_P(N^{-1/2}).       \tag{32}
\]
Indeed, the centered numerator divided by \(N\) is \(O_P(N^{-1/2})\), while \(Np_a/N_a-1=O_P(N^{-1/2})\); their product is \(O_P(N^{-1})\).  Conditional on the training observations, the same calculation and conditional Chebyshev inequality show that a fold-external random function with conditional \(L_2\)-norm \(o_P(1)\) contributes \(o_P(N^{-1/2})\).  Because the observational partition is deterministic and balanced and \(L\) is fixed, replacing equal fold weights by observation weights changes the average by \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).

The observed outcome-bridge equation and the selection-bridge change-of-measure identity imply, for each arm and every square-integrable early-argument function \(u\) and late-argument function \(f\),
\[
 E_O\{u(S_{2d_B},S_{1d_B},a,X)(Y-h)\mid A=a\}=0,             \tag{33}
\]
\[
 E_E\{f(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}
 =E_O\{qf(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}.                 \tag{34}
\]
Consequently, conditional on the training observations, the nuisance drift in one arm is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &E_E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)
 +E_O\{(q+\Delta q_\ell)(Y-h-\Delta h_\ell)\mid A=a\}\\
 &\hspace{35mm}=-E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a).   \tag{35}
\end{aligned}
\]
The two terms linear in \(\Delta h_\ell\) cancel by (34), and the terms multiplying \(Y-h\) vanish by (33).

Since \(\Delta q_\ell\) is an \(L_d\)-function, conditional expectation and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B}
          \|T_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B},\\
 |E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|T_{d_B}^{\star}\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}
          \|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}.                   \tag{36}
\end{aligned}
\]
The first line uses the observational strong-selection and weak-outcome errors.  In the second line, the artificial-law norms of the source-defined functions \(T_{d_B}^{\star}\Delta q_\ell\) and \(\Delta h_\ell\) control their observational norms because (31) keeps the observational mass of \(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) bounded away from zero.  The four maintained rates and the product-rate assumption make (35) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed arms and folds.

The centered experimental nuisance perturbation is \(\Delta h_\ell\), whose conditional empirical fluctuation is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) by the artificial-law strong-outcome rate and conditional Chebyshev inequality.  The centered observational perturbation is
\[
 \Delta q_\ell(Y-h)-q\Delta h_\ell-\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell. \tag{37}
\]
The multiplier condition makes the \(L_2(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\)-norm of (37), after multiplication by \(\mathbf1\{G=O\}\), equal to \(o_P(1)\), so its centered foldwise empirical contribution is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Replacing population arm probabilities by empirical ones creates an \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\) factor.  For a nuisance term \(g_B\), the accompanying unscaled population error is bounded by
\[
 C|\widehat p_a-p_a|\,|P\{\mathbf1(A=a)g_B\}|
 \le C|\widehat p_a-p_a|\,\|g_B\|_2.                        \tag{38}
\]
The strong outcome rate handles \(g_B=\Delta h_\ell\); the multiplier condition handles \(q\Delta h_\ell\) and \(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\); and \(\Delta q_\ell(Y-h)\) has zero arm mean by (33).  Hence every denominator-replacement error is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Training outside the observational evaluation fold and training the outcome bridge only on observational data supply the conditional independence used here.

Applying (32)--(38) and contrasting the arms yields
\[
 \widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)
 =\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 +o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{39}
\]
This expansion is centered at the observed bridge contrast and uses no common-identification restriction.

The leading sums in (39) are independent and centered, with variances \(V_{E,d_B}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d_B}/o_B\).  Take any subsequence and then a further subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant, which is possible because \(\mathcal D\) is finite.  Put
\[
 s_{0,B}^2(d,\alpha_B)
 =B\left\{\frac{V_{E,d}}{e_B}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{o_B}\right\}.
\]
Nondegeneracy and (31) bound this variance above and away from zero.  After multiplication by \(\sqrt B/s_{0,B}\), the maximal coefficient of the independent triangular array is \(O(B^{-1/2})\), and the sum of variances is one.  For every \(\zeta>0\), the finite second moments of the two fixed-design source scores and the threshold tending to infinity give the written-out Lindeberg condition
\[
 \sum_j E\{X_{B,j}^2\mathbf1(|X_{B,j}|>\zeta)\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{40}
\]
The Lindeberg--Feller argument, obtained by the second-order expansion of the product characteristic function with its remainder bounded by (40), gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B}{s_{0,B}(d_B,\alpha_B)}
 \left\{
 \frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 \right\}\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                  \tag{41}
\]
Every subsequence has such a further subsequence, so (41) holds along the original deterministic path.  Equations (39) and (41) prove the deterministic-path studentized limit.

\emph{Transfer through pilot selection.}  From (29), there is a deterministic \(t_B\downarrow0\) along the joint sequence such that
\[
 P\{|\widehat\alpha_m-\alpha_{widehat d_m}^*(P)|>t_B\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{42}
\]
The deterministic-path conclusions are uniform over
\[
 \{(d,\alpha):d\in\mathcal D,
 |\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)|\le t_B\}.                            \tag{43}
\]
Otherwise, a deterministic violating pair could be chosen from (43) along a subsequence, contradicting the conclusion for every deterministic path.  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}independent\text{-}pilot}\) permits conditioning on \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\).  Thus (39)--(43) imply
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)
 -\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                            \tag{44}
\]

The floor-count conclusion of Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).             \tag{45}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 \le{}&\frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &+\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.      \tag{46}
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficients are \(O_P(1)\) by (28)--(29), and the maintained main-study variance rate is \(o_P(1)\).  Combining (30), (45), and (46) proves
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).        \tag{47}
\]
Since \(\min_d\mathcal V_d^*(P)>0\), equations (30), (45), and (47) also yield
\[
 P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad \widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\longrightarrow_P1.             \tag{48}
\]
Combining (44) and (48) proves
\[
 \mathsf T_B\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1),                \tag{49}
\]
including the total zero extension outside \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\).

On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the event \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\) is exactly \(|\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\).  The complement of \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\) has probability tending to zero by (48), and the standard normal distribution is continuous at both critical values.  Equation (49) therefore gives
\[
 P\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}\longrightarrow1-\eta.
\]
Every step used only the sampling structure and the explicitly listed nuisance, product, multiplier, pilot-variance, and main-study-variance conditions.  In particular, the conclusion targets \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\); it becomes a statement about \(\tau\) only on the separately defined common-identification subclass.
