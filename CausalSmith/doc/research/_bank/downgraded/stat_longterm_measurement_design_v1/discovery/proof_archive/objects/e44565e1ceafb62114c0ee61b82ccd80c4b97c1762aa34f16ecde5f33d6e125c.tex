Write
\[
 v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P),\qquad
 \widehat v_d=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*,\qquad
 v_*=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d,
\]
and choose any \(d^*\in D^*(P)\).  The definition of \(\varepsilon_m\) and the empirical minimizing property of \(\widehat d_m\) give
\[
 v_{\widehat d_m}
 \le \widehat v_{\widehat d_m}+\varepsilon_m
 \le \widehat v_{d^*}+\varepsilon_m
 \le v_{d^*}+2\varepsilon_m
 =v_*+2\varepsilon_m.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Since \(v_*\) is the population minimum,
\[
 0\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m.                \tag{2}
\]
If \(\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\), the definition of \(\gamma(P)\) and (2) imply
\[
 \gamma(P)\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m.        \tag{3}
\]
This proves the asserted event inclusion.  If \(D^*(P)=\mathcal D\), both the event on the left of that inclusion and, under the convention \(\gamma(P)=+\infty\), the event on the right are empty.  Otherwise, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) implies \(\gamma(P)>0\).

For the stochastic conclusions, put
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}.
\]
The pilot variance assumptions give \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  The budget-profile theorem and the pilot construction give
\[
 \widehat v_d=F_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d}),
 \qquad
 v_d=F_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d}),                                  \tag{4}
\]
where
\[
 F_d(x,y)=\bigl(\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}\bigr)^2.
\]
The nondegeneracy assumption, positivity of the costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) place all population pairs \((V_{E,d},V_{O,d})\) in a common compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\).  With probability tending to one, all estimated pairs lie in a slightly larger compact subset.  The gradients of the finitely many maps \(F_d\) are uniformly bounded on that set, so the mean-value theorem yields
\[
 \varepsilon_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat v_d-v_d|
 =O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).                                      \tag{5}
\]
Equations (3) and (5) prove
\[
 P\{\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\}
 \le P\{2\varepsilon_m\ge\gamma(P)\}\longrightarrow0     \tag{6}
\]
when some design is suboptimal, while the all-optimal case was handled above.

We next sharpen the allocation conclusion by showing that truncation is asymptotically inactive.  Let
\[
 \nu_0=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0.
\]
Because \(Z_m=o_P(1)\) and \(\underline v_m=o(1)\), the event
\[
 \mathcal E_m=\{Z_m<\nu_0/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_0/2\}
\]
has probability tending to one.  On \(\mathcal E_m\), for every \(d\) and \(g\in\{E,O\}\),
\[
 \widehat V_{g,d}\ge V_{g,d}-Z_m>\nu_0/2>\underline v_m,
\]
so \(\widehat V_{g,d}\vee\underline v_m=\widehat V_{g,d}\) simultaneously for the entire finite catalogue.  Define
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}.
\]
The same compact-set mean-value argument, now on \(\mathcal E_m\), gives the stronger rate
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=O_P(r_{V,m}).           \tag{7}
\]
In particular, (7) implies the weaker displayed rate \(O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m)\) in the theorem.

For completeness, the sharper realized-allocation loss also follows.  Finiteness and positivity give a constant \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0,
 \qquad d\in\mathcal D.
\]
By (7), with probability tending to one every \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\) lies in \([a_0,1-a_0]\).  On this interval the second derivatives of the finitely many profiles
\[
 \mathcal V_d(\alpha;P)
 =\frac{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{\alpha}
  +\frac{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{1-\alpha}
\]
are uniformly bounded, while
\(\partial_\alpha\mathcal V_d(\alpha_d^*(P);P)=0\).  Taylor's theorem and (7) therefore imply
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 \left|\mathcal V_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m};P)
       -\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|=O_P(r_{V,m}^2).              \tag{8}
\]
For \(x\ge2\),
\[
 0\le\frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x\le\frac2{x^2}.       \tag{9}
\]
On the same interior event, both continuous source counts are bounded below by a positive constant times \(B\), uniformly in \(d\).  Applying (9) to both counts and using the finite upper bounds on costs and variances gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\Bigg|&B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}\\
 &\hspace{18mm}-\mathcal V_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m};P)\Bigg|
 =O_P(B^{-1}).
\end{aligned}                                                 \tag{10}
\]
The integer counts are positive on this event for all sufficiently large \(B\).  Define the expression in (10) arbitrarily, say as zero, on the vanishing event on which some denominator is zero.  Combining (8) and (10) proves the sharper conclusion
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1}).                                     \tag{11}
\]
Since \(r_{V,m}=o(1)\) and \(\underline v_m=o(1)\), (11) implies the theorem's stated \(O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m+B^{-1})=o_P(1)\) bound.  The tie rule entered only through the empirical minimizing inequality in (1); the population consistency conclusion is therefore membership in the full optimizer set \(D^*(P)\), not convergence to a particular tie-broken member.
