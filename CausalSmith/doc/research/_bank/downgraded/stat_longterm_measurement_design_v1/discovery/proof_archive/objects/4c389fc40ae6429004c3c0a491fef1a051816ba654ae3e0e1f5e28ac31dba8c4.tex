By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}clw\text{-}population\text{-}design\text{-}rule}\), the transported balanced-treatment population criterion is affine-linear residual variance divided by sample size.  Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}clw\text{-}linear\text{-}cost\text{-}selector}\) maps support only to \(M_d\) and imposes \(c(M_d,n)=nC_d\), so its continuous value is \(C_dr_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P)/B\).

Because \(X\) is constant, \(X_{\mathrm{nr}}\) is zero-dimensional and \(\xi_d=(1,W_x,W_y)^\top\).  Symmetry and conditional independence given \(U\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E(U)=\mathbb E(W_x)=\mathbb E(W_y)=0,
 \quad \mathbb E(UW_x)=x,
 \quad \mathbb E(UW_y)=y,
 \quad \mathbb E(W_xW_y)=xy.                              \tag{11}
\]
Thus
\[
 \Gamma_d(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&xy\\0&xy&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det\Gamma_d(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=1-x^2y^2>0,
 \qquad
 g_d(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=(0,x,y)^\top.                    \tag{12}
\]
Inverting the lower \(2\)-by-\(2\) block gives an intercept of zero and
\[
 \beta_1=\frac{x(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2},
 \qquad
 \beta_2=\frac{y(1-x^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.                       \tag{13}
\]
The projection identity gives
\[
 r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 =1-(x,y)
 \begin{pmatrix}1&xy\\xy&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}
 \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.                        \tag{14}
\]
For \(r,s\in\{-1,1\}\), direct summation over \(U\) gives
\[
 P(W_x=r,W_y=s\mid G=O)=\frac{1+xyrs}{4},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(U\mid W_x=r,W_y=s,G=O)=\frac{xr+ys}{1+xyrs}.
\]
Since \(U^2=1\), conditional-mean projection therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_d(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 &=1-\frac12\left\{
 \frac{(x+y)^2}{1+xy}+\frac{(x-y)^2}{1-xy}\right\}\\
 &=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}
 =r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,epsilon}).                 \tag{15}
\end{aligned}
\]
This equality has been proved only at the symmetric centers.  Moreover, \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\) and \(Z(1)=Z(0)\), so the transported affine residual distribution is the same in both randomized arms.  Finite support gives the moment conditions, and (12) gives full-Gram CLW admissibility.

We verify causal regularity separately.  Every latent center cell is strictly positive.  Consistency is built into the potential variables.  In source \(E\), \(T\) is independent of \((U,Z(0),Z(1),Y(0),Y(1))\); in source \(O\), conditioning on \(U\) makes treatment independent of the potential variables, and the treatment probabilities lie in \([3/8,5/8]\).  The law of \((U,Z(0),Z(1),X)\) is source-invariant, all required source-support ratios are finite, and conditional proxy independence given \(U\) proves the sequential-outcome restriction.  For signals \(x,y\ne0\), the binary matrices in the early- and late-proxy completeness conditions have nonzero determinants proportional to \(x(1-1/16)\) and \(y(1-1/16)\); the matrix representing \(T_d\) has determinant proportional to \(xy(1-1/16)\).  Hence both completeness restrictions and bijectivity hold.

The bridge candidates in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}witness}\) solve their equations.  Conditional proxy independence gives
\[
 \mathbb E(W_y/y\mid W_x,A,G=O)
 =\mathbb E(U\mid W_x,A,G=O)=\mathbb E(Y\mid W_x,A,G=O).
\]
Writing \(t=2a-1\), Bayes' rule gives
\[
 \mathbb E(W_x\mid W_y=l,T=t,G=O)
 =x\frac{t/4+yl}{1+tyl/4},
\]
and hence
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \frac{16}{15}\left(1-\frac{tW_x}{4x}\right)
 \mathrel{\Big|}W_y=l,T=t,G=O\right]
 =\frac1{1+tyl/4}.                                        \tag{16}
\]
The last expression is the experimental-to-observational density ratio for \(W_y\) within arm \(a\), proving selection-bridge existence.  The explicit moments below are finite and positive.  Thus the center belongs to \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\cap\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{CLW}}\).  Finally, experimental randomization and \(\mathbb E(U)=0\) give \(\mu_{d,a}=0\) for both arms, while \(Y(1)-Y(0)=0\), so \(\theta_d(P)=\tau=0\).

Substitute \((x,y)=(\epsilon,y_\chi)\) for \(d_{\mathrm p}\) and \((x,y)=(1/2,1/2)\) for \(d_{\mathrm e}\).  Equations (14)--(15) give
\[
 r_{d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{lin}}=R_{d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{3}{10\chi}
 \frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2(1-3/(10\chi))},
 \qquad
 r_{d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{lin}}=R_{d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac35. \tag{17}
\]
Since \(C_{d_{\mathrm p}}=5\chi\) and \(C_{d_{\mathrm e}}=5\),
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac32<3=B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}.           \tag{18}
\]
The inequality is strict because \(0<y_\chi<1\).  Equation (17) also gives the risk chain in the statement, so both continuous selectors choose \(d_{\mathrm p}\).

The integer conclusion is stronger than the stated eventual result.  For every \(z\ge1\), with \(k=\lfloor z\rfloor\ge1\),
\[
 z<k+1\le2k,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \frac z{\lfloor z\rfloor}<2,   \tag{19}
\]
while \(\lfloor z\rfloor\le z\) implies \(z/\lfloor z\rfloor\ge1\).  If \(B\ge C_{d_{\mathrm p}}=5\chi\), both integer counts are positive and (17)--(19) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{int}}
 &<2C_{d_{\mathrm p}}r_{d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{lin}}<3,\\
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{int}}
 &\ge C_{d_{\mathrm e}}r_{d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{lin}}=3.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{20}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{CLW}}^{\mathrm{int}}=\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\) for every \(B\ge5\chi\), and the statement's threshold may be taken as \(B_0(P_{\chi,epsilon})=5\chi\).  The primitive costs also give
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =\frac{5(\chi-1)}2>0.                                    \tag{21}
\]

For the source-score moments, write \(t=2a-1\).  Since \(h_d(W_y,a)=W_y/y\), \(\mu_{d,a}=0\), and \(p_{g,a}=1/2\),
\[
 \phi_{E,d}=\frac{2TW_y}{y},
 \qquad V_{E,d}=\frac4{y^2}.                              \tag{22}
\]
The observational score is
\[
 \phi_{O,d}=2T\frac{16}{15}
 \left(1-\frac{TW_x}{4x}\right)
 \left(U-\frac{W_y}{y}\right).
\]
Conditional on \(U\), \((T,W_x)\) is independent of \(W_y\), and
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\left(1-\frac{TW_x}{4x}\right)^2\middle|U\right]
 =\frac{1+14x^2}{16x^2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\left(U-\frac{W_y}{y}\right)^2\middle|U\right]
 =\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 V_{O,d}=\frac{64(1-y^2)(1+14x^2)}{225x^2y^2}.             \tag{23}
\]
At the two designs,
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{40\chi}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}=
 \frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14),              \tag{24}
\]
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}.
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) now gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot384/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25},                                        \tag{25}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*}{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*}
 =\frac{25}{3872}
 \left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3}}
 +\sqrt{\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}
 (\epsilon^{-2}+14)}
 \right\}^{2}.                                            \tag{26}
\]
Dropping the first nonnegative square-root term yields
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*}{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*}
 \ge\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14)
 >\frac{\epsilon^{-2}+14}{726}>1.                         \tag{27}
\]
Hence \(D^*(P_{\chi,epsilon})=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}\), and the named continuous and raw-risk ratios equal (26).

At every center \(p_{E,1}=p_{O,1}=1/2\).  Thus
\[
 V_{g,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=\frac14V_{g,d},
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^*=\frac14\mathcal V_d^*,
 \qquad g\in\{E,O\}.                                     \tag{28}
\]
The common factor preserves the optimizer and cancels from all best-tie ratios.  For fixed \(\chi\), expanding (26) gives
\[
 \rho(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 +O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}).                                  \tag{29}
\]

For the optimization, define
\[
 A(\chi)=10\chi+1+\frac3{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 D(\chi)=\frac{16}{75}+\frac{16}{15(10\chi-3)},
 \qquad C_0=\frac{25}{3872}.
\]
Equation (26) is equivalent to
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho=C_0\left[
 \epsilon^2A(\chi)+D(\chi)(1+14\epsilon^2)
 +2\epsilon\sqrt{1+14\epsilon^2}\sqrt{A(\chi)D(\chi)}
 \right].                                                  \tag{30}
\]
Set \(\chi=k\epsilon^{-2/3}\).  Locally uniformly for \(k\) in compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\),
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho
 =\frac1{726}+C_0\epsilon^{2/3}
 \left\{2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac8{75k}\right\}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3}).                                      \tag{31}
\]
The positive terms in (30) make the rescaled objective coercive as \(k\downarrow0\) or \(k\uparrow\infty\); comparison with a fixed positive \(k\) excludes \(\chi\downarrow1\) for small \(\epsilon\).  Hence every rescaled minimizer lies in one compact subset of \((0,\infty)\).  The derivative of the bracket in (31) is
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{32}{15}}k^{-1/2}-\frac8{75}k^{-2},
\]
which vanishes uniquely at \(k_\star=\sqrt[3]{18}/15\).  At that point
\[
 C_0\left\{2\sqrt{\frac{32k_\star}{15}}+\frac8{75k_\star}\right\}
 =\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}.
\]
This proves (9)--(10), including the assertion for every minimizing factor.
