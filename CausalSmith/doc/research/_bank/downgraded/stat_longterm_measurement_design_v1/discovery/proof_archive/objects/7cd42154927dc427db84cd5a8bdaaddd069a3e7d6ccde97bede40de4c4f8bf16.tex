The center-law regularity, the bridge calculations, the strict optimizer assignments, and the variance and ratio expansions are furnished by \(\texttt{lem:binary-witness-stability}\).  It remains to make explicit why the comparison is causal, to pass from the center laws to \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\), and to verify the claimed open cost range.

First consider any regular law \(Q\) in the constrained fixed-system model used to define \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\).  In this model \(X\) and every \(S_{2d}\) are constant.  Define
\[
 m_a(u)=\mathbb E_Q[Y(a)\mid U=u,G=O],
 \qquad
 g_{d,a}(u)=\mathbb E_Q[h_d(S_{3d}(a),a)\mid U=u,G=O].
\]
The observed outcome-bridge equation, consistency, observational latent exchangeability, and the sequential-outcome restriction imply
\[
 \mathbb E_Q[m_a(U)-g_{d,a}(U)\mid S_{1d}(a),A=a,G=O]=0. \tag{1}
\]
The discrepancy in (1) is a square-integrable function of \(U\).  Early-proxy completeness therefore yields
\[
 m_a(U)=g_{d,a}(U)\quad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{2}
\]
Experimental randomization and short-term consistency replace the observed experimental distribution conditional on \(A=a\) by the distribution of \(S_{3d}(a)\).  External validity then transfers that potential short-term distribution to source \(O\).  Using (2),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_{d,a}(Q)
 &=\mathbb E_Q[h_d(S_{3d}(a),a)\mid G=E]\\
 &=\mathbb E_Q[h_d(S_{3d}(a),a)\mid G=O]\\
 &=\mathbb E_Q[g_{d,a}(U)\mid G=O]\\
 &=\mathbb E_Q[Y(a)\mid G=O].
 \end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\mu_{d,1}(Q)-\mu_{d,0}(Q)=\tau(Q)\) for both designs throughout this local model.  At a center law \(P_\epsilon\), \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), so both sides equal zero.  The binary-witness-stability lemma gives bridge uniqueness and operator bijectivity, and \(\texttt{lem:ikmw-fixed-system-efficiency}\) then makes the displayed source-gradient variance the local efficiency bound for this common causal functional.  Hence the unique design-value optimizer supplied by that lemma is genuinely the causal-efficiency optimizer, and the failure is not an identification failure.

For the stated costs the binary-witness-stability lemma gives
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}=6>5
 =c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}},
\]
the unique predictive optimizer \(d_{\mathrm p}\), the unique causal-design-value optimizer \(d_{\mathrm e}\), and
\[
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025},
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{4}
\]

For each fixed \(\epsilon\), the same lemma supplies a radius \(r_\epsilon>0\) on which regularity, the strict predictive ordering, the strict design-value ordering, and
\(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)>\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)/2\) persist.  The definition of \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) chooses \(0<\delta_\epsilon<r_\epsilon\).  Consequently every \(P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) is regular and satisfies
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
 \qquad
 D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}. \tag{5}
\]
Every center \(P_\epsilon\) belongs to its own relative-open ball.  Since the leading coefficient in (4) is positive,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
 \ge \sup_{0<\epsilon<10^{-3}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\infty. \tag{6}
\]

Finally, keep the signal law fixed and vary the cap-feasible positive costs.  Put
\[
 C_{\mathrm p}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}},
 \qquad
 C_{\mathrm e}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}.
\]
For proxy signals \(x,y\), Bayes' rule gives
\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l]=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel},
\]
and direct averaging over the four proxy cells gives
\[
 R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35.
\]
The strict predictive ordering is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{C_{\mathrm p}}{C_{\mathrm e}}
 <\frac{R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)}{R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)}
 =\frac{3(16-9\epsilon^2)}{35(1-\epsilon^2)}. \tag{7}
\]
The right side of (7) tends to \(48/35\).  Hence every fixed cost vector satisfying
\[
 1<\frac{C_{\mathrm p}}{C_{\mathrm e}}<\frac{48}{35}
\]
makes \(d_{\mathrm p}\) strictly more expensive and uniquely prediction-selected for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\).  For any such fixed positive costs, \(\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)\) remains bounded, whereas
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}\longrightarrow\infty
\]
by (4).  Since there are only two designs, \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is eventually the unique efficiency optimizer and the approximation ratio diverges.  All displayed inequalities are strict and depend continuously on the positive costs, so this range is relatively open in the cap-feasible cost region.  This proves every claim.
