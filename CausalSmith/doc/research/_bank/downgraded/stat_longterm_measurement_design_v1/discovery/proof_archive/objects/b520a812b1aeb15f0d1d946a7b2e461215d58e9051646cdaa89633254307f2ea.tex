Write
\[
 v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P),\qquad
 \widehat v_d=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*,\qquad
 v_* = \min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d.
\]
Choose any \(d^*\in D^*(P)\).  By the definition of \(\varepsilon_m\) and the empirical minimizing property of \(\widehat d_m\),
\[
 v_{\widehat d_m}
 \le \widehat v_{\widehat d_m}+\varepsilon_m
 \le \widehat v_{d^*}+\varepsilon_m
 \le v_{d^*}+2\varepsilon_m
 =v_*+2\varepsilon_m.                                           \tag{1}
\]
Since \(v_*\) is the population minimum, (1) proves
\[
 0\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m.                    \tag{2}
\]
If \(\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\), the definition of \(\gamma(P)\) and (2) give
\[
 \gamma(P)\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m.           \tag{3}
\]
Thus the asserted event inclusion follows.  If \(D^*(P)=\mathcal D\), both the nonselection event and, under the convention \(\gamma(P)=+\infty\), the event \(\{2\varepsilon_m\ge\gamma(P)\}\) are empty.  Otherwise finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) implies \(\gamma(P)>0\).

The pilot construction and the budget-profile theorem give
\[
 \widehat v_d=F_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d}),\qquad
 v_d=F_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d}),
\]
where
\[
 F_d(x,y)=\bigl(\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}\bigr)^2.         \tag{4}
\]
The nondegeneracy assumption, positivity of the costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) put the population arguments in (4) in a common compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\).  The uniform pilot error is \(O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\), so with probability tending to one the estimated arguments lie in a slightly larger such compact set.  The gradients of the finitely many maps \(F_d\) are uniformly bounded there.  The mean-value theorem therefore yields
\[
 \varepsilon_m
 =\max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat v_d-v_d|
 =O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).                                           \tag{5}
\]
Combining (3) and (5) proves
\[
 P\{\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\}
 \le P\{2\varepsilon_m\ge\gamma(P)\}\longrightarrow0.       \tag{6}
\]

For the allocation, define
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}.
\]
For \(x\ge0\) and \(V>0\),
\[
 |(x\vee\underline v_m)-V|\le |x-V|+\underline v_m.            \tag{7}
\]
Applying the same compact-set mean-value argument to the finitely many maps \(A_d\), using (7), gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m).                                   \tag{8}
\]
Finally, the second-order allocation-loss lemma applies under precisely the nondegeneracy, pilot-rate, and truncation conditions used above.  It gives
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|
B\left\{
\frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
+\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
\right\}-v_d\right|
=O_P\!\left((r_{V,m}+\underline v_m)^2+B^{-1}\right).          \tag{9}
\]
Because \(r_{V,m}=o(1)\), \(\underline v_m=o(1)\), and \(B\to\infty\), the right-hand side of (9) is \(o_P(1)\).  Equations (2), (3), (5), (6), (8), and (9) prove the proposed sharpened statement.  The proof uses the tie rule only to ensure that \(\widehat d_m\) is an empirical minimizer; the population conclusion is correctly membership in the entire set \(D^*(P)\).
