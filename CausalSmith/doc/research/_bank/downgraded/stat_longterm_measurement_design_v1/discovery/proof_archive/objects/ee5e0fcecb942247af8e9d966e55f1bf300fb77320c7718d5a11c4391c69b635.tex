Put
\[
 \theta_d(P)=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0},\qquad
 s_{0,B}^2
 =B\left\{\frac{V_{E,\widehat d_m}}{N_{E,B}}
          +\frac{V_{O,\widehat d_m}}{N_{O,B}}\right\}.
\]
The pilot-oracle-regret theorem gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).                   \tag{1}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and every population variance and cost is positive and finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0,
 \qquad d\in\mathcal D.                                        \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1) and (2) imply that, with probability tending to one, both selected source counts are positive and of order \(B\).  Applying the sharp uniform floor-count conclusion of the pilot-oracle-regret theorem at the random member \(\widehat d_m\in\mathcal D\) yields
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).                \tag{3}
\]

Next, the definition of \(\widehat s_B^2\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 &\le \frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
       |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &\quad+\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
       |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.
\end{aligned}                                                    \tag{4}
\]
The two count ratios in (4) are \(O_P(1)\), while the assumed uniform main-study variance error is \(O_P(r_{\widetilde V,B})=o_P(1)\).  Therefore (3) and (4) imply
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2=o_P(1),\qquad
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).          \tag{5}
\]
The finite-family nondegeneracy condition and positive costs also give
\[
 0<\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \le\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty.           \tag{6}
\]
Thus (3)--(6) justify taking square roots and replacing \(s_{0,B}\) by \(\widehat s_B\) in a studentized statistic.

We next prove the limit law.  Conditional on the independent pilot \(\mathcal P_m\), the selected design, allocation, and two integer counts are fixed, and the main-study observations retain their unconditional source laws.  Consider any deterministic sequence of pilot realizations satisfying
\[
 d_B\in\mathcal D,
 \qquad
 \alpha_B-\alpha_{d_B}^*(P)\longrightarrow0.                   \tag{7}
\]
Finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) permits passage to a subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant.  By (2), the two source sample sizes on that subsequence are proportional to \(B\), their ratio converges to a positive finite constant, and both diverge.  The four uniform nuisance-rate assumptions give the corresponding fixed-design weak and strong convergence conditions.  Moreover,
\[
 \min\{\delta_{h,B}\rho_{q,B},
        \rho_{h,B}\delta_{q,B}\}=o(B^{-1/2})
 =o\bigl((N_{E,B}+N_{O,B})^{-1/2}\bigr),                       \tag{8}
\]
because \(N_{E,B}+N_{O,B}\asymp B\).  The maintained moment, bridge, and overlap premises are those mapped explicitly in the fixed-design Imbens--Kallus--Mao--Wang limit lemma.  Applying that lemma to the observed bridge contrast identified by the designwise-gradient lemma gives, along every sequence satisfying (7),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-\theta_{d_B}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                              \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, the denominator in (9) equals \(\sqrt B\) times the square root of the exact two-source gradient variance.

It remains to pass from deterministic pilot realizations to the random pilot rule.  From (1), there exists a deterministic sequence \(a_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\left\{\max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|>a_m\right\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{10}
\]
Let \(f\) be bounded and continuous.  If the conditional convergence corresponding to (9) were not uniform over pilot realizations in the event complementary to (10), there would be a deterministic violating sequence of such realizations.  A constant-design subsequence would satisfy (7), contradicting (9).  Therefore, if \(Z_0\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\),
\[
\mathbb E\left[
 f\left(
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}
 {s_{0,B}}
 \right)\mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal P_m\right]
 -\mathbb E\{f(Z_0)\}\longrightarrow0                          \tag{11}
\]
uniformly on that event.  Boundedness of \(f\), (10), and iterated expectation prove the unconditional version of (9) with \(d_B=\widehat d_m\).  By (3)--(6) and Slutsky's theorem,
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-
 \theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{\widehat s_B}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                              \tag{12}
\]

Finally,
\[
 \{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}
 =\left\{
 \left|\frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-
 \theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{\widehat s_B}\right|
 \le z_{1-\eta/2}\right\}.                                    \tag{13}
\]
The standard normal law is continuous at \(\pm z_{1-\eta/2}\), so (12) and (13) imply that the probability of this event tends to \(1-\eta\).  If \(\theta_d(P)=\tau\) for every \(d\in\mathcal D\), then \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)=\tau\) identically.  Substitution in (12) and (13) proves the causal centering and coverage assertions.
