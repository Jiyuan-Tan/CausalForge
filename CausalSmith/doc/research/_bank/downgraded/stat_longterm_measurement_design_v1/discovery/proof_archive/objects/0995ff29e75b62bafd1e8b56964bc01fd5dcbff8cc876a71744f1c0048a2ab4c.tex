Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and the singleton design \(d\).  Put
\[
 \alpha=\frac{c_{E,d}n_E}{B}.
\]
Because \(B=c_{E,d}n_E+c_{O,d}n_O\) and both counts and costs are positive,
\[
 0<\alpha<1,
 \qquad
 1-\alpha=\frac{c_{O,d}n_O}{B}.
\]
Substitution into the design-value profile gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac1B\mathcal V_d(\alpha;P)
 &=\frac1B\left\{
 \frac{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{c_{E,d}n_E/B}
 +\frac{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{c_{O,d}n_O/B}\right\}\\
 &=\frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O}.
\end{aligned}
\]
By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}designwise\text{-}gradient}\), the last expression is the fixed-system two-source pathwise-gradient variance for \(\theta_d(P)\).  If \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{id}}\), the causal-identification premise gives \(\theta_d(P)=\tau\).  Under the additional fixed-system canonical-gradient premises, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}ikmw\text{-}fixed\text{-}system\text{-}efficiency}\) identifies the corresponding two-source canonical-gradient variance as the causal semiparametric efficiency lower bound.  Without those causal-identification and canonical-gradient premises, the displayed equality remains only the observable bridge-functional identity.
