For the transported Carneiro--Lee--Wilhelm population linear-risk rule under \(c(M_d,n)=nC_d\), there is no finite uniform bridge-design approximation guarantee.  For every prescribed \(\chi>1\), the binary witness has \(C_{d_{\mathrm p}}=5\chi\) and \(C_{d_{\mathrm e}}=5\).  Every center \(P_\epsilon\), \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), belongs to \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\cap\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{CLW}}\), both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau=0\), and
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{CLW}}(P_\epsilon;B)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
 \qquad
 D^*(P_\epsilon)=D_{\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_\epsilon)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}. \tag{1}
\]
The CLW affine linear residual equals the unrestricted unadjusted risk only at the centers, where
\[
 r_{d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_\epsilon)
 =R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{3}{10\chi}\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2(1-3/(10\chi))},
 \qquad
 r_{d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_\epsilon)
 =R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35,                   \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 <\frac32<3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B).       \tag{3}
\]
For each fixed center, the exact floor-budget selector also chooses \(d_{\mathrm p}\) for all sufficiently large \(B\).  Furthermore,
\[
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}+O_\chi(1),
 \qquad
 V_{g,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=\frac14V_{g,d}\quad(g\in\{E,O\}), \tag{4}
\]
so
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 =\rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 +O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}).                                  \tag{5}
\]
Across centers, every minimizing cost-disadvantage factor satisfies
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)
 \sim\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15}\epsilon^{-2/3},              \tag{6}
\]
and, for either named-rule ratio \(\rho\) in (5),
\[
 \epsilon^2\inf_{\chi>1}\rho(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 =\frac1{726}+\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}\epsilon^{2/3}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3}).                                      \tag{7}
\]
The radii defining \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) may be chosen so that every member remains CLW-admissible and regular, both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau\),
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{CLW}}(P;B)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
 \qquad D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\},
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}(P;B)=\infty. \tag{8}
\]
On \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\), additionally
\[
 D_{\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\},
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}}
 \rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P;B)=\infty.          \tag{9}
\]
At every fixed law in these balls, the integer-budget selector agrees for all sufficiently large \(B\).  The generic unrestricted-risk selectors choose the same center design and have the same center ratios, but are secondary objective-separation results and are not identified with the CLW linear rule off center.  For every \(\chi>1\), the center cost vector lies in \(\mathcal C_\kappa\), and a relatively open cost-manifold neighborhood preserves the strict cost and named-selector orderings.  The result concerns the transported CLW population criterion under the stated linear cost specialization; it does not refute CLW's randomized-trial optimality theorem, empirical equation~(3.6) procedure, approximate solver, arbitrary predictive methods, or any CLW choice of bridge roles.
