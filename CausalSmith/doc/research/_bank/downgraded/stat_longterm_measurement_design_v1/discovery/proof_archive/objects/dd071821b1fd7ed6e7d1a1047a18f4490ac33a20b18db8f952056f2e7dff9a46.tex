Put \(r=1/4\), and first consider a generic center-law design whose early and late proxies have signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\), respectively. Conditional on \(U\), the signed treatment \(T\), the early proxy \(W_x\), and the late proxy \(W_y\) are mutually independent, with conditional means \(rU\), \(xU\), and \(yU\). Since \(U\) is Rademacher, marginalizing over \(U\) gives \(p_{g,a}=1/2\) in both sources.

We first verify regularity. Fix \(T=t\). The two-by-two matrix of \(T_d\), with rows indexed by the early proxy and columns indexed by the late proxy, has determinant
\[
 \det(T_d)=\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}>0.                   \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, Bayes' rule makes this determinant the difference between the two conditional probabilities of \(W_y=1\), namely
\[
 \frac y2\left\{
 \frac{x+rt}{1+rtx}-\frac{-x+rt}{1-rtx}
 \right\}
 =\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}.
\]
For a single proxy \(W_s\), the determinant of the conditional-expectation map from functions of \(U\) to functions of \(W_s\), conditional on \(T=t\), is
\[
 \frac{s(1-r^2)}{1-r^2s^2}.                                \tag{2}
\]
It is nonzero for \(s=x\) and \(s=y\). Thus the early- and late-proxy completeness implications hold, while (1) proves designwise operator bijectivity.

The outcome bridge \(h_d(l,a)=l/y\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E[h_d(W_y,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O]=u
 =\mathbb E[Y\mid U=u,T=t,G=O].                            \tag{3}
\]
Bayes' rule gives the latent source-density ratio
\[
 L_t(u):=\frac{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=E)}{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=O)}
 =\frac1{1+rtu}.                                            \tag{4}
\]
The proposed selection bridge \(q_d(e,a)=\{1-r(2a-1)e/x\}/(1-r^2)\) obeys
\[
 \mathbb E[q_d(W_x,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O]
 =\frac{1-rtu}{1-r^2}
 =\frac1{1+rtu}=L_t(u).                                     \tag{5}
\]
Iterating (3) conditional on \((W_x,T,G=O)\) gives the observed outcome-bridge equation. Iterating (5) conditional on \((W_y,T,G=O)\), and using the source-invariant proxy kernel, gives the observed selection-bridge equation. The nonzero binary proxy-channel determinants make the latent bridges unique, and (1) makes their observed representations unique.

Every primitive cell probability is positive because \(r,x,y\in(0,1)\). Hence observational and experimental overlap and cross-source support hold. Consistency follows from the definitions of \(Y\) and \(Z\); treatment is independent of all potential variables given \(U\) in source \(O\), and is independent of them unconditionally in source \(E\). The joint potential short-term vector and \((U,X)\) are independent of \(G\). Conditional on \(U\), the pair consisting of \(Y(a)=U\) and the late proxy is independent of the early proxy, so the sequential-outcome restriction holds. Finite support supplies square integrability. Together with (1)--(5) and the positive variances computed below, these facts verify every member condition of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

The latent bridge calculation also verifies causal identification. Experimental randomization, source invariance, and (3) imply
\[
 \mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E[h_d(W_y,a)\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mathbb E[U\mid G=O].                                    \tag{6}
\]
The right-hand side does not depend on \(a\), while \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\); hence \(\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=0=\tau\).

We next calculate the two objectives. The best predictor of \(U\) from \((W_x,W_y)=(e,l)\) is
\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l]=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}.
\]
Averaging its conditional variance over the four proxy cells gives
\[
 R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.                   \tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35.                   \tag{8}
\]
The paired-source costs are \(6\) and \(5\), respectively, and hence
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 =\frac{42(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2}
 <3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B).       \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is the unique predictive optimizer despite its larger cost.

The experimental arm-normalized score reduces to \(2TW_y/y\), and hence
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}.                                      \tag{10}
\]
The observational arm-normalized score is
\[
 2Tq_d(W_x,A)\left(U-\frac{W_y}{y}\right).
\]
Conditional on \(U\), the squared residual has expectation \((1-y^2)/y^2\), independent of \((T,W_x)\). In addition, \(\mathbb E[TW_x\mid G=O]=rx\), so
\[
 \mathbb E[q_d(W_x,A)^2\mid G=O]
 =\frac{1-2r^2+r^2/x^2}{(1-r^2)^2}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 V_O(x,y)=
 \frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
 \left(1-2r^2+\frac{r^2}{x^2}\right).                     \tag{11}
\]
These quantities are strictly positive and finite for fixed \(x,y\in(0,1)\).

For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), substituting \((x,y)=(\epsilon,3/4)\) into (10)--(11) gives
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{64}{9},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025}.            \tag{12}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), substituting \((x,y)=(1/2,1/2)\) gives
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}.                       \tag{13}
\]
The budget-profile theorem and the costs \(c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=2\), \(c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=3\) imply
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot\frac{384}{25}}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}.                                         \tag{14}
\]
For \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\),
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 >\frac{1568}{2025\cdot10^{-6}}
 >\frac{3872}{25}.                                         \tag{15}
\]
Since \(\mathcal D\) contains only two designs, \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is the unique arm-normalized design-value optimizer. Expanding the squared profile using (12) yields
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{1568}{2025\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}).            \tag{16}
\]
Dividing (16) by (14) proves
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}).              \tag{17}
\]

It remains to prove stability without assuming nearby bridge existence. Fix \(\epsilon\) and parameterize the constrained fixed-support model by its finitely many free latent-cell probabilities. For a nearby law \(Q\), let \(K_{j,Q}\) be the two-by-two matrix with \((u,w)\) entry \(Q(W_j=w\mid U=u)\). At \(P_\epsilon\), the determinant of each early or late matrix is its nonzero proxy contrast, so all four matrices remain invertible on a sufficiently small relative neighborhood.

For a design \(d\), construct the nearby latent outcome bridge by
\[
 K_{3d,Q}h_{d,a,Q}=(u:u\in\{-1,1\})^{\mathsf T}.           \tag{18}
\]
Put
\[
 \ell_{a,Q}(u)=
 \frac{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=E)}{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=O)}
\]
and construct the latent selection bridge by
\[
 K_{1d,Q}q_{d,a,Q}=(\ell_{a,Q}(u):u\in\{-1,1\})^{\mathsf T}.
                                                                    \tag{19}
\]
Strict cell positivity makes the ratio finite. Conditional independence of the proxy coordinates given \(U\), followed by iterated expectation, makes (18)--(19) solve the two observed bridge equations. The observed matrix of \(T_d\) is a product of the two invertible proxy channels and positive diagonal reweighting matrices, so it is invertible and the observed bridges are unique.

The coefficients in (18)--(19) are rational continuous functions of the cell probabilities. Prediction risks are finite-cell conditional-variance functionals and are continuous while cells remain positive. Source-score second moments and the arm-normalized profiled values are continuous functions of the cell probabilities and bridge coefficients. At \(P_\epsilon\), (1)--(2), (9), and (10)--(15) give strictly positive regularity, variance, predictive-selection, and arm-normalized design-selection margins. Continuity therefore furnishes a radius \(r_\epsilon>0\) on which regularity and the same two strict optimizer assignments persist. The denominator of \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}\) is positive by (13)--(14), so the ratio is continuous. Shrinking \(r_\epsilon\) if necessary gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(Q)>\frac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
\]
throughout the stated relative ball. This proves every assertion of the lemma.
