Write \(r=1/4\), and first consider a design whose early and late binary-proxy signals are \(x,y>0\).  For either treatment sign \(t=2a-1\), Bayes' rule and conditional independence of the proxies given \(U\) give
\[
 \det(T_d)=\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}>0. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, conditional on the early proxy taking value \(e\) and on \(T=t\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[U\mid W_x=e,T=t,G=O]
 =\frac{xe+rt}{1+rxet},
\]
and the difference between the two rows of the binary channel from \(W_x\) to \(W_y\) has determinant (1).  Thus the conditional-expectation operator is bijective.  The binary channel from \(U\) to \(W_x\) is
\[
 \frac12\begin{pmatrix}1+x&1-x\\1-x&1+x\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is \(x\); the corresponding determinant for \(W_y\) is \(y\).  Conditioning on either treatment value only changes the strictly positive prior weights of the two values of \(U\), so both designwise completeness implications hold.  Direct conditional expectation verifies the bridges
\[
 h_d(l,a)=\frac{l}{y},
 \qquad
 q_d(e,a)=\frac{1-r(2a-1)e/x}{1-r^2}. \tag{2}
\]
For every fixed \(\epsilon>0\), these bridges are bounded.  All primitive cells have positive mass, so treatment overlap and source support hold.  The consistency, latent-exchangeability, randomization, external-validity, and sequential-outcome restrictions follow from the construction, and finite support supplies the required moments.  Equations (1)--(2), together with the positive variances calculated below, therefore show directly that \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\); this is also the center-law part of \(\texttt{lem:binary-witness-stability}\).

We next verify that the efficiency comparison is genuinely causal throughout the relevant local submodel, rather than only at the single center \(P_\epsilon\).  Let \(Q\) be any regular law in the fixed-system model in which \(X\) and every \(S_{2d}\) are constant; this model contains the constrained neighborhood used to define \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\).  For each treatment level define
\[
 m_a(u)=\mathbb E_Q[Y(a)\mid U=u,G=O],
 \qquad
 g_{d,a}(u)=\mathbb E_Q[h_d(S_{3d}(a),a)\mid U=u,G=O].
\]
Consistency and observational latent exchangeability convert the observed outcome-bridge equation into an equation involving the potential outcomes under treatment \(a\).  Because the sequential restriction makes both \(Y(a)\) and \(S_{3d}(a)\) conditionally independent of \(S_{1d}(a)\) given \(U\) when \(X\) and \(S_{2d}\) are constant, that equation is
\[
 \mathbb E_Q\!\left[m_a(U)-g_{d,a}(U)
 \mid S_{1d}(a),A=a,G=O\right]=0. \tag{3}
\]
The early-proxy completeness condition applied to the square-integrable function \(m_a-g_{d,a}\) now yields
\[
 m_a(U)=g_{d,a}(U)\quad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{4}
\]
Experimental randomization, consistency, and external validity then imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_{d,a}(Q)
 &=\mathbb E_Q[h_d(S_{3d}(a),a)\mid G=E]\\
 &=\mathbb E_Q[h_d(S_{3d}(a),a)\mid G=O]\\
 &=\mathbb E_Q[g_{d,a}(U)\mid G=O]\\
 &=\mathbb E_Q[Y(a)\mid G=O].
 \end{aligned} \tag{5}
\]
Consequently \(\mu_{d,1}(Q)-\mu_{d,0}(Q)=\tau(Q)\) for both designs and for every law in this local fixed-system model.  Since the bridge is unique by (1), the fixed-system efficiency result \(\texttt{lem:ikmw-fixed-system-efficiency}\) applies.  Equality (5) holds as a functional identity along the whole local model, so its pathwise derivatives agree in every local direction and the bridge-functional canonical gradient is also the causal-target canonical gradient there.  The profiled quantities below are therefore causal efficiency values, not merely variances for a coincidentally equal estimand at \(P_\epsilon\).  In particular, at the center \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), whence \(\tau=0\), and (2) gives \(\mu_{d,1}=\mu_{d,0}=0\).

For the prediction criterion, Bayes' rule gives, for \(e,l\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l,G=O]
 =\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}
 =\frac{x(1-y^2)e+y(1-x^2)l}{1-x^2y^2}. \tag{6}
\]
Thus the conditional mean is in the linear span of \(W_x,W_y\).  Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\binom{W_x}{W_y}\right)
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&xy\\xy&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}\bigl(U,(W_x,W_y)\bigr)=(x,y),
\]
so the conditional residual variance is
\[
 R(x,y)
 =1-(x,y)
 \begin{pmatrix}1&xy\\xy&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}
 \binom{x}{y}
 =\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}. \tag{7}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), \((x,y)=(\epsilon,3/4)\); for \(d_{\mathrm e}\), \((x,y)=(1/2,1/2)\).  Hence
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35. \tag{8}
\]
The paired-source costs are \(6\) and \(5\), respectively.  Therefore
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 =\frac{42(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2}
 <3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B), \tag{9}
\]
because (9) is equivalent to \(42(1-\epsilon^2)<48-27\epsilon^2\).  Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is the unique predictive optimizer even though it is more expensive.

It remains to compare efficiency.  Under \(G=O\), formula (2) gives
\[
 \phi_{O,d}=2Tq_d(W_x,A)(U-W_y/y).
\]
Conditional on \(U\), the late proxy is independent of \((T,W_x)\), and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(U-W_y/y)^2\mid U,G=O]=\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}.
\]
Moreover, \(\mathbb E_P[TW_x\mid G=O]=rx\).  It follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_E(x,y)&=\frac4{y^2},\\
 V_O(x,y)
 &=\frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
 \mathbb E_P[(1-rTW_x/x)^2\mid G=O]\\
 &=\frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
 \left(1-2r^2+\frac{r^2}{x^2}\right). 
 \end{aligned} \tag{10}
\]
Substitution gives
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{64}{9},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025}, \tag{11}
\]
whereas
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}. \tag{12}
\]
By \(\texttt{thm:budget-profile}\),
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot384/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}. \tag{13}
\]
For the prediction-selected design,
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge \frac72V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 \ge\frac{1568}{2025\epsilon^2}
 >\frac{3872}{25} \tag{14}
\]
when \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\).  Thus \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is the unique causal-design-value optimizer.  Expanding the profiled square in (10) yields
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{1568}{2025\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}).
\]
Dividing by (13) proves
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{15}
\]

For each fixed \(\epsilon\), \(\texttt{lem:binary-witness-stability}\) supplies a radius \(r_\epsilon>0\) on which regularity, the two strict optimizer assignments, and
\(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)>\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)/2\) persist.  The definition of \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) chooses \(0<\delta_\epsilon<r_\epsilon\).  Hence every law in \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) is regular and satisfies
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
 \qquad
 D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}.
\]
Every center belongs to its own relative-open ball, and (15) diverges as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).  Therefore
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\infty. \tag{16}
\]

Finally, keep the signal law fixed and vary the cap-feasible positive costs.  Put
\[
 C_{\mathrm p}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}},
 \qquad
 C_{\mathrm e}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}.
\]
By (8), the strict predictive ordering is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{C_{\mathrm p}}{C_{\mathrm e}}
 <\frac{R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)}{R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)}
 =\frac{3(16-9\epsilon^2)}{35(1-\epsilon^2)}. \tag{17}
\]
The right side tends to \(48/35\).  Thus every fixed cost vector satisfying
\[
 1<\frac{C_{\mathrm p}}{C_{\mathrm e}}<\frac{48}{35}
\]
makes \(d_{\mathrm p}\) strictly more expensive and uniquely prediction-selected for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\).  For any such fixed positive costs, \(\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)\) remains bounded, whereas
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}\longrightarrow\infty
\]
by (11).  Since there are only two designs, \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is eventually the unique efficiency optimizer and the approximation ratio diverges.  The inequalities are strict and their two sides are continuous in the positive costs, so this cost set is relatively open in the cap-feasible region.  This proves all the claims.
