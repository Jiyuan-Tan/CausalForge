Write all quantities at the selected pair \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\) unless a deterministic pair is displayed.  We first isolate a high-probability interior event.  The pilot-oracle-regret theorem and (15) give
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).                \tag{24}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and every cost and every source variance is positive and finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0,
 \qquad d\in\mathcal D.                                    \tag{25}
\]
Thus, with probability tending to one, \(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\), and both selected source counts are bounded below by a positive constant times \(B\).  The floor-count conclusion of the pilot-oracle-regret theorem, evaluated at the random member \(\widehat d_m\) of the finite family, therefore gives, with the extension in (19),
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).            \tag{26}
\]

We next prove the arm-normalized expansion directly.  Fix deterministic sequences \(d_B\in\mathcal D\) and \(\alpha_B\in(0,1)\) satisfying (3).  From every subsequence, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) permits extraction of a further subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant.  Along that subsequence,
\[
 \frac{e_B}{B}\longrightarrow\frac{\alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{E,d}},
 \qquad
 \frac{o_B}{B}\longrightarrow\frac{1-\alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{O,d}},
 \qquad n_B\asymp B.                                       \tag{27}
\]
Hence the two source probabilities of \(P_{B,d,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) are bounded away from zero.

For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), abbreviate
\[
 r_{E,a}=h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a},
 \qquad
 r_{O,a}=q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)
 \{Y-h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\}.                             \tag{28}
\]
The definition of \(\mu_{d,a}\) gives \(\mathbb E_P(r_{E,a}\mid A=a,G=E)=0\).  The observed outcome-bridge equation gives \(\mathbb E_P(r_{O,a}\mid A=a,G=O)=0\).  Both second moments are finite by nondegeneracy.

We use the following elementary random-denominator calculation.  If \((I_i,R_i)_{i=1}^k\) are independent and identically distributed, \(I_i\) is Bernoulli with mean \(p\in(0,1)\), \(\mathbb E(I_iR_i)=0\), and \(\mathbb E(I_iR_i^2)<\infty\), then, on \(\sum_iI_i>0\),
\[
 \frac{\sum_{i=1}^kI_iR_i}{\sum_{i=1}^kI_i}
 -\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{I_iR_i}{p}
 =\frac{(p-\overline I_k)\overline{IR}_k}{p\overline I_k}
 =O_P(k^{-1}).                                               \tag{29}
\]
Indeed, Chebyshev's inequality gives \(\overline I_k-p=O_P(k^{-1/2})\) and \(\overline{IR}_k=O_P(k^{-1/2})\), while \(\overline I_k\) is bounded away from zero with probability tending to one.  Applying (29) to the oracle experimental arm mean and separately in each of the fixed \(L\) observational folds gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)-\mu_{d,a}
 &=\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{p_{E,a}}r_{E,a}(O_i)\\
 &\quad+\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{p_{O,a}}r_{O,a}(O_i)
 +o_P(B^{-1/2}).
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{30}
\]
Here each fold has size \(o_B/L+O(1)\); replacing the resulting fold coefficient by \(o_B^{-1}\) is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Experimental and observational treatment overlap and (27) imply that all arm denominators used in this calculation are positive with probability tending to one.

It remains to compare (11) with its oracle version.  For fold \(\ell\), write \(\Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d\) and \(\Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d\).  After replacing the empirical arm proportions by their population values, the conditional population contribution of fold \(\ell\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=E)\\
 &\quad+\mathbb E_P\{\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_d)-q_d\Delta h_\ell
                -\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O\}.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{31}
\]
The selection-bridge equation and change of measure imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=E)
 =\mathbb E_P(q_d\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O).                \tag{32}
\]
Because \(\Delta q_\ell\) is measurable with respect to \((S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X)\), the outcome-bridge equation implies
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_d)\mid A=a,G=O\}=0.         \tag{33}
\]
Consequently (31) equals \(-\mathbb E_P(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)\).  Iterated expectation in either direction and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathbb E_P(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)|
 \le C_a\min\{&
 \|T_d^\star\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 \|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ},\\
 &\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 \|T_d\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}\}.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{34}
\]
where \(C_a<\infty\) depends only on the fixed arm probabilities and on the source weights bounded away from zero in (27).  Conditions (4)--(6) make (34) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed folds.

Conditional on the training observations named in the statement, the evaluation observations in each relevant source or observational fold are independent of their fitted functions.  Conditional Chebyshev inequality therefore bounds each centered empirical nuisance average by its \(L_2(P_{B,d,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm divided by the square root of the relevant source or fold size.  The strong outcome rate in (4) controls the experimental \(\Delta h_\ell\) term, and (7) controls the three observational multiplier terms.  Since these norms are \(o_P(1)\) and all evaluation sizes are of order \(B\), their centered empirical averages are \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  The same bounds show that each nuisance empirical arm mean is \(o_P(1)\).  Its product with the \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\) arm-proportion error from (29) is therefore \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Combining this calculation with (31)--(34) yields
\[
 \widehat\mu_{B,d}(a)-\widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)=o_P(B^{-1/2}). \tag{35}
\]

Subtracting (30) and (35) at \(a=0\) from those at \(a=1\), and using (1), proves the fixed-path expansion
\[
 \widehat\tau_B(d,\alpha_B)-\tau
 =\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d}(O_i)
  +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d}(O_i)
  +o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                           \tag{36}
\]
Thus neither the random treatment-arm denominators nor the weak--strong remainder has been assumed as a premise.

The two leading sums in (36) are independent, centered, and have variances \(V_{E,d}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d}/o_B\).  If \(\lambda_d=\lim e_B/o_B\), then the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 n_B\left(\frac{V_{E,d}}{e_B}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{o_B}\right)
 &=\left(1+\frac{o_B}{e_B}\right)V_{E,d}
  +\left(1+\frac{e_B}{o_B}\right)V_{O,d}\\
 &\longrightarrow
 \frac{1+\lambda_d}{\lambda_d}V_{E,d}
 +(1+\lambda_d)V_{O,d}.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{37}
\]
For completeness, finite second moments suffice for the central limit step.  If \(R\) is centered with variance \(\sigma^2<\infty\), dominated convergence applied to the second-order expansion of \(e^{itR/\sqrt{k}}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E e^{itR/\sqrt{k}}
 =1-\frac{t^2\sigma^2}{2k}+o(k^{-1}).                      \tag{38}
\]
Raising (38) to the \(k\)-th power yields the Gaussian characteristic function.  Applying this argument to the two independent source sums, with their deterministic relative weights from (27), proves from (36)--(37) that
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\tau\}}
 {\left[B\{V_{E,d_B}/e_B+V_{O,d_B}/o_B\}\right]^{1/2}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)                           \tag{39}
\]
along every deterministic sequence satisfying (3).  Equations (36)--(39) use the exact arm-normalized scores \(\phi_{E,d}\) and \(\phi_{O,d}\); the differently normalized moments in the cited fixed-design theorem are not substituted for \(V_{E,d}\) or \(V_{O,d}\).

We now lift (39) through the independent pilot.  From (24), choose a deterministic sequence \(b_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\left\{\max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|>b_m\right\}
 \longrightarrow0,                                        \tag{40}
\]
and call the complementary event \(\mathsf H_m\).  Conditional on any pilot realization in \(\mathsf H_m\), the main-study law is a deterministic design-and-allocation law of the type considered above.  The convergence in (39) is uniform over these realizations.  Otherwise, there would be a positive constant, a subsequence, and one violating deterministic pilot realization at each index.  The associated designs and allocations satisfy (3); extracting a constant-design subsequence and applying (39) would force their bounded-Lipschitz distances from \(\mathcal N(0,1)\) to zero, a contradiction.  Pilot independence permits integration of these conditional distances, while the contribution of \(\mathsf H_m^c\) is bounded by its probability.  Hence
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1),                          \tag{41}
\]
where the stipulated extension is used off the positive-source-count event.

We next prove totality.  On the interior event from (24)--(25), both source counts are at least \(cB\) for some \(c>0\).  Put \(p_{g,a}=P(A=a\mid G=g)\).  Experimental overlap gives \(p_{E,a}>0\).  Observational latent overlap gives \(p_{O,a}>0\) by taking expectations.  Conditional on the pilot, the probability that experimental arm \(a\) is empty is at most \((1-p_{E,a})^{cB}\).  Every observational fold has at least \(cB/L-O(1)\) units, so the probability that its arm \(a\) is empty is at most \((1-p_{O,a})^{cB/L-O(1)}\).  There are only \(2+2L\) such events.  A finite union bound, together with (24), therefore shows
\[
 P\left(
 \{N_{E,B}\ge1,N_{O,B}\ge1\}
 \cap\bigcap_a\{e_{B,a}\ge1\}
 \cap\bigcap_{\ell,a}\{o_{B,\ell,a}\ge1\}
 \right)\longrightarrow1.                                 \tag{42}
\]
These are exactly all denominators in (9)--(14).

On the event in (42),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 &\le\frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &\quad+\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{43}
\]
The two count ratios are \(O_P(1)\) by (24)--(25), and (16) makes the uniform variance error \(o_P(1)\).  Thus, using (26),
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2=o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).      \tag{44}
\]
Nondegeneracy, positive costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) imply
\[
 0<\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \le\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty.        \tag{45}
\]
Equations (42)--(45) show that \(\widehat s_B^2>0\) with probability tending to one.  Therefore \(P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\to1\), proving (20), and (26) and (44) prove (21).  On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the nonnegative roots satisfy \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  Since every statistic has a fixed extension on the complement and that complement has vanishing probability, Slutsky's identity applied to (41) proves (22).

Finally, on \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\),
\[
 \{\tau\in I_B\}
 =\left\{\left|
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{\widehat s_B}
 \right|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\right\}.                          \tag{46}
\]
The standard normal distribution is continuous at the two critical values.  Equations (20), (22), and (46) therefore give \(P\{\tau\in I_B\}\to1-\eta\), as claimed.
