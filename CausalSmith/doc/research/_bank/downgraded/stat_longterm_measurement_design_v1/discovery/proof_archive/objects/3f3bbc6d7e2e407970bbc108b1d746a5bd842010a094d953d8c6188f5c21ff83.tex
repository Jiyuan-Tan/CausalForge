Long-term data fusion requires choosing which short-term outcomes to measure, assigning their sequential proxy roles, and allocating a fixed budget between randomized short-term units and observational long-term follow-up.  This note makes that measurement design the argument of the two-source bridge score profile.  For the source-faithful Carneiro--Lee--Wilhelm affine-linear specialization, an explicit regular two-design family shows that the exact integer-budget population rule selects \(d_{\mathrm p}\) for every \(B\ge5\chi\), while both the arm-normalized and IKM-normalized bridge profiles select \(d_{\mathrm e}\).  The corresponding integer-selector ratios are unbounded on relatively open common-identification regions; at fixed \(\chi\) their center ratio has leading coefficient \((5\chi+1)/\{363(10\chi-3)\}\), and optimization over jointly feasible \((\chi,B)\) gives the sharp \(1/726\) elbow and cube-root cost scale.  An exact finite-catalogue pilot OLS minimizer corresponding to Carneiro--Lee--Wilhelm equation~(3.6) is root-\(m\) risk consistent yet selects the same inefficient design along the binary triangular path.  The positive prescription is to enumerate the observable bridge design value from a full-catalogue pilot, use the profiled square-root source allocation, and conduct selected-design-only Wald inference under the declared learner and variance conditions.
