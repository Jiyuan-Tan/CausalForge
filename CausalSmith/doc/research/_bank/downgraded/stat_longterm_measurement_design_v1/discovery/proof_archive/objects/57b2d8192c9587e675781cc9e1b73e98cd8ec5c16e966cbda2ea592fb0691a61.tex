Write \(x=\epsilon\) and \(y=y_\chi\).  The center construction has strictly positive finite cells.  Long- and short-term consistency hold by construction; treatment is randomized in source \(E\); latent exchangeability and overlap hold in source \(O\); and the conditional proxy kernels are invariant across sources and treatments.  Conditional independence of the early and late proxies given \(U\) gives the sequential-outcome restriction.  For a binary proxy \(W_s\), the two conditional laws given \(U=1\) and \(U=-1\) differ whenever \(s\ne0\); hence the associated two-by-two conditional-expectation matrix has nonzero determinant.  Since \(x,y,1/2\ne0\), both completeness conditions and both designwise operator-bijectivity conditions hold.  The displayed bridges in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}witness}\) solve the observed equations and have finite, strictly positive score variances.  Thus \(P_{\chi,\epsilon}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

Because \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), \(\tau=0\).  For the displayed outcome bridge, \(h_d(W_y,a)=W_y/y\) and hence \(\mathbb E_P\{h_d(W_y,a)\mid U\}=U\).  External validity and experimental randomization imply \(\mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E_P(U)=0\) at the balanced center, so both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau\).

For a generic early--late signal pair \((x,y)\), Bayes' rule gives
\[
 P(W_x=e,W_y=l)=\frac{1+xyel}{4},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l)=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}.
\]
Summing the four conditional variances gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R(x,y)
 &=\sum_{e,l\in\{-1,1\}}\frac{1+xyel}{4}
 \left\{1-\left(\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}\right)^2\right\}\\
 &=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using \(x=\epsilon\) and \(y_\chi^2=1-3/(10\chi)\) yields
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{3}{10\chi}\frac{1-\epsilon^2}
 {1-\epsilon^2(1-3/(10\chi))}.
\]
The denominator of the final fraction minus its numerator is \(3\epsilon^2/(10\chi)>0\), so the fraction is strictly less than one.  At \(d_{\mathrm e}\), direct substitution gives
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{(1-1/4)^2}{1-1/16}=\frac35.
\]
This proves (1).

Let \(m_0=\mathbb E(Y\mid X,Z_{M_d},G=O)\) and \(m_A=\mathbb E(Y\mid A,X,Z_{M_d},G=O)\).  Expanding squares and using \(\mathbb E(Y-m_A\mid A,X,Z_{M_d},G=O)=0\) gives
\[
 R_d(P)-R_d^A(P)=\mathbb E_P\{(m_A-m_0)^2\mid G=O\}\ge0.                     \tag{11}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), \(X\) is constant and \((T,W_{1/2}^{(1)},W_{1/2}^{(2)})\) are three conditionally independent binary signals of \(U\), with signal strengths \((1/4,1/2,1/2)\).  Enumerating their eight cells gives the following three symmetry classes for the cell probability, the absolute posterior mean of \(U\), and the total contribution to \(\mathbb E\{\operatorname{Var}(U\mid T,W_{1/2}^{(1)},W_{1/2}^{(2)})\}\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{multiplicity}&P(\text{cell})&|\mathbb E(U\mid\text{cell})|&
\text{class contribution}\\ \hline
2&3/16&7/8&90/1024\\
4&3/32&1/4&360/1024\\
2&1/8&11/16&135/1024.
\end{array}
\]
Their sum is \(585/1024\).  Combining this identity with (11), (1), and \(3/10<585/1024\) proves (2) and both selector equalities in (3).

For a design with early signal \(x\) and late signal \(y\), \(h(W_y,a)=W_y/y\) satisfies the outcome-bridge equation because \(\mathbb E(W_y/y\mid U)=U=Y\) and \(W_y\) is conditionally independent of \((W_x,T)\) given \(U\).  For the proposed selection bridge,
\[
 \mathbb E\{q_d(W_x,a)\mid U,T\}
 =\frac{16}{15}\left(1-\frac{TU}{4}\right)
 =\frac1{1+TU/4},
\]
which is the experimental-to-observational latent density ratio conditional on \(T\).  Conditioning this identity on \((W_y,T)\) and applying Bayes' rule gives the displayed observed selection-bridge equation.  Thus the bridge formulas used below have been verified from the finite-cell law.  At the balanced experimental center,
\[
 \phi_{E,d}=2T\frac{W_y}{y},
 \qquad V_{E,d}=\frac4{y^2}.                                                    \tag{12}
\]
In source \(O\),
\[
 q_d(W_x,a)=\frac{16}{15}\left(1-\frac{TW_x}{4x}\right).
\]
Conditional on \(U\), the residual \(U-W_y/y\) has mean zero and variance \((1-y^2)/y^2\), while \(\mathbb E_P(TW_x\mid G=O)=x/4\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_P(q_d^2\mid G=O)=\frac{16(1+14x^2)}{225x^2},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d}=\frac{64(1-y^2)(1+14x^2)}{225x^2y^2}.                 \tag{13}
\]
Equations (12)--(13) give (5), with \(V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16\) and \(V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=384/25\).  The cost formulas give (4).  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot384/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}.
\]
Substituting the costs and (5) yields the braces-squared expression in (6) as the quotient \(\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*/\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*\).  Dropping its first nonnegative square-root term gives
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})}
 {\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})}
 \ge \frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14)
 >\frac{\epsilon^{-2}+14}{726},                             \tag{14}
\]
because \((5\chi+1)/(10\chi-3)>1/2\).  Since the last bound exceeds one, \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is the unique optimizer.  Both prediction selectors equal \(\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\), so their arm-normalized ratios coincide with this quotient.  Treatment is balanced in both sources at every center, so \(V_{g,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=V_{g,d}/4\).  Every normalized profile is one quarter of the arm-normalized profile, the optimizer remains \(d_{\mathrm e}\), and the two normalized ratios also coincide.  This proves (3), (6), and (7).

It remains to optimize over \(\chi\).  Put
\[
 A(\chi)=\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 D(\chi)=\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)},
 \qquad C_0=\frac{25}{3872}.
\]
The exact ratio implies
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =C_0\left[\epsilon^2A(\chi)+D(\chi)(1+14\epsilon^2)
 +2\epsilon\sqrt{1+14\epsilon^2}\sqrt{A(\chi)D(\chi)}\right],               \tag{15}
\]
where
\[
 A(\chi)=10\chi+1+\frac3{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 D(\chi)=\frac{16}{75}+\frac{16}{15(10\chi-3)}.                              \tag{16}
\]
The objective is continuous on \((1,\infty)\) and diverges as \(\chi\to\infty\).  A candidate \(\chi=k\epsilon^{-2/3}\) has leading limit \(C_0(16/75)=1/726\), whereas bounded \(\chi\) has the strictly larger leading constant \(C_0D(\chi)\).  Therefore the infimum is attained at an interior point for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), and every minimizer diverges.

Writing \(t=\epsilon^{2/3}\) and \(\chi=k/t\), (15)--(16) give, locally uniformly for \(k\in(0,\infty)\),
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho(P_{k/t,\epsilon})
 =\frac1{726}+C_0t\left\{2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac8{75k}\right\}+o(t).     \tag{17}
\]
The rescaled minimizers remain in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\): after division by \(t\), the positive excess diverges like \(8/(75k)\) as \(k\downarrow0\), and like \(2\sqrt{32k/15}\) as \(k\to\infty\).  For
\[
 q(k)=2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac8{75k}
\]
we have
\[
 q'(k)=\sqrt{\frac{32}{15}}k^{-1/2}-\frac8{75}k^{-2},
\]
which changes sign exactly once, at \(k_\star=\sqrt[3]{18}/15\).  Local uniformity and compactness yield (8).  Finally \(C_0q(k_\star)=5\sqrt[3]{12}/968\), proving (9)--(10) for all four ratios.
