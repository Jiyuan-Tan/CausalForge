The paper derives the optimal source allocation and profiled design value, shows that a full-catalogue pilot consistently selects the oracle set with sharp second-order allocation loss, and supplies post-selection inference for the selected bridge contrast, becoming inference for the causal effect under common identification. Its negative result is maximal over finite cost disadvantages: for every \(\chi>1\), the cost-aware predictive optimizer can cost \(\chi\) times as much at the center laws while both the arm-normalized and balanced-center IKM-normalized efficiency ratios diverge. The exact finite-catalogue constant is therefore only a law-specific enumeration; no primitive uniform predictive guarantee follows from catalogue finiteness.
