Fix \(\chi>1\) and write \(r=1/4\). Then \(y_\chi^2=1-3/(10\chi)\in(0,1)\). Consider first any center-law design whose early and late proxies have signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\). Conditional on the Rademacher variable \(U\), the signed treatment \(T\), early proxy \(W_x\), and late proxy \(W_y\) are mutually independent, with conditional means \(rU,xU,yU\), respectively. Treatment is balanced marginally in both sources, so \(p_{g,a}=1/2\).

Fix \(T=t\). Bayes' rule gives the determinant of the two-by-two matrix representing \(T_d\), with rows indexed by \(W_x\) and columns by \(W_y\), as
\[
 \det(T_d)
 =\frac y2\left\{
 \frac{x+rt}{1+rtx}-\frac{-x+rt}{1-rtx}\right\}
 =\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}>0. \tag{1}
\]
For a single proxy \(W_s\), the determinant of the conditional-expectation map from functions of \(U\) to functions of \(W_s\), conditional on \(T=t\), is
\[
 \frac{s(1-r^2)}{1-r^2s^2}\ne0. \tag{2}
\]
Thus both completeness implications and operator bijectivity hold for both designs.

The proposed bridge \(h_d(l,a)=l/y\) obeys
\[
 \mathbb E\{h_d(W_y,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O\}=u
 =\mathbb E(Y\mid U=u,T=t,G=O). \tag{3}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \frac{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=E)}{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=O)}
 =\frac1{1+rtu}, \tag{4}
\]
and
\[
 q_d(e,a)=\frac{1-r(2a-1)e/x}{1-r^2}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E\{q_d(W_x,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O\}
 =\frac{1-rtu}{1-r^2}=\frac1{1+rtu}. \tag{5}
\]
Iterated expectation proves both observed bridge equations. The nonzero determinants give uniqueness and bijectivity. Strict cell positivity gives both treatment overlaps and source support. Consistency, latent exchangeability, experimental randomization, external validity, and the sequential restriction follow from the stated factorization and conditional independence. Finite support gives every required moment. The positive variances computed below verify nondegeneracy. Hence \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

Experimental randomization and (3) yield
\[
 \mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E\{h_d(W_y,a)\mid A=a,G=E\}=\mathbb E(U)=0, \tag{6}
\]
independently of \(a\). Since \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), both bridge contrasts equal \(0=\tau\).

The conditional mean of \(U\) given \((W_x,W_y)=(e,l)\) is
\[
 \mathbb E(U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l)=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}.
\]
Averaging its conditional variance over the four cells gives
\[
 R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}. \tag{7}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), \((x,y)=(\epsilon,y_\chi)\), and for \(d_{\mathrm e}\), \((x,y)=(1/2,1/2)\). Since \(1-y_\chi^2=3/(10\chi)\), (7) and the paired costs give
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 =5\chi\frac{(1-\epsilon^2)3/(10\chi)}
 {1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac32, \tag{8}
\]
because \(0<y_\chi^2<1\). Also \(R(1/2,1/2)=3/5\), whence
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B)=5\cdot\frac35=3. \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is uniquely prediction-selected despite having the arbitrarily prescribed larger paired cost \(5\chi>5\).

The experimental score reduces to \(2TW_y/y\), so
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}. \tag{10}
\]
The observational score is \(2Tq_d(W_x,A)(U-W_y/y)\). Conditional on \(U\), the residual factor has second moment \((1-y^2)/y^2\) and is independent of \((T,W_x)\). Moreover, \(\mathbb E(TW_x\mid G=O)=rx\). Expanding the square gives
\[
 V_O(x,y)
 =\frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
 \left(1-2r^2+\frac{r^2}{x^2}\right). \tag{11}
\]
Substituting \(r=1/4\), \(x=\epsilon\), and \(y=y_\chi\) into (11) yields the exact identity
\[
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 +\frac{896}{75(10\chi-3)}. \tag{12}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), (10)--(11) give
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}. \tag{13}
\]
The costs of \(d_{\mathrm e}\) are \((2,3)\). The budget-profile theorem therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot\frac{384}{25}}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}. \tag{14}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), \(c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}=(5\chi+1)/2\), so (12) implies
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 >\frac{16}{75}\epsilon^{-2}. \tag{15}
\]
Here the last inequality follows from
\[
 \frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}>\frac{16}{75}.
\]
For \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), the right-hand side of (15) exceeds \(3872/25\); hence \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is the unique arm-normalized design-value optimizer.

Expanding the profiled square and using (10) and (12) gives, for fixed \(\chi\),
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 +O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{16}
\]
Division by (14), using \(3872=32\cdot121\), proves
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 +O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{17}
\]

It remains to prove stability. Fix \((\chi,\epsilon)\) and parameterize \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bin}}(\chi)\) by its finitely many strictly positive latent-cell probabilities. Near \(P_\epsilon\), each two-by-two proxy channel remains invertible. The nearby latent outcome and selection bridges are obtained by solving the corresponding two-by-two channel equations. Strict positivity keeps the source-density ratios finite, and conditional independence plus iterated expectation carries these latent equations to the observed bridge equations. The observed matrix of \(T_d\) is a product of invertible proxy channels and positive diagonal reweighting matrices, hence is invertible.

The bridge coefficients are rational continuous functions of the cell probabilities. Prediction risks, bridge means, score moments, and profiled values are consequently continuous while cells and determinants stay away from zero. In this constrained model \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\). The early-proxy completeness implication applied to the latent outcome-bridge discrepancy gives \(\mathbb E\{h_d(S_{3d},a)\mid U,A=a,G=O\}=U\); source invariance and experimental randomization then give \(\mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E(U)\). Thus both bridge contrasts continue to equal \(0=\tau\). At \(P_\epsilon\), equations (1)--(17) give strict regularity and strict prediction- and design-selection margins. A sufficiently small radius \(r_\epsilon>0\) therefore preserves regularity, common identification, and both optimizer assignments. Since the ratio is continuous with a positive denominator, shrinking the radius once more gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)>\frac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon).
