Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\). Since \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty, \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) is finite and nonempty. Positive costs, Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) give, for every \(d\in\mathcal D\),
\[
 0<\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 =\left(\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+
         \sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}\right)^2<\infty.       \tag{1}
\]
Substituting (1) in the definition of \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) proves the displayed diagnostic identity. Its numerator minimizes the same positive collection over the subset \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\), while its denominator minimizes over all of \(\mathcal D\). The ratio is therefore at least one, and its finiteness follows from (1) and finiteness of the catalogue.

For the actual best-tie rule, a constant \(C\) is valid exactly when
\[
 \min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \le C\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P).    \tag{2}
\]
The denominator in (2) is strictly positive. Division shows that (2) holds if and only if \(C\ge\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\), proving that the displayed ratio is the least best-tie constant.

For the pairwise assertion, define
\[
 C_{\mathrm{pair}}(P)=
 \max_{\substack{d,d'\in\mathcal D:\,
 \Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}(P;B)
 \le\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d'}(P;B)}}
 \frac{\mathcal V_d^*(P)}{\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P)}.      \tag{3}
\]
The indexing set is nonempty because it contains every diagonal pair, and it is finite. Every denominator is positive by (1), so (3) is finite. If every prediction-order implication holds with constant \(C\), applying it to each indexed pair gives \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{pair}}(P)\). Conversely, the definition of the maximum gives \(\mathcal V_d^*(P)\le C_{\mathrm{pair}}(P)\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P)\) for every diagnostic-ordered pair. Thus (3) is exactly the least pairwise constant.

Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}predictive\text{-}impossibility}\) shows that, for every prescribed \(\chi>1\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\infty, \tag{4}
\]
although every witness design is regular and commonly identifying and the uniquely diagnostic-selected design is costlier in both sources. At each center its paired cost is \(5\chi\), compared with \(5\) for the efficient design, so the paired-cost ratio is exactly \(\chi\).

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) strengthens this pointwise statement by optimizing over \(\chi\). For all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), every exact minimizing cost-disadvantage factor obeys
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)
 \sim\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15}\epsilon^{-2/3},          \tag{5}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \epsilon^2\inf_{\chi>1}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 &=\epsilon^2\inf_{\chi>1}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})\\
 &=\frac1{726}+\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}\epsilon^{2/3}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3}).                                  \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus even the cost sequence that makes the counterexample least unfavorable leaves the ratio asymptotic to \(1/(726\epsilon^2)\), and (6) gives its sharp next term. Equations (4)--(6) rule out a primitive uniform diagnostic-efficiency guarantee. Finiteness of the catalogue yields the exact law-specific enumeration (1)--(3), but nothing more.
