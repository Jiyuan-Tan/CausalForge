Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(d\in\mathcal D\), and abbreviate
\[
 H_d=(S_{3d},S_{2d},A,X),
 \qquad
 L_d=(S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X).
\]
Observed outcome-bridge existence gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{Y-h_d(H_d)\mid L_d,G=O\}=0.                 \tag{1}
\]
Equivalently, \(T_dh_d=\mathbb E_P(Y\mid L_d,G=O)\).  Since \(T_d\) is bijective, this equation has exactly one square-integrable solution.  Thus \(h_d\), the two observed experimental means \(\mu_{d,a}\), and their contrast \(\theta_d(P)=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}\) are identified from the two observed source laws.  No latent variable or potential outcome is involved in this conclusion.

Consider an arbitrary regular parametric submodel of the observable two-source model.  Let \(\ell_E\) and \(\ell_O\) be its source-conditional scores, and write \(\dot h_d\) for the derivative of the unique bridge.  Differentiating the conditional experimental mean gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot\mu_{d,a}
 ={}&\mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{E,a}}
   \{h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a}\}\ell_E
   \mathrel{\Big|}G=E\right]\\
 &+\mathbb E_P\{\dot h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E\}.
                                                               \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
All divisions are legitimate by source-arm positivity.

The selection-bridge equation is
\[
 T_d^\star q_d
 =\frac{p_P(S_{3d},S_{2d},X\mid A,G=E)}
 {p_P(S_{3d},S_{2d},X\mid A,G=O)}.
\]
Consequently, change of measure followed by the defining adjoint identity yields, for every square-integrable \(f(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P(f\mid A=a,G=E)
 =\mathbb E_P(q_df\mid A=a,G=O).                       \tag{3}
\]
In particular, (3) applies to \(f=\dot h_d\).

Differentiate (1) along the observational submodel.  The conditional-score centering term drops out because the residual in (1) has conditional mean zero, giving
\[
 T_d\dot h_d
 =\mathbb E_P[\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid L_d,G=O].       \tag{4}
\]
Using (3), then conditioning on \(L_d\), and finally applying (4), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P(\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E)
 &=\mathbb E_P(q_d\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=O)\\
 &=\mathbb E_P\{q_dT_d\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=O\}\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[q_d\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid A=a,G=O]. \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
Subtracting the version of (2)--(5) for \(a=0\) from the version for \(a=1\) gives
\[
 \dot\theta_d(P)
 =\mathbb E_P(\phi_{E,d}\ell_E\mid G=E)
  +\mathbb E_P(\phi_{O,d}\ell_O\mid G=O).             \tag{6}
\]
The experimental component has conditional mean zero by the definition of \(\mu_{d,a}\).  The observational component has conditional mean zero by (1), because \(q_d\) is measurable with respect to \(L_d\).  Finite second moments follow from the nondegeneracy assumption.  Hence \((\phi_{E,d},\phi_{O,d})\) is a two-source pathwise gradient of the identified observed bridge contrast.  For independent samples of sizes \(n_E\) and \(n_O\), independence and variance additivity give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(
 \frac1{n_E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_E}\phi_{E,d,i}
 +\frac1{n_O}\sum_{i=1}^{n_O}\phi_{O,d,i}
 \right)
 =\frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O}.
\]
Every step used only premises defining \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  In particular, no latent causal restriction, completeness condition, or equality with \(\tau\) was invoked.
