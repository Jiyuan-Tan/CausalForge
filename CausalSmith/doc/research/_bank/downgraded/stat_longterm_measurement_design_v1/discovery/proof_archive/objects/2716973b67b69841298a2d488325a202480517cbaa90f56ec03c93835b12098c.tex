For a nonzero vector \(z\), write \(r(z)=Q_1(z)/Q_0(z)\).  The generalized Rayleigh-quotient theorem in finite-dimensional linear algebra, obtained directly by diagonalizing \(Q_0^{-1/2}Q_1Q_0^{-1/2}\), gives
\[
 \lambda_-\le r(z)\le\lambda_+. \tag{1}
\]
If \(Q_0(z_d)\le Q_0(z_{d'})\), then
\[
 \frac{Q_1(z_d)}{Q_1(z_{d'})}
 =\frac{r(z_d)}{r(z_{d'})}
  \frac{Q_0(z_d)}{Q_0(z_{d'})}
 \le\frac{\lambda_+}{\lambda_-}. \tag{2}
\]
Taking \(d\) to be any predictive optimizer and \(d'\) to be a design-value optimizer proves the approximation bound.  Because the catalogue is finite and all quadratic values are positive, the least constant validating every catalogue pair is exactly the displayed finite maximum: necessity follows by selecting a maximizing pair, and sufficiency follows term by term from its definition.

All three inequalities used in (2) are equalities precisely when \(r(z_d)=\lambda_+\), \(r(z_{d'})=\lambda_-\), and \(Q_0(z_d)=Q_0(z_{d'})\).  This proves the catalogue-restricted attainment assertion.  It also shows why \(\lambda_+\) alone is insufficient: rescaling \(Q_1\), or allowing \(\lambda_-\) to approach zero while \(\lambda_+\) stays bounded, changes the necessary ratio without changing the claimed one-sided logic.

Finally, any transported representation satisfying the theorem identifies \(Q_0\) with the predictive objective and \(Q_1\) with the design value on every catalogue direction.  Therefore (2) implies \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\le\lambda_+(P)/\lambda_-(P)\).  The unequal-cost predictive-impossibility theorem gives a sequence \(P_\epsilon\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) for which \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)\to\infty\).  Hence the condition numbers of every valid family of such certificates are unbounded on \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\).  Finiteness alone supplies only the enumerated diagnostic at a fixed law and supplies no primitive uniform guarantee.
