Write \(p_d=R_d(P)\) and \(v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P)\).  The admissible family \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty.  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), positivity of the two unit costs, and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) give, for every \(d\in\mathcal D\),
\[
  0<v_d
  =\left\{\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+
             \sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}\right\}^{2}<\infty.       \tag{10}
\]
The predictive-benchmark definition gives \(0\le p_d<\infty\).  In particular, \(\mathcal O_P\) is a nonempty finite set because it contains every diagonal pair \((d,d)\).  Every ratio \(v_d/v_{d'}\) is finite and strictly positive by (10), and each diagonal ratio is one.  Hence
\[
  1\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty.                           \tag{11}
\]

We next prove the exact order constant.  If \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) and \(p_d\le p_{d'}\), then \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), so
\[
  \frac{v_d}{v_{d'}}\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C.
\]
Since \(v_{d'}>0\), this is \(v_d\le Cv_{d'}\).  Conversely, if all the implications in (2) hold, then for every \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), division by the positive number \(v_{d'}\) gives \(v_d/v_{d'}\le C\).  Taking the maximum over the finite set \(\mathcal O_P\) yields \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C\).  This proves (2), including its claimed minimality.

Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty, both \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) and the set of global design-value minimizers are nonempty.  Put
\[
  \bar v=\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}v_{d'},
  \qquad
  v_{\mathrm{pred}}=
  \min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}v_d.
\]
Both numbers are positive by (10).  The least constant in the best-tie guarantee \(v_{\mathrm{pred}}\le C\bar v\) is therefore exactly \(v_{\mathrm{pred}}/\bar v\), which is \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) by Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}predictive\text{-}benchmark}\).  Choose \(d_{mathrm{pred}}\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) with \(v_{d_{mathrm{pred}}}=v_{\mathrm{pred}}\), and choose \(d_0\in\mathcal D\) with \(v_{d_0}=\bar v\).  Prediction optimality gives
\[
  p_{d_{mathrm{pred}}}\le p_{d_0},
\]
so \((d_{mathrm{pred}},d_0)\in\mathcal O_P\).  Consequently,
\[
  1\le C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)
  =\frac{v_{d_{mathrm{pred}}}}{v_{d_0}}
  \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P),                                  \tag{12}
\]
which proves (3).

Let \(\mathcal Q\) be a nonempty subclass of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  If one finite constant \(C\) gives the best-tie guarantee at every \(P\in\mathcal Q\), the just-proved pointwise minimality implies \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\le C\) for every such \(P\), hence
\[
  \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\le C<\infty.
\]
Conversely, if this supremum is finite, choosing it as \(C\) gives the guarantee at every law.  Applying exactly the same two implications to the pointwise least pairwise constant \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) proves the second uniform equivalence.  Thus the arrays \((p_d:d\in\mathcal D)\) and \((v_d:d\in\mathcal D)\) determine the weakest exact lawwise comparison.  By the definitions of \(h_d,q_d,V_{E,d}\), and \(V_{O,d}\), the operator \(T_d\) affects \(v_d\) through the bridge solutions and source scores; the finite-order argument uses no additional relation among the operators.

Now fix a numerical realization of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{comp}}\) satisfying (4)--(5).  Positivity of \(Q_{R,d}\) on \(\mathcal X_d(P)\) makes
\[
  r_d(x):=\frac{F_d(x)}{Q_{R,d}(x)}
\]
well defined.  For constants \(0<L\le U<\infty\), the simultaneous envelope
\[
  LQ_{R,d}(x)\le F_d(x)\le UQ_{R,d}(x)
  \quad\text{for every }d\in\mathcal D,
  \ x\in\mathcal X_d(P)                                    \tag{13}
\]
holds if and only if
\[
  L\le\Lambda_-(P)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \Lambda_+(P)\le U.                                       \tag{14}
\]
Indeed, division in (13) by \(Q_{R,d}(x)>0\), followed by the relevant infimum or supremum and then the finite minimum or maximum over designs, gives (14); the reverse implication follows directly from the definitions in (6).  It follows that a finite strictly positive two-sided envelope exists if and only if (7) holds.  Moreover, \(\Lambda_-(P)\) is the largest valid common lower constant and \(\Lambda_+(P)\) is the smallest valid common upper constant, whether or not the infima and suprema are attained.  This proves the asserted weakest-envelope statement.

Under (7), evaluating the envelope at the transported directions in (4) gives
\[
  \Lambda_-(P)p_d\le v_d\le\Lambda_+(P)p_d
  \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).                                  \tag{15}
\]
If \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), then \(p_d\le p_{d'}\); using (15) twice and the strict positivity of \(\Lambda_-(P)\) gives
\[
  v_d\le\Lambda_+(P)p_d
      \le\Lambda_+(P)p_{d'}
      \le\frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}v_{d'}.
\]
Taking the maximum over \(\mathcal O_P\), and then using (12), proves (8).

It remains to justify the profiling and generalized-eigenvalue assertions.  For fixed \(d\) and \(x\), set
\[
  a_x=c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x),
  \qquad b_x=c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x).
\]
These numbers are nonnegative.  For \(0<\alpha<1\), the algebraic identity
\[
 \frac{a_x}{\alpha}+\frac{b_x}{1-\alpha}
 -(\sqrt{a_x}+\sqrt{b_x})^2
 =\frac{\{(1-\alpha)\sqrt{a_x}-\alpha\sqrt{b_x}\}^2}
        {\alpha(1-\alpha)}                                  \tag{16}
\]
shows that the leftmost two terms are minimized at
\(
\alpha=\sqrt{a_x}/(\sqrt{a_x}+\sqrt{b_x})
\)
when \(a_xb_x>0\), with minimum \((\sqrt{a_x}+\sqrt{b_x})^2\).  If exactly one of \(a_x,b_x\) is zero, the same infimum is obtained by taking \(\alpha\) to the appropriate endpoint; if both vanish, both sides are zero.  Substitution of the definitions of \(a_x,b_x\) proves (9).

The profiled function is not a quadratic form in general.  For example, on \(\mathbb R^2\) take
\[
  Q_{E,d}(x)=\frac{x_1^2}{c_{E,d}},
  \qquad
  Q_{O,d}(x)=\frac{x_2^2}{c_{O,d}}.
\]
Then \(F_d(x)=(|x_1|+|x_2|)^2\).  With coordinate vectors \(e_1,e_2\),
\[
 F_d(e_1+e_2)+F_d(e_1-e_2)=8
 \ne4=2F_d(e_1)+2F_d(e_2),                                 \tag{17}
\]
so the parallelogram identity fails and \(F_d\) is not quadratic.  At a fixed \(\alpha\), by contrast,
\[
 x\longmapsto
 \frac{c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)}{\alpha}
 +\frac{c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)}{1-\alpha}
\]
is quadratic.  When it and \(Q_{R,d}\) live on one finite-dimensional space with \(Q_{R,d}\) positive definite, its largest generalized eigenvalue controls only the upper ratio of this fixed-\(\alpha\) quadratic form to \(Q_{R,d}\), and therefore supplies at most a one-sided upper envelope for the infimum in (9).  It supplies neither the positive lower envelope required in (6) nor saturation of the inequalities in (15) by a law-realized ordered pair.  Since the spaces \(\mathcal H_d\) may also differ with \(d\), an eigenvalue comparison across designs first requires a common-space representation.  Equality with the exact constants can therefore occur only after supplying that representation, normalizing a strictly positive lower envelope, and exhibiting law-realized raw-risk-ordered directions that saturate the comparison.  Neither operator bijectivity in the observable law class nor the scalar ordering \(p_d\le p_{d'}\) supplies those additional properties.
