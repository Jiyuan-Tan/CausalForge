Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and abbreviate
\[
 v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P),\qquad
 \widehat v_d=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*,\qquad
 v_*=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d.
\]
Choose any \(d^*\in D^*(P)\).  By the definition of \(\varepsilon_m\) and the minimizing property of \(\widehat d_m\),
\[
 v_{\widehat d_m}
 \le \widehat v_{\widehat d_m}+\varepsilon_m
 \le \widehat v_{d^*}+\varepsilon_m
 \le v_*+2\varepsilon_m.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The reverse inequality \(v_*\le v_{\widehat d_m}\) is immediate.  This proves
\[
 0\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m.                  \tag{2}
\]
If \(\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\), the definition of \(\gamma(P)\) and (2) give
\[
 \gamma(P)\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m,          \tag{3}
\]
which is the asserted event inclusion.  If \(D^*(P)=\mathcal D\), the event on the left of (3) is empty.  Otherwise, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) and strict positivity of every nonzero gap imply \(\gamma(P)>0\).

Put
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}.
\]
Assumptions~\(\mathrm{ass{:}pilot\text{-}gradient\text{-}variance\text{-}rate}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}pilot\text{-}variance\text{-}rate\text{-}vanishes}\) give
\[
 Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).                                     \tag{4}
\]
By Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), positivity of the costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\), all population pairs \((V_{E,d},V_{O,d})\) lie in one compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\), and all costs lie between two positive finite constants.  On an event whose probability tends to one, (4) puts every estimated variance pair in a slightly larger compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\).

For
\[
 G_d(x,y)=\left\{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}\right\}^2,
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) and the pilot definition give
\[
 v_d=G_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d}),
 \qquad
 \widehat v_d=G_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d}).
\]
The gradients of the finitely many maps \(G_d\) are uniformly bounded on the preceding compact set.  The mean-value theorem and (4) therefore yield
\[
 \varepsilon_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat v_d-v_d|
 =O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).                                       \tag{5}
\]
Combining (3) and (5) proves
\[
 P\{\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\}\longrightarrow0.             \tag{6}
\]

Consider next
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}.
\]
Because \(\underline v_m=o(1)\) and all population variances are bounded away from zero, the truncation in \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\) is inactive for every \(d\) on an event whose probability tends to one.  The gradients of the finitely many maps \(A_d\) are uniformly bounded on the same compact set.  Hence
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=O_P(r_{V,m}).            \tag{7}
\]

Finally, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}second\text{-}order\text{-}allocation\text{-}loss}\) applies under precisely (4), the variance nondegeneracy condition, \(r_{V,m}=o(1)\), and the pilot construction.  It gives, along every joint sequence \(m,B\to\infty\),
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
\frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
+\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
\right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
=O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1})=o_P(1).                                \tag{8}
\]
The lemma also shows that the stipulated zero convention is invoked only on an event whose probability tends to zero.  Equations (2), (3), and (5)--(8) prove all claims.  The argument is entirely about the observable bridge functional; it uses no equality between \(\theta_d(P)\) and the causal estimand.
