The claim is established by an explicit finite-support witness. For the single design below, the observed outcome-bridge equation is
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{Y-h_d(S_3,S_2,A)\mid S_2,S_1,A,G=O\}=0. \tag{1}
\]
Take one design, let \(X\) be constant, let \(U\) be uniform on \(\{1,2,3\}\), and let \(S_2\) be an independent fair bit.  These variables have the same distribution in both sources.  In the experimental source let \(P(A=1\mid G=E)=1/2\); in the observational source set
\[
 P(A=1\mid U=u,G=O)=e_u,qquad (e_1,e_2,e_3)=(1/5,1/2,4/5).
\]
Conditional on \((S_2=s,U=u)\), let \(S_1\) and \(S_3\) be independent Bernoulli variables, unaffected by treatment or source, with common success probability \(p_{s,u}\), where
\[
 (p_{0,1},p_{0,2},p_{0,3})=(1/5,1/2,4/5),\qquad
 (p_{1,1},p_{1,2},p_{1,3})=(3/10,4/5,2/5).
\]
Use the same \(S_3\) in both treatment potential outcomes and set
\[
 Y(0)=S_3,qquad Y(1)=S_3+\delta_{S_2}(U),
\]
with
\[
 (\delta_0(1),\delta_0(2),\delta_0(3))=(4,-16/5,1),qquad
 (\delta_1(1),\delta_1(2),\delta_1(3))=(-16/5,-8/25,1).
\]
For each \(s\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 \sum_u e_u\delta_s(u)=0,qquad
 \sum_u e_up_{s,u}\delta_s(u)=0. \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(h_d(s_3,s_2,a)=s_3\) satisfies the displayed outcome-bridge equation for both treatment arms.  The matrix with rows \(1\), \(p_0\), and \(p_1\) has determinant \(-27/100\).  Hence, for either treatment arm, the equations
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{v(U)\mid S_1,S_2,A=a,G=O\}=0
\]
force \(v=0\), because the positive treatment weights merely multiply the three columns by a nonsingular diagonal matrix.  The identical argument with \(S_3\) proves the stated late-proxy completeness condition.  For each \((s,a)\), the two-by-two conditional-expectation matrix from \(S_3\) to \(S_1\) has determinant proportional to the strictly positive conditional variance of \(p_{s,U}\); it is therefore nonsingular.  Thus \(T_d\) is bijective, and in finite dimension \(T_d^\star\) is bijective, which supplies the required selection bridge.  All treatment and source probabilities are strictly positive.  Conditional independence supplies the sequential restriction, and the remaining consistency, exchangeability, external-validity, support, and moment conditions follow immediately from the construction.  The experimental gradient is nondegenerate because \(h_d=S_3\), and the observational gradient is nondegenerate because the residual in the treated arm is the nonzero \(\delta_{S_2}(U)\); hence this law is in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

The bridge contrast is zero because the distribution of \(S_3\) is treatment invariant in the randomized source.  On the other hand,
\[
 \tau=\mathbb E\{\delta_{S_2}(U)\}
 =\frac16\left(\frac95-\frac{63}{25}\right)=-\frac3{25}. \tag{3}
\]
The failed step in the original argument is now visible: (1) only yields a zero conditional mean for a discrepancy depending on \((S_2,U,X)\), whereas the frozen completeness premise is injective only for a common function of \(U\).  Equation (3) proves that the causal equality and therefore the claimed causal canonical gradient do not follow on the frozen class.
