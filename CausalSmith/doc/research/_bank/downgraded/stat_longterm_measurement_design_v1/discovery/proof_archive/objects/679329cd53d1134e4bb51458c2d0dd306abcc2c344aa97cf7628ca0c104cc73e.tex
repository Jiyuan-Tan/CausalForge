Let
\[
 \nu_0=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0,
 \qquad
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max_{g\in\{E,O\}}
 |\widehat V_{g,d}-V_{g,d}|.
\]
By hypothesis, \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  On the event
\[
 \mathcal E_m=\{Z_m<\nu_0/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_0/2\}, \tag{1}
\]
whose probability tends to one, every estimated variance exceeds \(\underline v_m\).  Hence all truncations in \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\) are exactly inactive.

For each design define
\[
 A_d(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}.
\]
The finitely many population pairs \((V_{E,d},V_{O,d})\) lie in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\).  On \(\mathcal E_m\), all estimated pairs lie in a slightly larger such compact set.  The derivatives of the finitely many maps \(A_d\) are uniformly bounded there, so the mean-value theorem gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=O_P(r_{V,m}).       \tag{2}
\]
Finiteness and positivity also give a constant \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) with
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).
\]
Thus, with probability tending to one, all \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\) belong to \([a_0,1-a_0]\).

Put \(a_d=c_{E,d}V_{E,d}\), \(b_d=c_{O,d}V_{O,d}\), and
\[
 F_d(\alpha)=\frac{a_d}{\alpha}+\frac{b_d}{1-\alpha}.
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) gives
\[
 \alpha_d^*(P)=\frac{\sqrt{a_d}}{\sqrt{a_d}+\sqrt{b_d}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_d^*(P)=(\sqrt{a_d}+\sqrt{b_d})^2.
\]
Another direct calculation yields the exact loss identity
\[
 F_d(\alpha)-\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 =\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \frac{\{\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)\}^2}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}.    \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3), the common interior interval, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) imply
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |F_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m})-\mathcal V_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2).                                      \tag{4}
\]

On the same high-probability event, both continuous source counts are at least a fixed positive multiple of \(B\), so for all sufficiently large \(B\) every floor count is positive and its continuous precursor is at least two.  Applying the floor inequality used in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\), uniformly over the finite family and the common interior interval, gives
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|
B\left\{
\frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
+\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
\right\}-F_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m})\right|=O_P(B^{-1}). \tag{5}
\]
Combining (4) and (5) proves the claimed \(O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1})\) bound.  The stipulated total value on zero-count outcomes is harmless because those outcomes are contained in an event whose probability tends to zero.  Finally, (1) shows that the truncation is not merely small but exactly inactive with probability tending to one, so it contributes no additional rate term.
