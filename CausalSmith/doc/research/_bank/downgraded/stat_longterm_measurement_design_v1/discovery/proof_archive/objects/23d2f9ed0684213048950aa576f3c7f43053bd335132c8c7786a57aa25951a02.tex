Fix \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\).  Lemma~\texttt{lem:binary-witness-stability} first establishes that \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).  We prove that every defining property of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\) persists on a relative neighborhood inside the constrained finite-cell model.

The consistency equalities, treatment-invariant potential outcomes, external validity, experimental randomization, observational latent exchangeability, and the conditional independences used by the sequential-outcome restriction hold identically throughout the constrained family by its potential-variable specification and factorization.  Strict positivity of all factor probabilities is an open condition.  On a sufficiently small relative neighborhood of \(P_\epsilon\), it supplies experimental and observational treatment overlap and finite source-density ratios.

It remains to check rank, bridge, and variance conditions.  For a binary proxy \(W_j\), let
\[
 K_{j,P}=\bigl(P(W_j=w\mid U=u)\bigr)_{u,w\in\{-1,1\}}.
\]
Because the conditional latent weights in every observational treatment arm are positive, the conditional-expectation map from functions of \(U\) to functions of \(W_j\) is injective if and only if \(K_{j,P}\) has full rank.  For a row-stochastic binary matrix this is equivalent to
\[
 P(W_j=1\mid U=1)-P(W_j=1\mid U=-1)\ne0. \tag{1}
\]
At \(P_\epsilon\), these contrasts for the early and late proxies of \(d_{\mathrm p}\) are \(\epsilon\) and \(3/4\), and those for \(d_{\mathrm e}\) are \(1/2\) and \(1/2\).  All are nonzero.  Their finitely many determinants are continuous functions of the cell probabilities, so both designwise completeness conditions remain valid on a relative neighborhood.

For each treatment arm and design, the matrix of \(T_d\) factors into the transpose of the early-proxy channel, a positive diagonal matrix of the conditional latent probabilities, the late-proxy channel, and invertible diagonal row normalizations.  Both proxy channels have full rank by (1), so \(T_d\) is bijective throughout a sufficiently small neighborhood.

We now construct the bridges at a nearby law \(P\), rather than importing the formulas that hold only at \(P_\epsilon\).  For design \(d\) and arm \(a\), let \(K_{3d,P}\) and \(K_{1d,P}\) be the late and early channel matrices.  The unique vector
\[
 h_{d,a,P}=K_{3d,P}^{-1}(u:u\in\{-1,1\})^{\mathsf T} \tag{2}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E_P[h_{d,a,P}(S_{3d})\mid U=u,A=a,G=O]=u
 =\mathbb E_P[Y\mid U=u,A=a,G=O], \tag{3}
\]
so it is the latent outcome bridge.  Put
\[
 \ell_{a,P}(u)=
 \frac{P(U=u\mid A=a,G=E)}{P(U=u\mid A=a,G=O)}.
\]
Strict positivity makes this ratio finite, and the unique vector
\[
 q_{d,a,P}=K_{1d,P}^{-1}(\ell_{a,P}(u):u\in\{-1,1\})^{\mathsf T} \tag{4}
\]
satisfies the latent selection-bridge equation.  By conditional independence of the proxy coordinates given \(U\), iterated expectation turns (3) into the observed outcome-bridge equation and turns (4) into
\[
 \mathbb E_P[q_{d,a,P}(S_{1d})\mid S_{3d},A=a,G=O]
 =\frac{p_P(S_{3d}\mid A=a,G=E)}{p_P(S_{3d}\mid A=a,G=O)}, \tag{5}
\]
the required observed selection-bridge equation.  Bijectivity of \(T_d\) makes the outcome bridge unique; in finite dimension its adjoint is also bijective, so the observed selection bridge is unique.  This proves bridge existence by nearby-law inversion and also verifies the published latent bridge equations throughout the neighborhood.

Finally, finite support makes all relevant moments finite.  On the open set where the preceding determinants do not vanish, the bridge coordinates in (2) and (4), and therefore \(V_{E,d}\) and \(V_{O,d}\), are continuous rational functions of the cell probabilities.  Lemma~\texttt{lem:binary-witness-stability} gives strict positivity of all four variance components at \(P_\epsilon\).  Their minimum consequently remains positive on a relative neighborhood.  Taking \(r_\epsilon^{\mathrm{reg}}\) smaller than the finitely many positivity, determinant, and variance-margin radii proves the claim.
