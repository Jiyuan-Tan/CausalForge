For each \((d,a)\), let
\[
 r_{h,d,a}=\mathbb E_P[b_{q,d,a}(L_d)Y\mid A=a,G=O],
 \qquad
 r_{q,d,a}=\mathbb E_P[b_{h,d,a}(H_d)\mid A=a,G=E].
\]
Exact bridge specification and the two observed bridge moment equations give
\[
 M_{d,a}\beta_{h,d,a}=r_{h,d,a},
 \qquad
 M_{d,a}^{\mathsf T}\beta_{q,d,a}=r_{q,d,a}.                 \tag{1}
\]
The lower singular-value bound makes both coefficient vectors unique.  Bounded bases, the \((4+\delta)\)-moment bound for \(Y\), and (1) also bound their Euclidean norms uniformly over the finite set of designs and arms.

Consider any training block of size \(N\) proportional to \(n\).  Every entry of \(b_qb_h^{\mathsf T}\), \(b_qY\), and \(b_h\) has a uniformly bounded second moment.  Chebyshev's inequality applied entrywise therefore yields
\[
 \|\widehat M_{d,a}-M_{d,a}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\|\widehat r_{h,d,a}-r_{h,d,a}\|
 +\|\widehat r_{q,d,a}-r_{q,d,a}\|
 =O_P(N^{-1/2})=O_P(n^{-1/2}).                               \tag{2}
\]
There are only finitely many designs, arms, and folds, so the maximum of the left side of (2) has the same order.  On the event
\(\|\widehat M_{d,a}-M_{d,a}\|_{\mathrm{op}}<c/2\), which has probability tending to one, \(\widehat M_{d,a}\) is nonsingular and
\[
 \|\widehat M_{d,a}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le 2c^{-1}.
\]
The identity
\[
 \widehat M^{-1}-M^{-1}
 =\widehat M^{-1}(M-\widehat M)M^{-1}                       \tag{3}
\]
and (1)--(2) now give
\[
 \max_{d,a,\ell}\bigl(
 \|\widehat\beta_{h,d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\beta_{h,d,a}\|
 +\|\widehat\beta_{q,d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\beta_{q,d,a}\|
 \bigr)=O_P(n^{-1/2}).                                      \tag{4}
\]
The zero extension on singular empirical matrices is immaterial because its event has probability tending to zero.

For either basis \(b\) and coefficient error \(v\), the upper Gram bound gives
\[
 \|b^{\mathsf T}v\|_2^2
 =v^{\mathsf T}\mathbb E_P[bb^{\mathsf T}]v
 \le C\|v\|^2.                                             \tag{5}
\]
Conditional expectation is an \(L_2\)-contraction, so
\[
 \|T_df\|_2\le\|f\|_2,
 \qquad \|T_d^\star u\|_2\le\|u\|_2.                    \tag{6}
\]
Combining (4)--(6) proves (1) of the theorem, both for the pilot with \(n=m\) and for every proportional main-study training block with \(n\asymp B\).

Write \(\Delta h=b_h^{\mathsf T}\Delta\beta_h\) and \(\Delta q=b_q^{\mathsf T}\Delta\beta_q\).  The bounded-basis condition and (4) imply
\[
 \|\Delta q\,\Delta h\|_2=O_P(B^{-1}).                    \tag{7}
\]
Together with (1), this proves (2).  The same boundedness and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \|\Delta q(Y-h_d)\|_2=O_P(B^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \|q_d\Delta h\|_2=O_P(B^{-1/2}),                          \tag{8}
\]
because \(Y-h_d\in L_2\) and \(q_d\) is bounded under the fixed-basis representation.  Equations (7)--(8), the finite fold count, and insertion of \(\mathbf1\{G=O\}\) prove (3).

It remains to prove the two variance rates.  Let \(\phi_{g,d}\) be a true score and \(\widehat\phi_{g,d}\) its foldwise plug-in version.  Empirical source-arm probabilities are root-sample-size consistent by Chebyshev's inequality and the positivity bound.  The experimental bridge means are also root-sample-size consistent by the same argument, (4), and bounded bases.  Expanding the two displayed score formulas in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}selected\text{-}wald\text{-}variance}\), then using (4), bounded bases, and Cauchy--Schwarz, gives conditionally on each training sample
\[
 \|\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d}\|_2=O_P(n^{-1/2}).          \tag{9}
\]
The \((4+\delta)\)-moment condition and bounded bases imply a uniformly finite fourth moment for every true score.  Hence Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 (\mathbb P_{g}^{\mathrm{ev}}-P_g)\phi_{g,d}^2
 =O_P(n^{-1/2}).                                             \tag{10}
\]
Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb P_g^{\mathrm{ev}}\widehat\phi_{g,d}^2-P_g\phi_{g,d}^2
 ={}&(\mathbb P_g^{\mathrm{ev}}-P_g)\phi_{g,d}^2\\
 &+\mathbb P_g^{\mathrm{ev}}
 [\{\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d}\}
  \{\widehat\phi_{g,d}+\phi_{g,d}\}].                    \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
\]
The first term is (10).  Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, (9), and the uniformly bounded empirical second moments of the two scores make the second term \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Maximization over the finite catalogue, sources, arms, and folds preserves the order.  Taking \(n=m\) proves the pilot assertion in (4); taking \(n\asymp B\) proves the main-study assertion, and uniformity permits substitution of the pilot-selected random design.  Thus all eight stated estimator conditions are consequences on the fixed finite-dimensional subclass, with the displayed rates, and no such conclusion has been asserted for the unrestricted observable bridge class.
