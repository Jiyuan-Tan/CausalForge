Fix a binary center design with signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\), and write \(r=W_x\), \(s=W_y\), and \(t=T\).  Under the observational law, direct summation over the Rademacher variable \(U\) gives
\[
 P(T=t,W_x=r,W_y=s\mid G=O)
 =\frac18\left\{1+xyrs+\frac t4(xr+ys)\right\},           \tag{40}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E(U\mid T=t,W_x=r,W_y=s,G=O)
 =\frac{t/4+xr+ys+(t/4)xyrs}
 {1+xyrs+(t/4)(xr+ys)}.                                   \tag{41}
\]
Because \(Y=U\), the treatment-aware squared-error risk is the expectation of the conditional variance \(1-\mathbb E(U\mid T,W_x,W_y,G=O)^2\).

To sum the eight cells, set \(q=rs\), \(v=tr\),
\[
 a_q=1+xyq,
 \qquad b_q=x+yq.
\]
For each \((q,v)\in\{-1,1\}^2\), the denominator in (41) is \(a_q+vb_q/4\), while the numerator, up to the irrelevant sign \(r\), is \(b_q+va_q/4\).  The elementary identity
\[
 (a_q+vb_q/4)^2-(b_q+va_q/4)^2
 =\frac{15}{16}(a_q^2-b_q^2)                              \tag{42}
\]
and summation first over \(v\) therefore give
\[
 R_d^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{15}{2}\sum_{q\in\{-1,1\}}
 \frac{a_q(a_q^2-b_q^2)}{16a_q^2-b_q^2}.                 \tag{43}
\]
Now
\[
 a_q^2-b_q^2=(1-x^2)(1-y^2)
\]
for both signs of \(q\).  Placing the two terms in (43) over their common denominator and simplifying yields
\[
 R_d^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{15(1-x^2)(1-y^2)(16-x^2-y^2-14x^2y^2)}
 {\{16(1+xy)^2-(x+y)^2\}\{16(1-xy)^2-(x-y)^2\}},
\]
which is (1).  Every denominator is positive because \(16a_q^2-b_q^2>15a_q^2>0\), where \(|b_q|<a_q\) follows from \(a_q^2-b_q^2=(1-x^2)(1-y^2)>0\).

Substitution of \(x=y=1/2\) gives
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=\frac{585}{1024}.
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), conditioning on the additional variable \(A\) cannot increase the optimal \(L_2\) prediction error, so
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \le R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon}).
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) supplies
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 <\frac3{10\chi}<\frac3{10}<\frac{585}{1024},
\]
so both unrestricted prediction selectors are the singleton \(\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\).  Since their selected design is identical, their best-tie arm-normalized ratios are identical, and the same is true for their IKM-normalized ratios.  The exact ratio, lower bound, optimizing scale, and second-order expansion now transfer verbatim from the sharp-elbow lemma.
