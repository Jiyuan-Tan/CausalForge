Fixed-proxy long-term data-combination theory and the surrogate-index literature take the measured short-term system and source ratio as given. Existing surrogate-efficiency comparisons ask whether a prespecified surrogate set helps, while existing optimal-measurement work studies different linear or Gaussian models or changes sampling probabilities for a fixed measured set. None supplies a cost-aware rule that jointly chooses the measured subset, sequential roles, and source allocation, and no prior result quantifies predictive screening when its chosen design can be more expensive in both sources. The remaining gap is therefore not merely whether prediction can fail, but whether any finite predictive approximation factor survives uniformly over finite sourcewise cost disadvantages.
