Write
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\{|\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\}.
\]
By hypothesis, \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  Finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) and the displayed uniform bounds furnish constants \(0<\nu_-<\nu_+<\infty\) containing every population variance and every cost.  On the event
\[
 \mathcal E_m=\{Z_m<\nu_-/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_-/2\}, \tag{1}
\]
whose probability tends to one, every estimated variance is positive and exceeds \(\underline v_m\).  Hence the truncation in \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\) is inactive.

For
\[
 A_d(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}},
\]
the gradients are uniformly bounded on a common compact rectangle around all population pairs.  The mean-value theorem and (1) therefore yield
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}). \tag{2}
\]
The population optimal shares form a finite subset of \((0,1)\); hence there is \(a_0>0\) such that, with probability tending to one,
\[
 a_0\le\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\le1-a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D). \tag{3}
\]

For fixed \(d\), set \(a=c_{E,d}V_{E,d}\), \(b=c_{O,d}V_{O,d}\), and \(F_d(u)=a/u+b/(1-u)\).  The budget-profile theorem gives \(u_d^*=\alpha_d^*(P)=\sqrt a/(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)\) and \(\mathcal V_d^*(P)=(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2\).  A direct calculation gives the exact loss identity
\[
 F_d(u)-\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 =\mathcal V_d^*(P)\frac{(u-u_d^*)^2}{u(1-u)}. \tag{4}
\]
Combining (2)--(4), uniformly over the finite design family, gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |F_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m})-\mathcal V_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2). \tag{5}
\]

On the event (3), both continuous source counts are at least a positive constant times \(B\).  For all sufficiently large \(B\), the floor inequality used in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) applies uniformly and gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 0\le{}&B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}-F_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m})\\
 &\le\frac{C}{B}
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
with one finite constant \(C\) for all \(d\).  Equations (5)--(6) prove the asserted \(O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1})\) bound.  The stipulated zero convention matters only off an event whose probability tends to zero, so it does not alter this stochastic order.  Equation (1) also shows that truncation is asymptotically inactive, which proves the final assertion.
