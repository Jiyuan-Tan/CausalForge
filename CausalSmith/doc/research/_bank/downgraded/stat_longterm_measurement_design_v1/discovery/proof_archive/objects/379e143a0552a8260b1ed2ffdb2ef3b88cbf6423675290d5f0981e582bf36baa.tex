Fix \(P\) as in the statement.  We first establish a deterministic selected-path expansion and then transfer it through the independent pilot.

For every \(d\in\mathcal D\), positive costs and nondegenerate source moments give \(0<\alpha_d^*(P)<1\).  Since \(\mathcal D\) is finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).                                  \tag{1}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).              \tag{2}
\]
Hence, with probability tending to one, \(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\), and both purchased source counts are bounded below by a positive constant times \(B\).  Conditional on the pilot, source-arm positivity and the conditional iid sampling assumption imply by Chebyshev's inequality that every empirical arm proportion converges to its strictly positive population value.  The observational folds are balanced and \(L\) is fixed.  A finite union bound over sources, arms, and folds therefore gives
\[
 P(\mathsf D_B)\longrightarrow1.                          \tag{3}
\]

Now take an arbitrary deterministic sequence \((d_B,\alpha_B)\) satisfying \(|\alpha_B-\alpha_{d_B}^*(P)|\to0\), and let \(e_B,o_B\) be its two floor counts.  By (1),
\[
 e_B\asymp B,
 \qquad
 o_B\asymp B.                                             \tag{4}
\]
Write \(h=h_{d_B}\), \(q=q_{d_B}\), and, on fold \(\ell\),
\[
 \Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-h,
 \qquad
 \Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-q.
\]

We use an elementary ratio expansion that accounts for random arm denominators.  If \(W_1,\ldots,W_N\) are iid within one source, \(p_a=P(A=a)>0\), \(N_a=\sum_i\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}\), and \(f\) is square integrable, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac1{N_a}\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}f(W_i)
 -\mathbb E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}
 ={}&\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{p_a}
 \bigl[f(W_i)-\mathbb E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}\bigr]\\
 &+o_P(N^{-1/2}).                                         \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, \(N_a/N-p_a=O_P(N^{-1/2})\), the centered average is \(O_P(N^{-1/2})\), and the product created by replacing \(Np_a/N_a\) with one is \(O_P(N^{-1})\).  Conditional on fold-external training data, the same variance calculation makes the centered empirical average of a random function \(f_N\) equal to \(o_P(N^{-1/2})\) whenever its conditional \(L_2\) norm is \(o_P(1)\).  Balanced-fold averaging changes a per-observation coefficient by only \(O(B^{-2})\), and thus has \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) effect.

For a nuisance function with nonzero arm mean, the denominator contribution must also be retained.  On the positive-count event it is bounded, before multiplication by \(\sqrt N\), by
\[
 C|\widehat p_a-p_a|\,|P\{\mathbf1(A=a)f_N\}|
 \le C|\widehat p_a-p_a|\,\|f_N\|_2
 =O_P(N^{-1/2}\|f_N\|_2).                               \tag{6}
\]
For \(f_N=\Delta h_\ell\), the outcome strong-rate condition makes (6) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  For \(f_N=q\Delta h_\ell\) and \(f_N=\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\), the multiplier condition gives the same conclusion.  The arm mean of \(\Delta q_\ell(Y-h)\) is zero by the outcome-bridge equation, so its denominator product is already of centered-product order.

For every square-integrable early-argument function \(u\) and late-argument function \(f\), the observed outcome and selection bridge equations imply
\[
 \mathbb E_O\{u(S_{2d_B},S_{1d_B},a,X)(Y-h)\mid A=a\}=0, \tag{7}
\]
\[
 \mathbb E_E\{f(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}
 =\mathbb E_O\{qf(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}.       \tag{8}
\]
Consequently, conditional on the training data, the exact nuisance drift in the estimator of the arm mean is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)
 +\mathbb E_O\{(q+\Delta q_\ell)(Y-h-\Delta h_\ell)\mid A=a\}\\
 &\hspace{32mm}=-\mathbb E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a). \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]
The terms linear in \(\Delta h_\ell\) cancel by (8), while the residual terms linear in \(q\) and \(\Delta q_\ell\) vanish by (7).

Source-arm positivity lets us compare conditional and source norms by fixed constants.  Iterated expectation through \(T_{d_B}\), and alternatively through its adjoint, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathbb E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B}
       \|T_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B},\\
 |\mathbb E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|T_{d_B}^\star\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}
       \|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}.                  \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]
The artificial two-source law along this path assigns both sources masses bounded away from zero by (4), so its \(L_2\) norm is uniformly equivalent to the source-conditional norms.  The weak, strong, and product-rate assumptions applied to (10) show that (9) is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed folds and two arms.

The centered experimental nuisance term has norm \(o_P(1)\) by the outcome strong-rate condition, so conditional Chebyshev and (5) make its empirical contribution \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  The centered observational nuisance term is
\[
 \Delta q_\ell(Y-h)-q\Delta h_\ell-\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell. \tag{11}
\]
Its \(L_2(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm is \(o_P(1)\) by the multiplier condition.  Conditional Chebyshev on each evaluation fold and a finite-fold union bound again give \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  The specified training scheme supplies the needed conditional independence: the outcome bridge used in the experimental term is trained on observational observations, while each observational evaluation fold is excluded from the training data for both bridges.

Apply (5) to the remaining oracle terms.  Combining the drift and centered-term bounds proves
\[
 \widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)
 =\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 +o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                         \tag{12}
\]
This expansion is centered at the observed bridge contrast and uses no causal equality.

The two leading sums are independent and centered, with variances \(V_{E,d_B}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d_B}/o_B\).  We verify their limit directly.  For any subsequence, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) supplies a further subsequence on which \(d_B\) is constant.  If \(Q\) is either corresponding centered square-integrable source score, dominated convergence applied to
\[
 \frac{e^{iuQ}-1-iuQ}{u^2}
\]
as \(u\to0\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E(e^{iuQ})=1-\frac{u^2}{2}\mathbb E(Q^2)+o(u^2).
\]
Substituting \(u\) equal to the source-specific coefficient of each summand, multiplying the characteristic functions within sources, and then multiplying the two independent source characteristic functions gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B}{s_{0,B}(d_B,\alpha_B)}
 \left\{
 \frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 \right\}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1),                       \tag{13}
\]
where
\[
 s_{0,B}^2(d_B,\alpha_B)
 =B\left(\frac{V_{E,d_B}}{e_B}+\frac{V_{O,d_B}}{o_B}\right).
\]
Nondegeneracy and (4) keep this variance bounded above and away from zero.  Since every subsequence has a further subsequence with the same limit, (13) holds along the original deterministic path.  Equations (12)--(13) prove its oracle-studentized limit without any moment beyond the declared second moments.

We next transfer the deterministic-path result to the independently selected random path.  From (2), choose deterministic \(t_B\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\{\,|\widehat\alpha_m-\alpha_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)|>t_B\,\}
 \longrightarrow0.                                      \tag{14}
\]
The conclusions above are uniform over
\[
 \{(d,\alpha):d\in\mathcal D,
 |\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)|\le t_B\}.                       \tag{15}
\]
Otherwise one could choose a deterministic violating pair from (15) along a subsequence, contradicting the result just proved for every deterministic path.  Pilot--main-study independence permits conditioning on \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\).  Thus (12)--(15) imply
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)
 -\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                       \tag{16}
\]

The floor-count allocation conclusion of Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\), evaluated at the selected design, gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).        \tag{17}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\), direct subtraction gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 \le{}&\frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &+\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|. \tag{18}
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficients are \(O_P(1)\) by (1)--(2), and the selected-design main-study variance conditions make the two errors \(o_P(1)\).  Combining (3), (17), and (18) gives
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).   \tag{19}
\]
The finite family and nondegeneracy imply \(\min_d\mathcal V_d^*(P)>0\).  Equations (3), (17), and (19) therefore show
\[
 P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad
 \widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\longrightarrow_P1.               \tag{20}
\]
The oracle-studentized statistic in (16) is tight, and (20) implies that multiplication by \(s_{0,B}/\widehat s_B\) changes it by \(o_P(1)\).  For every bounded Lipschitz test function, this \(o_P(1)\) change has vanishing expectation after truncating on a high-probability bounded set.  Hence the feasible statistic has the same standard-normal limit.  Because the complement of \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\) has vanishing probability, the declared zero extension has that limit as well.

On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the coverage event is exactly
\[
 \{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}
 =\{|\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\}.
\]
The complement of \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\) has vanishing probability, and the standard normal distribution is continuous at the two critical points.  The studentized limit therefore yields
\[
 P\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}\longrightarrow1-\eta.
\]
This completes the observable-contrast result.  Every rate, multiplier, and variance-consistency input used above was invoked as an explicit estimator-level hypothesis, not inferred from the observable law class or from fixed-design theory.
