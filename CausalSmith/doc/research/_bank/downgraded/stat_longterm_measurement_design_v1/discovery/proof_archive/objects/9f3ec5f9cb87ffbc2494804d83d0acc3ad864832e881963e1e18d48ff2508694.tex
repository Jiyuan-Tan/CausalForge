Put
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}.
\]
The pilot assumptions give \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  Write
\[
 \nu_0=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0.
\]
Because \(\underline v_m\downarrow0\), the event
\[
 \mathcal E_m
 =\{Z_m<\nu_0/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_0/2\}                 \tag{1}
\]
has probability tending to one.  On \(\mathcal E_m\), simultaneously for every \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(g\in\{E,O\}\),
\[
 \widehat V_{g,d}\ge V_{g,d}-Z_m>\nu_0/2>\underline v_m.          \tag{2}
\]
Thus every variance truncation in the pilot allocation is inactive on \(\mathcal E_m\).

For each \(d\), define
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}.
\]
The population variance pairs and, on \(\mathcal E_m\), the estimated variance pairs lie in a common compact rectangle contained in \((0,\infty)^2\).  Positive finite costs and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) imply that the first derivatives of all the maps \(A_d\) are uniformly bounded on that rectangle.  The mean-value theorem and (2) therefore yield
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}).                                                     \tag{3}
\]

We next record the exact curvature identity.  Fix \(d\) and abbreviate
\[
 a=c_{E,d}V_{E,d},\qquad b=c_{O,d}V_{O,d},
 \qquad
 F_d(\alpha)=\frac a\alpha+\frac b{1-\alpha},
\]
\[
 \alpha_d^*(P)=\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b},
 \qquad
 v_d^*=\mathcal V_d^*(P)=(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2.
\]
For every \(0<\alpha<1\), direct algebra gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F_d(\alpha)-v_d^*
 &=\frac{\{(1-\alpha)\sqrt a-\alpha\sqrt b\}^2}
 {\alpha(1-\alpha)}\\
 &=v_d^*\frac{\{\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)\}^2}
 {\alpha(1-\alpha)}.                                               \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]
This is an identity, not merely a Taylor bound.

For the exact integer-allocation decomposition, let
\[
 x_E=\frac{\alpha B}{c_{E,d}},\qquad
 x_O=\frac{(1-\alpha)B}{c_{O,d}},
 \qquad k_E=\lfloor x_E\rfloor,
 \qquad k_O=\lfloor x_O\rfloor.
\]
Whenever \(k_E,k_O\ge1\), define
\[
 R_{\mathrm{floor},d,B}(\alpha)
 =B V_{E,d}\left(\frac1{k_E}-\frac1{x_E}\right)
 +B V_{O,d}\left(\frac1{k_O}-\frac1{x_O}\right).                 \tag{5}
\]
Since \(k_E\le x_E\) and \(k_O\le x_O\), this remainder is nonnegative.  Moreover, since
\[
 B\left(\frac{V_{E,d}}{x_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{x_O}\right)
 =F_d(\alpha),
\]
equations (4) and (5) give the exact positive-count identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &B\left\{\frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\alpha B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\alpha)B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}\right\}
 -v_d^*\\
 &\qquad={}
 v_d^*\frac{\{\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)\}^2}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}
 +R_{\mathrm{floor},d,B}(\alpha).                                 \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
For every \(x>1\),
\[
 0\le \frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x
 =\frac{x-\lfloor x\rfloor}{x\lfloor x\rfloor}
 <\frac1{x(x-1)},                                                   \tag{7}
\]
because \(x-\lfloor x\rfloor<1\) and \(\lfloor x\rfloor>x-1\).  Hence, whenever both continuous counts exceed one,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 0\le R_{\mathrm{floor},d,B}(\alpha)
 <{}&\frac{V_{E,d}c_{E,d}^2}
 {\alpha(\alpha B-c_{E,d})}\\
 &+\frac{V_{O,d}c_{O,d}^2}
 {(1-\alpha)\{(1-\alpha)B-c_{O,d}\}}.                            \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]

It remains to take maxima uniformly.  Since \(\mathcal D\) is finite and all \(a,b\) are strictly positive, there exists a deterministic \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le \alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).
\]
By (3), with probability tending to one,
\[
 a_0\le\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\le1-a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).                                         \tag{9}
\]
On this event, the exact curvature term in (4), evaluated at \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\), is uniformly \(O_P(r_{V,m}^2)\).  For all sufficiently large \(B\), (9) also makes both continuous counts exceed one, and the right-hand side of (8) is uniformly \(O(B^{-1})\).  Thus (6), evaluated at \(\alpha=\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\), gives
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
\frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
+\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
\right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
=O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1}).                                            \tag{10}
\]
On the event (9) every floor count is eventually positive.  Its complement has probability tending to zero, so assigning the entire expression the value zero when a floor count vanishes does not change the stochastic order in (10).  Equation (4) proves that allocation estimation enters quadratically, and (1)--(2) prove that the truncation is asymptotically inactive, completing the proof.
