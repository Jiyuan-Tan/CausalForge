Write \(r=1/4\).  First consider a generic witness design whose early and late binary signals are \(x\) and \(y\), respectively.  Conditional independence of the proxies given \(U\) and Bayes' rule give, for each fixed treatment value, the determinant of the binary conditional-expectation matrix
\[
 \det(T_d)=\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}>0. \tag{1}
\]
The early- and late-proxy completeness matrices have determinants proportional to \(x\) and \(y\), so they are nonsingular whenever \(x,y>0\).  Direct conditional expectation verifies the displayed bridges
\[
 h_d(l,a)=\frac l y,
 \qquad
 q_d(e,a)=\frac{1-r(2a-1)e/x}{1-r^2}. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed,
\[
 \mathbb E_P[U\mid W_y=l,T=t,G=O]=\frac{rt+yl}{1+rtyl},
\]
and evaluating the conditional expectation of \(q_d(W_x,a)\) given \((W_y,T)=(l,t)\) gives \((1+rtyl)^{-1}\), which is precisely the experimental-to-observational density ratio for the late proxy at treatment \(t\).  This proves both bridge equations and, together with (1), operator bijectivity.  Every finite cell has positive probability, so observational and experimental treatment overlap and source support hold.  Consistency, latent exchangeability, randomization, full-short-term external validity, and the sequential restriction follow from the construction and conditional independence.  Finite support makes both bridges bounded and supplies finite moments of every order.  Thus \(P_{\epsilon}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\) for every \(\epsilon>0\).

Because \(Y=U\), least-squares projection on the two measured proxies gives
\[
 R(x,y)=\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{Var}(U\mid W_x,W_y)\mid G=O]
 =\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}. \tag{3}
\]
Consequently
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac35,
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
\]
and
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}-R_{d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{13+8\epsilon^2}{5(16-9\epsilon^2)}\ge\frac{13}{80}. \tag{4}
\]
Both designs measure two unit-cost variables.  Thus their experimental costs are \(3\), their observational costs are \(4\), and their jointly budget-feasible comparator sample sizes are \(B/7\).  Equation (4) gives
\[
 \Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\epsilon};B)
 =\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)(7/16)}{B(1-9\epsilon^2/16)}
 <\frac{21}{5B}
 =\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_{\epsilon};B),
\]
so \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\epsilon})=\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\), strictly.

We now compute the gradient variances.  Since \(p_{g,a}=1/2\), equation (2) and conditional independence give
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}. \tag{5}
\]
Moreover, direct summation over \((U,T,W_x,W_y)\), using
\(\mathbb E_P[(U-W_y/y)^2]=(1-y^2)/y^2\), gives
\[
 V_O(x,y)=\frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
 \left(1-2r^2+\frac{r^2}{x^2}\right). \tag{6}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), equations (5)--(6) yield
\[
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac4{(15/16)^2}\frac79
 \left(\frac78+\frac1{16\epsilon^2}\right)
 =\frac{4(1/16)(7/9)}{(15/16)^2\epsilon^2}+O(1), \tag{7}
\]
which in particular implies the displayed weaker \(O(\epsilon^{-1})\) remainder.  The experimental component for this design is \(64/9\).  For \(d_{\mathrm e}\),
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25},
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\epsilon})
 =\left(4\sqrt3+\frac{16\sqrt6}{5}\right)^2<225. \tag{8}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\epsilon})
 \ge4V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 \ge\frac{1792}{2025\epsilon^2}. \tag{9}
\]
For \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), equations (8)--(9) make \(d_{\mathrm e}\) uniquely efficiency-optimal.  They also show
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\epsilon})
 =\frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\epsilon})}
 {\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\epsilon})}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{10}
\]

It remains to verify the neighborhood assertion.  On the fixed binary support in the definition of \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\), every conditional probability, bridge coefficient, prediction risk, and gradient variance is a rational function of the complete latent-cell vector.  At \(P_{\epsilon}\), the probability denominators are uniformly separated from zero for \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\); the only vanishing determinant is the early-proxy determinant, which is of order \(\epsilon\).  Applying the two-by-two adjugate formula before and after a cell perturbation of size \(\delta\) gives, uniformly along the path,
\[
 \|h_d(P)-h_d(P_{\epsilon})\|_{\infty}=O(\delta\epsilon^{-2}),
 \quad
 \|q_d(P)-q_d(P_{\epsilon})\|_{\infty}=O(\delta\epsilon^{-2}),
 \quad
 |V_{g,d}(P)-V_{g,d}(P_{\epsilon})|=O(\delta\epsilon^{-3}), \tag{11}
\]
while the prediction risks vary by \(O(\delta)\).  These bounds follow by subtracting the rational expressions, using \(A^{-1}-\widetilde A^{-1}=A^{-1}(\widetilde A-A)\widetilde A^{-1}\), and noting that there are only finitely many cells and two designs.  With \(\delta<10^{-6}\epsilon^4\), the three errors in (11) are respectively \(O(10^{-6}\epsilon^2)\), \(O(10^{-6}\epsilon^2)\), and \(O(10^{-6}\epsilon)\).  The same calculation bounds determinant perturbations by \(O(10^{-6}\epsilon^4)\), whereas the determinants in (1) and the early completeness determinant are of order \(\epsilon\).

Thus the fixed predictive margin \(13/80\), all nonzero determinants, and the efficiency separation between the order-\(\epsilon^{-2}\) lower bound in (9) and the bounded value in (8) persist throughout every ball in the definition of \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\).  By construction the region is also intersected with \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), so every one of its laws is regular for both designs.  Hence
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\cap D^*(P)=\varnothing
 \qquad(P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}).
\]
Each center \(P_{\epsilon}\) itself belongs to its strict ball.  Equation (10) therefore proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\infty,
\]
which establishes the claimed lack of a finite prediction-based approximation guarantee.
