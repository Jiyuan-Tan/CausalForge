Write
\[
 v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P),\qquad
 \widehat v_d=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*,
 \qquad v_*=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d,
\]
and choose any \(d^*\in D^*(P)\).  By the definition of \(\varepsilon_m\) and the empirical minimizing property of \(\widehat d_m\),
\[
 v_{\widehat d_m}
 \le \widehat v_{\widehat d_m}+\varepsilon_m
 \le \widehat v_{d^*}+\varepsilon_m
 \le v_{d^*}+2\varepsilon_m
 =v_*+2\varepsilon_m.                                             \tag{1}
\]
Since \(v_*\) is the population minimum,
\[
 0\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m.                     \tag{2}
\]
If \(\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\), the definition of \(\gamma(P)\) and (2) imply
\[
 \gamma(P)\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m,             \tag{3}
\]
which proves the event inclusion.  If \(D^*(P)=\mathcal D\), the left event is empty and, under the convention \(\gamma(P)=+\infty\), so is the right event.  Otherwise finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) gives \(\gamma(P)>0\).

Set
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}.
\]
The pilot conditions yield \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  By the pilot construction and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\),
\[
 \widehat v_d=G_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d}),
 \qquad
 v_d=G_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d}),                                        \tag{4}
\]
where
\[
 G_d(x,y)=\bigl(\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}\bigr)^2.
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), positive costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) put the population variance pairs in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\).  With probability tending to one, the estimated pairs lie in a slightly larger compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\).  The gradients of the finitely many maps \(G_d\) are uniformly bounded there.  The mean-value theorem therefore gives
\[
 \varepsilon_m
 =\max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat v_d-v_d|
 =O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).                                             \tag{5}
\]
Equations (3) and (5) prove the asserted probability limit when a suboptimal design exists; the all-optimal case was handled above.

For the allocation assertion, put
\[
 \nu_0=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0
\]
and consider
\[
 \mathcal E_m
 =\{Z_m<\nu_0/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_0/2\}.             \tag{6}
\]
Its probability tends to one, and on it all estimated variances exceed \(\underline v_m\), so every truncation is inactive.  For
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}},
\]
the same compact-set mean-value argument gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=O_P(r_{V,m}).                \tag{7}
\]

We spell out the exact loss decomposition underlying the rate.  For a fixed \(d\), let
\[
 a=c_{E,d}V_{E,d},\qquad b=c_{O,d}V_{O,d},
 \qquad
 F_d(\alpha)=\frac a\alpha+\frac b{1-\alpha},
\]
\[
 \alpha_d^*(P)=\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b},
 \qquad
 v_d^*=(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2=\mathcal V_d^*(P).
\]
Then, for every \(0<\alpha<1\),
\[
 F_d(\alpha)-v_d^*
 =v_d^*\frac{\{\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)\}^2}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}.       \tag{8}
\]
Indeed, the numerator after placing the left-hand side over \(\alpha(1-\alpha)\) is
\(\{(1-\alpha)\sqrt a-\alpha\sqrt b\}^2
=(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2\{\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)\}^2\).

Whenever the two floor counts at \(\alpha\) are positive, set
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_{\mathrm{floor},d,B}(\alpha)
 ={}&B V_{E,d}\left\{
 \frac1{\lfloor\alpha B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 -\frac{c_{E,d}}{\alpha B}\right\}\\
 &+B V_{O,d}\left\{
 \frac1{\lfloor(1-\alpha)B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 -\frac{c_{O,d}}{(1-\alpha)B}\right\}.
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{9}
\]
This remainder is nonnegative, and (8) gives the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\alpha B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\alpha)B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}-v_d^*\\
 &\qquad={}
 v_d^*\frac{\{\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)\}^2}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}
 +R_{\mathrm{floor},d,B}(\alpha).                                 \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(x>1\),
\[
 0\le\frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x
 <\frac1{x(x-1)}.
\]
Consequently, if both continuous counts exceed one,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 0\le R_{\mathrm{floor},d,B}(\alpha)
 <{}&\frac{V_{E,d}c_{E,d}^2}
 {\alpha(\alpha B-c_{E,d})}\\
 &+\frac{V_{O,d}c_{O,d}^2}
 {(1-\alpha)\{(1-\alpha)B-c_{O,d}\}}.                            \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus (7) and (8) show directly that the continuous allocation loss is uniformly \(O_P(r_{V,m}^2)\), while (11), on the uniform interior event implied by (7), is uniformly \(O(B^{-1})\).

Equivalently, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}second\text{-}order\text{-}allocation\text{-}loss}\), which establishes (8)--(11) together with the total zero-count convention, gives
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
\frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
+\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
\right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
=O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1}).                                            \tag{12}
\]
Because \(r_{V,m}=o(1)\) and \(B\to\infty\), the right-hand side is \(o_P(1)\).  The deterministic tie rule was used only to choose an empirical minimizer in (1), so consistency is membership in the complete optimizer set \(D^*(P)\), not convergence to one arbitrarily chosen member.
