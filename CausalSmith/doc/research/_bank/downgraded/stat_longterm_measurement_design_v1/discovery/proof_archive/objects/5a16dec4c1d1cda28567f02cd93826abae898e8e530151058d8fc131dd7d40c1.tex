Let \(t_m:=r_{V,m}+\underline v_m\).  Positive nondegenerate population variances, positive costs, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\), and the pilot variance-rate assumption imply, by the mean-value theorem applied to the square-root allocation map,
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=O_P(t_m).                              \tag{1}
\]
Because the family is finite and every population variance component is strictly positive and finite, there is a compact interval \([a_0,1-a_0]\subset(0,1)\) containing every \(\alpha_d^*(P)\).  Equation (1) and \(t_m=o(1)\) therefore imply that, with probability tending to one, every \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\) lies in a common compact subinterval of \((0,1)\).

For fixed \(d\), the budget-profile theorem gives
\[
 \mathcal V_d(\alpha;P)
 =\frac{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{\alpha}
  +\frac{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{1-\alpha}.
\]
On the common compact interval,
\[
 \frac{\partial^2}{\partial\alpha^2}\mathcal V_d(\alpha;P)
 =\frac{2c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{\alpha^3}
  +\frac{2c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{(1-\alpha)^3}
\]
is uniformly bounded over \(d\in\mathcal D\), while
\(\partial_\alpha\mathcal V_d(\alpha_d^*(P);P)=0\).  Taylor's theorem and (1) yield
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 \left|\mathcal V_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m};P)-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
 =O_P(t_m^2).                                                               \tag{2}
\]

For \(x\ge2\),
\[
 0\le \frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x\le\frac2{x^2}.                    \tag{3}
\]
On the same high-probability compact-allocation event, both continuous source counts are bounded below by a positive constant times \(B\), uniformly over \(d\).  Applying (3) to each count therefore gives, after multiplication by \(B\),
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|
 B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}
 -\mathcal V_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m};P)
 \right|=O_P(B^{-1}).                                                       \tag{4}
\]
Combining (2) and (4) proves the displayed result.  The absence of a first-order term in (2) proves the final assertion.
