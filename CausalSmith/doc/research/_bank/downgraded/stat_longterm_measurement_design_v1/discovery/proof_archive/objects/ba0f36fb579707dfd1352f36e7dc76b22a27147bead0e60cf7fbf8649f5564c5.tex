Put \(r=1/4\), and first consider a generic center-law design whose early and late proxies have signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\). Conditional on \(U\), the signed treatment \(T\), the early proxy \(W_x\), and the late proxy \(W_y\) are mutually independent, with conditional means \(rU\), \(xU\), and \(yU\). Since \(U\) is Rademacher, \(p_{g,a}=1/2\) in both sources.

Fix \(T=t\). The two-by-two matrix of \(T_d\), with rows indexed by the early proxy and columns by the late proxy, has determinant
\[
\det(T_d)=\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}>0. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, Bayes' rule makes it the difference between the two conditional probabilities of \(W_y=1\),
\[
\frac y2\left\{\frac{x+rt}{1+rtx}-\frac{-x+rt}{1-rtx}\right\}
=\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}.
\]
For a single proxy \(W_s\), the determinant of the conditional-expectation map from functions of \(U\) to functions of \(W_s\), conditional on \(T=t\), is
\[
\frac{s(1-r^2)}{1-r^2s^2}\ne0. \tag{2}
\]
Thus both completeness implications and operator bijectivity hold.

The bridge \(h_d(l,a)=l/y\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E\{h_d(W_y,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O\}=u
=\mathbb E(Y\mid U=u,T=t,G=O). \tag{3}
\]
The latent source-density ratio is
\[
L_t(u)=\frac{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=E)}{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=O)}
=\frac1{1+rtu}. \tag{4}
\]
The proposed selection bridge \(q_d(e,a)=\{1-r(2a-1)e/x\}/(1-r^2)\) obeys
\[
\mathbb E\{q_d(W_x,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O\}
=\frac{1-rtu}{1-r^2}=\frac1{1+rtu}=L_t(u). \tag{5}
\]
Iterated expectation in (3) and (5) proves the two observed bridge equations. The nonzero channel determinants make the latent bridges unique, and (1) makes their observed representations unique. Strict cell positivity gives both overlaps and source support. Consistency, exchangeability, randomization, external validity, and the sequential restriction follow directly from the construction and conditional independence. Finite support gives all moments. The positive variances calculated below verify nondegeneracy, hence \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

Experimental randomization, source invariance, and (3) give
\[
\mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E\{h_d(W_y,a)\mid A=a,G=E\}
=\mathbb E(U\mid G=O), \tag{6}
\]
independently of \(a\). Since \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), both bridge contrasts equal \(0=\tau\).

The best predictor of \(U\) from \((W_x,W_y)=(e,l)\) is
\[
\mathbb E(U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l)=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}.
\]
Averaging its conditional variance over the four cells gives
\[
R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}. \tag{7}
\]
Therefore
\[
R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)
=\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
\qquad R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35. \tag{8}
\]
The paired costs are \(6\) and \(5\), so
\[
B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
=\frac{42(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2}
<3=B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B). \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is uniquely prediction-selected despite its higher cost.

The experimental arm-normalized score reduces to \(2TW_y/y\), and hence
\[
V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}. \tag{10}
\]
The observational score is \(2Tq_d(W_x,A)(U-W_y/y)\). Conditional on \(U\), its residual factor has second moment \((1-y^2)/y^2\), independent of \((T,W_x)\), and \(\mathbb E(TW_x\mid G=O)=rx\). Consequently
\[
V_O(x,y)=\frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
\left(1-2r^2+\frac{r^2}{x^2}\right). \tag{11}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), \((x,y)=(\epsilon,3/4)\), so
\[
V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{64}{9},\qquad
V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
=\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025}. \tag{12}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), \((x,y)=(1/2,1/2)\), so
\[
V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,\qquad V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}. \tag{13}
\]
The budget profile and costs \(2,3\) give
\[
\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
=\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot\frac{384}{25}}\right)^2
=\frac{3872}{25}. \tag{14}
\]
For \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\),
\[
\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
\ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
>\frac{1568}{2025\cdot10^{-6}}
>\frac{3872}{25}, \tag{15}
\]
so \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is uniquely design-value optimal. Expanding the squared profile in (12) yields
\[
\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
=\frac{1568}{2025\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}),
\]
and division by (14) proves
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
=\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{16}
\]

For stability, fix \(\epsilon\) and parameterize the constrained model by its finitely many positive latent-cell probabilities. For a nearby law \(Q\), let \(K_{j,Q}\) be the two-by-two proxy channel. Each \(K_{j,Q}\) remains invertible near \(P_\epsilon\). Construct the nearby latent outcome and selection bridges from
\[
K_{3d,Q}h_{d,a,Q}=(u:u\in\{-1,1\})^{\mathsf T}, \tag{17}
\]
\[
K_{1d,Q}q_{d,a,Q}
=\left(\frac{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=E)}
{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=O)}:u\in\{-1,1\}\right)^{\mathsf T}. \tag{18}
\]
Strict positivity makes the ratios finite. Conditional independence and iterated expectation make (17)--(18) solve the observed bridge equations. The observed matrix of \(T_d\) is a product of the invertible proxy channels and positive diagonal reweighting matrices, so it is invertible.

The bridge coefficients are rational continuous functions of the cell probabilities. Prediction risks, score moments, and profiled values are continuous while cells and determinants stay away from zero. At \(P_\epsilon\), (1)--(16) give strict regularity, prediction-selection, and design-selection margins. Hence there is \(r_\epsilon>0\) such that every constrained-model law within cell distance \(r_\epsilon\) remains regular and retains both strict optimizer assignments. The ratio has a positive denominator and is continuous; shrinking \(r_\epsilon\), if necessary, gives
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(Q)>\frac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon).
\]
This is the radius denoted \(r_\epsilon^{\mathrm{opt}}\) in the corrected binary-region definition.
