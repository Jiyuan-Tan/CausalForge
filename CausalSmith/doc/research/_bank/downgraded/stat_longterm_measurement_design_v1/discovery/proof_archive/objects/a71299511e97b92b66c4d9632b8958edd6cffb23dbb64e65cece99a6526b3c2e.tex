The full-catalogue pilot observes both sources with source and arm probabilities bounded away from zero.  On the fixed finite-dimensional subclass, every candidate design has known bounded outcome- and selection-basis vectors, exact bridge coefficients, uniformly nonsingular conditional-moment and Gram matrices, and a uniform \((4+\delta)\)-moment bound.  Fixed-fold cross-fitted sample-moment estimators are used for both bridges, and empirical plug-in score second moments estimate the two variance components.  Pilot source-arm samples and main source, arm, and fold samples are proportional to \(m\) and \(B\), respectively.  Uniformly over the finite catalogue, strong and weak bridge errors are \(O_P(m^{-1/2})\) in the pilot and \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\) in the main study; the main product remainder is \(O_P(B^{-1})\), the multiplier envelope is \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\), and pilot and selected-main score variances are \(O_P(m^{-1/2})\) and \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\) consistent.  Outside this subclass, the generic Wald theorem continues to require its displayed estimator-level conditions.
