Put \(t=2a-1\), and first consider a center design having early signal \(x\in(0,1)\) and late signal \(y\in(0,1)\). Write \(E=W_x\) and \(L=W_y\). All latent cells are strictly positive. Consistency follows from \(Y(a)=U\) and \(Z(a)=Z\). In source \(E\), treatment is independent of \((U,Z)\); in source \(O\), treatment is independent of \(Z\) conditional on \(U\). The overlap and source-support inequalities are therefore strict. The distribution of \((U,Z)\) is source invariant, and conditional independence of the proxies given \(U\) gives the sequential-outcome restriction.

The proposed outcome bridge obeys
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{L}{y}\mathrel{\Big|}U\right]=U.
\]
Conditional independence of \(E\) and \(L\) given \(U\), including after conditioning on \(T=t,G=O\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E[Y\mid E,T=t,G=O]
 =\mathbb E[U\mid E,T=t,G=O]
 =\mathbb E[L/y\mid E,T=t,G=O].                         \tag{7}
\]
Bayes' rule also gives
\[
 \mathbb E[E\mid L=l,T=t,G=O]
 =x\frac{t/4+yl}{1+tyl/4}.                              \tag{8}
\]
For
\[
 q(e,a)=\frac{1-te/(4x)}{1-1/16}
 =\frac{16}{15}\left(1-\frac{te}{4x}\right),           \tag{9}
\]
substitution of (8) yields
\[
 \mathbb E[q(E,a)\mid L=l,T=t,G=O]
 =\frac1{1+tyl/4}
 =\frac{p(L=l\mid T=t,G=E)}{p(L=l\mid T=t,G=O)}.        \tag{10}
\]
Thus (7) and (10) are the outcome- and selection-bridge equations.

For binary \(U\), conditional expectation given a binary proxy has rank two exactly when its two conditional rows differ. Since \(x,y\ne0\), both completeness conditions hold. Further,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(E,L\mid T=t,G=O)
 =xy-\frac{xt}{4}\frac{yt}{4}=\frac{15}{16}xy\ne0.     \tag{11}
\]
All four conditional cells are positive, so (11) says that the \(2\)-by-\(2\) conditional-expectation matrix from functions of \(L\) to functions of \(E\) has nonzero determinant. Hence the bridge operator is bijective. Finite support supplies every required moment, and the score variances calculated below are finite and positive. This verifies every member condition in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}regular\text{-}law\text{-}class}\), so \(P_{\chi,\epsilon}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

At the experimental center, \(p_{E,a}=1/2\), \(\mathbb E[L\mid A=a,G=E]=0\), and \(\mu_{d,a}=0\). Since \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), both bridge contrasts and \(\tau\) are zero. The experimental score is \(2TL/y\), whence
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}.                                  \tag{12}
\]
For the observational score, (9) gives \(2Tq(E,A)(U-L/y)\). The center moments
\[
 \mathbb E[TE]=\frac{x}{4},\qquad
 \mathbb E[UL]=y,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[TEUL]=\frac{xy}{4}                           \tag{13}
\]
therefore imply, after expanding both squares,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 V_O(x,y)
 &=\frac{1024}{225}\,
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left\{1-\frac{TE}{2x}+\frac1{16x^2}\right\}
 \left\{1+\frac1{y^2}-\frac{2UL}{y}\right\}
 \right]\\
 &=\frac{64}{225}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}\left(14+x^{-2}\right). \tag{14}
\end{aligned}
\]
The conditional mean of \(U\) given \((E,L)=(e,l)\) is
\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid E=e,L=l]=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel},
\]
and \(P(E=e,L=l)=(1+xyel)/4\). Averaging the conditional variance yields
\[
 R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.               \tag{15}
\]

For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), take \(x=\epsilon\) and \(y^2=1-3/(10\chi)\). Direct substitution in the primitive cost formulas gives
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{5\chi-1}{2},\qquad
 c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{5\chi+1}{2},
\]
whereas \(c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=2\) and \(c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=3\). Hence
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =\frac{5(\chi-1)}2>0.                                 \tag{16}
\]
Equation (15) and the paired costs give
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac32<3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B), \tag{17}
\]
so \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is uniquely diagnostic-selected.

Equations (12)--(14) give
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{40\chi}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14),           \tag{18}
\]
and, for \(d_{\mathrm e}\),
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,\qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}.                   \tag{19}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) now yields
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\left(\sqrt{32}+\sqrt{1152/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}.                                     \tag{20}
\]
For the other design, put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_\chi
 &=c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3},\\
 b_{\chi,\epsilon}
 &=c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}
   (\epsilon^{-2}+14).                                  \tag{21}
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \((5\chi+1)/(10\chi-3)>1/2\),
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \ge b_{\chi,\epsilon}
 >\frac{16}{75}\epsilon^{-2}
 >\frac{3872}{25}                                      \tag{22}
\]
for \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\). Thus \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is uniquely value-optimal. Dividing \((\sqrt{a_\chi}+\sqrt{b_{\chi,\epsilon}})^2\) by (20) proves the arm-normalized identity (1) in the proposed statement.

Treatment is balanced in both sources at every center, so \(p_{E,1}=p_{O,1}=1/2\). The IKM-normalized source moments are therefore one quarter of their arm-normalized counterparts, every profiled value is multiplied by one quarter, and the minimizing design and approximation ratio are unchanged. This proves the IKM equality in (1).

Discarding the nonnegative \(a_\chi\) and cross terms in the exact ratio gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \ge \frac{25}{3872}b_{\chi,\epsilon}
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14).     \tag{23}
\]
The identity
\[
 \frac{5\chi+1}{10\chi-3}-\frac12
 =\frac5{2(10\chi-3)}>0                                \tag{24}
\]
proves the strict pointwise inequality in (2). For fixed \(\epsilon\), subtract \((\epsilon^{-2}+14)/726\) from the exact ratio. The difference extends continuously to \(\chi=1\), is strictly positive at every finite \(\chi\ge1\) by (23)--(24), and tends to \(+\infty\) as \(\chi\to\infty\) because \(a_\chi\sim10\chi\). Compactness on a sufficiently large interval therefore makes its infimum strictly positive. This proves (3).

It remains to optimize the exact ratio sharply. Put
\[
 \delta=\epsilon^{2/3},\qquad \chi=\frac{k}{\delta},
 \qquad k>\delta,
\]
and use the exact decompositions
\[
 a_\chi=10\chi+1+\frac3{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 \frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}
 =\frac{16}{75}+\frac{16}{15(10\chi-3)}.              \tag{25}
\]
Define
\[
 A_\delta(k)=\delta^3a_{k/\delta},
 \qquad B_\delta(k)=\delta^3b_{k/\delta,\epsilon}.
\]
Because \(\epsilon^2=\delta^3\), (25) gives the exact formulas
\[
 A_\delta(k)
 =10k\delta^2+\delta^3+\frac{3\delta^4}{10k-3\delta}, \tag{26}
\]
\[
 B_\delta(k)
 =\left\{\frac{16}{75}
 +\frac{16\delta}{15(10k-3\delta)}\right\}
 (1+14\delta^3).                                      \tag{27}
\]
Consequently the exact scaled braces-squared expression is
\[
 J_\delta(k)=A_\delta(k)+B_\delta(k)
 +2\sqrt{A_\delta(k)B_\delta(k)},                     \tag{28}
\]
and, locally uniformly for \(k\in(0,\infty)\),
\[
 \frac{J_\delta(k)-16/75}{\delta}
 \longrightarrow
 q(k):=2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac{8}{75k}.          \tag{29}
\]
Indeed, on every compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), (26)--(27) imply uniformly
\[
 \frac{A_\delta(k)}{\delta^2}\longrightarrow10k,
 \qquad
 B_\delta(k)\longrightarrow\frac{16}{75},
 \qquad
 \frac{B_\delta(k)-16/75}{\delta}
 \longrightarrow\frac{8}{75k},                       \tag{30}
\]
and substitution of (30) into (28) proves (29).

We next justify that local uniformity suffices for the global infimum. For every fixed \(\epsilon\), the exact objective extends continuously to \(\chi=1\) and diverges as \(\chi\to\infty\), so it attains a minimum on \([1,\infty)\). At the boundary,
\[
 \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\epsilon^2b_{1,\epsilon}
 =\frac{64}{175}>\frac{16}{75},                        \tag{31}
\]
whereas (28) at any fixed \(k>0\) converges to \(16/75\). Thus, for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), no minimizer is \(\chi=1\), and the infimum over \(\chi>1\) is attained.

Let \(k_\delta=\delta\chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)\) for any minimizer. Comparison with a fixed positive \(k\) in (29) bounds
\[
 H_\delta(k_\delta)
 :=\frac{J_\delta(k_\delta)-16/75}{\delta}
\]
from above uniformly as \(\delta\downarrow0\). Every term beyond \(16/75\) in (26)--(28) is nonnegative. Hence (27) gives
\[
 H_\delta(k_\delta)
 \ge \frac{16}{15(10k_\delta-3\delta)},               \tag{32}
\]
which bounds \(k_\delta\) away from zero. Likewise, (26)--(28) give
\[
 H_\delta(k_\delta)
 \ge \frac{2\sqrt{A_\delta(k_\delta)B_\delta(k_\delta)}}{\delta}
 \ge2\sqrt{\frac{32k_\delta}{15}},                   \tag{33}
\]
which bounds \(k_\delta\) from above. Thus all minimizing sequences lie eventually in one compact subset of \((0,\infty)\). By (29), every subsequential limit minimizes \(q\).

Writing \(C=\sqrt{32/15}\), differentiation gives
\[
 q'(k)=\frac{C}{\sqrt{k}}-\frac{8}{75k^2}.
\]
This derivative is negative before and positive after its unique zero, and the zero satisfies
\[
 k^3=\frac{2}{375},
 \qquad
 k=k_\star:=\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15}.                  \tag{34}
\]
Therefore every minimizing sequence obeys \(k_\delta\to k_\star\), which is exactly (4). Minimality, (29), and compact localization now yield
\[
 \inf_{\chi>1}\epsilon^2
 (\sqrt{a_\chi}+\sqrt{b_{\chi,\epsilon}})^2
 =\frac{16}{75}+q(k_\star)\epsilon^{2/3}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3}).                                  \tag{35}
\]
At the stationary point, \(C\sqrt{k_\star}=8/(75k_\star)\), so
\[
 q(k_\star)=\frac{24}{75k_\star}
 =\frac45\sqrt[3]{12}.                                \tag{36}
\]
Finally,
\[
 \frac{25}{3872}\frac{16}{75}=\frac1{726},
 \qquad
 \frac{25}{3872}\frac45\sqrt[3]{12}
 =\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}.                          \tag{37}
\]
Multiplying (35) by \(25/3872\) proves (5)--(6). The IKM ratio is identically the same at every center, so every optimization and expansion applies to it verbatim.
