Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(d\in\mathcal D\), and abbreviate
\[
 H_d=(S_{3d},S_{2d},A,X),
 \qquad
 L_d=(S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X).
\]
Observed outcome-bridge existence gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{Y-h_d(H_d)\mid L_d,G=O\}=0. \tag{1}
\]
Equivalently, \(T_dh_d=\mathbb E_P(Y\mid L_d,G=O)\).  Pointwise bijectivity of \(T_d\) makes this solution unique.  Thus \(h_d\), the two experimental means \(\mu_{d,a}\), and the contrast \(\theta_d(P)=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}\) are identified from the observed source laws.  This step makes no claim about continuity or differentiability of \(T_d^{-1}\).

Now take a bridge-differentiable regular two-source submodel in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}bridge\text{-}differentiable\text{-}submodel}\), with source-conditional scores \(\ell_E,\ell_O\) and bridge derivative \(\dot h_d\).  Ordinary differentiation of the experimental arm mean, justified by regularity of the path and the stipulated \(L_2\) bridge derivative, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot\mu_{d,a}
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{E,a}}
   \{h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a}\}\ell_E
   \mathrel{\Big|}G=E\right]\\
 &\quad+\mathbb E_P\{\dot h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E\}.
                                                        \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
The selection-bridge equation says that \(T_d^\star q_d\) is the conditional density ratio of \((S_{3d},S_{2d},X)\) in source \(E\) relative to source \(O\), within treatment arm \(a\).  Therefore, for every square-integrable \(f(H_d)\), iterated expectation yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{f(H_d)\mid A=a,G=E\}
 =\mathbb E_P\{q_d(L_d)f(H_d)\mid A=a,G=O\}.             \tag{3}
\]

The last condition in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}bridge\text{-}differentiable\text{-}submodel}\) supplies, rather than derives from pointwise bijectivity, the differentiated bridge equation
\[
 T_d\dot h_d
 =\mathbb E_P[\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid L_d,G=O].        \tag{4}
\]
Apply (3) with \(f=\dot h_d\), then use the definition of \(T_d\), (4), and iterated expectation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P(\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E)
 &=\mathbb E_P(q_d\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=O)\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[q_d\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid A=a,G=O].   \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
Subtract the derivative for \(a=0\) from that for \(a=1\).  Source-arm positivity permits rewriting the arm-conditional terms as source-conditional inner products.  Substitution of (5) into (2) then gives
\[
 \dot\theta_d(P)
 =\mathbb E_P(\phi_{E,d}\ell_E\mid G=E)
  +\mathbb E_P(\phi_{O,d}\ell_O\mid G=O).               \tag{6}
\]
The definitions of \(\mu_{d,a}\) imply \(\mathbb E_P(\phi_{E,d}\mid G=E)=0\), while (1) implies \(\mathbb E_P(\phi_{O,d}\mid G=O)=0\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) gives square integrability and finite, strictly positive second moments.  Hence \(\phi_{E,d}\) and \(\phi_{O,d}\) are the two source components of the pathwise gradient on the declared submodel class.  For independent source samples of sizes \(n_E\) and \(n_O\), the variances of their sample averages add and equal
\[
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O}.
\]
Only observable bridge conditions and the declared path differentiability entered the gradient calculation.  No latent causal restriction, completeness condition, or equality with \(\tau\) was used.
