The fixed-proxy long-term data-combination theory and the surrogate-index literature take the measured short-term system and source ratio as given. Existing surrogate-efficiency comparisons ask whether a fixed surrogate set helps, and existing measurement-design results address different linear or Gaussian models or optimize sampling probabilities for a fixed variable set. None provides a cost-aware rule for jointly choosing the measured subset, sequential roles, and source allocation, nor determines whether ordinary long-term predictive selection approximates the resulting efficiency objective. In particular, no prior guarantee covers a prediction-selected design that may itself be arbitrarily, but finitely, more expensive.
