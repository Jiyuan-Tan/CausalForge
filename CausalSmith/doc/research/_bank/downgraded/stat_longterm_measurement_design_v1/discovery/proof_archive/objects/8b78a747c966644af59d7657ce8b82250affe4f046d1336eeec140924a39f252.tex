We first record the consequences of the pilot.  The pilot-oracle-regret theorem and \(r_{V,m}=o(1)\) imply
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).                 \tag{1}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and all costs and source variances are positive and finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0,
 \qquad d\in\mathcal D.                                      \tag{2}
\]
Thus the selected share lies in \([a_0,1-a_0]\) with probability tending to one, and both selected source counts are positive and proportional to \(B\).  The floor-count conclusion of the pilot-oracle-regret theorem, evaluated at the random member \(\widehat d_m\) of the finite family, gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1)              \tag{3}
\]
on the positive-count event, with the stipulated extension elsewhere.

We next prove the expansion needed for inference, without identifying the core's arm-normalized score with the score printed in the cited theorem.  Fix deterministic sequences \(d_B\in\mathcal D\) and \(\alpha_B\in(0,1)\) such that
\[
 |\alpha_B-\alpha_{d_B}^*(P)|\longrightarrow0.                \tag{4}
\]
Write \(e_B,o_B,n_B\) for the associated counts.  From every subsequence, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) permits extraction of a further subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant.  Along it,
\[
 \frac{e_B}{B}\longrightarrow\frac{\alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{E,d}},
 \qquad
 \frac{o_B}{B}\longrightarrow\frac{1-alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{O,d}},
 \qquad n_B\asymp B.                                         \tag{5}
\]
In particular, both source weights of \(P_{B,d,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) are bounded away from zero.

For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), abbreviate
\[
 r_{E,a}=h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a},
 \qquad
 r_{O,a}=q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)
 \{Y-h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\}.                              \tag{6}
\]
The outcome-bridge equation gives
\(\mathbb E_P(r_{O,a}\mid A=a,G=O)=0\), while the definition of \(\mu_{d,a}\) gives
\(\mathbb E_P(r_{E,a}\mid A=a,G=E)=0\).

We will repeatedly use the elementary random-denominator calculation.  If \((I_i,R_i)_{i=1}^k\) are independent and identically distributed, \(I_i\) is Bernoulli with mean \(p\in(0,1)\), \(\mathbb E(I_iR_i)=0\), and \(\mathbb E(I_iR_i^2)<\infty\), then, on the event \(\sum_iI_i>0\),
\[
 \frac{\sum_{i=1}^kI_iR_i}{\sum_{i=1}^kI_i}
 -\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{I_iR_i}{p}
 =\frac{(p-\overline I_k)\overline{IR}_k}{p\overline I_k}
 =O_P(k^{-1}),                                                \tag{7}
\]
because \(\overline I_k-p=O_P(k^{-1/2})\),
\(\overline{IR}_k=O_P(k^{-1/2})\), and \(\overline I_k\) is bounded away from zero with probability tending to one.  Treatment overlap and (5) also show that all experimental and foldwise observational arm counts are positive with probability tending to one.

Apply (7) first to the oracle experimental arm mean and then within each of the fixed number \(L\) of approximately equal observational folds.  Since every fold size is \(o_B/L+O(1)\), the oracle version of the estimator satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)-\mu_{d,a}
 &=\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{p_{E,a}}r_{E,a}(O_i)\\
 &\quad+\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{p_{O,a}}r_{O,a}(O_i)
 +o_P(B^{-1/2}).
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{8}
\]
The replacement of equal fold weights by \(o_B^{-1}\) in (8) contributes only \(O_P(B^{-1})\): the deterministic coefficient discrepancy is \(O(B^{-2})\), and each centered fold sum is \(O_P(B^{1/2})\).

It remains to compare the estimated and oracle bridge terms.  For fold \(\ell\), set
\(\Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d\) and
\(\Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d\).  After replacing each empirical arm proportion by its population value, the conditional population contribution of fold \(\ell\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=E)
 +\mathbb E_P\{\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_d)-q_d\Delta h_\ell
                -\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O\}.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{9}
\]
The selection-bridge equation implies
\[
 \mathbb E_P(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=E)
 =\mathbb E_P(q_d\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O),                \tag{10}
\]
and the outcome-bridge equation, because \(\Delta q_\ell\) is measurable with respect to \((S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X)\), implies
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_d)\mid A=a,G=O\}=0.        \tag{11}
\]
Consequently (9) reduces exactly to
\(-\mathbb E_P(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)\).
By iterated expectation in either direction and Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathbb E_P(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)|
 \le C_a\min\{&
 \|T_d^\star\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 \|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ},\\
 &\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 \|T_d\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}\}.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{12}
\]
where \(C_a<\infty\) depends only on the fixed treatment-overlap constants and the source weights bounded away from zero in (5).  The weak--strong product-rate premise makes (12) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed folds.

Conditional on the observations used to train a fold, cross-fitting makes its evaluation observations independent of \((\Delta h_\ell,\Delta q_\ell)\).  Conditional Chebyshev's inequality therefore bounds every centered empirical nuisance term by its \(L_2(P_{B,d,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm divided by the square root of the relevant source count.  The strong outcome rate and the displayed multiplier-stability condition make the sum of all such centered terms \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Moreover, the nuisance empirical arm means are \(o_P(1)\); multiplying them by the \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\) arm-proportion error in (7) shows that replacing the nuisance random denominators by population treatment probabilities also costs \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Combining these facts with (9)--(12) yields
\[
 \widehat\mu_{B,d}(a)-\widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)=o_P(B^{-1/2}). \tag{13}
\]

Subtracting (8) and (13) at \(a=0\) from those at \(a=1\), and using \(\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=\tau\), proves the exact fixed-path expansion requested in the directive:
\[
 \widehat\tau_B(d,\alpha_B)-\tau
 =\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d}(O_i)
  +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d}(O_i)
  +o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                           \tag{14}
\]
Thus the effects of the random treatment-arm denominators and the weak--strong remainder have both been derived, rather than assumed.

The two sums in (14) are independent and have conditional means zero and variances \(V_{E,d}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d}/o_B\).  If
\(\lambda_d=\lim e_B/o_B\), which exists along (5), then the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 n_B\left(\frac{V_{E,d}}{e_B}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{o_B}\right)
 &=\left(1+\frac{o_B}{e_B}\right)V_{E,d}
  +\left(1+\frac{e_B}{o_B}\right)V_{O,d}\\
 &\longrightarrow
 \frac{1+\lambda_d}{\lambda_d}V_{E,d}
 +(1+\lambda_d)V_{O,d}
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{15}
\]
holds.  Finite second moments and nondegeneracy permit the ordinary independent-source central limit theorem, and (14)--(15) give
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\tau\}}
 {\left[B\{V_{E,d_B}/e_B+V_{O,d_B}/o_B\}\right]^{1/2}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)                           \tag{16}
\]
along every deterministic sequence satisfying (4).  Notice that (14)--(16) use the exact arm-normalized scores \(\phi_{E,d}\) and \(\phi_{O,d}\); no printed Imbens--Kallus--Mao--Wang score moment is relabelled as \(V_{g,d}\).

We now lift (16) through the independent pilot.  From (1), choose deterministic \(a_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\left\{\max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|>a_m\right\}
 \longrightarrow0,                                         \tag{17}
\]
and call the complementary event \(\mathsf H_m\).  Let \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\) denote bounded-Lipschitz distance.  Conditional on any pilot realization in \(\mathsf H_m\), the main-study law is the deterministic design/allocation law analyzed above.  The convergence in (16) is uniform over these pilot realizations.  Indeed, failure of uniformity would supply a positive constant, a subsequence, and one violating deterministic pilot realization at each index.  The selected designs and allocations would satisfy (4); extracting a constant-design subsequence and applying (16) would force the corresponding bounded-Lipschitz distances to zero, a contradiction.  Independence of the pilot allows the conditional distances to be integrated.  Bounding the contribution of \(\mathsf H_m^c\) by (17) yields
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                           \tag{18}
\]

It remains to verify studentization and the event \(\mathsf G_{m,B}\).  On the selected positive-count event,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 &\le\frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &\quad+\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{19}
\]
The two count ratios are \(O_P(1)\) by (1)--(2), while the uniform main-study variance error is \(o_P(1)\).  Hence, by (3),
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2=o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).      \tag{20}
\]
Nondegeneracy, positive costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) give
\[
 0<\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \le\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty.    \tag{21}
\]
Equations (1)--(3) and (20)--(21) prove
\(P(\mathsf G_{m,B})\to1\), and the stipulated nonnegative root satisfies \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  Slutsky's theorem applied to (18) proves
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{\widehat s_B}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                           \tag{22}
\]
Finally, on \(\mathsf G_{m,B}\),
\[
 \{\tau\in I_B\}
 =\left\{\left|
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{\widehat s_B}
 \right|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\right\}.                         \tag{23}
\]
The standard normal distribution is continuous at the two critical values.  Equations (22)--(23) and \(P(\mathsf G_{m,B})\to1\) therefore give \(P\{\tau\in I_B\}\to1-\eta\).
