Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\). Positive unit costs, Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) give
\[
 0<v_d=(\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}})^2<\infty
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D). \tag{1}
\]
This is a source-score statement on the observable class and a gradient statement only along the declared bridge-differentiable submodels.

Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty, \(\mathcal O_P\) is finite and contains every diagonal pair. Thus its maximum exists, is finite, and is at least one. If the implication in equation (2) of the theorem holds with \(C\), division by the positive \(v_{d'}\) in (1) and maximization give \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C\). Conversely, if \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) and \(p_d\le p_{d'}\), then
\[
 v_d\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)v_{d'}\le Cv_{d'}.
\]
This proves the equivalence.

Choose
\[
 d_{\mathrm p}^*\in\arg\min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}v_d,
 \qquad d^*\in\arg\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d.
\]
Since \(d_{\mathrm p}^*\) minimizes raw risk, \(p_{d_{\mathrm p}^*}\le p_{d^*}\), and therefore
\[
 1\le\frac{v_{d_{\mathrm p}^*}}{v_{d^*}}
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P). \tag{2}
\]
The middle term is \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)=\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\). Division by the positive denominator shows that it is exactly the least best-tie constant. Taking suprema over any nonempty subclass proves both uniform characterizations.

Now impose the numerical-handle conditions. Positivity of \(Q_{R,d}\) on each cone makes every ratio well-defined, and the definitions of the infimum and supremum give
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)Q_{R,d}(x)\le F_d(x)
 \le\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}(x) \tag{3}
\]
for every \(d\) and \(x\in\mathcal X_d(P)\). Any common lower-envelope constant is at most \(\Lambda_-\), and any common upper-envelope constant is at least \(\Lambda_+\); conversely these two values satisfy (3). Hence the displayed positive finite condition is exactly the existence condition for a two-sided envelope on the chosen cones.

For \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), equations (3) and the handle identities yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_d
 &\le\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}\{x_d(P)\}
 =\Lambda_+(P)p_d\\
 &\le\Lambda_+(P)p_{d'}
 =\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d'}\{x_{d'}(P)\}\\
 &\le\frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}v_{d'}.
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
Maximizing and applying (2) proves the certificate. Notice that raw risks may vanish on the broad observable class; the exact finite-order part did not divide by them. Positivity is required only by this conditional quadratic-handle realization.

Finally put \(u=c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)\) and \(w=c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)\). For \(u,w>0\),
\[
 \frac{u}{\alpha}+\frac{w}{1-\alpha}-(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)^2
 =\frac{\{(1-\alpha)\sqrt u-\alpha\sqrt w\}^2}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\ge0. \tag{5}
\]
Equality holds at \(\alpha=\sqrt u/(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)\); endpoint limits cover zero components. Thus (5) proves the profiling identity.

Profiling need not preserve quadraticity. On \(\mathbb R^2\), with unit costs, \(Q_E(x)=x_1^2\) and \(Q_O(x)=x_2^2\), one obtains \(F(x)=(|x_1|+|x_2|)^2\). It violates the parallelogram identity because
\[
 F(e_1+e_2)+F(e_1-e_2)=8\ne4=2F(e_1)+2F(e_2).
\]
Hence a fixed-share generalized eigenvalue supplies at most a one-sided full-space envelope. Equality in (4) also requires law-realized directions to saturate the upper and lower envelopes while respecting the raw-risk order; comparing design-specific spaces by one eigenvalue additionally requires a common-space representation and normalization. None follows from operator bijectivity or raw-risk ordering. This proves the theorem.
