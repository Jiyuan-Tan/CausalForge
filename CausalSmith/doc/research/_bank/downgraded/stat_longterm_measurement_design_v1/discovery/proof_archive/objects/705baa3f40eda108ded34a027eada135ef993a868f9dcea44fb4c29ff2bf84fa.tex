Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).  By nondegeneracy, positive costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\), every profiled value is finite and strictly positive.  The budget-profile theorem gives
\[
 \mathcal V_d^*(P)
 =(\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}})^2. \tag{1}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) is a nonempty subset of the finite set \(\mathcal D\), substitution of (1) in the definition of \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) gives exactly the displayed ratio in the theorem.  Its numerator is no smaller than its denominator, while both are finite and positive.  Hence
\[
 1\le\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)<\infty. \tag{2}
\]

The actual best-tie guarantee is the inequality
\[
 \min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \le C(P)\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P).
\]
Division by the positive denominator shows that it holds if and only if
\(C(P)\ge\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\).  Thus its least constant is exactly \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\).

For the pairwise version, let
\[
 \mathcal A_P=\{(d,d')\in\mathcal D^2:
 \Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}(P;B)\le
 \Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d'}(P;B)\}.
\]
This finite set is nonempty because it contains every diagonal pair.  For a fixed \((d,d')\in\mathcal A_P\), the desired implication is equivalent, after division by \(\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P)>0\), to
\[
 C(P)\ge\frac{\mathcal V_d^*(P)}{\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P)}.
\]
Requiring this simultaneously for every pair proves that the least constant is the maximum displayed in the theorem.  The predictive-impossibility theorem supplies laws in \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) for which the best-tie ratio diverges.  Therefore no finite constant can be uniform over that region.

Finally, the finite-catalogue maximum is an extremum over the actually available design directions, not over an ambient linear space.  To see why an ambient generalized eigenvalue is not generally sharp, take quadratic forms \(Q(u)=u_1^2+u_2^2\) and \(H(u)=u_1^2+4u_2^2\).  The largest generalized eigenvalue of \((H,Q)\) is \(4\), but a catalogue containing only the direction \((1,0)\) has sharp ratio \(1\).  Equality with the spectral value therefore requires an additional catalogue-richness condition and attainment of the relevant extremal direction.

More generally, if transported quadratic forms satisfy
\[
 mQ(u)\le H(u)\le MQ(u)\qquad(0<m\le M<\infty),
\]
then \(Q(u)\le Q(v)\) implies
\[
 H(u)\le MQ(u)\le MQ(v)\le\frac{M}{m}H(v). \tag{3}
\]
Thus a two-sided comparison yields the generalized condition number \(M/m\); the largest eigenvalue \(M\) alone is sufficient only after a normalization that fixes the lower comparison at \(m=1\), together with catalogue richness and attainment if sharpness rather than a valid upper bound is claimed.  This proves the final assertions.
