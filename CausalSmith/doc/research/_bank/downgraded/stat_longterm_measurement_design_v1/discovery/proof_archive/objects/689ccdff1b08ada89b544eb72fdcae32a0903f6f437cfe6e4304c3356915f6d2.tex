Write \(V_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P)\), \(\widehat V_d=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*\), and \(V_{\min}=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}V_d\). Choose \(d^*\in D^*(P)\). By empirical optimality of \(\widehat d_m\),
\[
\widehat V_{\widehat d_m}\le\widehat V_{d^*}.
\]
Twice adding and subtracting the population values gives
\[
0\le V_{\widehat d_m}-V_{\min}
\le |V_{\widehat d_m}-\widehat V_{\widehat d_m}|
+|\widehat V_{d^*}-V_{d^*}|
\le2\varepsilon_m.
\]
If \(\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\), the definition of \(\gamma(P)\) makes the left side at least \(\gamma(P)\); hence
\[
\{\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\}
\subseteq\{2\varepsilon_m\ge\gamma(P)\}.
\]
When every design is optimal the first event is empty and \(\gamma(P)=+\infty\). Otherwise \(\gamma(P)>0\) because the family is finite.

Let \(f_d(x,y)=(\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y})^2\). Nondegeneracy and finiteness give a common compact rectangle inside \((0,\infty)^2\) containing every \((V_{E,d},V_{O,d})\) and, with probability tending to one, every \((\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d})\). The gradients of all finitely many \(f_d\) are bounded there. The mean-value theorem and the uniform pilot variance rate imply
\[
\varepsilon_m
=\max_d|f_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d})
-f_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d})|
=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).
\]
The event bound therefore tends to zero.

The same compact-neighborhood argument applied to
\[
g_d(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}
\]
and to the truncated inputs gives
\[
\max_d|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
=O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m).
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}second\text{-}order\text{-}allocation\text{-}loss}\) now yields the stronger uniform error
\[
O_P\!\left((r_{V,m}+\underline v_m)^2+B^{-1}\right).
\]
Because \(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m=o(1)\), this is also
\(O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m+B^{-1})=o_P(1)\). Its total convention is exactly the convention in the proposed statement, completing the proof.
