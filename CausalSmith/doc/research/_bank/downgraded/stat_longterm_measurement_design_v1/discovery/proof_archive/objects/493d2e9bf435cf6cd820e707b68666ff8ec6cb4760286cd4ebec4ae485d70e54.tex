Put \(r=1/4\), and first consider a center-law design whose early and late proxies have signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\).  Conditional on the Rademacher \(U\), the signed treatment \(T\), early proxy \(W_x\), and late proxy \(W_y\) are mutually independent, with conditional means \(rU,xU,yU\), respectively.  Treatment is balanced marginally in both sources, so \(p_{g,a}=1/2\).

Fix \(T=t\).  Bayes' rule shows that the determinant of the two-by-two conditional-expectation matrix \(T_d\), with rows indexed by \(W_x\) and columns by \(W_y\), is
\[
\det(T_d)=\frac y2\left\{
\frac{x+rt}{1+rtx}-\frac{-x+rt}{1-rtx}\right\}
=\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}>0.                                    \tag{1}
\]
For a single proxy \(W_s\), the determinant of the conditional-expectation map from functions of \(U\) to functions of \(W_s\), conditional on \(T=t\), is
\[
\frac{s(1-r^2)}{1-r^2s^2}\ne0.                                   \tag{2}
\]
Thus both completeness implications and operator bijectivity hold.

The proposed bridge \(h_d(l,a)=l/y\) obeys
\[
\mathbb E\{h_d(W_y,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O\}=u
=\mathbb E(Y\mid U=u,T=t,G=O).                                    \tag{3}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=E)}{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=O)}
=\frac1{1+rtu},                                                     \tag{4}
\]
and \(q_d(e,a)=\{1-r(2a-1)e/x\}/(1-r^2)\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E\{q_d(W_x,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O\}
=\frac{1-rtu}{1-r^2}=\frac1{1+rtu}.                               \tag{5}
\]
Iterated expectation proves both observed bridge equations.  The nonzero channel determinants give uniqueness and bijectivity.  Strict cell positivity gives treatment overlap and source support; consistency, latent exchangeability, experimental randomization, external validity, and the sequential restriction follow from the construction and conditional independence.  Finite support gives all moments.  The positive variances computed below verify nondegeneracy, proving \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

Experimental randomization and (3) give
\[
\mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E\{h_d(W_y,a)\mid A=a,G=E\}=\mathbb E(U),       \tag{6}
\]
independently of \(a\).  Since \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), both bridge contrasts equal \(0=\tau\).

The conditional mean of \(U\) given \((W_x,W_y)=(e,l)\) is
\[
\mathbb E(U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l)=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}.
\]
Averaging the corresponding conditional variance over the four cells gives
\[
R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.                            \tag{7}
\]
Therefore
\[
R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)
=\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
\qquad R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35.                    \tag{8}
\]
The paired costs are \(6\) and \(5\), and hence
\[
B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
=\frac{42(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2}<3
=B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B).              \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is uniquely prediction-selected despite its higher cost.

The experimental score reduces to \(2TW_y/y\), so
\[
V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}.                                               \tag{10}
\]
The observational score is \(2Tq_d(W_x,A)(U-W_y/y)\).  Conditional on \(U\), the residual factor has second moment \((1-y^2)/y^2\), independent of \((T,W_x)\), while \(\mathbb E(TW_x\mid G=O)=rx\).  Expanding the square therefore gives
\[
V_O(x,y)=\frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
\left(1-2r^2+\frac{r^2}{x^2}\right).                              \tag{11}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), \((x,y)=(\epsilon,3/4)\), so
\[
V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{64}{9},\qquad
V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
=\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025}.                    \tag{12}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), \((x,y)=(1/2,1/2)\), so
\[
V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,\qquad V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}.\tag{13}
\]
Using costs \((2,3)\) and the budget-profile formula,
\[
\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
=\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot\frac{384}{25}}\right)^2
=\frac{3872}{25}.                                                   \tag{14}
\]
For \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\),
\[
\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
\ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
>\frac{1568}{2025\cdot10^{-6}}>\frac{3872}{25},                   \tag{15}
\]
so \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is uniquely design-value optimal.  Expanding the squared profile in (12) yields
\[
\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
=\frac{1568}{2025\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}),
\]
and division by (14) gives
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
=\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}).                      \tag{16}
\]

For stability, fix \(\epsilon\) and parameterize the constrained model by its finitely many strictly positive latent-cell probabilities.  Near \(P_\epsilon\), each two-by-two proxy channel remains invertible.  The nearby latent outcome and selection bridges are obtained by solving
\[
K_{3d,Q}h_{d,a,Q}=(u:u\in\{-1,1\})^{\mathsf T},                  \tag{17}
\]
\[
K_{1d,Q}q_{d,a,Q}
=\left(\frac{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=E)}{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=O)}:
u\in\{-1,1\}\right)^{\mathsf T}.                                \tag{18}
\]
Strict positivity makes the ratios finite.  Conditional independence and iterated expectation make (17)--(18) solve the observed bridge equations.  The observed matrix of \(T_d\) is a product of invertible proxy channels and positive diagonal reweighting matrices and is therefore invertible.

The bridge coefficients are rational continuous functions of the cell probabilities.  Prediction risks, score moments, and profiled values are consequently continuous while cells and determinants stay away from zero.  At \(P_\epsilon\), (1)--(16) give strict regularity and strict prediction- and design-selection margins.  Hence some \(r_\epsilon>0\) preserves regularity and both optimizer assignments.  The ratio is continuous with positive denominator; shrinking the radius if needed gives
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(Q)>\frac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon).
