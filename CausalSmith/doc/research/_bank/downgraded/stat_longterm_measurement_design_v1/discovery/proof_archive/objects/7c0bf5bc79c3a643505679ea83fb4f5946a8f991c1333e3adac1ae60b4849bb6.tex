Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), \(d\in\mathcal D\), and \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  Write
\[
 H_d=(S_{3d},S_{2d},A,X),\qquad L_d=(S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X).
\]
The observed outcome-bridge equation is
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y-h_d(H_d)\mid L_d,G=O]=0. \tag{1}
\]
By short- and long-term consistency, observational latent exchangeability, the sequential-outcome restriction, and early-proxy completeness, equation (1) implies the latent bridge identity
\[
 \mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d}(a),S_{2d}(a),a,X)\mid S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O]
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O]. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, condition on \((S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O)\), subtract the two sides of (2), and then condition on \(S_{1d}(a)\).  The sequential restriction removes \(S_{1d}(a)\) from the conditional laws of \(Y(a)\) and \(S_{3d}(a)\); (1) makes the resulting conditional expectation zero, and early-proxy completeness makes the original difference zero almost surely.

The corrected full-short-term external-validity condition transports the joint law in (2) from \(G=O\) to \(G=E\).  Experimental randomization and consistency then give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid G=O]
 &=\mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d}(a),S_{2d}(a),a,X)\mid G=E]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mu_{d,a}.
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Subtracting (3) for \(a=0\) from (3) for \(a=1\) proves
\[
 \tau=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}.
\]

We next calculate the pathwise derivative.  Let a regular two-source submodel through \(P\) have conditional source scores \(\ell_E\) and \(\ell_O\), and let \(\dot h_d\) be the derivative of its outcome bridge.  Differentiating the experimental bridge mean yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot\mu_{d,a}
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{E,a}}
   \{h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a}\}\ell_E
   \mathrel{\Big|}G=E\right]\\
 &\quad+\mathbb E_P[\dot h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E].
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
The selection-bridge equation and change of measure give
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mathbb E_P[q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=O]. \tag{5}
\]
Differentiating (1) gives
\[
 T_d\dot h_d
 =\mathbb E_P[\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid L_d,G=O]. \tag{6}
\]
Using (6) in (5), and then iterated expectation, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mathbb E_P[q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O
   \mid A=a,G=O]. \tag{7}
\]
Subtracting (4)--(7) at \(a=0\) from the corresponding equations at \(a=1\) proves
\[
 \dot\tau
 =\mathbb E_P[\phi_{E,d}\ell_E\mid G=E]
  +\mathbb E_P[\phi_{O,d}\ell_O\mid G=O]. \tag{8}
\]
Equation (1) and the definitions of \(\mu_{d,a}\) show that both components in (8) have conditional mean zero.  Bijectivity of \(T_d\) implies bijectivity of its Hilbert-space adjoint \(T_d^\star\).  Thus the bridge derivative in (6) spans the admissible outcome-bridge tangent directions, and the Riesz representer in (8) lies in the two-source tangent space.  It is therefore the canonical gradient.

For independent samples, each experimental observation receives gradient \(\phi_{E,d}/n_E\) and each observational observation receives gradient \(\phi_{O,d}/n_O\).  Independence and the definitions of \(V_{E,d}\) and \(V_{O,d}\) give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(
  \frac1{n_E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_E}\phi_{E,d,i}
 +\frac1{n_O}\sum_{i=1}^{n_O}\phi_{O,d,i}\right)
 =\frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O},
\]
which is the asserted local variance.
