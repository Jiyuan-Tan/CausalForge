The proof is a finite-dimensional sample-moment argument, uniform because \(\mathcal D\), the treatment set, and the fold set are finite.

Fix \((d,a)\).  On a training sample of size \(n\), bounded bases, the \((4+\delta)\)-moment bound for \(Y\), source-arm positivity, and proportional cell sizes imply, entry by entry,
\[
 \widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a}=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat g^h_{d,a}-g^h_{d,a}=O_P(n^{-1/2}),                  \tag{1}
\]
and the same statements hold for \(\widehat M^q_{d,a}\) and \(\widehat g^q_{d,a}\).  These are ordinary fixed-dimensional sample-mean bounds; conditional sample means have the same order because the relevant cell probabilities are bounded away from zero.  A finite union bound makes (1) simultaneous over all designs, arms, and folds.

The singular-value bound and Weyl's inequality imply that, with probability tending to one, every empirical matrix in (4) is nonsingular and its inverse has operator norm at most \(2/c_0\).  On this event, the exact identities (3) give
\[
 \widehat\beta_{d,a}-\beta_{d,a}
 =(\widehat M^h_{d,a})^{-1}
 \{(\widehat g^h_{d,a}-g^h_{d,a})
 -(\widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a})\beta_{d,a}\},               \tag{2}
\]
and the analogous identity holds for \(\widehat\gamma_{d,a}-\gamma_{d,a}\).  The population coefficients are uniformly bounded because the right sides of (3) are bounded and the inverse matrices are uniformly bounded.  Equations (1)--(2) therefore show
\[
 \max_{d,a,\ell}
 \{\|\widehat\beta_{d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\beta_{d,a}\|
 +\|\widehat\gamma_{d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\gamma_{d,a}\|\}
 =O_P(n^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{3}
\]
The zero convention on singular matrices is irrelevant because its event has probability tending to zero.

The Gram eigenvalue bounds translate coefficient error into strong \(L_2\) error.  More directly, bounded bases make the same coefficient bound valid in \(L_2\) under either source, and conditional expectation preserves the resulting pointwise envelope.  Because the two source masses are bounded away from zero along the proportional-size paths, these sourcewise bounds also hold in the artificial-law norm.  Taking \(n\asymp B\) in (3) proves (5)--(6).  Multiplying one weak error of order \(B^{-1/2}\) by one strong error of the same order gives
\[
 \min\{\delta_{h,B}\rho_{q,B},\rho_{h,B}\delta_{q,B}\}
 =O_P(B^{-1})=o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                  \tag{4}
\]

It remains to verify the multiplier and variance conditions.  Bounded bridge bases and (3) imply pointwise bounds of the form
\[
 |\Delta h_\ell|\le C\|\widehat\beta-\beta\|,
 \qquad
 |\Delta q_\ell|\le C\|\widehat\gamma-\gamma\|.           \tag{5}
\]
The population selection bridge \(q_d\) is bounded because its basis and coefficient are bounded.  The moment condition on \(Y\) makes \(Y-h_d\) square-integrable.  Therefore (3) and (5) give, uniformly over designs and folds,
\[
 \|\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_d)\|_2
 +\|q_d\Delta h_\ell\|_2=O_P(B^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \|\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\|_2=O_P(B^{-1}).             \tag{6}
\]
Thus the multiplier expression is \(O_P(B^{-1/2})=o_P(1)\).

For either source, let \(\phi_{g,d}\) be the population score and \(\widehat\phi_{g,d}\) its fold-external plug-in version.  Empirical arm probabilities are root-\(n\) consistent by source-arm positivity.  The map from the finite coefficient vectors and arm probabilities to the score is locally Lipschitz away from zero denominators.  Equations (3), (5), and the \((4+\delta)\)-moment bound consequently give
\[
 \|\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d}\|_2=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\phi_{g,d}+\phi_{g,d}\|_2=O_P(1).                \tag{7}
\]
Moreover, \(E_P(\phi_{g,d}^4\mid G=g)<\infty\).  On an evaluation fold, conditional on its training observations,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathbb P_n\widehat\phi_{g,d}^2-P\phi_{g,d}^2|
 \le{}&| (\mathbb P_n-P)\phi_{g,d}^2|\\
 &+\mathbb P_n\{|\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d}|
 |\widehat\phi_{g,d}+\phi_{g,d}|\}.
                                                                    \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]
The first term is \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\) by the finite fourth moment.  Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, (7), and the law of large numbers make the second term \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Fixed-fold averaging and a finite union bound preserve the rate.  Taking \(n\asymp m\) for the full-catalogue pilot and \(n\asymp B\) for the main study proves (7) in the statement.

Equations (3)--(8) verify, on this subclass only, every maintained estimator-level premise named in Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) and \(\mathrm{thm{:}postselection\text{-}wald}\), with the rates stated in the theorem.  Applying those two theorems yields the asserted oracle-regret, limit-law, and coverage conclusions.
