Put \(r=1/4\).  Consider first a generic member of the construction whose early and late proxies have signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\), respectively.  Conditional on \(U\), the signed treatment \(T\), the early proxy \(W_x\), and the late proxy \(W_y\) are mutually independent, with conditional means \(rU\), \(xU\), and \(yU\).  Marginalizing over the Rademacher variable \(U\) gives \(p_{g,a}=1/2\) in both sources.

Fixing \(T=t\), the two-by-two matrix of \(T_d\), with rows indexed by the early proxy and columns indexed by the late proxy, has determinant
\[
 \det(T_d)=\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}>0. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, its determinant is the difference between the two conditional probabilities of \(W_y=1\), and Bayes' rule gives
\[
 \frac y2\left\{
 \frac{x+rt}{1+rtx}-\frac{-x+rt}{1-rtx}
 \right\}
 =\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}.
\]
Similarly, for a single proxy \(W_s\), the determinant of the conditional-expectation map from functions of \(U\) to functions of \(W_s\), conditional on \(T=t\), is
\[
 \frac{s(1-r^2)}{1-r^2s^2}. \tag{2}
\]
It is nonzero for both \(s=x\) and \(s=y\).  Thus the early- and late-proxy completeness implications hold, and (1) proves designwise operator bijectivity.

The bridge formulas in the construction can also be verified without an existence argument.  Conditional independence gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{W_y}{y}\,middle|,W_x,T,G=O\right]
 =\mathbb E[U\mid W_x,T,G=O]
 =\mathbb E[Y\mid W_x,T,G=O]. \tag{3}
\]
Hence \(h_d(l,a)=l/y\) solves the outcome-bridge equation.  Moreover,
\[
 \frac{p(W_y=l\mid T=t,G=E)}{p(W_y=l\mid T=t,G=O)}
 =\frac1{1+rtyl}. \tag{4}
\]
Since
\[
 \mathbb E[W_x\mid W_y=l,T=t,G=O]
 =x\frac{rt+yl}{1+rtyl},
\]
the proposed selection bridge obeys
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \frac{1-rtW_x/x}{1-r^2},middle|,W_y=l,T=t,G=O
 \right]
 =\frac1{1+rtyl}, \tag{5}
\]
which is exactly (4).  Thus \(q_d(e,a)=\{1-r(2a-1)e/x\}/(1-r^2)\) solves the selection-bridge equation.  Equation (1) also makes the outcome bridge unique.

Every primitive cell probability is positive because \(r,x,y\in(0,1)\).  Consequently observational and experimental overlap and cross-source support hold.  Consistency holds by the definitions of \(Y\) and \(Z\); treatment is independent of all potential variables given \(U\) in source \(O\), and is independent of them unconditionally in source \(E\).  The joint potential short-term vector and \((U,X)\) are independent of \(G\).  Finally, conditional on \(U\), the late proxy and \(Y(a)=U\) are independent of the early proxy, so the sequential-outcome restriction holds.  Finite support supplies square integrability and all fourth moments.  Together with (1)--(5) and the positive variances computed below, these facts verify every member condition of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), rather than assuming membership from the construction.

On this subclass \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), so
\[
 \tau=\mathbb E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid G=O]=0. \tag{6}
\]
Also \(W_y\) is marginally symmetric in source \(E\), whence \(\mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E[W_y/y\mid A=a,G=E]=0\) for both treatment levels.  Thus each design's observed bridge contrast equals the common causal target in (6).  By the fixed-system efficiency lemma, applied using bridge uniqueness and (1), the source gradients are canonical for this target on the witness subclass.

We next calculate the two objectives.  The best predictor of \(U\) from \((W_x,W_y)=(e,l)\) is
\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l]
 =\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}.
\]
Summing the corresponding conditional variances over the four proxy cells yields
\[
 R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}. \tag{7}
\]
Therefore
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35. \tag{8}
\]
The paired-source costs are \(6\) and \(5\), respectively.  It follows that
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 =\frac{42(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2}
 <3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B), \tag{9}
\]
where the strict inequality is equivalent to
\(42(1-\epsilon^2)<48-27\epsilon^2\).  Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is the unique predictive optimizer, despite its larger cost.

For the efficiency criterion, the experimental score reduces to \(2TW_y/y\), so
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}. \tag{10}
\]
The observational score is
\[
 2Tq_d(W_x,A)\left(U-\frac{W_y}{y}\right).
\]
Conditional on \(U\), the squared residual has expectation \((1-y^2)/y^2\), independent of \((T,W_x)\).  In addition,
\[
 \mathbb E[TW_x\mid G=O]=rx,
\]
and hence
\[
 \mathbb E[q_d(W_x,A)^2\mid G=O]
 =\frac{1-2r^2+r^2/x^2}{(1-r^2)^2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 V_O(x,y)=
 \frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
 \left(1-2r^2+\frac{r^2}{x^2}\right). \tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11) are strictly positive and finite for every fixed \(x,y\in(0,1)\), completing the earlier verification of nondegeneracy.

For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), substitution of \((x,y)=(\epsilon,3/4)\) into (10)--(11) gives
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{64}{9},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025}. \tag{12}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), substitution of \((x,y)=(1/2,1/2)\) gives
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}. \tag{13}
\]
The budget-profile theorem and the costs \(c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=2\), \(c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=3\) now give
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot\frac{384}{25}}\right)^2
 =\left(4\sqrt2+\frac{24\sqrt2}{5}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}. \tag{14}
\]
On the other hand, for \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\),
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 >\frac{1568}{2025\cdot10^{-6}}
 >\frac{3872}{25}. \tag{15}
\]
Since the family contains only two designs, (15) proves that \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is the unique design-value optimizer.  Expanding the squared profile using (12) yields
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{1568}{2025\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{16}
\]
Dividing (16) by (14) proves
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{17}
\]

It remains to prove stability.  Fix \(\epsilon\).  Parameterize the constrained fixed-support model by its finitely many free latent-cell probabilities.  In a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(P_\epsilon\), all conditioning denominators remain positive.  Conditional-probability matrices and their determinants are continuous functions of the cell probabilities; wherever the determinants are nonzero, the bridge coefficients obtained by inversion are rational and hence continuous.  The prediction risks and source-score second moments are finite sums of continuous functions of the cells and bridge coefficients.  The profiled values are continuous as compositions with the positive square-root profile.

At \(P_\epsilon\), (1)--(2), (9), and (15) give strictly positive regularity, predictive-selection, and design-value-selection margins.  Continuity therefore furnishes a radius \(r_\epsilon>0\) on which both determinants remain nonzero and the same two strict optimizer assignments persist.  The denominator of \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}\) is positive by (13)--(14), so that ratio is continuous as well.  Shrinking \(r_\epsilon\) if necessary gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)>rac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
\]
throughout the stated relative ball.  This proves every assertion of the lemma.
