Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  All probabilities below refer to the joint pilot and main-study law unless a source is displayed explicitly.  The proof proceeds in four steps.

\emph{Step 1: interior counts and the positive-denominator event.}
Positive costs and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) imply
\(0<\alpha_d^*(P)<1\) for every \(d\in\mathcal D\).  Since \(\mathcal D\) is finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D). \tag{1}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\), under the maintained pilot variance and vanishing-rate assumptions listed in the present theorem, gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1). \tag{2}
\]
Thus, with probability tending to one,
\(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\).  Positivity and finiteness of the costs over the finite family then imply
\[
 N_{E,B}\asymp B,\qquad N_{O,B}\asymp B \tag{3}
\]
on that event.  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}source\text{-}arm\text{-}positivity}\) gives fixed positive treatment probabilities in both sources.  Conditional on the pilot, each source arm count is binomial, and each deterministic balanced observational fold has cardinality of order \(B\).  For a count \(N_a\) from \(N\) independent trials with success probability \(p_a>0\),
\[
 P(N_a=0)=(1-p_a)^N\longrightarrow0.
\]
A finite union bound over the two experimental arms and the \(2L\) observational fold-arm cells, together with (3), proves
\[
 P(\mathsf D_B)\longrightarrow1. \tag{4}
\]

\emph{Step 2: an expansion along every deterministic selected path.}
Let \(d_B\in\mathcal D\) and \(\alpha_B\in(0,1)\) be deterministic with
\[
 |\alpha_B-\alpha_{d_B}^*(P)|\longrightarrow0,
\]
and let \(e_B,o_B\) be the corresponding floor counts.  By (1), positivity of the finite cost family, and the path condition,
\[
 e_B\asymp B,qquad o_B\asymp B. \tag{5}
\]
Abbreviate \(h=h_{d_B}\), \(q=q_{d_B}\),
\[
 \Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-h,
 \qquad
 \Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-q.
\]

We first record the elementary arm-ratio expansion used below.  Let \(W_1,\ldots,W_N\) be an independent evaluation sample, let \(p_a=P(A=a)>0\), let \(N_a=\sum_i\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}\), and let \(f_N\) be a square-integrable function independent of this evaluation sample.  Conditional on \(f_N\), write \(\mu_a(f_N)=E\{f_N(W)\mid A=a\}\) and \(\widehat p_a=N_a/N\).  On \(N_a>0\), the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac1{N_a}\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}f_N(W_i)-\mu_a(f_N)
 &={1\over N}\sum_{i=1}^N{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}\over p_a}
 \{f_N(W_i)-\mu_a(f_N)\}+R_{N,a}(f_N),\\
 R_{N,a}(f_N)
 &=\left({p_a\over\widehat p_a}-1\right)
 {1\over N}\sum_{i=1}^N{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}\over p_a}
 \{f_N(W_i)-\mu_a(f_N)\}
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
holds.  Conditional Chebyshev bounds give
\[
 \widehat p_a-p_a=O_P(N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 R_{N,a}(f_N)=O_P\{N^{-1}\|f_N\|_2\}. \tag{7}
\]
In particular, if \(\|f_N\|_2=o_P(1)\), its centered empirical contribution in (6) is \(o_P(N^{-1/2})\), and the remainder in (7) is smaller still.  This calculation also proves the asserted orders for random fold-external functions, because the bounds are conditional on their training data.

The outcome-bridge equation and the selection-bridge change-of-measure identity imply, for every square-integrable early-argument function \(u\) and late-argument function \(f\),
\[
 E_O\{u(S_{2d_B},S_{1d_B},a,X)(Y-h)\mid A=a\}=0, \tag{8}
\]
\[
 E_E\{f(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}
 =E_O\{qf(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}. \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, (8) is iterated expectation through the observed outcome-bridge equation in Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}observed\text{-}outcome\text{-}bridge\text{-}existence}\), while (9) follows by iterated expectation from Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}selection\text{-}bridge\text{-}existence}\).

Conditional on the fold-\(\ell\) training sample, combine the population means of the experimental bridge term and the observational residual correction.  Equations (8)--(9) give the exact drift identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &E_E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)
 +E_O\{(q+\Delta q_\ell)(Y-h-\Delta h_\ell)\mid A=a\}\\
 &\hspace{38mm}=-E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a). 
\end{aligned} \tag{10}
\]
The terms linear in \(\Delta h_\ell\) cancel by (9), and the two terms multiplying \(Y-h\) vanish by (8).

The drift in (10) is second order.  Since \(\Delta q_\ell\) is measurable in the early arguments and \(\Delta h_\ell\) in the late arguments, iterated expectation through \(T_{d_B}\) and through \(T_{d_B}^\star\), respectively, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C_P\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B}
          \|T_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B},\\
 |E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C_P\|T_{d_B}^\star\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}
          \|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}.
\end{aligned} \tag{11}
\]
The factor \(C_P<\infty\) only converts the source norm into its arm-conditional version, using source-arm positivity.  By (5), the source masses of \(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) are bounded above and away from zero.  Hence its \(L_2\) norm and the two source-conditional \(L_2\) norms are equivalent, uniformly along the path.  The four maintained weak and strong bridge-rate conditions and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}bridge\text{-}product\text{-}rate}\) therefore imply, uniformly over the fixed numbers of arms and folds,
\[
 \max_{\ell,a}|E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 =O_P\!\left(\min\{\delta_{h,B}\rho_{q,B},
                         \rho_{h,B}\delta_{q,B}\}\right)
 =o_P(B^{-1/2}). \tag{12}
\]

It remains to control the centered empirical nuisance terms.  In the experimental sample the perturbation is \(\Delta h_\ell\), whose \(L_2(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm is \(O_P(\rho_{h,B})=o_P(1)\).  In observational fold \(\ell\), subtraction of the oracle residual score leaves
\[
 \Delta q_\ell(Y-h)-q\Delta h_\ell-\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell. \tag{13}
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}postselection\text{-}multiplier\text{-}condition}\) says that the \(L_2(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm of (13) is \(o_P(1)\), uniformly over \(\ell\).  The prescribed training scheme makes \(\Delta h_\ell\) independent of the experimental evaluation sample and makes (13) independent of observational evaluation fold \(\ell\).  Applying (6)--(7), conditional Chebyshev bounds, and a finite union bound over arms and folds shows that all centered nuisance empirical terms are \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  The population nuisance means are \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) by (10)--(12).

Apply (6) once more to the oracle terms.  The experimental oracle part is the average of \(\phi_{E,d_B}\) over the \(e_B\) experimental observations.  The observational oracle part is initially a fold average.  If \(o_{B,\ell}=|\mathcal I_{O,B,\ell}|\), balanced folding gives \(|o_B-Lo_{B,\ell}|\le L\), and therefore
\[
 \left|{1\over Lo_{B,\ell}}-{1\over o_B}\right|=O(B^{-2}). \tag{14}
\]
Because the oracle observational score is centered and square-integrable, (14) and a conditional second-moment bound make replacement of the fold weights by \(1/o_B\) an \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) operation.  We have proved the deterministic-path expansion
\[
 \widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)
 ={1\over e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
  +{1\over o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
  +o_P(B^{-1/2}). \tag{15}
\]
This expansion is centered at the observed bridge contrast; it does not use or assert \(\theta_{d_B}(P)=\tau\).

We next derive the normal limit directly from square integrability.  Define
\[
 s_{0,B}^2(d_B,\alpha_B)
 =B\left({V_{E,d_B}\over e_B}+{V_{O,d_B}\over o_B}\right).
\]
By (5) and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), this quantity is bounded above and away from zero.  From any subsequence extract a further subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant, possible because \(\mathcal D\) is finite.  Along it, \(e_B/B\) and \(o_B/B\) converge to positive constants.  For any centered square-integrable random variable \(Q\), dominated convergence applied to
\((e^{iuQ}-1-iuQ)/u^2\) gives
\[
 E(e^{iuQ})=1-\frac{u^2}{2}E(Q^2)+o(u^2)
 \qquad(u\to0). \tag{16}
\]
Use (16) for the independent experimental and observational oracle scores with arguments
\(t\sqrt B/(e_Bs_{0,B})\) and \(t\sqrt B/(o_Bs_{0,B})\).  Taking the corresponding powers \(e_B\) and \(o_B\), the logarithm of the joint characteristic function tends to
\[
 -{t^2\over2s_{0,B}^2}
 B\left({V_{E,d}\over e_B}+{V_{O,d}\over o_B}\right)
 =-{t^2\over2}. \tag{17}
\]
Thus the normalized oracle sum converges in distribution to \(\mathcal N(0,1)\) along every constant-design further subsequence.  Every subsequence has such a further subsequence with the same limit, so the conclusion holds along the original deterministic path.  Combining this fact with (15) and the lower bound on \(s_{0,B}\) gives
\[
 {\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)\}
  \over s_{0,B}(d_B,\alpha_B)}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1). \tag{18}
\]

\emph{Step 3: transfer to the pilot-selected path and consistency of the studentizer.}
By (2), there is a deterministic sequence \(t_B\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\bigl\{|\widehat\alpha_m-alpha_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)|>t_B\bigr\}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{19}
\]
The conclusions (15) and (18) are uniform over
\[
 \mathcal A_B=
 \{(d,\alpha):d\in\mathcal D,
 |\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)|\le t_B\}. \tag{20}
\]
To verify this sequential claim, suppose uniformity failed for either conclusion.  For infinitely many \(B\), choose a deterministic violating pair in \(\mathcal A_B\).  Those choices form a deterministic path satisfying the hypotheses of Step 2 and therefore contradict (15) or (18).  This argument applies to the conditional main-study law for each fixed pilot choice.  Independence of the pilot and main study, (19), and conditioning on \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\) now give
\[
 {\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)
              -\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}\over s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1). \tag{21}
\]

The floor-count conclusion of Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\), evaluated at \(d=\widehat d_m\), yields
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1). \tag{22}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\), direct subtraction of the two variance formulas gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 \le{}&{B\over N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &+{B\over N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.
\end{aligned} \tag{23}
\]
The coefficients in (23) are \(O_P(1)\) by (1)--(3).  Assumptions~\(\mathrm{ass{:}main\text{-}gradient\text{-}variance\text{-}rate}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}main\text{-}variance\text{-}rate\text{-}vanishes}\) make the two variance-estimation errors \(o_P(1)\).  Equations (4), (22), and (23) therefore prove
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1). \tag{24}
\]
Finiteness of \(\mathcal D\), positive costs, and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) imply
\[
 \min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)>0. \tag{25}
\]
Combining (4), (24), and (25) gives
\[
 P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad
 {\widehat s_B\over s_{0,B}}\longrightarrow_P1. \tag{26}
\]
For completeness, multiplication by a random factor converging in probability to one preserves the limit in (21): tightness of the statistic in (21) implies that, for each \(\delta>0\), it lies in a fixed compact interval with probability at least \(1-\delta\); on that interval multiplication by \(s_{0,B}/\widehat s_B\) changes it uniformly by \(o_P(1)\).  Hence (21) and (26) imply
\[
 \mathsf T_B
 ={\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}\over\widehat s_B}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1), \tag{27}
\]
where the total zero extension off \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\) is asymptotically irrelevant by (26).

\emph{Step 4: Wald coverage.}
On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}selected\text{-}wald\text{-}variance}\) makes the event
\(\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}\) identical to
\[
 \{|\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\}.
\]
The complement of \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\) has probability tending to zero by (26).  Since the standard normal distribution has no mass at either endpoint, (27) and the definition of \(z_{1-\eta/2}\) yield
\[
 P\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}
 \longrightarrow1-\eta.
\]
Together with (22), (24), (26), and (27), this proves all asserted conclusions.  Every nuisance, product, multiplier, pilot-variance, and main-study-variance rate invoked above is one of the theorem's explicitly maintained estimator-level hypotheses; none was inferred from membership in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).
