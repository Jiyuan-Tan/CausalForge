Fix center signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\) and write \(T=2A-1\). Under the observational center law, the joint mass of \((T,W_x,W_y,U)=(t,r,s,u)\), apart from its common normalizer, is
\[
 (1+tu/4)(1+xur)(1+yus).                                   \tag{30}
\]
Summing over \(u\), with and without multiplication by \(u\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E(U\mid T=t,W_x=r,W_y=s)
 =\frac{t/4+xr+ys+txy\,rs/4}
 {1+txr/4+tys/4+xy\,rs}.                                  \tag{31}
\]
Because \(U^2=1\), the conditional risk is one minus the square of (31). Weighting by the eight positive cell probabilities and collecting even powers yields
\[
 \mathcal R_A(x,y)=
 \frac{15(1-x^2)(1-y^2)(16-x^2-y^2-14x^2y^2)}
 {\{16(1+xy)^2-(x+y)^2\}\{16(1-xy)^2-(x-y)^2\}}.         \tag{32}
\]
Every denominator is positive because it is proportional to a positive conditioning-cell probability.

Substitution of \(x=y=1/2\) gives \(R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A=585/1024\). Conditioning on \(A\) in addition to the unadjusted predictors cannot increase squared-error risk, so
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \le R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 <\frac3{10\chi}<\frac3{10}<\frac{585}{1024}.
\]
Thus both generic selectors choose \(d_{\mathrm p}\). Their numerator singleton is identical under each profile, proving the two ratio equalities. The exact formula, lower bound, minimizing scale, and expansion then follow from the sharp-elbow lemma.
