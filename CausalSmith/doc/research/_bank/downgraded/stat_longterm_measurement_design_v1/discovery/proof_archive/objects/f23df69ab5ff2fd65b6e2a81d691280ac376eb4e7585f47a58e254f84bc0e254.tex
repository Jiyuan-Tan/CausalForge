Fix \(P\) and \(B\), and use the abbreviations in the statement.  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\), positive costs, and nondegenerate source variances imply \(0<v_d<\infty\) for every \(d\).  The finite relation \(\mathcal O_P\) is nonempty because it contains every diagonal pair.  Therefore
\[
 1\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty. \tag{1}
\]
If all prediction-order implications hold with constant \(C\), applying them to every pair in \(\mathcal O_P\) and dividing by \(v_{d'}>0\) gives \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\).  Conversely, the definition of the maximum gives \(v_d/v_{d'}\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C\) for every ordered pair.  This proves (2).

Let \(d_p\) minimize \(v_d\) among the diagnostic minimizers and let \(d_*\) minimize \(v_d\) over all designs.  Because \(d_p\) minimizes \(p_d\), \((d_p,d_*)\in\mathcal O_P\), and hence
\[
 1\le\frac{v_{d_p}}{v_{d_*}}le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P).
\]
The middle ratio is both \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)\) and \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\).  The uniform-class equivalences follow immediately: one common finite constant exists exactly when the supremum of the respective exact lawwise constants is finite.

Now assume a numerical realization of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{comp}}\) satisfying (4)--(7).  By the definitions of \(\Lambda_-\) and \(\Lambda_+\), every \(x\in\mathcal X_d(P)\) satisfies
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)Q_{R,d}(x)\le F_d(x)le\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}(x). \tag{2}
\]
This is exactly the two-sided quadratic-form envelope on the chosen cones; positivity and finiteness of its sharp lower and upper constants are precisely condition (7).  For any \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), (4) and (2) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_d
 &\le\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}\{x_d(P)\}
 =\Lambda_+(P)Bp_d\\
 &\le\Lambda_+(P)Bp_{d'}
 =\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d'}\{x_{d'}(P)\}\\
 &\le\frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}v_{d'}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the maximum over ordered pairs and using the first part proves (8).

For nonnegative numbers \(u= c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)\) and \(w=c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)\), the same square identity as in the budget-profile theorem gives
\[
 \inf_{0<\alpha<1}\left\{\frac u\alpha+\frac w{1-\alpha}\right\}
 =(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)^2=F_d(x), \tag{3}
\]
with the boundary cases obtained by limits when one component vanishes.  This proves (9).  Unless the two source quadratic forms are everywhere proportional in the required way, the square-root cross term in \(F_d\) is not quadratic.  A largest generalized eigenvalue at a fixed \(\alpha\) can bound only the corresponding fixed-\(\alpha\) numerator from above relative to \(Q_{R,d}\); it supplies neither the positive lower envelope in (2) nor saturation at the law-realized, diagnostic-ordered directions.  Equality with the sharp constants therefore requires the extra common-space, normalization, and saturation conditions stated in the theorem, none of which follows from designwise operator bijectivity or diagnostic ordering.
