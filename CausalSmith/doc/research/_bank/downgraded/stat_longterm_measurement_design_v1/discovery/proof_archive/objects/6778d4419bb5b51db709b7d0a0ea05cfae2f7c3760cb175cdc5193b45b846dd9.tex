Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).  The admissible family is finite and nonempty.  Positive finite costs and the nondegenerate-component assumption imply, for every \(d\in\mathcal D\),
\[
 0<\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 =\left(\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}\right)^2<\infty. \tag{1}
\]
The predictive optimizer set is nonempty because it is the argmin of a real-valued objective over a finite nonempty set.  Substitution of (1) into the definition of \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) gives the displayed equality and proves
\[
 1\le\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)<\infty. \tag{2}
\]

Let
\[
 v_*:=\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P),
 \qquad
 v_{\mathrm{pred}}:=\min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}\mathcal V_d^*(P).
\]
The actual best-tie inequality \(v_{\mathrm{pred}}\le C(P)v_*\) holds exactly when \(C(P)\ge v_{\mathrm{pred}}/v_*\).  Its smallest admissible constant is therefore exactly the ratio in (2).

For the pairwise claim, let
\[
 \mathcal A_P=\{(d,d')\in\mathcal D^2:
 \Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}(P;B)\le
 \Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d'}(P;B)\}.
\]
This finite set is nonempty because it contains every diagonal pair.  A constant \(C(P)\) validates every stated implication if and only if
\[
 C(P)\ge \frac{\mathcal V_d^*(P)}{\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P)}
 \quad\hbox{for every }(d,d')\in\mathcal A_P.
\]
Taking the maximum proves both sufficiency and necessity of the displayed pairwise constant.  The predictive-impossibility theorem supplies laws in \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) for which the best-tie constant diverges, so no finite constant can be uniform on that region.

Finally, an ambient generalized eigenvalue need not equal either sharp finite-catalogue constant.  To see the elementary obstruction, take on \(\mathbb R^2\) the positive quadratic forms \(Q_0(z)=z^{\mathsf T}z\) and \(Q_1(z)=z^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(2,1)z\).  Their largest generalized eigenvalue is \(2\), attained in the first coordinate direction.  A finite catalogue containing only the second coordinate direction has sharp restricted ratio \(1\), not \(2\).  Thus equality with the ambient eigenvalue requires that the catalogue of transported directions be rich enough to contain, or attain in its closure, an extremizing direction.

There is a second obstruction when the desired conclusion compares two different catalogue directions.  If
\[
 \lambda_{\min}Q_0(z)\le Q_1(z)\le\lambda_{\max}Q_0(z)
 \qquad\hbox{for all }z,
\]
then an ordering \(Q_0(z)\le Q_0(z')\) implies only
\[
 Q_1(z)\le\lambda_{\max}Q_0(z)
 \le\lambda_{\max}Q_0(z')
 \le\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\lambda_{\min}}Q_1(z').
\]
Hence a two-sided quadratic-form transport generally yields the generalized condition number \(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}\), not \(\lambda_{\max}\) alone.  The law-specific finite maxima derived above remain the sharp constants without additional richness, attainment, and normalization conditions.
