Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{CLW}}\).  By the transported-domain definition, every Gram matrix is nonsingular and every \(r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P)\) is finite.  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) give

 0<\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).                                  \tag{35}
\]
Source-arm positivity gives \(0<1-p_{g,1}<1\); combined with the finite positive source-score variances, this also makes every IKM-normalized profile finite and positive.  Since the catalogue is finite and nonempty, the CLW selector is nonempty.  Substitution into the definitions proves (1)--(2).  In each case the numerator minimizes over a nonempty subset of the denominator's index set, so the ratio is at least one, while (35) makes it finite.  Dividing the selected-value inequality by the positive global minimum shows that the displayed ratio is its smallest constant.

For either positive profile \(w_d\), set

 C_{\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{CLW}}(P;B)

 \Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}(P;B)\le

The index set in (36) contains every diagonal pair and is finite.  Every constant satisfying all named-rule order implications must dominate each indexed ratio and therefore their maximum.  Conversely, the maximum itself satisfies every implication.  This proves (3).  Replacing the continuous criterion by the integer criterion gives the identical argument on the positive-count domain.  Evaluating the two criteria and then enumerating the ordered pairs requires at most \(|\mathcal D|^2\) comparisons.
