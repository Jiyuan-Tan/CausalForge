Let \(\mathcal F_m=\sigma(\mathcal P_m)\).  Pilot independence makes \(\widehat d_m\), \(\widehat\alpha_m\), \(N_{E,B}\), and \(N_{O,B}\) measurable with respect to \(\mathcal F_m\) and independent of the main-study observations.  The pilot theorem and nondegeneracy imply that, for some \(a_0>0\),
\[
 \Pr\{a_0\le\widehat\alpha_m\le1-a_0\}\longrightarrow1. \tag{1}
\]
On this event both realized counts are of order \(B\).  The pilot theorem, with its second-order refinement, gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1),\qquad
 s_{0,B}^2:=B\left\{\frac{V_{E,\widehat d_m}}{N_{E,B}}+
 \frac{V_{O,\widehat d_m}}{N_{O,B}}\right\}. \tag{2}
\]

For a fold \(\ell\), write \(e_{h,d,\ell}=\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d\) and \(e_{q,d,\ell}=\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d\).  The exact doubly robust score identity leaves a bilinear conditional-mean remainder bounded by a constant multiple of
\[
 \min\left\{
 \|e_{h,d,\ell}\|_{w,h,d}\|e_{q,d,\ell}\|_{2,O,q,d},
 \|e_{h,d,\ell}\|_{2,O,h,d}\|e_{q,d,\ell}\|_{w,q,d}
 \right\}. \tag{3}
\]
The uniform weak--strong product condition makes (3) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the finite family and fixed folds.  Cross-fitting centers the remaining empirical nuisance terms.  The established fixed-design limit result, applied to the observed bridge contrast identified in the narrowed designwise-gradient lemma, therefore gives uniformly for allocations in \([a_0,1-a_0]\),
\[
 \widehat\tau_{d,B}-\theta_d(P)
 =\frac1{n_{E,B}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{E,B}}\phi_{E,d}(O_{E,i})
 +\frac1{n_{O,B}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{O,B}}\phi_{O,d}(O_{O,i})
 +o_P(B^{-1/2}). \tag{4}
\]
Finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) and pilot independence permit substitution of \(d=\widehat d_m\) and the pilot-measurable counts.

Conditional on \(\mathcal F_m\), the two leading sums in (4) are independent and mean zero, and their variance after multiplication by \(B\) is \(s_{0,B}^2\).  The fixed-design central limit result is uniform over the finite set of designs and over integer sample sizes whose ratios stay in the compact interval from (1).  Hence
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}
 {s_{0,B}}\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1). \tag{5}
\]

Finally, on (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 &\le \frac B{N_{E,B}}|\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|
 +\frac B{N_{O,B}}|\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &=O_P(r_{\widetilde V,B})=o_P(1).
\end{aligned}\tag{6}
\]
Equations (2) and (6) prove variance consistency.  Positive nondegeneracy bounds every selected profile away from zero, so \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  Slutsky's theorem applied to (5) proves the studentized limit, and convergence at the two standard-normal quantiles proves limiting coverage \(1-\eta\) for \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\).  If every admissible bridge contrast equals \(\tau\), the same interval covers \(\tau\).
