Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(d\in\mathcal D\), and abbreviate
\[
 H_d=(S_{3d},S_{2d},A,X),\qquad L_d=(S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X).
\]
Observed outcome-bridge existence gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{Y-h_d(H_d)\mid L_d,G=O\}=0. \tag{1}
\]
Equivalently, \(T_dh_d=\mathbb E_P(Y\mid L_d,G=O)\).  Bijectivity of \(T_d\) makes this solution unique.  Thus \(h_d\), the two experimental means \(\mu_{d,a}\), and the contrast \(\theta_d(P)=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}\) are identified from the two observed source laws.

Consider a regular two-source parametric submodel through \(P\), with conditional source scores \(\ell_E\) and \(\ell_O\), and write \(\dot h_d\) for the derivative of its outcome bridge.  Differentiating the experimental bridge mean gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot\mu_{d,a}
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{E,a}}
   \{h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a}\}\ell_E
   \mathrel{\Big|}G=E\right]\\
 &\quad+\mathbb E_P\{\dot h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
The selection-bridge equation says that \(T_d^\star q_d\) is the conditional density ratio of \((S_{3d},S_{2d},X)\) in source \(E\) relative to source \(O\), within treatment arm \(a\).  Hence, for every square-integrable \(f(H_d)\), iterated expectation gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{f(H_d)\mid A=a,G=E\}
 =\mathbb E_P\{q_d(L_d)f(H_d)\mid A=a,G=O\}. \tag{3}
\]

Differentiate (1) along the observational submodel.  The conditional-score centering term vanishes because the conditional residual mean in (1) is zero, so
\[
 T_d\dot h_d
 =\mathbb E_P[\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid L_d,G=O]. \tag{4}
\]
Apply (3) with \(f=\dot h_d\), then use (4) and iterated expectation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P(\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E)
 &=\mathbb E_P(q_d\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=O)\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[q_d\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid A=a,G=O].
\end{aligned} \tag{5}
\]
Subtracting the derivative at \(a=0\) from that at \(a=1\), and using source-arm positivity to rewrite conditional expectations as source-conditional inner products, yields
\[
 \dot\theta_d(P)
 =\mathbb E_P(\phi_{E,d}\ell_E\mid G=E)
  +\mathbb E_P(\phi_{O,d}\ell_O\mid G=O). \tag{6}
\]
The definitions of \(\mu_{d,a}\) give \(\mathbb E_P(\phi_{E,d}\mid G=E)=0\), while (1) gives \(\mathbb E_P(\phi_{O,d}\mid G=O)=0\).  The two components are square-integrable by the finite-variance premise.  For independent samples of sizes \(n_E\) and \(n_O\), their sample-average variances add, giving
\[
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O}.
\]
Only the observable bridge equations, source-arm positivity, operator bijectivity, and finite source-score moments entered the argument.  No latent causal restriction, completeness condition, or equality with \(\tau\) was used.
