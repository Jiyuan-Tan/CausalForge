Put
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}.
\]
The pilot assumptions give \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  By the displayed nondegeneracy condition, define
\[
 \nu_0=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0.
\]
Because \(\underline v_m\downarrow0\), the event
\[
 \mathcal E_m
 =\{Z_m<\nu_0/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_0/2\}
 \tag{1}
\]
has probability tending to one.  On \(\mathcal E_m\), simultaneously for every \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(g\in\{E,O\}\),
\[
 \widehat V_{g,d}\ge V_{g,d}-Z_m>\nu_0/2>\underline v_m.
\]
Consequently the truncation in the pilot allocation is inactive throughout the finite catalogue:
\[
 \widehat V_{g,d}\vee\underline v_m=\widehat V_{g,d}.
 \tag{2}
\]

For each \(d\), define
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}.
\]
The population variance pairs and, on \(\mathcal E_m\), the estimated variance pairs lie in a common compact rectangle contained in \((0,\infty)^2\).  Positivity and finiteness of the costs and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) imply that the first derivatives of all the maps \(A_d\) are uniformly bounded on that rectangle.  By (2), the mean-value theorem therefore gives, on \(\mathcal E_m\),
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
 \le C Z_m
\]
for a finite deterministic constant \(C\).  Since \(P(\mathcal E_m)\to1\), it follows that
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}).
 \tag{3}
\]
This is the latent sharp allocation rate; the truncation sequence contributes no asymptotic term.

Write
\[
 a_d=c_{E,d}V_{E,d},\qquad b_d=c_{O,d}V_{O,d},\qquad
 F_d(\alpha)=\frac{a_d}{\alpha}+\frac{b_d}{1-\alpha}.
\]
The budget-profile theorem gives
\[
 F_d(\alpha_d^*(P))=\mathcal V_d^*(P),
 \qquad F_d'(\alpha_d^*(P))=0.
 \tag{4}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and every \(a_d,b_d\) is strictly positive and finite, there is a deterministic \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).
\]
Equation (3) implies that, with probability tending to one,
\(
\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\in[a_0,1-a_0]
\)
for every \(d\).  On this interval,
\[
 F_d''(\alpha)=\frac{2a_d}{\alpha^3}
 +\frac{2b_d}{(1-\alpha)^3}
\]
is bounded uniformly over the finite design family.  Taylor's theorem around \(\alpha_d^*(P)\), together with (3)--(4), yields
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 \left|F_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m})-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2).
 \tag{5}
\]

For every \(x\ge2\),
\[
 0\le\frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x
 \le\frac2{x^2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
On the preceding interior event, the two continuous source counts are bounded below by a positive constant times \(B\), uniformly over \(d\).  For all sufficiently large \(B\), (6) applied to both counts gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\Bigg|&B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}
 -F_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m})\Bigg|
 =O_P(B^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
The same event makes every floor count positive.  Its complement has probability tending to zero, so the stipulated total convention for zero counts does not change the stochastic order.  Combining (5) and (7) proves the stronger conclusion
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{8}
\]
Since \(r_{V,m}^2\le(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m)^2\), (8) implies the displayed claim.  It also shows directly that allocation estimation enters the profiled design value only at second order.
