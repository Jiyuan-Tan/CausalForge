Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), \(d\in\mathcal D\), and \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  Write
\[
 H_d=(S_{3d},S_{2d},A,X),\qquad L_d=(S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X).
\]
Because \(T_d\) is bijective, the square-integrable observed conditional mean of \(Y\) has a unique inverse image, namely \(h_d\), and the observed outcome-bridge equation is
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y-h_d(H_d)\mid L_d,G=O]=0. \tag{1}
\]
By short- and long-term consistency and observational latent exchangeability, equation (1), on the event \(A=a\), is the corresponding equation for \(Y(a)\) and the short-term potential outcomes under \(a\).  Define the latent conditional discrepancy
\[
 v_{a,d}(S_{2d}(a),U,X)
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[Y(a)-h_d(S_{3d}(a),S_{2d}(a),a,X)
   \mid S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O\right].
\]
The sequential-outcome restriction makes the conditional expectation of the bracket given \((S_{2d}(a),S_{1d}(a),U,X,G=O)\) equal to this same discrepancy.  Taking conditional expectations and using (1) shows
\[
 \mathbb E_P[v_{a,d}(S_{2d}(a),U,X)\mid
 S_{2d}(a),S_{1d}(a),A=a,X,G=O]=0.
\]
The designwise conditional early-proxy completeness condition therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d}(a),S_{2d}(a),a,X)\mid S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O]
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O]. \tag{2}
\]
Integrating (2) first yields the observational mean of \(Y(a)\).  Full-short-term external validity transports the joint distribution of \((S_{2d}(a),S_{3d}(a),U,X)\) from \(G=O\) to \(G=E\); experimental randomization and consistency then give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid G=O]
 &=\mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d}(a),S_{2d}(a),a,X)\mid G=E]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mu_{d,a}. 
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Subtracting (3) at \(a=0\) from (3) at \(a=1\) proves \(\tau=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}\).

It remains to calculate the canonical gradient.  Consider a regular two-source parametric submodel through \(P\), with conditional source scores \(\ell_E\) and \(\ell_O\), and write \(\dot h_d\) for the pathwise derivative of its outcome bridge.  Differentiation of the experimental bridge mean gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot\mu_{d,a}
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{E,a}}
   \{h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a}\}\ell_E
   \mathrel{\Big|}G=E\right]\\
 &\quad+\mathbb E_P[\dot h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E].
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
The selection-bridge equation and change of measure imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mathbb E_P[q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=O]. \tag{5}
\]
Differentiating (1) along the observational submodel gives
\[
 T_d\dot h_d
 =\mathbb E_P[\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid L_d,G=O]. \tag{6}
\]
Substituting (6) into (5) and applying iterated expectation yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mathbb E_P[q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O
   \mid A=a,G=O]. \tag{7}
\]
Subtracting equations (4)--(7) for \(a=0\) from those for \(a=1\) proves, for every regular score,
\[
 \dot\tau
 =\mathbb E_P[\phi_{E,d}\ell_E\mid G=E]
  +\mathbb E_P[\phi_{O,d}\ell_O\mid G=O]. \tag{8}
\]
Equation (1) and the definitions of \(\mu_{d,a}\) show that both displayed components have conditional mean zero.  Conditional expectation is a bounded Hilbert-space operator; because \(T_d\) is bijective, the bounded inverse theorem makes its inverse bounded, and hence \(T_d^\star\) is also bijective.  Consequently the representer in (8) lies in the two-source tangent space and is the canonical, rather than merely an influence, gradient.

For independent samples, the asymptotically linear average assigns \(\phi_{E,d}/n_E\) to each experimental observation and \(\phi_{O,d}/n_O\) to each observational observation.  Independence and the definitions of the two variance components give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(
  \frac1{n_E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_E}\phi_{E,d,i}
 +\frac1{n_O}\sum_{i=1}^{n_O}\phi_{O,d,i}\right)
 =\frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O},
\]
which is the claimed local semiparametric variance.
