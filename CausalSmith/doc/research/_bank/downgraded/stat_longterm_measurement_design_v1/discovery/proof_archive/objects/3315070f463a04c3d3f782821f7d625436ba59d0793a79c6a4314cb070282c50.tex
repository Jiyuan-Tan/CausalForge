We prove the population identities first.  The outcome-bridge equation and exact specification (1) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P[b_{q,d,a}(L_{d,a})Y\mid A=a,G=O]
 =M_{d,a}\beta_{h,d,a}.                                   \tag{11}
\]
For the selection bridge, iterated expectation and its conditional density-ratio equation give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[b_{h,d,a}(H_{d,a})q_d(L_{d,a},a)mid A=a,G=O]
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[b_{h,d,a}(H_{d,a})
 \frac{p_P(H_{d,a}\mid A=a,G=E)}
      {p_P(H_{d,a}\mid A=a,G=O)}\,middle|,A=a,G=O\right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[b_{h,d,a}(H_{d,a})\mid A=a,G=E].            \tag{12}
\end{aligned}
\]
By (1)--(2), the left side of (12) is \(M_{d,a}^\top\beta_{q,d,a}\).  Nonsingularity therefore identifies both coefficient vectors and shows that (4) is the direct sample analogue of (11)--(12); it is one data-driven rule using the fixed, ex-ante bases rather than law-specific oracle functions.

Each entry of the empirical matrix and right-hand sides in (4) is an average of independent observations with uniformly finite variance.  For \(b_qY\), this follows from bounded \(b_q\) and (3); the remaining summands are bounded.  Chebyshev's inequality and the proportional-count condition imply, uniformly over the finite set of designs, arms, and folds,
\[
 \|\widehat M_{d,a}^{(-\ell)}-M_{d,a}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\|\mathbb P_{O,-\ell,a}[b_qY]-M_{d,a}\beta_{h,d,a}\|
 +\|\mathbb P_{E,a}[b_h]-M_{d,a}^\top\beta_{q,d,a}\|
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                           \tag{13}
\]
The same display holds with \(B\) replaced by \(m\) in the pilot.  Since \(\operatorname{sv}_{\min}(M_{d,a})\ge\underline\lambda\), (13) implies that every sample matrix is nonsingular with probability tending to one.  On that event, the resolvent identity
\[
 \widehat M^{-1}-M^{-1}
 =-M^{-1}(\widehat M-M)\widehat M^{-1}                    \tag{14}
\]
and \(\|\widehat M^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/\underline\lambda\) yield
\[
 \max_{d,a,\ell}left{
 \|\widehat\beta_{h,d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\beta_{h,d,a}\|
 +\|\widehat\beta_{q,d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\beta_{q,d,a}\|
 \right}=O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                  \tag{15}
\]
The zero extension off the nonsingularity event does not alter (15), because that event has probability tending to zero.

Every fitted-function error is a fixed basis vector times a coefficient error.  The bounded-basis condition, or equivalently the upper Gram bound in each source, converts (15) into the two strong-norm rates in (6), including under the artificial two-source path law because its source weights are bounded away from zero and one by condition \(\mathrm{(iv)}\).  Moreover,
\[
 T_d(\widehat h-h)
 =\mathbb E_P[b_h^\top\mid L_d,A,G=O]
   (\widehat\beta_h-\beta_h),
\]
and analogously
\[
 T_d^\star(\widehat q-q)
 =\mathbb E_P[b_q^\top\mid H_d,A,G=O]
   (\widehat\beta_q-\beta_q).
\]
Conditional Jensen's inequality and bounded bases therefore give the two weak rates in (6).  Taking all four deterministic envelopes proportional to \(B^{-1/2}\) proves (7).

The population coefficient vectors are bounded uniformly over the finite catalogue by (11)--(12), the moment envelope, and \(\|M_{d,a}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\underline\lambda^{-1}\).  Hence \(h_d\) and \(q_d\) are bounded, while \(Y-h_d\) has a finite \((4+\delta)\)-moment.  Conditional on the training samples, bounded bases and (15) give
\[
 \|\Delta q_{B,\ell}(Y-h_d)\|_2=O_P(B^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \|q_d\Delta h_{B,\ell}\|_2=O_P(B^{-1/2}),
\]
and
\[
 \|\Delta q_{B,\ell}\Delta h_{B,\ell}\|_2=O_P(B^{-1}). \tag{16}
\]
Multiplication by \(\mathbf1\{G=O\}\) and passage to the artificial path norm only multiply these bounds by fixed constants.  The finite maximum in (16) proves (8).

It remains to prove the variance rates.  Positivity, bounded fitted and true bridge functions, and (3) imply that every true source score has a uniformly finite fourth moment.  Conditional on the fold-external fits, the empirical average of its squared value differs from its expectation by \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\) by Chebyshev's inequality.  Empirical arm proportions and bridge means are ordinary finite-dimensional sample averages and differ from their population counterparts by \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Expanding the plug-in score around the true score, using (15), and applying Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \|\widehat\phi_{g,d,B}-\phi_{g,d}\|_2=O_P(B^{-1/2}),
\]
and consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left|\mathbb P_g\widehat\phi_{g,d,B}^2
       -\mathbb E_P(\phi_{g,d}^2\mid G=g)\right|
 &\le
 \left|(\mathbb P_g-P_g)\phi_{g,d}^2\right|\\
 &\quad+mathbb P_g|\widehat\phi_{g,d,B}-\phi_{g,d}|
                    |\widehat\phi_{g,d,B}+\phi_{g,d}|\\
 &=O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                         \tag{17}
\end{aligned}
\]
All inverse arm probabilities are stable with probability tending to one by the positivity and proportional-count conditions.  The same calculation with the pilot folds and (13) with \(m\) in place of \(B\) proves (9).  Uniformity follows because only finitely many designs, arms, and folds occur.  It also permits evaluation at the pilot-measurable random design \(\widehat d_m\), proving (10).  Displays (6)--(10) are exactly the four root-sample-size bridge rates, their \(B^{-1}\) product, multiplier negligibility, and the two source-score variance rates claimed in the lemma.
