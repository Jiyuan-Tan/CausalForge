Put \(r=1/4\), and first consider a center-law design whose early and late proxies have signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\). Conditional on \(U\), the signed treatment \(T\), the early proxy \(W_x\), and the late proxy \(W_y\) are mutually independent, with conditional means \(rU\), \(xU\), and \(yU\). Since \(U\) is Rademacher, marginalization gives \(p_{g,a}=1/2\) in both sources.

Fix \(T=t\). The two-by-two matrix of \(T_d\), with rows indexed by the early proxy and columns indexed by the late proxy, has determinant
\[
 \det(T_d)=\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}>0. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, Bayes' rule gives the difference between the two conditional probabilities of \(W_y=1\) as
\[
 \frac y2\left\{
 \frac{x+rt}{1+rtx}-\frac{-x+rt}{1-rtx}
 \right\}
 =\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}.
\]
For a single proxy \(W_s\), the determinant of the conditional-expectation map from functions of \(U\) to functions of \(W_s\), conditional on \(T=t\), is
\[
 \frac{s(1-r^2)}{1-r^2s^2}. \tag{2}
\]
It is nonzero for \(s=x\) and \(s=y\). Thus early- and late-proxy completeness and designwise operator bijectivity hold.

The outcome bridge \(h_d(l,a)=l/y\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E[h_d(W_y,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O]=u
 =\mathbb E[Y\mid U=u,T=t,G=O]. \tag{3}
\]
Bayes' rule gives the latent source-density ratio
\[
 L_t(u)=\frac{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=E)}{p(U=u\mid T=t,G=O)}
 =\frac1{1+rtu}. \tag{4}
\]
The proposed selection bridge \(q_d(e,a)=\{1-r(2a-1)e/x\}/(1-r^2)\) obeys
\[
 \mathbb E[q_d(W_x,a)\mid U=u,T=t,G=O]
 =\frac{1-rtu}{1-r^2}
 =\frac1{1+rtu}=L_t(u). \tag{5}
\]
Iterated expectation turns (3) and (5) into the two observed bridge equations. The nonzero binary-channel determinants make the latent and observed solutions unique.

Every primitive cell probability is positive. Hence treatment overlap and source support hold. Consistency follows from the definitions of \(Y\) and \(Z\); treatment is conditionally independent of the potential variables given \(U\) in source \(O\), randomized in source \(E\), and the full short-term vector and \((U,X)\) are source invariant. Conditional on \(U\), \((Y(a),W_y)\) is independent of \(W_x\), proving the sequential restriction. Finite support gives all moment conditions. The positive variances calculated below therefore complete membership in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

The same latent bridge calculation proves causal identification:
\[
 \mu_{d,a}
 =\mathbb E[h_d(W_y,a)\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mathbb E[U\mid G=O]. \tag{6}
\]
This does not depend on \(a\), and \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), so \(\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=0=\tau\).

The best predictor of \(U\) from \((W_x,W_y)=(e,l)\) is
\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l]
 =\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}.
\]
Averaging the conditional variance gives
\[
 R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}. \tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35. \tag{8}
\]
The paired-source costs are \(6\) and \(5\), so
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 =\frac{42(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2}
 <3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B). \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is the unique predictive optimizer despite its larger cost.

The paper's experimental score is \(2TW_y/y\), hence
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}. \tag{10}
\]
Its observational score is \(2Tq_d(W_x,A)(U-W_y/y)\). Conditional on \(U\), the squared residual has expectation \((1-y^2)/y^2\), while \(\mathbb E[TW_x\mid G=O]=rx\), and therefore
\[
 V_O(x,y)=
 \frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
 \left(1-2r^2+\frac{r^2}{x^2}\right). \tag{11}
\]
These quantities are positive and finite for every fixed positive \(x,y\). Substitution gives
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{64}{9},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025}, \tag{12}
\]
and
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}. \tag{13}
\]
The budget-profile theorem and the costs of \(d_{\mathrm e}\) yield
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot\frac{384}{25}}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}. \tag{14}
\]
For \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\),
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 >\frac{1568}{2025\cdot10^{-6}}
 >\frac{3872}{25}, \tag{15}
\]
so \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is the unique arm-normalized design-value optimizer. Expanding the square gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{1568}{2025\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}),
\]
and division by (14) gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{16}
\]

For stability, fix \(\epsilon\) and parameterize the constrained finite-support model by its free positive latent-cell probabilities. At the center every early and late proxy-channel matrix is invertible, hence remains so locally. For a nearby law \(Q\), solve the finite latent systems
\[
 K_{3d,Q}h_{d,a,Q}=(u:u\in\{-1,1\})^{\mathsf T}, \tag{17}
\]
and
\[
 K_{1d,Q}q_{d,a,Q}
 =\left(
 \frac{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=E)}{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=O)}:
 u\in\{-1,1\}\right)^{\mathsf T}. \tag{18}
\]
Strict positivity makes every ratio finite. Conditional proxy independence and iterated expectation yield the observed bridge equations, and the observed matrix is a product of invertible proxy channels and positive diagonal reweighting matrices. Thus the bridges exist uniquely and \(T_d\) is bijective throughout a sufficiently small neighborhood.

The bridge coefficients are rational continuous functions of the cell vector. Prediction risks, treated probabilities, and finite score moments are continuous as long as cells remain positive. The strict predictive and arm-normalized design-value margins in (9) and (15) therefore persist locally. The ratio is continuous because its denominator is positive. Shrinking the radius gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(Q)>\frac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon).
\]
This proves all claims of the lemma. Notice separately that Lemma~\texttt{lem:ikmw-arm-score-normalization} gives \(V_{g,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=V_{g,d}/4\) at the centers; no unscaled paper variance is being called the anchor's absolute efficiency bound.
