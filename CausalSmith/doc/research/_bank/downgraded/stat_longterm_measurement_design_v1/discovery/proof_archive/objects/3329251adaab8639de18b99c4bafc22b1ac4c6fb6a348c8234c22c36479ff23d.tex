Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  By Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), all \(V_{E,d}\) and \(V_{O,d}\) are finite and strictly positive.  The unit costs are finite and strictly positive by their definitions.  Hence Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) gives, for every \(d\in\mathcal D\),
\[
 0<v_d
 =\left\{\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}\right\}^{2}
 <\infty.                                                     \tag{10}
\]
This conclusion concerns source-score variances on \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\); Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}bridge\text{-}differentiable\text{-}submodel}\) supplies the gradient interpretation only along its declared submodels.

Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty, \(\mathcal O_P\) is finite and nonempty: it contains every diagonal pair \((d,d)\).  Therefore the maximum defining \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) exists.  Equation (10) shows that all of its ratios are finite and positive, while a diagonal ratio equals one.  Thus
\[
 1\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty.                            \tag{11}
\]
If the implication in (2) holds for a constant \(C\), then for every \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), division by the positive number \(v_{d'}\) yields \(v_d/v_{d'}\le C\).  Maximizing over \(\mathcal O_P\) gives \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C\).  Conversely, if \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) and \(p_d\le p_{d'}\), then \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), so
\[
 v_d\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)v_{d'}\le Cv_{d'}.
\]
This proves (2), including the asserted minimality of the order-restricted constant.

Let
\[
 d_{\mathrm{pred}}^*\in
 \arg\min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}v_d,
 \qquad
 d^*\in\arg\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d.
\]
Both choices exist because the relevant sets are finite and nonempty.  Since \(d_{\mathrm{pred}}^*\) minimizes \(p_d\) over all designs,
\[
 p_{d_{\mathrm{pred}}^*}\le p_{d^*}.
\]
Consequently \((d_{\mathrm{pred}}^*,d^*)\in\mathcal O_P\), and (10)--(11) imply
\[
 1
 \le \frac{v_{d_{\mathrm{pred}}^*}}{v_{d^*}}
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P).                                   \tag{12}
\]
The middle ratio in (12) is exactly
\[
 \frac{\min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}v_d}
      {\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}v_{d'}}
 =\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
\]
by Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}predictive\text{-}benchmark}\).  It is the least constant for the best-tie rule because the corresponding inequality is a single inequality between the positive numerator and denominator.  This proves (3).

For a nonempty class \(\mathcal Q\), a finite constant valid for the best-tie inequality at every \(P\in\mathcal Q\) must dominate each lawwise least constant \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\), and therefore must dominate their supremum.  Conversely, a finite supremum itself is such a constant.  The identical argument with the lawwise least pairwise constant \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) proves the second uniform characterization.  This also shows directly that the finite enumeration in (1) is the weakest exact lawwise comparison: no further cross-design operator condition is needed to make that lawwise constant finite.

Now suppose the numerical realization of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{comp}}\) in (4)--(6) is given.  Each cone is nonempty because it contains \(x_d(P)\), and positivity of \(Q_{R,d}\) makes every quotient in (6) well-defined.  The definitions of the infimum and supremum imply, for every \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(x\in\mathcal X_d(P)\),
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)Q_{R,d}(x)
 \le F_d(x)
 \le \Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}(x).                               \tag{13}
\]
Conversely, if constants \(0<\lambda_-\le\lambda_+<\infty\) satisfy
\[
 \lambda_-Q_{R,d}(x)\le F_d(x)\le\lambda_+Q_{R,d}(x)
\]
on every chosen cone, then division by \(Q_{R,d}(x)>0\), followed by the infimum and supremum in (6), gives
\[
 \lambda_-\le\Lambda_-(P),
 \qquad
 \Lambda_+(P)\le\lambda_+.
\]
Thus a positive finite common two-sided envelope exists if and only if (7) holds; \(\Lambda_-(P)\) is the largest admissible common lower constant and \(\Lambda_+(P)\) is the smallest admissible common upper constant.  In particular, these are the weakest extremal envelope constants on the declared cones.

For any \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), apply (13) at the two transported law-specific directions and use (4):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_d
 &\le \Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}\{x_d(P)\}
   =\Lambda_+(P)p_d\\
 &\le \Lambda_+(P)p_{d'}
   =\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d'}\{x_{d'}(P)\}\\
 &\le \frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}v_{d'}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{14}
\]
The last division is valid by (7).  Maximizing (14) over \(\mathcal O_P\), and then using (3), proves (8).

It remains to establish the profiling and generalized-eigenvalue assertions.  Fix \(d\) and \(x\), and put
\[
 u=c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x),
 \qquad w=c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x).
\]
When \(u,w>0\), direct completion of the square gives
\[
 \frac{u}{\alpha}+\frac{w}{1-\alpha}
 -(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)^2
 =\frac{\{(1-\alpha)\sqrt u-\alpha\sqrt w\}^2}
        {\alpha(1-\alpha)}
 \ge0.                                                       \tag{15}
\]
Equality in (15) holds at \(\alpha=\sqrt u/(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)\).  If exactly one of \(u,w\) is zero, the same infimum follows by sending \(\alpha\) to the appropriate endpoint; if both vanish, both sides are zero.  Substitution of the definitions of \(u,w\) proves (9).

The infimum in (9) need not be a quadratic form.  For example, on \(\mathbb R^2\) take unit costs and
\[
 Q_E(x)=x_1^2,
 \qquad Q_O(x)=x_2^2.
\]
Then
\[
 F(x)=(|x_1|+|x_2|)^2.
\]
This function violates the parallelogram identity, because for the coordinate vectors \(e_1,e_2\),
\[
 F(e_1+e_2)+F(e_1-e_2)=8
 \ne 4=2F(e_1)+2F(e_2).                                    \tag{16}
\]
Hence the profiled numerator is not generally represented by a quadratic-form matrix, so its sharp comparison constant is not generally a generalized eigenvalue.

In the finite-dimensional full-space case described in the theorem, fixing \(\alpha\) before profiling does produce the quadratic numerator
\[
 x\longmapsto
 \frac{c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)}{\alpha}
 +\frac{c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)}{1-\alpha}.
\]
Its largest generalized eigenvalue relative to \(Q_{R,d}\) controls only the corresponding upper full-space ratio.  The selection argument (14) also needs a strictly positive lower ratio, and equality further requires the law-realized directions to attain the relevant upper and lower envelopes while satisfying the prediction ordering.  Moreover, comparing different \(\mathcal H_d\) by one eigenvalue requires a common-space representation and a normalization of that lower envelope.  Neither the observable-law bijectivity of \(T_d\) nor the ordering \(p_d\le p_{d'}\) supplies these additional properties.  The asserted generalized-eigenvalue limitations therefore follow.
