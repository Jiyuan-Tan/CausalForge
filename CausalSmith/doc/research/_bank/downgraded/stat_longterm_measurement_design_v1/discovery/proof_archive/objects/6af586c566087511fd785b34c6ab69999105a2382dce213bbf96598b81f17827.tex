For a fixed short-term-outcome system and a design \(d\), let \(V_{E,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\) and \(V_{O,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\) denote the two source-score second moments printed in Imbens--Kallus--Mao--Wang Theorem~5 for the causal target \(\tau\). Suppose the conditions of their Theorem~7 hold: their Assumptions~1--4 and~6 define the local semiparametric model; at the law under evaluation, their Assumptions~6 and~7 admit unique square-integrable outcome and selection bridges; the corresponding conditional-expectation operator is bijective; and \(V_{E,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\) and \(V_{O,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\) are finite. Suppose also that \(n_E,n_O\to\infty\) and \(n_E/n_O\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\). Then their Theorem~7 gives the local efficiency lower bound
\[
 \sigma_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^2
 =\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda}V_{E,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}
 +(1+\lambda)V_{O,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}
 =\lim (n_E+n_O)\left(
 \frac{V_{E,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}}{n_E}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}}{n_O}
 \right).
\]
The proof of their Theorem~7 identifies the full two-source influence function assembled from the experimental and observational source terms as the efficient influence function, equivalently the canonical gradient, for \(\tau\); the two conditional source-score terms are components of that influence function rather than separate canonical gradients.

For attainment, additionally impose the remaining sampling and estimation premises of their Theorem~5: independent identically distributed observations within the two sources, both identification-and-consistency premises in statements~1 and~2 of their Theorem~4, cross-fitting, and both parts of their Assumption~8 with nuisance limits equal to the true bridges, all weak- and strong-metric errors vanishing, and the minimum of the outcome-weak--selection-strong and outcome-strong--selection-weak error products equal to \(o((n_E+n_O)^{-1/2})\). Under these premises and the same fixed-ratio regime, Theorem~5 gives asymptotic normality with variance \(\sigma_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^2\), while Theorem~7 identifies that variance as the efficiency lower bound; hence their doubly robust estimator attains the bound for \(\tau\). Equivalently, on the original estimator scale, the leading variance is \(V_{E,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}/n_E+V_{O,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}/n_O\).
