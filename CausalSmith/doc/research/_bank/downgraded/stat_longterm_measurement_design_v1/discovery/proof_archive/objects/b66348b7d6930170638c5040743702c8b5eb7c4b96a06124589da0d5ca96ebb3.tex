The actionable selection rule is to estimate each admissible design's two source-gradient variances from an independent full-catalogue pilot, insert known costs, minimize the profiled value, and purchase the two sources at the estimated square-root allocation. Prediction risk is not a defensible surrogate for that criterion: a weak early proxy can be nearly irrelevant for predicting \(Y\) once a strong late proxy is present, yet its inverse selection bridge inflates the observational influence variance by order \(\epsilon^{-2}\). This failure persists on relative neighborhoods of regular, commonly identifying laws and for every prescribed finite cost disadvantage. On the full regular class, the rule optimizes inference for the observed bridge contrast; it optimizes causal inference for \(\tau\) only on a common-identification subclass, with the IKM-normalized profile supplying the absolute causal-efficiency scale.
