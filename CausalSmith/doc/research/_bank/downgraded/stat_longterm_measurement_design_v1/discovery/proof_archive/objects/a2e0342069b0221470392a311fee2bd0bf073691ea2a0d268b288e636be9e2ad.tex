Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(d\in\mathcal D\), and abbreviate
\[
 H_d=(S_{3d},S_{2d},A,X),\qquad L_d=(S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X).
\]
Observed outcome-bridge existence gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{Y-h_d(H_d)\mid L_d,G=O\}=0. \tag{1}
\]
Equivalently, \(T_dh_d=\mathbb E_P(Y\mid L_d,G=O)\).  Bijectivity of \(T_d\) makes this solution unique.  Thus \(h_d\), the two experimental means \(\mu_{d,a}\), and the contrast \(\theta_d(P)=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}\) are identified from the two observed source laws.

Now take a bridge-differentiable regular two-source submodel in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}bridge\text{-}differentiable\text{-}submodel}\), with source-conditional scores \(\ell_E,\ell_O\) and bridge derivative \(\dot h_d\).  Differentiability in quadratic mean, the declared \(L_2\) bridge differentiability, and the usual product rule give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot\mu_{d,a}
 &={\mathbb E}_P\!\left[
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{E,a}}
   \{h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a}\}\ell_E
   \mathrel{\Big|}G=E\right]\\
 &\quad+{\mathbb E}_P\{\dot h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E\}. \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
The selection-bridge equation states that \(T_d^\star q_d\) is the conditional density ratio of \((S_{3d},S_{2d},X)\) in source \(E\) relative to source \(O\), within each treatment arm.  Therefore, for every square-integrable \(f(H_d)\), iterated expectation gives
\[
 {\mathbb E}_P\{f(H_d)\mid A=a,G=E\}
 ={\mathbb E}_P\{q_d(L_d)f(H_d)\mid A=a,G=O\}. \tag{3}
\]

The defining submodel condition permits differentiation of (1) in conditional \(L_2\).  The conditional-score centering term is multiplied by the zero conditional residual mean in (1), and hence
\[
 T_d\dot h_d
 ={\mathbb E}_P[\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid L_d,G=O]. \tag{4}
\]
Apply (3) with \(f=\dot h_d\), then apply (4) and iterated expectation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 {\mathbb E}_P(\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E)
 &={\mathbb E}_P(q_d\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=O)\\
 &={\mathbb E}_P[q_d\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid A=a,G=O]. \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
Subtracting (2) at \(a=0\) from (2) at \(a=1\), substituting (5), and using source-arm positivity to express arm-conditional expectations as source-conditional inner products yields
\[
 \dot\theta_d(P)
 ={\mathbb E}_P(\phi_{E,d}\ell_E\mid G=E)
  +{\mathbb E}_P(\phi_{O,d}\ell_O\mid G=O). \tag{6}
\]
The definitions of \(\mu_{d,a}\) imply \({\mathbb E}_P(\phi_{E,d}\mid G=E)=0\), while (1) implies \({\mathbb E}_P(\phi_{O,d}\mid G=O)=0\).  The nondegenerate-component condition makes both components square-integrable.  Thus they are the two source components of a pathwise gradient on the declared submodel class.  For independent source samples of sizes \(n_E,n_O\), the variances of their sample averages add, giving
\[
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O}. \tag{7}
\]
This derivation uses the explicit bridge-differentiability restriction at the differentiation step (4); it does not infer local inverse stability from pointwise bijectivity.  The identification part and the definitions of the source-score variances remain meaningful without that path restriction.  No latent causal restriction, completeness condition, or equality with \(\tau\) was used.
