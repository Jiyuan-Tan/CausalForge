Put
\[
 s_{0,B}^2
 =B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,\widehat d_m}}{N_{E,B}}
 +\frac{V_{O,\widehat d_m}}{N_{O,B}}
 \right\}.
\]
The pilot-oracle-regret theorem gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).                 \tag{1}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and every population variance and cost is positive and finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0,
 \qquad d\in\mathcal D.                                     \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1) and (2) imply that, with probability tending to one, both selected source counts are positive and proportional to \(B\).  The uniform floor-count conclusion of the pilot-oracle-regret theorem, evaluated at the random member \(\widehat d_m\) of the finite family, therefore gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).             \tag{3}
\]

Next, by the definition of \(\widehat s_B^2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 &\le \frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
       |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &\quad+\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
       |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{4}
\]
The two count ratios in (4) are \(O_P(1)\), and the uniform main-study variance error is \(O_P(r_{\widetilde V,B})=o_P(1)\).  Hence
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2=o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).       \tag{5}
\]
Nondegeneracy and finiteness of the design family also imply
\[
 0<\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \le\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty.         \tag{6}
\]
Thus \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\), and the denominator is positive with probability tending to one.

We now establish the random-pilot limit law from the cited fixed-design theorem.  First fix any deterministic sequence of pilot realizations for which
\[
 d_B\in\mathcal D,
 \qquad
 \alpha_B-\alpha_{d_B}^*(P)\longrightarrow0.                 \tag{7}
\]
From every subsequence, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) permits extraction of a further subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant.  Along that further subsequence, (2), the cost formulas, and the floor operation give
\[
 \frac{N_{E,B}}{B}\longrightarrow
 \frac{\alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{E,d}},
 \qquad
 \frac{N_{O,B}}{B}\longrightarrow
 \frac{1-\alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{O,d}}.                            \tag{8}
\]
Consequently both counts diverge, their ratio converges to a number in \((0,\infty)\), and \(N_{E,B}+N_{O,B}\asymp B\).

Here is the premise-by-premise map to the corrected fixed-design lemma.  The fold count \(L\) is fixed.  Conditional on the pilot, independence leaves two mutually independent i.i.d. source samples.  The added subclass condition supplies, for this \(d\), both causal identification routes and the latent Assumptions~6--7 bridges, with nuisance limits \(h_d\) and \(q_d\).  The four displayed uniform rate hypotheses supply both parts of Assumption~8 for every fold.  Since the total main-study count in (8) is proportional to \(B\),
\[
 \min\{\delta_{h,B}\rho_{q,B},
          \rho_{h,B}\delta_{q,B}\}
 =o(B^{-1/2})
 =o\bigl((N_{E,B}+N_{O,B})^{-1/2}\bigr).                    \tag{9}
\]
Finally, the nondegeneracy assumption supplies finite strictly positive source-score variances.  Thus every premise of the cited fixed-design theorem holds along this deterministic interior-allocation sequence.  Under the two identification routes, the designwise-gradient lemma identifies the experimental and observational scores in the fixed-design expansion with \(\phi_{E,d}\) and \(\phi_{O,d}\); their second moments are \(V_{E,d}\) and \(V_{O,d}\).  Hence the cited variance, rewritten on the original estimator scale using the two independent sources, is
\[
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{N_{E,B}}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{N_{O,B}}.
\]
The cited theorem therefore yields
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)                            \tag{10}
\]
along every deterministic sequence satisfying (7).  Notice that the centering in (10) is \(\tau\), exactly as in the published theorem; no observed-bridge-centered version has been inferred outside its identification premises.

It remains to justify, rather than assume, uniformity over the random pilot.  By (1), there is a deterministic sequence \(a_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\left\{
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|>a_m
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.                                 \tag{11}
\]
Let \(\mathcal H_m\) be the complementary high-probability event.  Let \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\) denote bounded-Lipschitz distance between probability laws.  We claim that, uniformly over deterministic pilot realizations in \(\mathcal H_m\), the conditional law of the statistic in (10) tends to \(\mathcal N(0,1)\).  Otherwise, there would exist a positive constant, a subsequence, and one violating deterministic pilot realization on each index of that subsequence.  Those realizations satisfy (7); a further constant-design subsequence exists by finiteness of \(\mathcal D\), and (8)--(10) then force the bounded-Lipschitz distance to zero, a contradiction.  This sequential contradiction proves the claimed uniform conditional convergence.

The pilot is independent of the main study, so conditional on a pilot realization the main-study law is exactly the corresponding fixed-design law used above.  Integrating the uniform conditional convergence over \(\mathcal H_m\), and bounding its complement by (11), proves
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)                            \tag{12}
\]
under the joint pilot and main-study law.  Equations (5)--(6) and Slutsky's theorem now give
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{\widehat s_B}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                           \tag{13}
\]

Finally,
\[
 \{\tau\in I_B\}
 =\left\{
 \left|\frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{\widehat s_B}\right|
 \le z_{1-\eta/2}\right\}.                                 \tag{14}
\]
The standard normal distribution is continuous at \(\pm z_{1-\eta/2}\).  Applying (13) to (14) proves \(P\{\tau\in I_B\}\to1-\eta\), completing the proof of the proposed narrowed theorem.
