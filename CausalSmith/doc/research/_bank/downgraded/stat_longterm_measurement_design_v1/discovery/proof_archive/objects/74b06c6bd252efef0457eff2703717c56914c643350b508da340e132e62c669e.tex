We separate the proof into the deterministic-path expansion, the transfer to the pilot-selected path, and studentization.

First, positive costs, Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) imply that there is a constant \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le \alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).                                         \tag{18}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) and (9) of the statement give
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).                     \tag{19}
\]
Consequently, with probability tending to one, \(a_0\le\widehat\alpha_m\le1-a_0\), and then
\[
 N_{E,B}\asymp B,qquad N_{O,B}\asymp B                         \tag{20}
\]
uniformly over the selected design.  Here and below, all constants may depend on the fixed law \(P\) and the finite family, but not on \(m\), \(B\), or the selected member.

We next work along a deterministic sequence \((d_B,\alpha_B)\) satisfying (1).  Write \(e_B,o_B\) for its two source counts and define
\[
 L_B=\frac{\sqrt B}{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
     +\frac{\sqrt B}{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i},
 \qquad
 s_{0,B}^2=\frac{B V_{E,d_B}}{e_B}+\frac{B V_{O,d_B}}{o_B}.        \tag{21}
\]
The source scores have conditional mean zero by the bridge equations and the definition of \(\mu_{d,a}\).  The two samples are independent, so
\[
 \mathbb E_P(L_B)=0,qquad \operatorname{Var}_P(L_B)=s_{0,B}^2.  \tag{22}
\]
Condition (1), (18), and the floor inequality imply \(e_B,o_B\asymp B\).  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) then gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{d_B}^*(P)\longrightarrow0.                 \tag{23}
\]
In particular, because the family is finite and every variance component is finite and strictly positive, \(s_{0,B}^2\) is bounded above and bounded away from zero.

We now establish the asymptotic linear expansion.  For a fold \(\ell\), abbreviate
\[
 \Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_{d_B},
 \qquad
 \Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_{d_B}.
\]
Since the source masses of \(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) stay bounded away from zero, its \(L_2\) norm is uniformly equivalent to each source-conditional \(L_2\) norm.  Treatment overlap similarly makes arm-conditional and source-conditional bounds interchangeable up to fixed constants.

Conditional on the training data, every evaluation-fold empirical mean is an average of independent observations.  Thus, for any fold-trained function \(f_\ell\) with finite conditional second moment,
\[
 \sqrt B\,\{(\mathbb P_{g,\ell}-P_g)f_\ell\}
 =O_P(\|f_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}),                                 \tag{24}
\]
because every evaluation-fold size is of order \(B\).  The same conclusion holds for arm means: empirical overlap follows from the weak law, and the identity
\[
 \frac{\mathbb P_g\{\mathbf1(A=a)f\}}{\mathbb P_g(A=a)}
 -\frac{P_g\{\mathbf1(A=a)f\}}{p_{g,a}}
\]
is the sum of a centered empirical mean divided by \(p_{g,a}\) and a denominator remainder whose product form is \(O_P(B^{-1})\) when \(P_g\{\mathbf1(A=a)f\}=0\).  Balanced folds change the resulting full-source average by only \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  These elementary ratio expansions apply to the oracle residuals because their arm means are zero.

Apply (24) to the four nuisance-generated terms obtained by expanding
\[
 \widehat q_\ell\{Y-\widehat h_\ell\}
 =q_{d_B}(Y-h_{d_B})
  +\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_{d_B})
  -q_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell
  -\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell.                                  \tag{25}
\]
Conditions (2)--(3) make the experimental \(\Delta h_\ell\) fluctuation \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) before multiplication by \(\sqrt B\), and condition (5) does the same for all three observational multiplier terms in (25).  Cross-fitting supplies the evaluation--training independence required in (24).

It remains to control their conditional means.  The outcome-bridge equation gives, for each arm,
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_{d_B})\mid A=a,G=O\}=0.          \tag{26}
\]
The selection-bridge change-of-measure identity gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=E)
 =\mathbb E_P(q_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O),                 \tag{27}
\]
so the experimental first-order bridge error cancels the observational \(-q_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\) term.  The only population remainder is therefore a finite average of terms proportional to
\(
 \mathbb E_P(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)
\).
By adjointness and Cauchy--Schwarz it has both bounds
\[
 \bigl|\mathbb E_P(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)\bigr|
 \le C\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B}
       \|T_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B},                    \tag{28}
\]
and
\[
 \bigl|\mathbb E_P(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)\bigr|
 \le C\|T_{d_B}^{\star}\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}
       \|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}.                           \tag{29}
\]
The fixed overlap constants are absorbed into \(C\).  Conditions (2)--(4) show that the minimum of the right-hand orders in (28)--(29) is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed number of folds.

Combining the arm-ratio expansion, (24)--(29), and the definitions of the two oracle scores yields
\[
 \sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)\}
 =L_B+o_P(1).                                                       \tag{30}
\]
This also shows explicitly why the estimator is centered at the observed bridge contrast on the full class: no equality with \(\tau\) was used.

For clarity, the normal limit in (30) follows directly from the primitive second moments.  Normalize every summand in (21) by \(s_{0,B}\).  For every \(\zeta>0\), its Lindeberg sum is bounded by a finite sum of expressions of the form
\[
 C\,\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \phi_{g,d}^2\mathbf1\{|\phi_{g,d}|>c\sqrt B\}
 \mathrel{\Big|}G=g\right],                                     \tag{31}
\]
where \(c,C>0\) are uniform over \(g\in\{E,O\}\) and \(d\in\mathcal D\).  Each term tends to zero by integrability of \(\phi_{g,d}^2\), and the maximum also tends to zero because the family is finite.  To see the conclusion without adding any tail assumption, truncate the normalized summands at \(\zeta\), use
\[
 e^{itx}=1+itx-\tfrac12t^2x^2+o(x^2)
 \quad\text{uniformly for }|x|\le\zeta,
\]
sum the logarithms of their characteristic functions, and then let \(\zeta\downarrow0\); (31) makes the discarded quadratic contribution vanish.  The limiting characteristic function is \(\exp(-t^2/2)\).  Hence
\[
 \frac{L_B}{s_{0,B}}\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1),
 \qquad
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)\}}
 {s_{0,B}}
ightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                       \tag{32}
\]

We transfer (30)--(32) to the pilot-selected path.  By (19), one may choose a deterministic \(u_{m,B}\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\bigl\{|\widehat\alpha_m-alpha_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)|>u_{m,B}\bigr\}
 \longrightarrow0.                                                \tag{33}
\]
The deterministic-path hypotheses are sequentially uniform over
\[
 \{(d,\alpha):d\in\mathcal D,
 |\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)|\le u_{m,B}\}.                             \tag{34}
\]
Indeed, failure of uniformity would select a deterministic violating sequence from (34), contradicting the hypothesis that (1)--(5) hold along every deterministic sequence satisfying (1).  Conditional on the independent pilot, \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\) is a deterministic element of (34) on the event in (33).  Therefore conditional characteristic functions in (32) converge to \(\exp(-t^2/2)\) uniformly on that event.  Bounded convergence and (33) give the unconditional selected-path expansion and limit
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)
 -\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                                \tag{35}
\]

We next verify the total event and the variance estimators.  On the high-probability interior event from (19), both source counts and all balanced fold sizes diverge.  Experimental overlap and observational overlap imply \(p_{g,a}>0\) for every source and arm.  Conditional on the pilot, the probability that any one of the finitely many arm counts is zero is at most a finite sum of terms \((1-p_{g,a})^{k_B}\) with \(k_B\to\infty\).  Hence
\[
 P(\mathsf D_B)\longrightarrow1.                                  \tag{36}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}second\text{-}order\text{-}allocation\text{-}loss}\), evaluated at the selected member of the finite family, gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1})=o_P(1).                                   \tag{37}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\), (10), (20), and the definition of \(\widehat s_B^2\) give
\[
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 \le \frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|
 +\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|
 =O_P(r_{\widetilde V,B})=o_P(1).                                \tag{38}
\]
Outside \(\mathsf D_B\) the stipulated zero extension affects (38) only on an event of probability tending to zero.  Equations (37)--(38) prove both assertions in (16).  Moreover,
\[
 \min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)>0,
\]
so (36)--(38) imply \(P(\widehat s_B^2>0)\to1\).  This proves (15).

Equations (37)--(38) also imply \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  Combining this fact with (35), while noting that the zero extension is used only on \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}^c\) whose probability tends to zero, gives
\[
 \mathsf T_B\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                    \tag{39}
\]
On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the event \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\) is exactly \(\{|\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\}\).  The discrepancy outside \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\) has probability tending to zero.  Since the standard normal distribution is continuous,
\[
 P\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}
 \longrightarrow
 P\{|Z|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\}=1-\eta,
 \qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1).                                    \tag{40}
\]
Finally, if \(\theta_d(P)=\tau\) for every \(d\in\mathcal D\), then \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)=\tau\) identically.  Substitution in (35), (39), and (40) proves the final causal-centering and coverage assertions.
