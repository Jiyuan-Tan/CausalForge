Apply the Carneiro--Lee--Wilhelm population data-collection rule under the linear per-unit cost specialization
\[
 c(M_d,n)=n(c_{E,d}+c_{O,d}),
\]
mapping their selected support only to \(M_d\) and retaining this note's preattached role assignment \(r_d\). Choose the radii defining \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) and \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\) after the declared joint shrinkings. Then every \(P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) is regular, identifies the common contrast \(\tau\), and satisfies
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{CLW}}(P;B)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
 \qquad D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\},
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}(P;B)=\infty. \tag{1}
\]
Every \(P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\) also has \(D_{\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}\) and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}}
 \rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P;B)=\infty.             \tag{2}
\]
For each law in either region, the integer-budget criterion
\[
 d\longmapsto
 \frac{R_d(P)}{\lfloor B/(c_{E,d}+c_{O,d})\rfloor}
\]
is well defined and uniquely selects \(d_{\mathrm p}\) for all sufficiently large \(B\), with a law-specific threshold.

At every center, the CLW selector and both generic population squared-risk selectors choose \(d_{\mathrm p}\), whereas both bridge profiles choose \(d_{\mathrm e}\). All six resulting ratios therefore agree. For fixed \(\chi>1\), their common value obeys
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}+O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}
 =\frac1{726}+\frac5{726(10\chi-3)}.                       \tag{3}
\]
After optimizing the exact common ratio over \(\chi>1\),
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)
 \sim\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15}\epsilon^{-2/3},
\]
\[
 \epsilon^2\inf_{\chi>1}\rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 =\frac1{726}+\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}\epsilon^{2/3}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3}),                                      \tag{4}
\]
and the same expansion holds for the other five center ratios. The paired-cost disadvantage of the CLW-selected center design is the prescribed finite factor \(\chi\).

Thus the CLW linear-cost population rule, when transported to the bridge-design catalogue through its measured support, has no finite uniform bridge-efficiency guarantee even on a relatively open region where every design is regular and identifies the same causal effect. The claim is confined to this linear-cost population criterion. It does not refute CLW's randomized-trial optimality result, their empirical equation~(3.6) implementation, their approximate orthogonal greedy algorithm, arbitrary prediction methods, or any CLW choice of bridge roles.
