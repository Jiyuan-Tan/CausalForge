Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  For either prediction rule \(j\in\{0,A\}\), let \(R_d^0(P)=R_d(P)\), \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}^0(P)=\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\), and \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^0(P)=\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\).  Then the exact fixed-catalogue identity is
\[
 1\le\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^j(P)
 =\frac{\min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}^j(P)}
 (\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}})^2}
 {\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}
 (\sqrt{c_{E,d'}V_{E,d'}}+\sqrt{c_{O,d'}V_{O,d'}})^2}<\infty.                 \tag{1}
\]
The smallest constant in the actual best-tie inequality is exactly \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^j(P)\).  The least constant making every implication
\[
 R_d^j(P)\le R_{d'}^j(P)
 \Longrightarrow
 \mathcal V_d^*(P)\le C_j(P)\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P)
\]
valid is
\[
 C_j(P)=
 \max_{\substack{d,d'\in\mathcal D:\,R_d^j(P)\le R_{d'}^j(P)}}
 \frac{\mathcal V_d^*(P)}{\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P)}.                             \tag{2}
\]
Every displayed constant is finite at a fixed \(P\).  Nevertheless, for every prescribed finite \(\chi>1\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
 =\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^A(P)=\infty,     \tag{3}
\]
even though the design uniquely selected by both prediction rules has paired cost \(\chi\) times that of the arm-normalized source-score-optimal design at the centers.  Across all center cost-disadvantage factors, every minimizing sequence satisfies
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)
 \sim\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15}\epsilon^{-2/3},
\]
and, for each of the two arm-normalized and two IKM-normalized ratios,
\[
 \epsilon^2\inf_{\chi>1}\rho(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac1{726}+\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}\epsilon^{2/3}+o(\epsilon^{2/3}).      \tag{4}
\]
Thus finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) yields exact law-specific enumeration for each standard squared-risk rule, not a primitive uniform prediction-risk-to-source-score guarantee.  Conditioning prediction on treatment and optimizing the witness cost disadvantage do not remove the leading \(1/(726\epsilon^2)\) divergence.
