Fix \(\chi>1\). By Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}region}\), the radii used to construct \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) are chosen inside the joint stability radii. Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}predictive\text{-}binary\text{-}stability}\), together with Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}predictive\text{-}impossibility}\), therefore gives, for every \(P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\), regularity, equality of both designwise bridge contrasts with \(\tau\), and
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
 =\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}^A(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
 \qquad
 D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}. \tag{1}
\]
Likewise, Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}ikmw\text{-}region}\) chooses the smaller region inside the IKM-normalized stability radii. Hence every \(P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\) also satisfies
\[
 D_{\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}. \tag{2}
\]
These conclusions use only the declared finite-cell regularity, positivity, completeness, and strict-ordering results embodied in the cited frozen nodes; no boundary law with a singular bridge operator is introduced.

For every \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), the center \(P_{\chi,\epsilon}\) belongs to its defining ball in both regions. Lemmas~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}treatment\text{-}aware\text{-}sharp\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) give
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{A,\mathrm{IKM}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =:\rho_\chi(\epsilon), \tag{3}
\]
where the exact common value is
\[
 \rho_\chi(\epsilon)
 =\frac{25}{3872}
 \left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3}}
 +\sqrt{\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}
 \left(\epsilon^{-2}+14\right)}
 \right\}^{2}. \tag{4}
\]
In particular, the same lemma yields the uniform lower bound
\[
 \rho_\chi(\epsilon)
 >\frac{\epsilon^{-2}+14}{726}. \tag{5}
\]
Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) in (5), while keeping \(\chi\) fixed, and using the center inclusions proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
 =\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^A(P)
 =\infty, \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}}
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P)
 =\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}}
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{A,\mathrm{IKM}}(P)
 =\infty. \tag{7}
\]
Thus the failure in (6)--(7) occurs throughout regular, common-identification regions; it is an efficiency failure rather than an identification or boundary failure.

For clarity about the constants, expansion of (4) at fixed \(\chi\), already established in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\), gives
\[
 \rho_\chi(\epsilon)
 =c(\chi)\epsilon^{-2}+O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}),
 \qquad
 c(\chi)=\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}. \tag{8}
\]
Direct algebra shows
\[
 c(\chi)-\frac1{726}
 =\frac{2(5\chi+1)-(10\chi-3)}{726(10\chi-3)}
 =\frac5{726(10\chi-3)}>0. \tag{9}
\]
Therefore \(1/726\) is not the leading coefficient on any fixed-\(\chi\) path. The sharp optimization part of that lemma instead proves that every minimizing cost-disadvantage sequence satisfies
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)
 \sim\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15}\epsilon^{-2/3}, \tag{10}
\]
and, for the common center ratio in (3),
\[
 \epsilon^2\inf_{\chi>1}\rho_\chi(\epsilon)
 =\frac1{726}
 +\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}\epsilon^{2/3}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3}). \tag{11}
\]
Equations (8)--(11) prove the qualified fixed-\(\chi\) and optimized-envelope assertions. The cost formulas in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}predictive\text{-}impossibility}\) give paired costs \(5\chi\) and \(5\), so their ratio is exactly the prescribed factor \(\chi\).

It remains to verify simultaneous selection by both risk rules on the cost neighborhood. Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}predictive\text{-}impossibility}\) supplies a relatively open neighborhood of the center primitive cost vector on which \(d_{\mathrm p}\) remains strictly costlier and uniquely selected by the unadjusted risk for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\). At \(\epsilon=0\), the continuous extension of the treatment-aware center risks satisfies
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}^A
 \le R_{d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac3{10\chi}
 <\frac3{10}
 <\frac{585}{1024}
 =R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A. \tag{12}
\]
The first inequality follows because adding \(A\) enlarges the prediction sigma-field, and the last equality is Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}treatment\text{-}aware\text{-}sharp\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\). All relevant finite-cell conditional risks are continuous at the strictly positive cells for \(\epsilon>0\), and (12) is a strict limiting gap. Shrinking the original cost-manifold neighborhood therefore preserves unique selection of \(d_{\mathrm p}\) by the treatment-aware risk as well. Intersecting the two neighborhoods preserves strict sourcewise cost disadvantage and both selector inequalities.

Finally, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}predictive\text{-}impossibility}\) shows on this neighborhood that the arm-normalized numerator diverges like a positive multiple of \(\epsilon^{-2}\), while the value of \(d_{\mathrm e}\) remains finite and positive. At the balanced centers, Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}ikmw\text{-}normalized\text{-}design\text{-}value}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac14\mathcal V_d^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon}), \tag{13}
\]
so the factor \(1/4\) cancels from the IKM-normalized ratio and that ratio diverges as well. The conclusions concern exactly the two selectors defined by minimizing \(R_d\) and \(R_d^A\); no step identifies either selector with a named published workflow or with an arbitrary predictive procedure. This completes the proof.
