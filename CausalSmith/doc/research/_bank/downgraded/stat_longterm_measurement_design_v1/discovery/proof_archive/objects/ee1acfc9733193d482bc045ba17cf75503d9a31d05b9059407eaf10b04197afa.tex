Because \(X\) is constant and \(Y=U\) on the center family, \(R_d^A\) is the Bayes mean-square error for predicting \(U\) from \((T,W_x,W_y)\). In the observational source, conditional independence given \(U\) gives, for \(u,t,e,l\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 L_u(t,e,l):=P(T=t,W_x=e,W_y=l\mid U=u,G=O)
 =\frac18\left(1+\frac{tu}{4}\right)(1+xue)(1+yul). \tag{1}
\]
All eight observable cells have positive mass because \(x,y\in(0,1)\). With the Rademacher prior on \(U\), Bayes' rule gives
\[
 E(U\mid t,e,l,G=O)=\frac{L_1(t,e,l)-L_{-1}(t,e,l)}
 {L_1(t,e,l)+L_{-1}(t,e,l)}.
\]
The contribution of cell \((t,e,l)\) to the Bayes risk is therefore
\[
 \frac{L_1+L_{-1}}2
 \left\{1-\left(\frac{L_1-L_{-1}}{L_1+L_{-1}}\right)^2\right\}
 =\frac{2L_1L_{-1}}{L_1+L_{-1}}. \tag{2}
\]
Summing (2) over the eight triples and collecting the pairs with opposite signs yields
\[
 \sum_{t,e,l}\frac{2L_1(t,e,l)L_{-1}(t,e,l)}
 {L_1(t,e,l)+L_{-1}(t,e,l)}
 =\frac{15(1-x^2)(1-y^2)(16-x^2-y^2-14x^2y^2)}
 {\{16(1+xy)^2-(x+y)^2\}\{16(1-xy)^2-(x-y)^2\}}, \tag{3}
\]
which proves (1) in the statement. The two denominator factors in (3) are positive because they are products of sums of the positive likelihoods in (1).

For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), substituting \(x=y=1/2\) into (3) gives
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{15(3/4)^2(117/8)}{24\cdot9}
 =\frac{585}{1024}. \tag{4}
\]
For any square-integrable response, enlarging the predictor sigma-field cannot increase the optimal squared-error risk: conditional expectation is the orthogonal projection in \(L_2\). Since
\[
 \sigma(X,Z_{M_d})\subseteq\sigma(A,X,Z_{M_d}),
\]
we have \(R_d^A(P)\le R_d(P)\). Applying this to \(d_{\mathrm p}\) and using the strict raw-risk chain in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) proves (2). In particular, the treatment-aware risk gap is bounded below uniformly in \(\epsilon\) by
\[
 \frac{585}{1024}-\frac3{10}=\frac{1389}{5120}>0. \tag{5}
\]
Thus both prediction selectors have the same unique center optimizer \(d_{\mathrm p}\). Their best-tie numerators are consequently identical for each of the arm-normalized and IKM-normalized profiles, proving (3). Every exact ratio and every optimization over \(\chi\) then agrees pointwise with the corresponding quantity in the sharp-elbow lemma, so all its constants and expansions carry over without alteration.
