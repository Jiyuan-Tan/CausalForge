For \(d\in\mathcal D\), abbreviate the identified bridge contrast by
\[
 \theta_d(P):=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0},
\]
and let \(\mathcal F_m:=\sigma(\mathcal P_m)\).  Pilot independence makes \(\widehat d_m\), \(\widehat\alpha_m\), \(N_{E,B}\), and \(N_{O,B}\) measurable with respect to \(\mathcal F_m\) and independent of the main-study observations.  The pilot-oracle-regret theorem, the explicit premise \(\underline v_m=o(1)\), and nondegeneracy imply that there is an \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 \Pr\{a_0\le\widehat\alpha_m\le1-a_0\}\longrightarrow1.                \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the finite set of population shares \(\alpha_d^*(P)\) is contained in the interior of \((0,1)\), and the pilot theorem gives uniform convergence of the estimated shares.  On the event in (1), both realized counts are of order \(B\).  Uniformly over \(d\), the allocation conclusion of that theorem gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1),
 \qquad
 s_{0,B}^2:=B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,\widehat d_m}}{N_{E,B}}
 +\frac{V_{O,\widehat d_m}}{N_{O,B}}
 \right\}.                                                               \tag{2}
\]

We next adapt the established fixed-design limit to the pilot-selected observed bridge functional.  For fold \(\ell\), set
\[
 e_{h,d,\ell}:=\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d,
 \qquad
 e_{q,d,\ell}:=\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d.
\]
The exact doubly robust score algebra, using the outcome-bridge equation and the adjoint identity, leaves the conditional-mean remainder
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_{d,\ell}
 &=\sum_{a=0}^1(-1)^{1-a}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{O,a}}
 e_{q,d,\ell}\,(T_de_{h,d,\ell})\mathrel{\Big|}G=O\right]\\
 &=\sum_{a=0}^1(-1)^{1-a}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{O,a}}
 (T_d^\star e_{q,d,\ell})e_{h,d,\ell}\mathrel{\Big|}G=O\right].
\end{aligned}                                                              \tag{3}
\]
Since \(p_{O,a}>0\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied separately to the two representations in (3) gives a finite constant \(C_P\), independent of \(d\), \(\ell\), and \(B\), such that
\[
 |\mathcal R_{d,\ell}|
 \le C_P\min\left\{
 \|e_{h,d,\ell}\|_{w,h,d}\|e_{q,d,\ell}\|_{2,O,q,d},
 \|e_{h,d,\ell}\|_{2,O,h,d}\|e_{q,d,\ell}\|_{w,q,d}
 \right\}.                                                               \tag{4}
\]
The constant can be chosen uniformly because the design family and treatment set are finite.  The uniform weak--strong product premise makes (4) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Conditional on each training fold, cross-fitting centers the remaining empirical nuisance terms; the strong-rate premises make their conditional \(L_2\) norms \(o_P(1)\), hence their empirical averages are \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  This is precisely the empirical-negligibility step in the established fixed-design limit lemma, applied here to the observed bridge functional whose gradient is given by the designwise-gradient lemma.  Consequently, uniformly over the finite design family and over allocations in the compact interval in (1),
\[
 \widehat\tau_{d,B}-\theta_d(P)
 =\frac1{n_E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_E}\phi_{E,d}(O_{E,i})
  +\frac1{n_O}\sum_{i=1}^{n_O}\phi_{O,d}(O_{O,i})
  +o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                                          \tag{5}
\]
Here \(\widehat\tau_{d,B}\) is the same cross-fitted estimator run at design \(d\) and the displayed source counts.  The asserted uniformity follows from finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) and the deterministic-sequence version of the fixed-design lemma: a violating sequence would admit a subsequence with a fixed design and a convergent allocation in \([a_0,1-a_0]\), contradicting that lemma.  Thus pilot independence permits substitution of \(d=\widehat d_m\) and the pilot-measurable counts in (5).

Conditional on \(\mathcal F_m\), the two leading sums are independent and mean zero, and their variance after multiplication by \(B\) is \(s_{0,B}^2\).  The same subsequence argument applied to the standardized fixed-design limit, followed by averaging the conditional distribution over \(\mathcal F_m\), gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}
 {s_{0,B}}\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                               \tag{6}
\]

On the event in (1), the uniform main-study variance-rate premise gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 &\le \frac B{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &\quad+\frac B{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &=O_P(r_{\widetilde V,B})=o_P(1).
\end{aligned}                                                              \tag{7}
\]
Equations (2) and (7) prove both asserted variance conclusions.  Nondegeneracy and positive costs bound the finitely many values \(\mathcal V_d^*(P)\) away from zero, so \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  To transfer (6) to the estimated scale without invoking any additional result, fix a continuity point \(x\) of the standard normal distribution and \(0<\delta<1\).  On \(\{|\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}-1|\le\delta\}\), the distribution function of the statistic divided by \(\widehat s_B\) is bounded by the distribution functions of the statistic divided by \(s_{0,B}\), evaluated at the corresponding factors \(x(1-\delta)\) and \(x(1+\delta)\), with the inequalities reversed when \(x<0\).  The complement has probability tending to zero.  First taking \(m,B\to\infty\) in (6), and then \(\delta\downarrow0\), proves
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-(\mu_{\widehat d_m,1}-\mu_{\widehat d_m,0})\}}
 {\widehat s_B}\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                          \tag{8}
\]

By the definition of \(I_B\), the event that its random center covers the pilot-selected bridge contrast is
\[
 \left\{
 \left|
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-(\mu_{\widehat d_m,1}-\mu_{\widehat d_m,0})\}}
 {\widehat s_B}
 \right|\le z_{1-\eta/2}
 \right\}.
\]
If the statistic in (8) is denoted by \(T_B\) and \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function, convergence of its distribution function at the two continuity points \(\pm z_{1-\eta/2}\) gives
\[
 P\{|T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\}
 \longrightarrow
 \Phi(z_{1-\eta/2})-\Phi(-z_{1-\eta/2})=1-\eta.
\]
Hence
\[
 P\{\mu_{\widehat d_m,1}-\mu_{\widehat d_m,0}\in I_B\}\longrightarrow1-\eta.
\]
Finally, if \(\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=\tau\) for every \(d\in\mathcal D\), then the selected bridge contrast equals \(\tau\) for every pilot realization.  Substitution into (8) and the last display proves the causal specialization.
