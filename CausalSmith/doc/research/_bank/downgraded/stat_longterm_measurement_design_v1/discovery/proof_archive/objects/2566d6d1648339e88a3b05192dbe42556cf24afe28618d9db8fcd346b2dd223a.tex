Fix \(P\) as in the theorem.  We first prove a deterministic-design-and-allocation expansion and then transfer it through the independent pilot.

\emph{Interior counts.}
For every \(d\in\mathcal D\), positivity of the costs and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) imply \(0<\alpha_d^*(P)<1\).  Since \(\mathcal D\) is finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le \alpha_d^*(P)\le 1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).                                      \tag{1}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).                  \tag{2}
\]
Consequently, with probability tending to one, \(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\), and both purchased source counts are bounded below by a positive multiple of \(B\).  Source-arm positivity, conditional independent and identically distributed sampling, and Chebyshev's inequality give convergence of every empirical source-arm proportion to its strictly positive population value.  A finite union bound over the two experimental arms and the \(2L\) observational fold-arm cells therefore gives
\[
 P(\mathsf D_B)\longrightarrow1.                               \tag{3}
\]

\emph{Deterministic-path linearization.}
Take any deterministic path \((d_B,\alpha_B)\) such that \(d_B\in\mathcal D\) and \(|\alpha_B-\alpha_{d_B}^*(P)|\to0\).  Let \(e_B,o_B\) be its two floor counts.  Equation (1) and finiteness of the cost catalogue imply
\[
 e_B\asymp B,\qquad o_B\asymp B.                              \tag{4}
\]
Write \(h=h_{d_B}\), \(q=q_{d_B}\),
\[
 \Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-h,
 \qquad
 \Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-q.
\]

We use an elementary arm-ratio expansion.  If \(W_1,\ldots,W_N\) are independent and identically distributed, \(p_a=P(A=a)>0\), \(N_a=\sum_i\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}\), and \(f\in L_2\), then
\[
 \frac1{N_a}\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}f(W_i)
 -E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}
 =\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{p_a}
 \bigl[f(W_i)-E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}\bigr]
 +o_P(N^{-1/2}).                                                \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the centered numerator divided by \(N\) is \(O_P(N^{-1/2})\), whereas \(Np_a/N_a-1=O_P(N^{-1/2})\); their product is \(O_P(N^{-1})\).  Conditional on the training observations, the same calculation and conditional Chebyshev inequality show that a fold-external random function with conditional \(L_2\)-norm \(o_P(1)\) contributes \(o_P(N^{-1/2})\).  Because the observational partition is deterministic and balanced with fixed \(L\), replacing equal fold weights by observation weights changes the resulting average by \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).

The observed outcome-bridge equation and the selection-bridge change-of-measure identity imply, for each arm and every square-integrable early-argument function \(u\) and late-argument function \(f\),
\[
 E_O\{u(S_{2d_B},S_{1d_B},a,X)(Y-h)\mid A=a\}=0,              \tag{6}
\]
\[
 E_E\{f(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}
 =E_O\{qf(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}.                  \tag{7}
\]
Thus, conditional on the training observations, the nuisance drift in one arm is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &E_E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)
 +E_O\{(q+\Delta q_\ell)(Y-h-\Delta h_\ell)\mid A=a\}\\
 &\hspace{35mm}=-E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a).
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{8}
\]
The two terms linear in \(\Delta h_\ell\) cancel by (7), and the terms multiplying \(Y-h\) vanish by (6).

This is precisely where the corrected observational norms are needed.  Because \(\Delta q_\ell\) is an \(L_d\)-function, conditional expectation and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B}
          \|T_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B},\\
 |E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|T_{d_B}^{\star}\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}
          \|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}.
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{9}
\]
The first line uses only the observational strong-selection and weak-outcome errors.  In the second line, the maintained artificial-law norms of the source-defined functions \(T_{d_B}^{\star}\Delta q_\ell\) and \(\Delta h_\ell\) control their observational norms because (4) keeps the observational mass of \(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) bounded away from zero.  The four maintained rates and the product-rate assumption therefore make (8) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed arms and folds.  No value of \(T_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\) or \(\Delta q_\ell\) on experimental \(L_d\)-support is used.

The centered experimental nuisance perturbation is \(\Delta h_\ell\).  Its conditional empirical fluctuation is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) by the retained artificial-law strong-outcome rate and conditional Chebyshev inequality.  The centered observational perturbation is
\[
 \Delta q_\ell(Y-h)-q\Delta h_\ell-\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell. \tag{10}
\]
The multiplier condition makes the \(L_2(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\)-norm of (10), after multiplication by \(\mathbf1\{G=O\}\), equal to \(o_P(1)\), so its foldwise centered empirical contribution is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  When empirical arm probabilities replace population probabilities, their difference is \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\).  For a nuisance term \(g_B\), the resulting unscaled population error is bounded by
\[
 C|\widehat p_a-p_a|\,|P\{\mathbf1(A=a)g_B\}|
 \le C|\widehat p_a-p_a|\,\|g_B\|_2.                         \tag{11}
\]
The strong outcome rate handles \(g_B=\Delta h_\ell\); the multiplier condition handles \(q\Delta h_\ell\) and \(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\); and \(\Delta q_\ell(Y-h)\) has zero arm mean by (6).  Hence every denominator-replacement error is also \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  The training scheme supplies the required conditional independence: experimental score evaluation uses outcome bridges trained on observational data, and each observational evaluation fold is excluded from both bridge fits.

Applying (5)--(11) and contrasting the two arms yields
\[
 \widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)
 =\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 +o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{12}
\]
This expansion is centered at the observed bridge contrast and uses no common-identification restriction.

The two leading sums in (12) are independent and centered, with variances \(V_{E,d_B}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d_B}/o_B\).  To verify their normal limit, take an arbitrary subsequence and then a further subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant; this is possible because \(\mathcal D\) is finite.  Put
\[
 s_{0,B}^2(d,\alpha_B)
 =B\left\{\frac{V_{E,d}}{e_B}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{o_B}\right\}.
\]
Nondegeneracy and (4) bound this quantity above and away from zero.  After multiplication by \(\sqrt B/s_{0,B}\), each summand in the two independent arrays has coefficient \(O(B^{-1/2})\), and the sum of their variances is one.  For every \(\zeta>0\), finite second moments and these vanishing coefficients imply
\[
 \sum_j E\{X_{B,j}^2\mathbf1(|X_{B,j}|>\zeta)\}\longrightarrow0, \tag{13}
\]
because the two tail expectations are evaluated beyond thresholds tending to infinity.  Expanding each characteristic function to second order, the sum of the remainders is bounded by the left side of (13) plus a constant times the maximal coefficient, while the sum of the quadratic terms tends to \(-t^2/2\).  Thus the product characteristic function tends to \(e^{-t^2/2}\), proving
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B}{s_{0,B}(d_B,\alpha_B)}
 \left\{
 \frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 \right\}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                             \tag{14}
\]
Since every subsequence has such a further subsequence, (14) holds along the original deterministic path.  Equations (12) and (14) prove the deterministic-path studentized limit.

\emph{Transfer through pilot selection.}
By (2), there exists a deterministic \(t_B\downarrow0\) along the joint sequence such that
\[
 P\{|\widehat\alpha_m-\alpha_{widehat d_m}^*(P)|>t_B\}
 \longrightarrow0.                                           \tag{15}
\]
The deterministic-path conclusions are uniform over
\[
 \{(d,\alpha):d\in\mathcal D,
 |\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)|\le t_B\}.                             \tag{16}
\]
Otherwise, one could choose a deterministic violating pair from (16) along a subsequence, contradicting the conclusion proved for every deterministic path.  Independence of the pilot and main study permits conditioning on \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\).  Equations (12)--(16) therefore imply
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)
 -\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                             \tag{17}
\]

The floor-count conclusion of Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).              \tag{18}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\), direct subtraction yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 \le{}&\frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &+\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{19}
\]
The two coefficients are \(O_P(1)\) by (1)--(2).  The maintained main-study variance condition and its vanishing-rate companion make the right side \(o_P(1)\).  Combining (3), (18), and (19) proves
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).         \tag{20}
\]
Because \(\min_d\mathcal V_d^*(P)>0\), equations (3), (18), and (20) also yield
\[
 P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad \widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\longrightarrow_P1.              \tag{21}
\]
Combining (17) and (21), with an elementary product-convergence argument, proves
\[
 \mathsf T_B\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1),                  \tag{22}
\]
including the total zero extension off \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\).

On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the event \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\) is exactly \(|\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\).  The complement of \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\) has probability tending to zero by (21), and the standard normal distribution is continuous at both critical values.  Equation (22) therefore gives
\[
 P\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}\longrightarrow1-\eta.
\]
This proves every assertion under the explicitly listed maintained conditions, with the two corrected observational norms used only on observational support.
