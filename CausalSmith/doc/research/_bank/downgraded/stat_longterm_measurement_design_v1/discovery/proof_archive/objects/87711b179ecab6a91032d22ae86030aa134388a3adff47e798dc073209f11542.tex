Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(B>0\).  By Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}observable\text{-}bridge\text{-}law\text{-}class}\) and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), every \(V_{E,d}\) and \(V_{O,d}\) is finite and strictly positive.  The costs \(c_{E,d}\) and \(c_{O,d}\) are strictly positive by construction.  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) therefore gives
\[
 0<v_d
 =\left(\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+
          \sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}\right)^2
 <\infty                                                     \tag{10}
\]
for every \(d\in\mathcal D\).  Since \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty, \(\mathcal O_P\) is finite and nonempty: it contains \((d,d)\) for every \(d\).  Each diagonal ratio is one, and every ratio is finite by (10).  Consequently,
\[
 1\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty.                           \tag{11}
\]

Suppose first that the implication on the left side of (2) holds for a constant \(C\).  For every \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), it gives \(v_d\le C v_{d'}\).  Division by the strictly positive \(v_{d'}\) and maximization over \(\mathcal O_P\) yield \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C\).  Conversely, if \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) and \(p_d\le p_{d'}\), then \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), so
\[
 v_d/v_{d'}\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C.
\]
Multiplying by \(v_{d'}>0\) proves the implication.  This establishes (2), including sharpness of \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\).

Finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) also ensures that both minimizer sets in (3) are nonempty.  Choose
\[
 d_{\mathrm{sel}}\in\arg\min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}v_d,
 \qquad
 d_*\in\arg\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d.
\]
The best-tie rule incurs value \(v_{d_{\mathrm{sel}}}\), while the oracle value is \(v_{d_*}\).  Thus the smallest multiplicative constant in the best-tie inequality is exactly
\[
 \frac{v_{d_{\mathrm{sel}}}}{v_{d_*}}
 =C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)
 =\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P).                                 \tag{12}
\]
The numerator is minimized over a subset of \(\mathcal D\), so (12) is at least one.  Moreover, diagnostic optimality gives \(p_{d_{\mathrm{sel}}}\le p_{d_*}\), hence \((d_{\mathrm{sel}},d_*)\in\mathcal O_P\).  Therefore
\[
 1\le C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)
 =\frac{v_{d_{\mathrm{sel}}}}{v_{d_*}}
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty,                           \tag{13}
\]
which proves (3).

Let \(\varnothing\ne\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  If \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal Q}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=K<\infty\), then (12) shows that the single constant \(K\) works for every law in \(\mathcal Q\).  Conversely, any finite constant that works uniformly must be at least the sharp lawwise constant (12) for every \(P\in\mathcal Q\), so their supremum is finite.  Replacing (12) by the equivalence (2) gives the identical argument for all pairwise implications and \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal Q}C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\).  This proves both uniform-class equivalences using no restriction beyond the observable bridge class.

Now fix a numerical realization of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{comp}}\) satisfying (4)--(7).  Positivity of \(Q_{R,d}\) on \(\mathcal X_d(P)\) permits division.  From (6), for every \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(x\in\mathcal X_d(P)\),
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)
 \le\frac{F_d(x)}{Q_{R,d}(x)}
 \le\Lambda_+(P),                                         \tag{14}
\]
or equivalently
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)Q_{R,d}(x)
 \le F_d(x)
 \le\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}(x).                               \tag{15}
\]
The definitions by the global infimum and supremum show at the same time that \(\Lambda_-(P)\) is the largest lower-envelope constant and \(\Lambda_+(P)\) is the smallest upper-envelope constant on the chosen cones.  Hence a nondegenerate finite two-sided envelope holds exactly under (7).

For any \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), equations (4) and (15) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_d
 &=F_d\{x_d(P)\}\\
 &\le\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}\{x_d(P)\}
 =\Lambda_+(P)Bp_d\\
 &\le\Lambda_+(P)Bp_{d'}
 =\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d'}\{x_{d'}(P)\}\\
 &\le\frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}
 F_{d'}\{x_{d'}(P)\}
 =\frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}v_{d'}.
\end{aligned}                                              \tag{16}
\]
The last inequality uses \(\Lambda_-(P)>0\).  Maximizing (16) over \(\mathcal O_P\), and then using (13), proves (8).

It remains to establish (9) and the generalized-eigenvalue qualification.  Put
\[
 u=c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x),\qquad w=c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x),
\]
so \(u,w\ge0\).  For \(0<\alpha<1\), direct expansion gives
\[
 \frac{u}{\alpha}+\frac{w}{1-\alpha}
 -(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)^2
 =\frac{\{(1-\alpha)\sqrt u-\alpha\sqrt w\}^2}
        {\alpha(1-\alpha)}
 \ge0.                                                     \tag{17}
\]
When \(u,w>0\), equality holds at \(\alpha=\sqrt u/(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)\).  If exactly one of \(u,w\) is zero, the same value is approached at the corresponding endpoint of \((0,1)\); if both vanish, equality holds for every \(\alpha\).  Taking the infimum in (17) therefore yields
\[
 \inf_{0<\alpha<1}
 \left\{\frac{u}{\alpha}+\frac{w}{1-\alpha}\right\}
 = (\sqrt u+\sqrt w)^2=F_d(x),
\]
which is (9).

At a fixed \(\alpha\), the numerator in (9) is a quadratic form.  In the stated finite-dimensional full-space setting, its largest generalized eigenvalue relative to the positive-definite \(Q_{R,d}\) supplies its sharp upper Rayleigh-quotient bound.  Profiling introduces the cross term
\[
 2\sqrt{c_{E,d}c_{O,d}Q_{E,d}(x)Q_{O,d}(x)},               \tag{18}
\]
which is not generally a quadratic form.  Even when special structure makes (18) quadratic, a largest eigenvalue supplies only an upper envelope; the selection comparison (16) also needs the strictly positive lower envelope.  Finally, equality throughout (16) requires law-realized directions that attain the relevant upper and lower envelopes and obey the diagnostic order.  A single generalized-eigenvalue identity across designs therefore additionally requires the common-space and normalization structure needed to compare those envelopes, together with this saturation.  None of these properties is implied by the definition of \(\mathcal O_P\) or by designwise bijectivity of \(T_d\).  This proves the remaining assertions.
