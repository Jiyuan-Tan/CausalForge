For brevity, put
\[
 \theta_d(P)=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0},\qquad
 s_{0,B}^2
 =B\left\{\frac{V_{E,\widehat d_m}}{N_{E,B}}
          +\frac{V_{O,\widehat d_m}}{N_{O,B}}\right\}.
\]
The pilot-oracle-regret theorem gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).                     \tag{1}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and every population variance and cost is positive and finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\leq\alpha_d^*(P)\leq1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).
\]
Hence (1) implies that, with probability tending to one, both selected source counts are positive and of order \(B\).  The uniform floor-count conclusion of the pilot-oracle-regret theorem, evaluated at the random member \(\widehat d_m\) of the finite family, therefore proves
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).                  \tag{2}
\]

Next, the definition of \(\widehat s_B^2\) gives
\[
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 \leq \frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
       |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|
     +\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
       |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.      \tag{3}
\]
The count ratios in (3) are \(O_P(1)\), while the assumed uniform main-study variance error is
\(O_P(r_{\widetilde V,B})=o_P(1)\).  Thus (2) and (3) yield
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2=o_P(1),\qquad
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).             \tag{4}
\]
Moreover, the finite-family nondegeneracy condition and positive costs imply
\[
 0<\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \leq\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty.              \tag{5}
\]
Consequently (2)--(5) justify taking square roots and replacing \(s_{0,B}\) by \(\widehat s_B\) in a studentized statistic.

We now prove the limit law.  Conditional on the pilot \(\mathcal P_m\), the selected design, allocation, and two integer counts are fixed, and the main-study observations retain their original law by pilot independence.  On any deterministic sequence of pilot realizations satisfying
\[
 d_B\in\mathcal D,qquad
 \alpha_B-\alpha_{d_B}^*(P)\longrightarrow0,                      \tag{6}
\]
finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) permits passage to a subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant.  The two sample sizes on that subsequence are proportional to \(B\) and diverge.  The four uniform bridge-rate assumptions imply the corresponding fixed-design strong and weak convergence conditions, and
\[
 \min\{\delta_{h,B}\rho_{q,B},
        \rho_{h,B}\delta_{q,B}\}=o(B^{-1/2})
\]
is also the required \(o(n^{-1/2})\) product condition because both source sample sizes are of order \(B\).  The fixed-design Imbens--Kallus--Mao--Wang limit lemma, with the identified center \(\theta_d(P)\) supplied by the designwise-gradient lemma, therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-\theta_{d_B}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)                                 \tag{7}
\]
along every deterministic sequence satisfying (6).

For completeness, (7) also yields the required random-design conclusion rather than merely a collection of pointwise limits.  Since the left side of (1) converges to zero in probability, there is a deterministic sequence \(a_m\downarrow0\) for which
\[
 \Pr\left\{\max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|>a_m\right\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{8}
\]
If the conditional convergence in (7) were not uniform over pilot realizations in the event complementary to (8), one could choose a violating deterministic sequence of such realizations.  A constant-design subsequence would then satisfy (6), contradicting (7).  Thus, for every bounded continuous function \(f\), conditional on the pilot and on the event in (8),
\[
 \mathbb E\left[
 f\left(\frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-  heta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}
 {s_{0,B}}\right)\mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal P_m\right]
 -\mathbb E\{f(Z_0)\}\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly, where \(Z_0\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\).  The boundedness of \(f\), (8), and iterated expectation prove the unconditional version of (7) with \(d_B=\widehat d_m\).  By (2)--(5) and Slutsky's theorem,
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-  heta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}
 {\widehat s_B}\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                   \tag{9}
\]

Finally,
\[
 \{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}
 =\left\{
 \left|\frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-  heta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}
 {\widehat s_B}\right|\leq z_{1-\eta/2}\right\}.
\]
The standard normal distribution is continuous at \(\pm z_{1-\eta/2}\), so (9) implies that the probability of this event tends to \(1-\eta\).  If \(\theta_d(P)=\tau\) for every \(d\in\mathcal D\), then \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)=\tau\) identically; substituting this equality in (9) and in the last event proves the causal centering and coverage assertions.
