For every prescribed finite cost-disadvantage factor \(\chi>1\), the generalized unequal-cost binary witness has paired costs \(5\chi\) for \(d_{\mathrm p}\) and \(5\) for \(d_{\mathrm e}\). For every \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), the center law \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\) is regular for both designs and both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau=0\). Nevertheless, \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is the unique minimizer of the cost-aware prediction objective, while \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is the unique minimizer of both the arm-normalized profile and the IKM-normalized local causal-efficiency profile. More precisely,
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac32<3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B),
\]
\[
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}+O_\chi(1),
 \qquad
 V_{E,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=\frac14V_{E,d},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=\frac14V_{O,d},
\]
\[
 \mathcal V_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac14\mathcal V_d^*(P_\epsilon),
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 +O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}).
\]
Every \(P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) is regular for both designs, both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau\), \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\), \(D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}\), and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\infty.
\]
On the relatively open subregion \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\subseteq\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\), every design remains regular and identifies \(\tau\), \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\), \(D_{\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}\), and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}}
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P)=\infty.
\]
For each prescribed \(\chi>1\), the center cost vector belongs to \(\mathcal C_\kappa\), has paired-cost ratio exactly \(\chi\), and has a relatively open neighborhood in \(\mathcal C_\kappa\) on which \(d_{\mathrm p}\) remains strictly more expensive and uniquely prediction-selected for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\), while both the arm-normalized and balanced-center IKM-normalized ratios diverge along the center-law path. Thus predictive selection has no finite uniform efficiency guarantee even when its chosen design is required to bear any prescribed finite cost disadvantage; the failure is neither an equal-cost artifact nor an identification failure. Absolute causal-efficiency claims use the IKM-normalized profile and the fixed-system premises, whereas the arm-normalized profile is a bridge-gradient design value on the full regular class.
