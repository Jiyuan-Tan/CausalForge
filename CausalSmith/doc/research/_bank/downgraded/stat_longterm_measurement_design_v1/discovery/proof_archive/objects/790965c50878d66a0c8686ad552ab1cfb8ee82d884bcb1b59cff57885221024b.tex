Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Positive unit costs, Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) give, for every \(d\in\mathcal D\),
\[
 0<v_d
 =\left\{\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}\right\}^{2}
 <\infty.                                                     \tag{10}
\]
This is a source-score statement on \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\); by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}designwise\text{-}gradient}\), it is a gradient-variance statement only along the bridge-differentiable submodels declared in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}bridge\text{-}differentiable\text{-}submodel}\).

Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty, \(\mathcal O_P\) is finite, nonempty, and contains \((d,d)\) for every \(d\).  By (10), every ratio in (1) is well-defined, positive, and finite.  Its maximum therefore exists, is finite, and is at least one.  If the implication on the left of (2) holds, then for every \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), division by \(v_{d'}>0\) yields
\[
 \frac{v_d}{v_{d'}}\le C.
\]
Taking the maximum gives \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C\).  Conversely, if \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\) and \(p_d\le p_{d'}\), then \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\) and
\[
 v_d\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)v_{d'}\le Cv_{d'}.
\]
This proves (2), including sharpness of \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\).

Choose
\[
 d_{\mathrm p}^{\star}\in
 \arg\min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}v_d,
 \qquad
 d^{\star}\in\arg\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d.
\]
Both choices exist because the relevant sets are nonempty subsets of the finite family.  Since \(d_{\mathrm p}^{\star}\) minimizes \(p_d\),
\[
 p_{d_{\mathrm p}^{\star}}\le p_{d^{\star}},
\]
and hence \((d_{\mathrm p}^{\star},d^{\star})\in\mathcal O_P\).  The definition of the two choices and (10) now give
\[
 1
 \le \frac{v_{d_{\mathrm p}^{\star}}}{v_{d^{\star}}}
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P).                                   \tag{11}
\]
The middle quantity in (11) is exactly the displayed \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)=\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\).  If a constant \(C\) bounds the best-tie rule at this law, division by \(v_{d^{\star}}>0\) forces \(C\ge C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)\); conversely \(C=C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)\) gives equality.  Thus it is the least best-tie constant.  Taking suprema over a nonempty \(\mathcal Q\) proves both uniform characterizations: a finite uniform constant exists exactly when the corresponding supremum of the pointwise least constants is finite.  This also proves that the finite preorder maximum in (1), without any operator comparison, is the weakest exact lawwise pairwise certificate.

Now impose the stated numerical realization of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{comp}}\).  Positivity of \(Q_{R,d}\) on \(\mathcal X_d(P)\) makes every quotient in (6) well-defined.  By the definitions of the infimum and supremum, condition (7) is equivalent to the simultaneous inequalities
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)Q_{R,d}(x)
 \le F_d(x)
 \le \Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}(x)                                \tag{12}
\]
for every \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(x\in\mathcal X_d(P)\), with common strictly positive finite constants.  Moreover, no larger common lower constant than \(\Lambda_-(P)\), and no smaller common upper constant than \(\Lambda_+(P)\), can satisfy (12).  Hence (7) is precisely the existence condition for, and (6) gives the sharp endpoints of, the two-sided envelope on the chosen cones.

For any \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), apply (12) at the transported law-specific directions from (4).  The raw-risk ordering then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_d
 &\le \Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d}\{x_d(P)\}
   =\Lambda_+(P)p_d\\
 &\le \Lambda_+(P)p_{d'}
   =\Lambda_+(P)Q_{R,d'}\{x_{d'}(P)\}\\
 &\le \frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}v_{d'}.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{13}
\]
Maximizing (13) over \(\mathcal O_P\), and then using (11), proves (8).  Notice that the exact finite-order argument did not divide by \(p_d\); prediction risks may therefore vanish there.  Strict positivity is needed only for the conditional quotient representation in (6).

It remains to prove the profiling and generalized-eigenvalue assertions.  Put
\[
 u=c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x),\qquad w=c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x).
\]
When \(u,w>0\), direct algebra gives
\[
 \frac{u}{\alpha}+\frac{w}{1-\alpha}
 -(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)^2
 =\frac{\{(1-\alpha)\sqrt u-\alpha\sqrt w\}^{2}}
        {\alpha(1-\alpha)}\ge0.                             \tag{14}
\]
Equality holds at \(\alpha=\sqrt u/(\sqrt u+\sqrt w)\).  If exactly one of \(u,w\) is zero, the same infimum is obtained by taking the corresponding endpoint limit; if both vanish, both sides are zero.  Thus (14) proves (9) for all nonnegative \(u,w\).

The infimum in (9) need not be a quadratic form.  For an explicit counterexample, take \(\mathcal H_d=\mathbb R^2\), unit costs,
\[
 Q_{E,d}(x)=x_1^2,
 \qquad Q_{O,d}(x)=x_2^2.
\]
Then \(F_d(x)=(|x_1|+|x_2|)^2\).  With \(e_1=(1,0)\) and \(e_2=(0,1)\),
\[
 F_d(e_1+e_2)+F_d(e_1-e_2)=8,
 \qquad
 2F_d(e_1)+2F_d(e_2)=4,
\]
so the parallelogram identity fails and \(F_d\) is not a quadratic form.  A largest generalized eigenvalue for a fixed \(\alpha\) consequently controls only the upper quotient for that fixed-share quadratic form.  The selection comparison (13) also requires a strictly positive lower envelope, and equality further requires the transported, law-realized directions to attain the relevant upper and lower envelopes while satisfying the raw-risk ordering.  Comparing different \(\mathcal H_d\) by one eigenvalue additionally requires a common-space representation and a normalization of the lower envelope.  Neither operator bijectivity nor raw-risk ordering supplies any of these conditions.  This proves every assertion.
