For every fixed \(\chi>1\), the paper proves that the named exact integer population selector chooses \(d_{\mathrm p}\) at the centers and throughout suitably shrunk constrained-model neighborhoods for every \(B\ge5\chi\).  Its arm-normalized and IKM-normalized integer-selector ratios equal the corresponding continuous center ratios, have fixed-\(\chi\) leading coefficient \((5\chi+1)/\{363(10\chi-3)\}>1/726\), and remain unbounded on the relatively open regions.  The value \(1/726\) arises after optimization over jointly feasible \((\chi,B)\), with \(\chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)\sim\sqrt[3]{18}\epsilon^{-2/3}/15\) and the stated second-order term; it is not an unrestricted fixed-finite-budget optimization over \(\chi\).  Along the bounded finite-cell path with uniformly nonsingular full Grams, the independently funded pilot OLS criterion is uniformly root-\(m\) consistent and its exact integer selector inherits the divergent ratio without any rate link between \(m\) and \(\epsilon_m\).  Generic unrestricted \(R_d(P)\) and \(R_d^A(P)\) results are secondary and remain distinct from affine linear risk off center.  The proposed positive rule enumerates observable bridge values; Wald inference targets the selected observed bridge contrast on the observable class and the causal long-term effect only on the common-identification subclass.  Absolute IKM efficiency and broader learner attainability retain their separately stated premises.
