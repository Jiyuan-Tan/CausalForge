Fix \(\chi>1\) and \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), and first consider a generic design whose early and late proxy signals are \(x,y\in(0,1)\).  Write \(E=W_x\), \(L=W_y\), and \(t=2a-1\).  All latent cells at the center have strictly positive mass.  Consistency follows from \(Y(a)=U\) and \(Z(a)=Z\).  In the experimental source, \(T\) is independent of \((U,Z)\); in the observational source, \(T\) is independent of \(Z\) conditional on \(U\).  The overlap probabilities are bounded strictly between zero and one.  The joint law of \((U,Z)\) is source invariant, and conditional independence of the proxies given \(U\) gives the sequential-outcome restriction.  Thus all causal, overlap, support, and sequential conditions in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}regular\text{-}law\text{-}class}\) hold.

We next verify the bridge and rank conditions rather than assuming them from the construction.  The proposed outcome bridge satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{L}{y}\mathrel{\Big|}U\right]=U.
\]
Conditional independence of \(E\) and \(L\) given \(U\), including after conditioning on \(T=t,G=O\), therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E[Y\mid E,T=t,G=O]
 =\mathbb E[U\mid E,T=t,G=O]
 =\mathbb E[L/y\mid E,T=t,G=O].                       \tag{1}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \mathbb E[E\mid L=l,T=t,G=O]
 =x\,\frac{t/4+yl}{1+tyl/4}.                           \tag{2}
\]
For
\[
 q(e,a)=\frac{1-te/(4x)}{1-1/16}=\frac{16}{15}
       \left(1-\frac{te}{4x}\right),                   \tag{3}
\]
substitution of (2) yields
\[
 \mathbb E[q(E,a)\mid L=l,T=t,G=O]
 =\frac{1}{1+tyl/4}
 =\frac{p(L=l\mid T=t,G=E)}{p(L=l\mid T=t,G=O)}.       \tag{4}
\]
Thus (1) and (4) are exactly the two observed bridge equations.

For binary \(U\), conditional expectation given a binary proxy has rank two precisely when the proxy's two conditional rows differ.  The early and late signals \(x,y\) are nonzero, so both completeness conditions hold.  Also, conditional on \(T=t,G=O\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(E,L\mid T=t,G=O)
 =xy-\frac{xt}{4}\frac{yt}{4}=\frac{15}{16}xy\ne0.    \tag{5}
\]
Because all four conditional cells are positive, (5) says that the \(2\)-by-\(2\) conditional-expectation matrix from functions of \(L\) to functions of \(E\) has nonzero determinant.  Hence \(T_d\) is bijective.  Equations (1)--(5) prove bridge existence, uniqueness, both completeness requirements, and operator bijectivity.  Finite support gives all moment conditions.  The score variances computed below are finite and strictly positive.  Consequently \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\) for both designs.

At the experimental center, \(p_{E,a}=1/2\), \(\mathbb E[L\mid A=a,G=E]=0\), and hence \(\mu_{d,a}=0\).  Since \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\),
\[
 \theta_d(P_\epsilon)=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=0
 =\tau.                                                \tag{6}
\]
The experimental score is \(\phi_{E,d}=2TL/y\), and therefore
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac{4}{y^2}.                               \tag{7}
\]
For the observational score, (3) gives
\[
 \phi_{O,d}=2Tq(E,A)(U-L/y).
\]
Under the observational center law,
\[
 \mathbb E[TE]=\frac{x}{4},\qquad
 \mathbb E[UL]=y,qquad
 \mathbb E[TEUL]=\frac{xy}{4}.                         \tag{8}
\]
Expanding the two squares and using (8),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 V_O(x,y)
 &=\frac{1024}{225}\,
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left\{1-\frac{TE}{2x}+\frac{1}{16x^2}\right\}
 \left\{1+\frac1{y^2}-\frac{2UL}{y}\right\}
 \right]\\
 &=\frac{64}{225}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}\left(14+x^{-2}\right).       \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]

The best square-integrable predictor of \(U\) from \((E,L)\) is its conditional mean.  Bayes' rule gives
\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid E=e,L=l]=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel}.
\]
Since \(P(E=e,L=l)=\{1+xyel\}/4\), averaging the conditional variance gives
\[
 R(x,y)=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(U\mid E,L)]
 =\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.                    \tag{10}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), \(x=\epsilon\) and
\(y^2=y_\chi^2=1-3/(10\chi)\).  Its catalogue cost equals the cap, and direct substitution in the cost formulas gives
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{5\chi-1}{2},\qquad
 c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{5\chi+1}{2},
\]
whereas \(c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=2\) and \(c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=3\).  Thus the paired costs are \(5\chi\) and \(5\).  Equation (10) now gives
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon;B)
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac32<3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon;B),             \tag{11}
\]
because \(0<y_\chi^2<1\).  Hence \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is the unique predictive optimizer.

Substitution in (9) yields the exact formula
\[
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
  +\frac{896}{75(10\chi-3)}.                         \tag{12}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), \(x=y=1/2\), so (7)--(9) give
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}.
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\left(\sqrt{32}+\sqrt{1152/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}.                                    \tag{13}
\]
On the other hand, (12) and \(\mathcal V_d^*\ge c_{O,d}V_{O,d}\) imply
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge \frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
 \ge\frac{16}{75}\epsilon^{-2}
 >\frac{3872}{25},                                    \tag{14}
\]
where the middle inequality uses
\((5\chi+1)/(10\chi-3)>1/2\), and the last uses
\(\epsilon<10^{-3}\).  Hence \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is the unique arm-normalized design-value optimizer for every stated center.

Let
\[
 A_\chi=\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}.
\]
From (7), (12), and the profiled formula in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\),
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}A_\chi\epsilon^{-2}
  +O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}).                             \tag{15}
\]
Dividing (15) by (13) gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}
  +O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}).                             \tag{16}
\]

It remains to justify the asserted neighborhood rather than merely the center calculation.  In the relative-interior coordinates of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bin}}(\chi)\), every relevant conditional probability and every finite moment is continuous.  At \(P_\epsilon\), the early- and late-proxy conditional matrices and the two operators have nonzero determinants by (5), all support probabilities are positive, and both score variances are positive.  Determinants, support inequalities, and positive variances remain nonzero on a sufficiently small relative neighborhood.  On that neighborhood the bridge solutions are obtained by inverses of finite matrices, so the bridges, score variances, prediction risks, and both profiled values are continuous.

Moreover, throughout such a neighborhood the binary late-proxy matrix is invertible, so there is a latent outcome bridge \(h^\dagger\) satisfying
\(\mathbb E[h^\dagger(L)\mid U]=U\).  Experimental randomization and source invariance then imply
\[
 \mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E[h^\dagger(L)\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mathbb E[U]=\mathbb E[Y(a)\mid G=O],
\]
for both \(a\).  Because \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\) throughout the constrained model, both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau=0\).  Finally, the optimizer inequalities (11) and (14) are strict, and the ratio is a continuous positive function on the regular neighborhood.  Shrinking the radius if necessary preserves both optimizer assignments and ensures
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)>\frac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon).
\]
This furnishes the required \(r_\epsilon>0\) and completes the proof.
