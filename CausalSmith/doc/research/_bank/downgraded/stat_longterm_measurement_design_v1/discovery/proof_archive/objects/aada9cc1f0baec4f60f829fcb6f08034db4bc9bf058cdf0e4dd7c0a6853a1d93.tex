Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), \(d\in\mathcal D\), and \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  Write
\[
 H_d=(S_{3d},S_{2d},A,X),\qquad L_d=(S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X).
\]
The observed outcome-bridge equation is
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y-h_d(H_d)\mid L_d,G=O]=0. \tag{1}
\]
By short- and long-term consistency, observational latent exchangeability, and the sequential-outcome restriction, conditioning (1) at \(A=a\) shows that
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[v_{d,a}(U,S_{2d}(a),X)\mid
 S_{1d}(a),S_{2d}(a),A=a,X,G=O\right]=0, \tag{2}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_{d,a}(U,S_{2d}(a),X)
={}&\mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d}(a),S_{2d}(a),a,X)mid
 S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O]\\
&-\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O].
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the sequential restriction removes \(S_{1d}(a)\) from the conditional laws of both \(Y(a)\) and \(S_{3d}(a)\) once \((S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O)\) is held fixed.  Applying the designwise early-proxy completeness condition in (2), with \((S_{2d}(a),a,X)\) held fixed, gives the latent bridge identity
\[
 \mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d}(a),S_{2d}(a),a,X)mid S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O]
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid S_{2d}(a),U,X,G=O]. \tag{3}
\]
The corrected full-short-term external-validity condition transports the joint law of \((S_{2d}(a),S_{3d}(a),U,X)\) from \(G=O\) to \(G=E\).  Experimental randomization and consistency then imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid G=O]
 &=\mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d}(a),S_{2d}(a),a,X)\mid G=E]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mu_{d,a}. 
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
Subtracting (4) at \(a=0\) from (4) at \(a=1\) proves
\[
 \tau=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}. \tag{5}
\]

It remains to calculate the canonical gradient.  Consider an arbitrary regular two-source parametric submodel through \(P\), with conditional source scores \(\ell_E\) and \(\ell_O\), and let \(\dot h_d\) denote the pathwise derivative of its outcome bridge.  Differentiating the experimental bridge mean gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot\mu_{d,a}
 ={}&\mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{E,a}}
   \{h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a}\}\ell_E
   \mathrel{\Big|}G=E\right]\\
 &+\mathbb E_P[\dot h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid A=a,G=E].
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
The selection-bridge equation, its definition through \(T_d^\star\), and change of measure yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mathbb E_P[q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=O]. \tag{7}
\]
Differentiating (1) along the observational submodel gives
\[
 T_d\dot h_d
 =\mathbb E_P[\{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid L_d,G=O]. \tag{8}
\]
There is no additional conditional-score centering term in (8), because the conditional mean in (1) is zero.  Substitution of (8) into (7), followed by iterated expectation, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\dot h_d\mid A=a,G=E]
 =\mathbb E_P[q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)
 \{Y-h_d(H_d)\}\ell_O\mid A=a,G=O]. \tag{9}
\]
Taking the signed difference of (6)--(9) over \(a\in\{0,1\}\) proves
\[
 \dot\tau
 =\mathbb E_P[\phi_{E,d}\ell_E\mid G=E]
  +\mathbb E_P[\phi_{O,d}\ell_O\mid G=O]. \tag{10}
\]
Equation (1) and the definitions of \(\mu_{d,a}\) show that both components in (10) have conditional mean zero.  The operator \(T_d\) is a bounded linear map between Hilbert spaces.  Its assumed bijectivity and the bounded-inverse theorem imply that \(T_d^{-1}\) is bounded; consequently \(T_d^\star\) is also bijective.  Thus (8) describes all admissible local outcome-bridge derivatives, and the representer in (10) belongs to the two-source tangent space rather than merely to the ambient \(L_2\) space.  It is therefore the canonical gradient.

For independent samples, the linear representation assigns \(\phi_{E,d}/n_E\) to each experimental observation and \(\phi_{O,d}/n_O\) to each observational observation.  Independence, conditional mean zero, and the definitions of the two variance components give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(
  \frac1{n_E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_E}\phi_{E,d,i}
 +\frac1{n_O}\sum_{i=1}^{n_O}\phi_{O,d,i}\right)
 =\frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O},
\]
as claimed.
