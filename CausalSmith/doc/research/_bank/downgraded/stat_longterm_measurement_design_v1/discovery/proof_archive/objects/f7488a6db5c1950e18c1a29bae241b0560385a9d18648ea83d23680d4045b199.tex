Write
\[
 v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P),\qquad
 \widehat v_d=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*,\qquad
 v_*=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d,
\]
and choose any \(d^*\in D^*(P)\).  The definition of \(\varepsilon_m\) and the empirical minimizing property of \(\widehat d_m\) give
\[
 v_{\widehat d_m}
 \le \widehat v_{\widehat d_m}+\varepsilon_m
 \le \widehat v_{d^*}+\varepsilon_m
 \le v_{d^*}+2\varepsilon_m
 =v_*+2\varepsilon_m.
 \tag{1}
\]
Since \(v_*\) is the population minimum,
\[
 0\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*
 \le2\varepsilon_m.
 \tag{2}
\]
If \(\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\), the definition of \(\gamma(P)\) and (2) imply
\[
 \gamma(P)\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*le2\varepsilon_m.
 \tag{3}
\]
This proves the asserted event inclusion.  If \(D^*(P)=\mathcal D\), the event on its left is empty, and the convention \(\gamma(P)=+\infty\) makes the event on its right empty as well.  Otherwise, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) implies \(\gamma(P)>0\).

For the stochastic conclusions, put
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}.
\]
The pilot assumptions give \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  By the pilot construction and the budget-profile theorem,
\[
 \widehat v_d=F_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d}),
 \qquad
 v_d=F_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d}),
 \tag{4}
\]
where
\[
 F_d(x,y)=
 \bigl(\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}\bigr)^2.
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), positivity of the costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) place all population variance pairs in a common compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\).  With probability tending to one, all estimated pairs lie in a slightly larger such compact set.  The gradients of the finitely many maps \(F_d\) are uniformly bounded there.  The mean-value theorem therefore yields
\[
 \varepsilon_m
 =\max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat v_d-v_d|
 =O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).
 \tag{5}
\]
Equations (3) and (5) prove
\[
 P\{\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\}
 \le P\{2\varepsilon_m\ge\gamma(P)\}
 \longrightarrow0
 \tag{6}
\]
whenever some design is suboptimal; the all-optimal case was handled above.

It remains to sharpen the allocation conclusion.  Let
\[
 \nu_0=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0.
\]
Since \(Z_m=o_P(1)\) and \(\underline v_m=o(1)\), the event
\[
 \mathcal E_m
 =\{Z_m<\nu_0/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_0/2\}
 \tag{7}
\]
has probability tending to one.  On \(\mathcal E_m\),
\[
 \widehat V_{g,d}>\nu_0/2>\underline v_m
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D,\ g\in\{E,O\}),
\]
so every truncation in \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\) is inactive.  Define
\[
 A_d(x,y)=
 \frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}.
\]
The same compact-set mean-value argument applied to the finitely many maps \(A_d\) gives the stronger rate
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}).
 \tag{8}
\]
In particular, (8) implies the theorem's displayed \(O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m)\) rate.

For completeness, (8) also yields the sharp realized-allocation loss.  Positivity and finiteness give a deterministic \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).
\]
By (8), with probability tending to one every \(\widehat\alpha_{d,m}\) lies in \([a_0,1-a_0]\).  The first derivative of
\[
 H_d(\alpha)=
 \frac{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{\alpha}
 +\frac{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{1-\alpha}
\]
vanishes at \(\alpha_d^*(P)\), and the second derivatives of the finitely many profiles \(H_d\) are uniformly bounded on this interval.  Taylor's theorem and (8) imply
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |H_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m})-\mathcal V_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2).
 \tag{9}
\]
For \(x\ge2\), the inequality
\[
 0\le\frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x\le\frac2{x^2}
 \tag{10}
\]
shows, uniformly on the same interior event, that replacing both continuous counts by their floors changes the budget-normalized value by \(O_P(B^{-1})\).  All counts are positive on this event for sufficiently large \(B\), and the declared zero-count extension concerns only an event whose probability tends to zero.  Combining (9)--(10) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
 +\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
 \right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
Because \(r_{V,m}=o(1)\) and \(\underline v_m=o(1)\), (11) implies the stated uniform bound
\[
 O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m+B^{-1})=o_P(1).
\]
The deterministic tie rule was used only to choose an empirical minimizer in (1); the population consistency conclusion is therefore membership in the entire optimizer set \(D^*(P)\), not convergence to a particular tie-broken design.
