Fix \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), and temporarily suppress the subscripts \((d,a)\).  Write \(H=H_{d,a}\), \(L=L_{d,a}\), \(b_h=b_{h,d,a}\), \(b_q=b_{q,d,a}\), and \(M=M_{d,a}\).

We first verify the population moment equations underlying the estimators.  By the observed outcome-bridge equation, iterated expectation, and the exact representation in (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[b_q(L)Y\mid A=a,G=O\right]
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 b_q(L)\mathbb E_P\{Y\mid L,A=a,G=O\}
 \mathrel{\Big|}A=a,G=O\right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 b_q(L)\mathbb E_P\{h_d(H,a)\mid L,A=a,G=O\}
 \mathrel{\Big|}A=a,G=O\right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[b_q(L)b_h(H)^\top\mid A=a,G=O\right]
 \beta_{h,d,a}
 =M\beta_{h,d,a}.                                      \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
\]
For the selection bridge, put
\[
 r(H)=\frac{p_P(H\mid A=a,G=E)}{p_P(H\mid A=a,G=O)}.
\]
The selection-bridge equation says that
\(r(H)=\mathbb E_P\{q_d(L,a)\mid H,A=a,G=O\}\).  Hence a change of measure followed by iterated expectation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[b_h(H)\mid A=a,G=E\right]
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[b_h(H)r(H)\mid A=a,G=O\right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 b_h(H)\mathbb E_P\{q_d(L,a)\mid H,A=a,G=O\}
 \mathrel{\Big|}A=a,G=O\right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[b_h(H)b_q(L)^\top\mid A=a,G=O\right]
 \beta_{q,d,a}
 =M^\top\beta_{q,d,a}.                                  \tag{12}
\end{aligned}
\]
All quantities in (11)--(12) are integrable by boundedness of the bases and the moment condition in (3).  Since the smallest singular value of \(M\) is at least \(\underline\lambda\), (11)--(12) identify both coefficient vectors and show that (4) is their direct empirical analogue.  In particular, the construction is a single data-driven rule based on the fixed, known bases.

We next establish coefficient rates.  Work first on the event in condition \(\mathrm{(iv)}\), on which every relevant main-study arm sample and training-fold arm sample has size between fixed positive multiples of \(B\).  Each entry of \(b_qb_h^\top\) and \(b_h\) is bounded.  Each entry of \(b_qY\) has finite variance by (3).  Conditional on the arm counts, the usual variance calculation for a sample mean, followed by Chebyshev's inequality, therefore yields, uniformly over the finite collection of designs, arms, and folds,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\left\|\widehat M_{d,a}^{(-\ell)}-M_{d,a}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\left\|\mathbb P_{O,-\ell,a}[b_{q,d,a}Y]
             -M_{d,a}\beta_{h,d,a}\right\|\\
 &\qquad
 +\left\|\mathbb P_{E,a}[b_{h,d,a}]
             -M_{d,a}^\top\beta_{q,d,a}\right\|
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                         \tag{13}
\end{aligned}
\]
The complement of the count event has probability tending to zero, so (13) also holds unconditionally.  The identical argument in the pilot gives (13) with \(B\) replaced by \(m\).

Weyl's singular-value inequality and (13) imply that all the empirical matrices in (4) are nonsingular with probability tending to one and that their inverse operator norms are at most \(2/\underline\lambda\) on an event whose probability tends to one.  On that event the resolvent identity
\[
 \widehat M^{-1}-M^{-1}
 =-M^{-1}(\widehat M-M)\widehat M^{-1}                   \tag{14}
\]
and (11)--(13) imply
\[
 \max_{d,a,\ell}\left\{
 \left\|\widehat\beta_{h,d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\beta_{h,d,a}\right\|
 +\left\|\widehat\beta_{q,d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\beta_{q,d,a}\right\|
 \right\}=O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                \tag{15}
\]
Assigning zero coefficients on the singularity event does not change this stochastic order because that event has probability tending to zero.

Every fitted-function error is a fixed basis vector multiplied by the corresponding coefficient error.  The bounded-basis condition and (15) consequently give both strong-norm bounds in (6), uniformly under the artificial two-source laws defining \(\|\cdot\|_{2,B}^{\circ}\).  Moreover,
\[
 T_d(\widehat h_d-h_d)
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[b_{h,d,a}(H_{d,a})^\top
 \mid L_{d,a},A=a,G=O\right]
 (\widehat\beta_{h,d,a}-\beta_{h,d,a}),                  \tag{16}
\]
and
\[
 T_d^\star(\widehat q_d-q_d)
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[b_{q,d,a}(L_{d,a})^\top
 \mid H_{d,a},A=a,G=O\right]
 (\widehat\beta_{q,d,a}-\beta_{q,d,a}).                 \tag{17}
\]
Conditional Jensen's inequality, or directly boundedness of the conditional basis means, gives the two weak-norm bounds in (6).  Because there are only finitely many arms, designs, and folds, these bounds hold for the maximum displayed there.  Choosing each of \(\delta_{h,B}\), \(\rho_{h,B}\), \(\delta_{q,B}\), and \(\rho_{q,B}\) proportional to \(B^{-1/2}\) then proves (7).

We now prove the multiplier bound.  Equations (11)--(12), bounded bases, \(\|M_{d,a}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\underline\lambda^{-1}\), and the moment bound in (3) show that the finitely many population coefficient vectors are bounded.  Thus \(h_d\) and \(q_d\) are bounded, while \(Y-h_d\) has a finite \((4+\delta)\)-moment.  Bounded bases and (15) also give
\[
 \|\Delta h_{B,\ell}\|_\infty=O_P(B^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \|\Delta q_{B,\ell}\|_\infty=O_P(B^{-1/2}).            \tag{18}
\]
It follows from H\"older's inequality that, uniformly over \(d\) and \(\ell\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\mathbf 1\{G=O\}\Delta q_{B,\ell}(Y-h_d)\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 &=O_P(B^{-1/2}),\\
 \|\mathbf 1\{G=O\}q_d\Delta h_{B,\ell}\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 &=O_P(B^{-1/2}),\\
 \|\mathbf 1\{G=O\}\Delta q_{B,\ell}\Delta h_{B,\ell}\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 &=O_P(B^{-1}).                                           \tag{19}
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing the three terms proves (8).

It remains to establish (9)--(10).  Let \(\phi_{g,d}\) denote the relevant true source score and \(\widehat\phi_{g,d}\) its foldwise plug-in version.  Source-arm positivity keeps every population inverse arm probability bounded, and the empirical inverse arm probabilities are bounded with probability tending to one by condition \(\mathrm{(iv)}\).  The true experimental score is bounded.  The true observational score has a finite \((4+\delta)\)-moment because \(q_d\) and \(h_d\) are bounded and \(Y\) satisfies (3).  Therefore \(\phi_{g,d}^2\) has finite variance in each source.

Conditional on each fold's training data, the evaluation observations are independent of the fold-external fits.  Expanding the exact plug-in score around the true score, the arm-probability errors and empirical bridge-mean errors are \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\), while (15), (18), and (19) control the bridge terms.  Consequently, uniformly over the finite collection,
\[
 \|\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d}\|_{L_2(P(\cdot\mid G=g))}
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_g(\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d})^2=O_P(B^{-1}), \tag{20}
\]
where \(\mathbb P_g\) is the corresponding source empirical mean.  Chebyshev's inequality applied to \(\phi_{g,d}^2\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left|\mathbb P_g\widehat\phi_{g,d}^{\,2}
       -\mathbb E_P\!\left[\phi_{g,d}^2\mid G=g\right]\right|
 &\le
 \left|(\mathbb P_g-P_g)\phi_{g,d}^2\right|\\
 &\quad+
 \left\{\mathbb P_g(\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d})^2\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\mathbb P_g(\widehat\phi_{g,d}+\phi_{g,d})^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &=O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                         \tag{21}
\end{aligned}
\]
The same proof on the full-catalogue pilot, using the pilot version of (13)--(15), yields
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\left\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|
 \right\}=O_P(m^{-1/2}),                                 \tag{22}
\]
which is (9).  Uniformity in \(d\) in (21) permits evaluation at the pilot-measurable random choice \(\widehat d_m\), and hence
\[
 \max_{g\in\{E,O\}}
 \left|\widetilde V_{g,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{g,\widehat d_m}\right|
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}),                                          \tag{23}
\]
which is (10).  Equations (6)--(10) now follow from (15)--(23), proving every claimed root-sample-size rate on the stated fixed-dimensional regime.
