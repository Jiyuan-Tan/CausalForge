Let \(\mathcal F_m=\sigma(\mathcal P_m)\).  Pilot independence makes \(\widehat d_m\), \(\widehat\alpha_m\), \(N_{E,B}\), and \(N_{O,B}\) measurable with respect to \(\mathcal F_m\) and independent of the main-study observations.  The pilot-oracle theorem gives
\[
 \widehat\alpha_m-\alpha_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).
\]
Finiteness of \(\mathcal D\), positivity of the costs, and nondegeneracy of the variance components therefore give an \(a_0>0\) such that
\[
 \Pr\{a_0\le\widehat\alpha_m\le1-a_0\}\longrightarrow1. \tag{1}
\]
On this event \(N_{E,B}\asymp B\) and \(N_{O,B}\asymp B\).  Applying the uniform floor-count conclusion of the pilot-oracle theorem at \(d=\widehat d_m\) gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1),\qquad
 s_{0,B}^2:=B\left\{\frac{V_{E,\widehat d_m}}{N_{E,B}}
 +\frac{V_{O,\widehat d_m}}{N_{O,B}}\right\}. \tag{2}
\]

We next verify that pilot selection does not disturb the fixed-design expansion.  For a fold \(\ell\), write
\[
 e_{h,d,\ell}=\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d,
 \qquad e_{q,d,\ell}=\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d.
\]
The exact doubly robust score identity leaves, after subtracting the true-score averages, centered cross-fitted evaluation terms and a bilinear conditional-mean remainder.  For each treatment level, the absolute value of that remainder is bounded, using first \(T_d\) and then its adjoint, by a fixed multiple of
\[
 \min\left\{
 \|e_{h,d,\ell}\|_{w,h,d}\|e_{q,d,\ell}\|_{2,O,q,d},
 \|e_{h,d,\ell}\|_{2,O,h,d}\|e_{q,d,\ell}\|_{w,q,d}
 \right\}. \tag{3}
\]
The four uniform nuisance-rate assumptions and the weak--strong product condition make (3) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the finite family and the fixed number of folds.  Cross-fitting centers the remaining empirical nuisance terms.  For a deterministic share \(\beta\in[a_0,1-a_0]\), let \(\widehat\tau_{d,B}(\beta)\) denote the fixed-design estimator with counts \(n_{E,B}(d,\beta)\) and \(n_{O,B}(d,\beta)\).  The fixed-system theorem in \(\mathrm{lem{:}ikmw\text{-}fixed\text{-}design\text{-}limit\), applied to each member of the finite family, consequently yields the simultaneous expansions
\[
 \widehat\tau_{d,B}(\beta)-\tau
 =\frac1{n_{E,B}(d,\beta)}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{E,B}(d,\beta)}\phi_{E,d}(O_{E,i})
 +\frac1{n_{O,B}(d,\beta)}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{O,B}(d,\beta)}\phi_{O,d}(O_{O,i})
 +o_P(B^{-1/2}) \tag{4}
\]
for allocations in \([a_0,1-a_0]\).  Simultaneity follows simply by taking the maximum of the finitely many fixed-design remainders.  Because the family is finite and the pilot is independent, (4) remains valid after substituting \(d=\widehat d_m\) and the pilot-measurable counts.

For completeness, the random-index limit can be checked without imposing a rate relation between \(m\) and \(B\).  Conditional on \(\mathcal F_m\), the selected design and counts are fixed, the two leading sums in (4) are independent, centered, and have variance \(s_{0,B}^2/B\).  For every fixed design, the fixed-system limit holds along every deterministic pair of integer sample sizes whose two budget shares stay in \([a_0,1-a_0]\).  If conditional convergence were not uniform over those integer pairs and the finite set of designs, a violating sequence would contain a subsequence with one fixed design and a convergent allocation share in \([a_0,1-a_0]\), contradicting the fixed-system theorem.  Hence, after integrating over \(\mathcal F_m\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1). \tag{5}
\]
Population ties cause no mixture problem because every conditional statistic in (5) is divided by its own selected-design standard deviation.

Finally, on the event in (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 &\le \frac B{N_{E,B}}|\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|
 +\frac B{N_{O,B}}|\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &=O_P(r_{\widetilde V,B})=o_P(1).
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
Equations (2) and (6) prove
\(\widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1)\).  Nondegeneracy and positive costs bound every \(\mathcal V_d^*(P)\) away from zero, so \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  Combining (5) and (6) yields
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B(\widehat\tau_B-\tau)}{\widehat s_B}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
If \(z_{1-\eta/2}\) is the corresponding standard-normal quantile, convergence at the two continuity points \(\pm z_{1-\eta/2}\) shows that
\[
 \left[\widehat\tau_B-z_{1-\eta/2}\frac{\widehat s_B}{\sqrt B},
       \widehat\tau_B+z_{1-\eta/2}\frac{\widehat s_B}{\sqrt B}\right]
\]
has coverage tending to \(1-\eta\).
