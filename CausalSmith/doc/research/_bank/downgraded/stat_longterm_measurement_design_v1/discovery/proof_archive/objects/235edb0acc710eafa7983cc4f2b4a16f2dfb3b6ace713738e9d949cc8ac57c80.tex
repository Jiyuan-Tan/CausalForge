Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and write
\[
 v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P),
 \qquad
 \widehat v_d=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*,
 \qquad
 v_*=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}v_d.
\]
Choose any \(d^*\in D^*(P)\).  The definition of \(\varepsilon_m\) and empirical optimality of \(\widehat d_m\) give
\[
 v_{\widehat d_m}
 \le\widehat v_{\widehat d_m}+\varepsilon_m
 \le\widehat v_{d^*}+\varepsilon_m
 \le v_{d^*}+2\varepsilon_m
 =v_*+2\varepsilon_m.                                     \tag{1}
\]
Since \(v_*\le v_{\widehat d_m}\),
\[
 0\le v_{\widehat d_m}-v_*\le2\varepsilon_m.             \tag{2}
\]
If \(\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\), the definition of \(\gamma(P)\) and (2) imply \(\gamma(P)\le2\varepsilon_m\), proving the event inclusion.  When \(D^*(P)=\mathcal D\), the left event is empty and the right event is empty under the convention \(\gamma(P)=+\infty\).  Otherwise, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) gives \(\gamma(P)>0\).

Set
\[
 Z_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}.
\]
The pilot conditions yield \(Z_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1)\).  By the pilot construction and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\),
\[
 \widehat v_d=G_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d}),
 \qquad
 v_d=G_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d}),
\]
where
\[
 G_d(x,y)=(\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y})^2.
\]
Nondegeneracy, positive costs, and finiteness put the population variance pairs in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)^2\); with probability tending to one, the estimated pairs lie in a slightly larger such compact set.  The gradients of the finitely many \(G_d\) are uniformly bounded there.  Hence the mean-value theorem gives
\[
 \varepsilon_m=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat v_d-v_d|
 =O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).                                   \tag{3}
\]
Together with the positive fixed margin when suboptimal designs exist, (3) and the event inclusion prove
\[
 P\{\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\}\longrightarrow0.
\]

For allocation, let \(\nu_0=\min_d\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0\).  On
\[
 \{Z_m<\nu_0/2\}\cap\{\underline v_m<\nu_0/2\},
\]
whose probability tends to one, every truncation is inactive.  Applying the same compact-set mean-value argument to
\[
 A_d(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}
\]
gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=O_P(r_{V,m}).       \tag{4}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}second\text{-}order\text{-}allocation\text{-}loss}\), applied to (4) and the declared total convention, now yields
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|B\left\{
\frac{V_{E,d}}{\lfloor\widehat\alpha_{d,m}B/c_{E,d}\rfloor}
+\frac{V_{O,d}}{\lfloor(1-\widehat\alpha_{d,m})B/c_{O,d}\rfloor}
\right\}-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\right|
=O_P(r_{V,m}^2+B^{-1}).                                  \tag{5}
\]
Since \(r_{V,m}=o(1)\) and \(B\to\infty\), (5) is \(o_P(1)\).  The deterministic tie rule was used only to select an empirical minimizer in (1); the conclusion is therefore consistency for the complete population optimizer set, not for an arbitrarily singled-out member.
