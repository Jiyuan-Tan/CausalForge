Measurement design is an argument of the long-term-effect efficiency problem, not a harmless preprocessing choice. A full-catalogue pilot can rank the finite admissible designs by their profiled bridge-gradient value and learn the optimal budget allocation; under the stated nuisance conditions, the resulting allocation loss is second order. In contrast, for every prescribed finite cost-disadvantage factor \(\chi>1\), there is a relatively open regular binary family on which the uniquely prediction-selected proxy design costs \(\chi\) times as much at the center laws yet has an efficiency ratio diverging like a positive \(\chi\)-dependent multiple of \(\epsilon^{-2}\). The arm-normalized value concerns the identified bridge contrast on the full class; the IKM-normalized value has the absolute causal-efficiency interpretation only where common identification and the fixed-system premises hold.
