Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(B>0\), and abbreviate
\[
 p_d=\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}(P;B),
 \qquad
 v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P).
\]
Define the prediction-order relation and its order-restricted efficiency constant by
\[
 \mathcal O_P
 =\{(d,d')\in\mathcal D^2:p_d\le p_{d'}\},
 \qquad
 C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)
 =\max_{(d,d')\in\mathcal O_P}\frac{v_d}{v_{d'}}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Then \(1\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty\), and, for every \(C\ge0\),
\[
 \bigl[p_d\le p_{d'}\Longrightarrow v_d\le C v_{d'}
 \text{ for all }d,d'\in\mathcal D\bigr]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C\ge C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P).
 \tag{2}
\]
The smallest constant for the actual best-tie paired-source diagnostic rule is instead
\[
 C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)
 =\frac{\min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}v_d}
 {\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}v_{d'}}
 =\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P),
 \qquad
 1\le C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty.
 \tag{3}
\]
Consequently, for any nonempty subclass \(\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), a finite constant uniform over \(\mathcal Q\) exists for the best-tie rule if and only if
\(\sup_{P\in\mathcal Q}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)<\infty\); for all pairwise prediction-order implications it exists if and only if
\(\sup_{P\in\mathcal Q}C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty\).  Thus (1), rather than an operator norm, is the weakest exact lawwise comparison for selection by this newly defined diagnostic.  The design-specific operators \(T_d\) enter (1) through the bridges and hence through \(v_d\), but no further comparison of the operators is necessary for lawwise finiteness.

More specifically, consider any numerical realization of the handle \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{comp}}\) with the following properties.  For each \(d\), it supplies a real vector space \(\mathcal H_d\), a cone \(\mathcal X_d(P)\subseteq\mathcal H_d\setminus\{0\}\) containing a transported law-specific direction \(x_d(P)\), a positive prediction quadratic form \(Q_{R,d}\) on that cone, and two nonnegative source-gradient quadratic forms \(Q_{E,d}\) and \(Q_{O,d}\), with
\[
 Bp_d=Q_{R,d}\{x_d(P)\},
 \qquad
 v_d=F_d\{x_d(P)\},
 \tag{4}
\]
where
\[
 F_d(x)
 =\left\{
 \sqrt{c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)}
 +\sqrt{c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)}
 \right\}^2.
 \tag{5}
\]
Set
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)
 =\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\inf_{x\in\mathcal X_d(P)}
 \frac{F_d(x)}{Q_{R,d}(x)},
 \qquad
 \Lambda_+(P)
 =\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X_d(P)}
 \frac{F_d(x)}{Q_{R,d}(x)}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The weakest two-sided quadratic-form envelope on the chosen cones is precisely
\[
 0<\Lambda_-(P)\le\Lambda_+(P)<\infty,
 \tag{7}
\]
and it implies the valid, generally conservative certificate
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)
 \le\frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}.
 \tag{8}
\]

The sharp constant in (2) or (3) is not, in general, a largest generalized eigenvalue.  Indeed,
\[
 F_d(x)=\inf_{0<\alpha<1}
 \left\{
 \frac{c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)}{\alpha}
 +\frac{c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)}{1-\alpha}
 \right\},
 \tag{9}
\]
so the profiled numerator is generally not a quadratic form.  If \(\mathcal H_d\) is finite dimensional, \(Q_{R,d}\) is positive definite, and \(\mathcal X_d(P)=\mathcal H_d\setminus\{0\}\), a largest generalized eigenvalue at a fixed \(\alpha\) gives only a one-sided full-space envelope.  Selection requires both the upper and lower envelopes in (6), and equality in (8) additionally requires saturation by law-realized, diagnostic-ordered directions.  Hence equality with a largest generalized eigenvalue can hold only after adding a common-space representation, a normalization of the positive lower envelope, and the required saturation; none follows from the lawwise operator bijectivity or from the diagnostic ordering alone.
