Fix \(P\) as in the proposed statement.  The proof has four parts.

\emph{1. Interior source sizes and arm counts.}
For every \(d\in\mathcal D\), positivity of the costs and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) imply \(0<\alpha_d^*(P)<1\).  Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le \alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D).                                 \tag{1}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\), explicitly conditional on the maintained pilot variance condition, gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1).             \tag{2}
\]
Thus, with probability tending to one, \(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\), and both purchased source counts are bounded below by a positive constant times \(B\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}source\text{-}arm\text{-}positivity}\) gives \(p_{E,a},p_{O,a}>0\).  Conditional on the pilot, each source arm count is binomial with a fixed positive success probability, and every balanced observational fold has size diverging at rate \(B\).  Chebyshev's inequality and a union bound over the finitely many arms and folds therefore give
\[
 P(\mathsf D_B)\longrightarrow1.                          \tag{3}
\]

\emph{2. A deterministic-path asymptotic expansion.}
Consider a deterministic sequence \((d_B,\alpha_B)\) satisfying the allocation requirement in the maintained selected-path assumptions.  Write \(e_B,o_B\) for its source sizes and abbreviate \(h=h_{d_B}\), \(q=q_{d_B}\).  By (1), the path condition, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\),
\[
 e_B\asymp B,
 \qquad
 o_B\asymp B.                                             \tag{4}
\]

We first record an elementary arm-ratio expansion.  If \(W_1,\ldots,W_N\) are independent observations from one source, \(p_a=P(A=a)>0\), \(N_a=\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}\), and \(f\) is square integrable, then direct subtraction of the conditional mean and \(N_a/N=p_a+O_P(N^{-1/2})\) give
\[
 \frac{1}{N_a}\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}f(W_i)
 -\mathbb E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}
 =\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{p_a}
 \bigl[f(W_i)-\mathbb E\{f(W)\mid A=a\}\bigr]
 +o_P(N^{-1/2}).                                          \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the centered average on the right is \(O_P(N^{-1/2})\), while \(Np_a/N_a-1=O_P(N^{-1/2})\); their product is \(O_P(N^{-1})\).  Conditional on a fold-external random function \(f_N\), the same variance calculation shows that the empirical-minus-population term is \(o_P(N^{-1/2})\) whenever \(\|f_N\|_2=o_P(1)\).  Balanced-fold averaging changes the coefficient \(1/o_B\) by only \(O(B^{-2})\) per observation and therefore has an \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) effect.  These conclusions hold simultaneously over the fixed number of folds.

The denominator term must also be retained when the fold-trained nuisance function has nonzero arm mean.  If \(g_N\) is such a function, then on the positive-count event the exact difference between replacing \(\widehat p_a^{-1}\) by \(p_a^{-1}\) in its population contribution is bounded, before multiplication by \(\sqrt N\), by
\[
 C|\widehat p_a-p_a|\,|P\{\mathbf1(A=a)g_N\}|
 \le C|\widehat p_a-p_a|\,\|g_N\|_2
 =O_P(N^{-1/2}\|g_N\|_2).                                \tag{5a}
\]
For the experimental term \(g_N=\Delta h_\ell\), this is \(O_P(B^{-1/2}\rho_{h,B})=o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  For the observational terms \(q\Delta h_\ell\) and \(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\), it is respectively
\[
 O_P\!\left(B^{-1/2}\|\mathbf1\{G=O\}q\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}\right)
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 O_P\!\left(B^{-1/2}\|\mathbf1\{G=O\}\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}\right), \tag{5b}
\]
both \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) under maintained Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}postselection\text{-}multiplier\text{-}condition}\).  The \(\Delta q_\ell(Y-h)\) arm mean is zero by the outcome-bridge equation, so its denominator product is of the smaller centered-product order.  Thus no nonzero-mean denominator remainder is omitted.

For fold \(\ell\), write
\[
 \Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-h,
 \qquad
 \Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-q.
\]
The outcome bridge equation and the selection-bridge change-of-measure identity imply, for every square-integrable function \(u\) of the early bridge arguments and every square-integrable function \(f\) of the late bridge arguments,
\[
 \mathbb E_O\{u(S_{2d_B},S_{1d_B},a,X)(Y-h)\mid A=a\}=0, \tag{6}
\]
\[
 \mathbb E_E\{f(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}
 =\mathbb E_O\{qf(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}.      \tag{7}
\]
Consequently, conditional on the training data, the nuisance drift in the estimator of the arm mean is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)
 +\mathbb E_O\{(q+\Delta q_\ell)(Y-h-\Delta h_\ell)\mid A=a\}\\
 &\hspace{35mm}=-\mathbb E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a). \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]
The terms linear in \(\Delta h_\ell\) cancel by (7), and both residual terms linear in \(q\) or \(\Delta q_\ell\) vanish by (6).

Since treatment probabilities are positive, conditional and unconditional source norms differ only by constants depending on the fixed law.  Iterated expectation first through \(T_{d_B}\), and then through its adjoint, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathbb E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B}
       \|T_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,q,d_B},\\
 |\mathbb E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 &\le C\|T_{d_B}^{\star}\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}
       \|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,O,h,d_B}.
                                                               \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]
The source probabilities under \(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) are bounded away from zero by (4), so its \(L_2\) norm is uniformly equivalent to the two source-conditional \(L_2\) norms.  The four maintained selected-path weak and strong rate conditions and the maintained product-rate condition, together with their declared vanishing rates, make (8) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed folds and treatment arms.

It remains to control centered nuisance terms.  The experimental perturbation is \(\Delta h_\ell\), whose conditional empirical fluctuation is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) by (5), the maintained strong-norm rate, its vanishing rate, and (4).  The observational perturbation is
\[
 \Delta q_\ell(Y-h)-q\Delta h_\ell-\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell. \tag{10}
\]
Maintained Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}postselection\text{-}multiplier\text{-}condition}\) makes its \(L_2(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm \(o_P(1)\).  Conditional Chebyshev bounds on each evaluation fold, followed by the finite-fold union bound, make its centered empirical contribution \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  The stipulated training scheme supplies the conditional independence used in these variance bounds: the experimental term uses outcome bridges trained only on observational data, and each observational evaluation fold is excluded from the training data for both bridges.

Apply (5) to the remaining oracle terms and use balanced-fold equivalence.  This proves
\[
 \widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)
 =\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
  +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
  +o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                         \tag{11}
\]
This expansion is centered at the observed bridge contrast and does not assert equality with \(\tau\).

The two leading sums are independent and centered, with variances \(V_{E,d_B}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d_B}/o_B\).  They obey a central limit theorem under the finite second moments in Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\).  To see this when \(d_B\) varies, take any subsequence and then a further subsequence on which \(d_B\) is constant, possible because \(\mathcal D\) is finite.  For a centered square-integrable variable \(Q\), dominated convergence applied to
\[
 \frac{e^{iuQ}-1-iuQ}{u^2}
\]
as \(u\to0\) gives \(\mathbb E(e^{iuQ})=1-u^2\mathbb E(Q^2)/2+o(u^2)\).  Applying this expansion to the characteristic functions of the two independent sample averages gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B}{s_{0,B}(d_B,\alpha_B)}
 \left\{
 \frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 \right\}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1),                        \tag{12}
\]
where
\[
 s_{0,B}^2(d_B,\alpha_B)
 =B\left(\frac{V_{E,d_B}}{e_B}+\frac{V_{O,d_B}}{o_B}\right).
\]
Every subsequence has a further constant-design subsequence with the same limit, so (12) holds for the original sequence.  Nondegeneracy and (4) keep \(s_{0,B}\) bounded above and away from zero.  Equations (11)--(12) prove the deterministic-path studentized limit.

\emph{3. Transfer to the pilot-selected path and variance consistency.}
By (2), choose a deterministic \(t_B\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\left\{
 |\widehat\alpha_m-\alpha_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)|>t_B
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.                              \tag{13}
\]
The deterministic-path conclusions above are uniform over
\[
 \{(d,\alpha):d\in\mathcal D,
 |\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)|\le t_B\}.                         \tag{14}
\]
Indeed, failure of uniformity would select a deterministic violating pair from (14) for infinitely many \(B\), contradicting the hypotheses and conclusions just proved along every deterministic path.  Independence of the pilot and main study permits conditioning on the realized pair \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\).  Equations (11)--(14) give
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)
 -\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                        \tag{15}
\]

The pilot allocation result, evaluated at the selected design, gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).          \tag{16}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\), direct subtraction of the estimated and oracle variance formulas gives
\[
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 \le \frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|
 +\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.  \tag{17}
\]
The coefficients are \(O_P(1)\) by (1)--(2).  The maintained selected-design main-study variance condition and its declared vanishing rate make the maximum variance error \(o_P(1)\) under the stipulated joint pilot--main-study law.  Combining (3), (16), and (17) proves
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1).    \tag{18}
\]
The finite family and nondegeneracy imply \(\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\mathcal V_d^*(P)>0\).  Hence (3), (16), and (18) show that \(P(\widehat s_B^2>0)\to1\), proving
\[
 P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\longrightarrow1             \tag{19}
\]
and \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  Equations (15) and (19), together with this ratio convergence and Slutsky's theorem, prove
\[
 \mathsf T_B\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1),             \tag{20}
\]
including the declared zero extension off \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\).

\emph{4. Coverage.}
On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the event \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\) is exactly \(|\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\).  The complement of \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\) has vanishing probability by (19).  Since the standard normal distribution is continuous at both critical points, (20) gives
\[
 P\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}\longrightarrow1-\eta.
\]
This establishes every conclusion for the selected observable bridge contrast under the eight explicitly maintained estimator-level conditions and the separately declared vanishing-rate conditions.  None is derived from the observable law class or from the cited fixed-design theory.
