Fix \(\chi>1\).  Along the triangular center sequence, every observational cell of \((A,X,Z_{M_d})\) has probability bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(\chi\), uniformly in \(m\).  Indeed, the observational treatment probabilities conditional on \(U\) lie in \([3/8,5/8]\); the early signal lies in \((0,10^{-3})\); the late signal \(y_\chi\) is fixed and strictly below one; and all other signals equal \(1/2\).  The source probability is \(1/2\).  Hence every training and validation cell count is of order \(m\) with probability tending to one, uniformly over the two designs.  The zero-denominator convention is therefore invoked with probability exponentially small in \(m\).

Let
\[
 f_{d,m}^*(v)=\mathbb E_{P_{\chi,\epsilon_m}}(Y\mid A,X,Z_{M_d}=v,G=O).
\]
Because \(Y\in\{-1,1\}\), every saturated cell mean, including its zero extension, lies in \([-1,1]\).  Standard variance calculation for each finite cell, using its uniform positive probability, gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max_v
 |\widehat f_{d,m}^A(v)-f_{d,m}^*(v)|=O_P(m^{-1/2}).        \tag{37}
\]
The training and validation halves are independent.  Conditional on the training half, each validation squared loss is bounded by four.  The conditional variance of its empirical average is therefore \(O(m^{-1})\), uniformly over the finite design family, so
\[
 \max_d\left|\widehat R_{d,m}^A-
 \mathbb E\!\left[\{Y-\widehat f_{d,m}^A(A,X,Z_{M_d})\}^2
 \mid G=O,\mathcal I_{m,O}^{\mathrm{tr}}\right]\right|
 =O_P(m^{-1/2}).                                           \tag{38}
\]
Conditional-expectation orthogonality gives the exact excess-risk identity
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E\!\left[\{Y-\widehat f_{d,m}^A(A,X,Z_{M_d})\}^2\mid G=O,
 \mathcal I_{m,O}^{\mathrm{tr}}\right]-R_d^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})\\
 &\qquad=\mathbb E\!\left[
 \{\widehat f_{d,m}^A(A,X,Z_{M_d})-f_{d,m}^*(A,X,Z_{M_d})\}^2
 \mid G=O,\mathcal I_{m,O}^{\mathrm{tr}}\right]
 =O_P(m^{-1}),                                             \tag{39}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality follows from (37) and the finite number of cells.  Combining (38)--(39) proves (1).

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}treatment\text{-}aware\text{-}sharp\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) gives, uniformly along the sequence,
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})<\frac3{10},
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})=\frac{585}{1024}.
\]
Thus the population gap is bounded below by \(585/1024-3/10>0\).  Equation (1) makes the empirical maximal error \(o_P(1)\), so deterministic tie breaking selects \(d_{\mathrm p}\) with probability tending to one.  This proves (2).

On the selection event, each random ratio in (3) equals its deterministic \(d_{\mathrm p}\) center ratio.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) gives for both normalizations
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 {\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 =\frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p},\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 {\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e},\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 >\frac{\epsilon_m^{-2}+14}{726}\longrightarrow\infty.  \tag{40}
\]
Since the selection-event probability tends to one, (40) proves both divergences in probability.  The pilot is independent of the main study by Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}independent\text{-}pilot}\), so the rule is feasible before main-study measurement and no inverse bridge learner enters the selection calculation.
