Fix \(\chi>1\), \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), and write \(x=\epsilon\) and \(y=y_\chi\). The center construction has positive finite cells, consistency by construction, randomized treatment in source \(E\), latent exchangeability and overlap in source \(O\), source-invariant conditional proxy kernels, and conditional independence of the early and late proxies given \(U\). Hence external validity and the sequential-outcome restriction hold. For a binary proxy \(W_s\), the two conditional laws given \(U=1\) and \(U=-1\) differ whenever \(s\ne0\); the associated two-by-two conditional-expectation matrix therefore has nonzero determinant. Since \(x,y,1/2\ne0\) and every arm has positive mass, both completeness conditions and both designwise operator-bijectivity conditions hold. The displayed bridges solve the observed equations, and their finite scores have strictly positive variances. Thus \(P_{\chi,\epsilon}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

Because \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), \(\tau=0\). Completeness and the sequential restriction give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid U,A=a,G=O\}=U.
\]
External validity and experimental randomization then imply \(\mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E_P(U)=0\), so both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau\).

The residual-risk formula from the binary construction and \(y_\chi^2=1-3/(10\chi)\) give
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{3}{10\chi}\frac{1-\epsilon^2}
 {1-\epsilon^2(1-3/(10\chi))}. \tag{1}
\]
The denominator in the last fraction minus its numerator is \(3\epsilon^2/(10\chi)>0\), so the fraction is strictly below one. For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), direct substitution gives
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{(1-1/4)^2}{1-1/16}=\frac35. \tag{2}
\]
Since \(\chi>1\), (1)--(2) prove the displayed strict raw-risk chain and \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\).

For a design with early signal \(x\) and late signal \(y\), the outcome bridge is \(h(W_y,a)=W_y/y\). At the balanced experimental center,
\[
 \phi_{E,d}=2T\frac{W_y}{y},
 \qquad V_{E,d}=\frac4{y^2}. \tag{3}
\]
In source \(O\),
\[
 q_d(W_x,a)=\frac{16}{15}\left(1-\frac{TW_x}{4x}\right).
\]
Conditional on \(U\), the residual \(U-W_y/y\) has mean zero and variance \((1-y^2)/y^2\). Also \(\mathbb E_P(TW_x\mid G=O)=x/4\), whence direct expansion gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P(q_d^2\mid G=O)=\frac{16(1+14x^2)}{225x^2},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d}=\frac{64(1-y^2)(1+14x^2)}{225x^2y^2}. \tag{4}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), (3)--(4) yield
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{40\chi}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14). \tag{5}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), they yield \(V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16\) and \(V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=384/25\). Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot384/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}. \tag{6}
\]
The cost formulas give the two stated sourcewise gaps. Combining (5)--(6) with the profiled formula gives exactly equation (1) in the statement. At the centers, both treatment probabilities equal \(1/2\), so every IKM-normalized source moment is one quarter of its arm-normalized counterpart. Every profiled value is multiplied by one quarter and the ratio is unchanged.

Dropping the first nonnegative square-root term from the exact ratio gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 \ge \frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14)
 >\frac{\epsilon^{-2}+14}{726}, \tag{7}
\]
because \((5\chi+1)/(10\chi-3)>1/2\). Since \(\epsilon<10^{-3}\), the lower bound exceeds one; hence \(d_{\mathrm e}\) uniquely minimizes both profiles. This proves the ratio identities and inequalities.

For the sharp optimization, set
\[
 A(\chi)=\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 D(\chi)=\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)},
 \qquad C_0=\frac{25}{3872}.
\]
The exact ratio implies
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 =C_0\left[\epsilon^2A(\chi)+D(\chi)(1+14\epsilon^2)
 +2\epsilon\sqrt{1+14\epsilon^2}\sqrt{A(\chi)D(\chi)}\right], \tag{8}
\]
where
\[
 A(\chi)=10\chi+1+\frac3{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 D(\chi)=\frac{16}{75}+\frac{16}{15(10\chi-3)}. \tag{9}
\]
The objective is continuous on \((1,\infty)\) and diverges as \(\chi\to\infty\) for fixed \(\epsilon>0\). A candidate \(\chi=k\epsilon^{-2/3}\) has leading limit \(C_0(16/75)=1/726\), whereas every bounded sequence of \(\chi\)'s has a strictly larger leading constant \(C_0D(\chi)\). Hence, for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), the infimum is attained at an interior point and every minimizing sequence diverges.

Writing \(t=\epsilon^{2/3}\) and \(\chi=k/t\), (8)--(9) give, locally uniformly for \(k\in(0,\infty)\),
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{k/t,epsilon})
 =\frac1{726}+C_0t\left\{2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac8{75k}\right\}+o(t). \tag{10}
\]
The rescaled minimizers stay in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\): as \(k\downarrow0\), the positive excess from \(D(k/t)\), divided by \(t\), diverges like \(8/(75k)\), while as \(k\to\infty\), the cross term divided by \(t\) diverges like \(2\sqrt{32k/15}\). Let
\[
 q(k)=2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac8{75k}.
\]
Then
\[
 q'(k)=\sqrt{\frac{32}{15}}k^{-1/2}-\frac8{75}k^{-2},
\]
which changes sign once, at \(k_\star=\sqrt[3]{18}/15\). Local uniformity and compactness yield \(\chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)\sim k_\star\epsilon^{-2/3}\). Finally,
\[
 C_0q(k_\star)=\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968},
\]
which proves the sharp second-order expansion. The common one-quarter IKM normalization proves the same expansion for \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\).
