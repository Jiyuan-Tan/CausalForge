Put \(r=1/4\).  First fix a design whose early and late proxy signals are \(x,y>0\).  Conditional independence of the two proxies given \(U\), followed by Bayes' rule under the observational treatment law, gives the determinant of the binary conditional-expectation operator
\[
 \det(T_d)=\frac{xy(1-r^2)}{1-r^2x^2}>0. \tag{1}
\]
The channel matrix from \(U\) to \(W_x\) is
\[
 \frac12\begin{pmatrix}1+x&1-x\\1-x&1+x\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is \(x\); the analogous late-proxy matrix has determinant \(y\).  Conditioning further on either treatment value multiplies rows and columns by strictly positive Bayes factors and therefore preserves rank.  Hence both completeness implications hold.  Direct conditional expectation also verifies the two bridge equations with
\[
 h_d(l,a)=\frac{l}{y},
 \qquad
 q_d(e,a)=\frac{1-r(2a-1)e/x}{1-r^2}. \tag{2}
\]
For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), these functions are bounded.  Every primitive cell probability is positive.  Thus overlap and source support hold; consistency, latent exchangeability, experimental randomization, external validity, and the sequential restriction follow from the construction; and finite support gives every required fourth moment.  Equation (1) gives bijectivity, (2) gives bridge existence, and the positive variances computed below give nondegeneracy.  Consequently \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

The witness also identifies the causal target, rather than merely a bridge functional.  Indeed, \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), so \(\tau=0\).  In the experimental source, randomization and symmetry give \(\mathbb E_P[W_y\mid A=a,G=E]=y\mathbb E_P[U]=0\).  Therefore (2) yields \(\mu_{d,a}=0\) for both treatment values, and hence \(\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=\tau\) for each design.  Together with the unique bridges and (1), the fixed-system efficiency lemma applies; thus the profiled values below are the causal efficiency values on this witness subclass.

For the prediction criterion, symmetry implies that \(\mathbb E_P[U\mid W_x,W_y]\) lies in the linear span of \(W_x,W_y\).  Since
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(W_x,W_y)
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&xy\\xy&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}\bigl(U,(W_x,W_y)\bigr)=(x,y),
\]
the conditional residual variance is
\[
 R(x,y)=1-(x,y)
 \begin{pmatrix}1&xy\\xy&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}
 \binom{x}{y}
 =\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}. \tag{3}
\]
Thus
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac35. \tag{4}
\]
The corrected catalogue costs give paired-source costs \(C_{\mathrm p}=6\) and \(C_{\mathrm e}=5\).  Hence
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{42(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2}
 <3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}, \tag{5}
\]
because the strict inequality is equivalent to \(42(1-\epsilon^2)<48-27\epsilon^2\), or \(0<6+15\epsilon^2\).  Therefore \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is the unique predictive optimizer although \(C_{\mathrm p}>C_{\mathrm e}\).

Since \(p_{g,a}=1/2\), direct summation of the squared source scores using (2) gives
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2},
 \qquad
 V_O(x,y)=\frac4{(1-r^2)^2}\frac{1-y^2}{y^2}
 \left(1-2r^2+\frac{r^2}{x^2}\right). \tag{6}
\]
For the prediction-selected design this becomes
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{64}{9},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025}. \tag{7}
\]
For the efficiency-selected design,
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25},
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\left(4\sqrt2+\frac{24\sqrt2}{5}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}. \tag{8}
\]
The budget-profile theorem and (7) imply
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge \frac72 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 \ge \frac{1568}{2025\epsilon^2}
 >\frac{3872}{25}
\]
whenever \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\).  Since the catalogue contains only these two designs, (8) shows \(D^*(P_\epsilon)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}\).  Expanding the profiled square gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{1568}{2025\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}),
\]
and division by (8) yields
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{9}
\]

It remains to pass from the path to the relative open region.  The binary-witness stability lemma supplies, for each fixed \(\epsilon\), a radius \(r_\epsilon>0\) on which regularity, both strict optimizer assignments, and the lower bound \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)>\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)/2\) persist.  The definition of \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) chooses \(0<\delta_\epsilon<r_\epsilon\), so every law in that region is regular and has \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\) and \(D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}\).  Each center \(P_\epsilon\) itself belongs to its open ball.  Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) in (9) therefore proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\infty. \tag{10}
\]

Finally vary the cap-feasible cost vector while keeping the signal law fixed, and write \(C_{mathrm p}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}\) and \(C_{mathrm e}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}\).  By (4), the predictive ordering is exactly
\[
 \frac{C_{mathrm p}}{C_{mathrm e}}
 <\frac{R_{d_{\mathrm e}}}{R_{d_{\mathrm p}}}
 =\frac{3(16-9\epsilon^2)}{35(1-\epsilon^2)}. \tag{11}
\]
The right side tends to \(48/35\).  Hence every fixed cost vector satisfying
\[
 1<\frac{C_{mathrm p}}{C_{mathrm e}}<\frac{48}{35}
\]
makes \(d_{\mathrm p}\) strictly more expensive and uniquely prediction-selected for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\).  For every such positive cost vector, \(\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*\) remains bounded while \(\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*\ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}\to\infty\) by (7).  Thus \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is uniquely efficiency-selected and the ratio diverges.  Because the two cost-ratio inequalities are strict and the ratio is continuous in the positive costs, this set is relatively open within the cap-feasible cost region.  This proves every assertion.
