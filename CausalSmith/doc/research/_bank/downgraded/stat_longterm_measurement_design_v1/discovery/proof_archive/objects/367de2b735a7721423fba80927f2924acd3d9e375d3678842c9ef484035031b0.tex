Fix \(\chi>1\) and write \(P_{\chi,\epsilon}=P_\epsilon\). Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) proves center-law regularity and common identification, the paired costs \(5\chi\) and \(5\), the strict sourcewise cost disadvantage of \(d_{\mathrm p}\), and
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{3}{10\chi}\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2(1-3/(10\chi))}
 <\frac{3}{10\chi}<\frac3{10}<\frac35
 =R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon}). \tag{1}

Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is uniquely selected by raw population prediction risk, whereas \(d_{\mathrm e}\) uniquely minimizes the arm-normalized source-score profile. Multiplying (1) by the paired costs gives

 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}<\frac32<3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B), \tag{2}

which is the asserted secondary calculation. The same lemma gives

 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}+O_\chi(1). \tag{3}

At every center, treatment is balanced in both sources. Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}ikmw\text{-}normalized\text{-}stability}\) therefore gives
\[
 V_{E,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=\frac14V_{E,d},
 \qquad V_{O,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=\frac14V_{O,d},
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac14\mathcal V_d^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon}). \tag{4}

It follows that \(d_{\mathrm e}\) also uniquely minimizes the IKM-normalized profile and that the common factor cancels from the ratios. The sharp-elbow lemma supplies

 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}\epsilon^{-2}+O_\chi(\epsilon^{-1}), \tag{5}

as well as

 \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)
 \sim\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15}\epsilon^{-2/3} \tag{6}

and

\begin{aligned}
 \epsilon^2\inf_{\chi>1}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 &=\epsilon^2\inf_{\chi>1}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})\\
 &=\frac1{726}+\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}\epsilon^{2/3}+o(\epsilon^{2/3}). \tag{7}
\end{aligned}

Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}region}\) chooses each ball inside the radius from Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}witness\text{-}stability}\). Hence every member of \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) is regular, both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau\), and

 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
 \qquad D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}. \tag{8}

Each center belongs to its corresponding ball. Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) in (5), with \(\chi\) fixed, proves

 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\infty. \tag{9}

Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}ikmw\text{-}region}\) further shrinks the balls inside the normalized-stability radii without removing their centers.  \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}}
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P)=\infty. \tag{10}
\]
Under its separately stated premises, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}ikmw\text{-}fixed\text{-}system\text{-}efficiency}\) supplies the absolute causal-efficiency interpretation of the normalized profile.

It remains to verify the cost-manifold clause. The center primitive cost vector and cap are
\[
 \left(1,2,\frac{5\chi-3}{4},\frac{5\chi-3}{4},\frac12,\frac12\right),
 \qquad \kappa=\frac{5\chi-3}{2}. \tag{11}
\]
All entries are positive, \(b_E<b_O\), the measurement cost of \(d_{\mathrm p}\) equals the cap, and that of \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is one. Hence (11) belongs to \(\mathcal C_\kappa\), and its paired-cost ratio is exactly \(\chi\). At \(\epsilon=0\), the continuous extensions of the raw risks are \(3/(10\chi)\) and \(3/5\), so their gap is strict. The raw-risk gap, the two sourcewise cost gaps, and the cap inequalities are continuous in the relevant relative product topology. A relatively open cost neighborhood, together with a sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\), therefore preserves strict cost disadvantage and unique raw-risk selection. The secondary diagnostic gap in (2) is also strict and continuous, so it persists after a further shrinking.

On that neighborhood, \(c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}\) stays bounded below and both costs of \(d_{\mathrm e}\) stay bounded above. By (3),

 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}\asymp_\chi\epsilon^{-2},
\]
whereas \(\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*\) stays finite and positive. The arm-normalized ratio diverges. The balanced-center factorization in (4) holds for every positive primitive cost vector, so the IKM-normalized ratio diverges as well. Equations (1)--(11) establish every formula, optimizer, neighborhood, and divergence in the theorem.
