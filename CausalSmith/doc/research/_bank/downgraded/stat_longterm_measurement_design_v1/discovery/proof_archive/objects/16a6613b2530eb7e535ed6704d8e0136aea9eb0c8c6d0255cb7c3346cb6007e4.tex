The argument is uniform because the design set, the treatment set, all basis dimensions, and the fold count are finite and fixed.

Fix \((d,a)\), and let \(n\) denote the relevant training-sample size.  Bounded bases, the \((4+\delta)\)-moment bound for \(Y\), source-arm positivity, and the proportional cell-size condition imply, entry by entry,
\[
 \widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a}=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat g^h_{d,a}-g^h_{d,a}=O_P(n^{-1/2}),                  \tag{1}
\]
and the same bounds hold for \(\widehat M^q_{d,a}-M^q_{d,a}\) and \(\widehat g^q_{d,a}-g^q_{d,a}\).  For example, each coordinate has finite variance, so Chebyshev's inequality gives the displayed order; conditioning on a source-arm cell preserves it because its size is proportional to \(n\).  A finite union bound makes (1) simultaneous over all designs, arms, and folds.

If \(\|\widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a}\|_{\mathrm{op}}<c_0/2\), then for every vector \(x\),
\[
 \|\widehat M^h_{d,a}x\|
 \ge \|M^h_{d,a}x\|
      -\|(\widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a})x\|
 \ge \frac{c_0}{2}\|x\|.
\]
Thus \(\widehat M^h_{d,a}\) is nonsingular and its inverse has operator norm at most \(2/c_0\).  The identical argument applies to \(\widehat M^q_{d,a}\).  Equation (1) shows that these events hold simultaneously with probability tending to one.  On them, the exact population moment equations give
\[
 \widehat\beta_{d,a}-\beta_{d,a}
 =(\widehat M^h_{d,a})^{-1}
 \left\{(\widehat g^h_{d,a}-g^h_{d,a})
 -(\widehat M^h_{d,a}-M^h_{d,a})\beta_{d,a}\right\},        \tag{2}
\]
with the analogous identity for \(\widehat\gamma_{d,a}-\gamma_{d,a}\).  The population coefficient vectors are uniformly bounded: the inverses of the population matrices have norm at most \(c_0^{-1}\), the outcome right-hand sides are bounded by the moment condition, and the selection right-hand sides are bounded by the basis envelope.  Hence (1)--(2) imply
\[
 \max_{d,a,\ell}\left\{
 \|\widehat\beta_{d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\beta_{d,a}\|
 +\|\widehat\gamma_{d,a}^{(-\ell)}-\gamma_{d,a}\|
 \right\}=O_P(n^{-1/2}).                                    \tag{3}
\]
The stipulated zero convention is irrelevant because the joint singularity event has probability tending to zero.

The Gram eigenvalue bounds translate coefficient errors into strong \(L_2\) errors.  Bounded bases also give the corresponding pointwise envelopes.  Conditional expectation is an \(L_2\) contraction, so applying \(T_d\) and \(T_d^\star\) cannot increase the relevant source-conditional \(L_2\) error.  Along the proportional-size paths, the artificial-law source masses are bounded away from zero, and its \(L_2\) norm is therefore uniformly equivalent to the two source-conditional norms.  Taking \(n\asymp B\) in (3) proves (5)--(6) of the theorem.  In particular, choosing all four deterministic bridge-rate envelopes proportional to \(B^{-1/2}\) gives
\[
 \delta_{h,B}\rho_{q,B}=O(B^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \rho_{h,B}\delta_{q,B}=O(B^{-1})=o(B^{-1/2}).               \tag{4}
\]

It remains to verify the multiplier and variance conditions.  Bounded bridge bases and (3) imply, uniformly in \(d,a,\ell\),
\[
 |\Delta h_{B,\ell}|\le C\|\widehat\beta-\beta\|,
 \qquad
 |\Delta q_{B,\ell}|\le C\|\widehat\gamma-\gamma\|.       \tag{5}
\]
The population selection bridge is bounded because its basis and coefficient are bounded, while the outcome moment assumption makes \(Y-h_d\) square-integrable.  Equations (3) and (5) therefore give
\[
 \|\mathbf1\{G=O\}\Delta q_{B,\ell}(Y-h_d)\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 +\|\mathbf1\{G=O\}q_d\Delta h_{B,\ell}\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}),                                               \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 \|\mathbf1\{G=O\}\Delta q_{B,\ell}\Delta h_{B,\ell}\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
 =O_P(B^{-1}).                                                 \tag{7}
\]
Thus the multiplier expression is \(O_P(B^{-1/2})=o_P(1)\), uniformly over the finite index set.

For either source, let \(\phi_{g,d}\) be the population score and \(\widehat\phi_{g,d}\) its fold-external plug-in version.  Empirical arm probabilities are root-\(n\) consistent and bounded away from zero with probability tending to one.  On this event the score is a locally Lipschitz function of the finite coefficient vectors and arm probabilities.  Equations (3), (5), and the \((4+\delta)\)-moment bound imply
\[
 \|\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d}\|_2=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\phi_{g,d}+\phi_{g,d}\|_2=O_P(1),               \tag{8}
\]
and \(E_P(\phi_{g,d}^4\mid G=g)<\infty\).  Conditional on the training data for an evaluation fold,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathbb P_n\widehat\phi_{g,d}^2-P\phi_{g,d}^2|
 \le{}&| (\mathbb P_n-P)\phi_{g,d}^2|\\
 &+\mathbb P_n\left[
 |\widehat\phi_{g,d}-\phi_{g,d}|
 |\widehat\phi_{g,d}+\phi_{g,d}|
 \right].
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{9}
\]
The first term is \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\) by Chebyshev's inequality applied to \(\phi_{g,d}^2\).  Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, (8), and the law of large numbers make the second term \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Fixed-fold averaging and a finite union bound preserve the rate.  Taking \(n\asymp m\) in the full-catalogue pilot and \(n\asymp B\) in the main study proves
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\bigl\{
 |\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\bigr\}=O_P(m^{-1/2})             \tag{10}
\]
and
\[
 \max_{g\in\{E,O\}}
 |\widetilde V_{g,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{g,\widehat d_m}|
 =O_P(B^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{11}
\]

Equations (3)--(11) verify on this fixed-dimensional subclass every learner-rate, product-rate, multiplier, pilot-variance, and main-study-variance condition named in Theorems~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) and \(\mathrm{thm{:}postselection\text{-}wald}\), with the rates asserted in the theorem.  Applying those theorems gives the claimed oracle-regret, selected-design normal limit, and Wald coverage.  The proof uses the additional finite-dimensional conditions stated here and therefore makes no claim over the whole observable bridge-law class.
