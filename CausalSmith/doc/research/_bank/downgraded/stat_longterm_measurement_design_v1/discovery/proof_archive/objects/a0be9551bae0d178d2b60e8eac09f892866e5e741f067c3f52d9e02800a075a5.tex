Fix \(\chi>1\), \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), and write \(x=\epsilon\) and \(y=y_\chi\).  The center construction has positive finite cells, consistency by construction, randomized treatment in source \(E\), latent exchangeability and overlap in source \(O\), source-invariant conditional proxy kernels, and conditional independence of the early and late proxies given \(U\).  Thus external validity and the sequential-outcome restriction hold.  For a binary proxy \(W_s\), the two conditional distributions given \(U=1\) and \(U=-1\) are distinct whenever \(s\ne0\); the associated two-by-two conditional-expectation matrix has nonzero determinant.  Since \(x,y,1/2\) are nonzero and every arm has positive mass, both completeness conditions and both designwise operator-bijectivity conditions hold.  The displayed bridges in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}witness}\) solve the observed equations, and all scores have finite, strictly positive variance.  Hence \(P_{\chi,\epsilon}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).  Because \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), \(\tau=0\).  Moreover, completeness and the sequential restriction imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\mid U,A=a,G=O\}=U.
\]
External validity and experimental randomization then give \(\mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E_P(U)=0\), so both designwise bridge contrasts equal \(\tau\).

For a design whose early and late proxy signals are \(x\) and \(y\), the outcome bridge is \(h(W_y,a)=W_y/y\).  At the balanced experimental center, \(p_{E,a}=1/2\) and \(\mu_{d,a}=0\), whence
\[
 \phi_{E,d}=2T\frac{W_y}{y},
 \qquad V_{E,d}=\frac4{y^2}. \tag{1}
\]
In source \(O\), the selection bridge is
\[
 q_d(W_x,a)=\frac{16}{15}\left(1-\frac{TW_x}{4x}\right).
\]
Conditional on \(U\), the residual \(U-W_y/y\) has mean zero and variance \((1-y^2)/y^2\).  Also
\[
 \mathbb E_P(TW_x\mid G=O)=\frac{x}{4},
\]
so
\[
 \mathbb E_P(q_d^2\mid G=O)
 =\frac{16(1+14x^2)}{225x^2}.
\]
Since \(p_{O,a}=1/2\), it follows that
\[
 V_{O,d}=\frac{64(1-y^2)(1+14x^2)}{225x^2y^2}. \tag{2}
\]

For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), \(x=\epsilon\) and \(y^2=(10\chi-3)/(10\chi)\).  Equations (1)--(2) yield
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{40\chi}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14). \tag{3}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm e}\), \(x=y=1/2\), so \(V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16\), \(V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=384/25\), and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 =\left(\sqrt{2\cdot16}+\sqrt{3\cdot384/25}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}. \tag{4}
\]
The cost formulas give
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =\frac{5(\chi-1)}2>0. \tag{5}
\]

The residual-risk formula in the binary definition gives
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,epsilon};B)
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac32,
 \qquad
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_{\chi,epsilon};B)=3. \tag{6}
\]
Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is uniquely diagnostic-selected.  Combining (3)--(4) with the profiled-value formula yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 =\frac{25}{3872}\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3}}
 +\sqrt{\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}
 (\epsilon^{-2}+14)}\right\}^2. \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
At the centers, both treatment probabilities are \(1/2\), so the IKM-normalized source moments are one quarter of the arm-normalized moments.  Every profiled value is therefore multiplied by one quarter, and the ratio in (7) is unchanged.

Dropping the first nonnegative term inside the square in (7) gives
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 \ge\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14)
 >\frac{\epsilon^{-2}+14}{726}, \tag{8}
\]
because \((5\chi+1)/(10\chi-3)>1/2\).  In particular the ratio exceeds one, so \(d_{\mathrm e}\) uniquely minimizes the arm-normalized and IKM-normalized profiles.  Taking infima proves (2)--(3) of the statement.

It remains to optimize (7).  Define
\[
 A(\chi)=\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 D(\chi)=\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)},
 \qquad C_0=\frac{25}{3872}.
\]
Then
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,epsilon})
 =C_0\left[\epsilon^2A(\chi)
 +D(\chi)(1+14\epsilon^2)
 +2\epsilon\sqrt{1+14\epsilon^2}\sqrt{A(\chi)D(\chi)}\right]. \tag{9}
\]
The exact identities
\[
 A(\chi)=10\chi+1+\frac3{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 D(\chi)=\frac{16}{75}+\frac{16}{15(10\chi-3)} \tag{10}
\]
will be useful.  For fixed \(\epsilon\), the objective is continuous on \((1,\infty)\) and tends to infinity as \(\chi\to\infty\).  A candidate \(\chi=k\epsilon^{-2/3}\) has limiting leading constant \(C_0(16/75)=1/726\), whereas every bounded \(\chi\) has the strictly larger leading constant \(C_0D(\chi)\).  Therefore, for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), the infimum is attained at an interior point and every minimizer diverges.

Put \(t=\epsilon^{2/3}\) and \(\chi=k/t\).  Uniformly for \(k\) in compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\), (9)--(10) give
\[
 \epsilon^2\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{k/t,\epsilon})
 =\frac1{726}+C_0t\left\{
 2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac8{75k}\right\}+o(t). \tag{11}
\]
The rescaled minimizers remain in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\): if \(k\downarrow0\), the positive excess \(\{D(k/t)-16/75\}/t\) diverges like \(8/(75k)\), whereas if \(k\to\infty\), the cross term in (9), divided by \(t\), diverges like \(2\sqrt{32k/15}\).  Comparison with any fixed positive \(k\) rules out both cases.  Hence the locally uniform expansion (11) applies to every minimizing sequence.

Let
\[
 q(k)=2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac8{75k}.
\]
Differentiation gives
\[
 q'(k)=\sqrt{\frac{32}{15}}k^{-1/2}-\frac8{75}k^{-2}.
\]
Thus \(q'(k)=0\) exactly when
\[
 k=k_\star=\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15},
\]
and the derivative changes from negative to positive there.  The compactness argument and uniqueness of this minimizer imply
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)\sim k_\star\epsilon^{-2/3}. \tag{12}
\]
Substituting \(k_\star\) into (11) and simplifying gives
\[
 C_0q(k_\star)=\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}.
\]
Equations (11)--(12) prove the sharp expansion (6), and the common one-quarter normalization proves it simultaneously for \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\).
