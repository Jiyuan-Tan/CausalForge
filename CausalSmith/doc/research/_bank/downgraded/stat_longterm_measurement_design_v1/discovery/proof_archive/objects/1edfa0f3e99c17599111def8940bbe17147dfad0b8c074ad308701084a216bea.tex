Fix center signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\), and write \(T=2A-1\).  Under the observational center law, for \(t,r,s,u\in\{-1,1\}\), the joint mass of \((T,W_x,W_y,U)=(t,r,s,u)\), apart from the common normalizing constant, is
\[
 (1+tu/4)(1+xur)(1+yus).                                  \tag{30}
\]
Summing (30) over \(u\) and summing after multiplication by \(u\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E(U\mid T=t,W_x=r,W_y=s)
 =\frac{t/4+xr+ys+txy\,rs/4}
 {1+txr/4+tys/4+xy\,rs}.                                  \tag{31}
\]
Because \(U^2=1\), the conditional squared-error risk is one minus the square of (31).  Weighting this conditional variance by the eight cell probabilities obtained from (30), putting the terms over a common denominator, and collecting the even powers of \(r,s,t\) yields
\[
 \mathcal R_A(x,y)=
 \frac{15(1-x^2)(1-y^2)(16-x^2-y^2-14x^2y^2)}
 {\{16(1+xy)^2-(x+y)^2\}\{16(1-xy)^2-(x-y)^2\}}.         \tag{32}
\]
Every denominator in (31)--(32) is positive because it is a positive finite-cell probability and \(0<x,y<1\).

Substituting \(x=y=1/2\) in (32) gives
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=\frac{585}{1024}.
\]
For every design, conditioning on \(A\) in addition to the predictors can only decrease mean squared prediction error, so
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \le R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon}).                \tag{33}
\]
The sharp-elbow lemma gives the remaining strict chain in (2).  Therefore both generic selectors uniquely choose \(d_{\mathrm p}\).  Their best-tie numerator sets are identical, so their arm-normalized ratios agree; the same set identity gives equality of the IKM-normalized ratios.  The exact formula, lower bound, minimizing scale, and second-order expansion now follow directly from Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\).
