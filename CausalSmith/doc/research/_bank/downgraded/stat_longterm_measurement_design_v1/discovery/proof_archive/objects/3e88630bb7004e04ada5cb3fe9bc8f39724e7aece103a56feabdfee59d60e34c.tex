Put
\[
 v_-:=\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\min\{V_{E,d},V_{O,d}\}>0,
 \qquad
 Z_m:=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\{|\widehat V_{E,d}-V_{E,d}|,
 |\widehat V_{O,d}-V_{O,d}|\}.
\]
For \(x,y>0\), define
\[
 F_d(x,y)=\left(\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}\right)^2.
\]
The budget-profile theorem gives \(F_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d})=\mathcal V_d^*(P)\), and the pilot construction gives \(F_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d})=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*\).  The gradients of the finitely many maps \(F_d\) are uniformly bounded on a fixed positive rectangle around the population variance pairs.  Hence, on an event whose probability tends to one,
\[
 \varepsilon_m\le C Z_m,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_m=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1). \tag{1}
\]

Choose \(d^*\in D^*(P)\).  Empirical optimality gives pathwise
\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\le \mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)-\mathcal V_{d^*}^*(P)\\
&\le |\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)-\widehat{\mathcal V}_{\widehat d_m,m}^*|
 +\{\widehat{\mathcal V}_{\widehat d_m,m}^*-\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d^*,m}^*\}
 +|\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d^*,m}^*-\mathcal V_{d^*}^*(P)|\\
&\le2\varepsilon_m.
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
\]
If \(D^*(P)\ne\mathcal D\), every misselection incurs loss at least \(\gamma(P)\), so (2) yields
\[
 \{\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\}\subseteq\{2\varepsilon_m\ge\gamma(P)\}.
\]
The asserted probability convergence follows from (1).  If every design is optimal, both events are empty under the convention \(\gamma(P)=+\infty\).

For the allocation map
\[
 A_d(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
 {\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}},
\]
the inequality \(|(x\vee\underline v_m)-V|\le|x-V|+\underline v_m\) and a finite-family mean-value argument give
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
 =O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m). \tag{3}
\]
The second-order allocation-loss lemma sharpens the resulting budget-normalized profile error to
\[
 O_P\!\left((r_{V,m}+\underline v_m)^2+B^{-1}\right). \tag{4}
\]
Because \(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m=o(1)\), (4) implies the theorem's stated, weaker
\(O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m+B^{-1})=o_P(1)\) bound.  The deterministic tie rule affects only which empirical minimizer is implemented; (2) concerns membership in the full population optimizer set.
