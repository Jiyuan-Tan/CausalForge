Write all quantities at \((\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)\) unless a deterministic pair is displayed. The pilot-oracle-regret theorem and condition~(9) of the proposed statement give
\[
\max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1). \tag{18}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and all costs and source variances are positive and finite, there is \(a_0\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0,\qquad d\in\mathcal D. \tag{19}
\]
Thus, with probability tending to one, \(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\), and both selected source counts are bounded below by a positive multiple of \(B\). The totalized conclusion of the pilot-oracle-regret theorem, evaluated at the random member \(\widehat d_m\) of the finite family, therefore gives
\[
s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1). \tag{20}
\]

We next establish the arm-normalized expansion directly. Fix deterministic sequences \(d_B\in\mathcal D\) and \(\alpha_B\) satisfying condition~(1). From every subsequence, finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) permits extraction of a further subsequence on which \(d_B=d\) is constant. Along it,
\[
\frac{e_B}{B}\longrightarrow\frac{\alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{E,d}},
\qquad
\frac{o_B}{B}\longrightarrow\frac{1-\alpha_d^*(P)}{c_{O,d}},
\qquad n_B\asymp B. \tag{21}
\]
Consequently the two source probabilities of \(P_{B,d,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) are bounded away from zero.

For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), abbreviate
\[
r_{E,a}=h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)-\mu_{d,a},
\quad
r_{O,a}=q_d(S_{2d},S_{1d},a,X)
\{Y-h_d(S_{3d},S_{2d},a,X)\}. \tag{22}
\]
The definition of \(\mu_{d,a}\) gives
\(\mathbb E(r_{E,a}\mid A=a,G=E)=0\), and the observed outcome-bridge equation gives
\(\mathbb E(r_{O,a}\mid A=a,G=O)=0\).

We use the elementary random-denominator identity. If \((I_i,R_i)_{i=1}^k\) are i.i.d., \(I_i\) is Bernoulli with mean \(p\in(0,1)\), \(\mathbb E(I_iR_i)=0\), and \(\mathbb E(I_iR_i^2)<\infty\), then, on \(\sum_iI_i>0\),
\[
\frac{\sum_iI_iR_i}{\sum_iI_i}
-\frac1k\sum_i\frac{I_iR_i}{p}
=\frac{(p-\overline I_k)\overline{IR}_k}{p\overline I_k}
=O_P(k^{-1}). \tag{23}
\]
This follows because both centered sample means are \(O_P(k^{-1/2})\), while \(\overline I_k\) is bounded away from zero with probability tending to one. Applying (23) to the oracle experimental arm mean and separately in each of the fixed \(L\) observational folds yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)-\mu_{d,a}
&=\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}
\frac{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{p_{E,a}}r_{E,a}(O_i)\\
&\quad+\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}
\frac{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{p_{O,a}}r_{O,a}(O_i)
+o_P(B^{-1/2}),
\end{aligned} \tag{24}
\]
where \(\widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)\) denotes the oracle version of (6). Replacing fold weights by \(o_B^{-1}\) costs \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) because folds are balanced and \(L\) is fixed.

For fold \(\ell\), write \(\Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d\) and \(\Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d\). After replacing empirical arm proportions by their population values using (23), the conditional population nuisance contribution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=E)\\
&\quad+\mathbb E\{\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_d)-q_d\Delta h_\ell
-\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O\}. 
\end{aligned} \tag{25}
\]
The selection-bridge equation and change of measure imply
\[
\mathbb E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=E)
=\mathbb E(q_d\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O), \tag{26}
\]
while measurability of \(\Delta q_\ell\) with respect to \((S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X)\) and the observed outcome-bridge equation imply
\[
\mathbb E\{\Delta q_\ell(Y-h_d)\mid A=a,G=O\}=0. \tag{27}
\]
Thus (25) is \(-\mathbb E(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)\). Iterating in either direction and applying Cauchy--Schwarz gives, for a finite arm-probability constant \(C_a\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathbb E(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a,G=O)|
\le C_a\min\{&
\|T_d^\star\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
\|\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ},\\
&\|\Delta q_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}
\|T_d\Delta h_\ell\|_{2,B}^{\circ}\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{28}
\]
Conditions~(2)--(4) make (28) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the fixed folds.

Conditional on the training observations, every relevant evaluation observation is independent of its fitted nuisance functions. Conditional Chebyshev inequality bounds a centered empirical nuisance average by its \(L_2(P_{B,d,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm divided by the square root of its source or fold size. Condition~(2) controls the experimental \(\Delta h_\ell\) term, and condition~(5) controls the three observational multiplier terms. Their norms are \(o_P(1)\) and every evaluation size is of order \(B\), so all centered nuisance averages are \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\). The nuisance arm averages themselves are \(o_P(1)\); multiplying by the \(O_P(B^{-1/2})\) arm-proportion error in (23) remains \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\). Combining these bounds with (25)--(28) proves
\[
\widehat\mu_{B,d}(a)-\widetilde\mu_{B,d}(a)
=o_P(B^{-1/2}). \tag{29}
\]

Subtracting (24) and (29) at \(a=0\) from those at \(a=1\) yields the fixed-path expansion
\[
\widehat\tau_B(d,\alpha_B)-\theta_d(P)
=\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d}(O_i)
+\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d}(O_i)
+o_P(B^{-1/2}). \tag{30}
\]
This is the direct arm-normalized expansion; the differently normalized published IKM moments are not used.

The two leading sums in (30) are independent, centered, and have variances \(V_{E,d}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d}/o_B\). If \(\lambda_d=\lim e_B/o_B\), then
\[
n_B\left(\frac{V_{E,d}}{e_B}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{o_B}\right)
\longrightarrow
\frac{1+\lambda_d}{\lambda_d}V_{E,d}
+(1+\lambda_d)V_{O,d}. \tag{31}
\]
For completeness, finite second moments suffice for the central-limit step. If \(R\) is centered with variance \(\sigma^2<\infty\), dominated convergence applied to the second-order expansion of \(e^{itR/\sqrt{k}}\) gives
\[
\mathbb E e^{itR/\sqrt{k}}
=1-\frac{t^2\sigma^2}{2k}+o(k^{-1}).
\]
Raising this expression to the \(k\)-th power gives the Gaussian characteristic function. Applying this calculation separately to the two independent source sums, using their deterministic weights from (21), and then using (30)--(31), gives
\[
\frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)\}}
{\left[B\{V_{E,d_B}/e_B+V_{O,d_B}/o_B\}\right]^{1/2}}
\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1) \tag{32}
\]
along every deterministic path satisfying condition~(1).

We lift (32) through the independent pilot. From (18), choose deterministic \(b_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
P\!\left\{\max_d|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|>b_m\right\}\to0. \tag{33}
\]
Conditional on a pilot realization in the complementary event, the main-study design and allocation form a deterministic path covered by (32). This convergence is uniform over such realizations: otherwise a violating sequence of pilot realizations would, after extracting a constant-design subsequence, contradict (32). Pilot independence permits integration of conditional bounded-Lipschitz distances, while the exceptional pilot event has vanishing probability. Therefore
\[
\frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1) \tag{34}
\]
under the stipulated extensions.

It remains to establish totality and variance consistency. On the high-probability interior event from (18)--(19), both source counts are at least \(cB\). Experimental overlap gives \(p_{E,a}>0\), and observational latent overlap gives \(p_{O,a}>0\) after integration over \((U,X)\). Conditional on the pilot, an experimental arm is empty with probability at most \((1-p_{E,a})^{cB}\); an observational fold-arm is empty with probability at most \((1-p_{O,a})^{cB/L-O(1)}\). A finite union bound proves
\[
P(\mathsf D_B)\longrightarrow1. \tag{35}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
&\le\frac{B}{N_{E,B}}
|\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
&\quad+\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}
|\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|.
\end{aligned} \tag{36}
\]
The count ratios are \(O_P(1)\), and condition~(10) makes the uniform variance error \(o_P(1)\). Thus (20) and (36) imply
\[
\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2=o_P(1),\qquad
\widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1). \tag{37}
\]
Nondegeneracy, positive costs, and finiteness give
\[
0<\min_d\mathcal V_d^*(P)\le\max_d\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty. \tag{38}
\]
Equations (35)--(38) show \(P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\to1\) and \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\) on that event. To justify denominator replacement directly, let \(Z_B=\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}/s_{0,B}\) and \(R_B=\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\) on \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), using the stipulated extensions elsewhere. Equation~(34) makes \((Z_B)\) tight, while \(R_B\to_P1\). For any bounded Lipschitz \(f\), on \(\{|Z_B|\le M,\ |R_B-1|\le\delta<1/2\}\),
\[
|f(Z_B/R_B)-f(Z_B)|\le2M\delta\,\operatorname{Lip}(f).
\]
First choose \(M\) to control the tightness tail, then pass to the limit, and finally let \(\delta\downarrow0\). Boundedness of \(f\) controls the complement. Hence \(Z_B/R_B\) and \(Z_B\) have the same weak limit. Together with \(P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}^c)\to0\), this proves \(\mathsf T_B\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\).

On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\),
\[
\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}
=\{|\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\}.
\]
The symmetric difference after total extension is contained in \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}^c\). Continuity of the standard normal law at the critical values yields coverage \(1-\eta\). Finally, if \(\theta_d(P)=\tau\) for all \(d\), then \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)=\tau\) for every pilot realization, so substitution proves the causal-centered limit and coverage.
