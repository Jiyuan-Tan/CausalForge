Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).  Suppose a primitive operator comparison transports the catalogue to nonzero directions \(z_d\) in a finite-dimensional real vector space and produces positive-definite quadratic forms \(Q_0,Q_1\) satisfying
\[
 Q_0(z_d)=B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}(P;B),\qquad
 Q_1(z_d)=\mathcal V_d^*(P),\qquad d\in\mathcal D.
\]
Let \(0<\lambda_-\le\lambda_+<\infty\) be the smallest and largest generalized eigenvalues of \((Q_1,Q_0)\).  Then every predictive optimizer has approximation ratio at most the generalized condition number \(\lambda_+/\lambda_-\).  The sharp catalogue-restricted pairwise constant is
\[
 C_{\mathcal D}(P)=
 \max_{\substack{d,d'\in\mathcal D:\,
 Q_0(z_d)\le Q_0(z_{d'})}}
 \frac{Q_1(z_d)}{Q_1(z_{d'})}
 \le\frac{\lambda_+}{\lambda_-}.
\]
Equality with the ambient condition number requires catalogue-restricted attainment: a compared pair must attain the upper and lower generalized eigenvalues and have equal \(Q_0\)-norm.  Thus a largest-eigenvalue certificate alone is invalid without a lower comparison and normalization.  Under the frozen maintained conditions no finite certificate of this type is uniform over \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\); the unequal-cost predictive-impossibility theorem forces every valid uniform condition number to diverge.  The finite enumerated maximum above is therefore a law-specific diagnostic, not a primitive guarantee supplied by finiteness of \(\mathcal D\).
