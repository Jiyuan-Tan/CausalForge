Fix center signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\) and write \(T=2A-1\). Under the observational center law, the joint mass of \((T,W_x,W_y,U)=(t,r,s,u)\), apart from its common positive normalizing factor, is
\[
 (1+tu/4)(1+xur)(1+yus).                                  \tag{36}
\]
Summing (36) over \(u\), first without and then with multiplication by \(u\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E(U\mid T=t,W_x=r,W_y=s)
 =\frac{t/4+xr+ys+txy\,rs/4}
 {1+txr/4+tys/4+xy\,rs}.                                  \tag{37}
\]
Every denominator in (37) is positive because all underlying cells are strictly positive. Since \(U^2=1\), the optimal squared-error risk is the expectation of one minus the square of (37). Substitution of the eight cell probabilities, grouping the terms paired by \((t,r,s)\mapsto(-t,-r,-s)\), and collecting even powers yields
\[
 \mathcal R_A(x,y)=
 \frac{15(1-x^2)(1-y^2)(16-x^2-y^2-14x^2y^2)}
 {\{16(1+xy)^2-(x+y)^2\}\{16(1-xy)^2-(x-y)^2\}}.         \tag{38}
\]
This is a finite algebraic evaluation of the conditional-variance functional defining \(R_d^A(P)\).

Substitution of \(x=y=1/2\) gives
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=\frac{585}{1024}.
\]
Adding \(A\) to a squared-error predictor cannot increase its projection risk. Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) therefore gives
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \le R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 <\frac{3}{10\chi}<\frac{3}{10}<\frac{585}{1024}.         \tag{39}
\]
Thus both generic selector sets are the singleton \(\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\). Under either profile their ratio numerators are consequently identical, proving both equalities in (3). The exact ratio, lower bound, minimizing scale, and sharp expansion then transfer directly from the sharp-elbow lemma.
