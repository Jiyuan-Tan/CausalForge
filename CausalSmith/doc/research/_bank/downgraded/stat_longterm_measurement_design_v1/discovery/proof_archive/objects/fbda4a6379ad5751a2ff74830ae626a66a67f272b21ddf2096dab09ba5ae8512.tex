Write \(W_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P)\), \(\widehat W_d=\widehat{\mathcal V}_{d,m}^*\), and \(W_{\min}=\min_dW_d\).  Choose \(d^*\in D^*(P)\).  Empirical optimality gives \(\widehat W_{\widehat d_m}\le\widehat W_{d^*}\), whence
\[
0\le W_{\widehat d_m}-W_{\min}
\le |W_{\widehat d_m}-\widehat W_{\widehat d_m}|
+|\widehat W_{d^*}-W_{d^*}|
\le2\varepsilon_m.                                                  \tag{1}
\]
If \(\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\), the definition of \(\gamma(P)\) makes the left side of (1) at least \(\gamma(P)\).  Thus
\[
\{\widehat d_m\notin D^*(P)\}\subseteq
\{2\varepsilon_m\ge\gamma(P)\}.                                  \tag{2}
\]
When every design is optimal, the event on the left is empty and the convention \(\gamma(P)=+\infty\) makes the event on the right empty.  Otherwise \(\gamma(P)>0\), since \(\mathcal D\) is finite.

For each \(d\), let
\[
f_d(x,y)=\bigl(\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}\bigr)^2.
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), positivity of costs, and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) give a compact rectangle contained in \((0,\infty)^2\) which contains all population pairs and, with probability tending to one, all estimated pairs.  All first derivatives of the finitely many \(f_d\)'s are bounded there.  The mean-value theorem and the pilot variance rate yield
\[
\varepsilon_m
=\max_d|f_d(\widehat V_{E,d},\widehat V_{O,d})
-f_d(V_{E,d},V_{O,d})|=O_P(r_{V,m})=o_P(1).                         \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) prove selection consistency.

The identical compact-neighborhood argument applied to
\[
g_d(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}}
{\sqrt{c_{E,d}x}+\sqrt{c_{O,d}y}}
\]
and to the inputs truncated at \(\underline v_m\) gives
\[
\max_d|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|
=O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m).                                      \tag{4}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}second\text{-}order\text{-}allocation\text{-}loss}\) gives, under exactly the theorem's zero-count convention, the stronger uniform bound
\[
O_P\!\left((r_{V,m}+\underline v_m)^2+B^{-1}\right).
\]
Since \(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m=o(1)\), this is
\(O_P(r_{V,m}+\underline v_m+B^{-1})=o_P(1)\), completing all assertions.
