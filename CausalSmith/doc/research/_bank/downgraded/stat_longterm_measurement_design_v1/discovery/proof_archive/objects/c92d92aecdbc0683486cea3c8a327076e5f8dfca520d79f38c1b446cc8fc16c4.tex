Fix \(P\) as in the statement.  Because \(\mathcal D\) is finite and every profiled share is interior, there is \(a_0>0\) such that
\[
 2a_0\le\alpha_d^*(P)\le1-2a_0\qquad(d\in\mathcal D). \tag{1}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}pilot\text{-}oracle\text{-}regret}\) gives
\[
 \max_d|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=o_P(1). \tag{2}
\]
Thus, with probability tending to one, \(\widehat\alpha_m\in[a_0,1-a_0]\), and both purchased source counts are of order \(B\).  Conditional on the pilot, source-arm positivity and the conditional i.i.d. sampling assumption imply by Chebyshev's inequality and a finite union bound that every source, arm, and balanced fold-arm count is positive with probability tending to one.  Hence
\[
 P(\mathsf D_B)\to1. \tag{3}
\]

Consider first a deterministic path \((d_B,\alpha_B)\) with \(|\alpha_B-\alpha_{d_B}^*(P)|\to0\), and write its source counts as \(e_B,o_B\).  Then \(e_B\asymp B\) and \(o_B\asymp B\).  For a source sample of size \(N\), arm probability \(p_a>0\), and square-integrable \(f\), the exact ratio expansion is
\[
 \frac1{N_a}\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}f_i-E(f\mid A=a)
 =\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{p_a}
 \{f_i-E(f\mid A=a)\}+o_P(N^{-1/2}). \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, \(N_a/N-p_a=O_P(N^{-1/2})\), the centered average is \(O_P(N^{-1/2})\), and their product is \(O_P(N^{-1})\).  Conditional on fold-external training data, the same calculation applies to random functions.  The possible nonzero arm means created by nuisance errors are also harmless: replacing an empirical inverse arm probability by its population value produces a term bounded by a constant times \(N^{-1/2}\) times the relevant \(L_2\) norm.  The outcome strong-rate condition controls the experimental term, while the three observational terms are controlled by the foldwise multiplier condition.  All are \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).

For fold \(\ell\), abbreviate \(h=h_{d_B}\), \(q=q_{d_B}\), \(\Delta h_\ell=\widehat h_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-h\), and \(\Delta q_\ell=\widehat q_{d_B,B}^{(-\ell)}-q\).  The outcome bridge and selection-bridge change of measure give
\[
 E_O\{u(S_{2d_B},S_{1d_B},a,X)(Y-h)\mid A=a\}=0 \tag{5}
\]
for every square-integrable \(u\), and
\[
 E_E\{f(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}
 =E_O\{qf(S_{3d_B},S_{2d_B},a,X)\mid A=a\}. \tag{6}
\]
Consequently the conditional nuisance drift in an arm mean is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &E_E(\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)
 +E_O\{(q+\Delta q_\ell)(Y-h-\Delta h_\ell)\mid A=a\}\\
 &\hspace{35mm}=-E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a). \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
Iterated expectation through \(T_{d_B}\), or through its adjoint, and Cauchy--Schwarz give the two bounds
\[
 |E_O(\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell\mid A=a)|
 \le C\min\{\|\Delta q_\ell\|_2\|T_{d_B}\Delta h_\ell\|_2,
 \|T_{d_B}^\star\Delta q_\ell\|_2\|\Delta h_\ell\|_2\}. \tag{8}
\]
The source masses in \(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ}\) are bounded away from zero, so its norm is uniformly equivalent to the two source-conditional norms.  The selected-path weak, strong, and product-rate assumptions make (8) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).

The centered experimental nuisance fluctuation is \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\) by conditional Chebyshev and the strong outcome rate.  The centered observational nuisance perturbation is
\[
 \Delta q_\ell(Y-h)-q\Delta h_\ell-\Delta q_\ell\Delta h_\ell.
\]
Its \(L_2(P_{B,d_B,\alpha_B}^{\circ})\) norm is \(o_P(1)\) by the multiplier condition, so conditional Chebyshev and a finite-fold union bound make its empirical contribution \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\).  Cross-fitting supplies the required independence from each evaluation fold.  Combining these facts with (4) yields the asymptotic linear expansion
\[
 \widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)
 =\frac1{e_B}\sum_{i\in E_B}\phi_{E,d_B,i}
 +\frac1{o_B}\sum_{i\in O_B}\phi_{O,d_B,i}
 +o_P(B^{-1/2}). \tag{9}
\]

The leading sums are independent, centered, and have variances \(V_{E,d_B}/e_B\) and \(V_{O,d_B}/o_B\).  Finite second moments suffice for the central limit theorem.  When \(d_B\) varies, every subsequence has a further constant-design subsequence because \(\mathcal D\) is finite, and the same standard-normal limit holds on each such subsequence.  Therefore
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(d_B,\alpha_B)-\theta_{d_B}(P)\}}
 {s_{0,B}(d_B,\alpha_B)}\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1). \tag{10}
\]
Nondegeneracy and interior allocation keep the oracle standard error bounded above and away from zero.

To transfer (9)--(10) to the pilot-selected pair, choose deterministic \(t_B\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\{|\widehat\alpha_m-\alpha_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)|>t_B\}\to0. \tag{11}
\]
The deterministic-path conclusions are uniform over the finite designs and shares satisfying \(|\alpha-\alpha_d^*(P)|\le t_B\): otherwise one could select a deterministic violating sequence, contradicting the just-proved path result.  Pilot independence permits conditioning on the realized selected pair.  Hence
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B(\widehat d_m,\widehat\alpha_m)
 -\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}{s_{0,B}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1). \tag{12}
\]

The second-order allocation result gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1). \tag{13}
\]
On \(\mathsf D_B\), direct subtraction yields
\[
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 \le\frac{B}{N_{E,B}}|\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|
 +\frac{B}{N_{O,B}}|\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|. \tag{14}
\]
The coefficients are \(O_P(1)\), and the selected-design variance condition makes the maximum error \(o_P(1)\).  Equations (3), (13)--(14) give
\[
 \widehat s_B^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1). \tag{15}
\]
The finite-family minimum of the profiled values is strictly positive.  Thus (3), (13), and (15) imply \(P(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}})\to1\) and \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  Slutsky's theorem applied to (12) proves \(\mathsf T_B\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\), including the zero extension off \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\).

On \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{tot}}\), coverage is equivalent to \(|\mathsf T_B|\le z_{1-\eta/2}\).  The complement of that event has vanishing probability, and the standard normal law is continuous at both critical points.  Hence
\[
 P\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}\to1-\eta.
\]
The proof is centered throughout at the selected observable bridge contrast; the separate ATE corollary supplies the causal translation on \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{id}}\).
