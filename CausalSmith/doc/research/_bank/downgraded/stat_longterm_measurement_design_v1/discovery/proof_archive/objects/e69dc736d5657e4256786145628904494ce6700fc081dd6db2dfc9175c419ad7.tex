Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(d\in\mathcal D\), and write
\[
 H_d=(S_{3d},S_{2d},A,X),\qquad L_d=(S_{2d},S_{1d},A,X).
\]
A regular two-source parametric submodel \(\{P_t:|t|<t_0\}\) through \(P=P_0\), with source-conditional scores \(\ell_E\) and \(\ell_O\), is called \emph{bridge-differentiable for design \(d\) at \(P\)} when the following conditions hold.  First, the source-conditional laws are dominated on a common support and the path is differentiable in quadratic mean in each source.  Second, for all sufficiently small \(|t|\), \(P_t\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and its design-\(d\) outcome bridge \(h_{d,t}\) is unique.  Third, after identifying the common \(L_2\) coordinate spaces along the dominated path, there is \(\dot h_d\in L_2(H_d\mid G=O)\) such that
\[
 \left\|\frac{h_{d,t}-h_{d,0}}{t}-\dot h_d\right\|_{L_2(P(\cdot\mid G=O))}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and the conditional bridge equation
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_t}\{Y-h_{d,t}(H_d)\mid L_d,G=O\}=0
\]
may be differentiated at \(t=0\) in \(L_2(L_d\mid G=O)\) using the conditional-score rule.  This is a restriction on submodels, not a consequence of pointwise bijectivity of \(T_d\).
