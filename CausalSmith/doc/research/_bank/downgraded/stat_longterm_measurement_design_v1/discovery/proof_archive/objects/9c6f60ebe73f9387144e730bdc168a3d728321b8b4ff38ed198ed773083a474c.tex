\paragraph{Construction.}
Take \(K=3\), let \(\mathcal D=\{d\}\), and let \(d\) measure all three catalogue variables, assigning them respectively to roles one, two, and three.  For example, take \(c_1=c_2=c_3=1\), \(b_E=1\), \(b_O=2\), and \(\kappa=3\), so the cost restrictions are satisfied.  Let \(X\) be constant.  Let \(U\) be uniform on \(\{1,2,3\}\), let \(S_2\) be an independent Bernoulli random variable with success probability \(1/2\), and make \((U,S_2)\) independent of \(G\), where both sources have positive probability.  In source \(E\), let \(A\) be Bernoulli with success probability \(1/2\), independently of all latent and potential variables.  In source \(O\), set
\[
 P(A=1\mid U=u,G=O)=e_u,\qquad
 (e_1,e_2,e_3)=\left(\frac15,\frac12,\frac45\right).
 \tag{1}
\]
Conditional on \((S_2=s,U=u)\), let \(S_1\) and \(S_3\) be independent Bernoulli variables with the same success probability \(p_{s,u}\), where
\[
 (p_{0,1},p_{0,2},p_{0,3})
 =\left(\frac15,\frac12,\frac45\right),
 \qquad
 (p_{1,1},p_{1,2},p_{1,3})
 =\left(\frac3{10},\frac45,\frac25\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
Their conditional law is invariant across source and treatment.  Define, for both \(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 Z(a)=(S_1,S_2,S_3),
 \qquad
 Y(0)=S_3,
 \qquad
 Y(1)=S_3+\delta_{S_2}(U),
 \tag{3}
\]
where
\[
 \bigl(\delta_0(1),\delta_0(2),\delta_0(3)\bigr)
 =\left(4,-\frac{16}{5},1\right),
 \qquad
 \bigl(\delta_1(1),\delta_1(2),\delta_1(3)\bigr)
 =\left(-\frac{16}{5},-\frac8{25},1\right).
 \tag{4}
\]
Finally set \(Z=Z(A)\) and \(Y=Y(A)\).  This specifies a finite-support full-catalogue law.

\paragraph{Causal and support conditions.}
Consistency follows from the last sentence of the construction.  In source \(O\), treatment depends only on \(U\), so \((Y(a),Z(a))\perp A\mid U,X,G=O\).  In source \(E\), treatment was made independent of \((Y(a),Z(a),U,X)\).  Equation~(1) gives observational overlap, while experimental overlap is immediate.  The joint law of \((S_1,S_2,S_3,U,X)\) is source invariant, proving external validity.  Because every \(e_u\) and \(1-e_u\) is strictly positive, the conditional law of \(U\) in every observational treatment arm has full support; hence the source density ratio is finite.  Given \((S_2,U)\), \(S_1\) and \(S_3\) are independent, and \(Y(a)\) is a function of \((S_3,S_2,U)\).  Therefore
\[
 (Y(a),S_3(a))\perp S_1(a)\mid S_2(a),U,X,G=O,
\]
which is the sequential-outcome restriction.

\paragraph{Completeness.}
Fix \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  Write \(w_{a,u}=e_u\) when \(a=1\) and \(w_{a,u}=1-e_u\) when \(a=0\).  These three weights are positive.  If
\[
 \mathbb E_P[v(U)\mid S_1,S_2,A=a,G=O]=0,
\]
then, after multiplying by the positive conditional denominators and using the equations for \(S_1=0\) and \(S_1=1\), one obtains
\[
 \sum_{u=1}^3 w_{a,u}v(u)=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{u=1}^3 w_{a,u}p_{s,u}v(u)=0,
 \quad s=0,1.
 \tag{5}
\]
The coefficient matrix before multiplication of its columns by the positive numbers \(w_{a,u}\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&1\\
 1/5&1/2&4/5\\
 3/10&4/5&2/5
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det=-\frac{27}{100}\ne0.
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus (5) forces \(v(1)=v(2)=v(3)=0\).  This proves early-proxy completeness.  Since \(S_3\) has exactly the same conditional success probabilities as \(S_1\), the identical calculation proves late-proxy completeness.

\paragraph{Observed bridges and operator regularity.}
For each \(s\in\{0,1\}\), direct substitution into (1)--(4) gives
\[
 \sum_{u=1}^3 e_u\delta_s(u)=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{u=1}^3 e_up_{s,u}\delta_s(u)=0.
 \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, for \(s=0\) the two sums are respectively
\[
 \frac45-\frac85+\frac45=0,
 \qquad
 \frac4{25}-\frac45+\frac{16}{25}=0,
\]
and for \(s=1\) they are respectively
\[
 -\frac{16}{25}-\frac4{25}+\frac45=0,
 \qquad
 -\frac{24}{125}-\frac{16}{125}+\frac8{25}=0.
\]
Set
\[
 h_d(s_3,s_2,a)=s_3.
 \tag{8}
\]
When \(A=0\), the residual \(Y-h_d(S_3,S_2,0)\) is identically zero.  When \(A=1\), it equals \(\delta_{S_2}(U)\).  Conditional on \(S_2=s\) and \(A=1\), the numerator of its conditional mean given \(S_1=1\) is the second sum in (7), while the numerator given \(S_1=0\) is the first sum minus the second.  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid S_2,S_1,A,X,G=O]
 =\mathbb E_P[h_d(S_3,S_2,A,X)\mid S_2,S_1,A,X,G=O].
 \tag{9}
\]

For fixed \((s,a)\), the block of \(T_d\) mapping functions of the binary \(S_3\) to functions of the binary \(S_1\) has rank two exactly when \(S_1\) and \(S_3\) are conditionally dependent.  Here
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_P(S_1,S_3\mid S_2=s,A=a,G=O)
 =\operatorname{Var}_P(p_{s,U}\mid S_2=s,A=a,G=O)>0,
 \tag{10}
\]
because the posterior of \(U\) has full support and the three entries in each row of (2) are not all equal.  Thus every \((s,a)\)-block is nonsingular, so the finite-dimensional operator \(T_d\) is bijective.  Equation~(9) and bijectivity also make (8) the unique square-integrable outcome bridge.  Its adjoint \(T_d^\star\) is bijective as well.  All finite cells have positive mass, so the source density ratio appearing in the selection-bridge equation is finite and square-integrable.  Surjectivity of \(T_d^\star\) therefore supplies a square-integrable selection bridge \(q_d\).

\paragraph{Nondegeneracy.}
All variables and both bridges are square-integrable because the support is finite.  The experimental component has positive variance: by (8), its armwise centered term is \(S_3-\mathbb E_P(S_3\mid A=a,G=E)\), and \(S_3\) is nondegenerate.  For the observational component, the residual is zero in arm zero and equals \(\delta_{S_2}(U)\) in arm one.  Every value displayed in (4) is nonzero.  Moreover, for each \(s\), the equality \(T_d^\star q_d>0\) rules out \(q_d(s,S_1,1)=0\) at both values of \(S_1\).  Hence \(q_d(S_2,S_1,1)\delta_{S_2}(U)\) is nonzero with positive probability.  Consequently \(0<V_{E,d},V_{O,d}<\infty\).  We have now verified every member property of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), so \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).

\paragraph{Separation of the observed and causal targets.}
In the experimental source, randomization makes the distribution of \(S_3\) the same in both arms.  By (8),
\[
 \theta_d(P)
 =\mathbb E_P(S_3\mid A=1,G=E)
  -\mathbb E_P(S_3\mid A=0,G=E)
 =0.
 \tag{11}
\]
On the other hand, \(U\) is uniform and \(S_2\) is an independent fair bit in source \(O\), so
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \tau
 &=\mathbb E_P\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid G=O\}
 =\mathbb E_P\{\delta_{S_2}(U)\mid G=O\}\\
 &=\frac16\left[
 \left(4-\frac{16}{5}+1\right)
 +\left(-\frac{16}{5}-\frac8{25}+1\right)
 \right]\\
 &=\frac16\left(\frac95-\frac{63}{25}\right)
 =-\frac3{25}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{12}
\]
Thus this finite-support member of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\) is not in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{id}}\).  The discrepancy arises despite completeness and regularity: equation~(9) controls a treatment-weighted residual depending on \((S_2,U)\), whereas the frozen completeness implications apply to a single function of \(U\).  Therefore the observed bridge contrast cannot be identified with the long-term average treatment effect throughout \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).
