The center-law regularity, bridge calculations, strict optimizer assignments, and variance and ratio expansions are furnished by Lemma~\texttt{lem:binary-witness-stability}.  In particular, for every \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), both designs at \(P_\epsilon\) have unique latent outcome and selection bridges, a bijective observed conditional-expectation operator, and finite nonzero source-score variances.  The same lemma gives
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}=6>5
 =c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}},
\]
the unique predictive optimizer \(d_{\mathrm p}\), the unique design-value optimizer \(d_{\mathrm e}\), and
\[
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025},
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{1}
\]

We make the causal conclusion explicit before invoking the cited efficiency theorem.  At every center law, the latent outcome bridge satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E[h_d(S_{3d},a)\mid U,A=a,G=O]=U,
\]
and the latent selection bridge satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E[q_d(S_{1d},a)\mid U,A=a,G=O]
 =\frac{p(U\mid A=a,G=E)}{p(U\mid A=a,G=O)}.
\]
These equations were derived by direct binary-matrix calculation in the stability lemma, and their solutions are unique.  Moreover,
\[
 \mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E[U\mid G=O],
 \qquad
 \tau=\mathbb E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid G=O]=0. \tag{2}
\]
Thus both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau\).  The remaining IKM Theorem 7 premises---Assumptions 1--4, operator bijectivity, and finite score moments---were also verified before the invocation.  Lemma~\texttt{lem:ikmw-fixed-system-efficiency} and the budget-profile theorem therefore show that the profiled quantities compared in (1) are the budget-normalized local causal efficiency bounds at \(P_\epsilon\), rather than merely bridge-functional variances.

We next pass from the center laws to the relative-open region.  Every \(Q\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) belongs to a ball whose radius is smaller than both \(r_\epsilon^{\mathrm{reg}}\) and the optimizer-stability radius for some \(\epsilon\).  Lemma~\texttt{lem:binary-regular-locus-relatively-open} therefore gives \(Q\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), while Lemma~\texttt{lem:binary-witness-stability} gives
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(Q)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
 \qquad
 D^*(Q)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\},
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(Q)>\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon). \tag{3}
\]
For completeness, the causal-efficiency interpretation also persists there.  At such a law the nonzero early and late proxy contrasts make the two binary channel matrices invertible.  Solving the late-channel system with right-hand side \((u:u\in\{-1,1\})^{\mathsf T}\) constructs the unique latent outcome bridge; solving the early-channel system with right-hand side
\[
 \left(
 \frac{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=E)}{Q(U=u\mid A=a,G=O)}:
 u\in\{-1,1\}\right)^{\mathsf T}
\]
constructs the unique latent selection bridge.  Iterated expectation gives their observed equations.  Since \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\) and the proxy kernels are source invariant, both bridge contrasts equal \(0=\tau(Q)\).  Hence the cited fixed-system efficiency conclusion applies only after, and because, these latent equations have been verified throughout the named region.

Every center \(P_\epsilon\) belongs to its own relative-open ball.  Since the leading coefficient in (1) is positive,
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(Q)
 \ge \sup_{0<\epsilon<10^{-3}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\infty. \tag{4}
\]

Finally, keep the signal law fixed and vary the cap-feasible positive costs.  Put
\[
 C_{\mathrm p}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}},
 \qquad
 C_{\mathrm e}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}.
\]
For proxy signals \(x,y\), Bayes' rule gives
\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid W_x=e,W_y=l]=\frac{xe+yl}{1+xyel},
\]
and direct averaging over the four proxy cells gives
\[
 R(x,y)=\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{7(1-\epsilon^2)}{16-9\epsilon^2},
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35.
\]
The strict predictive ordering is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{C_{\mathrm p}}{C_{\mathrm e}}
 <\frac{R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)}{R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)}
 =\frac{3(16-9\epsilon^2)}{35(1-\epsilon^2)}. \tag{5}
\]
The right side of (5) tends to \(48/35\).  Hence every fixed cost vector satisfying
\[
 1<\frac{C_{\mathrm p}}{C_{\mathrm e}}<\frac{48}{35}
\]
makes \(d_{\mathrm p}\) strictly more expensive and uniquely prediction-selected for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\).  For any such fixed positive costs, \(\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)\) remains bounded, whereas
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 \ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}\longrightarrow\infty
\]
by (1).  Since there are only two designs, \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is eventually the unique causal-efficiency optimizer and the approximation ratio diverges.  All displayed inequalities are strict and depend continuously on the positive costs, so this range is relatively open in the cap-feasible cost region.  This proves every claim.
