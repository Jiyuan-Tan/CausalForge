Fix \(\chi>1\), \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), and write \(y=y_\chi\).  We first verify the center law and compute its exact values.

All latent cells in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}witness}\) have positive probability.  Consistency holds by construction because \(Z(a)\) and \(Y(a)\) do not depend on the realized treatment.  Conditional on \(U\), the proxies are independent of treatment; hence latent exchangeability holds in source \(O\).  Its treatment kernel is bounded strictly between zero and one.  Treatment is independent of \((U,Z(a),Y(a))\) in source \(E\), with both arms having probability one half.  The law of \((U,Z(a))\) is invariant across sources, so external validity and source support hold.  Finally, \((Y(a),S_{3d}(a))\) is conditionally independent of \(S_{1d}(a)\) given \(U\), because \(Y(a)=U\) and the two proxies are conditionally independent.

For a design with early signal \(x\ne0\) and late signal \(y\ne0\), the two binary conditional-probability matrices from \(U\) to the early and late proxies have nonzero determinants.  This proves both completeness implications.  Conditional on \(T=t\) in source \(O\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(W_x,W_y\mid T=t,G=O)
 =xy\operatorname{Var}(U\mid T=t,G=O)
 =\frac{15}{16}xy\ne0.                                  \tag{1}
\]
For binary variables with positive cells, (1) is equivalent to nonsingularity of the conditional-expectation matrix from functions of \(W_y\) to functions of \(W_x\).  Thus \(T_d\) is bijective for both designs.

The displayed bridge candidates satisfy the observed equations.  Indeed,
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left(\frac{W_y}{y}\mathrel{\Big|}U\right)=U,
\]
so conditioning further through the early proxy gives the outcome bridge equation.  Direct conditioning over the two values of \(U\) gives
\[
 q_d(W_x,A)=\frac{16}{15}\left\{1-\frac{TW_x}{4x}\right\}, \tag{2}
\]
and substitution in the two binary cells verifies the selection-bridge equation.  All bridges are bounded at fixed \((\chi,\epsilon)\), and the score moments computed below are finite and positive.  Therefore \(P_{\chi,\epsilon}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\).  Since treatment is randomized in source \(E\) and \(h_d=W_y/y\),
\[
 \mu_{d,a}=\mathbb E(U)=0
 \qquad(a=0,1),
\]
while \(Y(1)-Y(0)=0\) identically.  Hence both bridge contrasts equal \(\tau=0\).

The best predictor of \(U\) from the two conditionally independent binary proxies gives, by summing their four cells,
\[
 R(x,y)=\mathbb E\{\operatorname{Var}(U\mid W_x,W_y)\}
 =\frac{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}{1-x^2y^2}.                       \tag{3}
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), use \(1-y_\chi^2=3/(10\chi)\) and paired cost \(5\chi\); for \(d_{\mathrm e}\), use \(x=y=1/2\) and paired cost five.  Equation (3) then gives
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 =\frac32\frac{1-\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon^2y_\chi^2}
 <\frac32<3
 =B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B). \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is the unique diagnostic minimizer.  The cost formulas in the definition also give
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}-c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}
 =\frac{5(\chi-1)}2>0.                                  \tag{5}
\]

At the balanced centers, \(p_{E,a}=p_{O,a}=1/2\).  The experimental score is \(2TW_y/y\), so
\[
 V_E(x,y)=\frac4{y^2}.                                   \tag{6}
\]
For the observational score, substitute (2) and sum the eight cells of \((U,T,W_x,W_y)\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 V_O(x,y)
 &=4\mathbb E_O\!\left[
 \left\{\frac{16}{15}\left(1-\frac{TW_x}{4x}\right)\right\}^2
 \left(U-\frac{W_y}{y}\right)^2\right]\\
 &=\frac{64(14x^2+1)(1-y^2)}{225x^2y^2}.                \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) give
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac{40\chi}{10\chi-3},
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
 =\frac{64}{75(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14),            \tag{8}
\]
and, for \(d_{\mathrm e}\),
\[
 V_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}=16,
 \qquad
 V_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}=\frac{384}{25}.                    \tag{9}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\), together with \((c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}},c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}})=(2,3)\), now yields
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\left(\sqrt{32}+\sqrt{\frac{1152}{25}}\right)^2
 =\frac{3872}{25}.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Substituting (8) and the two costs of \(d_{\mathrm p}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})}
 {\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon})}
 =\frac{25}{3872}\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{20\chi(5\chi-1)}{10\chi-3}}
 +\sqrt{\frac{32(5\chi+1)}{75(10\chi-3)}
 (\epsilon^{-2}+14)}\right\}^2.                        \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
\]
Discarding the first nonnegative term inside the square gives
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*}{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*}
 \ge\frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}(\epsilon^{-2}+14)
 >\frac{\epsilon^{-2}+14}{726}>1,                       \tag{12}
\]
where the strict middle inequality follows from \(2(5\chi+1)>10\chi-3\).  Hence \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is the unique arm-normalized optimizer, and (11) is \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})\).

Balanced treatment and the definition of the IKM normalization give
\[
 V_{g,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=\frac14V_{g,d},
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^*=\frac14\mathcal V_d^*.
\]
The common factor cancels from the ratio, proving the IKM equality in (1) of the statement and transferring (12) to that ratio.  Taking infima in (12) proves (2)--(3).

It remains to optimize (11) sharply.  Let \(C_0=25/3872\), and let \(H_\epsilon(\chi)\) be \(\epsilon^2\) times the braces-squared expression in (11).  For every compact interval \(K\subset(0,\infty)\), elementary Taylor expansion of the rational functions in (11), uniformly for \(k\in K\), gives
\[
 H_\epsilon(k\epsilon^{-2/3})
 =\frac{16}{75}+\epsilon^{2/3}q(k)+O_K(\epsilon^{4/3}), \tag{13}
\]
where
\[
 q(k)=2\sqrt{\frac{32k}{15}}+\frac8{75k}.               \tag{14}
\]
For clarity, the two terms in (14) have distinct sources: the cross product of the two square roots contributes \(2\sqrt{32k/15}\), while
\[
 \frac{5\chi+1}{10\chi-3}
 =\frac12+\frac5{2(10\chi-3)}
\]
contributes \(8/(75k)\).  The remaining squared first-root term and the constant fourteen enter only at order \(\epsilon^{4/3}\) or smaller on compact \(k\)-sets.

We justify that the local expansion captures every minimizer.  For fixed \(\epsilon\), the exact objective is continuous on \((1,\infty)\) and diverges as \(\chi\to\infty\).  At bounded \(\chi\), the leading \(\epsilon^{-2}\) coefficient in (11) is strictly larger than its limit as \(\chi\to\infty\), so comparison with \(\chi=k\epsilon^{-2/3}\) excludes the boundary \(\chi\downarrow1\) for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\).  Hence the infimum is attained.  If a minimizing sequence had \(k_\epsilon=\chi\epsilon^{2/3}\to0\), then either \(\chi\) stayed bounded, which leaves a strictly larger leading coefficient, or \(\chi\to\infty\), in which case the positive rational correction in (13), divided by \(\epsilon^{2/3}\), is asymptotic to \(8/(75k_\epsilon)\to\infty\).  If \(k_\epsilon\toinfty\), the nonnegative cross term divided by \(\epsilon^{2/3}\) is asymptotic to \(2\sqrt{32k_\epsilon/15}\to\infty\).  Comparison with any fixed \(k\) rules out both cases.  Thus all rescaled minimizers lie in one compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), where (13) is uniform.

Differentiating (14) gives
\[
 q'(k)=\sqrt{\frac{32}{15}}k^{-1/2}-\frac8{75}k^{-2}.
\]
It changes sign exactly once, at
\[
 k_\star^3=\frac2{375},
 \qquad
 k_\star=\frac{\sqrt[3]{18}}{15}.                       \tag{15}
\]
Therefore uniform argmin comparison in (13) yields
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)epsilon^{2/3}\longrightarrow k_\star,
\]
which is (4).  Taking minima in (13), multiplying by \(C_0\), and simplifying \(C_0q(k_\star)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \epsilon^2\inf_{\chi>1}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 &=C_0\frac{16}{75}+C_0q(k_\star)\epsilon^{2/3}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3})\\
 &=\frac1{726}+\frac{5\sqrt[3]{12}}{968}\epsilon^{2/3}
 +o(\epsilon^{2/3}).                                    \tag{16}
\end{aligned}
\]
The IKM ratio is identical at every center, so (16) holds for it as well.  This proves all claims.
