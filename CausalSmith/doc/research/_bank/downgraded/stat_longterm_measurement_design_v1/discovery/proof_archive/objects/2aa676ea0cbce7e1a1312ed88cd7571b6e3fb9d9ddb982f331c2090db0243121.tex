For \(d\in\mathcal D\), abbreviate the identified observed bridge contrast by
\[
 \theta_d(P):=\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0},
\]
and let \(\mathcal F_m:=\sigma(\mathcal P_m)\).  Pilot independence makes \(\widehat d_m\), \(\widehat\alpha_m\), \(N_{E,B}\), and \(N_{O,B}\) measurable with respect to \(\mathcal F_m\) and independent of the main-study observations.  The pilot theorem and nondegeneracy imply that there is an \(a_0>0\) such that
\[
 \Pr\{a_0\le\widehat\alpha_m\le1-a_0\}\longrightarrow1.                  \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the finite set of population shares \(\alpha_d^*(P)\) is contained in the interior of \((0,1)\), and the pilot theorem gives uniform convergence of the estimated shares.  On the event in (1), both realized counts are of order \(B\).  Uniformly over \(d\), the allocation part of the pilot theorem, or its second-order refinement, gives
\[
 s_{0,B}^2-\mathcal V_{\widehat d_m}^*(P)=o_P(1),
 \qquad
 s_{0,B}^2:=B\left\{
 \frac{V_{E,\widehat d_m}}{N_{E,B}}
 +\frac{V_{O,\widehat d_m}}{N_{O,B}}\right\}.                             \tag{2}
\]

We next establish the fixed-design expansion for the observed bridge functional.  For fold \(\ell\), set
\[
 e_{h,d,\ell}:=\widehat h_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-h_d,
 \qquad
 e_{q,d,\ell}:=\widehat q_{d,B}^{(-\ell)}-q_d.
\]
The exact doubly robust score algebra, using the outcome-bridge equation and the adjoint identity, leaves the conditional-mean remainder
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_{d,\ell}
 &=\sum_{a=0}^1(-1)^{1-a}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{O,a}}
 e_{q,d,\ell}\,(T_d e_{h,d,\ell})\mathrel{\Big|}G=O\right]\\
 &=\sum_{a=0}^1(-1)^{1-a}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A=a\}}{p_{O,a}}
 (T_d^\star e_{q,d,\ell})e_{h,d,\ell}\mathrel{\Big|}G=O\right].
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{3}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the two representations in (3), together with \(\min_a p_{O,a}>0\), gives a finite constant \(C_P\), independent of \(d\), \(\ell\), and \(B\), such that
\[
 |\mathcal R_{d,\ell}|
 \le C_P
 \min\left\{
 \|e_{h,d,\ell}\|_{w,h,d}\|e_{q,d,\ell}\|_{2,O,q,d},
 \|e_{h,d,\ell}\|_{2,O,h,d}\|e_{q,d,\ell}\|_{w,q,d}
 \right\}.                                                                \tag{4}
\]
Thus the uniform weak--strong product assumption makes (4) \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the finite family and the fixed folds.  Conditional on the training folds, cross-fitting centers the remaining empirical nuisance terms.  The strong-rate assumptions make their conditional \(L_2\) norms \(o_P(1)\), so their empirical averages are \(o_P(B^{-1/2})\); this is the empirical-negligibility step in the established fixed-design limit lemma, now applied to the observed functional whose gradient was proved in the designwise-gradient lemma.  Consequently, for fixed source counts \(n_E,n_O\) of order \(B\), uniformly over \(d\in\mathcal D\) and allocations in \([a_0,1-a_0]\),
\[
 \widehat\tau_{d,B}-\theta_d(P)
 =\frac1{n_E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_E}\phi_{E,d}(O_{E,i})
  +\frac1{n_O}\sum_{i=1}^{n_O}\phi_{O,d}(O_{O,i})
  +o_P(B^{-1/2}).                                                           \tag{5}
\]
Here \(\widehat\tau_{d,B}\) denotes the same cross-fitted estimator with design \(d\) and the displayed source counts.  Finiteness of \(\mathcal D\), (1), and pilot independence permit substitution of \(d=\widehat d_m\) and of the pilot-measurable counts in (5).

Conditional on \(\mathcal F_m\), the two leading sums in (5) are independent and mean zero, and the variance of their sum after multiplication by \(B\) is \(s_{0,B}^2\).  The established fixed-design limit lemma, applied to the observed functional through (3)--(5), gives standardized normal convergence for each fixed \(d\) and each limiting source-count ratio in the compact set furnished by (1).  This convergence is uniform over the finite family and the compact count-ratio set: otherwise a violating sequence would have a subsequence with a fixed design and a convergent count ratio, contradicting the fixed-design conclusion.  Conditioning on \(\mathcal F_m\) and then averaging its conditional distribution therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B\{\widehat\tau_B-\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\}}
 {s_{0,B}}\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1).                                \tag{6}
\]

On the event in (1), the uniform main-study variance-rate assumption gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat s_B^2-s_{0,B}^2|
 &\le \frac B{N_{E,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{E,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{E,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &\quad+\frac B{N_{O,B}}
 |\widetilde V_{O,\widehat d_m,B}-V_{O,\widehat d_m}|\\
 &=O_P(r_{\widetilde V,B})=o_P(1).
\end{aligned}                                                              \tag{7}
\]
Equations (2) and (7) prove both variance conclusions.  Nondegeneracy and positive costs bound the finitely many profiled values away from zero.  Hence \(\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}\to_P1\).  For any continuity point \(x\) of the standard normal distribution and any \(0<\delta<1\), the event \(|\widehat s_B/s_{0,B}-1|\le\delta\) sandwiches the distribution function of the statistic standardized by \(\widehat s_B\) between the distribution functions in (6) evaluated at the corresponding factors \(x(1-\delta)\) and \(x(1+\delta)\), with the order reversed when \(x<0\).  First let \(B,m\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Continuity of the normal distribution proves the asserted studentized limit.

Let \(z_{1-\eta/2}\) be the \((1-\eta/2)\)-quantile of the standard normal distribution and set
\[
 I_B:=\left[
 \widehat\tau_B-z_{1-\eta/2}\frac{\widehat s_B}{\sqrt B},
 \widehat\tau_B+z_{1-\eta/2}\frac{\widehat s_B}{\sqrt B}
 \right].
\]
The studentized convergence gives
\[
 P\{\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)\in I_B\}\longrightarrow1-\eta.
\]
Finally, if \(\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=\tau\) for every \(d\in\mathcal D\), then \(\theta_{\widehat d_m}(P)=\tau\) for every pilot realization.  Substitution in (6) and in the last display proves the causal specialization without imposing that equality on the full class.
