Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}witness\text{-}stability}\) supplies, for every \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), membership \(P_\epsilon\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\), regularity and causal identification for both designs, the strict predictive and arm-normalized profile orderings, and
\[
V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}
=\frac{448}{2025\epsilon^2}+\frac{6272}{2025},
\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
=\frac{49}{9801\epsilon^2}+O(\epsilon^{-1}). \tag{1}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}ikmw\text{-}normalized\text{-}stability}\) gives, at every balanced center,
\[
V_{g,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=\frac14V_{g,d},\qquad
\mathcal V_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_\epsilon)
=\frac14\mathcal V_d^*(P_\epsilon). \tag{2}
\]
Thus the common factor preserves the unique optimizer \(d_{\mathrm e}\), cancels from the predictive ratio, and proves all center equalities. Since the binary lemma verifies causal identification, unique square-integrable bridges, bijectivity, and finite moments, cited Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}ikmw\text{-}fixed\text{-}system\text{-}efficiency}\) applies design by design and supplies the absolute causal-efficiency interpretation for the normalized, rather than arm-normalized, profile.

The corrected binary-region definition chooses each ball inside the regularity and optimizer-stability radius. Therefore every \(P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) is regular and satisfies
\[
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
\qquad D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}.
\]
Every center belongs to its own ball, so (1) implies
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\infty. \tag{3}
\]
The normalized-stability lemma and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}ikmw\text{-}region}\) shrink each ball to preserve regularity, identification, and the normalized strict ordering. Hence on \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\),
\[
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},\qquad
D_{\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}.
\]
The shrunken region retains every center, so (1)--(2) yield
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}}
\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}(P)=\infty. \tag{4}
\]

It remains to check the cost range in its genuine feasible geometry. Put
\[
C_{\mathrm p}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}},
\qquad
C_{\mathrm e}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}.
\]
The prediction risks calculated in the binary lemma show that strict prediction selection is equivalent to
\[
\frac{C_{\mathrm p}}{C_{\mathrm e}}
<\frac{3(16-9\epsilon^2)}{35(1-\epsilon^2)}. \tag{5}
\]
The right side tends to \(48/35\). Hence any fixed point of \(\mathcal C_\kappa\) satisfying
\[
1<C_{\mathrm p}/C_{\mathrm e}<48/35
\]
makes \(d_{\mathrm p}\) strictly more expensive and uniquely prediction-selected for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\). The conditions defining \(\mathcal C_\kappa\) enforce the common base-cost relation
\[
c_{O,d}-c_{E,d}=b_O-b_E
\]
for every design, together with the subset/cap constraints; thus no four design costs are varied independently. Relative to this induced manifold, the two strict ratio inequalities define an open set.

For every fixed cost point in that set, the \(d_{\mathrm e}\) profile stays bounded while
\[
\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
\ge c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}V_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus the arm-normalized ratio diverges. Balanced treatment makes (2) independent of the positive cost vector, so the normalized causal-efficiency ratio diverges as well.
