Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\) and suppose that \(\mathcal D=\{d\}\), that \(n_E,n_O\) are positive, and that
\[
 B=c_{E,d}n_E+c_{O,d}n_O.
\]
Because the unit costs and counts are strictly positive, \(B>0\).  Define
\[
 \alpha_0=\frac{c_{E,d}n_E}{B}.
\]
Both summands defining \(B\) are positive, so \(0<\alpha_0<1\), and the budget identity also gives
\[
 1-\alpha_0
 =\frac{B-c_{E,d}n_E}{B}
 =\frac{c_{O,d}n_O}{B}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Membership of \(P\) in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}\) makes the sole admissible design regular.  Hence the budget-profile theorem and the definition of the regular design-value profile give
\[
 \mathcal V_d(\alpha_0;P)
 =\frac{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{\alpha_0}
  +\frac{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{1-\alpha_0}.                         \tag{2}
\]
Substituting the definition of \(\alpha_0\) and (1) into (2) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{1}{B}\mathcal V_d(\alpha_0;P)
 &=\frac{1}{B}\left\{
   \frac{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{c_{E,d}n_E/B}
   +\frac{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{c_{O,d}n_O/B}
   \right\}\\
 &=\frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O}.                  \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the displayed algebraic identity.  The nondegeneracy assumption ensures that both variance components in (3) are finite and strictly positive.

By the designwise-gradient lemma, the contrast \(\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}\) is an identified observed-data functional with conditional mean-zero source-gradient components \(\phi_{E,d}\) and \(\phi_{O,d}\).  For independent samples of sizes \(n_E\) and \(n_O\), the gradient of the corresponding two-sample functional is the pair of empirical-average gradients.  Independence and conditional centering therefore give its variance as
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(\phi_{E,d}\mid G=E)}{n_E}
 +\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(\phi_{O,d}\mid G=O)}{n_O}
 =\frac{V_{E,d}}{n_E}+\frac{V_{O,d}}{n_O}.                  \tag{4}
\]
Thus the right-hand side of (3) is exactly the fixed-system pathwise-gradient variance for the observed bridge contrast.

For the conditional causal assertion, now additionally suppose that \(\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=\tau\) and that \((\phi_{E,d},\phi_{O,d})\) is the canonical gradient for \(\tau\) in the stated Imbens--Kallus--Mao--Wang fixed-system model.  By the defining Hilbert-space characterization of a canonical gradient, the local semiparametric efficiency bound is its squared norm in the product two-source tangent space.  With independent source samples and fixed counts, that squared norm is precisely the variance in (4), which equals the left-hand side of (3).  The listed bridge-existence, bridge-uniqueness, operator-bijectivity, moment, and interior sampling-ratio conditions delimit the fixed-system regime in which this conditional identification-and-canonicality premise is invoked.  If either causal equality or canonicality is absent, the designwise-gradient lemma justifies only (4) for the observed bridge functional, so no unconditional causal-efficiency conclusion follows.
