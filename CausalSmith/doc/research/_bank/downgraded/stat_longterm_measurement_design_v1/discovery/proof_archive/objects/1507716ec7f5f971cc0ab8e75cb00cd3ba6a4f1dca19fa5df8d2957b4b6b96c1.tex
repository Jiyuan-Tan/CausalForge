Let \(t_m=r_{V,m}+\underline v_m\).  Positive nondegenerate population variances and finiteness of \(\mathcal D\) imply, by the mean-value theorem applied to the square-root allocation map, that
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}|\widehat\alpha_{d,m}-\alpha_d^*(P)|=O_P(t_m). \tag{1}
\]
All population optimizers are uniformly separated from zero and one.  Therefore, with probability tending to one, every estimated allocation lies in a common compact subinterval of \((0,1)\).  On that interval the second derivative
\[
 \frac{\partial^2}{\partial\alpha^2}\mathcal V_d(\alpha;P)
 =\frac{2c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}{\alpha^3}
  +\frac{2c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}{(1-\alpha)^3}
\]
is uniformly bounded over the finite family, while the first derivative vanishes at \(\alpha_d^*(P)\).  Taylor's theorem and (1) thus give
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\{\mathcal V_d(\widehat\alpha_{d,m};P)-\mathcal V_d^*(P)\}
 =O_P(t_m^2). \tag{2}
\]
Uniform application of the floor inequality
\[
 0\le \frac1{\lfloor x\rfloor}-\frac1x\le\frac2{x^2},\qquad x\ge2,
\]
on the same compact allocation interval contributes \(O(B^{-1})\) after budget normalization.  Combining this rounding term with (2) proves the claim.
