The stability lemma proves that every center \(P_\epsilon\) is a regular law, that \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is its unique predictive optimizer, that \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is its unique design-value optimizer, and that the displayed exact variance and ratio expansion hold.  For completeness, the causal claim at each center follows directly from the construction: \(Y(1)=Y(0)=U\), so \(\tau=0\), while the bridge \(h_d(W_y,a)=W_y/y\) has mean zero in each randomized treatment arm.  Hence \(\mu_{d,1}-\mu_{d,0}=0=\tau\) for both designs.  Their bridge operators are bijective by the nonzero determinant in the stability lemma, and the fixed-system efficiency result therefore gives the asserted causal efficiency interpretation.  The costs in the construction satisfy
\[
 c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}=\frac52+\frac72=6
 >5=2+3=c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}.
\]
Thus all assertions concerning a fixed center follow.

Now take \(P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\).  By the definition of that region, for some \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\), the law \(P\) belongs to the constrained regular model and satisfies
\[
 \|P-P_\epsilon\|_{\mathrm{cell}}<\delta_\epsilon<r_\epsilon.
\]
The stability lemma consequently gives
\[
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\},
 \qquad
 D^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}. \tag{1}
\]
Each center \(P_\epsilon\) itself belongs to its corresponding ball because its cell distance is zero.  Hence, using the center expansion from the lemma,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
 \ge \sup_{0<\epsilon<10^{-3}}\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P_\epsilon)
 =\infty. \tag{2}
\]
This proves the relatively open regular-region assertion.

Finally, keep the stochastic construction fixed but allow any positive cap-feasible cost vector.  Write
\[
 C_{\mathrm p}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm p}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}},
 \qquad
 C_{\mathrm e}=c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}+c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}.
\]
The risk calculation in the binary construction is cost invariant and gives
\[
 \frac{R_{d_{\mathrm e}}(P_\epsilon)}{R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_\epsilon)}
 =\frac{3(16-9\epsilon^2)}{35(1-\epsilon^2)}. \tag{3}
\]
By the definition of the paired-source prediction objective, \(d_{\mathrm p}\) is its unique minimizer precisely when
\[
 \frac{C_{\mathrm p}}{C_{\mathrm e}}
 <\frac{3(16-9\epsilon^2)}{35(1-\epsilon^2)}. \tag{4}
\]
As a function of \(z=\epsilon^2\), the right side of (4) has derivative \(3/[5(1-z)^2]>0\) and value \(48/35\) at \(z=0\).  Therefore every cost vector satisfying
\[
 1<\frac{C_{\mathrm p}}{C_{\mathrm e}}<\frac{48}{35} \tag{5}
\]
makes the strictly more expensive design \(d_{\mathrm p}\) uniquely prediction-optimal for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\) (indeed, for every \(0<\epsilon<10^{-3}\)).

For any fixed positive cost vector, (12) from the stability calculation and the budget-profile formula imply
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\frac{448c_{O,d_{\mathrm p}}}{2025\epsilon^2}
 +O(\epsilon^{-1}), \tag{6}
\]
whereas
\[
 \mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_\epsilon)
 =\left(\sqrt{16c_{E,d_{\mathrm e}}}
 +\sqrt{\frac{384}{25}c_{O,d_{\mathrm e}}}\right)^2 \tag{7}
\]
is finite and strictly positive.  Thus for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), \(d_{\mathrm e}\) is uniquely design-value optimal and the approximation ratio diverges at order \(\epsilon^{-2}\).  The strict inequalities in (5) define a relatively open subset of the cap-feasible cost region.  It is nonempty because the displayed witness costs are cap feasible and give \(C_{\mathrm p}/C_{\mathrm e}=6/5\in(1,48/35)\).  This proves that the failure is neither caused by equal costs nor by failure of identification.
