For one fixed short-term-outcome system, take independent and identically distributed experimental and observational samples whose sizes both diverge and whose ratio converges to a constant \(\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), and use a fixed finite number of observational cross-fitting folds.  The maintained premises mapped to Imbens--Kallus--Mao--Wang are their Assumptions 1--3 (observational exchangeability and overlap, experimental randomization and overlap, external validity and source support), Assumptions 4--7 (the sequential-outcome restriction, both completeness conditions, and existence of square-integrable outcome and selection bridges satisfying the observed bridge equations), and finite, strictly positive second moments for the two source influence summands.  Suppose both parts of their Assumption 8 hold with the limiting functions equal to those two bridges: the outcome- and selection-bridge estimators converge in their respective weak and strong \(L_2\) metrics, all four errors vanish, and the minimum of the two weak--strong products is \(o((n_E+n_O)^{-1/2})\).  Then the cross-fitted doubly robust estimator has the two-source asymptotic linear expansion in the proof of their Theorem 5 and, after division by the square root of the sum of the two source-component variances divided by their respective sample sizes, converges in law to \(\mathcal N(0,1)\).  Its center is the observed bridge contrast delivered by the two bridge equations; under the identification premises of their Theorem 5 that contrast is the causal average treatment effect.  Bridge uniqueness, operator bijectivity, and the canonical-gradient conclusion are not premises of this limit law; they are the additional premises used for the efficiency conclusion in their Theorem 7.
