%%% FIELD sharp-proof
\emph{Proof.}
Write \(p_d=R_d(P)\) and \(v_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P)\).  The family \(\mathcal D\) is finite and nonempty.  Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}predictive\text{-}benchmark}\) gives \(0\le p_d<\infty\).  By Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), the costs are positive and \(0<V_{E,d},V_{O,d}<\infty\).  The profile formula in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) therefore gives
\[
 0<v_d=
 \left(\sqrt{c_{E,d}V_{E,d}}+
       \sqrt{c_{O,d}V_{O,d}}\right)^2<\infty .             \tag{10}
\]
These are the support and boundary facts needed below: all denominators involving \(v_d\) are strictly positive, every displayed finite-set minimum or maximum is attained, and \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) is nonempty.

The order set \(\mathcal O_P\) is finite and nonempty because it contains every diagonal pair \((d,d)\).  Every ratio \(v_d/v_{d'}\) on it is positive and finite, while each diagonal ratio is one.  Thus
\[
 1\le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)<\infty .                         \tag{11}
\]
For \(C\ge0\), the implication in equation~\((2)\) holds for all \(d,d'\) exactly when
\[
 \frac{v_d}{v_{d'}}\le C
 \quad\text{for every }(d,d')\in\mathcal O_P,             \tag{12}
\]
where division is legitimate by equation~\((10)\).  Since the index set in equation~\((12)\) is finite, equation~\((12)\) is equivalent to \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\le C\).  This proves equations~\((1)\)--\((2)\) and the claimed sharpness of the pairwise constant.

Let \(d_{\mathrm{sel}}\) minimize \(v_d\) over \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\), and let \(d_*\) minimize \(v_d\) over \(\mathcal D\).  The best-tie selector has the exact multiplicative loss
\[
 \frac{v_{d_{\mathrm{sel}}}}{v_{d_*}}
 =\frac{\min_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)}v_d}
        {\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}v_{d'}}
 =\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P).                                \tag{13}
\]
Consequently the least \(C\) bounding the selected value by \(C\min_{d'}v_{d'}\) is the ratio in equation~\((13)\) itself.  Since \(d_*\) is globally optimal, this ratio is at least one.  Since \(d_{\mathrm{sel}}\) minimizes prediction risk whereas \(d_*\) is merely an admissible design,
\[
 p_{d_{\mathrm{sel}}}\le p_{d_*};
\]
hence \((d_{\mathrm{sel}},d_*)\in\mathcal O_P\), and
\[
 1\le C_{\mathrm{sel}}(P)=\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)
 =\frac{v_{d_{\mathrm{sel}}}}{v_{d_*}}
 \le C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P).                                 \tag{14}
\]
Together with equation~\((11)\), this proves equation~\((3)\).

Now let \(\mathcal Q\) be nonempty.  If one finite constant works for the best-tie rule at every \(P\in\mathcal Q\), equation~\((13)\) shows that the supremum of \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}(P)\) over \(\mathcal Q\) is bounded by that constant.  Conversely, if that supremum is finite, choosing it as \(C\) gives the uniform bound.  The identical argument using the exact equivalence in equation~\((12)\) proves the pairwise assertion with \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\).  This establishes the two uniform if-and-only-if statements.  It also shows why \(C_{\mathrm{ord}}(P)\), which uses only the realized prediction ordering and realized values, is the weakest exact lawwise pairwise comparison.  The operators \(T_d\) affect those realized values through the design-specific bridges, but finiteness in equation~\((11)\) needs no comparison between different operators.

We next prove the quadratic-form certificate.  For any numerical realization specified in the theorem, put
\[
 r_d(x)=\frac{F_d(x)}{Q_{R,d}(x)},
 \qquad x\in\mathcal X_d(P).                              \tag{15}
\]
The denominator is strictly positive by the stipulated positivity of \(Q_{R,d}\) on the nonzero cone.  By the definitions in equation~\((6)\), \(\Lambda_-(P)\) is the greatest constant \(L\) such that
\[
 LQ_{R,d}(x)\le F_d(x)
 \quad\text{for every }d\in\mathcal D,
 \ x\in\mathcal X_d(P),                                  \tag{16}
\]
and \(\Lambda_+(P)\) is the least constant \(U\) such that
\[
 F_d(x)\le UQ_{R,d}(x)
 \quad\text{for every }d\in\mathcal D,
 \ x\in\mathcal X_d(P).                                  \tag{17}
\]
Indeed, equations~\((16)\)--\((17)\) are respectively equivalent to \(L\le\inf_xr_d(x)\) for every \(d\), and to \(U\ge\sup_xr_d(x)\) for every \(d\).  Thus a strictly positive finite two-sided envelope exists if and only if
\[
 0<\Lambda_-(P)\le\Lambda_+(P)<\infty,                   \tag{18}
\]
and the two endpoints in equation~\((18)\) are its sharp global lower and upper coefficients on the chosen cones.  This proves the asserted ``weakest envelope'' characterization.

Apply equations~\((16)\)--\((17)\) to the transported directions \(x_d(P)\).  Equations~\((4)\) and~\((18)\) yield, for every \(d\),
\[
 \Lambda_-(P)p_d\le v_d\le\Lambda_+(P)p_d.               \tag{19}
\]
In particular, equation~\((19)\) also forces every \(p_d\) in this numerical realization to be positive.  If \((d,d')\in\mathcal O_P\), then
\[
 \frac{v_d}{v_{d'}}
 \le
 \frac{\Lambda_+(P)p_d}{\Lambda_-(P)p_{d'}}
 \le \frac{\Lambda_+(P)}{\Lambda_-(P)}.                  \tag{20}
\]
Taking the maximum over \(\mathcal O_P\), and combining the result with equation~\((14)\), proves equation~\((8)\).

It remains to justify the profiling and eigenvalue assertions.  For fixed \(d\) and \(x\), set
\[
 a=c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x),\qquad b=c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x),
\]
so \(a,b\ge0\).  For every \(0<\alpha<1\), direct expansion gives
\[
 \frac a\alpha+\frac b{1-\alpha}-(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2
 =\frac{\{(1-\alpha)\sqrt a-\alpha\sqrt b\}^2}
        {\alpha(1-\alpha)}\ge0.                           \tag{21}
\]
If \(a,b>0\), equality occurs at
\(
 \alpha=\sqrt a/(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)
\).
If \(a=0<b\), or \(b=0<a\), the lower bound is approached as \(\alpha\downarrow0\), or \(\alpha\uparrow1\), respectively; if \(a=b=0\), equality holds for every \(\alpha\).  Taking the infimum in equation~\((21)\) proves equation~\((9)\) in every boundary case.

The profiled map need not itself be quadratic.  For example, on \(\mathbb R^2\), take unit costs,
\[
 Q_E(x_1,x_2)=x_1^2,
 \qquad Q_O(x_1,x_2)=x_2^2.
\]
Then
\[
 F(x_1,x_2)=(|x_1|+|x_2|)^2.
\]
For the coordinate vectors \(e_1,e_2\),
\[
 F(e_1+e_2)+F(e_1-e_2)=8
 \ne4=2F(e_1)+2F(e_2),                                   \tag{22}
\]
contradicting the parallelogram identity satisfied by every quadratic form.  Thus a generalized-eigenvalue representation is unavailable in general.

Even in the finite-dimensional full-space case, at a fixed \(\alpha\) the quadratic numerator
\[
 G_{d,\alpha}(x)=
 \frac{c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)}\alpha+
 \frac{c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)}{1-\alpha}
\]
satisfies \(F_d(x)\le G_{d,\alpha}(x)\) by equation~\((9)\).  Hence the largest generalized Rayleigh quotient of \(G_{d,\alpha}\) relative to positive-definite \(Q_{R,d}\) supplies an upper full-space envelope only.  It supplies neither the strictly positive lower coefficient in equation~\((16)\) nor equality in the two successive inequalities of equation~\((20)\).  Equality in equation~\((20)\) requires a law-realized ordered pair whose numerator direction attains \(\Lambda_+(P)\), whose denominator direction attains \(\Lambda_-(P)\), and whose prediction risks saturate the ordering.  Comparing such directions by one eigenproblem also requires placing the design-specific forms on a common space and normalizing by the positive lower envelope.  Bijectivity of each \(T_d\) gives none of these cross-design metric or attainment properties, and the scalar ordering \(p_d\le p_{d'}\) gives none either.  This proves the final assertions. \(\square\)

%%% FIELD treatment-aware-proof
\emph{Proof.}
Fix one of the two profiles in the statement and denote it by \(w_d\).  For the arm-normalized choice, positivity and finiteness follow from Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\), positive costs, and Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\).  For the IKM-normalized choice, Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}ikmw\text{-}normalized\text{-}design\text{-}value}\) gives
\[
 V_{g,d}^{\mathrm{IKM}}=(1-p_{g,1})^2V_{g,d}.
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}source\text{-}arm\text{-}positivity}\) makes each factor \(1-p_{g,1}\) strictly positive, and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) makes \(V_{g,d}\) finite and strictly positive.  Consequently
\[
 0<w_d<\infty
 \qquad\text{for every }d\in\mathcal D                 \tag{10}
\]
under either profile.  The finite nonempty family \(\mathcal D\) therefore has nonempty minimizer sets, and every ratio below is well-defined.

Put \(r_d=R_d^A(P)\) and
\[
 \mathcal O_P^A=\{(d,d')\in\mathcal D^2:r_d\le r_{d'}\}.
\]
This set is finite and contains all diagonal pairs.  For \(C\ge0\), all treatment-aware order implications hold exactly when
\[
 \frac{w_d}{w_{d'}}\le C
 \qquad\text{for every }(d,d')\in\mathcal O_P^A.          \tag{11}
\]
By finiteness, equation~\((11)\) is equivalent to
\[
 C\ge
 \max_{(d,d')\in\mathcal O_P^A}\frac{w_d}{w_{d'}},       \tag{12}
\]
which proves equation~\((1)\) and its least-constant assertion.

Choose \(d_{\mathrm{sel}}\) to minimize \(w_d\) over the treatment-aware prediction minimizers \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}^A(P)\), and choose \(d_*\) to minimize \(w_d\) over all of \(\mathcal D\).  Then
\[
 r_{d_{\mathrm{sel}}}\le r_{d_*},
 \qquad
 1\le\frac{w_{d_{\mathrm{sel}}}}{w_{d_*}}
 \le \max_{(d,d')\in\mathcal O_P^A}\frac{w_d}{w_{d'}}.  \tag{13}
\]
For \(w_d=\mathcal V_d^*(P)\), the middle ratio in equation~\((13)\) is exactly \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^A(P)\) by Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}predictive\text{-}benchmark}\).  For \(w_d=\mathcal V_{d,\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P)\), it is exactly \(\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{A,\mathrm{IKM}}(P)\) by Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}ikmw\text{-}normalized\text{-}design\text{-}value}\).  Thus each named ratio is finite at the fixed law and is bounded by equation~\((1)\).  Over a nonempty law class, any finite uniform constant bounds the corresponding exact ratio; conversely, the finite supremum of those ratios is itself a uniform constant.  This proves the stated uniform equivalence.

For the numerical envelope, define
\[
 s_d(x)=\frac{F_d(x)}{Q_{R,d}^A(x)}.
\]
Positivity of the prediction form on the chosen nonzero cone makes the denominator positive.  The definitions of \(\Lambda_-^A(P)\) and \(\Lambda_+^A(P)\) therefore imply
\[
 \Lambda_-^A(P)Q_{R,d}^A(x)
 \le F_d(x)
 \le\Lambda_+^A(P)Q_{R,d}^A(x)                          \tag{14}
\]
for every represented \(d\) and every cone direction \(x\).  Evaluating equation~\((14)\) at \(x_d(P)\) gives
\[
 \Lambda_-^A(P)R_d^A(P)
 \le\mathcal V_d^*(P)
 \le\Lambda_+^A(P)R_d^A(P).                              \tag{15}
\]
If \(R_d^A(P)\le R_{d'}^A(P)\), then equation~\((15)\) yields
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_d^*(P)}{\mathcal V_{d'}^*(P)}
 \le
 \frac{\Lambda_+^A(P)R_d^A(P)}
      {\Lambda_-^A(P)R_{d'}^A(P)}
 \le\frac{\Lambda_+^A(P)}{\Lambda_-^A(P)}.              \tag{16}
\]
Taking the maximum over the treatment-aware order relation and using equation~\((13)\) proves equation~\((2)\).

Finally, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\text{-}predictive\text{-}operator\text{-}certificate}\) proves algebraically that profiling the two source-score quadratic forms produces
\[
 F_d(x)=\inf_{0<\alpha<1}
 \left\{
 \frac{c_{E,d}Q_{E,d}(x)}\alpha+
 \frac{c_{O,d}Q_{O,d}(x)}{1-\alpha}
 \right\},                                                \tag{17}
\]
which is not a quadratic form in general.  Its explicit parallelogram-law counterexample applies unchanged after replacing the prediction denominator by \(Q_{R,d}^A\).  Moreover, equality in equation~\((16)\) requires simultaneous attainment of the upper and lower envelope endpoints by law-realized directions and saturation of the treatment-aware risk order.  A largest generalized eigenvalue at fixed \(\alpha\) supplies only an upper full-space quadratic envelope and does not supply these conditions.  Hence the two-sided certificate can be conservative and the sharp treatment-aware selection constant is not generally a largest generalized eigenvalue. \(\square\)
