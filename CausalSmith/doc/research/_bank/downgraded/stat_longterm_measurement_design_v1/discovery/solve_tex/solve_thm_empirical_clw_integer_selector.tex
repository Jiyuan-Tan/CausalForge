 r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =1-(x,y)

 &=1-\frac12\left\{

Fix a binary center design with signals \(x,y\in(0,1)\), and write \(r=W_x\), \(s=W_y\), and \(t=T\).  Under the observational law, direct summation over the Rademacher variable \(U\) gives
\[
 P(T=t,W_x=r,W_y=s\mid G=O)
 =\frac18\left\{1+xyrs+\frac t4(xr+ys)\right\},           \tag{40}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E(U\mid T=t,W_x=r,W_y=s,G=O)
 =\frac{t/4+xr+ys+(t/4)xyrs}
 {1+xyrs+(t/4)(xr+ys)}.                                   \tag{41}
\]
Because \(Y=U\), the treatment-aware squared-error risk is the expectation of the conditional variance \(1-\mathbb E(U\mid T,W_x,W_y,G=O)^2\).

To sum the eight cells, set \(q=rs\), \(v=tr\),
\[
 a_q=1+xyq,
 \qquad b_q=x+yq.
\]
For each \((q,v)\in\{-1,1\}^2\), the denominator in (41) is \(a_q+vb_q/4\), while the numerator, up to the irrelevant sign \(r\), is \(b_q+va_q/4\).  The elementary identity
\[
 (a_q+vb_q/4)^2-(b_q+va_q/4)^2
 =\frac{15}{16}(a_q^2-b_q^2)                              \tag{42}
\]
and summation first over \(v\) therefore give
\[
 R_d^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{15}{2}\sum_{q\in\{-1,1\}}
 \frac{a_q(a_q^2-b_q^2)}{16a_q^2-b_q^2}.                 \tag{43}
\]
Now
\[
 a_q^2-b_q^2=(1-x^2)(1-y^2)
\]
for both signs of \(q\).  Placing the two terms in (43) over their common denominator and simplifying yields
\[
 R_d^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 =\frac{15(1-x^2)(1-y^2)(16-x^2-y^2-14x^2y^2)}
 {\{16(1+xy)^2-(x+y)^2\}\{16(1-xy)^2-(x-y)^2\}},
\]
which is (1).  Every denominator is positive because \(16a_q^2-b_q^2>15a_q^2>0\), where \(|b_q|<a_q\) follows from \(a_q^2-b_q^2=(1-x^2)(1-y^2)>0\).

Substitution of \(x=y=1/2\) gives
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})=\frac{585}{1024}.
\]
For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), conditioning on the additional variable \(A\) cannot increase the optimal \(L_2\) prediction error, so
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 \le R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon}).
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) supplies
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon})
 <\frac3{10\chi}<\frac3{10}<\frac{585}{1024},
\]
so both unrestricted prediction selectors are the singleton \(\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\).  Since their selected design is identical, their best-tie arm-normalized ratios are identical, and the same is true for their IKM-normalized ratios.  The exact ratio, lower bound, optimizing scale, and second-order expansion now transfer verbatim from the sharp-elbow lemma.

%%% FIELD joint-predictive-binary-stability-proof
Fix \((\chi,\epsilon)\).  On the relative interior of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bin}}(\chi)\), every observed cell of \((A,X,Z_{M_d},Y)\) has positive probability.  Hence
\[
 R_d^A(P)=
 \mathbb E_P\{\operatorname{Var}_P(Y\mid A,X,Z_{M_d},G=O)\mid G=O\}
\]
is a finite sum of cell probabilities and conditional-moment ratios with positive denominators, and is continuous in the complete latent-cell vector.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}treatment\text{-}aware\text{-}sharp\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) gives a strict center gap
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}^A(P_\epsilon)<R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A(P_\epsilon).
\]
Thus a relative neighborhood of the center preserves \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{pred}}^A(P)=\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\).

The arm-normalized and IKM-normalized profiles are continuous and strictly positive on the regular balls already supplied by Lemmas~\(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}witness\text{-}stability}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}ikmw\text{-}normalized\text{-}stability}\).  Therefore both treatment-aware best-tie ratios are continuous there.  Since each center ratio is positive, further relative neighborhoods satisfy
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^A(P)
 >\frac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^A(P_\epsilon),
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{A,\mathrm{IKM}}(P)
 >\frac12\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}^{A,\mathrm{IKM}}(P_\epsilon),
\]
respectively.  Intersecting these finitely many neighborhoods with the two pre-existing balls, and replacing each radius by the minimum of the corresponding positive radii, loses none of the earlier conclusions and proves all assertions.

%%% FIELD two-rule-predictive-impossibility-proof
Fix \(\chi>1\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}witness\text{-}stability}\) permits the radii in \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}\) to preserve regularity, CLW admissibility, common identification, the named CLW selector, raw-risk selector, and arm-normalized optimizer in (1).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}predictive\text{-}binary\text{-}stability}\) decreases the same radii without losing those conclusions and adds the treatment-aware selector identity.  The integer conclusion follows from the threshold established in the binary-stability lemma.

Every center belongs to its defining ball.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) proves equality of the named ratios and unadjusted generic ratios, and Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}treatment\text{-}aware\text{-}sharp\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) adds equality of both treatment-aware ratios.  Their common center value exceeds \((\epsilon^{-2}+14)/726\).  Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves the three arm-normalized suprema in (2).  On \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{bin}}^{\mathrm{IKM}}\), the IKM and joint stability lemmas preserve the strict IKM optimizer and more than one half of each diverging center ratio, proving (3).

For fixed \(\chi\), the exact common center formula gives (4), and
\[
 \frac{5\chi+1}{363(10\chi-3)}-\frac1{726}
 =\frac5{726(10\chi-3)}>0.                                \tag{44}
\]
The rescaled optimization in the sharp-elbow lemma proves (5)--(6); equality of all six center ratios transfers these conclusions to each envelope.  The construction uses the transported CLW population affine criterion only for the named conclusion.  The two unrestricted risks are distinct secondary objects, and none of the broader excluded procedures enters this proof.

%%% FIELD two-rule-finite-catalogue-predictive-guarantee-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{CLW}}\) and \(B>0\).  The moment and full-Gram conditions make every \(r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P)\), and hence the continuous named criterion, finite.  Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}budget\text{-}profile}\) and Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate\text{-}components}\) imply \(0<\mathcal V_d^*(P)<\infty\).  Source-arm positivity gives \(0<(1-p_{g,1})^2<1\), so the IKM profiles are also finite and positive.  The finite named selector is nonempty.  Substitution of the profile formulas gives (1)--(2).  Each numerator minimizes over a nonempty subset of the denominator's index set, proving that each ratio is finite and at least one.  Division by the positive denominator shows that the smallest constant in each actual best-tie inequality is the displayed ratio.

For either positive profile \(w_d\), set
\[
 C_{\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{CLW}}(P;B)
 =\max_{\substack{d,d'\in\mathcal D:\,
 \Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d}(P;B)
 \le\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d'}(P;B)}}\frac{w_d}{w_{d'}}.       \tag{45}
\]
Every constant satisfying all named-rule order implications must dominate each indexed ratio; conversely, (45) dominates every such pair.  This proves (3).  On the domain where every compared integer count is positive, the identical finite argument with \(\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW}}^{\mathrm{int}}\) proves the exact integer constant.  Computing the criteria and minima takes \(O(|\mathcal D|)\) comparisons, and enumerating every ordered pair takes at most \(O(|\mathcal D|^2)\).

Now fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  The unadjusted identity is Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}catalogue\text{-}predictive\text{-}guarantee}\).  Direct substitution into the definitions proves the treatment-aware identity in (4) and both IKM analogues.  Repeating (45) with the ordering criteria \(R_d(P)\) or \(R_d^A(P)\) proves their exact pairwise maxima without imposing the narrower CLW domain.

Finally, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}two\text{-}rule\text{-}predictive\text{-}impossibility}\) proves unbounded ratios for the named continuous rule and both generic rules on the jointly shrunk regions.  Lemmas~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}treatment\text{-}aware\text{-}sharp\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) prove equality of all six center ratios and the common expansion (5).  Hence the finite enumerations are exact and computable law by law but furnish no law-uniform guarantee.

%%% FIELD empirical-treatment-aware-predictive-selector-proof
Let \(m_O\) be the number of observational pilot records.  At every law on the triangular center path, \(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m}(G=O)=1/2\), and hence
\[
 \frac{m_O}{m}=\frac12+O_P(m^{-1/2}),
 \qquad P(m_O<m/4)\longrightarrow0.                       \tag{46}
\]
Conditional on the source labels, the ordered observational records are independent and identically distributed from the observational source law, and the deterministic training and validation halves are independent.  For fixed \(\chi\), all cells of \((A,X,Z_{M_d})\) under both designs have probabilities bounded below by a positive constant uniformly over \(0<\epsilon_m<10^{-3}\).  This follows from the strictly positive treatment kernels, the fixed signal \(y_\chi\in(0,1)\), the two fixed one-half signals, and the fact that the weak signal lies in \((0,10^{-3})\).  The number of designs and cells is fixed, and \(|Y|=1\).

Let
\[
 f_d^A(v)=\mathbb E_{P_{\chi,\epsilon_m}}(Y\mid A,X,Z_{M_d}=v,G=O)
\]
be the oracle finite-cell regression.  Binomial concentration for the training cell counts, followed by Chebyshev's inequality for each bounded cell mean, gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max_v
 |\widehat f_{d,m}^A(v)-f_d^A(v)|=O_P(m^{-1/2}).            \tag{47}
\]
The zero-denominator convention is harmless because the probability of any empty training cell tends to zero exponentially, uniformly over the finitely many cells.

Conditional on the training half, every validation loss is bounded by \(4\).  The conditional variance bound, (46), and a finite union bound give
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left|
 \widehat R_{d,m}^A-
 \mathbb E\{(Y-\widehat f_{d,m}^A(A,X,Z_{M_d}))^2
 \mid\text{training},G=O\}
 \right|=O_P(m^{-1/2}).                                   \tag{48}
\]
The conditional-mean projection identity gives, design by design,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E\{(Y-\widehat f_{d,m}^A(A,X,Z_{M_d}))^2
 \mid\text{training},G=O\}\\
 &\qquad=R_d^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})
 +\mathbb E\{(\widehat f_{d,m}^A-f_d^A)^2
 \mid\text{training},G=O\}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{49}
\]
The last term is \(O_P(m^{-1})\) uniformly in \(d\) by (47).  Equations (48)--(49) prove (1).

The center risk gap is bounded away from zero uniformly in \(m\).  Indeed, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}treatment\text{-}aware\text{-}sharp\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) gives
\[
 R_{d_{\mathrm e}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})
 =\frac{585}{1024},
 \qquad
 R_{d_{\mathrm p}}^A(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})
 <\frac3{10\chi}<\frac3{10}.
\]
Thus the gap exceeds \(585/1024-3/10>0\), and (1) implies
\[
 P_{\chi,\epsilon_m}\{\widehat d_m^A=d_{\mathrm p}\}\longrightarrow1,
\]
regardless of the deterministic rate at which \(\epsilon_m\downarrow0\).

On this selection event, each achieved ratio equals its population center ratio at \(d_{\mathrm p}\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) gives, under both normalizations,
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 {\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 =\frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p},\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 {\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e},\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 >\frac{\epsilon_m^{-2}+14}{726}\longrightarrow\infty.  \tag{50}
\]
For any finite threshold, (50) eventually exceeds it and the selection event has probability tending to one.  This proves both convergences in (3).  Independence of the pilot and main study is exactly Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}independent\text{-}pilot}\); the construction itself uses no bridge learner or growing sieve.

%%% FIELD exact-integer-binary-stability-proof
At a center, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) gives
\[
 C_{d_{\mathrm p}}=5\chi,
 \qquad C_{d_{\mathrm e}}=5,
 \qquad
 r_{d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_\epsilon)<\frac3{10\chi},
 \qquad
 r_{d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_\epsilon)=\frac35.
\]
Consequently
\[
 2C_{d_{\mathrm p}}r_{d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_\epsilon)
 <3=C_{d_{\mathrm e}}r_{d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_\epsilon). \tag{51}
\]

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}witness\text{-}stability}\) supplies a relative finite-cell ball on which regularity, CLW admissibility, common identification, the continuous named ordering, the raw-risk ordering, the arm-normalized optimizer ordering, and their one-half ratio bounds hold.  Its proof expresses
\[
 r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}(P)=
 \mathbb E_P(Y^2\mid G=O)-g_d(P)^\top\Gamma_d(P)^{-1}g_d(P),
\]
where all moments are continuous functions of the positive cell vector and the full Gram determinants stay nonzero.  Thus both sides of (51) are continuous.  Shrinking the ball preserves the strict margin (51).  Intersecting with the smaller ball allowed by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}predictive\text{-}binary\text{-}stability}\) loses none of these properties.

For every \(B\ge C_{d_{\mathrm p}}=5\chi\), set \(z_d=B/C_d\).  Then \(z_d\ge1\), and the elementary floor inequalities \(z/\lfloor z\rfloor<2\) and \(z/\lfloor z\rfloor\ge1\) give
\[
 \frac{B}{\lfloor B/C_{d_{\mathrm p}}\rfloor}<2C_{d_{\mathrm p}},
 \qquad
 \frac{B}{\lfloor B/C_{d_{\mathrm e}}\rfloor}\ge C_{d_{\mathrm e}}. \tag{52}
\]
Multiplying by the two positive residuals and using the preserved margin yields
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{int}}(P;B)
 <B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{int}}(P;B).
\]
Because \(\mathcal D=\{d_{\mathrm p},d_{\mathrm e}\}\), the exact integer selector is \(\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\).  The continuous selector is the same singleton throughout the ball, so the two arm-normalized best-tie ratios are identical.  The inherited one-half bound proves (2).

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}ikmw\text{-}normalized\text{-}stability}\) supplies a further ball on which \(D_{\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P)=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}\) and the continuous IKM ratio exceeds half its center value.  Intersect it with the preceding ball and the joint-prediction ball.  The integer and continuous named selectors are again both \(\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\), so their IKM ratio numerators coincide and (3) follows.  At the center, the same floor calculation applies directly, proving the last assertion.

%%% FIELD exact-integer-clw-predictive-failure-proof
The center residual and cost formulas in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\), combined with the floor inequalities (52), give
\[
 B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{int}}<3
 \le B\Psi_{\mathrm{CLW},d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{int}}
\]
for every \(B\ge5\chi\).  Hence the integer selector is \(\{d_{\mathrm p}\}\).  The same lemma gives \(D^*=D_{\mathrm{IKM}}^*=\{d_{\mathrm e}\}\), equality of the continuous named and unadjusted generic ratios, and formula (1).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}treatment\text{-}aware\text{-}sharp\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) adds equality of the treatment-aware ratios.  Because the integer and continuous named selectors are the same singleton, the two integer ratios equal their continuous analogues.  This proves all eight equalities.  Dropping the first nonnegative square-root term in (1) gives the stated lower bound, and expansion at fixed \(\chi\) gives (2).

Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}region}\) chooses the radii after Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}exact\text{-}integer\text{-}binary\text{-}stability}\).  Hence for each center ball and every \(B\ge5\chi\), the integer ratio equals the continuous named ratio and exceeds half the corresponding center value.  Every center belongs to its ball, while the center lower bound diverges as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\); this proves the first equality in (3).  The IKM region and conclusion (3) of the stability lemma prove the second in the same way.

For every feasible pair \((\chi,B)\), the integer ratio equals \(\rho_\chi(\epsilon)\) and is independent of \(B\).  Conversely, for every \(\chi>1\), the feasible set of budgets is nonempty, for example at \(B=5\chi\).  Therefore taking the infimum over all jointly feasible pairs gives exactly
\[
 \inf_{\substack{\chi>1\\B\ge5\chi}}
 \rho_{\mathrm{CLW}}^{\mathrm{int}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon};B)
 =\inf_{\chi>1}\rho_\chi(\epsilon),
\]
and the identical argument applies to the IKM-normalized ratio.  The rescaled optimization in the sharp-elbow lemma then gives (5) and the cube-root minimizing scale.  The final budget-schedule qualification follows because feasibility of the minimizing center is exactly \(B_\epsilon\ge5\chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)\).

%%% FIELD empirical-clw-integer-selector-proof
Let \(m_O\) denote the number of observational pilot records.  At every center \(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m}(G=O)=1/2\), so
\[
 \frac{m_O}{m}=\frac12+O_P(m^{-1/2}),
 \qquad P(m_O<m/4)\longrightarrow0.                       \tag{53}
\]
Every coordinate product in \(Y^2\), \(\xi_dY\), or \(\xi_d\xi_d^\top\) is bounded uniformly on the binary family.  Expressing an observational average as the ratio of its \(m^{-1}\)-scaled source-weighted sum to \(m_O/m\), Chebyshev's inequality and (53) give, uniformly over the two designs and finitely many coordinates,
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\left(
 |\widehat s_{d,m}-s_d|
 +\|\widehat g_{d,m}-g_d\|
 +\|\widehat\Gamma_{d,m}-\Gamma_d\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \right)=O_P(m^{-1/2}),                                   \tag{54}
\]
where these empirical moments are proof intermediates obtained by expanding the quadratic in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}clw\text{-}empirical\text{-}linear\text{-}cost\text{-}selector}\), and the population moments are evaluated at \(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m}\).

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}sharp\text{-}uniform\text{-}binary\text{-}elbow}\) gives Gram eigenvalues \(1,1\pm xy\).  For \(d_{\mathrm p}\), \(xy=\epsilon_my_\chi<10^{-3}\), and for \(d_{\mathrm e}\), \(xy=1/4\).  Hence
\[
 \min_{d\in\mathcal D}\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma_d)\ge\frac34. \tag{55}
\]
Equations (54)--(55) imply that, with probability tending to one, every empirical Gram is nonsingular with smallest eigenvalue at least \(3/8\).  On this event,
\[
 \widehat r_{d,m}^{\mathrm{lin}}
 =\widehat s_{d,m}-\widehat g_{d,m}^\top
 \widehat\Gamma_{d,m}^{-1}\widehat g_{d,m},
 \qquad
 r_d^{\mathrm{lin}}=s_d-g_d^\top\Gamma_d^{-1}g_d.          \tag{56}
\]
The inverse identity
\[
 \widehat\Gamma_{d,m}^{-1}-\Gamma_d^{-1}
 =\widehat\Gamma_{d,m}^{-1}
 (\Gamma_d-\widehat\Gamma_{d,m})\Gamma_d^{-1}             \tag{57}
\]
and the uniform inverse bounds from (55) show that the difference in (56) is \(O_P(m^{-1/2})\), uniformly in \(d\).  On the exceptional singular event the minimized residual remains in \([0,1]\), since the zero coefficient is feasible and \(Y^2=1\); the exceptional probability tends to zero, so it does not alter the \(O_P(m^{-1/2})\) conclusion.  This proves (1).

Fix \(B\ge5\chi\).  Both floor counts are positive.  Multiplication of (1) by the fixed constants \(C_d/B\) proves the continuous criterion rate, and division by the fixed positive integers \(\lfloor B/C_d\rfloor\) proves (2).

It remains to show that the population gaps do not collapse as \(\epsilon_m\downarrow0\).  The center formulas give
\[
 C_{d_{\mathrm p}}r_{d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})<\frac32,
 \qquad
 C_{d_{\mathrm e}}r_{d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})=3, \tag{58}
\]
so the continuous population gap is bounded below by \(3/(2B)\).  For the integer criterion, set
\[
 k_{\mathrm p}=\left\lfloor\frac{B}{5\chi}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 k_{\mathrm e}=\left\lfloor\frac B5\right\rfloor.
\]
Both integers are positive.  If \(z=B/(5\chi)\), then \(z<2\lfloor z\rfloor=2k_{\mathrm p}\), so
\[
 k_{\mathrm e}\le\chi z<2\chi k_{\mathrm p}.
\]
Consequently the fixed number
\[
 \delta_{\chi,B}
 =\frac{3}{5k_{\mathrm e}}-
 \frac{3}{10\chi k_{\mathrm p}}>0.                        \tag{59}
\]
Since
\[
 r_{d_{\mathrm p}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})
 <\frac3{10\chi},
 \qquad
 r_{d_{\mathrm e}}^{\mathrm{lin}}(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})=\frac35,
\]
the exact integer criterion gap exceeds \(\delta_{\chi,B}\) for every \(m\).  The criterion consistency and the two fixed positive gaps imply
\[
 P_{\chi,\epsilon_m}\!\left\{
 \widehat d_{\mathrm{CLW},m}(B)
 =\widehat d_{\mathrm{CLW},m}^{\mathrm{int}}(B)
 =d_{\mathrm p}\right\}\longrightarrow1.                \tag{60}
\]
At \(B=5\chi\), \(k_{\mathrm p}=1\) and (59) remains strict, so the boundary is included.

On the event in (60), each achieved empirical ratio equals the corresponding population profile ratio at \(d_{\mathrm p}\).  Balanced treatment makes the IKM profile one quarter of the arm-normalized profile design by design, so the ratios agree, and the sharp-elbow lower bound gives
\[
 \frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 {\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 =\frac{\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm p},\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 {\mathcal V_{d_{\mathrm e},\mathrm{IKM}}^*(P_{\chi,\epsilon_m})}
 >\frac{\epsilon_m^{-2}+14}{726}\longrightarrow\infty.  \tag{61}
\]
For every finite threshold, (61) eventually exceeds it and the event in (60) has probability tending to one.  This proves (4).  No relation between \(m\) and \(\epsilon_m\) was used.  Each \(\epsilon_m\) is strictly positive, so every law on the path is regular; \(\epsilon=0\) is only a singular limit.  No step uses OGA or a bridge learner.
