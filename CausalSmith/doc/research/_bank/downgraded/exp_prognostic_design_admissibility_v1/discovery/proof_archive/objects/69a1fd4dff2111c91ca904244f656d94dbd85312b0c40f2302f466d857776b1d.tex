\paragraph{Full score space.}
Suppose first that \(S=H_n\).  If \(u\in H_n\) and \(\|u\|_2=1\), then \(u^\top P_Su=1\), so the score-quality constraint is automatic for every \(\rho\in[0,1]\).  Hence
\[
q_\rho(A;H_n)=\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n).
\tag{1}
\]
Every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) is positive semidefinite, annihilates \(\mathbf 1_n\), has unit diagonal, and has trace \(n\).  Since \(H_n\) has dimension \(n-1\),
\[
\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n)
\geq\frac{\operatorname{tr}(A\mid H_n)}{n-1}
=\frac{n}{n-1}=c_n.
\tag{2}
\]
Complete randomization has covariance \(c_nP_H\), so \((1)\)--\((2)\) and the definition of \(v^*\) give
\[
v^*(\rho;H_n)=c_n
\quad\text{for every }\rho\in[0,1].
\tag{3}
\]
After multiplication by \(4M/n\), this is \(4M/(n-1)=V_{\mathrm{CRD}}(\rho;H_n)\).  Thus no \(\rho<1\) yields strict improvement, and \(H_n\notin\mathfrak A_n\).

\paragraph{Pair-space specialization and the four-unit law.}
The partition frontier lemma, specialized to homogeneous blocks of size two, gives for every even \(n\geq4\)
\[
v^*(\rho;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\min\left\{\frac{n}{n-1},2(1-\rho^2)\right\},
\qquad
\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0,
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
\gamma(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)},
\qquad
\rho^*(S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}.
\tag{5}
\]
It also identifies complete randomization and independent fair within-pair orientations as the two attaining endpoint designs.  For \(n=4\), equations \((4)\)--\((5)\), together with \(S_4=S_{\mathrm{pair}}\), become
\[
v^*(\rho;S_4)=\min\left\{\frac43,2(1-\rho^2)\right\},
\quad
\kappa(S_4)=0,
\quad
\gamma(S_4)=\frac13,
\quad
\rho^*(S_4)=\frac1{\sqrt3}.
\tag{6}
\]
The two branches meet exactly when \(\rho^2=1/3\); hence complete randomization attains the minimum for \(\rho\leq1/\sqrt3\), and the within-pair design attains it for \(\rho\geq1/\sqrt3\).

To identify the displayed law, put
\[
a=(1,-1,1,-1)^\top,
\qquad
b=(1,-1,-1,1)^\top.
\]
The support of \(d_{\mathrm{pair},4}\) is \(\{a,-a,b,-b\}\), with probability \(1/4\) on each point.  It is balanced and sign symmetric, so it belongs to \(\mathcal D_4\).  Direct averaging gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{d_{\mathrm{pair},4}}[zz^\top]
&=\frac12aa^\top+\frac12bb^\top\\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&0&0\\
-1&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&-1\\
0&0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}
=A_{\mathrm{pair},4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
These are precisely the four outcomes of two independent fair pair orientations.  Thus the second branch in \((6)\) is attained by the displayed law.

For general even \(n\), equation \((4)\) gives \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0<c_n\); the admissibility-frontier theorem therefore gives \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\in\mathfrak A_n\).  Finally, at \(n=8\), \(M=1\), and \(\rho=1\),
\[
V^*(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\frac{4M}{n}v^*(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{CRD}}(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\frac{4M}{n-1}=\frac47.
\tag{8}
\]
This proves all assertions.  \(\square\)
