Let \(T=H_n\cap S_{\mathrm{pair}}^\perp\).  The pair-score space consists of centered vectors constant within each of the \(k=n/2\) pairs, so \(\dim(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=k-1\).  Its orthogonal complement in \(H_n\) consists of the within-pair contrasts, so \(\dim(T)=k\) and
\[
 H_n=S_{\mathrm{pair}}\mathbin\oplus T,
 \qquad P_T=P_H-P_S.
\]
We first derive the oracle formula from the full covariance hull.  Let \(G\) be the finite group generated by permutations of the \(k\) pairs and by independent swaps of the two units within each pair.  Every \(g\in G\) is represented by a permutation matrix, maps \(\mathcal Z_n\) onto itself, and preserves both \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\) and \(T\).  Hence \(gAg^\top\in\mathcal C_n\) whenever \(A\in\mathcal C_n\), and
\[
 q_{\sqrt{s}}(gAg^\top;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =q_{\sqrt{s}}(A;S_{\mathrm{pair}}).
\]
Since \(q_{\sqrt{s}}(\,\cdot\,;S_{\mathrm{pair}})\) is a maximum of linear functionals, it is convex.  Therefore the group average
\[
 \overline A=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}gAg^\top\in\mathcal C_n
\]
satisfies
\[
 q_{\sqrt{s}}(\overline A;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 \leq q_{\sqrt{s}}(A;S_{\mathrm{pair}}).
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus the minimization defining \(v^*(\sqrt{s};S_{\mathrm{pair}})\) may be restricted, without loss, to \(G\)-invariant covariances.

We now derive, rather than assume, the two-eigenspace form.  Choose the normalized within-pair contrast basis of \(T\).  Swapping only pair \(j\) changes the sign of its \(j\)-th contrast basis vector and fixes \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\) and all other contrast basis vectors.  Invariance under these independent sign changes forces every \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\)-to-\(T\) block and every off-diagonal entry of the \(T\)-block to vanish.  Pair permutations then force all diagonal entries of the \(T\)-block to be equal, say \(b\).  On the pair-constant coordinates, invariance under every pair permutation forces the representing \(k\)-by-\(k\) matrix to have one common diagonal and one common off-diagonal.  Restricted to the centered pair-constant space, it is therefore a scalar multiple \(aI\).  Consequently
\[
 \overline A=aP_S+bP_T,
 \qquad a\geq0,\quad b\geq0.
 \tag{5}
\]
Every cut-hull covariance has trace \(n\), while the two subspaces have dimensions \(k-1\) and \(k\).  Thus
\[
 (k-1)a+kb=n.
 \tag{6}
\]
For a unit vector with score energy \(t\), (5) gives quadratic form \(at+b(1-t)\).  If \(a\geq b\), its maximum over \(t\in[s,1]\) is \(a\geq c_n\), where the last inequality follows from (6).  If \(a\leq b\), then (6) gives
\[
 b=2-\frac{k-1}{k}a,
\]
and the maximum over \(t\in[s,1]\) is
\[
 as+b(1-s)
 =2(1-s)+a\left\{s-\frac{k-1}{k}(1-s)\right\}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The coefficient of \(a\) in (7) is nonpositive exactly when
\[
 s\leq\frac{k-1}{2k-1}=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}=\alpha_n.
\]
Within the region \(a\leq b\), equations (6) and nonnegativity give \(0\leq a\leq c_n\).  Hence the relaxed minimum permitted by (5)--(6) is attained at \(a=c_n\), \(b=c_n\) when \(s\leq\alpha_n\), and at \(a=0\), \(b=2\) when \(s\geq\alpha_n\).  Both endpoints are feasible covariances: the first is complete randomization and the second is the within-pair design constructed below.  Combining this fact with (4) proves the full-hull identity
\[
 v^*(\rho;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =\min\{c_n,2(1-\rho^2)\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
At \(\rho=1\), (8) gives \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0\).  The strict-improvement set in the squared-quality coordinate is, again by (8), exactly \((\alpha_n,1]\).  The admissibility-frontier theorem identifies that set as \((\gamma(S_{\mathrm{pair}}),1]\), and therefore
\[
 \gamma(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\alpha_n,
 \qquad
 \rho^*(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\sqrt{\alpha_n}.
\]

Under independent fair within-pair orientations, the assignment on pair \(j\) is \((\varepsilon_j,-\varepsilon_j)\), where the \(\varepsilon_j\)'s are independent symmetric signs.  Its covariance has diagonal blocks
\(\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\) and zero off-diagonal blocks; hence it equals \(2P_T\).  It is a balanced mean-zero design.  Mixing it with complete randomization with weight \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) gives
\[
 A_\alpha=\alpha c_nP_H+(1-\alpha)2P_T\in\mathcal C_n.
\]
On \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\), this operator has eigenvalue \(a_\alpha=\alpha c_n\), while on \(T\) it has eigenvalue
\(b_\alpha=\alpha c_n+2(1-\alpha)\).  If a unit vector has score energy \(t\), its quadratic risk is
\[
 a_\alpha t+b_\alpha(1-t).
\]
Since \(b_\alpha\geq a_\alpha\), the maximum over \(t\geq s\), where \(s=\rho^2\), occurs at \(t=s\).  Both summands have positive dimension for \(n\geq4\), so every \(s\in[0,1]\) is attainable.  Therefore
\[
 q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_\alpha;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =\alpha c_n+2(1-\alpha)(1-s).
 \tag{9}
\]

Now set
\[
 \alpha=\alpha_n=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}
 =1-\frac{c_n}{2}.
\]
The oracle formula switches branches at \(s=\alpha_n\): it equals \(c_n\) for \(s\leq\alpha_n\) and \(2(1-s)\) for \(s\geq\alpha_n\).  Because \(2(1-\alpha_n)=c_n\), equation (9) gives, on the first branch,
\[
 q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_{\alpha_n};S_{\mathrm{pair}})-v^*(\sqrt{s};S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =c_n(\alpha_n-s),
 \qquad 0\leq s\leq\alpha_n,
\]
whose maximum is \(c_n\alpha_n\) at \(s=0\).  On the second branch it gives
\[
 q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_{\alpha_n};S_{\mathrm{pair}})-v^*(\sqrt{s};S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =\alpha_nc_n-(2-c_n)(1-s),
 \qquad \alpha_n\leq s\leq1,
\]
whose maximum is \(c_n\alpha_n\) at \(s=1\).  Thus the two endpoint constraints bind jointly and
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(A_{\alpha_n};S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =c_n\alpha_n
 =\frac{n(n-2)}{2(n-1)^2}.
 \tag{10}
\]

For the matching converse, take an arbitrary \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) and define
\[
 L=\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n),
 \qquad K=\lambda_{\max}(P_SAP_S\mid S_{\mathrm{pair}}).
\]
At \(s=0\), the regret is \(L-c_n\); at \(s=1\), it is \(K\), since the pair-space oracle value is zero there.  Hence
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(A;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 \geq r:=\max\{L-c_n,K\}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Every cut-hull covariance is positive semidefinite, annihilates \(\mathbf1_n\), and has trace \(n\).  Taking traces along the orthogonal decomposition \(H_n=S_{\mathrm{pair}}\oplus T\), and using the dimensions \(k-1\) and \(k\), yields
\[
 n=\operatorname{tr}(A)
 \leq(k-1)K+kL
 \leq(k-1)r+k(c_n+r)
 =kc_n+(n-1)r.
\]
Therefore
\[
 r\geq\frac{n-kc_n}{n-1}
 =\frac{n(n-2)}{2(n-1)^2}.
\]
Together with (10) and (11), this proves the lower bound against every covariance in the full class \(\mathcal C_n\), not merely against pair-symmetric designs.  Balanced-covariance realizability turns the attaining covariance into an assignment law in \(\mathcal D_n\).  Multiplication by \(4M/n\), as prescribed by \(\text{def:regret-object}\), gives
\[
 R^*(n,S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =\frac{4M}{n}\frac{n(n-2)}{2(n-1)^2}
 =\frac{2M(n-2)}{(n-1)^2}.
\]

When \(n=4\), \(\alpha_4=1/3\).  Complete randomization places weight \(1/3\) on each of the three balanced sign-pairs, whereas the within-pair design places weight \(1/2\) on each of two sign-pairs and zero on the third.  Their mixture therefore has weights
\[
 \left(\frac13\cdot\frac13,\,
 \frac13\cdot\frac13+\frac23\cdot\frac12,\,
 \frac13\cdot\frac13+\frac23\cdot\frac12\right)
 =\left(\frac19,\frac49,\frac49\right),
\]
up to ordering.  Formula (10) gives normalized regret \(4/9\), and the factor \(4M/n=M\) gives \(R^*(4,S_4)=4M/9\).
