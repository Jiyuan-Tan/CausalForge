First take \(S=H_n\).  For every unit \(u\in H_n\), \(u^\top P_Su=1\), so the quality constraint is automatic for every \(\rho\in[0,1]\).  Therefore
\[
q_\rho(A;H_n)=\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n).
\]
Every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) is positive semidefinite, annihilates \(\mathbf1_n\), and has trace \(n\).  Its restriction to the \((n-1)\)-dimensional space \(H_n\) consequently satisfies
\[
\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n)\geq\frac{\operatorname{tr}(A)}{n-1}
=\frac{n}{n-1}=c_n.
\]
Complete randomization has covariance \(A_{\mathrm{CRD}}=c_nP_H\) and attains equality.  Thus \(v^*(\rho;H_n)=c_n\) for every \(\rho\).  After multiplication by \(4M/n\), this is exactly the complete-randomization risk \(4M/(n-1)\), so no \(\rho<1\) yields strict improvement and \(H_n\notin\mathfrak A_n\).

Now apply Lemma \(\text{lem:pair-space-exact-frontier}\).  It proves, over the unrestricted covariance hull rather than merely within a matched-design subclass, that for every even \(n\geq4\),
\[
v^*(\rho;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\min\left\{\frac{n}{n-1},\,2(1-\rho^2)\right\},
\qquad
\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0,
\]
and
\[
\gamma(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)},
\qquad
\rho^*(S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}.
\tag{14}
\]
The attaining covariance is complete randomization below the threshold in \((14)\) and the covariance of independent fair within-pair orientations above it; equality holds for both at the threshold.

Specialize to \(n=4\).  Then \(c_4=4/3\), the squared threshold in \((14)\) is \(1/3\), and \(S_4=S_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  Hence
\[
v^*(\rho;S_4)=\min\left\{\frac43,2(1-\rho^2)\right\},
\qquad
\kappa(S_4)=0,
\qquad
\gamma(S_4)=\frac13,
\qquad
\rho^*(S_4)=\frac1{\sqrt3}.
\]
For the explicit smallest-instance check, set
\[
v_4=\frac12(1,-1,1,-1)^\top,
\qquad
w_4=\frac12(1,-1,-1,1)^\top.
\]
Together with \(u_4\), these vectors form an orthonormal basis of \(H_4\), and the three balanced sign-pairs are represented by \(2u_4\), \(2v_4\), and \(2w_4\).  Complete randomization puts sign-pair weights \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\).  The displayed law \(d_{\mathrm{pair},4}\) puts weights \((0,1/2,1/2)\), because its four assignments are exactly the two signs of \(2v_4\) and the two signs of \(2w_4\).  Direct averaging gives
\[
\mathbb E_{d_{\mathrm{pair},4}}[zz^\top]
=2v_4v_4^\top+2w_4w_4^\top
=\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&0&0\\
-1&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&-1\\
0&0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}
=A_{\mathrm{pair},4}.
\]
Thus enumeration of every balanced assignment verifies the displayed covariance and both branches, including the switching boundary \(\rho=1/\sqrt3\).

Finally, equation \((14)\) gives \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0<c_n\) for every even \(n\geq4\), so the admissibility-frontier theorem yields \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\in\mathfrak A_n\).  At \(n=8\), perfect quality gives \(v^*(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0\).  With \(M=1\), the exact risk scaling and the complete-randomization benchmark are
\[
V^*(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\frac4{8}\,0=0,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{CRD}}(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\frac4{8-1}=\frac47,
\]
as claimed.
