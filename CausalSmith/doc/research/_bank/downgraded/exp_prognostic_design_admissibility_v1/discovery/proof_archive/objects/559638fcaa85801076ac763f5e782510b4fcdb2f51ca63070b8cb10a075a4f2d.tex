Write \(s=\rho^2\) and let
\[
K_s=\{X\succeq0:X=P_HXP_H,\ \operatorname{tr}(X)=1,
\ \operatorname{tr}(P_SX)\geq s\}=\mathcal X_\rho(S).
\]
This set is nonempty and compact: a rank-one projector onto any unit vector in \(S\) belongs to it.  Every feasible unit vector \(u\) in the definition of \(q_\rho(A;S)\) gives the feasible rank-one matrix \(X=uu^\top\), with \(\operatorname{tr}(AX)=u^\top Au\).

We next prove that a linear functional on \(K_s\) has a rank-one maximizer.  A maximizer may be chosen to be an extreme point because \(K_s\) is compact and convex.  Suppose an extreme point \(X\) had rank \(r\geq2\), and let \(R=\operatorname{range}(X)\subseteq H_n\).  The vector space of symmetric operators on \(R\) has dimension \(r(r+1)/2\geq3\).  If the score constraint is active, choose a nonzero symmetric operator \(D\), supported on \(R\), satisfying
\[
\operatorname{tr}(D)=0,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}(P_SD)=0;
\]
such a \(D\) exists because these are at most two homogeneous linear equations.  If the score constraint is inactive, impose only \(\operatorname{tr}(D)=0\).  Since \(X\) is positive definite on \(R\), for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\), both \(X+\epsilon D\) and \(X-\epsilon D\) are positive semidefinite.  They retain the support and trace constraints; in the active case they retain score energy exactly, and in the inactive case feasibility persists by strict slack and continuity.  Hence \(X\) is the midpoint of two distinct feasible matrices, a contradiction.  Every extreme point therefore has rank one.  A rank-one member of \(K_s\) is \(uu^\top\) for a unit \(u\in H_n\) satisfying \(u^\top P_Su\geq s\).  This proves
\[
q_\rho(A;S)=\max_{X\in\mathcal X_\rho(S)}\operatorname{tr}(AX)
\]
and proves the claimed rank-one attainment.

For the scalar dual, attach a multiplier \(\eta\geq0\) to \(\operatorname{tr}(P_SX)-s\geq0\).  Maximizing the Lagrangian over positive semidefinite trace-one operators on \(H_n\) gives
\[
\max_{X\succeq0,\ X=P_HXP_H,\ \operatorname{tr}(X)=1}
\operatorname{tr}\{(A+\eta P_S)X\}-\eta s
=\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_S\mid H_n)-\eta s.
\]
For \(s<1\), there is a positive definite density operator on \(H_n\) whose score trace is strictly larger than \(s\): put sufficiently large mass on \(S\) and a positive amount on its orthogonal complement, with the evident simplification when \(S=H_n\).  Finite-dimensional separating-hyperplane duality therefore has no gap, yielding the displayed infimum over \(\eta\geq0\).

It remains to check the boundary \(s=1\), where strict feasibility is unavailable.  Positive semidefiniteness and
\(\operatorname{tr}(P_SX)=\operatorname{tr}(X)=1\) force \(X=P_SXP_S\), so the primal value is
\(\lambda_{\max}(P_SAP_S\mid S)\).  Relative to the orthogonal decomposition \(H_n=S\oplus(H_n\cap S^\perp)\), the largest eigenvalue of \(A+\eta P_S\), minus \(\eta\), converges as \(\eta\to\infty\) to \(\lambda_{\max}(P_SAP_S\mid S)\).  This follows directly from the Rayleigh quotient: maximizing vectors with a nonvanishing complementary component lose order \(\eta\) after subtraction, while vectors in \(S\) give the compressed Rayleigh quotient.  Weak duality gives the reverse inequality for every finite \(\eta\).  Thus the dual equality also holds at \(s=1\), possibly with its infimum attained only in the limit \(\eta=\infty\).
