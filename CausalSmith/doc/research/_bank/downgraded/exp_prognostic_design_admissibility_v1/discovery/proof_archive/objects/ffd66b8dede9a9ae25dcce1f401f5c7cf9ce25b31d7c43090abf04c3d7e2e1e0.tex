\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(s=\rho^2\), \(d_0=n-1\), \(p=\dim(S)\), and \(\beta=p/d_0\).  Every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) is a convex combination of \(zz^\top\) with \(z\in\mathcal Z_n\), and therefore
\[
A\succeq0,
\qquad
A\mathbf1_n=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{diag}(A)=\mathbf1_n,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(A\mid H_n)=n.
\tag{1}
\]
It follows that \(\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n)\geq c_n=n/d_0\), with equality at \(A_{\mathrm{CRD}}=c_nP_H\).

At \(s=1\), the admissible unit vectors are precisely those in \(S\), so
\[
q_1(A;S)=\lambda_{\max}(P_SAP_S\mid S),
\qquad
v^*(1;S)=\kappa(S).
\tag{2}
\]
If improvement occurs at some \(s<1\), nesting of the adversary shells gives \(\kappa(S)<c_n\).  Conversely, if an optimizer \(A_0\) in the definition of \(\kappa(S)\) satisfies \(q_1(A_0;S)<c_n\), compactness of the unit sphere gives
\[
q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_0;S)\longrightarrow q_1(A_0;S)
\qquad\text{as }s\uparrow1.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, any sequence of maximizing unit vectors has a convergent subsequence, and a limit of vectors whose score energy tends to one lies in \(S\).  Thus \(S\in\mathfrak A_n\) if and only if \(\kappa(S)<c_n\).

By \(\text{thm:cone-lift-dual}\), and because the multiplier-zero value is at least \(c_n\),
\[
v^*(\sqrt{s};S)<c_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
s>\inf_{A\in\mathcal C_n,\,\eta>0}
\frac{\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_S\mid H_n)-c_n}{\eta}
=\gamma(S).
\tag{4}
\]
Taking \(A=c_nP_H\) gives \(\gamma(S)\leq1\).  Hence the improving squared-quality set is \((\gamma(S),1]\) when \(\gamma(S)<1\), and is empty when \(\gamma(S)=1\).  This proves the three-way equivalence and the formula for \(\rho^*(S)\).

We next establish the affine-hull fact needed to identify \(\gamma(S)\) exactly.  Let
\[
\mathcal L=\{B\in\mathbb S^n:\operatorname{diag}(B)=0,\ B\mathbf1_n=0\}.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal C_n)=c_nP_H+\mathcal L,
\qquad
c_nP_H\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal C_n).
\tag{5}
\]
The forward affine inclusion follows from (1).  For the reverse inclusion, suppose that a symmetric matrix \(Q=(q_{ij})\) has \(z^\top Qz\) constant over balanced sign vectors.  The alternating sum obtained by swapping two treated and two control indices yields
\[
q_{ik}-q_{jk}-q_{i\ell}+q_{j\ell}=0
\qquad\text{for all distinct }i,j,k,\ell.
\tag{6}
\]
Choosing three anchors and applying (6) shows that \(q_{ij}=a_i+a_j\) off the diagonal.  Thus \(Q\) differs from \(\mathbf1_na^\top+a\mathbf1_n^\top\) by a diagonal matrix and is orthogonal to \(\mathcal L\).  Equality of the orthogonal complements proves the affine equality in (5).  The uniform average of the distinct balanced matrices \(zz^\top\) is \(c_nP_H\).  Every element of \(\mathcal L\) is a zero-sum linear combination of these matrices by the affine equality, and sufficiently small positive and negative perturbations of their uniform weights remain nonnegative.  This proves the relative-interior assertion.

Define the affine spectral value
\[
g(S)=\inf_{\substack{K\in\mathbb S^n:\ K\mathbf1_n=0,\\
\operatorname{diag}(K)=\operatorname{diag}(P_S)}}
\lambda_{\max}(K\mid H_n).
\tag{7}
\]
For every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) and \(\eta>0\), set
\[
K=P_S+\eta^{-1}(A-c_nP_H).
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (1) and \(\operatorname{diag}(c_nP_H)=\mathbf1_n\) show that \(K\mathbf1_n=0\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(K)=\operatorname{diag}(P_S)\).  Moreover, on \(H_n\),
\[
A+\eta P_S=c_nP_H+\eta K,
\]
so
\[
\frac{\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_S\mid H_n)-c_n}{\eta}
=\lambda_{\max}(K\mid H_n).
\tag{9}
\]
Taking the infimum over \((A,\eta)\) gives \(\gamma(S)\geq g(S)\).

Conversely, let \(K\) satisfy the affine constraints in (7).  Then \(K-P_S\in\mathcal L\), so (5) implies that for every sufficiently small \(t>0\),
\[
A_t=c_nP_H+t(K-P_S)\in\mathcal C_n.
\tag{10}
\]
Using the multiplier \(\eta=t\), we obtain
\[
A_t+tP_S=c_nP_H+tK,
\]
and hence the quotient in the definition of \(\gamma(S)\) equals \(\lambda_{\max}(K\mid H_n)\).  Taking the infimum over affine-feasible \(K\) gives \(\gamma(S)\leq g(S)\).  Together with (9),
\[
\gamma(S)=g(S).
\tag{11}
\]

We now evaluate (7) using a polygon--Gram construction.  For nonnegative numbers \(r_1,\ldots,r_n\), vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) with
\[
\|v_i\|_2=r_i,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n v_i=0
\tag{12}
\]
exist if and only if
\[
2\max_i r_i\leq\sum_{i=1}^n r_i.
\tag{13}
\]
Necessity follows by isolating a longest vector and applying the triangle inequality.  For sufficiency, the set of lengths attainable by a sum of planar vectors with prescribed lengths \(r_1,\ldots,r_k\) is
\[
\left[
\max\left\{0,\,2\max_{i\leq k}r_i-\sum_{i\leq k}r_i\right\},
\ \sum_{i\leq k}r_i
\right].
\tag{14}
\]
This follows by induction: after the first \(k-1\) vectors have resultant length \(x\), adding a vector of length \(r_k\) produces every length in \([|x-r_k|,x+r_k]\); as \(x\) ranges over the preceding interval, these intervals overlap and have the endpoints in (14).  Condition (13) makes the left endpoint zero.  Taking \(D_{ij}=v_i^\top v_j\) shows that, for prescribed \(d_i\geq0\),
\[
D\succeq0,
\qquad
D\mathbf1_n=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{diag}(D)=d
\tag{15}
\]
is possible if and only if
\[
2\max_i\sqrt{d_i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^n\sqrt{d_i}.
\tag{16}
\]
The converse follows from a Gram representation of \(D\), because \(D\mathbf1_n=0\) forces the Gram vectors to sum to zero.

Every affine-feasible \(K\) in (7) has trace \(p\) on the \((n-1)\)-dimensional space \(H_n\), so \(\lambda_{\max}(K\mid H_n)\geq\beta\).  The choice \(K=P_S\) shows that the infimum is at most one.  Fix \(t\in[\beta,1]\).  If an affine-feasible \(K\) satisfies \(\lambda_{\max}(K\mid H_n)\leq t\), then
\[
D=tP_H-K\succeq0,
\qquad
D\mathbf1_n=0,
\qquad
D_{ii}=\frac{n-1}{n}t-\ell_i=d_i(t).
\tag{17}
\]
By (15)--(16), \(t\) belongs to the set defining \(U(\ell)\).  Conversely, if \(t\) belongs to that set, (15)--(16) produce a matrix \(D\) satisfying (17).  Then
\[
K=tP_H-D
\tag{18}
\]
annihilates \(\mathbf1_n\), has diagonal \(\ell\), and obeys \(K\preceq tP_H\), so \(\lambda_{\max}(K\mid H_n)\leq t\).  The defining set for \(U(\ell)\) is closed in the compact interval \([\beta,1]\).  It is nonempty at \(t=1\), because \(P_H-P_S\) itself satisfies (15) with diagonal \(d_i(1)\).  Therefore its infimum is attained.  Equations (17)--(18) prove
\[
g(S)=U(\ell),
\tag{19}
\]
and also prove that the infimum in (7) is a minimum.  Combining (11) and (19) gives both exact equalities in the theorem.

For the sharp leverage lower bound, one may also read the constraint directly from the original design problem.  If
\(lambda=\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_S\mid H_n)\), then positive semidefiniteness and support on \(H_n\) give
\[
A+\eta P_S\preceq\lambda P_H.
\]
Taking diagonal entries and using (1) yields
\[
1+\eta\ell_i\leq\lambda\frac{n-1}{n},
\qquad
\frac{\lambda-c_n}{\eta}\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\ell_i.
\tag{20}
\]
Maximizing over \(i\) and taking the infimum gives
\[
\gamma(S)\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\max_i\ell_i\geq\frac{p}{n-1},
\tag{21}
\]
where the second inequality uses \(\sum_i\ell_i=p\).  If \(\gamma(S)=\beta\), (21) forces every \(\ell_i=p/n\).  Conversely, equal leverage makes \(d_i(\beta)=0\) for every \(i\), so (19) gives \(\gamma(S)=U(\ell)=\beta\).  This proves the equality characterization.

For the four-unit checks, the three leverage vectors are
\[
\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac14,\tfrac14,\tfrac14\right),
\qquad
\left(\tfrac16,\tfrac16,0,\tfrac23\right),
\qquad
\left(\tfrac34,\tfrac1{12},\tfrac1{12},\tfrac1{12}\right).
\tag{22}
\]
For the first, \(t=1/3\) makes every \(d_i(t)=0\).  For the second, diagonal nonnegativity forces \(t\geq8/9\), and at \(t=8/9\) the squared lengths are \((1/2,1/2,2/3,0)\), which satisfy (16).  For the third, diagonal nonnegativity of the first coordinate forces \(t\geq1\).  Formula (19) therefore gives the exact values \(1/3\), \(8/9\), and \(1\).

Finally, (4) and the positive scaling \(V^*=(4M/n)v^*\) give the displayed causal-risk implications.  The leverage vector is computed from any score basis by forming its orthogonal projector.  Formula (19) is a one-dimensional continuous feasibility problem on \([\beta,1]\), so certified bisection or root isolation computes \(\gamma(S)\) and \(\rho^*(S)\) without enumerating \(\mathcal Z_n\).  For a specified quality, \(\text{thm:exact-oracle-saddle}\) supplies the finite semidefinite program and cut-hull weights, and sign symmetrization yields an actual balanced assignment law.  The causal target remains the fixed finite-population SATE; the optimized objects are its design risks. \(\square\)
