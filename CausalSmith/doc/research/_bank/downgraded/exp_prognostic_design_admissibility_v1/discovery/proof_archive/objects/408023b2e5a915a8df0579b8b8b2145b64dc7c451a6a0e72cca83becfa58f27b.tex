\textbf{Proof.}
Suppose first that \(S=H_n\). The score constraint is then automatic for every unit vector in \(H_n\), and hence
\[
q_\rho(A;H_n)=\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n).
\tag{1}
\]
Every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) is positive semidefinite, annihilates \(\mathbf1_n\), and has trace \(n\). Therefore
\[
\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n)\geq\frac{n}{n-1}=c_n.
\tag{2}
\]
Complete randomization has covariance \(c_nP_H\), so \((1)\)--\((2)\) give \(v^*(\rho;H_n)=c_n\) for every \(\rho\), and thus \(H_n\notin\mathfrak A_n\).

For the singleton-versus-rest geometry, put \(w=P_Hr_1\) and \(u_{\mathrm{out}}=w/\|w\|_2\), consistently with the statement. Since
\[
w=r_1-\frac1n\mathbf1_n,
\qquad
\|w\|_2^2=r_1^\top P_Hr_1=1-\frac1n=\frac{n-1}{n},
\tag{3}
\]
every balanced assignment \(z\in\mathcal Z_n\) satisfies
\[
(u_{\mathrm{out}}^\top z)^2
=\frac{(r_1^\top z)^2}{\|w\|_2^2}
=\frac{n}{n-1}=c_n,
\tag{4}
\]
because \(\mathbf1_n^\top z=0\) and \(z_1^2=1\). For any \(A\in\mathcal C_n\), convex averaging of \((4)\) gives
\[
u_{\mathrm{out}}^\top Au_{\mathrm{out}}=c_n.
\tag{5}
\]
The vector \(u_{\mathrm{out}}\) lies in \(S_{\mathrm{out}}\), so it is feasible for every quality level. Thus \((5)\) implies \(q_\rho(A;S_{\mathrm{out}})\geq c_n\) for every \(A\), while complete randomization attains equality. Therefore
\[
v^*(\rho;S_{\mathrm{out}})=c_n
\qquad(0\leq\rho\leq1).
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(S_{\mathrm{out}}\) is one-dimensional, equation \((5)\) also gives \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{out}})=c_n\). The admissibility-frontier theorem then gives \(S_{\mathrm{out}}\notin\mathfrak A_n\) and \(\gamma(S_{\mathrm{out}})=1\).

We next prove the intermediate four-unit calculation by an exact enumeration. Choose the three balanced sign-pair representatives
\[
z^{(1)}=(1,1,-1,-1)^\top,
\quad
z^{(2)}=(1,-1,1,-1)^\top,
\quad
z^{(3)}=(1,-1,-1,1)^\top,
\tag{7}
\]
and put \(e_j=z^{(j)}/2\). Every balanced four-unit assignment is one of \(\pm z^{(1)},\pm z^{(2)},\pm z^{(3)}\), and direct multiplication gives \(e_j^\top e_\ell=\mathbf1\{j=\ell\}\). Hence \((e_1,e_2,e_3)\) is an orthonormal basis of \(H_4\), and every \(A\in\mathcal C_4\) is diagonal in this basis with eigenvalues
\[
\lambda_j=4a_j\geq0,
\qquad
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=4,
\tag{8}
\]
for some \((a_1,a_2,a_3)\) in the probability simplex. The coordinates of \(u_{\mathrm{mid}}\) are
\[
(e_1^\top u_{\mathrm{mid}},e_2^\top u_{\mathrm{mid}},e_3^\top u_{\mathrm{mid}})
=\left(\sqrt{\frac23},\sqrt{\frac16},-\sqrt{\frac16}\right).
\tag{9}
\]
It follows from \((8)\)--\((9)\) that
\[
u_{\mathrm{mid}}^\top Au_{\mathrm{mid}}
=\frac23\lambda_1+\frac16\lambda_2+\frac16\lambda_3
\geq\frac23.
\tag{10}
\]
Equality in \((10)\) is attained, for example, by \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)=(0,4,0)\), which is the feasible covariance \(z^{(2)}z^{(2)\top}\). Since \(S_{\mathrm{mid}}\) is one-dimensional, this proves \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{mid}})=2/3\) with both a lower-bound certificate and an attaining covariance.

It remains to calculate \(\gamma(S_{\mathrm{mid}})\). Reverse the sign of \(e_3\); this preserves every diagonal representation in \((8)\) and changes the coordinate vector in \((9)\) to
\[
c=\frac1{\sqrt6}(2,1,1)^\top.
\tag{11}
\]
Let \(r=3^{-1/2}(1,1,1)^\top\). For every feasible diagonal covariance \(A=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\) in this basis and every \(\eta>0\), the Rayleigh quotient at \(r\) gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_{S_{\mathrm{mid}}}\mid H_4)
\geq r^\top\{A+\eta cc^\top\}r
=\frac{\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3}{3}
+\eta(r^\top c)^2
=\frac43+\frac89\eta.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus every quotient defining \(\gamma(S_{\mathrm{mid}})\) is at least \(8/9\).

For the matching attainment, fix any \(0<\eta\leq3\) and choose the feasible covariance whose eigenvalues in the sign-adjusted cut basis are
\[
\lambda_1=\frac43-\frac49\eta,
\qquad
\lambda_2=\lambda_3=\frac43+\frac29\eta.
\tag{13}
\]
They are nonnegative and sum to four, so \((8)\) shows that this covariance belongs to \(\mathcal C_4\). Direct multiplication shows that the three eigenvalues of \(A+\eta cc^\top\) are
\[
\frac43+\frac89\eta,
\qquad
\frac43+\frac29\eta,
\qquad
\frac43-\frac19\eta,
\tag{14}
\]
with respective eigenvectors proportional to \((1,1,1)^\top\), \((0,1,-1)^\top\), and \((-2,1,1)^\top\). The first value in \((14)\) is the largest. Therefore the quotient in the definition of \(\gamma(S_{\mathrm{mid}})\) equals \(8/9\), and \((12)\)--\((14)\) prove
\[
\gamma(S_{\mathrm{mid}})=\frac89.
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(8/9<1\), the admissibility-frontier theorem yields
\[
S_{\mathrm{mid}}\in\mathfrak A_4,
\qquad
\rho^*(S_{\mathrm{mid}})=\sqrt{\frac89}=\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}.
\tag{16}
\]

For the pair geometry, \(\text{lem:pair-space-exact-frontier}\), specialized to homogeneous blocks of size two, gives for every even \(n\geq4\)
\[
v^*(\rho;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\min\left\{\frac{n}{n-1},2(1-\rho^2)\right\},
\qquad
\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0,
\tag{17}
\]
and
\[
\gamma(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)},
\qquad
\rho^*(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}.
\tag{18}
\]
It also identifies complete randomization and independent fair within-pair orientations as the two attaining endpoint designs. At \(n=4\), \((17)\)--\((18)\), together with \(S_4=S_{\mathrm{pair}}\), become
\[
v^*(\rho;S_4)=\min\left\{\frac43,2(1-\rho^2)\right\},
\quad
\kappa(S_4)=0,
\quad
\gamma(S_4)=\frac13,
\quad
\rho^*(S_4)=\frac1{\sqrt3}.
\tag{19}
\]
Moreover, \(P_{S_4}=u_4u_4^\top\) has every diagonal entry equal to \(1/4\), so \(S_4\) is equal leverage and attains the rank-one dimension floor. In contrast, \((15)\) and \((6)\) give the strictly larger rank-one thresholds \(8/9\) and \(1\) for \(S_{\mathrm{mid}}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{out}}\). The two branches in \((19)\) meet at \(\rho^2=1/3\), giving the stated ranges of the attaining designs.

To verify the displayed four-unit law directly, put
\[
a=(1,-1,1,-1)^\top,
\qquad
b=(1,-1,-1,1)^\top.
\]
The support of \(d_{\mathrm{pair},4}\) is \(\{a,-a,b,-b\}\), with probability \(1/4\) on each point. It is balanced and sign symmetric, so it belongs to \(\mathcal D_4\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{d_{\mathrm{pair},4}}[zz^\top]
&=\frac12aa^\top+\frac12bb^\top\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&0&0\\
-1&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&-1\\
0&0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}
=A_{\mathrm{pair},4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
These are exactly the four outcomes of two independent fair pair orientations, so the second branch in \((19)\) is attained by the displayed law. For general even \(n\), \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0<c_n\), and the admissibility-frontier theorem gives \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\in\mathfrak A_n\). Finally, at \(n=8\), \(M=1\), and \(\rho=1\),
\[
V^*(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\frac{4M}{n}v^*(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{CRD}}(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\frac{4M}{n-1}=\frac47.
\tag{21}
\]
This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
