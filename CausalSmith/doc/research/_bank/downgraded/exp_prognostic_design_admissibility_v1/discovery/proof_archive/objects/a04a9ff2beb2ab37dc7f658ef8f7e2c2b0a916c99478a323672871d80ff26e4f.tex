\textbf{Proof.}
We divide the argument into the arbitrary-partition reduction, the parity classification, the homogeneous calculation, and the pair corollary.

\paragraph{Exact orbit reduction for an arbitrary partition.}
Let \(\mathfrak G_{\mathcal P}=\prod_{j=1}^B\mathfrak S(G_j)\) be the finite group of permutations acting separately within the blocks, and let \(U_g\) be the permutation matrix associated with \(g\in\mathfrak G_{\mathcal P}\).  Every \(U_g\) preserves \(\mathcal Z_n\), \(H_n\), and \(S_{\mathcal P}\).  Hence, for any \(A\in\mathcal C_n\),
\[
\overline A
=\frac1{|\mathfrak G_{\mathcal P}|}
\sum_{g\in\mathfrak G_{\mathcal P}}U_gAU_g^\top
\in\mathcal C_n.
\tag{1}
\]
The function \(A\mapsto q_\rho(A;S_{\mathcal P})\) is a pointwise maximum of linear functionals \(A\mapsto u^\top Au\), hence is convex.  Its feasible unit-vector set is invariant under \(\mathfrak G_{\mathcal P}\).  Jensen's inequality and this invariance give
\[
q_\rho(\overline A;S_{\mathcal P})
\leq \frac1{|\mathfrak G_{\mathcal P}|}
\sum_{g\in\mathfrak G_{\mathcal P}}
q_\rho(U_gAU_g^\top;S_{\mathcal P})
=q_\rho(A;S_{\mathcal P}).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus orbit averaging cannot increase the loss, even when the original covariance is completely unrestricted inside \(\mathcal C_n\).

For \(z\in\mathcal Z_n\), let
\[
r(z)=W^\top z,
\qquad
r_j(z)=\sum_{i\in G_j}z_i.
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(r(z)\in\mathcal R_{\mathcal P}\).  Conditional on the block sums \(r=(r_1,\ldots,r_B)\), uniform within-block permutation gives, for \(i\in G_j\) and \(i'\in G_\ell\),
\[
\mathbb E[z_i z_{i'}\mid r]
=\frac{r_jr_\ell}{m_jm_\ell}
\quad(j\ne\ell),
\tag{4}
\]
while, for distinct \(i,i'\in G_j\),
\[
\mathbb E[z_i z_{i'}\mid r]
=\frac{r_j^2-m_j}{m_j(m_j-1)}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equation \((5)\) follows from
\(r_j^2=m_j+\sum_{i\ne i'\in G_j}z_i z_{i'}\) and exchangeability.  The diagonal entries equal one.  Comparing \((4)\)--\((5)\) with the definition of \(A_{\mathcal P}(G)\) shows that the orbit average of \(zz^\top\) is exactly
\[
A_{\mathcal P}(rr^\top).
\tag{6}
\]
When \(m_j=1\), the residual projector is absent and the first term in the definition supplies the unit diagonal, so \((6)\) remains valid without division by zero.

Choose a convex representation \(A=\sum_\ell a_\ell z^{(\ell)}z^{(\ell)\top}\) and put
\[
G=\sum_\ell a_\ell r(z^{(\ell)})r(z^{(\ell)})^\top
\in\mathcal G_{\mathcal P}.
\tag{7}
\]
The map \(G\mapsto A_{\mathcal P}(G)\) is affine, so \((6)\)--\((7)\) imply
\[
\overline A=A_{\mathcal P}(G).
\tag{8}
\]
Together, \((2)\) and \((8)\) prove the full-hull converse part of the reduction.

Conversely, take \(G\in\mathcal G_{\mathcal P}\) and write
\[
G=\sum_{\ell=1}^L a_\ell r^{(\ell)}r^{(\ell)\top},
\qquad
a_\ell\geq0,
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^L a_\ell=1,
\qquad
r^{(\ell)}\in\mathcal R_{\mathcal P}.
\tag{9}
\]
For each \(r^{(\ell)}\), its parity and range conditions permit a sign vector with block sum \(r_j^{(\ell)}\) in block \(G_j\); the condition \(\sum_jr_j^{(\ell)}=0\) makes the resulting full assignment exactly balanced.  Choose such a vector, permute it uniformly within every block, and finally multiply the entire vector by an independent fair sign.  The last step makes the assignment mean zero without changing its second moment.  Mixing these laws with weights \(a_\ell\) gives a design in \(\mathcal D_n\), and \((6)\)--\((9)\) show that its covariance is \(A_{\mathcal P}(G)\).  Thus every reduced candidate is realizable.  Since \(\mathcal G_{\mathcal P}\) is a compact finite polytope and the loss is continuous, the reduced minimum is attained.  Equations \((2)\), \((8)\), and \((9)\) prove
\[
v^*(\rho;S_{\mathcal P})
=\min_{G\in\mathcal G_{\mathcal P}}
q_\rho(A_{\mathcal P}(G);S_{\mathcal P}).
\tag{10}
\]
The vertices \(r r^\top\) are explicitly enumerable from the block sizes, and each loss in \((10)\) is the compact eigenvalue optimization defining \(q_\rho\).  Hence \((10)\) is a finite construct-and-determine procedure; it does not assert a two-scalar formula for unequal blocks.

\paragraph{Parity classification.}
If every \(m_j\) is even, then \(0\in\mathcal R_{\mathcal P}\).  The preceding construction with \(G=0\) gives the feasible covariance
\[
A_{\mathcal P}(0)
=\sum_{j=1}^B\frac{m_j}{m_j-1}P_j,
\tag{11}
\]
where every denominator is positive because every even block is nonempty.  This covariance annihilates every block-constant vector and therefore annihilates \(S_{\mathcal P}\).  Positive semidefiniteness then gives \(\kappa(S_{\mathcal P})=0\).

For the converse, suppose \(\kappa(S_{\mathcal P})=0\).  Compactness of \(\mathcal C_n\) supplies an attaining covariance \(A=\sum_\ell a_\ell z^{(\ell)}z^{(\ell)\top}\).  Its compression to \(S_{\mathcal P}\) is positive semidefinite with largest eigenvalue zero, and hence, for every \(x\in S_{\mathcal P}\),
\[
0=x^\top Ax
=\sum_\ell a_\ell(x^\top z^{(\ell)})^2.
\tag{12}
\]
Fix \(\ell\) with \(a_\ell>0\) and write \(r=r(z^{(\ell)})\).  A vector \(x\in S_{\mathcal P}\) has the form \(x_i=t_j\) on \(G_j\), where \(\sum_jm_jt_j=0\).  Equation \((12)\) therefore gives
\[
t^\top r=0
\quad\text{whenever}\quad
t^\top(m_1,\ldots,m_B)^\top=0.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(r\) is proportional to \((m_1,\ldots,m_B)^\top\).  But the assignment is balanced, so \(\sum_jr_j=0\); since \(\sum_jm_j=n>0\), the proportionality constant is zero.  Hence \(r_j=0\) for every block.  A sum of \(m_j\) signs can equal zero only if \(m_j\) is even.  This proves
\[
\kappa(S_{\mathcal P})=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
m_j\text{ is even for every }j.
\tag{14}
\]

\paragraph{Homogeneous even blocks.}
Assume now that all \(B\) blocks have the same even size \(m\).  Let
\[
T=H_n\cap S_B^\perp,
\qquad
p=\dim S_B=B-1,
\qquad
h=\dim T=n-B,
\tag{15}
\]
so that \(p+h=n-1\) and \(H_n=S_B\mathbin\oplus T\).  In addition to within-block permutations, average over all block permutations.  The same convexity argument as in \((2)\) shows that this further averaging cannot increase the loss.  An invariant covariance has one common off-diagonal entry within blocks and one common entry between distinct blocks.  Because it annihilates \(\mathbf1_n\), its restriction to \(H_n\) consequently has the form
\[
\overline A=aP_S+bP_T,
\tag{16}
\]
where here \(P_S\) projects onto \(S_B\).  Positive semidefiniteness gives \(a,b\geq0\).  Every covariance in \(\mathcal C_n\) has unit diagonal and trace \(n\), so
\[
pa+hb=n.
\tag{17}
\]

Put \(s=\rho^2\).  If a unit vector \(u=x+y\) follows the decomposition in \((15)\), its score energy is \(r=\|x\|_2^2\).  Both summands in \((15)\) are nonzero, so every \(r\in[s,1]\) is attainable.  Therefore
\[
q_\rho(\overline A;S_B)
=\max_{r\in[s,1]}\{ar+b(1-r)\}
=\max\{a,\,as+b(1-s)\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Let
\[
c_n=\frac{n}{n-1},
\qquad
\delta_m=\frac{n}{h}=\frac{m}{m-1},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{p}{n-1}.
\tag{19}
\]
Equation \((17)\) implies
\[
b-a=\frac{n-(n-1)a}{h}.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(a\geq c_n\), then \((18)\) is at least \(c_n\).  If \(0\leq a\leq c_n\), then \((20)\) gives \(b\geq a\), and elimination of \(b\) from \((17)\) yields
\[
as+b(1-s)
=\delta_m(1-s)
+a\left\{s-\frac{p}{h}(1-s)\right\}.
\tag{21}
\]
The coefficient of \(a\) in \((21)\) has the sign of \((n-1)s-p\).  Thus, when \(s\leq\beta\), the right side is minimized over \(0\leq a\leq c_n\) at \(a=c_n\), where \((17)\) also gives \(b=c_n\), and its value is \(c_n\).  When \(s\geq\beta\), it is minimized at \(a=0\), where its value is \(\delta_m(1-s)\).  This relaxation over all nonnegative \((a,b)\) satisfying \((17)\), combined with the orbit-averaging inequality, proves the full-hull lower bound
\[
q_\rho(A;S_B)
\geq \min\{c_n,\delta_m(1-\rho^2)\}
\quad\text{for every }A\in\mathcal C_n.
\tag{22}
\]

Both endpoints are feasible.  Complete randomization has covariance \(c_nP_H\) and loss \(c_n\).  Independently within each block, draw uniformly from the signs having exactly \(m/2\) plus entries and \(m/2\) minus entries.  Every resulting full assignment is balanced, each coordinate has mean zero, and the covariance within block \(j\) is \(\delta_mP_j\).  Independence and zero means make the cross-block covariances zero.  The resulting design belongs to \(\mathcal D_n\) and has covariance
\[
A_W=\delta_m\sum_{j=1}^BP_j
=\delta_m(P_H-P_S).
\tag{23}
\]
It has \(a=0\), \(b=\delta_m\), so \((18)\) gives loss \(\delta_m(1-s)\).  Equations \((22)\)--\((23)\) prove
\[
v^*(\rho;S_B)
=\min\{c_n,\delta_m(1-\rho^2)\}.
\tag{24}
\]
Equation \((23)\) annihilates \(S_B\), so \(\kappa(S_B)=0\).  The two branches in \((24)\) meet because
\[
\delta_m(1-s)=c_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
s=1-\frac{c_n}{\delta_m}
=1-\frac{h}{n-1}
=\frac{p}{n-1}=\beta.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus strict improvement occurs exactly for \(s>\beta\).  The admissibility-frontier theorem and \((25)\) give
\[
\gamma(S_B)=\beta,
\qquad
\rho^*(S_B)=\sqrt\beta.
\tag{26}
\]

\paragraph{Exact homogeneous-block regret.}
Mix complete randomization with the within-block law using weight \(\beta\) on complete randomization.  Its feasible covariance is
\[
A_{B,\mathrm{mix}}
=\beta c_nP_H+(1-\beta)\delta_mP_T.
\tag{27}
\]
Its eigenvalue on \(S_B\) is \(\beta c_n\), and its eigenvalue on \(T\) is \(\beta c_n+(1-\beta)\delta_m\).  Hence \((18)\) gives
\[
q_{\sqrt s}(A_{B,\mathrm{mix}};S_B)
=\beta c_n+(1-\beta)\delta_m(1-s).
\tag{28}
\]
Since \(c_n=\delta_m(1-\beta)\), equations \((24)\) and \((28)\) imply
\[
q_{\sqrt s}(A_{B,\mathrm{mix}};S_B)-v^*(\sqrt s;S_B)
=c_n(\beta-s)
\quad(0\leq s\leq\beta),
\tag{29}
\]
and
\[
q_{\sqrt s}(A_{B,\mathrm{mix}};S_B)-v^*(\sqrt s;S_B)
=\beta\delta_m(s-\beta)
\quad(\beta\leq s\leq1).
\tag{30}
\]
Both pieces have maximum \(c_n\beta\), attained respectively at \(s=0\) and \(s=1\).  Therefore
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(A_{B,\mathrm{mix}};S_B)
=c_n\beta
=\frac{np}{(n-1)^2}.
\tag{31}
\]

For the matching converse, fix any \(A\in\mathcal C_n\), and put
\[
L=\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n),
\qquad
K=\lambda_{\max}(P_SAP_S\mid S_B),
\qquad
r=\Delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(A;S_B).
\tag{32}
\]
At \(s=0\), the oracle value is \(c_n\), while the loss is \(L\); at \(s=1\), the oracle value is zero, while the loss is \(K\).  Hence
\[
r\geq\max\{L-c_n,K\}.
\tag{33}
\]
Taking traces in the decomposition \(H_n=S_B\mathbin\oplus T\), and bounding the eigenvalues of the two compressions by \(K\) and \(L\), gives
\[
n=\operatorname{tr}(A)
\leq pK+hL
\leq pr+h(c_n+r)
=hc_n+(n-1)r.
\tag{34}
\]
Consequently,
\[
r\geq\frac{n-hc_n}{n-1}
=\frac{np}{(n-1)^2}.
\tag{35}
\]
Equations \((31)\)--\((35)\) certify optimality over the full covariance hull.  Scaling by \(4M/n\) according to the definition of \(R^*\) gives
\[
R^*(n,S_B)=\frac{4Mp}{(n-1)^2}.
\tag{36}
\]

\paragraph{Pair corollary.}
Let \(n=2k\) and take \(B=k\), \(m=2\).  Then \(S_B=S_{\mathrm{pair}}\), \(\delta_m=2\), and
\[
\beta=\frac{k-1}{2k-1}=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}.
\tag{37}
\]
The within-block design in \((23)\) draws the two possible orientations of every pair independently and fairly, so it belongs to \(\mathcal D_n\).  Substitution of \((37)\) into \((24)\), \((26)\), and \((23)\) yields
\[
v^*(\rho;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\min\{c_n,2(1-\rho^2)\},
\quad
\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0,
\quad
\gamma(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)},
\quad
\rho^*(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}.
\tag{38}
\]
This is the asserted pair specialization, including its attaining endpoint designs.  \(\square\)
