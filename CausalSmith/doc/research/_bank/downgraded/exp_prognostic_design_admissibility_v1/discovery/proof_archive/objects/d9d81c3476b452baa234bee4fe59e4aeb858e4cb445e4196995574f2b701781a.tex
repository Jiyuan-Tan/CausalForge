\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(s=\rho^2\), \(d_0=n-1\), \(p=\dim(S)\), and \(\beta=p/d_0\).  Every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) is a convex combination of \(zz^\top\) with \(z\in\mathcal Z_n\), and therefore
\[
A\succeq0,\qquad A\mathbf1_n=0,\qquad
\operatorname{diag}(A)=\mathbf1_n,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(A\mid H_n)=n.
\tag{1}
\]
It follows that \(\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n)\geq c_n=n/d_0\), with equality at \(A_{\mathrm{CRD}}=c_nP_H\).

At \(s=1\), the admissible unit vectors are precisely those in \(S\), so
\[
q_1(A;S)=\lambda_{\max}(P_SAP_S\mid S),
\qquad v^*(1;S)=\kappa(S).
\tag{2}
\]
If improvement occurs at some \(s<1\), nesting of the adversary shells gives \(\kappa(S)<c_n\).  Conversely, if an optimizer \(A_0\) in the definition of \(\kappa(S)\) satisfies \(q_1(A_0;S)<c_n\), compactness of the unit sphere shows
\[
q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_0;S)\longrightarrow q_1(A_0;S)
\quad\text{as }s\uparrow1.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, any sequence of maximizing unit vectors has a convergent subsequence, and a limit of vectors whose score energy tends to one lies in \(S\).  Thus \(S\in\mathfrak A_n\) if and only if \(\kappa(S)<c_n\).

By \(\text{thm:cone-lift-dual}\), and because the multiplier-zero value is at least \(c_n\),
\[
v^*(\sqrt{s};S)<c_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
s>\inf_{A\in\mathcal C_n,\,\eta>0}
\frac{\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_S\mid H_n)-c_n}{\eta}
=\gamma(S).
\tag{4}
\]
Taking \(A=c_nP_H\) gives \(\gamma(S)\leq1\).  Hence the improving squared-quality set is \((\gamma(S),1]\) when \(\gamma(S)<1\), and is empty when \(\gamma(S)=1\).  This proves the three-way equivalence and the formula for \(\rho^*(S)\).

We next derive the leverage lower bound.  Fix \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) and \(\eta>0\), and write
\[
\lambda=\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_S\mid H_n).
\]
Because \(A+\eta P_S\) is positive semidefinite and supported on \(H_n\),
\(A+\eta P_S\preceq\lambda P_H\).  Taking the \(i\)-th diagonal entry and using (1) gives
\[
1+\eta\ell_i\leq\lambda\frac{n-1}{n},
\qquad
\frac{\lambda-c_n}{\eta}\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\ell_i.
\tag{5}
\]
After maximizing over \(i\) and taking the infimum over \((A,\eta)\),
\[
\gamma(S)\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\max_i\ell_i.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(\sum_i\ell_i=\operatorname{tr}(P_S)=p\), the maximum leverage is at least \(p/n\), and (6) implies \(\gamma(S)\geq\beta\).

We prove the upper envelope constructively.  We first establish the elementary polygon--Gram equivalence used below.  For nonnegative numbers \(r_1,\ldots,r_n\), there are vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) in a Euclidean space satisfying
\[
\|v_i\|_2=r_i,qquad \sum_i v_i=0
\tag{7}
\]
if and only if \(2\max_i r_i\leq\sum_i r_i\).  Necessity follows by isolating a longest vector in (7) and applying the triangle inequality.  For sufficiency, the set of lengths attainable by a sum of planar vectors with prescribed lengths \(r_1,\ldots,r_k\) is the interval
\[
\left[\max\left\{0,\,2\max_{i\leq k}r_i-\sum_{i\leq k}r_i\right\},\ 
\sum_{i\leq k}r_i\right].
\tag{8}
\]
This is proved by induction: after the first \(k-1\) vectors have resultant length \(x\), adding a vector of length \(r_k\) produces every length in \([|x-r_k|,x+r_k]\); as \(x\) ranges over the preceding interval these intervals overlap, and their union has exactly the endpoints in (8).  Under the polygon inequality the left endpoint in (8) is zero, proving (7).  Taking \(D_{ij}=v_i^\top v_j\) now shows that, for prescribed \(d_i\geq0\),
\[
D\succeq0,\quad D\mathbf1_n=0,\quad \operatorname{diag}(D)=d
\tag{9}
\]
is possible if and only if \(2\max_i\sqrt{d_i}\leq\sum_i\sqrt{d_i}\).  The reverse implication follows from a Gram representation of \(D\) and the fact that \(D\mathbf1_n=0\) forces the sum of the Gram vectors to have squared norm zero.

We also record, directly from the balanced cut hull, its needed relative-interior fact.  Let
\[
\mathcal L=\{B\in\mathbb S^n:\operatorname{diag}(B)=0,\ B\mathbf1_n=0\}.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal C_n)=c_nP_H+\mathcal L,
\qquad c_nP_H\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal C_n).
\tag{10}
\]
The forward affine inclusion is immediate from (1).  For the reverse one, if a symmetric \(Q=(q_{ij})\) has \(z^\top Qz\) constant over balanced sign vectors, the alternating sum obtained by swapping two treated and two control indices yields
\[
q_{ik}-q_{jk}-q_{i\ell}+q_{j\ell}=0
\quad\text{for all distinct }i,j,k,\ell.
\tag{11}
\]
Choosing three anchors and applying (11) shows that \(q_{ij}=a_i+a_j\) off the diagonal.  Thus \(Q\) differs from \(\mathbf1_na^\top+a\mathbf1_n^\top\) by a diagonal matrix, and is orthogonal to \(\mathcal L\).  This proves equality of the relevant orthogonal complements and hence the affine equality in (10).  Finally, the uniform average of the distinct balanced matrices \(zz^\top\) is \(c_nP_H\).  If \(B\in\mathcal L\), express it as a zero-sum linear combination of these matrices; sufficiently small positive and negative perturbations of their uniform weights remain nonnegative.  This proves the relative-interior assertion.

Now take any \(t\) in the set defining \(U(\ell)\).  Apply (9) with \(d_i=d_i(t)\), and put
\[
C=tP_H-D.
\tag{12}
\]
Then
\[
C\mathbf1_n=0,qquad \operatorname{diag}(C)=\operatorname{diag}(P_S),
\qquad C\preceq tP_H.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently \(C-P_S\in\mathcal L\).  By (10), for every sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
A_\epsilon=c_nP_H+\epsilon(C-P_S)\in\mathcal C_n.
\tag{14}
\]
Choosing the multiplier \(\eta=\epsilon\) and using (13),
\[
A_\epsilon+\epsilon P_S=c_nP_H+\epsilon C
\preceq(c_n+\epsilon t)P_H.
\]
Therefore the quotient defining \(\gamma(S)\) is at most \(t\), and taking the infimum gives \(\gamma(S)\leq U(\ell)\).

The defining set for \(U\) is nonempty at \(t=1\): the matrix \(P_H-P_S\) is positive semidefinite, annihilates \(\mathbf1_n\), and has diagonal \(d_i(1)=(n-1)/n-\ell_i\), so the necessity part of the polygon--Gram equivalence verifies the polygon inequality.  Hence \(U(\ell)\leq1\).  Together with (6), this proves all four inequalities.

If \(\gamma(S)=\beta\), (6) and the identity \(\sum_i\ell_i=p\) force every \(\ell_i=p/n\).  Conversely, equal leverage makes \(d_i(\beta)=0\) for every \(i\), so \(U(\ell)=\beta\), and the lower and upper bounds coincide.  This proves the equality characterization.

For the four-unit checks, the leverage vectors are respectively
\[
\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac14,\tfrac14,\tfrac14\right),\qquad
\left(\tfrac16,\tfrac16,0,\tfrac23\right),\qquad
\left(\tfrac34,\tfrac1{12},\tfrac1{12},\tfrac1{12}\right).
\tag{15}
\]
For the first, \(t=1/3\) makes every \(d_i(t)=0\).  For the second, diagonal nonnegativity forces \(t\geq8/9\), and at \(t=8/9\) the squared lengths are \((1/2,1/2,2/3,0)\), which satisfy the polygon inequality.  For the third, nonnegativity of the first diagonal forces \(t\geq1\).  The lower bound (6) matches these three upper values, proving \(\gamma=1/3,8/9,1\).

The causal conclusions follow from (4) and the positive scaling \(V^*=(4M/n)v^*\).  The quantities \(\ell_i\) are obtained from any score basis by forming its orthogonal projector.  The set defining \(U\) is a compact one-dimensional semialgebraic set after squaring only with the displayed nonnegativity guards, so root isolation or certified one-dimensional search computes its infimum.  When the leverage screens are inconclusive, the finite semidefinite programs in \(\text{thm:exact-oracle-saddle}\), followed by bisection in \(s\), compute the exact frontier to prescribed tolerance and return cut-hull weights; sign symmetrization gives the assignment mixture.  This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
