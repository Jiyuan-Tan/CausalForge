The score matrix determines how much realized outcome energy must lie in its span before restriction can improve on complete randomization: concentrated leverage raises the polygon feasibility threshold, while equal leverage attains the dimension floor.  This threshold is exactly computable from \(\operatorname{diag}(P_S)\), even though the realized alignment itself depends on fixed potential outcomes and is unavailable at design time.  The regret design therefore hedges across quality regimes.  In an unequal two-block score, the smaller block controls the adverse within-block eigenvalue, parity fixes the best attainable perfect-quality block sum, and a single explicit mixture equalizes the zero-quality and perfect-quality regret constraints.
