\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(n=2k\) with \(k\geq 2\), and define the orthonormal pair-sum and pair-difference vectors
\[
e_j^+=\frac{e_{2j-1}+e_{2j}}{\sqrt 2},
\qquad
e_j^-=\frac{e_{2j-1}-e_{2j}}{\sqrt 2},
\qquad j=1,\ldots,k.
\]
Let
\[
T=\operatorname{span}\{e_1^-,\ldots,e_k^-\}.
\]
The definition of \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\) gives the orthogonal decomposition
\[
H_n=S_{\mathrm{pair}}\mathbin{\oplus}T,
\qquad
\dim(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=k-1,
\qquad
\dim(T)=k.
\tag{1}
\]
Both summands are nonzero because \(k\geq2\).

We first prove a converse over the full covariance hull.  Let \(G\) be the finite group generated by arbitrary permutations of the \(k\) pairs and arbitrary swaps of the two units within each pair, and let \(U_g\) be the permutation matrix associated with \(g\in G\).  Every \(U_g\) preserves \(\mathcal Z_n\), \(H_n\), and \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  Consequently, for any \(A\in\mathcal C_n\),
\[
\overline A=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}U_gAU_g^\top
\in\mathcal C_n.
\tag{2}
\]
The feasible unit-vector set in the definition of \(q_\rho(\,cdot\,;S_{\mathrm{pair}})\) is invariant under \(G\).  Moreover, \(q_\rho(\,cdot\,;S_{\mathrm{pair}})\) is convex because it is the pointwise maximum of linear functionals \(A\mapsto u^\top Au\).  Invariance and Jensen's inequality therefore imply
\[
q_\rho(\overline A;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
\leq \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}
q_\rho(U_gAU_g^\top;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=q_\rho(A;S_{\mathrm{pair}}).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus it is enough for the lower bound to analyze the invariant covariance \(\overline A\); equation \((3)\) is the step that makes the resulting bound valid for every member of the full hull \(\mathcal C_n\).

We next derive the form of \(\overline A\).  Swapping the two units of pair \(j\) fixes every pair-sum vector, negates \(e_j^-\), and fixes \(e_\ell^-\) for \(\ell\neq j\).  Since \(\overline A\) commutes with each such swap, its cross block between the pair-sum space and \(T\) vanishes, and its restriction to \(T\) is diagonal in the basis \(e_1^-,\ldots,e_k^-\).  Pair permutations force these diagonal entries to be equal; denote the common value by \(b\).  In the pair-sum basis, pair-permutation invariance forces all diagonal entries to be equal and all off-diagonal entries to be equal.  Every \(zz^\top\), with \(z\in\mathcal Z_n\), annihilates \(\mathbf 1_n\), so \(\overline A\mathbf 1_n=0\).  It follows that the restriction of \(\overline A\) to the centered pair-sum space is scalar; denote that eigenvalue by \(a\).  Hence, on \(H_n\),
\[
\overline A=aP_{S_{\mathrm{pair}}}+bP_T.
\tag{4}
\]
Positive semidefiniteness gives \(a,b\geq0\).  Every matrix in \(\mathcal C_n\) has diagonal entries one and therefore trace \(n=2k\).  Taking traces in \((4)\) and using \((1)\) gives
\[
a(k-1)+bk=2k.
\tag{5}
\]

Put \(s=\rho^2\).  For a unit vector \(u=x+y\) under decomposition \((1)\), write \(r=\|x\|_2^2\).  The score-quality constraint is exactly \(r\in[s,1]\), and every value in this interval is attainable because both summands in \((1)\) are nonzero.  Therefore
\[
q_\rho(\overline A;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\max_{r\in[s,1]}\{ar+b(1-r)\}
=\max\{a,\,as+b(1-s)\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(c_n=2k/(2k-1)\).  Equation \((5)\) yields
\[
b-a=\frac{2k-(2k-1)a}{k}.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(a\geq c_n\), then \((6)\) is at least \(a\), hence at least \(c_n\).  If \(0\leq a\leq c_n\), equation \((7)\) gives \(b\geq a\), and \((6)\) equals its second entry.  Eliminating \(b\) by \((5)\), that entry is
\[
as+b(1-s)
=2(1-s)+a\left\{s-\frac{k-1}{k}(1-s)\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
The coefficient of \(a\) in \((8)\) has the sign of
\[
(2k-1)s-(k-1).
\]
Set
\[
s_0=\frac{k-1}{2k-1}=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(s\leq s_0\), the affine expression \((8)\) is minimized over \(0\leq a\leq c_n\) at \(a=c_n\), where equation \((5)\) gives \(b=c_n\), and its value is \(c_n\).  For \(s\geq s_0\), it is minimized at \(a=0\), where its value is \(2(1-s)\).  Combining these cases with the preceding case \(a\geq c_n\), then applying \((3)\), gives the full-class converse
\[
q_\rho(A;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
\geq \min\{c_n,\,2(1-s)\}
\qquad\text{for every }A\in\mathcal C_n.
\tag{10}
\]

Both endpoints in \((10)\) are feasible.  Complete randomization has covariance
\[
A_{\mathrm{CRD}}=c_nP_H,
\]
so its loss is \(c_n\).  For the second endpoint, draw independent fair signs \(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_k\) and set
\[
z_{2j-1}=\epsilon_j,
\qquad
z_{2j}=-\epsilon_j,
\qquad j=1,\ldots,k.
\tag{11}
\]
Every assignment in the support of this law is exactly balanced, because each pair sums to zero.  Each coordinate has mean zero, so the law lies in \(\mathcal D_n\) by the definition of the balanced mean-zero design space.  Independence gives the block-diagonal covariance with \(k\) copies of
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is \(2P_T\).  Thus this covariance has \(a=0\) and \(b=2\), and equation \((6)\) gives loss \(2(1-s)\).  The two feasible covariances attain the two branches of the lower bound \((10)\).  Hence
\[
v^*(\rho;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
=\min\{c_n,\,2(1-\rho^2)\}.
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to identify the invariants.  The feasible covariance \(2P_T\) annihilates \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\), while every covariance in \(\mathcal C_n\) is positive semidefinite.  Therefore
\[
\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(c_n>0\), equation \((13)\) implies \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})<c_n\), so the admissibility-frontier theorem gives \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\in\mathfrak A_n\).  By \((12)\), strict improvement over complete randomization occurs exactly when
\[
2(1-\rho^2)<c_n,
\]
or equivalently when \(\rho^2>s_0\).  The definition of \(\rho^*(S_{\mathrm{pair}})\) and equation \((9)\) therefore yield
\[
\rho^*(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\sqrt{s_0}
=\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}.
\tag{14}
\]
The admissibility-frontier theorem also gives \(\rho^*(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\sqrt{\gamma(S_{\mathrm{pair}})}\).  Squaring \((14)\) proves
\[
\gamma(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}.
\tag{15}
\]
Equations \((12)\)--\((15)\) prove every asserted conclusion.  As boundary checks, at \(\rho=0\) complete randomization attains the trace lower bound \(c_n\), while at \(\rho=1\) the within-pair design has zero loss because its covariance annihilates the entire score space.  \(\square\)
