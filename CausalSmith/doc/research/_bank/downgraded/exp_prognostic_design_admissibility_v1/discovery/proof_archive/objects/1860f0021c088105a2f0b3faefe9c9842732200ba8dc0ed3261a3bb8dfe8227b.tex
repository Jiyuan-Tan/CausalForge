Set \(s=\rho^2\).  Since every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) is a convex combination of matrices \(zz^\top\), it is positive semidefinite, annihilates \(\mathbf 1_n\), has diagonal entries one, and has trace \(n\).  On the \((n-1)\)-dimensional space \(H_n\), therefore,
\[
\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n)\geq\frac{\operatorname{tr}(A)}{n-1}
=\frac{n}{n-1}=c_n.
\]
Complete randomization has covariance \(c_nP_H\), so its normalized worst-case risk is exactly \(c_n\) at every quality level.

At perfect quality, the feasible unit vectors lie in \(S\), and hence
\[
q_1(A;S)=\lambda_{\max}(P_SAP_S\mid S),
\qquad v^*(1;S)=\kappa(S).
\]
If improvement occurs at some \(s<1\), then for its witnessing covariance \(A\),
\[
\kappa(S)\leq q_1(A;S)\leq q_{\sqrt{s}}(A;S)<c_n.
\]
Conversely, suppose \(\kappa(S)<c_n\) and choose an attaining \(A_0\).  As \(s\uparrow1\), the compact feasible spherical sets decrease to the unit sphere in \(S\), so
\[
q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_0;S)\longrightarrow q_1(A_0;S)=\kappa(S).
\]
To verify this limit without an unstated continuity assumption, take maximizers along any sequence \(s_j\uparrow1\), pass to a convergent subsequence on the unit sphere, and observe that the limit has score energy one and hence lies in \(S\).  Thus \(q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_0;S)<c_n\) for some \(s<1\).  This proves
\[
S\in\mathfrak A_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\kappa(S)<c_n.
\]

The scalar dual gives the threshold exactly.  Because the value at \(\eta=0\) is at least \(c_n\), strict improvement satisfies
\[
v^*(\sqrt{s};S)<c_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{there exist }A\in\mathcal C_n\text{ and }\eta>0\text{ such that }
\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_S\mid H_n)-\eta s<c_n.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
v^*(\sqrt{s};S)<c_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
s>inf_{A\in\mathcal C_n,\,\eta>0}
\frac{\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_S\mid H_n)-c_n}{\eta}
=\gamma(S).
\]
The numerator is nonnegative because adding \(\eta P_S\succeq0\) cannot reduce the largest eigenvalue below \(c_n\); hence \(\gamma(S)\geq0\).  Complete randomization gives quotient one for every \(\eta>0\), so \(\gamma(S)\leq1\).  It follows that improvement at some \(s<1\) is equivalent to \(\gamma(S)<1\), and, in that case, the improving squared-quality set is exactly \((\gamma(S),1]\).  Therefore its infimum in the original quality coordinate is
\[
\rho^*(S)=\sqrt{\gamma(S)}.
\]
If \(\gamma(S)=1\), the improvement set is empty and the stipulated value is \(1\).  Combining this with the preceding equivalence proves all three parts of the dichotomy.

We finally make computability explicit.  Enumerate one representative from each sign-pair in \(\mathcal Z_n\), say \(z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(m)}\), and write
\(A(a)=\sum_{j=1}^m a_jz^{(j)}z^{(j)\top}\) for \(a\in\Delta_m\).  If \(Q_S\) is an orthonormal basis matrix for \(S\), then \(\kappa(S)\) is the finite semidefinite program
\[
\min_{a\in\Delta_m,\,t}\ t
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
tI_{\dim S}-Q_S^\top A(a)Q_S\succeq0.
\]
For fixed \(s\), the strict-improvement test is the one-scalar semidefinite feasibility condition
\[
a\in\Delta_m,\quad \eta>0,
\qquad (c_n+\eta s)I_{n-1}-Q_H^\top\{A(a)+\eta P_S\}Q_H\succ0,
\]
where \(Q_H\) is any orthonormal basis matrix for \(H_n\).  The test is monotone in \(s\), so bisection on \([0,1]\), with non-strict semidefinite bounds at the boundary, computes \(\gamma(S)\) and hence \(\rho^*(S)\) to any prescribed tolerance.  At a requested \(\rho\), the finite saddle program in the exact-oracle theorem and its finite dual compute an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal covariance and sign-pair weights.  Symmetrization produces the corresponding assignment mixture.  All matrices in these programs are obtained from \(n\), the centered score basis, and the enumerated balanced assignments; neither outcomes nor the unknown realized quality enter.
