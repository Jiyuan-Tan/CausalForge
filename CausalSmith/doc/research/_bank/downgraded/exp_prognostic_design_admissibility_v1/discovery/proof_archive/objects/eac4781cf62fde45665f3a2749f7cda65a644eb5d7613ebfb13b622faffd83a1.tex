Put \(T=H_n\cap S_{\mathrm{pair}}^\perp\).  By the pair-partition definition,
\[
 H_n=S_{\mathrm{pair}}\mathbin\oplus T,
 \qquad
 \dim(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=k-1,
 \qquad
 \dim(T)=k,
 \qquad
 P_T=P_H-P_S.
 \tag{1}
\]
The homogeneous-block result in Lemma \(\text{lem:pair-space-exact-frontier}\), specialized to \(B=k\) blocks of the even size \(m=2\), has \(p=B-1=k-1\) and \(d=m/(m-1)=2\).  It therefore gives, over the full balanced covariance hull,
\[
 v^*(\rho;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =\min\{c_n,2(1-\rho^2)\},
 \qquad
 \kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0,
 \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 \gamma(S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =\frac{k-1}{2k-1}
 =\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}
 =\alpha_n,
 \qquad
 \rho^*(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\sqrt{\alpha_n}.
 \tag{3}
\]
This is the requested \(m=2\) corollary of the partition result.  We now give the regret certificate explicitly, including its converse against every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\).

Let \(A_W=2P_T\) be the covariance of independent fair within-pair orientations; its feasibility is part of the same lemma and also follows directly by writing the assignment in pair \(j\) as \((\epsilon_j,-\epsilon_j)\), where the \(\epsilon_j\)'s are independent fair signs.  Complete randomization has covariance \(A_{\mathrm{CRD}}=c_nP_H\).  Hence, for every \(\beta\in[0,1]\), randomizing first between these two designs gives the feasible covariance
\[
 A_\beta=\beta c_nP_H+(1-\beta)2P_T\in\mathcal C_n.
 \tag{4}
\]
Its eigenvalue on \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\) is
\(a_\beta=\beta c_n\), and its eigenvalue on \(T\) is
\[
 b_\beta=\beta c_n+2(1-\beta)\geq a_\beta.
\]
Write \(s=\rho^2\).  If a unit vector \(u\in H_n\) has score energy
\(t=\|P_Su\|_2^2\), then (1) gives
\[
 u^\top A_\beta u=a_\beta t+b_\beta(1-t).
\]
Both summands in (1) are nonzero, so every \(t\in[0,1]\) occurs for some unit vector.  Since \(b_\beta\geq a_\beta\), maximizing over \(t\in[s,1]\) yields
\[
 q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_\beta;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =\beta c_n+2(1-\beta)(1-s).
 \tag{5}
\]

Take
\[
 \beta=\alpha_n=1-\frac{c_n}{2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
By (2), the oracle switches branches where \(2(1-s)=c_n\), namely at \(s=\alpha_n\).  Equations (5)--(6) imply that, for \(0\leq s\leq\alpha_n\),
\[
 q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_{\alpha_n};S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 -v^*(\sqrt{s};S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =c_n(\alpha_n-s),
 \tag{7}
\]
whose maximum is \(c_n\alpha_n\) at \(s=0\).  For \(\alpha_n\leq s\leq1\), they give
\[
 q_{\sqrt{s}}(A_{\alpha_n};S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 -v^*(\sqrt{s};S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =\alpha_nc_n-2\alpha_n(1-s),
 \tag{8}
\]
whose maximum is the same value \(c_n\alpha_n\) at \(s=1\).  Thus the two endpoint constraints are jointly binding and
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(A_{\alpha_n};S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =c_n\alpha_n
 =\frac{n(n-2)}{2(n-1)^2}.
 \tag{9}
\]

For the matching full-class converse, fix an arbitrary \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) and set
\[
 L=\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n),
 \qquad
 K=\lambda_{\max}(P_SAP_S\mid S_{\mathrm{pair}}).
\]
At \(s=0\), equation (2) gives oracle value \(c_n\), while the loss of \(A\) is \(L\).  At \(s=1\), equation (2) gives oracle value zero, while the loss of \(A\) is \(K\).  Consequently,
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(A;S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 \geq r:=\max\{L-c_n,K\}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) is positive semidefinite, annihilates \(\mathbf 1_n\), and has trace \(n\).  Taking traces along (1), bounding the eigenvalues of the compression to \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\) by \(K\), and bounding those of the compression to \(T\) by \(L\), gives
\[
 n=\operatorname{tr}(A)
 \leq(k-1)K+kL
 \leq(k-1)r+k(c_n+r)
 =kc_n+(n-1)r.
 \tag{11}
\]
Therefore
\[
 r\geq\frac{n-kc_n}{n-1}
 =\frac{n(n-2)}{2(n-1)^2}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Equations (9)--(12) prove optimality of \(A_{\alpha_n}=A_{\mathrm{mix}}\) over the entire hull \(\mathcal C_n\), not merely within pair-symmetric designs.  The balanced-covariance realizability lemma turns the covariance certificate into an actual mean-zero balanced assignment law.  Finally, the definition of the causal regret rescales (9) by \(4M/n\), so
\[
 R^*(n,S_{\mathrm{pair}})
 =\frac{4M}{n}\frac{n(n-2)}{2(n-1)^2}
 =\frac{2M(n-2)}{(n-1)^2}.
 \tag{13}
\]

For the exact smallest-instance check, let \(n=4\).  Then \(\alpha_4=1/3\).  Up to sign, the six balanced assignments form three sign-pairs.  Complete randomization assigns weight \(1/3\) to each sign-pair, whereas the within-pair design in \(\text{def:four-unit-score-mixture}\) assigns weights \((0,1/2,1/2)\), with zero on the omitted sign-pair.  Mixing with complete-randomization weight \(1/3\) therefore produces
\[
 \left(
 \frac13\frac13,
 \frac13\frac13+\frac23\frac12,
 \frac13\frac13+\frac23\frac12
 \right)
 =\left(\frac19,\frac49,\frac49\right).
\]
Equation (9) specializes to normalized regret \(4/9\); since \(4M/n=M\) at \(n=4\), equation (13) gives \(R^*(4,S_4)=4M/9\).  This enumeration also checks that all weights are nonnegative and sum to one, including the boundary mixture asserted in the theorem.
