\textbf{Proof.}
Suppose first that \(S=H_n\). The score constraint is automatic for every unit vector in \(H_n\), and hence
\[
q_\rho(A;H_n)=\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n).
\tag{1}
\]
Every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) is positive semidefinite, annihilates \(\mathbf1_n\), and has trace \(n\). Therefore
\[
\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n)\geq\frac{n}{n-1}=c_n.
\tag{2}
\]
Complete randomization has covariance \(c_nP_H\), so (1)--(2) give \(v^*(\rho;H_n)=c_n\) for every \(\rho\), and thus \(H_n\notin\mathfrak A_n\).

For the singleton-versus-rest geometry, put \(w=P_Hr_1\) and \(u_{\mathrm{out}}=w/\|w\|_2\). Since
\[
w=r_1-\frac1n\mathbf1_n,
\qquad
\|w\|_2^2=r_1^\top P_Hr_1=1-\frac1n=\frac{n-1}{n},
\tag{3}
\]
every balanced assignment \(z\in\mathcal Z_n\) satisfies
\[
(u_{\mathrm{out}}^\top z)^2
=\frac{(r_1^\top z)^2}{\|w\|_2^2}
=\frac{n}{n-1}=c_n,
\tag{4}
\]
because \(\mathbf1_n^\top z=0\) and \(z_1^2=1\). For any \(A\in\mathcal C_n\), convex averaging of (4) gives
\[
u_{\mathrm{out}}^\top Au_{\mathrm{out}}=c_n.
\tag{5}
\]
The vector \(u_{\mathrm{out}}\) lies in \(S_{\mathrm{out}}\), so it is feasible for every quality level. Thus (5) implies \(q_\rho(A;S_{\mathrm{out}})\geq c_n\) for every \(A\), while complete randomization attains equality. Therefore
\[
v^*(\rho;S_{\mathrm{out}})=c_n
\qquad(0\leq\rho\leq1).
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(S_{\mathrm{out}}\) is one-dimensional, (5) also gives \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{out}})=c_n\). The admissibility-frontier theorem then gives \(S_{\mathrm{out}}\notin\mathfrak A_n\) and \(\gamma(S_{\mathrm{out}})=1\).

For the intermediate four-unit calculation, choose the three balanced sign-pair representatives
\[
z^{(1)}=(1,1,-1,-1)^\top,
\quad
z^{(2)}=(1,-1,1,-1)^\top,
\quad
z^{(3)}=(1,-1,-1,1)^\top,
\tag{7}
\]
and put \(e_j=z^{(j)}/2\). They form an orthonormal basis of \(H_4\), and every \(A\in\mathcal C_4\) is diagonal in this basis with eigenvalues
\[
\lambda_j=4a_j\geq0,
\qquad
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=4,
\tag{8}
\]
for a probability vector \((a_1,a_2,a_3)\). The coordinates of \(u_{\mathrm{mid}}\) are
\[
(e_1^\top u_{\mathrm{mid}},e_2^\top u_{\mathrm{mid}},e_3^\top u_{\mathrm{mid}})
=\left(\sqrt{\frac23},\sqrt{\frac16},-\sqrt{\frac16}\right).
\tag{9}
\]
It follows that
\[
u_{\mathrm{mid}}^\top Au_{\mathrm{mid}}
=\frac23\lambda_1+\frac16\lambda_2+\frac16\lambda_3
\geq\frac23.
\tag{10}
\]
Equality is attained by \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)=(0,4,0)\), so \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{mid}})=2/3\).

Reverse the sign of \(e_3\); the score coordinate becomes \(c=6^{-1/2}(2,1,1)^\top\). Let \(r=3^{-1/2}(1,1,1)^\top\). For every feasible diagonal covariance and every \(\eta>0\),
\[
\lambda_{\max}(A+\eta P_{S_{\mathrm{mid}}}\mid H_4)
\geq r^\top\{A+\eta cc^\top\}r
=\frac43+\frac89\eta.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(0<\eta\leq3\), choose the feasible covariance with eigenvalues
\[
\lambda_1=\frac43-\frac49\eta,
\qquad
\lambda_2=\lambda_3=\frac43+\frac29\eta.
\tag{12}
\]
Direct multiplication gives the eigenvalues of \(A+\eta cc^\top\) as
\[
\frac43+\frac89\eta,
\qquad
\frac43+\frac29\eta,
\qquad
\frac43-\frac19\eta.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(\gamma(S_{\mathrm{mid}})=8/9\), and the frontier theorem gives \(\rho^*(S_{\mathrm{mid}})=2\sqrt2/3\) and \(S_{\mathrm{mid}}\in\mathfrak A_4\).

The pair formulas, including their attaining endpoint designs, follow by specializing \(\text{lem:pair-space-exact-frontier}\) to blocks of size two. At \(n=4\), they give
\[
v^*(\rho;S_4)=\min\left\{\frac43,2(1-\rho^2)\right\},
\quad
\kappa(S_4)=0,
\quad
\gamma(S_4)=\frac13,
\quad
\rho^*(S_4)=\frac1{\sqrt3}.
\tag{14}
\]
To verify the displayed law directly, put \(a=(1,-1,1,-1)^\top\) and \(b=(1,-1,-1,1)^\top\). Its support is \(\{a,-a,b,-b\}\), with probability \(1/4\) on each point, and
\[
\mathbb E[zz^\top]
=\frac12aa^\top+\frac12bb^\top
=\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&0&0\\
-1&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&-1\\
0&0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}
=A_{\mathrm{pair},4}.
\tag{15}
\]
For general even \(n\), \(\kappa(S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0<c_n\), so \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}\in\mathfrak A_n\). Finally, at \(n=8\), \(M=1\), and \(\rho=1\),
\[
V^*(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})=0,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{CRD}}(1;S_{\mathrm{pair}})=\frac47.
\]
This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
