Put \(s=\rho^2\), and abbreviate \(q_{\sqrt{s}}(A;S)\) and \(v^*(\sqrt{s};S)\) by \(q_s(A)\) and \(v(s)\), respectively.  For a unit vector \(u\in H_n\), put \(t(u)=u^\top P_Su\).  We first prove the reduction
\[
 \sup_{0\leq s\leq1}\{q_s(A)-v(s)\}
 =\sup_{\substack{u\in H_n\\ \|u\|_2=1}}
   \{u^\top Au-v(t(u))\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
For the lower bound, fix \(u\) and use \(s=t(u)\); then \(u\) is feasible for \(q_s(A)\), so \(q_s(A)\geq u^\top Au\).  For the upper bound, compactness of the feasible spherical shell gives a maximizer \(u_s\) of \(q_s(A)\).  If \(t=t(u_s)\), then \(t\geq s\).  The oracle value \(v(\cdot)\) is nonincreasing because the adversary class shrinks as its squared-quality lower bound increases.  Consequently,
\[
 q_s(A)-v(s)=u_s^\top Au_s-v(s)
 \leq u_s^\top Au_s-v(t),
\]
which is bounded above by the right side of (1).

Enumerate one representative from each balanced sign-pair and write every \(B\in\mathcal C_n\) as \(A(b)\), where \(b\in\Delta_m\).  The scalar dual in \(\text{thm:cone-lift-dual}\), followed by minimization over the covariance hull, gives, for every \(t\in[0,1]\),
\[
 v(t)=
 \inf_{\substack{b\in\Delta_m,\ \eta\geq0,\ \ell\in\mathbb R\\
 \ell I_{n-1}-Q_H^\top\{A(b)+\eta P_S\}Q_H\succeq0}}
 \{\ell-\eta t\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the matrix inequality is equivalent to
\(\ell\geq\lambda_{\max}(A(b)+\eta P_S\mid H_n)\).  Formula (2) remains valid at \(t=1\): as proved in the boundary part of \(\text{thm:cone-lift-dual}\), the infimum over \(\eta\) may then be achieved only in the limit \(\eta\to\infty\), and that limit is the compressed eigenvalue on \(S\).

Substitution of (2) into (1) turns the negative infimum into a supremum and yields
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(A(a);S)
 =\sup_{\substack{u\in H_n,\ \|u\|_2=1;\ b\in\Delta_m,\ \eta\geq0,\ \ell\in\mathbb R\\
 \ell I_{n-1}-Q_H^\top\{A(b)+\eta P_S\}Q_H\succeq0}}
 \left[u^\top A(a)u+\eta u^\top P_Su-\ell\right].
 \tag{3}
\]
Minimizing (3) over \(a\in\Delta_m\) and applying \(\text{def:regret-object}\) proves the displayed robust semidefinite representation.  Introducing an epigraph variable \(r\) gives the equivalent semi-infinite epigraph statement.

We next justify the finite computation claim.  The semidefinite inequality in (3) is equivalent to nonnegativity of its finitely many principal minors.  Together with \(\|u\|_2^2=1\), the simplex equalities, and the coordinate nonnegativity constraints, these are finitely many polynomial equalities and inequalities whose coefficients are determined by the score projector and the enumerated balanced signs.  Decompose this finite semialgebraic system into sign-invariant cells by recursively isolating the real roots of the input polynomials, their discriminants, and their resultants, passing an identically zero polynomial to the appropriate lower-dimensional stratum.  On every cell, the active simplex face is fixed, and the active eigenvalue branches are among the roots of
\[
 \det\!\left(\ell I_{n-1}-Q_H^\top\{A(b)+\eta P_S\}Q_H\right)=0.
\]
Thus only finitely many cell boundaries, active-face changes, and generalized eigenvalue crossings arise.  Compactify the unbounded multiplier by \(h=(1+\eta)^{-1}\) and clear the positive denominator.  At \(h=0\), a finite value relevant to the supremum can occur only when \(t(u)=1\); the boundary value is the compressed eigenvalue \(\lambda_{\max}(P_SA(b)P_S\mid S)\).  Optimizing on each cell and comparing the finitely many cell optima gives the asserted finite real-algebraic optimization.  Certified real-root isolation and semidefinite upper and lower bounds therefore compute the value and an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal solution from \(n\) and a basis of \(S\).  No potential outcome or realized score quality enters this procedure.

It remains to prove attainment and the design-based converse.  We first record continuity without assuming it.  If \(S=H_n\), then \(q_s(A)\) is independent of \(s\).  Otherwise write \(T=H_n\cap S^\perp\).  For \(t\in[0,1]\), every unit vector of exact score energy \(t\) has the form
\(u=\sqrt t\,x+\sqrt{1-t}\,y\), where \(x\) and \(y\) range over the unit spheres of \(S\) and \(T\).  Hence the maximum quadratic form at exact energy \(t\) is the maximum, over a fixed compact product of spheres, of
\[
 t x^\top Ax+(1-t)y^\top Ay
 +2\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,x^\top Ay.
\]
This expression is jointly continuous in \((t,A,x,y)\), including at \(t=0,1\).  Taking its maximum first over \((x,y)\) and then over \(t\in[s,1]\) proves that \((s,A)\mapsto q_s(A)\) is continuous on \([0,1]\times\mathcal C_n\).  Compact minimization over \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) then proves continuity of \(v(s)\).  Equation (1) consequently expresses \(\Delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(A;S)\) as the maximum of a jointly continuous function over the compact unit sphere, so it is continuous in \(A\).  Since \(\mathcal C_n\) is compact, an outer minimizer \(A_{\mathrm{reg}}\) exists.  By \(\text{lem:balanced-covariance-realizability}\), symmetrizing any convex representation of \(A_{\mathrm{reg}}\) produces a design \(d_{\mathrm{reg}}\in\mathcal D_n\).

The exact oracle saddle theorem translates the normalized game into causal mean squared error.  For every \(d\in\mathcal D_n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_{\rho\in[0,1]}
 \left\{
 \sup_{\mu\in\mathcal Y(\rho,M;S)}
 \mathbb E_d[(\widehat\tau_d-\tau_n)^2]-V^*(\rho;S)
 \right\}\\
&\qquad =\frac{4M}{n}\Delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(A_d;S)
 \geq R^*(n,S).
\end{aligned}
\]
Equality holds for the design realizing \(A_{\mathrm{reg}}\).  This proves both attainment and the matching converse over the entire class \(\mathcal D_n\).

Finally, at \(\rho=0\),
\(q_0(A;S)=\lambda_{\max}(A\mid H_n)\).  Every \(A\in\mathcal C_n\) is positive semidefinite, has trace \(n\), and acts on the \((n-1)\)-dimensional space \(H_n\); therefore \(q_0(A;S)\geq n/(n-1)=c_n\).  Complete randomization has covariance \(c_nP_H\) and attains equality, so it is a \(\rho=0\) oracle.  Its support is all of \(\mathcal Z_n\).  Every symmetrized cut-hull representation of \(A_{\mathrm{reg}}\) uses assignments in \(\mathcal Z_n\), and its support is therefore contained in the support of this one oracle design.  The claimed finite-union support property follows a fortiori.
