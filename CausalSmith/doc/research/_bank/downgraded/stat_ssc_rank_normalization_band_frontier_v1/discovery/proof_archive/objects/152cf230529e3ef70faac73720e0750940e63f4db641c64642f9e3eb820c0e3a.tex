For any fixed positive contrast separation, a deterministic singular-value threshold tending to zero reduces rank choice to testing whether the centered contrast operator is zero.  If its type-I contribution is charged to the rank-one band and its type-II error is at most the honesty tolerance, switching between the two oracle bands adds no first-order width.  Uniformity over all unknown positive lower bounds is different: rank-two alternatives whose second singular value is of the testing order are contiguous to rank one, so no uniformly consistent selector exists without a declared separation sequence.  The frozen handle specifies neither that sequence nor a quantitative concentration inequality for the censoring-corrected cross-fold singular value, and the oracle attaining theorem needed to price the switching band is still open.
