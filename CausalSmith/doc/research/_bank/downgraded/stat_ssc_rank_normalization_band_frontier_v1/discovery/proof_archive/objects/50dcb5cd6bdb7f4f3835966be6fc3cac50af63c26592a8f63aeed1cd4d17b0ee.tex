\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\).  We first construct the donor channel.  Keep the centered polynomial donor scores, all factor curves, all pre-period laws, and target loading zero fixed, and replace only the post baseline by
\[
 s_1^{(u)}(t)=e^{-\lambda_\circ t}+ucn_D^{-1/2}\psi_\circ(t),\qquad u\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]
Choose the fixed \(c>0\) small enough below.  For every sufficiently large triple, \(cn_D^{-1/2}\le v_\circ\).  The pointwise inequalities in \(\mathrm{ass:explicit\mbox{-}witness\mbox{-}compatibility}\) then verify survival validity, event overlap, the density floor, and hazard smoothness.  Adding a common post-period curve does not alter the centered donor operator, so exact rank and its nonzero singular values are unchanged.  The pre-factor Gram, loading bounds, factor envelope, and endpoint normalization are likewise unchanged.  With censoring identically infinite, all censoring and sampling conditions hold.

The two donor-channel laws differ only in the \(n_D\) donor-post records and their target curves have separation
\[
 \Delta_D=2c\|\psi_\circ\|_\infty n_D^{-1/2}.
\]
The density calculation from the rank-two lemma, now with \(c n_D^{-1/2}\) in place of \(a_{\circ,0}h_\circ K_T^{-1/2}\), gives
\[
 \|Q_{D,+}-Q_{D,-}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le Cc.
\]
This construction applies at rank one using the zero-dimensional convention and at every fixed higher rank using the fixed orthogonal backbone.

For the target channel at rank \(r\ge2\), use \(P_{r,+}\) and \(P_{r,-}\) from the corrected fixed-rank construction.  Centering is preserved by Gram--Schmidt and
\[
 N_0^{-1}\sum_mz_mz_m^\top=I_d.
\]
The joint-period factor columns are orthogonal with common squared norm \((a_{\circ,0}^2+a_{\circ,1}^2)\|\psi_\circ\|_2^2\), so the centered operator has exactly \(d\) nonzero singular values, all in the permitted interval.  Similarly,
\[
 \int_{\mathcal I}A_{0,d}(t)A_{0,d}(t)^\top\,dt
 =a_{\circ,0}^2\|\psi_\circ\|_2^2I_d.
\]
The row bound gives donor incoherence.  Since \(M_\star>0\), eventually
\[
 h_\circ K_T^{-1/2}\le\min\{M_\star,R_d\}.
\]
The remaining class properties follow from the explicit compatibility inequalities.  Only the first target-loading coordinate changes, so the separation and total-variation bounds are
\[
 \Delta_T=2|a_{\circ,1}|h_\circ\|\psi_\circ\|_\infty K_T^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \|P_{r,+}-P_{r,-}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le2|a_{\circ,0}|h_\circ c_f^{-1/2}\|\psi_\circ'\|_2.
\]

It remains to convert either pair into a width bound.  Let \(Q_-,Q_+\) be a pair, let \(\theta_-,\theta_+\) be its two targets, and put
\[
 \eta=\alpha+\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha).
\]
Honesty under each law and the definition of total variation imply
\[
 Q_-\{\theta_-,\theta_+\in B\}
 \ge1-2\eta-\|Q_--Q_+\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
On this event, at a time maximizing \(|\theta_+-\theta_-|\), one has \(w(B)\ge\|\theta_+-\theta_-\|_\infty\).  The definition of the coverage schedule gives
\[
 1-2\eta\ge\frac32(1/2-\alpha).
\]
The last numerical inequality in the compatibility assumption makes the target-channel total variation at most \(\frac34(1/2-\alpha)\).  Choose \(c\) so the same is true in the donor channel.  Thus the preceding event has probability at least \(\frac34(1/2-\alpha)>0\) in both channels.  Taking expectations yields constants \(c_D,c_T>0\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_Pw(B)\ge c_Dn_D^{-1/2},
\]
and, when \(r\ge2\), also
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_Pw(B)\ge c_TK_T^{-1/2}.
\]
For \(r=1\) the first inequality is the claim.  For \(r\ge2\), use \(\max\{a,b\}\ge(a+b)/2\) and decrease the constant to obtain the asserted sum. \(\square\)
