\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\), and
\(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\).  The canonical grid and
the interpolated-population stability lemma give
\[
 h^{q_\beta}\le\rho_r,
 \qquad
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta,
 \qquad
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g)
 \quad(g\in\mathcal G).                                    \tag{1}
\]

If \(r=1\), apply
\(\mathrm{lem:pooled\mbox{-}noniid\mbox{-}km\mbox{-}linearization}\), set
\(\phi_{T,j}=0\), put the donor-pre influences equal to zero, and include
the exponentially small pooled product-limit bias in \(Q_1\).  This gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_1-\theta
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}\phi_{D,1,m,j}+Q_1,
 \qquad
 E\|Q_1\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\{n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}\}^2.                \tag{2}
\]
If \(r\ge2\),
\(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}gridwise\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}transfer}\)
gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =K_T^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}\phi_{T,j}
 +n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j}
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r,                                       \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le\eta_D(K_D),
 \qquad E\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C\upsilon_r^2. \tag{4}
\]
The patient samples are disjoint, so all displayed influence processes are
independent and mean zero.  Equations (1)--(4) prove the expansion and its
grid centering.

By definition,
\[
 \omega_r=C_\omega\varepsilon^{-1/2}\upsilon_r.            \tag{5}
\]
Choose the fixed \(C_\omega\) large enough that Markov's inequality in
(2) or (4) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}
 P\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le\varepsilon/4.                                         \tag{6}
\]

The corrected critical radius is
\[
 c_r^\xi=\widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_{r,n}.                    \tag{7}
\]
The random-quantile theorem and
\(\delta_{r,n}(\alpha)=\min\{\alpha/2,\varepsilon/2\}\)
imply, for every sufficiently large triple,
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le c_r^\xi\}
 \ge1-\alpha+\delta_{r,n}/2
 =1-\alpha+\varepsilon/4.                                 \tag{8}
\]
The equality holds eventually because \(\varepsilon<\alpha\).  Enlarging
the threshold in (8) by \(\omega_r/2\) therefore yields exactly
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\omega_r/2\}
 \le\alpha-\varepsilon/4.                                 \tag{9}
\]

It remains to verify the small-order buffer.  Let
\(A=\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\).  Dividing the terms in \(\upsilon_r\) by the
corresponding summands of \(\rho_r\) bounds the resulting ratios by fixed
multiples of
\[
 K_T^{-1/2},\quad K_D^{-1/2},\quad (N_0K_D)^{-1/2},\quad
 \eta_D(K_D),                                               \tag{10}
\]
and
\[
 K_T^{1/2}e^{-c_\dagger K_T/2},qquad
 (N_0K_D)^{1/2}e^{-c_\dagger N_0K_D/2}.                    \tag{11}
\]
The reciprocal of \(\varepsilon\) grows only as an iterated logarithm of
\(A\).  Thus (10)--(11), even after multiplication by
\(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\), converge uniformly to zero on every
three-dimensional tail.  Hence \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\), with no comparison
between \(K_T\) and \(N_0K_D\).  Equation (1) also shows why the
interpolation perturbation inside the \(L^2\) factor map is used only for
spectrum and Gram stability, while the off-grid discrepancy is retained in
the final interpolation allowance. \(\square\)
