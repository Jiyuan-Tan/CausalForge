\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\mathrm{lem:normalization\mbox{-}collapse}\), every donor-post event survival curve equals \(s_1\), so every donor-post failure hazard equals a common \(\lambda_1\), although the censoring survivals \(G_{1,m}\) may differ. For \(i=(m,j)\), define
\[
 N_i(u)=\mathbf1\{X_{1,m,j}\le u,\Delta_{1,m,j}=1\},
 \quad Y_i(u)=\mathbf1\{X_{1,m,j}\ge u\},
 \quad M_i(u)=N_i(u)-\int_0^uY_i(v)\lambda_1(v)\,dv.       \tag{1}
\]
The maintained assumptions make the \(M_i\)'s independent mean-zero martingales. With \(Y=\sum_iY_i\), \(M=\sum_iM_i\), and \(J_Y=\mathbf1\{Y>0\}\), equal donor cell sizes give
\[
 EY(u)=n_DH_D(u),\qquad H_D(u)=s_1(u)\bar G_D(u)\ge h_0.   \tag{2}
\]
For the un-interpolated pooled product-limit curve, put \(R=\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}/s_1\). Product integration gives
\[
 R(t)-1=-\int_0^tR(u-)\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)}\,dM(u)
 +\int_0^tR(u-)\{1-J_Y(u)\}\lambda_1(u)\,du.              \tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
 \phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}(t)
 =-s_1(t)\int_0^tH_D(u)^{-1}\,dM_i(u).                    \tag{4}
\]
Subtracting \(n_D^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}\) leaves a martingale with integrand \(R J_Y/Y-1/(n_DH_D)\) and the empty-risk drift. The horizon risk set is Poisson--binomial and (2) gives
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)=0\}\le e^{-h_0n_D},
 \qquad P\{Y(\bar\tau)<n_Dh_0/2\}\le e^{-cn_D}.          \tag{5}
\]
On the complement of the second event,
\[
 \frac{R}{Y}-\frac1{n_DH_D}
 =\frac{R-1}{Y}-\frac{Y-n_DH_D}{n_DH_DY}.                  \tag{6}
\]
Doob's inequality, \(E(Y-n_DH_D)^2\le n_D\), and the predictable isometry applied to (6) give a centered remainder second moment \(Cn_D^{-2}\). On the rare event, the fourth-moment inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz give the same polynomial bound. The drift is bounded and supported on the first event in (5), giving the displayed bias and centered second moment. Interpolation is a deterministic supremum contraction and fixes grid knots.

The predictable isometry and integration by parts for (4) give
\[
 E\left\|n_D^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}\right\|_\infty^2
 \le Cn_D^{-1}.                                           \tag{7}
\]
Conditional on the records, the ordered failure increments of the empirical multiplier form a Gaussian martingale with weights \(-\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}J_Y/Y\). On \(\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge n_Dh_0/2\}\), its conditional quadratic variation is at most \(C/n_D\). On the complement it is at most \(n_D\), while (5) makes the event exponentially rare. Conditional Doob inequality and \(n_De^{-cn_D}\le C/n_D\) yield
\[
 E E_\xi\|\widehat Z_1^\xi\|_\infty^2\le Cn_D^{-1}.      \tag{8}
\]
At time zero every process is zero. \(\square\)
