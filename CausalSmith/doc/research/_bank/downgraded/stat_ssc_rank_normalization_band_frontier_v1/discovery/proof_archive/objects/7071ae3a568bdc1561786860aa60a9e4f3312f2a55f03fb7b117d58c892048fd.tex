\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\), and \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\). The canonical grid and interpolated-population stability give
\[
 h^{q_\beta}\le\rho_r,qquad
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta,qquad
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g).       \tag{1}
\]
If \(r=1\), the pooled non-identically censored lemma, with all absent donor-pre and target influences set to zero, gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_1-\theta=n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}\phi_{D,1,m,j}+Q_1,
 \qquad E\|Q_1\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\{n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}\}^2.              \tag{2}
\]
For \(r\ge2\), the stabilized transfer lemma gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =K_T^{-1}\sum_j\phi_{T,j}+n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j}
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r,                                      \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le\eta_D(K_D),qquad
 E\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C\upsilon_r^2.      \tag{4}
\]
All patient processes are independent and mean zero by patient independence and the martingale constructions.

Since \(\omega_r=C_\omega\varepsilon^{-1/2}\upsilon_r\), a sufficiently large fixed \(C_\omega\) and Markov's inequality give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le\varepsilon/4.                                       \tag{5}
\]
The corrected definition has \(c_r^\xi=\widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_{r,n}\). By the grid-uniform random-quantile theorem and, eventually, \(\delta_{r,n}=\varepsilon/2\),
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le c_r^\xi\}
 \ge1-\alpha+\varepsilon/4.                              \tag{6}
\]
Enlarging the threshold proves the second tail inequality in the statement.

Finally, divide every term of \(\upsilon_r\) by the corresponding summand of \(\rho_r\). The ratios are bounded by fixed multiples of
\[
 K_T^{-1/2},\quad K_D^{-1/2},\quad n_D^{-1/2},\quad
 \eta_D(K_D),\quad K_T^{1/2}e^{-c_\dagger K_T/2},\quad
 n_D^{1/2}e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}.                           \tag{7}
\]
The reciprocal of \(\varepsilon\) grows only at an iterated-logarithm rate. Hence \(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\upsilon_r/\rho_r\to0\) uniformly on every three-dimensional tail, with no relative-growth restriction. Equations (1)--(7) prove the expansion, the two tail bounds, and \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\). The off-grid error remains solely the interpolation allowance in the band. \(\square\)
