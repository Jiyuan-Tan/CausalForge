\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(Z_r=\sum_iL_i\).  The pooled linearization gives \(\|L_i\|_\infty\le Cn_D^{-1}\) for \(r=1\).  For \(r\ge2\), the stabilized transfer gives
\[
 \|L_i\|_\infty\le
 \begin{cases}
 CK_T^{-1},&i\text{ is target-pre},\\
 Cn_D^{-1},&i\text{ is a donor patient}.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{1}
\]

For rank one the pooled formulas are
\[
 \Sigma_1(s,t)=\frac{s_1(s)s_1(t)}{n_D}
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{\lambda_1(u)}{H_D(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_1(s,t)=\widehat S_D(s-)\widehat S_D(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)^2}\,dN(u).       \tag{2}
\]
On \(\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge n_Ds_-g_-/2\}\), substitute \(dN=dM+Y\lambda_1du\).  Predictable isometry, the ordered risk-process maximal inequality, and the pooled expansion imply
\[
 E\{\rho_1^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\|_\infty\}
 \le Cn_D^{-1/2}.                                       \tag{3}
\]
The complement is exponentially small and the empty-risk convention bounds (2), so (3) is uniform under non-identical donor censoring.

Now suppose \(r\ge2\), and use the measurable event \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) constructed in the stabilized-transfer proof.  On that event, all population-to-observed and one-replacement covariance segments lie in one fixed-gap neighborhood.  For the coordinate-free regular prediction map \(F\), the projector resolvent identity, the positive-square-root Sylvester equation, and the inverse-Gram identity give, without selecting a Lipschitz eigenvector frame,
\[
 E\bigl[\|D\widehat F-DF\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}\}\bigr]
 \le C\{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)\}.           \tag{4}
\]
For a stabilized input of size \(k\), put \(H_k=S^0G\) and
\[
 V_k(s,t)=S^0(s)S^0(t)\int_0^{s\wedge t}
 \frac{\lambda^0(u)}{H_k(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat V_k(s,t)=k\widehat S_k^\dagger(s-)
 \widehat S_k^\dagger(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\{Y_k^\dagger(u)\}^{-2}\,dN_k(u). \tag{5}
\]
After substituting \(dN_k=dM_k+Y_k\lambda^0du\), clipping bounds every inverse risk on every dataset.  Predictable isometry and the stabilized cell expansion give
\[
 E\|\widehat V_k-V_k\|_{\infty,\mathcal I^2}\le Ck^{-1/2}. \tag{6}
\]
Composing (4)--(6), summing the target and donor covariance blocks, and using the deterministic bias bound yields
\[
 E\left[\rho_r^{-2}
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}\}\right]
 \le Ca_{r,n}.                                          \tag{7}
\]

We next control the complement pointwise, rather than multiplying its probability by an unconditional moment.  On every dataset assigned to the regular branch, the hard thresholds and stabilized coefficients give
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r\|_\infty\le C\rho_r^2.            \tag{8}
\]
On the pooled fallback each of the \(n_D\) coefficients has supremum norm at most one, hence
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r\|_\infty\le n_D.                  \tag{9}
\]
Equation (1) also gives \(\|\Sigma_r\|_\infty\le C\rho_r^2\), and \(\rho_r^2\ge n_D^{-1}\).  Therefore, on every dataset,
\[
 \rho_r^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\}
 \le C(1+n_D^2)\mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\}.        \tag{10}
\]
The exponential margin bound implies
\[
 E[\text{left side of (10)}]
 \le Cn_D^2e^{-cn_D}\le Ce^{-c'n_D}.                   \tag{11}
\]
After reducing \(c'\), (11) is dominated by the exponential term in \(a_{r,n}\).  Combining (3), (7), and (11) proves
\[
 E\{\rho_r^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty\}
 \le Ca_{r,n}.                                          \tag{12}
\]
Markov's inequality at level \(a_{r,n}^{1/2}\), intersected with \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) for \(r\ge2\), supplies a measurable covariance event with complement probability at most \(Ca_{r,n}^{1/2}\) and covariance error at most \(C\rho_r^2a_{r,n}^{1/2}\).

For the semimetric, let \(J=(g,g']\) be a grid interval, and let \(N_T(J)\) and \(N_D(J)\) count target-pre and donor failures, omitting \(N_T\) at rank one.  Hazard smoothness gives
\[
 EN_T(J)\le LK_T|J|,
 \qquad EN_D(J)\le Ln_D|J|.                             \tag{13}
\]
The Bernoulli exponential-moment inequality and a union bound over the finite grid give a measurable event on which, simultaneously over all such intervals,
\[
 N_T(J)\le CK_T(|J|+h),
 \qquad N_D(J)\le Cn_D(|J|+h),                           \tag{14}
\]
and whose complement is at most \(C|\mathcal G|^2e^{-cmh}\).  Since \(h\asymp\rho_r^{1/q_\beta}\), \(q_\beta>3/2\), and \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\), this probability is bounded by \(Ca_{r,n}^{1/2}\) on every sufficiently large three-dimensional tail.

On the intersection with the covariance event and \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\), the empirical coefficients remain on the regular branch.  Smooth coefficient changes contribute \(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|^2\); the failures in \(J\), (1), and (14) contribute
\[
 C\{K_T^{-2}N_T(J)+n_D^{-2}N_D(J)\}.
\]
For distinct knots \(|g-g'|\ge h\), so
\[
 \widehat d_r(g,g')^2\le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|.              \tag{15}
\]
Taking expectations in the same calculation yields \(d_r(g,g')^2\le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\).  This intersection is the asserted measurable \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\).

Finally, every oracle increment is a sum of independent centered variables, has variance at most \(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\), and has summand envelope at most \(C\rho_rm^{-1/2}\).  Bernstein's exponential-moment calculation gives
\[
 P\{|Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')|>x\}
 \le2\exp\left[-c\min\left\{
 \frac{x^2}{\rho_r^2|g-g'|},
 \frac{x\sqrt m}{\rho_r}\right\}\right].             \tag{16}
\]
Insert
\[
 x=C\rho_r\left[\{u\log(ep_{\mathcal G})\}^{1/2}
 +m^{-1/2}\log(ep_{\mathcal G})\right]
\]
and take a union bound over at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^2\) signed pairs.  Enlarging \(C\) gives the stated \(2p_{\mathcal G}^{-4}\) bound.  No variance was inverted. \(\square\)
