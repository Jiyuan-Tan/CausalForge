For any declared fixed positive contrast-spectrum and pre-Gram lower bounds, a deterministic singular-value threshold tending to zero can consistently separate exact rank one from that fixed separated rank-two class, and sample splitting can charge its selection error to \(\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\). Uniformity over all unknown positive lower bounds is different: the union contains triangular sequences for which the second singular value or pre-Gram eigenvalue approaches zero. A complete theorem requires an explicit separation sequence and a normalization-preserving testing pair linking that sequence to selection error, target-loading instability, excess expected width, and the common coverage schedule.
