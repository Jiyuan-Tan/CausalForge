\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(k_D=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\), and \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\). Define the channel-specific standardized coupling rates
\[
 a_T(K_T)=K_T^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T),
 \qquad
 a_D(n_D)=n_D^{-1/6}\log^2(e+n_D).
\]
Set
\[
 \chi_r=\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)
       +n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)+\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)
\]
and
\[
 v_r=\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}
 \{K_T^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0K_T}\}
 +n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0n_D}.
\]
By the definition of the finite-tail buffer,
\[
 \omega_r=C_\omega\{\varepsilon^{-1}\chi_r+\varepsilon^{-1/2}v_r\}. \tag{1}
\]

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:kaplan\mbox{-}meier\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}exhaustion}\) to every full donor cell, both deterministic donor folds, and the target-pre cell, and then restrict every process to \(\mathcal G=\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\). The interpolation remainder in that lemma is
\[
 a_{\mathcal G}=I_{\mathcal G}S^0-S^0.
\]
It vanishes at every \(g\in\mathcal G\). Thus no deterministic interpolation term occurs in the following expansion in the finite-dimensional Banach space \(\ell^\infty(\mathcal G)\). The bias on the smaller donor fold is exactly
\[
 C_0e^{-c_0k_D}=C_0\exp\{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\}. \tag{2}
\]
Choose the fixed \(C_\eta\) in \(\eta_D\) larger than \(C_0\) times the uniform derivative envelope of the functional-PCR map derived below. The full-cell donor means then obey, on the grid,
\[
 \widehat s_p-s_p
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}\varphi^s_{p,m,j}+b^s_p+q^s_p,
 \quad
 \|b^s_p\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le\eta_D(K_D),
 \quad
 E\|q^s_p\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le Cn_D^{-1}K_D^{-2}. \tag{3}
\]
The target-pre curve has the analogous gridwise expansion with deterministic bias at most \(Ce^{-c_0K_T}\) and centered second-order remainder with squared grid supremum norm at most \(CK_T^{-2}\).

Let \(Y_m=(b_0^\top z_m,b_1^\top z_m)\) be the population centered joint-period donor curve. For fold \(a\in\{1,2\}\), write
\[
 \widehat Y_m^{(a)}=Y_m+U_m^{(a)}+b_m^{(a)}+q_m^{(a)}. \tag{4}
\]
The two \(U_m^{(a)}\)'s are independent mean-zero martingale averages conditional on the fixed panel. Censor overlap, event overlap, and the hazard bound yield
\[
 \sup_mE\|U_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-1},\qquad
 \sup_m\|b_m^{(a)}\|_2\le Ce^{-c_0k_D},\qquad
 \sup_mE\|q_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-2}. \tag{5}
\]
Expanding the symmetrized cross-fold operator gives
\[
 \widehat\Gamma-\Gamma=\mathbb L_D+E_D, \tag{6}
\]
where \(\mathbb L_D\) is the average of the terms \(Y_m\otimes U_m^{(a)}+U_m^{(a)}\otimes Y_m\). Donor incoherence, the factor envelope, patient independence, and the martingale isometry imply
\[
 \|\mathbb L_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=O_P(n_D^{-1/2}). \tag{7}
\]
Cross-fold independence gives \(E(U_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)})=0\), and independence across donors gives
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mU_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)}\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le CN_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}. \tag{8}
\]
The same calculation for terms containing \(q_m^{(a)}\), together with (2), gives
\[
 \|E_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le C\eta_D(K_D)+O_P(N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}). \tag{9}
\]
Thus cross-folding removes the same-fold expectation \(N_0^{-1}\sum_mE(U_m\otimes U_m)\), but it does not average away product-limit risk-exhaustion bias.

The selected population spectrum is separated from zero by \(\gamma_-^2\), and the target-loading Gram matrix is bounded below by \(\kappa_-I_d\). The spectral resolvent identity and
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1} \tag{10}
\]
show that, whenever \(\|\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\gamma_-^2/4\) and \(\|\widehat H_0-H_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\kappa_-/2\), the rotation-invariant composite map from the donor means, selected positive square root, and target-pre curve to its grid values is twice Fr\'echet differentiable. Its first two derivative norms are bounded by a fixed polynomial in the fixed class constants. Equations (3)--(9), together with \(K_D,N_0,K_T\to\infty\) and \(\eta_D(K_D)\to0\), show that this regular event has probability tending to one uniformly. Internal selected-eigenspace ties cause no difficulty because the fitted curve depends on the selected projector and its positive square root rather than an individual eigenbasis.

Taylor expansion of this grid-valued composite map gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =D_T\mathcal F_{\mathcal G}\!\left[K_T^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_{0,\star,j}\right]
 +D_D\mathcal F_{\mathcal G}\!\left[n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\varphi_{p,m,j}\right]
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r
 \quad\hbox{in }\ell^\infty(\mathcal G). \tag{11}
\]
Define \(\phi_{T,j}=D_T\mathcal F_{\mathcal G}[\varphi_{0,\star,j}]\) and define \(\phi_{D,p,m,j}\) analogously, including the deterministic fold weights. Patient independence makes the target and donor sums independent and mean zero. At rank one, normalization collapse and the explicit pooled branch imply \(D_T\mathcal F_{\mathcal G}=0\), so \(\phi_{T,j}\equiv0\). The choice of \(C_\eta\) and (2)--(9) give
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D). \tag{12}
\]
The second-order Taylor formula, (3), (5), (8), and the exponentially small fallback probability imply
\[
 \sup_P E_P\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\left[
 \mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\{K_T^{-2}+N_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}+e^{-2c_0K_T}\}
 +n_D^{-2}+e^{-2c_0n_D}
 \right]
 \le Cv_r^2. \tag{13}
\]
Because (1) gives \(\omega_r\ge C_\omega v_r/\sqrt\varepsilon\), Markov's inequality and a sufficiently large fixed \(C_\omega\) yield
\[
 \sup_P P\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le\varepsilon/4. \tag{14}
\]

It remains to transfer the random multiplier quantile at the same finite triple. Divide each channel by its own standard error before discretizing the nested counting-process class. Its \(L^2\)-covering number is at most \(C\delta^{-2}\), and censor overlap bounds every integrand. A dyadic-net Lindeberg replacement of one patient at a time in the target array yields absolute Lipschitz-test error at most \(CK_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)\); the same replacement in the donor array yields at most \(Cn_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)\). The empirical derivative differs from its oracle derivative by at most \(C\eta_D(K_D)\), and the first-order process has scale \(\rho_r\). Independence and the triangle inequality give the one-sided Lipschitz comparison error
\[
 C\chi_r=C\left\{\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)
 +n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)+\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)\right\}. \tag{15}
\]
Apply the ramp that equals one below the multiplier critical value and zero one buffer unit above it. Equation (15) gives
\[
 \sup_P P\!\left\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\frac12\omega_r\right\}
 \le1-q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}+\frac{2C\chi_r}{\omega_r}. \tag{16}
\]
For every sufficiently large triple, \(\varepsilon<\alpha\), so \(q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}=1-\alpha+\varepsilon/2\). By (1), increasing \(C_\omega\) makes the last ratio in (16) at most \(\varepsilon/4\). Hence (16) is at most \(\alpha-\varepsilon/4\). This transfer uses no uniform anti-concentration constant, which would fail at the variance-zero normalization point \(t=0\) and on laws with a degenerate channel.

Finally, \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) grows no faster than an iterated logarithm of \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\), and
\[
 \frac{K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)/\varepsilon}{K_T^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_T^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T)}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow0,
\]
\[
 \frac{n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)/\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{n_D^{-1/6}\log^2(e+n_D)}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow0. \tag{17}
\]
The convergence is uniform on every three-dimensional tail because \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\le K_T\) and \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\le n_D\). Likewise,
\[
 \frac{K_T^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{K_T^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_T^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0,
 \qquad
 \frac{N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0,
\]
\[
 \frac{n_D^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{n_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0. \tag{18}
\]
The exponential terms divided by their corresponding standard-error channels also tend to zero, and
\[
 \frac{\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)/\varepsilon}{\rho_r}
 =\frac{C_\eta e^{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor}}{\varepsilon}\to0. \tag{19}
\]
Equations (17)--(19) prove \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) uniformly over every three-dimensional tail, with no comparison between \(K_T\) and \(n_D\). Equations (11)--(16) prove the proposed gridwise statement. The continuous-time interpolation term was absent because the restriction map makes \(a_{\mathcal G}\) exactly zero; it is reintroduced only in the band. \(\square\)
