\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(k_D=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\), and \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\). Define the two channel-specific standardized coupling rates
\[
 a_T(K_T)=K_T^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T),
 \qquad
 a_D(n_D)=n_D^{-1/6}\log^2(e+n_D).
\]
The logarithm in \(a_T\) depends only on the target effective size \(K_T\), and the logarithm in \(a_D\) depends only on the donor effective size \(n_D\). Set
\[
 \chi_r=\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)
       +n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)+\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)
\]
and
\[
 v_r=\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}
 \{K_T^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0K_T}\}
 +n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0n_D}.
\]
The definition of the finite-tail buffer is equivalently
\[
 \omega_r=C_\omega\{\varepsilon^{-1}\chi_r+\varepsilon^{-1/2}v_r\}. \tag{1}
\]

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:kaplan\mbox{-}meier\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}exhaustion}\) to every full donor cell, to both deterministic donor folds, and to the target-pre cell. Its bias bound on the smaller donor fold is exactly
\[
 C_0e^{-c_0k_D}=C_0\exp\{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\}. \tag{2}
\]
Choose the fixed \(C_\eta\) in \(\eta_D\) larger than \(C_0\) times a uniform bound for the first two derivatives of the functional-PCR map derived below. The full-cell donor means then obey
\[
 \widehat s_p-s_p
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}\varphi^s_{p,m,j}+b^s_p+q^s_p,
 \qquad
 \|b^s_p\|_\infty\le\eta_D(K_D),
 \qquad
 E\|q^s_p\|_\infty^2\le Cn_D^{-1}K_D^{-2}. \tag{3}
\]
The target-pre curve has the analogous expansion with deterministic bias at most \(Ce^{-c_0K_T}\) and centered second-order remainder with squared supremum norm at most \(CK_T^{-2}\).

Let \(Y_m=(b_0^\top z_m,b_1^\top z_m)\) be the population centered joint-period donor curve. For fold \(a\in\{1,2\}\), write
\[
 \widehat Y_m^{(a)}=Y_m+U_m^{(a)}+b_m^{(a)}+q_m^{(a)}. \tag{4}
\]
The two \(U_m^{(a)}\)'s are independent mean-zero martingale averages conditional on the fixed panel. Censor overlap, event overlap, and the hazard bound yield
\[
 \sup_mE\|U_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-1},\qquad
 \sup_m\|b_m^{(a)}\|_2\le Ce^{-c_0k_D},\qquad
 \sup_mE\|q_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-2}. \tag{5}
\]
Expanding the symmetrized cross-fold operator gives
\[
 \widehat\Gamma-\Gamma=\mathbb L_D+E_D, \tag{6}
\]
where \(\mathbb L_D\) is the average of the terms \(Y_m\otimes U_m^{(a)}+U_m^{(a)}\otimes Y_m\). Donor incoherence, the factor envelope, patient independence, and the martingale isometry imply
\[
 \|\mathbb L_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=O_P(n_D^{-1/2}). \tag{7}
\]
Cross-fold independence gives \(E(U_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)})=0\), and independence across donors gives
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mU_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)}\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le CN_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}. \tag{8}
\]
The same calculation for terms containing \(q_m^{(a)}\), together with (2), gives the correctly typed bound
\[
 \|E_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le C\eta_D(K_D)+O_P(N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}). \tag{9}
\]
Thus cross-folding removes the same-fold expectation \(N_0^{-1}\sum_mE(U_m\otimes U_m)\), but it does not average away product-limit risk-exhaustion bias.

The selected population spectrum is separated from zero by \(\gamma_-^2\), and the target-loading Gram matrix is bounded below by \(\kappa_-I_d\). The spectral resolvent identity and
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1} \tag{10}
\]
show that, whenever \(\|\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\gamma_-^2/4\) and \(\|\widehat H_0-H_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\kappa_-/2\), the rotation-invariant composite map from the donor means, selected positive square root, and target-pre curve to \(s_1+b_1^\top z_\star\) is twice Fr\'echet differentiable. Its first two derivative norms are bounded by a fixed polynomial in the fixed class constants. Equations (3)--(9), together with \(K_D,N_0,K_T\to\infty\) and \(\eta_D(K_D)\to0\), show that this regular event has probability tending to one uniformly. Internal selected-eigenspace ties cause no difficulty because the fitted curve depends on the selected projector and its positive square root rather than an individual eigenbasis.

Taylor expansion of the composite map gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =D_T\mathcal F\!\left[K_T^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_{0,\star,j}\right]
 +D_D\mathcal F\!\left[n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\varphi_{p,m,j}\right]
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r. \tag{11}
\]
Define \(\phi_{T,j}=D_T\mathcal F[\varphi_{0,\star,j}]\) and define \(\phi_{D,p,m,j}\) analogously, including the deterministic fold weights. Patient independence makes the target and donor sums independent and mean zero. The choice of \(C_\eta\) and (2)--(9) give
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_\infty\le\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D). \tag{12}
\]
After placing the target-pre and pooled rank-one exponential biases in \(Q_r\), the second-order Taylor formula, (3), (5), (8), and the exponentially small fallback probability imply
\[
 \sup_P E_P\|Q_r\|_\infty^2
 \le C\left[
 \mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\{K_T^{-2}+N_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}+e^{-2c_0K_T}\}
 +n_D^{-2}+e^{-2c_0n_D}
 \right]
 \le Cv_r^2. \tag{13}
\]
By (1), \(\omega_r\ge C_\omega v_r/\sqrt\varepsilon\). Markov's inequality and a sufficiently large fixed \(C_\omega\) therefore give
\[
 \sup_P P\{\|Q_r\|_\infty>\omega_r/2\}\le\varepsilon/4. \tag{14}
\]

It remains to transfer the random multiplier quantile at the same finite triple. Divide each channel by its own standard error before discretizing the nested class \(\{\mathbf 1_{[0,t]}:t\in\mathcal I\}\). Its \(L^2\)-covering number is at most \(C\delta^{-2}\), and censor overlap bounds every counting-process integrand. A dyadic-net Lindeberg replacement of one patient at a time in the target array yields absolute Lipschitz-test error at most \(CK_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)\); the same replacement in the donor array yields at most \(Cn_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)\). The empirical derivative differs from its oracle derivative by at most \(C\eta_D(K_D)\), and the first-order process has scale \(\rho_r\). Independence of the two arrays and the triangle inequality consequently give the one-sided Lipschitz comparison error
\[
 C\chi_r
 =C\left\{\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)
 +n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)+\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)\right\}. \tag{15}
\]
Apply the ramp that equals one below the multiplier critical value and zero one buffer unit above it. Equation (15) gives
\[
 \sup_P P\!\left\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\frac12\omega_r\right\}
 \le1-q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}+\frac{2C\chi_r}{\omega_r}. \tag{16}
\]
For every sufficiently large triple, \(\varepsilon<\alpha\), so \(q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}=1-\alpha+\varepsilon/2\). By (1), increasing \(C_\omega\) makes the last ratio in (16) at most \(\varepsilon/4\). Hence (16) is at most \(\alpha-\varepsilon/4\). This transfer uses no uniform anti-concentration constant, which would be invalid at the variance-zero normalization point \(t=0\) and on laws with a degenerate channel.

We finally verify the arbitrary-relative-growth claim. Since \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) grows no faster than an iterated logarithm of \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\),
\[
 \frac{K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)/\varepsilon}{K_T^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_T^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T)}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow0, \tag{17}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)/\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{n_D^{-1/6}\log^2(e+n_D)}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow0. \tag{18}
\]
The convergence is uniform on every three-dimensional tail: in (17), \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\le K_T\), and in (18), \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\le n_D\), so the iterated logarithm is controlled by the same effective size as the decaying power. Likewise,
\[
 \frac{K_T^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{K_T^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_T^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0,
 \qquad
 \frac{N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0, \tag{19}
\]
\[
 \frac{n_D^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{n_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0. \tag{20}
\]
The exponential terms divided by their corresponding standard-error channels also tend to zero, and
\[
 \frac{\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)/\varepsilon}{\rho_r}
 =\frac{C_\eta e^{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor}}{\varepsilon}\to0. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (17)--(21) prove from (1) that \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) uniformly over every three-dimensional tail, with no comparison between \(K_T\) and \(n_D\). Equations (11)--(16) prove all remaining assertions. \(\square\)
