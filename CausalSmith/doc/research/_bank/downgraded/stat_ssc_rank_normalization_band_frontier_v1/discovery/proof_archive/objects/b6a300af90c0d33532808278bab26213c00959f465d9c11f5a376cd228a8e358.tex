\textbf{Proof.}
Suppress the cell label.  For patient \(j\), define
\[
 N_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\leq u,\Delta_j=1\},\qquad
 Y_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\geq u\},\qquad
 M_j(u)=N_j(u)-\int_0^uY_j(v)\lambda^0(v)\,dv.             \tag{1}
\]
By control consistency and independent censoring, the last integral is the
compensator of \(N_j\).  Patient independence makes the processes \(M_j\)
independent, mean-zero, square-integrable martingales.  Put
\(Y=\sum_{j=1}^kY_j\), \(N=\sum_{j=1}^kN_j\),
\(M=\sum_{j=1}^kM_j\), and
\[
 H(u)=E Y_j(u)=S^0(u)G(u).
\]
The event- and censor-overlap assumptions imply
\[
 H(u)\geq h_0:=s_-g_->0,qquad u\in\mathcal I.             \tag{2}
\]

We first make the treatment of ties explicit.  At a tied failure time, order
failures by patient index and remove each earlier ordered failure from the risk
set before processing the next.  If the pre-tie risk set is \(y\) and there are
\(a\) failures, then
\[
 prod_{q=0}^{a-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{y-q}\right)=1-\frac{a}{y}. \tag{3}
\]
Thus this measurable ordered convention agrees with the ordinary product-limit
factor.  On this ordered event axis replace every risk denominator by
\(Y^\dagger=max\{Y,\ell_k\}\), where
\(\ell_k=max\{1,kh_0/8\}\).  Every resulting factor lies in \([0,1]\).
If \(Y(\bar\tau)\geq\ell_k\), monotonicity of \(Y\) gives
\(Y(u)\geq\ell_k\) at every earlier event.  Hence no denominator is clipped,
and (3) proves assertion (i).

The variables \(Y_j(\bar\tau)\) are independent Bernoulli variables and
\(\mu:=E Y(\bar\tau)\geq kh_0\).  For \(s>0\),
\[
 E e^{-sY(\bar\tau)}
 \leq \exp\{(e^{-s}-1)\mu\}.
\]
Exponential Markov inequality, optimized at
\(s=-\log(1-\delta)\), and
\((1-\delta)\log(1-\delta)+\delta\geq\delta^2/2\) yield
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)\leq(1-\delta)\mu\}
 \leq e^{-\delta^2\mu/2}.                                 \tag{4}
\]
Taking \(\delta=7/8\) proves
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)<kh_0/8\}
 \leq e^{-49h_0k/128}
 \leq e^{-h_0k/3}=e^{-c_0k}.                              \tag{5}
\]

We next prove the deterministic guards.  For
\(f(y)=1-1/\max\{y,\ell_k\}\),
\[
 |f(y+e)-f(y)|\leq |e|\ell_k^{-2},\qquad |e|\leq1.        \tag{6}
\]
Changing one record inserts and deletes at most two ordered failure factors and
changes each remaining ordered risk count by at most one.  The telescoping
identity for products of numbers in \([0,1]\), (6), and the fact that there are
at most \(k\) factors give, for all sufficiently large \(k\),
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathcal I}
 |\widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O)-\widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O')|
 \leq 2\ell_k^{-1}+k\ell_k^{-2}\leq C/k.                 \tag{7}
\]
Piecewise-linear interpolation is a contraction in supremum norm.  A patient
has at most one observed failure, so its observed product-limit derivative is
bounded on every dataset by
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathcal I}\widehat S_k^\dagger(t-)
 \int_0^t\{Y^\dagger(u)\}^{-1}\,dN_j(u)
 \leq\ell_k^{-1}\leq C/k.                                \tag{8}
\]
This proves (iii) and (iv), including all exhausted-risk configurations.

For the expansion, put \(R=\widehat S_k^\dagger/S^0\).  The two
product-integral equations, (1), and integration by parts give the exact clipped
ratio identity
\[
 R(t)-1
 =-\int_0^tR(u-)\{Y^\dagger(u)\}^{-1}\,dM(u)
  +\int_0^tR(u-)\left\{1-\frac{Y(u)}{Y^\dagger(u)}\right\}
             \lambda^0(u)\,du.                            \tag{9}
\]
This is an equality, not a good-risk-set approximation.  Since every clipped
product factor is in \([0,1]\), \(0\leq R\leq s_-^{-1}\).  The drift in
(9) is supported on \(\{Y(u)<\ell_k\}\), which by monotonicity is contained
in \(\{Y(\bar\tau)<\ell_k\}\).  Also
\(\int_0^{\bar\tau}\lambda^0(u)\,du\leq L\bar\tau\).
Writing \(A_k\) for the drift in (9) after multiplication by \(S^0(t)\),
(5) therefore gives
\[
 \|EA_k\|_\infty\leq Ce^{-c_0k},\qquad
 E\|A_k-EA_k\|_\infty^2\leq Ce^{-c_0k}.                 \tag{10}
\]

Define the independent mean-zero influence processes
\[
 \varphi_{k,j}(t)
 =-S^0(t)\int_0^tH(u)^{-1}\,dM_j(u).                      \tag{11}
\]
After subtracting \(k^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_{k,j}\) from (9), the remaining
martingale term is
\[
 V_k(t)=-S^0(t)\int_0^t
 \left\{\frac{R(u-)}{Y^\dagger(u)}-\frac{1}{kH(u)}\right\}
 dM(u).                                                     \tag{12}
\]
Let \(\mathcal E_k=\{Y(\bar\tau)\geq kh_0/2\}\).  On
\(\mathcal E_k\), \(Y^\dagger=Y\), and
\[
 \frac{R}{Y}-\frac{1}{kH}
 =\frac{R-1}{Y}-\frac{Y-kH}{kHY}.                          \tag{13}
\]
Stop (9) just before \(Y<kh_0/2\).  The predictable isometry, Doob's
\(L^2\) inequality, (2), and independence give
\[
 E\{\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}\|R-1\|_\infty^2\}\leq Ck^{-1},
 \qquad E\{Y(u)-kH(u)\}^2\leq k.                          \tag{14}
\]
Substitution of (13)--(14) into the predictable quadratic variation gives
\[
 E\left[\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}\int_0^{\bar\tau}
 \left\{\frac{R}{Y^\dagger}-\frac{1}{kH}\right\}^2
 Y\lambda^0\right]\leq Ck^{-2}.                          \tag{15}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_k^c\), both reciprocal denominators are at most \(C/k\).
The fourth-moment martingale inequality followed by Cauchy--Schwarz and (4)
gives
\[
 E\left[\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k^c}\int_0^{\bar\tau}
 \left\{\frac{R}{Y^\dagger}-\frac{1}{kH}\right\}^2
 Y\lambda^0\right]
 \leq Ck^{-1}e^{-ck}\leq Ck^{-2}.                         \tag{16}
\]
Doob's inequality applied to (12) now yields
\(E\|V_k\|_\infty^2\leq Ck^{-2}\).  Set
\[
 b_k^\dagger=EA_k,
 \qquad q_k^\dagger=V_k+A_k-EA_k.                         \tag{17}
\]
Equations (10), (15)--(17) prove (v) on the event-time axis.  Applying
\(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) preserves expectations and all supremum bounds,
and changes the deterministic center to
\(\widetilde S^0=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0\).

It remains to justify the donor-average assertion without a grid union or a
regularity claim about rare paths.  We use the following elementary ordered
bounded-variation inequality.  If \(F_1,\ldots,F_N\) are independent random
c\`adl\`ag functions with \(F_m(0)=0\), then
\[
 E\left\|N^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^N(F_m-EF_m)\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq \frac{16}{N^2}\sum_{m=1}^NE\{\operatorname{TV}(F_m)^2\}. \tag{18}
\]
For completeness, first condition on deterministic bounded-variation paths and
use their Jordan decompositions.  A nonnegative variation component has the form
\(F_m^+(t)=\mu_m^+([0,t])\), where \(\mu_m^+\) is a finite positive measure
with mass \(a_m^+\).  Introduce, only for this calculation, an auxiliary
\(T_m^+\) with conditional distribution \(\mu_m^+/a_m^+\).  Conditional Jensen
inequality gives
\[
 E_\epsilon\sup_t\left|\sum_m\epsilon_mF_m^+(t)\right|^2
 \leq E_{\epsilon,T}\sup_t
 \left|\sum_m\epsilon_ma_m^+\mathbf1\{T_m^+\leq t\}\right|^2. \tag{18a}
\]
Given the auxiliary times, ordering donors by \(T_m^+\) turns the process on the
right into the partial sums of the independent variables
\(\epsilon_ma_m^+\), in an order independent of their signs.  Doob's scalar
\(L^2\) maximal inequality bounds (18a) by
\(4\sum_m(a_m^+)^2\).  Apply the same argument to the negative variation
components and use summation by parts to include the initial values.  Since
\(a_m^++a_m^-\leq\operatorname{TV}(F_m)\), conditional symmetrization with an
independent copy gives (18), after enlarging the universal numerical constant.
Step-function approximation in variation norm and Fatou's lemma remove atoms
and continuity restrictions.  This is a time-ordered bounded-variation and
donor-index Doob argument; neither a uniform Lipschitz modulus nor the number of
grid points occurs.

For cell \(m\), let \(A_{k,m}\) be the rare drift in (10).  The cumulative
integral in (9) is increasing, \(S_m^0\) has total variation at most one, and the
integral is bounded by a model-class constant.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(A_{k,m})
 \leq C\mathbf 1\{Y_m(\bar\tau)<\ell_k\}.                 \tag{19}
\]
Apply (18), (19), and (5) to the independent donor cells.  With
\(U_m=A_{k,m}-EA_{k,m}\),
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}U_m\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq CN_0^{-1}e^{-c_0k}
 \leq C(N_0k^2)^{-1}                                      \tag{20}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(k\).  This proves the last assertion and completes
the proof. \(\square\)
