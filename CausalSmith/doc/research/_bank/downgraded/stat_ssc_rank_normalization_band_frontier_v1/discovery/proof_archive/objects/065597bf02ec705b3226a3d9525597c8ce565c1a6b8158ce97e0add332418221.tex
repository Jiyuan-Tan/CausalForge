\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\),
\(p_{\mathcal G}=2|\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}|\), and abbreviate
\(n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\), \(\delta_{r,n}\), \(\chi_{r,n}\), and
\(\tau_{r,n}\) by \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\), \(\delta_n\), \(\chi_n\), and
\(\tau_n\).  The factor two in \(p_{\mathcal G}\) records both signs, so the
maximum of the resulting vector is \(\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\).

We first record the two quantitative facts supplied by the patient-level
linearization lemmas.  At rank one,
\(\mathrm{lem:pooled\mbox{-}noniid\mbox{-}km\mbox{-}linearization}\) applies to
the pooled non-identically censored donor-post sample.  At fixed rank at least
two,
\(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}gridwise\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}transfer}\)
applies to the target-pre and donor blocks.  In both cases, after division by
\(\rho_r\), every weighted patient contribution has deterministic supremum
envelope at most \(C n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1/2}\).  For \(r\ge2\), this follows
separately from
\[
 \frac{C}{K_T\rho_r}\le \frac{C}{\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}},
 \qquad
 \frac{C}{n_D\rho_r}\le \frac{C}{\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}},             \tag{1}
\]
and the rank-one assertion is the second inequality with
\(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=n_D\).  The inequalities use only
\(\rho_r\ge K_T^{-1/2}\) and \(\rho_r\ge n_D^{-1/2}\) in the blocks that
are present.

Let \(\Sigma_r\) be the covariance kernel of \(Z_r\), and let
\(\widehat\Sigma_r\) be the conditional covariance kernel of the explicit
multiplier.  Define, as in the corrected band construction,
\[
 a_{r,n}=n_D^{-1/2}+\mathbf1\{r\ge2\}
 \left\{K_T^{-1/2}+K_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)
 +e^{-c_0K_T/2}+e^{-c_0n_D/2}\right\},
 \qquad c_0=s_-g_-/3.                                      \tag{2}
\]
The covariance conclusions of the two patient-level lemmas give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P\left[
 \rho_r^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_{infty,\mathcal I^2}
 \right]\le C a_{r,n}.                                    \tag{3}
\]
For traceability, the terms in (3) have distinct origins.  The empirical
target-pre covariance contributes \(K_T^{-1/2}\); the independent pooled
donor covariance contributes \(n_D^{-1/2}\); replacing cellwise oracle risk
denominators by stabilized empirical denominators contributes
\(K_D^{-1/2}\).  The selected projector, positive square root, and inverse
pre-Gram derivatives are Lipschitz on the aggregate regular event by the
fixed spectral and pre-Gram gaps, so substituting their estimated arguments
contributes \(K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)\).  Finally, the aggregate
fallback events contribute the two exponential terms in (2).  Odd--even
cross-fitting makes the donor covariance products conditionally centered;
there is no all-donor-cells-good union bound.  The bounded-variation
martingale kernels and the fixed-rank derivative map also give, for the
canonical semimetrics of the oracle Gaussian process \(G_r\) and the
conditional multiplier Gaussian process \(\widehat G_r\),
\[
 d_r(s,t)+\widehat d_r(s,t)
 \le C\rho_r|s-t|^{1/2},\qquad s,t\in\mathcal I,            \tag{4}
\]
on the covariance-regular event.  Equations (3)--(4) are uniform in the
number of canonical grid knots: their proof uses the polynomial entropy of
the one-parameter counting-process kernels, rather than a union bound over
the grid.

We next compare the oracle linear process with its Gaussian analogue.  List
the signed, grid-evaluated, \(\rho_r\)-normalized patient summands as
independent vectors \(X_i\in\mathbb R^{p_{\mathcal G}}\).  By (1),
\[
 \sum_iE_P\|X_i\|_2^3
 \le C p_{\mathcal G}^{3/2}
 \{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}\}
 \le C p_{\mathcal G}^{3/2}n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1/2}.        \tag{5}
\]
The absent target block is omitted when \(r=1\).  Apply
\(\mathrm{lem:yurinskii\mbox{-}degeneracy\mbox{-}robust\mbox{-}coupling}\)
with coupling distance proportional to
\(B_{r,n}^{1/4}\), where
\[
 B_{r,n}=p_{\mathcal G}^{3/2}n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1/2}.       \tag{6}
\]
After enlarging the fixed constant in \(\chi_n\), the coupling implies,
for every \(x\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 P\{\|G_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\}
 \le P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
          \le x+\rho_r\chi_n/3\}+\chi_n/3.                \tag{7}
\]
This use of Euclidean coupling requires no inverse covariance matrix.

We now compare the oracle and estimated Gaussian laws without imposing a
variance floor.  Markov's inequality in (3), with
\(v_n=a_{r,n}^{1/2}\), gives an observed-data event \(\mathcal E_n\) such
that
\[
 \sup_P P(\mathcal E_n^c)\le Cv_n,
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal I^2}
 \le C\rho_r^2v_n\quad\hbox{on }\mathcal E_n.              \tag{8}
\]
Take a deterministic auxiliary time net of mesh
\(u_n=v_n^{1/3}\), with \(k_n\le C/u_n\) knots.  On this auxiliary net,
the covariance matrices differ entrywise by at most \(C\rho_r^2v_n\),
hence in operator norm by at most \(Ck_n\rho_r^2v_n\).  The
Powers--St\o rmer square-root inequality and the optimal common-normal
coupling therefore give
\[
 E\|G_r-\widehat G_r\|_{2,\mathrm{aux}}
 \le C\rho_r k_nv_n^{1/2}=C\rho_r v_n^{1/6}.               \tag{9}
\]
No eigenvalue is divided by in this step.  From (4), the covering number of
either process under its canonical semimetric is at most \(C\epsilon^{-2}\).
The elementary chaining sum over dyadic time nets gives
\[
 E\sup_{|s-t|\le u_n}|G_r(s)-G_r(t)|
 +E_\xi\sup_{|s-t|\le u_n}|\widehat G_r(s)-\widehat G_r(t)|
 \le C\rho_r\{u_n\log(e/u_n)\}^{1/2}.                     \tag{10}
\]
Combining (9)--(10) with Markov's inequality yields, conditionally on the
data in \(\mathcal E_n\), a coupling for which
\[
 P\left\{
 \big|\|G_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
       -\|\widehat G_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\big|
 >\rho_r\chi_n/3\ \middle|\ O\right\}\le\chi_n/3.        \tag{11}
\]
Indeed, the right side required by (9)--(10) is at most a constant times
\(a_{r,n}^{1/24}\{1+|\log a_{r,n}|\}\), which is dominated by the more
conservative \(a_{r,n}^{1/48}\{1+|\log a_{r,n}|\}\) term used in
\(\chi_n\).  The probability in (8) is dominated by the same term.

Let
\[
 F_{r,P}(x)=P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\},
 \qquad
 \widehat F_r(x)=P_\xi\{\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x
 \mid O\}.
\]
Equations (7) and (11), with constants absorbed into \(C_\chi\), imply on
\(\mathcal E_n\), simultaneously for every real \(x\),
\[
 \widehat F_r(x)
 \le F_{r,P}(x+\rho_r\chi_n)+\chi_n.                       \tag{12}
\]
This uniform-in-\(x\) inequality is used through a deterministic quantile
sandwich; we do not insert a random critical value into an unconditional
distribution function.

To verify the deterministic rates, the canonical grid bound and
\(\rho_r\ge n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1/2}\) give
\[
 p_{\mathcal G}\le Cn_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/(2q_\beta)},
 \qquad
 B_{r,n}\le Cn_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-\kappa_\beta},
 \qquad
 \kappa_\beta=\frac12-\frac{3}{4q_\beta}>0,               \tag{13}
\]
where positivity follows exactly from \(\beta>1/2\).  Also, on every tail
with \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\ge A\), (2) is bounded by
\(CA^{-1/2}+Ce^{-cA}\).  Hence \(\chi_n\to0\) uniformly on every
three-dimensional tail.  Because
\(\delta_n=\min\{\alpha/2,\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)/2\}\)
decreases only at the displayed iterated-logarithm rate, (13) and (2) imply
that, for all sufficiently large evaluated triples,
\[
 2\chi_n\le\delta_n/2.                                     \tag{14}
\]

Let \(q_n=1-\alpha+\delta_n\), and let
\(c_{r,P}(u)=\inf\{x\ge0:F_{r,P}(x)\ge u\}\).  The conditional Gaussian
maximum is a Borel function of a finite standard-normal vector and the
observed covariance matrix.  Its conditional distribution function is
jointly measurable in \((O,x)\); taking the infimum over nonnegative
rationals proves measurability of the stated generalized inverse
\(\widetilde c_r^\xi\).  This remains true if the covariance is singular.
The generalized-inverse identity gives
\(\widehat F_r(\widetilde c_r^\xi)\ge q_n\), including the case in which
the entire process is zero.  Applying (12) at this value pathwise on
\(\mathcal E_n\) gives
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi+\rho_r\chi_n
 \ge c_{r,P}(q_n-\chi_n).                                  \tag{15}
\]
The right side is deterministic for fixed \(P\).  Therefore (8), (14), and
(15), now applied to the actual oracle statistic, yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
       \le\widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_n\}
 &\ge P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
       \le c_{r,P}(q_n-\chi_n),\ \mathcal E_n\}\\
 &\ge q_n-\chi_n-P(\mathcal E_n^c)\\
 &\ge1-\alpha+\delta_n/2.                                 \tag{16}
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\tau_n=\rho_r\chi_n\).  This is the promised degeneracy-robust
random-quantile argument.

Finally, \(q_n\le1-\alpha/2\).  Conditional Markov inequality therefore
gives
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi
 \le\left(\frac{2}{\alpha}
 E_\xi\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{infty,\mathcal G}^2\right)^{1/2}. \tag{17}
\]
The second-moment conclusions of the pooled and stabilized patient-level
lemmas bound the expectation of the quantity under the square root by
\(C\rho_r^2\).  Jensen's inequality in (17) proves
\(\sup_PE_P\widetilde c_r^\xi\le C\rho_r\).  Equations (13)--(14) also
give \(\tau_n=o(\rho_r)\) uniformly.  This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
