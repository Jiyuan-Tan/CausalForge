\textbf{Proof.}
Fix one observed control cell and suppress its \((p,m)\) subscripts until the donor-averaging step.  Let \(\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}\) denote the un-interpolated product-limit step curve.  For \(1\le j\le k\), define
\[
 N_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\le u,\Delta_j=1\},\qquad
 Y_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\ge u\},\qquad
 Y(u)=\sum_{j=1}^kY_j(u),
\]
using the predictable left-continuous version of the at-risk process at jump times.  Independent censoring implies that the compensator of \(N_j\) is
\[
 \int_0^uY_j(v)\lambda^0(v)\,dv.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 M_j(u)=N_j(u)-\int_0^uY_j(v)\lambda^0(v)\,dv
\]
is a mean-zero square-integrable martingale.  Patient independence makes these martingales independent.  Put
\[
 M=\sum_{j=1}^kM_j,\qquad
 J(u)=\mathbf 1\{Y(u)>0\},\qquad
 H(u)=E Y_j(u)=S^0(u)G(u).
\]
The last equality uses independent censoring and continuity of the event-time law induced by its hazard.  Censoring and event overlap give
\[
 H(u)\ge h_0:=s_-g_->0,
 \qquad u\in\mathcal I.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Hazard smoothness gives
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal I}\lambda^0(u)\le L,
 \qquad
 \Lambda^0(\bar\tau):=\int_0^{\bar\tau}\lambda^0(u)\,du
 \le L\bar\tau.                                               \tag{2}
\]

Set \(R=\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}/S^0\).  The product-integral equations are
\[
 d\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}(u)
 =-\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}(u-)\frac{J(u)}{Y(u)}\,dN(u),
 \qquad
 dS^0(u)=-S^0(u)\lambda^0(u)\,du,
\]
where \(J/Y\) is defined to be zero when the risk set is empty.  Integration by parts and \(dN=dM+Y\lambda^0du\) give the exact identity
\[
 R(t)-1
 =-\int_0^tR(u-)\frac{J(u)}{Y(u)}\,dM(u)
  +\int_0^tR(u-)\{1-J(u)\}\lambda^0(u)\,du.                 \tag{3}
\]
Thus the identity never divides by zero.  Moreover, event overlap and \(0\le\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}\le1\) imply
\[
 0\le R(u)\le s_-^{-1}.                                      \tag{4}
\]

Define the un-interpolated influence process
\[
 \varphi_j^{\mathrm{PL}}(t)
 =-S^0(t)\int_0^tH(u)^{-1}\,dM_j(u).                         \tag{5}
\]
Its deterministic integrand is bounded by \(h_0^{-1}\), so the \(\varphi_j^{\mathrm{PL}}\) are independent and mean zero.  Subtracting \(k^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_j^{\mathrm{PL}}\) from (3), after multiplication by \(S^0(t)\), yields
\[
 \widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}(t)-S^0(t)
 -k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_j^{\mathrm{PL}}(t)
 =V_k(t)+A_k(t),                                               \tag{6}
\]
where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_k(t)&=-S^0(t)\int_0^tD_k(u)\,dM(u),\\
 D_k(u)&=R(u-)\frac{J(u)}{Y(u)}-\frac{1}{kH(u)},\\
 A_k(t)&=S^0(t)\int_0^tR(u-)\{1-J(u)\}\lambda^0(u)\,du.
 \end{aligned}                                                 \tag{7}
\]
All integrands in (5) and (7) are predictable.

We first isolate the exponentially small risk-exhaustion bias.  The stochastic integral defining \(V_k\) is integrable and has expectation zero.  Since the risk indicators are nonincreasing, \(A_k\) can be nonzero only on
\[
 \mathcal X_k=\{Y(\bar\tau)=0\}.
\]
Patient independence and (1) imply
\[
 \Pr(\mathcal X_k)=\{1-H(\bar\tau)\}^k
 \le e^{-kH(\bar\tau)}\le e^{-h_0k}.                         \tag{8}
\]
By (2), (4), and Fubini's theorem,
\[
 \|EA_k\|_\infty
 \le s_-^{-1}L\bar\tau e^{-h_0k}.                            \tag{9}
\]
Set
\[
 b_k^{\mathrm{PL}}=EA_k,
 \qquad
 q_k^{\mathrm{PL}}=V_k+A_k-EA_k.                             \tag{10}
\]
Equations (6) and (10) give the desired step-curve decomposition, and \(Eq_k^{\mathrm{PL}}=0\).  Since \(c_0=h_0/3<h_0\), a fixed enlargement of the constant gives
\[
 \|b_k^{\mathrm{PL}}\|_\infty\le C_0e^{-c_0k}.             \tag{11}
\]

We next bound the second-order remainder.  Let
\[
 \mathcal E_k=\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge kh_0/2\}.
\]
The variable \(Y(\bar\tau)\) is binomial with success probability at least \(h_0\).  The Chernoff bound, obtained by applying exponential Markov inequality to \(e^{-sY(\bar\tau)}\) and optimizing over \(s>0\), therefore gives
\[
 \Pr(\mathcal E_k^c)\le e^{-c_1k}                            \tag{12}
\]
for a fixed \(c_1=c_1(h_0)>0\).  On \(\mathcal E_k\), monotonicity gives \(Y(u)\ge kh_0/2\) and \(J(u)=1\) throughout \(\mathcal I\), and hence
\[
 D_k(u)=\frac{R(u-)-1}{Y(u)}
 -\frac{Y(u)-kH(u)}{kH(u)Y(u)}.                              \tag{13}
\]

Stop (3) the first time that the risk set falls below \(kh_0/2\).  Before this time the exhaustion term is zero, and the predictable quadratic variation of the stopped stochastic integral is bounded, by (2) and (4), as
\[
 \int_0^{\bar\tau}R(u-)^2\frac{J(u)}{Y(u)}\lambda^0(u)\,du
 \le Ck^{-1}.                                                 \tag{14}
\]
The square-integrable martingale maximal inequality gives \(E\sup_{u\le\bar\tau}|U(u)|^2\le4E\langle U\rangle(\bar\tau)\).  On \(\mathcal E_k\) the stopping time exceeds \(\bar\tau\), so (14) yields
\[
 E\bigl[\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}\|R-1\|_\infty^2\bigr]
 \le Ck^{-1}.                                                 \tag{15}
\]
For every fixed \(u\), patient independence gives
\[
 E\{Y(u)-kH(u)\}^2=kH(u)\{1-H(u)\}\le k.                  \tag{16}
\]
Using (13), \(Y\ge kh_0/2\), (2), (15), and (16), we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E\left[\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}
   \int_0^{\bar\tau}D_k(u)^2Y(u)\lambda^0(u)\,du\right]
 &\le \frac{C}{k}E\bigl[\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}\|R-1\|_\infty^2\bigr]\\
 &\quad+\frac{C}{k^3}\int_0^{\bar\tau}
       E\{Y(u)-kH(u)\}^2\lambda^0(u)\,du\\
 &\le Ck^{-2}.
 \end{aligned}                                                 \tag{17}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_k^c\), (1), (2), and (4) yield the pointwise bound
\[
 D_k(u)^2Y(u)
 \le 2s_-^{-2}\frac{J(u)}{Y(u)}+\frac{2Y(u)}{k^2h_0^2}
 \le C.
\]
Consequently, (12) implies
\[
 E\left[\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k^c}
   \int_0^{\bar\tau}D_k(u)^2Y(u)\lambda^0(u)\,du\right]
 \le Ce^{-c_1k}\le Ck^{-2}.                                 \tag{18}
\]
The martingale maximal inequality and (17)--(18) show
\[
 E\|V_k\|_\infty^2\le Ck^{-2}.                              \tag{19}
\]
The process \(A_k\) vanishes off \(\mathcal X_k\), and (2)--(4) bound its supremum norm by a fixed constant.  Thus (8) gives
\[
 E\|A_k-EA_k\|_\infty^2\le Ce^{-h_0k}\le Ck^{-2}.           \tag{20}
\]
Combining (10), (19), and (20), and enlarging \(C_0\) once more, proves
\[
 E\|q_k^{\mathrm{PL}}\|_\infty^2\le C_0k^{-2}.             \tag{21}
\]
Every constant through (21) depends only on the fixed overlap, hazard, and horizon bounds, so the estimates are uniform over the exact-rank model class.

We now prove the asserted cross-donor rate in supremum norm.  Restore the donor index and write
\[
 U_m(t)=\int_0^tD_{m,k}(u)\,dM_m(u),
 \qquad
 V_{m,k}(t)=-S_m^0(t)U_m(t).
\]
The calculation in (17)--(18) gives, uniformly in \(m\),
\[
 E\int_0^{\bar\tau}D_{m,k}(u)^2Y_m(u)\lambda_m^0(u)\,du
 \le Ck^{-2}.                                                   \tag{22}
\]
Integration by parts gives
\[
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal D}S_m^0(t)U_m(t)
 =\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\int_0^tS_m^0(u)\,dU_m(u)
  +\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\int_0^tU_m(u)\,dS_m^0(u).          \tag{23}
\]
The first term on the right is a martingale.  Independence of donor cells makes all cross predictable covariations zero, so (22) and the maximal inequality bound its expected squared supremum by \(CN_0k^{-2}\).  For the second term, use \(dS_m^0(u)=-S_m^0(u)\lambda_m^0(u)du\), Cauchy--Schwarz in \(u\), and independence together with \(EU_m(u)=0\):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E\sup_{t\in\mathcal I}
 \left|\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\int_0^tU_m(u)\,dS_m^0(u)\right|^2
 &\le \bar\tau\int_0^{\bar\tau}
 E\left|\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}S_m^0(u)\lambda_m^0(u)U_m(u)\right|^2du\\
 &=\bar\tau\int_0^{\bar\tau}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 S_m^0(u)^2\lambda_m^0(u)^2EU_m(u)^2du\\
 &\le CN_0k^{-2}.
 \end{aligned}                                                 \tag{24}
\]
After division by \(N_0^2\), (23)--(24) prove
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}V_{m,k}\right\|_\infty^2
 \le \frac{C}{N_0k^2}.                                      \tag{25}
\]

The centered exhaustion processes have the same donor rate.  Each \(A_{m,k}\) vanishes unless \(\mathcal X_{m,k}\) occurs.  Differentiating (7) away from its exhaustion time gives
\[
 A_{m,k}'(t)=-\lambda_m^0(t)A_{m,k}(t)
 +\widehat S_m^{\mathrm{PL}}(t)\{1-J_m(t)\}\lambda_m^0(t).
\]
Thus \(A_{m,k}(0)=0\) and
\[
 |A_{m,k}(t)-A_{m,k}(s)|
 \le C|t-s|\mathbf 1_{\mathcal X_{m,k}},
 \qquad
 \Pr(\mathcal X_{m,k})\le e^{-h_0k}.                        \tag{26}
\]
For completeness, condition on the \(A_{m,k}\)'s and symmetrize by independent Rademacher signs \(\epsilon_m\).  On a dyadic partition of \(\mathcal I\) at level \(\ell\), (26) makes every normalized signed adjacent increment sub-Gaussian with variance proxy at most
\[
 C2^{-2\ell}N_0^{-2}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \mathbf 1_{\mathcal X_{m,k}}.
\]
Exponential Markov inequality and a union bound over at most \(2^{\ell+1}\) increments bound the conditional expected maximum at level \(\ell\) by a constant times
\[
 2^{-\ell}\sqrt{\ell+1}\,
 N_0^{-1}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \mathbf 1_{\mathcal X_{m,k}}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The sum over \(\ell\) is finite; integrating the same sub-Gaussian tail gives the corresponding squared bound.  Ghost-sample symmetrization and (26) therefore yield
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \{A_{m,k}-EA_{m,k}\}\right\|_\infty^2
 \le \frac{C}{N_0^2}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \Pr(\mathcal X_{m,k})
 \le \frac{Ce^{-h_0k}}{N_0}
 \le \frac{C}{N_0k^2}.                                      \tag{27}
\]
Combining (10), (25), and (27) proves
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}q_{m,k}^{\mathrm{PL}}
 \right\|_\infty^2
 \le \frac{C}{N_0k^2}.                                      \tag{28}
\]

It remains to transfer the decomposition to the interpolated estimator without absorbing interpolation error into the Kaplan--Meier bias.  Let \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) denote the deterministic piecewise-linear interpolation operator on the common grid, and define
\[
 \widetilde S^0=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0,
 \qquad
 \varphi_j=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\varphi_j^{\mathrm{PL}},
 \qquad
 b_k=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}b_k^{\mathrm{PL}},
 \qquad
 q_k=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}q_k^{\mathrm{PL}}.
\]
The operator \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) is linear, deterministic, and a contraction in supremum norm.  Since the construction defines \(\widehat S^{\mathrm{KM}}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}\), applying it to (6) gives the exact, correctly centered identity
\[
 \widehat S^{\mathrm{KM}}-\widetilde S^0
 =k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_j+b_k+q_k.                       \tag{29}
\]
The operator preserves expectations and patient independence, does not increase the bounds in (11), (21), or (28), and commutes with donor averaging because all cells use the same deterministic grid.  This proves every stochastic assertion in the corrected statement.

We finally quantify the deterministic difference between the true and interpolated population curves.  The hazard equation
\[
 (S^0)'=-\lambda^0S^0
\]
and the uniform \(C^\beta\) bound on \(\lambda^0\) imply a uniform \(C^{\beta+1}\) bound on \(S^0\).  Put \(\nu=\min\{\beta,1\}\).  On a grid interval \([g_0,g_1]\) of length at most the mesh, write \(t=g_0+u(g_1-g_0)\), where \(0\le u\le1\).  Cancellation of the constant derivative term gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0)(t)-S^0(t)
 &=u\int_{g_0}^{g_1}\{(S^0)'(v)-(S^0)'(t)\}\,dv\\
 &\quad-\int_{g_0}^{t}\{(S^0)'(v)-(S^0)'(t)\}\,dv.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The \(C^\nu\) modulus of \((S^0)'\) therefore yields, with \(q_\beta=1+\nu=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\),
\[
 \|\widetilde S^0-S^0\|_\infty
 \le c_{\mathrm{int}}h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{q_\beta}.              \tag{30}
\]
The interpolation operator reproduces endpoint values, so
\[
 \widetilde S^0(g)=S^0(g)
 \quad\text{for every }g\in\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}.         \tag{31}
\]
Integrating the squared pointwise bound in (30) over an interval of length \(\bar\tau\) gives
\[
 \|\widetilde S^0-S^0\|_{L^2(\mathcal I)}
 \le \bar\tau^{1/2}c_{\mathrm{int}}
 h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{q_\beta}.                                  \tag{32}
\]
Thus, with \(a_{\mathcal G}=\widetilde S^0-S^0\), the expansion about the original population curve is exactly
\[
 \widehat S^{\mathrm{KM}}-S^0
 =k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_j+b_k+q_k+a_{\mathcal G},        \tag{33}
\]
where (30)--(32), rather than the exponential bias bound, control \(a_{\mathcal G}\).  In particular, \(a_{\mathcal G}\) generally does not vanish inside the internal \(L^2\) factor map even though it vanishes at every grid point.

By (28), contraction, and Markov's inequality,
\[
 \left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}q_{p,m,k}\right\|_\infty
 =O_P(N_0^{-1/2}k^{-1}).                                    \tag{34}
\]
Dividing (34) by \((N_0k)^{-1/2}\) gives \(k^{-1/2}\to0\).  No assumption supplies cross-donor cancellation for either the deterministic biases or the interpolation remainders, so their averages retain the respective bounds in (11) and (30).  Finally, for the deterministic odd--even split in the functional-PCR construction, the smaller fold has \(\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\) patients; substituting this value into (11) gives \(C_0\exp\{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\}\).  Since all preceding constants are fixed and uniform, \(C_0\) may be enlarged to the conservative constant used in \(\eta_D\).  This completes the proof. \(\square\)
