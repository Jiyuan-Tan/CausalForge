With a known fixed positive separation, rank selection reduces to testing a zero centered contrast operator against a nonzero second singular value.  A level \(q_{N_0,K_D}\) test with \(q_{N_0,K_D}\le(N_0K_D)^{-1/2}\) would add at most the rank-one order to expected width under the null, while a type-II error bounded by \(\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\) would preserve coverage under the separated alternative.  Thus fixed separation need not cause a new first-order width term.  Uniformity over all unknown positive lower bounds, however, is different from pointwise eventual validity for each fixed bound: without a stated separation regime and a proved singular-value concentration inequality, alternatives approaching rank one cannot be classified uniformly, and the smallest excess width is not determined by the frozen handle.
