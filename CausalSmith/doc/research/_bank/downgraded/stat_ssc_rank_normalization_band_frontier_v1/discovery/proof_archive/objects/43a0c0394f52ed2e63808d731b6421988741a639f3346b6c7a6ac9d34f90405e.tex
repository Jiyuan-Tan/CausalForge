For each fixed \(r\ge1\), there is \(a_0<\infty\) such that, at every evaluated triple with \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\ge a_0\), the band \(\widehat B_r\) belongs to \(\mathcal H_r(N_0,K_T,K_D;\alpha)\). There is a constant \(c_{\mathrm{up}}<\infty\), independent of \(N_0\), \(K_T\), and \(K_D\), such that at every such triple,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)}E_P[w(\widehat B_r)]
 \le c_{\mathrm{up}}\bar\rho_r(N_0,K_T,K_D).
\]
Both assertions hold uniformly over every three-dimensional tail, with no balanced-growth or relative-rate restriction. In particular, \(\bar\rho_1=\rho_1=(N_0K_D)^{-1/2}\); for fixed \(r\ge2\), the displayed upper bound is \(O(\rho_r)\) on every subregime satisfying \(\eta_D(K_D)=O(\rho_r)\). No unrestricted higher-rank \(O(\rho_r)\) claim is made by this theorem.
