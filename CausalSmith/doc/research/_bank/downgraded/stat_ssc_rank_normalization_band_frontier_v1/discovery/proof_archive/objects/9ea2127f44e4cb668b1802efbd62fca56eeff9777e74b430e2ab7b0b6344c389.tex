\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\mathrm{lem:normalization\mbox{-}collapse}\), every donor-post event survival curve equals \(s_1\), so every donor-post failure hazard equals a common \(\lambda_1\), although the censoring survivals \(G_{1,m}\) may differ. For \(i=(m,j)\), define
\[
 N_i(u)=\mathbf1\{X_{1,m,j}\le u,\Delta_{1,m,j}=1\},
 \quad Y_i(u)=\mathbf1\{X_{1,m,j}\ge u\},
 \quad M_i(u)=N_i(u)-\int_0^uY_i(v)\lambda_1(v)\,dv.      \tag{1}
\]
The maintained assumptions make the \(M_i\)'s independent mean-zero martingales. With \(Y=\sum_iY_i\), \(M=\sum_iM_i\), and \(J_Y=\mathbf1\{Y>0\}\), equal donor cell sizes give
\[
 EY(u)=n_DH_D(u),\qquad H_D(u)=s_1(u)\bar G_D(u)\ge h_0.  \tag{2}
\]
For the un-interpolated pooled product-limit curve, put \(R=\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}/s_1\). Product integration gives
\[
 R(t)-1=-\int_0^tR(u-)\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)}\,dM(u)
 +\int_0^tR(u-)\{1-J_Y(u)\}\lambda_1(u)\,du.             \tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
 \phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}(t)
 =-s_1(t)\int_0^tH_D(u)^{-1}\,dM_i(u).                   \tag{4}
\]
Subtracting \(n_D^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}\) leaves a martingale with integrand \(RJ_Y/Y-1/(n_DH_D)\) and the empty-risk drift. The horizon risk set is Poisson--binomial, and (2) gives
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)=0\}\le e^{-h_0n_D},
 \qquad P\{Y(\bar\tau)<n_Dh_0/2\}\le e^{-cn_D}.         \tag{5}
\]
On the complement of the second event,
\[
 \frac{R}{Y}-\frac1{n_DH_D}
 =\frac{R-1}{Y}-\frac{Y-n_DH_D}{n_DH_DY}.                 \tag{6}
\]
Doob's inequality, \(E(Y-n_DH_D)^2\le n_D\), and the predictable isometry applied to (6) give a centered remainder second moment \(Cn_D^{-2}\). On the rare event, the fourth-moment inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz give the same polynomial bound. The drift is uniformly bounded and supported on the first event in (5), giving the displayed deterministic bias and centered remainder bound. Applying the deterministic interpolation operator preserves the exact identity, contracts the supremum norm, and fixes every grid knot.

The predictable isometry and Doob's inequality applied to (4) give the explicit oracle bound
\[
 E\left\|n_D^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}\right\|_\infty^2
 \le \frac{4L\bar\tau}{h_0^2n_D}
 \le C_Zn_D^{-1}.                                       \tag{7}
\]
Conditional on the records, the ordered failure increments of the empirical multiplier form a Gaussian martingale with weights \(-\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}J_Y/Y\). On \(\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge n_Dh_0/2\}\), its conditional quadratic variation is at most \(4/(n_Dh_0^2)\). On the complement it is at most \(n_D\), while (5) makes that event exponentially rare. Conditional Doob inequality and \(n_De^{-cn_D}\le C/n_D\) yield
\[
 E E_\xi\|\widehat Z_1^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C_Zn_D^{-1}.                                       \tag{8}
\]

We finally verify covariance estimation, without using it as a coverage premise. The time-indexed classes \(\{Y_i(u):u\in\mathcal I\}\) and \(\{N_i(u):u\in\mathcal I\}\) are nested. The same time-ordered maximal calculation as in the stabilized-cell lemma, now with unit variation and normalization by \(n_D\), gives
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal I}|Y(u)/n_D-H_D(u)|=O_P(n_D^{-1/2}),
 \quad
 \|\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}-s_1\|_\infty=O_P(n_D^{-1/2}). \tag{9}
\]
On the event in (5), substitute (9) in the empirical optional-variation formula for \(\widehat\Sigma_1\); the map \((S,H)\mapsto S(s)S(t)\int_0^{s\wedge t}H^{-1}\lambda_1\) is uniformly Lipschitz on \(H\ge h_0/2\). Patient-level Bernstein applied to the bounded ordered increments gives
\[
 n_D\|\widehat\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\|_{\infty,\mathcal I^2}=o_P(1). \tag{10}
\]
The complement in (5) is exponentially negligible, and interpolation is a supremum contraction. Equations (7)--(10) hold uniformly over donor-specific censoring arrays because only the averaged deterministic risk \(H_D\ge h_0\) was used. At time zero every process and covariance is zero. No random critical threshold enters this proof. \(\square\)
