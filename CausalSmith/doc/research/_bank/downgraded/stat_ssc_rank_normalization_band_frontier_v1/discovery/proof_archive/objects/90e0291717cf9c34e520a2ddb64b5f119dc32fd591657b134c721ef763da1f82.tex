\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\).  Apply the cellwise decomposition in \(\mathrm{lem:kaplan\mbox{-}meier\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}exhaustion}\) to every full donor cell and to both deterministic half-samples used by the cross-fold operator.  A half-sample has size at least \(K_D/3\) for all sufficiently large \(K_D\); hence its deterministic bias is at most \(C_0e^{-c_0K_D}\) after the conservative constants in \(\eta_D\) are enlarged.  The donor mean curves consequently satisfy
\[
 \widehat s_p-s_p
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}\varphi^{s}_{p,m,j}+b^s_p+q^s_p,\qquad
 \|b^s_p\|_\infty\le\eta_D(K_D),\qquad
 \|q^s_p\|_\infty=o_P(n_D^{-1/2}).
\]
The corresponding target-pre expansion has bias \(O(e^{-c_0K_T})=o(K_T^{-1/2})\) and centered second-order remainder \(O_P(K_T^{-1})\).

Let \(Y_m=(b_0^\top z_m,b_1^\top z_m)\) be the population centered joint-period donor curve and write the two fold estimates as
\[
 \widehat Y_m^{(a)}=Y_m+U_m^{(a)}+b_m^{(a)}+q_m^{(a)},\qquad a\in\{1,2\},
\]
where \(U_m^{(a)}\) is the first-order martingale average.  Conditional on the fixed survival panel, the two \(U\)'s are independent and mean zero, \(\sup_mE\|U_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le C/K_D\), \(\sup_m\|b_m^{(a)}\|_2\le C\eta_D(K_D)\), and \(E\|q_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le C/K_D^2\).  Expanding the symmetrized cross-fold operator gives
\[
 \widehat\Gamma-\Gamma=\mathbb L_D+E_D,
\]
where \(\mathbb L_D\) is the sum of the terms \(Y_m\otimes U_m^{(a)}+U_m^{(a)}\otimes Y_m\), divided by \(2N_0\).  Donor incoherence and the factor envelope imply
\[
 \|\mathbb L_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=O_P(n_D^{-1/2}).
\]
Independence of the folds makes \(E(U_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)})=0\).  The martingale isometry and independence across donors therefore give
\[
 \left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mU_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)}\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 =O_P(N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1})=o_P(n_D^{-1/2}).
\]
The same calculation for the \(q\)-terms, and the deterministic bias bound for the \(b\)-terms, yield
\[
 \|E_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le C\eta_D(K_D)+o_P(n_D^{-1/2}).
\]
This calculation is precisely where cross-folding removes the same-fold errors-in-variables term \(N_0^{-1}\sum_mE(U_m\otimes U_m)\), but it also shows why risk-exhaustion bias cannot be averaged away.

The population operator has \(d\) positive eigenvalues in \([\gamma_-^2,\gamma_+^2]\) and the remaining spectrum is zero.  Its selected spectral projector is thus separated from the null spectrum by \(\gamma_-^2\).  The target loading regression has Gram matrix bounded below by \(\kappa_-I_d\).  Resolvent identities and
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}
\]
show that, on the event \(\|\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\gamma_-^2/4\) and \(\|\widehat H_0-H_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\kappa_-/2\), the rotation-invariant map from the donor means, the selected positive square root of \(\Gamma\), and the target-pre curve to \(s_1+b_1^\top z_\star\) is twice Fréchet differentiable.  Its first two derivative norms are bounded by a fixed polynomial in \(r,M_b,M_\star,M_z,\gamma_-^{-1},\gamma_+\), and \(\kappa_-^{-1}\).  The constant defining \(\eta_D\) was chosen larger than this polynomial times the cellwise bias constant.  Since \(\eta_D(K_D)\to0\), the regular event has probability tending to one uniformly; internal eigenvalue ties cause no difficulty because the composite map depends only on the selected projector and positive square root, not on a chosen basis.

Taylor expansion of this composite map now gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =D_T\mathcal F\,[K_T^{-1}\textstyle\sum_j\varphi_{0,\star,j}]
 +D_D\mathcal F\,[n_D^{-1}\textstyle\sum_{p,m,j}\varphi_{p,m,j}]+R_r.
\]
Define \(\phi_{T,j}=D_T\mathcal F[\varphi_{0,\star,j}]\) and define \(\phi_{D,p,m,j}\) analogously, with the deterministic fold weights included.  Patient independence makes the two displayed sums independent and mean zero.  The Taylor remainder, the preceding operator bounds, and \(K_T,n_D\to\infty\) give
\[
 \|R_r\|_\infty
 \le\eta_D(K_D)+O_P(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1})+o_P(\rho_r)
 =\eta_D(K_D)+o_P(\rho_r)
\]
when \(r\ge2\).  For \(r=1\), normalization collapse makes the map independent of the target-pre curve and the estimator is the pooled donor-post Kaplan--Meier curve.  Applying the cellwise martingale identity to that pooled risk set of size \(n_D\) gives bias \(O(e^{-c_0n_D})=o(n_D^{-1/2})\); hence \(\phi_{T,j}=0\) and \(\|R_1\|_\infty=o_P(\rho_1)\).

It remains to verify the multiplier assertion.  After division by \(\rho_r\), the coefficient of either independent patient array is at most one.  Overlap bounds every martingale integrand, and the nested indicator class \(\{\mathbf 1_{[0,t]}:t\in\mathcal I\}\) has \(L^2\)-covering number at most \(C\epsilon^{-2}\).  A dyadic net, Bernstein's inequality for martingale increments, and the Lindeberg replacement identity yield, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_P(\|Z_r\|_\infty/\rho_r),
 \mathcal L_\xi(\|Z_r^\xi\|_\infty/\rho_r\mid O)
 \right)
 \le C\{\min(K_T,n_D)^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T+n_D)+o_P(1)\},
\]
with the target term omitted at rank one.  Consistency of the empirical derivative follows from the same operator expansion and the fixed spectral and Gram gaps.  The right-hand side tends to zero for every sequence on the three-dimensional tail, irrespective of the ratio \(K_T/n_D\).  This proves the corrected statement. \(\square\)
