\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\).  The normalization-preserving converse gives, at the
same evaluated triple and over the same honesty class,
\[
 W_1^*\ge c_1n_D^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 W_r^*\ge c_r\{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}\}=c_r\rho_r
 \quad(r\ge2).                                             \tag{1}
\]
The attaining theorem supplies the single explicit measurable band
\(\widehat B_r\) with
\[
 \widehat B_r\in\mathcal H_r(N_0,K_T,K_D;\alpha),
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_Pw(\widehat B_r)
 \le C_r\bar\rho_r.                                       \tag{2}
\]
Substituting this non-oracle band into the minimax infimum gives
\(W_r^*\le C_r\bar\rho_r\).  Since
\(\bar\rho_1=\rho_1=n_D^{-1/2}\), (1)--(2) yield
\[
 W_1^*(N_0,K_T,K_D;\alpha)\asymp(N_0K_D)^{-1/2}.           \tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(r\ge2\), they yield only the unrestricted bracket
\[
 c_r\{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}\}
 \le W_r^*
 \le C_r\{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)\}.            \tag{4}
\]
If \(\eta_D(K_D)=O(\rho_r)\), the two sides of (4) have the same order.
Without that condition, (4) is a bracket and its tightness is not implied by
the random-quantile validity theorem.  Both input theorems hold uniformly on
three-dimensional tails, impose no relative-growth restriction, and enforce
coverage at the same triple at which width is evaluated. \(\square\)
