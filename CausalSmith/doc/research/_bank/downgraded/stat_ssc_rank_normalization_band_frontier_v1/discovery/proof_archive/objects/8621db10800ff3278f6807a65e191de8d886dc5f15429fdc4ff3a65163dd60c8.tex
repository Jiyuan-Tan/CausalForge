\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_1(N_0,K_T,K_D)\). Since \(d=r-1\), exact rank one gives \(d=0\). The zero-dimensional clause of the corrected canonical construction, justified by \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\mbox{-}target\mbox{-}coordinate}\), turns every factor-loading product into an empty sum. Compatibility of the normalized factor model gives
\[
 S^0_{p,m}(t)=s_p(t),
 \qquad p\in\{0,1\},\ m\in\mathcal M,\ t\in\mathcal I.  \tag{1}
\]
The target definition then yields \(\theta(t)=S^0_{1,\star}(t)=s_1(t)\).

Let \(\{P_\eta:|\eta|<\eta_0\}\) be a regular submodel of the type quantified in the statement, and let \(q_{0,\star,\eta}\) be the density of one target-pre record with respect to a common dominating measure. Its target-pre marginal is fixed, so
\[
 q_{0,\star,\eta}=q_{0,\star,0},
 \qquad
 \left.\partial_\eta\log q_{0,\star,\eta}(O_{0,\star,j})
 \right|_{\eta=0}=0.                                      \tag{2}
\]
Patient independence makes the target-pre sample score the sum of the zero terms in (2). This is ancillarity only for the stated fixed-target-pre paths.

Now fix \(r\ge2\) and a loading path \(z_{\star,\eta}=z_\star+\eta v\) which remains in the model. The canonical-coordinate lemma makes this a path in the unique common donor-SVD coordinates. The factor curves are fixed, and the target formula gives
\[
 \left.\partial_\eta\theta_\eta(t)\right|_{\eta=0}
 =\left.\partial_\eta\{s_1(t)+b_1(t)^\top(z_\star+\eta v)\}
 \right|_{\eta=0}=b_1(t)^\top v.                          \tag{3}
\]
The derivative in (3) is a nonzero element of \(C(\mathcal I)\) exactly when \(b_1^\top v\) is not identically zero. \(\square\)
