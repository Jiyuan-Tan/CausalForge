\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\), suppress the sample-size indices, and put
\[
 \mathcal H=L^2(\{0,1\}\times\mathcal I).
\]
Let \((\bar b_p,\bar z_m)\) be the compatible representation in the statement.  Thus, for every \(m\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 S^0_{p,m}(t)-s_p(t)=\bar b_p(t)^\top\bar z_m. \tag{1}
\]
Because \(s_p\) is the donor mean in \(\mathrm{def:canonical\mbox{-}factorization}\), averaging (1) over \(m\in\mathcal D\) and using donor centering gives the same centered donor curves on both sides.

Define the joint factor map \(\bar B:\mathbb R^d\to\mathcal H\) and the donor loading matrix \(\bar Z\in\mathbb R^{N_0\times d}\) by
\[
 (\bar Bx)_p(t)=\bar b_p(t)^\top x,
 \qquad
 \bar Z_{m\cdot}=\bar z_m^\top. \tag{2}
\]
For every \(a\in\mathbf 1^\perp\), the definition of the centered donor operator and (1) imply
\[
 \mathcal C_Da
 =\bar B\!\left(N_0^{-1/2}\bar Z^\top a\right). \tag{3}
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:exact\mbox{-}centered\mbox{-}rank}\), \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C_D)=d\).  Since (3) factors \(\mathcal C_D\) through the \(d\)-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 d=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C_D)
 \leq \min\{\operatorname{rank}(\bar B),\operatorname{rank}(\bar Z)\}
 \leq d. \tag{4}
\]
Consequently both \(\bar B\) and \(\bar Z\) have rank \(d\).  This rank conclusion uses no pre-period Gram condition.

It follows from (4) that the donor loading covariance
\[
 \Sigma=N_0^{-1}\bar Z^\top\bar Z \tag{5}
\]
is positive definite.  Covariance-normalize the compatible coordinates by setting
\[
 \widetilde b_p=\Sigma^{1/2}\bar b_p,
 \qquad
 \widetilde z_m=\Sigma^{-1/2}\bar z_m. \tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
 \widetilde b_p(t)^\top\widetilde z_m
 =\bar b_p(t)^\top\bar z_m,
 \qquad
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\widetilde z_m\widetilde z_m^\top=I_d. \tag{7}
\]
Let \(\widetilde Bx=(\widetilde b_0^\top x,\widetilde b_1^\top x)\).  If
\[
 Y_m=(S^0_{0,m}-s_0,S^0_{1,m}-s_1)\in\mathcal H,
\]
then (7) yields
\[
 \Gamma:=N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}Y_m\otimes Y_m
 =\widetilde B\widetilde B^*. \tag{8}
\]

Now let \(B:\mathbb R^d\to\mathcal H\) be the canonical donor-SVD factor map, so that \((Bx)_p=b_p^\top x\).  The singular-value construction in \(\mathrm{def:canonical\mbox{-}factorization}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal C_D\mathcal C_D^*=BB^*. \tag{9}
\]
On the other hand, donor centering implies directly from the definition of \(\mathcal C_D\) that
\[
 \mathcal C_D\mathcal C_D^*
 =N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}Y_m\otimes Y_m
 =\Gamma. \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, for \(f\in\mathcal H\), the vector with entries \(N_0^{-1/2}\langle Y_m,f\rangle_{\mathcal H}\) lies in \(\mathbf 1^\perp\), and applying \(\mathcal C_D\) to it gives the middle operator in (10).  Combining (8)--(10),
\[
 BB^*=\widetilde B\widetilde B^*. \tag{11}
\]
Both maps have rank \(d\), by (4) and the \(d\) positive singular values of \(\mathcal C_D\).  Hence their ranges coincide.  Let \(B^\dagger=(B^*B)^{-1}B^*\) be the left inverse of \(B\), and define
\[
 R=B^\dagger\widetilde B. \tag{12}
\]
Range equality gives \(BR=\widetilde B\).  Moreover, by (11),
\[
 RR^*
 =B^\dagger\widetilde B\widetilde B^*B^{\dagger *}
 =B^\dagger BB^*B^{\dagger *}
 =I_d. \tag{13}
\]
Thus \(R\) is orthogonal.  Equivalently,
\[
 b_p=R\widetilde b_p. \tag{14}
\]
For a donor \(m\), (7), \(BR=\widetilde B\), and injectivity of \(B\) show
\[
 Bz_m=Y_m=\widetilde B\widetilde z_m=BR\widetilde z_m
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 z_m=R\widetilde z_m. \tag{15}
\]
Equations (14)--(15) establish the asserted common orthogonal relation after covariance normalization.  The deterministic sign and tie-breaking rule in the canonical singular-value decomposition merely selects one representative of this orthogonal equivalence class.

Assume next that \(d>0\).  Compatibility in (1), now applied to the target unit, and (6) give
\[
 S^0_{0,\star}-s_0
 =\widetilde b_0^\top\widetilde z_\star
 =b_0^\top R\widetilde z_\star. \tag{16}
\]
Thus the target-pre contrast belongs to the span of the canonical pre-period factors.  Only at this point do we invoke \(\mathrm{ass:pre\mbox{-}contrast\mbox{-}gram}\): for the canonical factors,
\[
 H_0:=\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)b_0(t)^\top\,dt
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
 \lambda_{\min}(H_0)\geq\kappa_->0. \tag{17}
\]
Hence \(H_0\) is invertible.  Multiplying (16) by \(b_0(t)\), integrating over \(\mathcal I\), and using (17) yields
\[
 R\widetilde z_\star
 =H_0^{-1}\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)
 \{S^0_{0,\star}(t)-s_0(t)\}\,dt. \tag{18}
\]
If \(x,x'\in\mathbb R^d\) are two compatible canonical coefficients for the target-pre curve, then \(b_0^\top(x-x')=0\) in \(L^2(\mathcal I)\), and therefore
\[
 0=\int_{\mathcal I}\{b_0(t)^\top(x-x')\}^2\,dt
 =(x-x')^\top H_0(x-x'). \tag{19}
\]
Positive definiteness in (17) forces \(x=x'\).  Thus the coefficient in (18) is the unique compatible target coordinate and is exactly the \(z_\star\) selected in \(\mathrm{def:canonical\mbox{-}factorization}\).

Finally, if \(d=0\), a compatible \(d\)-coordinate representation has an empty factor term.  Equation (1) then reads \(S^0_{p,m}=s_p\) for every \(p\in\{0,1\}\) and every \(m\in\mathcal M\).  This proves the zero-rank assertion and completes the proof. \(\square\)
