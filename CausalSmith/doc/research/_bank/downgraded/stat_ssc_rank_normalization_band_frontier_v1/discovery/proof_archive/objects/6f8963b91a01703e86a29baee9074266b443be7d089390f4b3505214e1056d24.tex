\textbf{Proof.}
Suppress the cell label. For patient \(j\), let
\[
 N_j(u)=\mathbf1\{X_j\le u,\Delta_j=1\},\qquad
 Y_j(u)=\mathbf1\{X_j\ge u\},\qquad
 M_j(u)=N_j(u)-\int_0^uY_j(v)\lambda^0(v)\,dv.             \tag{1}
\]
Control consistency and independent censoring give the compensator in (1), and patient independence makes the \(M_j\)'s independent mean-zero square-integrable martingales. Write \(Y=\sum_jY_j\), \(N=\sum_jN_j\), \(M=\sum_jM_j\), and \(H(u)=EY_j(u)=S^0(u)G(u)\). The two overlap conditions give
\[
 H(u)\ge h_0:=s_-g_->0,\qquad u\in\mathcal I.             \tag{2}
\]

On a tied dataset, order failures by \((u,j)\), remove an earlier ordered failure from the risk set before processing the next, and use that ordered risk count. This agrees with the usual product-limit factor because
\[
 \prod_{a=0}^{d-1}\left(1-\frac1{Y-a}\right)=1-\frac dY.  \tag{3}
\]
It also makes the map measurable on the null sets on which continuous event times tie. Put \(Y^\dagger=\max\{Y,\ell_k\}\) on this ordered event axis. Every factor \(1-dN/Y^\dagger\) lies in \([0,1]\). If \(Y(\bar\tau)\ge\ell_k\), monotonicity gives \(Y(u)\ge\ell_k\) at every earlier ordered event, so \(Y^\dagger=Y\) and (3) proves equality with ordinary Kaplan--Meier.

The horizon risk indicators are independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(\mu=EY(\bar\tau)\ge kh_0\). Independence, exponential Markov inequality, and optimization give the Poisson--binomial multiplicative bound
\[
 P\{Y\le(1-\delta)\mu\}\le\exp\{-\delta^2\mu/2\},
 \qquad0<\delta<1.                                        \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, \(Ee^{-sY}\le\exp\{(e^{-s}-1)\mu\}\), and \((1-\delta)\log(1-\delta)+\delta\ge\delta^2/2\). With \(\delta=7/8\), the event \(Y<kh_0/8\) is contained in \(\{Y<(1-\delta)\mu\}\); hence
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)<kh_0/8\}
 \le e^{-49h_0k/128}\le e^{-h_0k/3}=e^{-c_0k}.            \tag{5}
\]

For \(f(y)=1-1/\max\{y,\ell_k\}\), the maximum map is one-Lipschitz and
\[
 |f(y+e)-f(y)|\le |e|\ell_k^{-2},\qquad |e|\le1.           \tag{6}
\]
Changing one record inserts or removes at most one failure factor and changes every remaining ordered risk count by at most one. The telescoping identity for products of numbers in \([0,1]\), (6), and at most \(k\) factors give
\[
 \sup_t|\widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O)-\widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O')|
 \le \ell_k^{-1}+k\ell_k^{-2}\le C/k.                    \tag{7}
\]
Interpolation contracts the supremum norm. A patient has at most one observed failure, so its multiplier contribution is bounded by
\[
 \sup_t\widehat S_k^\dagger(t-)
 \int_0^t(Y^\dagger)^{-1}\,dN_j
 \le\ell_k^{-1}\le C/k.                                  \tag{8}
\]
Equations (7)--(8) hold on every dataset.

Put \(R=\widehat S_k^\dagger/S^0\). The product-integral equations and integration by parts give the exact identity
\[
 R(t)-1=-\int_0^tR(u-)\{Y^\dagger(u)\}^{-1}\,dM(u)
 +\int_0^tR(u-)\left\{1-\frac{Y(u)}{Y^\dagger(u)}\right\}
          \lambda^0(u)\,du.                               \tag{9}
\]
The drift is supported where \(Y<\ell_k\); monotonicity places its support inside \(\{Y(\bar\tau)<\ell_k\}\). Moreover \(0\le R\le s_-^{-1}\) and \(\int_0^{\bar\tau}\lambda^0\le L\bar\tau\). Thus, if \(A_k\) denotes the drift after multiplication by \(S^0(t)\),
\[
 \|EA_k\|_\infty\le Ce^{-c_0k},
 \qquad E\|A_k-EA_k\|_\infty^2\le Ce^{-c_0k}.             \tag{10}
\]

Define independent mean-zero processes
\[
 \varphi_j^{\mathrm{PL}}(t)
 =-S^0(t)\int_0^tH(u)^{-1}\,dM_j(u).                      \tag{11}
\]
Subtracting \(k^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_j^{\mathrm{PL}}\) from (9) leaves
\[
 V_k(t)=-S^0(t)\int_0^t
 \left\{\frac{R(u-)}{Y^\dagger(u)}-\frac1{kH(u)}\right\}
 dM(u).                                                     \tag{12}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_k=\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge kh_0/2\}\), one has \(Y^\dagger=Y\), and
\[
 \frac{R}{Y}-\frac1{kH}
 =\frac{R-1}{Y}-\frac{Y-kH}{kHY}.                          \tag{13}
\]
Stopping (9) before \(Y<kh_0/2\), the martingale maximal inequality and independence give
\[
 E\{\mathbf1_{\mathcal E_k}\|R-1\|_\infty^2\}\le Ck^{-1},
 \qquad E\{Y(u)-kH(u)\}^2\le k.                           \tag{14}
\]
Substitution into the predictable isometry yields
\[
 E\left[\mathbf1_{\mathcal E_k}\int_0^{\bar\tau}
 \left\{\frac{R}{Y^\dagger}-\frac1{kH}\right\}^2
 Y\lambda^0\right]\le Ck^{-2}.                           \tag{15}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_k^c\), the integrand in braces is bounded by \(C/k\). Applying the fourth-moment martingale inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz with the Chernoff bound gives
\[
 E\left[\mathbf1_{\mathcal E_k^c}\int_0^{\bar\tau}
 \left\{\frac{R}{Y^\dagger}-\frac1{kH}\right\}^2
 Y\lambda^0\right]
 \le Ck^{-1}e^{-ck/2}\le Ck^{-2}.                         \tag{16}
\]
The maximal inequality proves \(E\|V_k\|_\infty^2\le Ck^{-2}\). Set
\[
 b_k^{\mathrm{PL}}=EA_k,qquad q_k^{\mathrm{PL}}=V_k+A_k-EA_k. \tag{17}
\]
Equations (10), (15)--(17) give the asserted bias and remainder. Applying \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) preserves means, independence, and all bounds, and replaces the center by \(\widetilde S^0\).

For independent donor cells let \(U_m\) be the centered rare-event contribution. Equation (10) gives \(E\|U_m\|_\infty^2\le Ce^{-c_0k}\), and the paths are uniformly Lipschitz on the rare event. Symmetrization on dyadic time partitions followed by the scalar variance identity gives
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mU_m\right\|_\infty^2
 \le C N_0^{-1}e^{-c_0k}\le C(N_0k^2)^{-1}.               \tag{18}
\]
No union over cells has been taken. \(\square\)
