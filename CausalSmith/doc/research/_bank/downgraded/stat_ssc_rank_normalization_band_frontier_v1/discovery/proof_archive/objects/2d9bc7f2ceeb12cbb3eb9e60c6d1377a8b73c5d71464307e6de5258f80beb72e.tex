\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(m=n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\), and
\(p_{\mathcal G}=2|\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}|\).  Abbreviate
\(a_{r,n}\), \(\delta_{r,n}\), \(\chi_{r,n}\), and \(\tau_{r,n}\) by
\(a_n\), \(\delta_n\), \(\chi_n\), and \(\tau_n\).  The covariance and
modulus transfer lemma supplies the oracle summand envelopes, an event
\(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), the covariance comparison, and the oracle and empirical
semimetric bounds.  For \(r\ge2\), this event is contained in the strengthened
regular-margin event, so every empirical coefficient used below belongs to one
fixed branch.  Its complement satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)
 \le Ca_n^{1/2}.                                        \tag{1}
\]

We first couple the oracle process to a Gaussian process with the same
covariance.  Put \(k=k_{r,n}\), and choose a deterministic subset
\(\mathcal T_n\subset\mathcal G\) of at most \(k+1\) knots and physical-time
mesh at most \(C/k\).  Evaluate both signs of each normalized patient vector
on \(\mathcal T_n\); the ambient dimension is at most
\(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}=2(k+1)\).  The patient envelopes and
\(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\) give
\[
 \sum_iE\|L_i/\rho_r\|_{2,\mathcal T_n}^3
 \le Cp_{\mathrm c,r,n}^{3/2}m^{-1/2}.                  \tag{2}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:yurinskii\mbox{-}degeneracy\mbox{-}robust\mbox{-}coupling}\)
with dimension \(p=p_{\mathrm c,r,n}\) and
\(a=U_{r,n}^{1/4}\).  Its dimensioned quantity obeys
\[
 B_0\le Cp_{mathrm c,r,n}^{5/2}m^{-1/2}a^{-3}
 \le U_{r,n}^{1/4}.                                     \tag{3}
\]
Hence a centered Gaussian process \(G_r\) with the covariance of \(Z_r\) can
be coupled on \(\mathcal T_n\) with normalized error at most
\(C\zeta_{r,n}\), except on an event of probability at most
\(C\zeta_{r,n}\).  No covariance nonsingularity is required.

Use the modulus conclusion of the covariance-transfer lemma with \(u=C/k\).
The oracle modulus, and the Gaussian modulus obtained from the same
semimetric by a Gaussian exponential-moment and union-bound calculation, are
both at most \(C\rho_r\zeta_{r,n}\), with total exceptional probability at
most \(C\zeta_{r,n}\).  Therefore, for every real \(x\),
\[
 P\{\|G_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\}
 \le P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le x+C\rho_r\zeta_{r,n}\}+C\zeta_{r,n}.              \tag{4}
\]

Next let \(\widehat G_r\), conditionally on the observations, be Gaussian with
covariance \(\widehat\Sigma_r\); this is exactly the law of the explicitly
defined multiplier process.  On \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), put
\(v_n=a_n^{1/2}\).  The covariance-transfer lemma gives
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \le C\rho_r^2v_n.                                      \tag{5}
\]
Choose an auxiliary subset of the grid with mesh at most
\(u_n=v_n^{1/3}\) and cardinality \(K_n\le Cv_n^{-1/3}\).  On this subset
the two covariance matrices differ in trace norm by at most
\(CK_n^2\rho_r^2v_n\).  By
\(\mathrm{lem:powers\mbox{-}stormer\mbox{-}square\mbox{-}root}\), a common
standard-normal vector couples the two Gaussian vectors so that
\[
 E\|G_r-\widehat G_r\|_{2,\mathrm{aux}}
 \le C\rho_rK_nv_n^{1/2}
 \le C\rho_ra_n^{1/12}.                                 \tag{6}
\]
The two semimetric bounds and Gaussian exponential moments transfer (6) to
the canonical grid with expected modulus at most
\(C\rho_ra_n^{1/12}\{1+|\log a_n|\}^{1/2}\).  Markov's inequality at the
larger threshold
\[
 \rho_ra_n^{1/48}\{1+|\log a_n|\}                       \tag{7}
\]
and (1) show, after enlarging the fixed constant in \(\chi_n\), that on
\(\mathcal E_{r,n}\)
\[
 \widehat F_r(x)
 \le F_{r,P}(x+\rho_r\chi_n)+\chi_n
 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R,                   \tag{8}
\]
where
\[
 F_{r,P}(x)=P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\},
 \qquad
 \widehat F_r(x)=P_\xi\{\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le x\mid O\}.
\]
This comparison uses neither anti-concentration nor a coordinate-variance
lower bound.

We record the deterministic rates.  Since
\(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}=O(m^{1/9})\),
\[
 U_{r,n}=O(m^{-2/9}),
 \qquad
 \zeta_{r,n}=O\{m^{-1/18}(1+\log m)\}.                  \tag{9}
\]
The explicit expression for \(a_n\) tends to zero uniformly on every
three-dimensional tail, including all relative-growth sequences.  Hence
\[
 \chi_n\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \tau_n/\rho_r=\chi_n\longrightarrow0.           \tag{10}
\]
If \(A=\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\), all terms in \(\chi_n\) decrease as a positive
power of \(A\), up to logarithms, whereas
\(\delta_n\) decreases no faster than the specified reciprocal iterated
logarithm.  Consequently, uniformly for sufficiently large evaluated
triples,
\[
 2\chi_n\le\delta_n/2.                                   \tag{11}
\]

Let \(q_n=1-\alpha+\delta_n\) and define
\(c_{r,P}(u)=\inf\{x\ge0:F_{r,P}(x)\ge u\}\).  The conditional Gaussian
maximum is a Borel function of a finite standard-normal vector and the
observed covariance matrix.  Taking the infimum over nonnegative rationals
proves measurability of \(\widetilde c_r^\xi\), including a singular
covariance matrix and the identically zero process.  The generalized-inverse
property and (8) imply on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) that
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi+\rho_r\chi_n
 \ge c_{r,P}(q_n-\chi_n).                                \tag{12}
\]
The right side is deterministic for fixed \(P\).  Therefore no independence
between \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) and \(Z_r\) is needed:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
       \le\widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_n\}
 &\ge q_n-\chi_n-P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\\
 &\ge1-\alpha+\delta_n/2,
 \end{aligned}                                          \tag{13}
\]
where (1), the definition of \(\chi_n\), and (11) were used in the second
line.  The final quantity is \(1-\alpha-o(1)\) because \(\delta_n\to0\).

Finally, \(q_n\le1-\alpha/2\).  Conditional Markov inequality therefore
gives
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi
 \le\left\{\frac2\alpha E_\xi
 \|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{14}
\]
The pooled linearization lemma and the branchwise multiplier moment bound in
the stabilized transfer lemma, followed by Jensen's inequality, prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P\widetilde c_r^\xi\le C\rho_r. \tag{15}
\]
Equations (10), (13), and (15) prove all claims, including zero-variance time
zero. \(\square\)
