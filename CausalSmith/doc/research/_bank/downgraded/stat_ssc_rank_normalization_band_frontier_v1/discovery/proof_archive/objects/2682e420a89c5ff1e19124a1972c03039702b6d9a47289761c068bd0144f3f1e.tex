\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(k_D=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\).  Apply
\(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}product\mbox{-}limit\mbox{-}cell}\) to
the target-pre cell, every full donor cell, and each deterministic donor
half-fold.  Each interpolated input has the exact form
\[
 \widehat S^\dagger=\widetilde S^0+L+b+q,                 \tag{1}
\]
where \(L\) is an independent-patient mean-zero martingale average, the
deterministic bias is at most \(Ce^{-c_0k}\), and the centered remainder
has second moment \(Ck^{-2}\).  For independent donor blocks, direct
averaging and the last assertion of that lemma give
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mq_m\right\|_\infty^2
 \le\frac{C}{N_0K_D^2},
 \qquad
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_m(b_m-Eb_m)\right\|_\infty^2
 \le\frac{Ce^{-c_0k_D}}{N_0}.                              \tag{2}
\]
The deterministic mean in (2) is at most
\(C e^{-c_0k_D}\), which is dominated by \(\eta_D(K_D)\).  Since the odd
and even patient sets are independent, the cross products of their
centered \(L\) and \(q\) terms have expectation zero.  Thus cross-folding
removes the same-fold errors-in-variables expectation; only the displayed
deterministic clipping biases remain.

We next prove aggregate spectral regularity without an all-cells-good
event.  Let \(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\) be the interpolated population donor
operator.  By
\(\mathrm{lem:interpolated\mbox{-}population\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}stability}\),
its selected spectrum has a fixed gap and its pre-period Gram has minimum
eigenvalue at least \(\kappa_-/2\) for all sufficiently fine canonical
grids.  Cross-fold independence and (1)--(2) give
\[
 \|E\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le C\eta_D(K_D)+C K_D^{-1}.                              \tag{3}
\]
The centered-remainder term in (3) is in fact \(O(K_D^{-1})\) and tends to
zero; it is not multiplied by \(N_0\).

The stabilized cell map changes by at most \(C/K_D\) when one donor
record changes.  Centering across donors is linear, and the donor curves
are bounded by one.  The rank-one identity
\(\|x\otimes y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|x\|_2\|y\|_2\) therefore shows that one
donor patient changes \(\widehat\Gamma\) in Hilbert--Schmidt norm by at
most
\[
 c_i\le \frac{C}{N_0K_D}.                                  \tag{4}
\]
Expose the donor patients one at a time and let \(D_i\) be the
Hilbert--Schmidt-valued Doob differences.  Conditional symmetrization and
the elementary inequality
\[
 E_\epsilon\exp\left\{s\left\|\sum_i\epsilon_iD_i\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 -\frac{s^2}{2}\sum_i\|D_i\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\right\}\le1 \tag{5}
\]
follow by pairing each Rademacher sign and using
\(\cosh x\le e^{x^2/2}\); finite-dimensional approximation of the
Hilbert--Schmidt space and monotone convergence remove any dimension
restriction.  Equations (4)--(5) and
\(\sum_i c_i^2\le C/(N_0K_D)\) imply
\[
 P\{\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}>x\}
 \le2\exp\{-cN_0K_Dx^2\}                                  \tag{6}
\]
for fixed \(x\).  No union over cells occurs.  By (3), for large \(K_D\)
the expectation is strictly inside the fixed spectral thresholds.  The
variational eigenvalue inequality and the resolvent identity for the
selected projector transfer (6) to the selected spectrum.  The selected
positive-square-root map is Lipschitz on this fixed-gap neighborhood, and
the pre-factor restriction followed by
\(A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}\) transfers it to the empirical pre-Gram.
Consequently the aggregate fallback probability is at most
\[
 P(\mathcal R^c)\le Ce^{-cn_D}.                            \tag{7}
\]

We now display the derivatives used on \(\mathcal R\).  Let \(\Pi(\Gamma)\)
be the selected spectral projector and let \(A=\Gamma_+^{1/2}\) on its
range.  If \(E_p\) restricts a joint-period function to period \(p\), put
\(A_p=E_pA\),
\[
 H=A_0^*A_0,qquad z=H^{-1}A_0^*x,qquad
 F(s,A,x)=s_1+A_1z.                                       \tag{8}
\]
This is the coordinate-free post-period prediction.  For a perturbation
\((\dot s,\dot A,\dot x)\),
\[
 \dot H=\dot A_0^*A_0+A_0^*\dot A_0,
 \qquad
 \dot z=H^{-1}\{\dot A_0^*x+A_0^*\dot x-\dot Hz\},       \tag{9}
\]
\[
 DF[\dot s,\dot A,\dot x]
 =\dot s_1+\dot A_1z+A_1\dot z.                           \tag{10}
\]
Along a twice differentiable path,
\[
 \ddot H=\ddot A_0^*A_0+A_0^*\ddot A_0+2\dot A_0^*\dot A_0,
\]
\[
 \ddot z=H^{-1}\{\ddot A_0^*x+2\dot A_0^*\dot x
 +A_0^*\ddot x-\ddot Hz-2\dot H\dot z\},                 \tag{11}
\]
\[
 D^2F[\dot{},\dot{}]
 =\ddot s_1+\ddot A_1z+2\dot A_1\dot z+A_1\ddot z.       \tag{12}
\]
The selected projector derivative is explicitly
\[
 D\Pi_\Gamma[E]
 =\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\mathscr C}
 (w-\Gamma)^{-1}E(w-\Gamma)^{-1}\,dw,                    \tag{13}
\]
where \(\mathscr C\) separates the selected spectrum from zero.  The
square-root derivative is the unique solution of the Sylvester equation
\[
 A\,DA_\Gamma[E]+DA_\Gamma[E]A
 =D\{\Pi\Gamma\Pi\}_\Gamma[E]                             \tag{14}
\]
on the selected range, with the off-range part determined by (13).
Differentiating (13)--(14) once more gives
\[
 \|DA_\Gamma[E]\|\le C\|E\|,
 \qquad
 \|D^2A_\Gamma[E_1,E_2]\|
 \le C\|E_1\|\|E_2\|,                                   \tag{15}
\]
because the contour distance and the positive selected eigenvalues are
fixed.  Equations (9)--(15) are the required first two Fr\'echet
derivatives, including the projector, positive square root, and inverse
pre-Gram.

Composition with the deterministic stabilized-cell difference bound now
gives, on every dataset in \(\mathcal R\),
\[
 \|D_j^TF\|_\infty\le C/K_T,
 \qquad
 \|D_{p,m,j}^DF\|_\infty\le C/(N_0K_D).                   \tag{16}
\]
The second derivative bound in (15), Taylor's identity with integral
remainder, (1)--(2), and (7) yield the expansion in the statement.  The
deterministic half-fold means form \(\mathfrak b_r\) and are bounded by
\(\eta_D\).  Assigning the bounded estimator discrepancy on
\(\mathcal R^c\) to \(Q_r\) gives
\[
 E\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\left[K_T^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}+n_D^{-1}
 +\rho_r\eta_D+e^{-c_0K_T/2}+e^{-c_0n_D/2}\right]^2
 \le C\upsilon_r^2.                                       \tag{17}
\]

It remains to prove the continuous-time moment bounds.  The martingale
terms in (10) can be written as a fixed finite sum, depending only on
\(r\), of processes
\[
 a(t)M(t)+\int_0^tK(t,u)\,dM(u),                           \tag{18}
\]
where the coefficient curves and their total variations are uniformly
bounded by the model constants.  Integration by parts in (18), followed by
the martingale isometry and Doob's inequality, bounds the expected squared
continuous supremum by the sum of the patient squared weights.  Equation
(16) therefore gives
\[
 E\|Z_r\|_\infty^2\le C(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}).                \tag{19}
\]
Conditionally on the records the multiplier has the same representation;
on \(\mathcal R\), (16) gives (19) with \(E_\xi\), while on
\(\mathcal R^c\) the ordinary pooled derivative has squared supremum at
most \(Cn_D\) and (7) makes its expectation exponentially small.  Hence
\[
 E E_\xi\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}).                                \tag{20}
\]
Because (18)--(20) control the continuous supremum before restriction to
the grid, no grid-cardinality or \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G|}\) loss appears.
Finally, \(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\le\rho_r^2\le
2(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1})\).  This completes the proof. \(\square\)
