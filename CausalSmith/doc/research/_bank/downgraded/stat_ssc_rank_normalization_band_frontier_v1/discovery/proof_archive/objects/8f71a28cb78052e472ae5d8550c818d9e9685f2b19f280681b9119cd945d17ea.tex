\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\), and let \(Z_r\),
\(\mathfrak b_r\), and \(Q_r\) be the grid processes in
\(\mathrm{lem:uniform\mbox{-}linearization}\).  At every sufficiently large
triple, that lemma gives
\[
 \sup_P P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\omega_r/2\}
 \le\alpha-\varepsilon/4,
 \qquad
 \sup_P P\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le\varepsilon/4,                                          \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D).                         \tag{2}
\]
A union bound in (1), followed by the gridwise expansion and (2), gives,
uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 P\!\left\{
 \max_{g\in\mathcal G}|\widehat\theta_r(g)-\theta(g)|
 \le c_r^\xi+\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D)+\omega_r
 \right\}\ge1-\alpha.                                       \tag{3}
\]
The equality between the interpolated population comparison and \(\theta\)
at the grid points, which is used in (3), follows only after the internal
\(L^2\) spectrum and Gram perturbations have been controlled.

Set \(q=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\).  By
\(\mathrm{prop:grid\mbox{-}interpolation}\),
\[
 \|\widehat\theta_r-\theta\|_{\infty,\mathcal I}
 \le\max_{g\in\mathcal G}|\widehat\theta_r(g)-\theta(g)|
 +c_{\mathrm{int}}h^q,
 \qquad h^q\le\rho_r.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase distance from
\(\theta(g)\in[0,1]\).  Linear interpolation of the projected grid values
has its largest deviation from the linear interpolant of \(\theta\) at a
grid endpoint, and \(\theta(0)=1\).  The radius \(A_r\) in
\(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\) is exactly the right
side of (3) plus the final term in (4).  Hence (3)--(4) imply
\[
 P\{\widehat B_r^{\mathrm{lo}}(t)\le\theta(t)\le
       \widehat B_r^{\mathrm{up}}(t)
       \text{ for every }t\in\mathcal I\}\ge1-\alpha.       \tag{5}
\]
This is stronger than \(1-\alpha-\varepsilon\), so
\(\widehat B_r\in\mathcal H_r(N_0,K_T,K_D;\alpha)\).

Clipping the endpoints to \([0,1]\) can only reduce width.  Therefore
\[
 w(\widehat B_r)
 \le2c_r^\xi+2\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D)
      +2\omega_r+2c_{\mathrm{int}}h^q.                     \tag{6}
\]
For all sufficiently large triples,
\(q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\le1-\alpha/2\).  Conditional on the data, let
\(Z_r^\xi\) denote the multiplier linear process whose quantile is
\(c_r^\xi\).  The bounded martingale integrands and the dyadic-net
calculation in the proof of the linearization lemma give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P E_\xi
       \|Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C\rho_r^2.       \tag{7}
\]
Conditional Markov inequality at tail probability at least \(\alpha/2\)
gives
\[
 c_r^\xi\le (2/\alpha)^{1/2}
       \{E_\xi\|Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\}^{1/2}.
\]
Jensen's inequality and (7) yield
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_Pc_r^\xi\le C\rho_r.             \tag{8}
\]
Also \(h^q\le\rho_r\), and the channel-by-channel calculation in the
linearization lemma gives \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) uniformly on every
three-dimensional tail.  Taking expectations in (6), using (8), and
increasing the common lower-tail cutoff \(a_0\) if necessary proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_Pw(\widehat B_r)
 \le C\{\rho_r+\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D)\}
 =C\bar\rho_r.                                               \tag{9}
\]
At \(r=1\), the risk-exhaustion term is absent by definition, so
\(\bar\rho_1=\rho_1=(N_0K_D)^{-1/2}\).  If \(r\ge2\) and
\(\eta_D(K_D)=O(\rho_r)\), then \(\bar\rho_r=O(\rho_r)\).
No step compares the relative growth of \(K_T\), \(N_0\), and \(K_D\), so
(5) and (9) are uniform on every three-dimensional tail. \(\square\)
