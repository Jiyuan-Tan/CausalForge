\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(k_1=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\), and
\(k_2=K_D-k_1\).  All constants below depend only on the fixed model
constants and the fixed rank.  Apply
\(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}product\mbox{-}limit\mbox{-}cell}\) to the
target-pre cell, every donor full cell, and both deterministic donor
half-folds.  For an input containing \(k\) patients it gives
\[
 \widehat S_k^\dagger-widetilde S^0
 =k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_{k,j}+b_k^\dagger+q_k^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \|b_k^\dagger\|_\infty\le Ce^{-c_0k},
 \qquad
 E\|q_k^\dagger\|_\infty^2\le Ck^{-2}.                 \tag{1}
\]
The patient terms are independent and mean zero.  Replacing one record
changes the stabilized interpolated curve by at most \(C/k\), on every
dataset.

We first construct the margin event required by the hard switch.  Let
\(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\) be the covariance operator of the interpolated
population donor panel.  Cross-fitting the odd and even half-folds removes
the same-fold product-limit variance, and (1) gives
\[
 \|E\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le Ce^{-c_0k_1}.                                      \tag{2}
\]
If two datasets differ in one donor record, the deterministic sensitivity in
(1), donor centering, and
\(x\otimes y-x'\otimes y'=(x-x')\otimes y+x'\otimes(y-y')\)
give
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma(O)-\widehat\Gamma(O')\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le \frac{C}{N_0K_D}=\frac C{n_D}.                    \tag{3}
\]
Symmetrization with independent record replacements yields
\[
 E\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le \frac C{n_D}.                                     \tag{4}
\]
For clarity, the needed exponential tail follows directly from the same
replacement bound.  Reveal the donor records successively and let
\(D_i\) be the Doob differences of
\(f(O)=\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\).
Conditional on the preceding records, the range of \(D_i\) has length at
most \(C/n_D\) by (3).  The conditional exponential-moment inequality
\(E\{e^{sD_i}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}\}\le
\exp\{s^2C^2/(8n_D^2)\}\), iterated over the \(O(n_D)\)
donor records, proves
\[
 P\{f-Ef>x\}\le \exp(-cn_Dx^2).                        \tag{5}
\]
Equations (2), (4), and (5) therefore imply, for every fixed \(x>0\) and
all sufficiently large triples,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}
 P\{\|\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}>x\}
 \le Ce^{-cn_D}.                                        \tag{6}
\]

The interpolated-population stability lemma gives
\[
 \lambda_d(\Gamma_{\mathcal G})\ge\gamma_-^2/2,
 \qquad \lambda_{d+1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal G})=0,
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(H_{0,\mathcal G})\ge\kappa_-/2. \tag{7}
\]
The eigenvalue variational inequality, the contour-resolvent formula for the
selected projector, the selected positive-square-root perturbation identity,
and
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}                        \tag{8}
\]
show that, on a fixed neighborhood of \(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\), the selected
factors and their pre-period Gram are Lipschitz functions of the donor
covariance.  By (3), the entire segment joining a donor input panel to any
one-donor-patient replacement changes its covariance by at most \(C/n_D\).
Combining this fact with (6)--(8) and the fixed slack in (7) proves for the
margin event \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) defined in the corrected band construction
that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P(\mathcal M_{r,n}^c)
 \le Ce^{-cn_D}.                                        \tag{9}
\]
In particular, the ordinary regular-event failure probability has the same
bound.  This argument is aggregate: it never intersects cellwise good-risk
events and hence incurs no union over donors.  A target-pre replacement does
not alter \(\widehat\Gamma\), its selected factor, or \(\widehat H_0\), so it
cannot change the hard branch.

We next linearize only where the total hard-switched map is stable.  On the
fixed-gap regular neighborhood write the coordinate-free prediction map as
\[
 F(s,A,x)=s_1+A_1z,
 \qquad H=A_0^*A_0,
 \qquad z=H^{-1}A_0^*x.                                 \tag{10}
\]
Along a differentiable path,
\[
 \dot H=\dot A_0^*A_0+A_0^*\dot A_0,
 \qquad
 \dot z=H^{-1}\{\dot A_0^*x+A_0^*\dot x-\dot Hz\},
\]
\[
 \dot F=\dot s_1+\dot A_1z+A_1\dot z.                 \tag{11}
\]
The contour formula for the selected projector and the selected-range
Sylvester equation
\[
 A\,DA[E]+DA[E]A=D(\Pi\Gamma\Pi)[E]                    \tag{12}
\]
bound the first two Fr\'echet derivatives of \(F\) uniformly on this
neighborhood.  The only denominators are the fixed spectral gap, the positive
selected eigenvalue, and the positive pre-period Gram in (7).

On \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\), every donor replacement and the corresponding input
line segment remain in that neighborhood.  Composition of (11)--(12) with
the stabilized-input sensitivity therefore gives the finite-replacement and
pathwise derivative bounds for the total estimator
\[
 \|D_j^T F\|_\infty\le C/K_T,
 \qquad
 \|D_{p,m,j}^D F\|_\infty\le C/(N_0K_D).               \tag{13}
\]
No assertion in (13) is made across the cutoff on
\(\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\).  Nevertheless, both branches are uniformly bounded:
the regular branch is bounded by the fixed inverse-Gram and factor thresholds,
and the pooled product-limit branch lies in \([0,1]\).  The oracle linear sum
is also deterministically bounded because its patient envelopes sum to a
constant.  Consequently every possible branch-switch term can be written as
\(Q_r^{\mathrm{sw}}\) with
\[
 \|Q_r^{\mathrm{sw}}\|_\infty
 \le C\mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\},
 \qquad
 E\|Q_r^{\mathrm{sw}}\|_\infty^2\le Ce^{-cn_D}.        \tag{14}
\]

Taylor's formula at the interpolated population panel, (1), the donor-average
version of its centered remainder bound, and (10)--(14) now give, at every
grid knot,
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =K_T^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}\phi_{T,j}
 +n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j}
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r.                                    \tag{15}
\]
The population center is \(\theta\) at the knots by the canonical target
coordinate and interpolated-population stability lemmas.  The deterministic
stabilization bias is passed through a uniformly bounded derivative, so the
definition of \(C_\eta\) gives
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le\eta_D(K_D). \tag{16}
\]
The target-cell centered remainder, the donor-averaged centered remainder,
the quadratic Taylor term, the bias--noise interaction, risk-exhaustion
terms, and (14) have respective \(L^2\)-supremum bounds
\[
 K_T^{-1},\quad N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1},\quad
 K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1},\quad \rho_r\eta_D(K_D),\quad
 e^{-c_\dagger K_T/2},\quad e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}.       \tag{17}
\]
The registered constant \(c_\dagger\) is smaller than the fixed concentration
constants above.  Thus (17), the triangle inequality in \(L^2\), and the
definition of \(\upsilon_r\) prove
\[
 E\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C\upsilon_r^2.      \tag{18}
\]

It remains to justify multiplier moments without differentiating the hard
switch.  The corrected construction defines one coefficient array by
differentiating the regular map on the regular branch and a separate array
from the pooled map on the fallback branch.  On every dataset assigned to the
regular branch, the defining thresholds and the stabilized cell bound give
the branchwise envelopes \(C/K_T\) and \(C/n_D\).  For the pooled branch an
individual coefficient is always at most one; on the aggregate-risk event it
is at most \(C/n_D\), while the complementary event is exponentially rare.
The ordered product-limit calculation consequently gives
\[
 E E_\xi\|\widehat Z_{r,\mathrm{pool}}^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le Cn_D^{-1}.                                        \tag{19}
\]
For the regular coefficients, Gaussian isometry, predictable isometry for the
patient martingales, and the time-ordered bounded-variation maximal inequality
give
\[
 E\|Z_r\|_\infty^2+E E_\xi
 \|\widehat Z_{r,\mathrm{reg}}^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}).                              \tag{20}
\]
Selecting the appropriate branch can only decrease the sum of the two
nonnegative bounds in (19)--(20).  Hence
\[
 \sup_PE_P\|Z_r\|_\infty^2+
 \sup_PE_PE_\xi\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\}=C\rho_r^2.                \tag{21}
\]
All maximal inequalities used above are ordered in physical time before any
restriction to the grid.  Therefore no \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G|}\) factor is
introduced. \(\square\)
