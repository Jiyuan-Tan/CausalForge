\textbf{Proof.}
Write every oracle linear process as \(Z_r=\sum_iL_i\).  The pooled
linearization lemma gives \(\|L_i\|_\infty\le Cn_D^{-1}\) when \(r=1\).
For \(r\ge2\), the stabilized transfer lemma gives
\[
 \|L_i\|_\infty\le
 \begin{cases}
 CK_T^{-1},&i\text{ is target-pre},\\
 Cn_D^{-1},&i\text{ is a donor patient}.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{1}
\]
These are oracle bounds and therefore do not involve the empirical hard
switch.

We first compare covariance kernels.  In rank one, with
\(H_D=s_1\bar G_D\), the pooled product-limit calculation gives
\[
 \Sigma_1(s,t)=\frac{s_1(s)s_1(t)}{n_D}
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{\lambda_1(u)}{H_D(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_1(s,t)=\widehat S_D(s-)\widehat S_D(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)^2}\,dN(u).        \tag{2}
\]
On \(\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge n_Ds_-g_-/2\}\), substitute
\(dN=dM+Y\lambda_1du\) in (2).  Predictable isometry, the time-ordered
risk-process maximal inequality, and the pooled survival expansion imply
\[
 E\{\rho_1^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\|_\infty\}
 \le Cn_D^{-1/2}.                                       \tag{3}
\]
The complementary risk event has probability \(Ce^{-cn_D}\), and the empty
risk convention bounds its contribution, so (3) is uniform over the
non-identical donor censoring laws.

Now suppose \(r\ge2\).  On \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\), all empirical donor inputs
and their one-replacement segments lie in one fixed-gap regular neighborhood.
Let \(F\) be the population coordinate-free prediction map and
\(\widehat F\) its empirical regular-branch version.  The projector contour
identity, the selected-square-root Sylvester equation, and the inverse-Gram
identity, each differentiated once, yield
\[
 E\|D\widehat F-DF\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C\{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)\}.            \tag{4}
\]
This is a comparison of two regular-branch derivatives on
\(\mathcal M_{r,n}\), not a derivative of the hard switch.

For a stabilized input of size \(k\), put \(H_k=S^0G\) and define
\[
 V_k(s,t)=S^0(s)S^0(t)\int_0^{s\wedge t}
 \frac{\lambda^0(u)}{H_k(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat V_k(s,t)=k\widehat S_k^\dagger(s-)
 \widehat S_k^\dagger(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\{Y_k^\dagger(u)\}^{-2}\,dN_k(u). \tag{5}
\]
After substituting \(dN_k=dM_k+Y_k\lambda^0du\), the difference in (5) is
the martingale term with integrand \(k/Y_k^{\dagger2}\), the inverse-risk
replacement term, and the two survival-factor replacement terms.  Clipping
gives deterministic denominators on all datasets, while predictable isometry
and the stabilized cell expansion give
\[
 E\|\widehat V_k-V_k\|_{\infty,\mathcal I^2}
 \le Ck^{-1/2}.                                         \tag{6}
\]
Compose (4)--(6), sum the independent target and donor covariance blocks, and
use the deterministic bias bound.  On \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) this gives
\[
 E\left[
 \rho_r^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}\}\right]
 \le Ca_{r,n}.                                          \tag{7}
\]
On \(\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\), the selected regular-branch coefficient array and
the pooled-branch coefficient array have the separate global moment bounds
proved in the transfer lemma.  Since
\(P(\mathcal M_{r,n}^c)\le Ce^{-cn_D}\) and
\(\rho_r^2\ge n_D^{-1}\), its normalized contribution is bounded by
\(Cn_De^{-cn_D}\), which is dominated by the exponential term in
\(a_{r,n}\).  Thus (3) and (7) imply, for every rank,
\[
 E\{\rho_r^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty\}
 \le Ca_{r,n}.                                          \tag{8}
\]
Markov's inequality supplies a covariance event with complement probability
at most \(Ca_{r,n}^{1/2}\) and covariance error at most
\(C\rho_r^2a_{r,n}^{1/2}\).  At ranks at least two, intersect it with
\(\mathcal M_{r,n}\); its exponentially small complement is already dominated
by the same displayed probability.

It remains to obtain the semimetric and modulus bounds.  For a grid interval
\(J=(g,g']\), let \(N_T(J)\) and \(N_D(J)\) count observed failures in the
target-pre and donor blocks, omitting \(N_T\) at rank one.  Hazard smoothness
and patient independence imply
\[
 EN_T(J)\le LK_T|J|,
 \qquad EN_D(J)\le Ln_D|J|.                             \tag{9}
\]
For a sum \(V\) of independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(\mu\), the
exponential Markov calculation
\(E e^{s(V-EV)}\le\exp\{\mu(e^s-1-s)\}\) gives, uniformly over all grid
intervals, an event on which
\[
 N_T(J)\le CK_T(|J|+h),
 \qquad N_D(J)\le Cn_D(|J|+h),                           \tag{10}
\]
whose complement has probability at most
\(C|\mathcal G|^2e^{-cmh}\).  Since
\(h\asymp\rho_r^{1/q_\beta}\),
\(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}>3/2\), and
\(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\), this probability is smaller than
\(Ca_{r,n}^{1/2}\) on every sufficiently large three-dimensional tail.

On the margin event the empirical coefficients are evaluated on a single
regular branch.  Their smooth coefficient changes contribute
\(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|^2\), while failures in \(J\), (1), and (10) contribute
\[
 C\{K_T^{-2}N_T(J)+n_D^{-2}N_D(J)\}.
\]
For distinct grid points \(|g-g'|\ge h\), so intersection with the covariance
and count events gives
\[
 \widehat d_r(g,g')^2\le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|.               \tag{11}
\]
Taking expectations in the same calculation gives
\(d_r(g,g')^2\le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\).  This constructs
\(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) and proves its first three assertions.

Finally, each oracle increment is a sum of independent centered variables,
has variance at most \(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\), and by (1) has summand envelope at
most \(C\rho_rm^{-1/2}\).  The same exponential-moment calculation gives
\[
 P\{|Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')|>x\}
 \le2\exp\left[-c\min\left\{
 \frac{x^2}{\rho_r^2|g-g'|},
 \frac{x\sqrt m}{\rho_r}\right\}\right].              \tag{12}
\]
Insert
\[
 x=C\rho_r\left[\{u\log(ep_{\mathcal G})\}^{1/2}
 +m^{-1/2}\log(ep_{\mathcal G})\right]
\]
and take a union bound over at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^2\) signed pairs.  After
enlarging \(C\), (12) gives the stated \(2p_{\mathcal G}^{-4}\) bound.  No
variance was inverted, so zero-variance coordinates are included. \(\square\)
