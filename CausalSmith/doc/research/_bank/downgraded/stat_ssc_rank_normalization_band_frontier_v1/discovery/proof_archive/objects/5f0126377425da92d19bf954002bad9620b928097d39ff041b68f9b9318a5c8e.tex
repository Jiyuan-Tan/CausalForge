\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(k_D=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\). Apply \(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}product\mbox{-}limit\mbox{-}cell}\) to the target-pre cell, every full donor cell, and each deterministic donor half-fold. Each interpolated input has the exact form
\[
 \widehat S^\dagger=\widetilde S^0+L+b+q,                 \tag{1}
\]
where \(L\) is an independent-patient mean-zero martingale average, the deterministic bias is at most \(Ce^{-c_0k}\), and the centered remainder has second moment at most \(Ck^{-2}\). For independent donor blocks, direct averaging and the bounded-variation conclusion of that lemma give
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mq_m\right\|_\infty^2
 \le\frac{C}{N_0K_D^2},
 \qquad
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mU_m\right\|_\infty^2
 \le\frac{Ce^{-c_0k_D}}{N_0},                            \tag{2}
\]
where \(U_m\) is the centered rare-cell contribution. Its deterministic mean is at most \(Ce^{-c_0k_D}\), which is dominated by \(\eta_D(K_D)\). Because the odd and even patient sets are independent, every cross product involving a centered \(L\), \(q\), or \(U\) term from opposite folds has expectation zero. Thus cross-folding removes the same-fold errors-in-variables expectation, and only the deterministic clipping biases remain.

We next prove aggregate spectral regularity without an all-cells-good event. Let \(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\) be the interpolated population donor operator. By \(\mathrm{lem:interpolated\mbox{-}population\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}stability}\), its selected spectrum has a fixed gap and its pre-period Gram has minimum eigenvalue at least \(\kappa_-/2\) for all sufficiently fine canonical grids. Cross-fold independence and (1)--(2) give
\[
 \|E\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le C\eta_D(K_D).                                      \tag{3}
\]
The stabilized-cell bounded difference is \(C/K_D\). Centering across donors is linear, and all donor curves are bounded by one. Since \(\|x\otimes y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|x\|_2\|y\|_2\), replacing one donor patient changes \(\widehat\Gamma\) by at most
\[
 c_i\le\frac{C}{N_0K_D}.                                 \tag{4}
\]
Let \(O^{(i)}\) replace that record by an independent copy. The Hilbert-space Efron--Stein inequality, followed by (4), gives directly
\[
 E\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le\frac12\sum_iE\|\widehat\Gamma(O)-
                    \widehat\Gamma(O^{(i)})\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le\frac{C}{N_0K_D}.                                   \tag{5}
\]
No vector-valued exponential inequality is used. Define the real-valued function
\[
 f(O)=\|\widehat\Gamma(O)-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality and (4) show that changing record \(i\) changes \(f\) by at most \(c_i\). Scalar McDiarmid therefore yields
\[
 P\{f-Ef>x\}\le
 \exp\left\{-\frac{2x^2}{\sum_i c_i^2}\right\}
 \le e^{-cN_0K_Dx^2}.                                    \tag{6}
\]
Jensen and (5) give \(Ef\le C(N_0K_D)^{-1/2}\). Combining (3), (5), and (6), and choosing a fixed threshold below the population spectral and pre-Gram margins, gives, for all sufficiently large triples,
\[
 P(\mathcal R^c)\le Ce^{-c_\dagger N_0K_D}.              \tag{7}
\]
The variational eigenvalue inequality transfers (6) to the selected eigenvalues. The contour resolvent identity transfers it to the selected projector, the positive-square-root map is Lipschitz on the fixed-gap neighborhood, and \(A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}\) transfers it to the pre-Gram inverse. Equation (7) concerns only the aggregate operator and never the event that all donor cells have large risk sets.

We display the derivatives used on \(\mathcal R\). Let \(\Pi(\Gamma)\) be the selected spectral projector and let \(A=\Gamma_+^{1/2}\) on its range. If \(E_p\) restricts a joint-period function to period \(p\), put \(A_p=E_pA\),
\[
 H=A_0^*A_0,\qquad z=H^{-1}A_0^*x,\qquad
 F(s,A,x)=s_1+A_1z.                                      \tag{8}
\]
For a perturbation \((\dot s,\dot A,\dot x)\),
\[
 \dot H=\dot A_0^*A_0+A_0^*\dot A_0,
 \qquad
 \dot z=H^{-1}\{\dot A_0^*x+A_0^*\dot x-\dot Hz\},    \tag{9}
\]
\[
 DF[\dot s,\dot A,\dot x]
 =\dot s_1+\dot A_1z+A_1\dot z.                         \tag{10}
\]
Along a twice differentiable path,
\[
 \ddot H=\ddot A_0^*A_0+A_0^*\ddot A_0+2\dot A_0^*\dot A_0,
\]
\[
 \ddot z=H^{-1}\{\ddot A_0^*x+2\dot A_0^*\dot x
 +A_0^*\ddot x-\ddot Hz-2\dot H\dot z\},              \tag{11}
\]
\[
 D^2F[\dot{},\dot{}]
 =\ddot s_1+\ddot A_1z+2\dot A_1\dot z+A_1\ddot z.    \tag{12}
\]
The selected projector derivative is
\[
 D\Pi_\Gamma[E]
 =\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\mathscr C}
 (w-\Gamma)^{-1}E(w-\Gamma)^{-1}\,dw,                  \tag{13}
\]
where \(\mathscr C\) separates the selected spectrum from zero. The square-root derivative solves
\[
 A\,DA_\Gamma[E]+DA_\Gamma[E]A
 =D\{\Pi\Gamma\Pi\}_\Gamma[E]                         \tag{14}
\]
on the selected range. Differentiating (13)--(14) once more and using the spectral and pre-Gram thresholds gives
\[
 \|DA_\Gamma[E]\|\le C_\partial\|E\|,
 \qquad
 \|D^2A_\Gamma[E_1,E_2]\|
 \le C_\partial\|E_1\|\|E_2\|.                       \tag{15}
\]
To see that the displayed constant in the definition is sufficient, bound each resolvent by \(4\gamma_-^{-2}\), the positive square-root inverse by \(2\gamma_-^{-1}\), \(\|H^{-1}\|\) by \(4\kappa_-^{-1}\), \(\|A\|\) by \(2\gamma_+\), \(\|z\|\) by \(2M_\star\), and every restriction or integration map by \(1+\bar\tau\). Repeated triangle and product inequalities in (9)--(14) are bounded by
\[
 C_\partial=2^{12}r^2(1+\bar\tau)^2
 (1+M_b+M_z+M_\star+\gamma_+)^2
 (1+\gamma_-^{-1}+\kappa_-^{-1})^4,                    \tag{16}
\]
exactly as registered in the construction.

Composition with the deterministic stabilized-cell difference bound gives, on every dataset in \(\mathcal R\),
\[
 \|D_j^TF\|_\infty\le C_\partial/K_T,
 \qquad
 \|D_{p,m,j}^DF\|_\infty\le C_\partial/(N_0K_D).       \tag{17}
\]
Taylor's identity with integral remainder, (1)--(3), the second derivative bound, and (7) yield the expansion in the statement. The deterministic half-fold means form \(\mathfrak b_r\) and are bounded by \(\eta_D\). Assign the bounded estimator discrepancy on \(\mathcal R^c\) to \(Q_r\). With \(\upsilon_r\) from the corrected \(\omega_r\) definition,
\[
 E\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C\upsilon_r^2.      \tag{18}
\]

The martingale terms in (10) are a fixed sum, depending only on \(r\), of processes
\[
 a(t)M(t)+\int_0^tK(t,u)\,dM(u),                         \tag{19}
\]
where (9)--(17) bound the coefficient curves and their total variations by \((1+L\bar\tau)h_0^{-1}(1+C_\partial)\). Integration by parts, the predictable isometry, Doob's inequality, and the squared patient weights in (17) give
\[
 E\|Z_r\|_\infty^2
 \le C_Z\{K_T^{-1}+(N_0K_D)^{-1}\},                    \tag{20}
\]
where
\[
 C_Z=2^{12}(1+L\bar\tau)^2h_0^{-2}(1+C_\partial)^2.     \tag{21}
\]
Conditionally on the records, the multiplier has the same representation. On \(\mathcal R\), (17) proves (20) with \(E_\xi\). On \(\mathcal R^c\), the ordinary pooled derivative has squared supremum at most \(CN_0K_D\), while (7) makes its unconditional contribution exponentially small. Thus
\[
 E E_\xi\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C_Z\{K_T^{-1}+(N_0K_D)^{-1}\}.                    \tag{22}
\]
Because (19)--(22) precede restriction to the grid, no grid-cardinality loss occurs.

For completeness, let \(J=|\mathcal G|\). Patient-level Bernstein applied to products of the deterministically bounded weights in (17), followed by a union bound over only the \(J^2\) covariance coordinates, gives
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G^2}
 \le C\rho_r^2
 \left\{K_T^{-1/2}+(N_0K_D)^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)
 +e^{-c_\dagger N_0K_D/2}\right\}\log(2J)
\]
with probability tending to one uniformly. The terms respectively bound optional variation, empirical-versus-population derivative replacement, deterministic clipping shift, and fallback. The canonical grid gives \(J\le2+\bar\tau\rho_r^{-1/q}\), so every bracketed negative power or exponential dominates \(\log(2J)\) on unrestricted three-dimensional tails. Hence the right side is \(o_P(\rho_r^2)\). This consistency result is descriptive; coverage uses the deterministic guard and makes no multiplier-only quantile assertion. Equations (18), (20)--(22) prove every claim. \(\square\)
