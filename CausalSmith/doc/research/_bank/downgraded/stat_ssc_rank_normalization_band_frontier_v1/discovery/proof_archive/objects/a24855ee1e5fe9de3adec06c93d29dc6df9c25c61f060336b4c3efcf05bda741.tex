\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(k_1=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\), and \(k_2=K_D-k_1\). Apply the stabilized product-limit lemma to the target-pre cell, all donor full cells, and both deterministic donor half-folds. Each input of size \(k\) has
\[
 \widehat S_k^\dagger=\widetilde S^0+k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_{k,j}
 +b_k^\dagger+q_k^\dagger,                               \tag{1}
\]
where the patient terms are independent and mean zero,
\[
 \|b_k^\dagger\|_\infty\le Ce^{-c_0k},qquad
 E\|q_k^\dagger\|_\infty^2\le Ck^{-2},                  \tag{2}
\]
and replacing one patient changes the interpolated curve by at most \(C/k\).

For completeness, donor averaging improves the centered remainder. Write its martingale part as
\[
 V_{k,m}(t)=-S_m^0(t)\int_0^tD_{k,m}(u)\,dM_m(u),qquad
 E\int D_{k,m}^2Y_m\lambda_m^0\le Ck^{-2}.               \tag{3}
\]
Integration by parts separates a martingale and a bounded-variation integral. Independence across donor cells, predictable isometry, and the time-ordered bounded-variation maximal inequality give
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mq_{k,m}^\dagger\right\|_\infty^2
 \le C(N_0k^2)^{-1}+CN_0^{-1}e^{-c_0k}.                  \tag{4}
\]
This calculation is in continuous time and contains no grid union.

Let \(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\) be the interpolated population covariance operator. Independence of the odd and even folds removes the same-fold errors-in-variables term, and (1)--(2) imply
\[
 \|E\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le Ce^{-c_0k_1}.                                       \tag{5}
\]
One patient replacement changes \(\widehat\Gamma\) by at most \(C/(N_0K_D)\). Hilbert-space Efron--Stein and scalar bounded differences therefore give
\[
 E\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le Cn_D^{-1},
\]
\[
 P\{\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 >Cn_D^{-1/2}+x\}\le2e^{-cn_Dx^2}.                       \tag{6}
\]
The interpolated-population stability lemma supplies a fixed selected spectral gap and a pre-Gram eigenvalue at least \(\kappa_-/2\). The eigenvalue variational inequality, the spectral-projector resolvent identity, continuity of the selected positive square root, and the inverse identity therefore turn (5)--(6) into
\[
 \sup_PP(\mathcal R^c)\le Ce^{-cn_D},                    \tag{7}
\]
where \(\mathcal R\) is the aggregate regular event in the band definition. No all-cells-good event was used.

On the fixed-gap neighborhood, write the coordinate-free map as
\[
 F(s,A,x)=s_1+A_1z,qquad
 H=A_0^*A_0,qquad z=H^{-1}A_0^*x.                        \tag{8}
\]
Along a differentiable path,
\[
 \dot H=\dot A_0^*A_0+A_0^*\dot A_0,qquad
 \dot z=H^{-1}\{\dot A_0^*x+A_0^*\dot x-\dot Hz\},
\]
\[
 \dot F=\dot s_1+\dot A_1z+A_1\dot z.                    \tag{9}
\]
The contour formula for the selected projector and the Sylvester equation
\[
 A\,DA[E]+DA[E]A=D(\Pi\Gamma\Pi)[E]                       \tag{10}
\]
show that the first two Fr\'echet derivatives of \(F\) are uniformly bounded; the spectral and pre-Gram lower bounds are exactly the denominators used here. Composing (9)--(10) with the all-dataset cell sensitivity gives
\[
 \|D_j^TF\|_\infty\le C/K_T,qquad
 \|D_{p,m,j}^DF\|_\infty\le C/(N_0K_D)                 \tag{11}
\]
on every regular dataset. On fallback, the total measurable pooled derivative is used.

Taylor's formula at the interpolated population panel, (1), (4), and (7)--(11) now yield at every grid knot
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =K_T^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}\phi_{T,j}
 +n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j}
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r.                                      \tag{12}
\]
The population center equals \(\theta\) on the grid by canonical target-coordinate uniqueness and interpolated-population stability. The deterministic donor half-fold bias is mapped by a uniformly Lipschitz derivative, so the definition of \(C_\eta\) gives
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le\eta_D(K_D). \tag{13}
\]
The target cell remainder, averaged donor remainder, quadratic Taylor term, bias--noise interaction, and fallback terms have respective \(L^2\)-supremum orders
\[
 K_T^{-1},\quad N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1},\quad
 K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1},\quad \rho_r\eta_D(K_D),\quad
 e^{-c_\dagger K_T/2}+e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}.               \tag{14}
\]
These are precisely dominated by the registered \(\upsilon_r\). Squaring after the triangle inequality proves
\[
 E\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C\upsilon_r^2.       \tag{15}
\]

Finally, each first-order term is a fixed-rank sum of a bounded-variation coefficient times a patient martingale and a time-ordered stochastic integral. Predictable isometry, the maximal inequality, (11), and independence give
\[
 E\|Z_r\|_\infty^2\le C(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}).              \tag{16}
\]
Conditionally on the data, Gaussian isometry and the same ordered argument give the multiplier analogue on \(\mathcal R\); the pooled fallback contribution is absorbed using (7). Thus
\[
 E E_\xi\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1})=C\rho_r^2.                     \tag{17}
\]
All estimates precede restriction to the grid, so no \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G|}\) factor appears. \(\square\)
