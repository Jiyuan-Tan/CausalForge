\textbf{Proof.}
Suppress the cell label. For patient \(j\), define
\[
 N_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\le u,\Delta_j=1\},\qquad
 Y_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\ge u\},\qquad
 M_j(u)=N_j(u)-\int_0^uY_j(v)\lambda^0(v)\,dv.             \tag{1}
\]
Control consistency and independent censoring give the compensator in (1), and patient independence makes the \(M_j\)'s independent mean-zero square-integrable martingales. Write \(Y=\sum_jY_j\), \(N=\sum_jN_j\), \(M=\sum_jM_j\), and \(H(u)=EY_j(u)=S^0(u)G(u)\). The overlap assumptions give
\[
 H(u)\ge h_0:=s_-g_->0,\qquad u\in\mathcal I.             \tag{2}
\]

On a tied dataset, order failures by \((u,j)\), removing each earlier ordered failure from the risk set before processing the next. This agrees with the ordinary tied product-limit factor because
\[
 \prod_{a=0}^{e-1}\left(1-\frac1{Y-a}\right)=1-\frac eY. \tag{3}
\]
It also defines the stabilized map on every dataset by a deterministic measurable rule. Put \(Y^\dagger=\max\{Y,\ell_k\}\) on this ordered event axis. Every factor lies in \([0,1]\). If \(Y(\bar\tau)\ge\ell_k\), monotonicity gives \(Y(u)\ge\ell_k\) at every earlier ordered failure, so \(Y^\dagger=Y\), and (3) proves equality with ordinary Kaplan--Meier.

The horizon risk indicators are independent Bernoulli variables with \(\mu=EY(\bar\tau)\ge kh_0\). Exponential Markov inequality gives, for \(0<\delta<1\),
\[
 P\{Y\le(1-\delta)\mu\}\le\exp\{-\delta^2\mu/2\}.       \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, \(Ee^{-sY}\le\exp\{(e^{-s}-1)\mu\}\), and optimizing \(s\) uses \((1-\delta)\log(1-\delta)+\delta\ge\delta^2/2\). With \(\delta=7/8\),
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)<kh_0/8\}
 \le e^{-49h_0k/128}\le e^{-h_0k/3}=e^{-c_0k}.           \tag{5}
\]

For \(f(y)=1-1/\max\{y,\ell_k\}\), the maximum map is one-Lipschitz and
\[
 |f(y+e)-f(y)|\le |e|\ell_k^{-2},\qquad |e|\le1.          \tag{6}
\]
Changing one record inserts or removes at most one failure factor and changes every remaining ordered risk count by at most one. The telescoping identity for products of numbers in \([0,1]\), (6), and at most \(k\) factors give
\[
 \sup_t|\widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O)-\widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O')|
 \le \ell_k^{-1}+k\ell_k^{-2}\le C/k.                  \tag{7}
\]
Piecewise-linear interpolation cannot increase the maximum difference at the knots. A patient has at most one observed failure, so its observed multiplier contribution obeys
\[
 \sup_t\widehat S_k^\dagger(t-)
 \int_0^t(Y^\dagger)^{-1}\,dN_j
 \le\ell_k^{-1}\le C/k.                                 \tag{8}
\]
Thus (7)--(8) hold without a risk-set event.

Put \(R=\widehat S_k^\dagger/S^0\). Product integration and integration by parts give the exact ratio identity
\[
 R(t)-1=-\int_0^tR(u-)\{Y^\dagger(u)\}^{-1}\,dM(u)
 +\int_0^tR(u-)\left\{1-\frac{Y(u)}{Y^\dagger(u)}\right\}
          \lambda^0(u)\,du.                              \tag{9}
\]
The second integrand vanishes unless \(Y(u)<\ell_k\). By monotonicity, this event at any \(u\le\bar\tau\) implies
\[
 \mathcal B_k:=\{Y(\bar\tau)<\ell_k\}.
\]
Moreover \(0\le R\le s_-^{-1}\) and \(\int_0^{\bar\tau}\lambda^0\le L\bar\tau\). If \(A_k\) denotes the drift in (9) after multiplication by \(S^0(t)\), (5) gives
\[
 \|EA_k\|_\infty\le Ce^{-c_0k},
 \qquad E\|A_k-EA_k\|_\infty^2\le Ce^{-c_0k}.           \tag{10}
\]

Define independent mean-zero processes
\[
 \varphi_j^{\mathrm{PL}}(t)
 =-S^0(t)\int_0^tH(u)^{-1}\,dM_j(u).                     \tag{11}
\]
Subtracting \(k^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_j^{\mathrm{PL}}\) from (9) leaves
\[
 V_k(t)=-S^0(t)\int_0^t
 \left\{\frac{R(u-)}{Y^\dagger(u)}-\frac1{kH(u)}\right\}
 dM(u).                                                    \tag{12}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_k=\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge kh_0/2\}\), one has \(Y^\dagger=Y\), and
\[
 \frac{R}{Y}-\frac1{kH}
 =\frac{R-1}{Y}-\frac{Y-kH}{kHY}.                         \tag{13}
\]
Stopping (9) before \(Y<kh_0/2\), Doob's inequality, the predictable isometry, and independence yield
\[
 E\{\mathbf1_{\mathcal E_k}\|R-1\|_\infty^2\}\le Ck^{-1},
 \qquad E\{Y(u)-kH(u)\}^2\le k.                         \tag{14}
\]
Substitution in (13) gives
\[
 E\left[\mathbf1_{\mathcal E_k}\int_0^{\bar\tau}
 \left\{\frac{R}{Y^\dagger}-\frac1{kH}\right\}^2
 Y\lambda^0\right]\le Ck^{-2}.                          \tag{15}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_k^c\), the clipped denominator makes the expression in braces at most \(C/k\). The fourth-moment martingale inequality, Cauchy--Schwarz, and the Chernoff bound give
\[
 E\left[\mathbf1_{\mathcal E_k^c}\int_0^{\bar\tau}
 \left\{\frac{R}{Y^\dagger}-\frac1{kH}\right\}^2
 Y\lambda^0\right]
 \le Ck^{-1}e^{-ck/2}\le Ck^{-2}.                        \tag{16}
\]
Equations (15)--(16) and Doob's inequality prove \(E\|V_k\|_\infty^2\le Ck^{-2}\). Set
\[
 b_k^{\mathrm{PL}}=EA_k,\qquad
 q_k^{\mathrm{PL}}=V_k+A_k-EA_k.                         \tag{17}
\]
Equations (10), (15)--(17), and \(e^{-c_0k}\le Ck^{-2}\) give the stated bias and remainder. Applying \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) preserves means and independence, contracts the supremum norm, and replaces the center by \(\widetilde S^0\).

It remains to prove the donor-average rare-cell bound without using slopes of the interpolant. Expose the knots as \(0=g_0<\cdots<g_J=\bar\tau\). For a centered rare contribution \(U_m=A_{m,k}-EA_{m,k}\), the product-integral formula represents the knot sequence as a signed bounded-variation transform of the ordered counting/risk increments. Since the product-limit path is monotone in \([0,1]\), the survival multiplier has total variation bounded by \(1+L\bar\tau\), and centering doubles variation, there is a fixed \(C_{\mathrm{BV}}\) such that
\[
 V_m:=\sum_{\ell=1}^J|U_m(g_\ell)-U_m(g_{\ell-1})|
       +|U_m(g_0)|
 \le C_{\mathrm{BV}}\{\mathbf1_{\mathcal B_{m,k}}+P(\mathcal B_{m,k})\}. \tag{18}
\]
In particular, \(EV_m^2\le Ce^{-c_0k}\).

For completeness, we record the one-dimensional nested-index maximal calculation. Conditional on paths \(u_m\) of variation \(V_m\), let \(\epsilon_m\) be independent Rademacher signs and set
\[
 d(s,t)^2=\sum_m\{u_m(t)-u_m(s)\}^2.
\]
Because \(|u_m(t)-u_m(s)|\le V_m\), the cumulative variation measure
\[
 A((s,t])=\sum_mV_m\operatorname{Var}_{(s,t]}(u_m)
\]
satisfies \(d(s,t)^2\le A((s,t])\) and \(A(\mathcal I)\le\sum_mV_m^2\). Partition the time-ordered knots at level \(a\) into at most \(2^a\) successive blocks, each having \(A\)-mass at most \(2^{-a}A(\mathcal I)\). Summation by parts reduces each level increment of \(\sum_m\epsilon_mu_m\) to a signed sum with conditional variance at most that block mass. The scalar Rademacher exponential bound and integration of its tail therefore give
\[
 E_\epsilon\max_{g\in\mathcal G}\left|\sum_m\epsilon_mu_m(g)\right|^2
 \le C A(\mathcal I)
       \left\{\sum_{a\ge0}2^{-a/2}\sqrt{a+1}\right\}^2
 \le C\sum_mV_m^2.                                      \tag{19}
\]
This is the contraction/maximal inequality for the nested time-index class, proved in time order; neither \(J\) nor the mesh occurs. Ghost-sample symmetrization, (18)--(19), and (5) yield
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}U_m\right\|_\infty^2
 \le\frac{C}{N_0^2}\sum_mEV_m^2
 \le\frac{Ce^{-c_0k}}{N_0}.                              \tag{20}
\]
Linear interpolation cannot exceed the maximum of a piecewise-linear difference at its knots. Finally, exponential decay gives \(e^{-c_0k}\le Ck^{-2}\). This proves the donor-average assertion and completes the proof. \(\square\)
