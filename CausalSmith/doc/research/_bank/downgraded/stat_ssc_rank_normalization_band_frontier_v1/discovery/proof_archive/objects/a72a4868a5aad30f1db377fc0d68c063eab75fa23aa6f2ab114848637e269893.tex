\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(m=n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\),
\(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\), and
\(p_{\mathcal G}=2|\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}|\).  Abbreviate
\(\delta_{r,n}\), \(\chi_{r,n}\), and \(\tau_{r,n}\) by
\(\delta_n\), \(\chi_n\), and \(\tau_n\).  The quantities
\(a_{r,n}\), \(k_{r,n}\), \(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}\), \(U_{r,n}\), and
\(\zeta_{r,n}\) are the explicit deterministic sequences in the corrected
band construction.

\emph{Covariance and modulus derivation.}
Write \(m=n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\), \(h=h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\), and let \(Z_r=\sum_iL_i\) be the patient decomposition furnished by the pooled lemma when \(r=1\) and by the stabilized transfer lemma when \(r\ge2\). The deterministic patient envelopes in those constructions give
\[
 \|L_i\|_\infty\le
 \begin{cases}
 Cn_D^{-1},&r=1,\\
 CK_T^{-1},&i\text{ is target-pre},\\
 Cn_D^{-1},&i\text{ is a donor patient}.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{A.1}
\]
The same bounds hold for the observed multiplier coefficients on every regular branch. The pooled fallback has the donor bound except on the aggregate exhaustion event, whose probability is exponentially small by the risk-set argument in the pooled and stabilized lemmas.

We first compare covariance kernels. In rank one, the explicit formulas in the pooled product-limit calculation are
\[
 \Sigma_1(s,t)=\frac{s_1(s)s_1(t)}{n_D}
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{\lambda_1(u)}{H_D(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_1(s,t)=\widehat S_D(s-)\widehat S_D(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)^2}\,dN(u).          \tag{A.2}
\]
On \(\{\inf_{u\le\bar\tau}Y(u)\ge n_Dh_0/2\}\), substitute \(dN=dM+Y\lambda_1du\) in (A.2). Predictable isometry, the maximal inequality, and the time-ordered empirical-risk bound give
\[
 E\sup_v\left|
 \int_0^v\frac{n_D}{Y(u)^2}\,dN(u)
 -\int_0^v\frac{\lambda_1(u)}{H_D(u)}\,du
 \right|\le Cn_D^{-1/2}.                                  \tag{A.3}
\]
The complement has probability at most \(Ce^{-cn_D}\), and the pooled survival error has squared supremum expectation at most \(Cn_D^{-1}\). Hence
\[
 E\{\rho_1^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\|_\infty\}
 \le Cn_D^{-1/2}=Ca_{1,n}.                                \tag{A.4}
\]

Now let \(r\ge2\). On the aggregate regular event, write the coordinate-free prediction map as \(F\) and its population derivative as \(DF\). Let \(\Pi\) select the positive rank-\(d\) spectral subspace and let \(A=(\Pi\Gamma\Pi)^{1/2}\) on its selected range. For a contour \(\mathscr C\) lying in the fixed spectral gap, the resolvent identity gives the explicit selected-projector perturbation
\[
 \widehat\Pi-\Pi
 =\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathscr C}
 (z-\widehat\Gamma)^{-1}(\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma)
 (z-\Gamma)^{-1}\,dz.
\]
The positive-square-root derivative is the unique selected-range solution of
\[
 A\,DA_\Gamma[E]+DA_\Gamma[E]A
 =D(\Pi\Gamma\Pi)_\Gamma[E].
\]
The contour distance and the selected eigenvalue lower bound make both displayed derivative maps uniformly Lipschitz. With \(H=A_0^*A_0\), the inverse pre-Gram perturbation is controlled by
\[
 \widehat H^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\widehat H^{-1}(H-\widehat H)H^{-1},
\]
and the two pre-Gram inverses are uniformly bounded by the fixed positive Gram thresholds. These three identities, differentiated once where necessary, imply
\[
 \|D\widehat F-DF\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C\{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)\}              \tag{A.5}
\]
in mean, after truncation at the fixed spectral and pre-Gram thresholds. For a stabilized cell of size \(k\), write \(H_k=S^0G\), and define the naturally normalized oracle and empirical cell covariance kernels by
\[
 V_k(s,t)=S^0(s)S^0(t)\int_0^{s\wedge t}
 \frac{\lambda^0(u)}{H_k(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat V_k(s,t)=k\widehat S_k^\dagger(s-)
 \widehat S_k^\dagger(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\{Y_k^\dagger(u)\}^{-2}\,dN_k(u).
\]
Substitution of \(dN_k=dM_k+Y_k\lambda^0du\), clipping at \(kh_0/8\), the Lipschitz identity for the inverse risk, and predictable isometry give
\[
 E\sup_{s,t}\left|\widehat V_k(s,t)-V_k(s,t)\right|
 \le Ck^{-1/2},                                           \tag{A.6}
\]
The difference is the martingale term with integrand \(k/Y_k^{\dagger 2}\), the inverse-risk replacement term, and the two survival-factor replacements; clipping bounds every integrand on every dataset, while the stabilized cell lemma bounds the last two replacements. The deterministic stabilized bias contributes \(C\eta_D(K_D)\), while target and aggregate fallback events contribute \(Ce^{-c_0K_T/2}+Ce^{-c_0n_D/2}\). Composing (A.5)--(A.6), using (A.1), and summing the independent target and donor covariance blocks yields
\[
 E\{\rho_r^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty\}
 \le Ca_{r,n}.                                            \tag{A.7}
\]
Markov's inequality applied to (A.4) or (A.7), together with the exponentially small aggregate fallback event, gives a measurable event \(\mathcal E_{r,n}^{\mathrm{cov}}\) such that
\[
 \sup_PP\{(\mathcal E_{r,n}^{\mathrm{cov}})^c\}\le Ca_{r,n}^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \le C\rho_r^2a_{r,n}^{1/2}                              \tag{A.8}
\]
on that event.

It remains to control time increments of the realized derivative. For an interval \(J=(g,g']\) with grid endpoints, let \(N_T(J)\) and \(N_D(J)\) count observed failures in the target-pre and pooled donor blocks, respectively; omit \(N_T\) at rank one. Hazard smoothness implies
\[
 EN_T(J)\le LK_T|J|,
 \qquad EN_D(J)\le Ln_D|J|.                               \tag{A.9}
\]
Bernstein's exponential-moment argument for sums of independent Bernoulli variables, followed by a union bound over at most \(|\mathcal G|^2\) intervals, gives an event \(\mathcal E_{r,n}^{\mathrm{inc}}\) on which
\[
 N_T(J)\le C K_T(|J|+h),
 \qquad N_D(J)\le C n_D(|J|+h)                            \tag{A.10}
\]
for every grid interval, and
\[
 \sup_PP\{(\mathcal E_{r,n}^{\mathrm{inc}})^c\}
 \le C|\mathcal G|^2e^{-cmh}.                             \tag{A.11}
\]
Here \(mh\to\infty\) polynomially because \(h\asymp\rho_r^{1/q_\beta}\), \(q_\beta>3/2\), and \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\). Thus (A.11) is bounded by \(Ca_{r,n}^{1/2}\) on every sufficiently large tail.

The derivative increment between \(g\) and \(g'\) is the sum of a Lipschitz coefficient change and the patient failures in \(J\). Equations (A.1) and (A.10), Gaussian isometry, and \(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\le\rho_r^2\) give, for distinct grid points,
\[
 \widehat d_r(g,g')^2
 \le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|^2
 +C\{K_T^{-2}N_T(J)+n_D^{-2}N_D(J)\}
 \le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|.                                    \tag{A.12}
\]
The last step uses \(|g-g'|\ge h\). Taking expectations in the same calculation gives the oracle bound \(d_r(g,g')^2\le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\). Intersecting the two events in (A.8) and (A.11) proves the covariance and semimetric assertions.

Finally, for fixed \(g,g'\), the independent centered increment \(Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')\) has variance at most \(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\) and summand envelope at most \(C\rho_r m^{-1/2}\) by (A.1) and \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\). The exponential Markov proof of Bernstein's inequality therefore gives
\[
 P\{|Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')|>x\}
 \le2\exp\left[-c\min\left\{
 \frac{x^2}{\rho_r^2|g-g'|},
 \frac{x\sqrt m}{\rho_r}\right\}\right].               \tag{A.13}
\]
Set
\[
 x=C\rho_r\left[\{u\log(e p_{\mathcal G})\}^{1/2}
 +m^{-1/2}\log(e p_{\mathcal G})\right].
\]
For \(|g-g'|\le u\), (A.13) is at most \(2p_{\mathcal G}^{-6}\) after enlarging \(C\). A union bound over at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^2\) signed pairs proves the displayed modulus probability. No variance was inverted at any step.

\emph{Coarse-net and conditional Gaussian comparison.}
We first couple the oracle process to its Gaussian counterpart. Let \(k=k_{r,n}\), and choose a deterministic subset \(\mathcal T_n\subset\mathcal G\) of at most \(k+1\) knots whose physical-time mesh is at most \(C/k\). Evaluate both signs of every normalized patient vector on \(\mathcal T_n\); the ambient dimension is at most \(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}\). From (A.1), separately summing the target and donor blocks,
\[
 \sum_iE\|L_i/\rho_r\|_{2,\mathcal T_n}^3
 \le Cp_{\mathrm c,r,n}^{3/2}m^{-1/2}.                    \tag{B.2}
\]
We invoke Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato (2014), Theorem~4.2, directly.  In the finite-dimensional form used here, if \(X_1,\ldots,X_N\) are independent centered vectors in \(\mathbb R^p\), \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_N\) are independent centered Gaussian vectors with \(E(Y_iY_i^\top)=E(X_iX_i^\top)\), and \(a>0\), then, with
\[
 B_0=pa^{-3}\sum_{i=1}^N E\|X_i\|_2^3,
\]
there is a coupling satisfying
\[
 P\left\{\left\|\sum_iX_i-\sum_iY_i\right\|_2>3a\right\}
 \le C B_0\left\{1+\frac{|\log(1/B_0)|}{p}\right\}.
\]
No covariance nonsingularity condition is imposed.  Apply this theorem to the signed coarse-net patient vectors with \(a=U_{r,n}^{1/4}\).  By (B.2), its dimension-adjusted quantity satisfies
\[
 B_0\le C p_{\mathrm c,r,n}^{5/2}m^{-1/2}a^{-3}
 \le U_{r,n}^{1/4}.                                      \tag{B.3}
\]
Thus a centered Gaussian vector \(G_r\) with the covariance of \(Z_r\) can be coupled on \(\mathcal T_n\) so that the Euclidean error is at most \(C\zeta_{r,n}\) except on an event of probability at most \(C\zeta_{r,n}\). This step permits a singular covariance matrix.

The interval-count, semimetric, and Bernstein bounds (A.9)--(A.13) transfer this coupling to the canonical grid. Indeed, with \(u=C/k\), its Bernstein bound gives
\[
 \max_{\substack{g,g'\in\mathcal G\\|g-g'|\le u}}
 |Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')|\le C\rho_r\zeta_{r,n}                  \tag{B.4}
\]
outside an event of probability at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^{-4}\). For the Gaussian process, the same semimetric bound makes each such increment Gaussian with variance at most \(C\rho_r^2u\); a union bound over at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^2\) pairs gives the analogue of (B.4) with failure at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^{-4}\). The relations \(k\asymp m^{1/9}\), \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\), and \(|\mathcal G|\le C\rho_r^{-1/q_\beta}\) show that both modulus allowances and failures are dominated by \(C\zeta_{r,n}\). Consequently, for every real \(x\),
\[
 P\{\|G_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\}
 \le P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le x+C\rho_r\zeta_{r,n}\}+C\zeta_{r,n}.              \tag{B.5}
\]

We next compare \(G_r\) with the conditional Gaussian process \(\widehat G_r\) having covariance \(\widehat\Sigma_r\). Put \(a_n=a_{r,n}\) and \(v_n=a_n^{1/2}\). On the event \(\mathcal E_{r,n}=\mathcal E_{r,n}^{\mathrm{cov}}\cap\mathcal E_{r,n}^{\mathrm{inc}}\) constructed in (A.8)--(A.11),
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \le C\rho_r^2v_n,\qquad
 P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\le Cv_n.                           \tag{B.6}
\]
Choose an auxiliary subset of the canonical grid with mesh at most \(u_n=v_n^{1/3}\) and cardinality \(K_n\le Cv_n^{-1/3}\). On that subset the two covariance matrices differ in trace norm by at most \(CK_n^2\rho_r^2v_n\). Powers and St{\o}rmer (1970), Lemma~4.1, states in finite dimension that, for positive semidefinite matrices \(A\) and \(B\),
\[
 \|A^{1/2}-B^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le\|A-B\|_*.
\]
Applying this inequality and using a common standard-normal vector therefore gives a coupling satisfying
\[
 E\|G_r-\widehat G_r\|_{2,\mathrm{aux}}
 \le C\rho_rK_nv_n^{1/2}\le C\rho_r a_n^{1/12}.          \tag{B.7}
\]
The semimetric bound and a Gaussian union bound transfer (B.7) to the canonical grid with expected modulus at most
\(C\rho_r a_n^{1/12}\{1+|\log a_n|\}^{1/2}\). Markov's inequality at the larger threshold
\[
 \rho_r a_n^{1/48}\{1+|\log a_n|\}                      \tag{B.8}
\]
shows, conditionally on the data in \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), that the difference of the two Gaussian suprema exceeds (B.8) with probability at most the normalized quantity in (B.8), for every sufficiently large tail. The complement probability \(Cv_n\) is also dominated by (B.8).

Combining (B.5)--(B.8) and the definition of \(\chi_n\) yields, on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), the one-sided comparison
\[
 \widehat F_r(x)
 \le F_{r,P}(x+\rho_r\chi_n)+\chi_n
 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R,                    \tag{B.9}
\]
where
\[
 F_{r,P}(x)=P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\},
 \quad
 \widehat F_r(x)=P_\xi\{\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\mid O\}.
\]
No anti-concentration or coordinate-variance division is used.

The rates are explicit. Since \(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}=O(m^{1/9})\),
\[
 U_{r,n}=O(m^{-2/9}),\qquad
 \zeta_{r,n}=O\{m^{-1/18}(1+\log m)\}.                   \tag{B.10}
\]
Also \(a_{r,n}\to0\) uniformly on every three-dimensional tail. Hence \(\chi_n\to0\) and \(\tau_n/\rho_r=\chi_n\to0\). Because \(\delta_n\) decreases only at the displayed iterated-logarithm rate, while both terms in \(\chi_n\) decrease at a positive power of the tail index up to logarithms, eventually
\[
 2\chi_n\le\delta_n/2.                                    \tag{B.11}
\]

Let \(q_n=1-\alpha+\delta_n\), and let
\(c_{r,P}(u)=\inf\{x\ge0:F_{r,P}(x)\ge u\}\). The conditional Gaussian maximum is a Borel function of a finite standard-normal vector and its observed covariance matrix. Taking the infimum over nonnegative rationals proves measurability of \(\widetilde c_r^\xi\), including singular covariance and the identically zero process. Its generalized-inverse property and (B.9) give on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\)
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi+\rho_r\chi_n
 \ge c_{r,P}(q_n-\chi_n).                                 \tag{B.12}
\]
The right side is deterministic for fixed \(P\). Therefore, without any independence between \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) and \(Z_r\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le
 \widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_n\}
 &\ge q_n-\chi_n-P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\\
 &\ge1-\alpha+\delta_n/2,
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{B.13}
\]
after absorbing the event probability into \(\chi_n\) and using (B.11).

Finally, \(q_n\le1-\alpha/2\). Conditional Markov inequality gives
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi\le
 \left\{\frac2\alpha E_\xi
 \|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{B.14}
\]
The pooled and stabilized moment bounds, followed by Jensen's inequality, yield
\(\sup_PE_P\widetilde c_r^\xi\le C\rho_r\). Equations (B.10)--(B.14) prove every assertion, including zero-variance time zero. \(\square\)
