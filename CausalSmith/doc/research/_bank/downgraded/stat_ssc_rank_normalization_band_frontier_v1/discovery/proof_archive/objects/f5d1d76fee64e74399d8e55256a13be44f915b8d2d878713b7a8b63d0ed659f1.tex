\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(k_1=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\), and \(k_2=K_D-k_1\).  Constants below depend only on the fixed model constants and \(r\).  Apply \(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}product\mbox{-}limit\mbox{-}cell}\) to the target-pre cell, every donor full cell, and both deterministic half-folds.  For an input containing \(k\) patients it gives
\[
 \widehat S_k^\dagger-\widetilde S^0
 =k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_{k,j}+b_k^\dagger+q_k^\dagger,
 \quad
 \|b_k^\dagger\|_\infty\le Ce^{-c_0k},
 \quad
 E\|q_k^\dagger\|_\infty^2\le Ck^{-2}.                 \tag{1}
\]
The patient processes are independent and mean zero, and one-record replacement changes the stabilized interpolated input by at most \(C/k\) on every dataset.

Let
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathcal G}=N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \widetilde Y_m\otimes\widetilde Y_m=\widetilde\Gamma. \tag{2}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:interpolated\mbox{-}population\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}stability}\), for all sufficiently large triples,
\[
 \lambda_d(\Gamma_{\mathcal G})\ge\gamma_-^2/2,
 \quad \lambda_{d+1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal G})=0,
 \quad \lambda_{\min}(H_{0,\mathcal G})\ge\kappa_-/2. \tag{3}
\]
Work with the selected spectral projector and the coordinate-free positive square-root operator, rather than with a particular eigenvector frame.  The contour-resolvent identity for the projector, the Sylvester equation for the square-root derivative, and
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}                       \tag{4}
\]
show that the rotation-invariant prediction map and its first two derivatives are uniformly bounded on a fixed neighborhood of \(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\).  This argument uses only the selected-cluster gap from zero and is valid under ties inside the positive cluster.  Let \(C_H\) be a uniform Lipschitz constant for the induced pre-period Gram on that neighborhood, and choose
\[
 \epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}\le
 \min\{\gamma_-^2/8,\kappa_-/(4C_H)\},
 \qquad \epsilon_0=\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}/2.           \tag{5}
\]

Cross-fitting the two donor half-folds removes their same-fold product-limit variance.  Equation (1) gives
\[
 \|E\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le Ce^{-c_0k_1}.                                      \tag{6}
\]
Changing one donor record changes one half-fold curve by \(C/K_D\).  Donor centering, the factor \(N_0^{-1}\), and
\(x\otimes y-x'\otimes y'=(x-x')\otimes y+x'\otimes(y-y')\)
therefore imply the deterministic sensitivity
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma(O)-\widehat\Gamma(O')\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le C/(N_0K_D)=C/n_D.                                  \tag{7}
\]
If \(O^{(i)}\) replaces record \(i\) by an independent copy, symmetrization and (7) give the Hilbert-space variance inequality
\[
 E\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le\frac12\sum_iE\|\widehat\Gamma(O)-\widehat\Gamma(O^{(i)})\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le Cn_D^{-1}.                                         \tag{7a}
\]
For completeness, reveal the donor records successively and let \(D_i\) be the Doob differences of the measurable scalar map
\(O\mapsto\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\).
Conditional on the preceding records, (7) makes the range of \(D_i\) at most \(C/n_D\).  Convexity of the exponential gives the conditional Hoeffding bound
\[
 E\{\exp(sD_i)\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}\}
 \le\exp\{s^2C^2/(8n_D^2)\}.                            \tag{7b}
\]
Iterating (7b) over \(O(n_D)\) records and optimizing exponential Markov inequality yields
\[
 P\left\{\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 -E\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}>x\right\}
 \le e^{-cn_Dx^2}.                                      \tag{8}
\]
Thus, for the measurable proof event
\[
 \mathcal M_{r,n}=
 \{\|\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\epsilon_0\}, \tag{9}
\]
with the deterministic requirements \(k_1,k_2\ge1\), equations (6)--(8) imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P(\mathcal M_{r,n}^c)\le Ce^{-cn_D}. \tag{10}
\]
No infimum over a replacement class occurs in (9), so measurability is immediate.

By (3)--(5), on \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) the straight segment from \(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\) to \(\widehat\Gamma\) remains in one fixed-gap regular neighborhood.  By (7), once \(n_D\) is large enough that \(C/n_D\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}/2\), every one-donor-record replacement and the entire observed-to-replacement segment also remain in that neighborhood.  The selected eigenvalue, selected-to-complement gap, and pre-period Gram remain above the construction's thresholds.  A target-pre replacement leaves \(\widehat\Gamma\), its selected square root, and \(\widehat H_0\) unchanged and hence cannot switch the branch.  Consequently the population-to-observed Taylor formula and all finite-replacement paths of the total estimator are valid on \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\), with envelopes
\[
 \|D_j^TF\|_\infty\le C/K_T,
 \qquad \|D_{p,m,j}^DF\|_\infty\le C/n_D.              \tag{11}
\]
There is no such total-map assertion across the hard cutoff on \(\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\).

Both returned branches are nevertheless uniformly bounded.  On the regular branch this follows from the hard selected-eigenvalue and inverse-Gram thresholds and the bounded input curves; the pooled branch lies in \([0,1]\).  Hence every possible switch contribution can be included in a remainder \(Q_r^{\mathrm{sw}}\) satisfying
\[
 \|Q_r^{\mathrm{sw}}\|_\infty
 \le C\mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\},
 \qquad E\|Q_r^{\mathrm{sw}}\|_\infty^2\le Ce^{-cn_D}. \tag{12}
\]

Taylor's formula on \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\), (1), and (12) now give at every grid knot
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =K_T^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}\phi_{T,j}
 +n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j}
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r.                                    \tag{13}
\]
The population center is \(\theta\) at the knots by the canonical-coordinate and interpolated-population stability lemmas.  The fixed derivative bound sends the deterministic stabilization bias into
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le\eta_D(K_D). \tag{14}
\]
The target centered remainder, donor-averaged centered remainder, quadratic Taylor term, bias--noise interaction, and risk-exhaustion and switching terms have \(L^2\)-supremum bounds, respectively,
\[
 K_T^{-1},\quad N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1},\quad
 K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1},\quad \rho_r\eta_D(K_D),\quad
 e^{-c_\dagger K_T/2}+e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}.             \tag{15}
\]
These are exactly the components dominated by \(\upsilon_r\) in the definition of \(\omega_r\).  Minkowski's inequality therefore proves
\[
 E\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C\upsilon_r^2.      \tag{16}
\]

It remains to control the multiplier without differentiating the switch.  On every dataset assigned to the regular branch, its hard thresholds and the stabilized cell bound give the explicitly defined local regular coefficients the envelopes \(C/K_T\) and \(C/n_D\).  On the pooled branch each coefficient is pointwise at most one; on the aggregate-risk event it is \(C/n_D\), and the complement has probability \(Ce^{-cn_D}\).  The ordered product-limit maximal inequality gives
\[
 EE_\xi\|\widehat Z_{r,\mathrm{pool}}^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le Cn_D^{-1}.                                        \tag{17}
\]
For the regular coefficients, Gaussian isometry, predictable isometry, and the time-ordered bounded-variation maximal inequality give
\[
 E\|Z_r\|_\infty^2+EE_\xi
 \|\widehat Z_{r,\mathrm{reg}}^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}).                              \tag{18}
\]
Selecting the construction's branch and combining (17)--(18) proves
\[
 \sup_PE_P\|Z_r\|_\infty^2+
 \sup_PE_PE_\xi\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\}=C\rho_r^2.                \tag{19}
\]
All maximal inequalities were applied in physical time before restriction to the grid, so no \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G|}\) factor appears. \(\square\)
