\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a member law and suppress its sample-size indices.  The compatible factor
representation, including the target unit, is
\[
 S^0_{p,m}-s_p=\bar b_p^\top\bar z_m,
 \qquad m\in\mathcal M.                                    \tag{1}
\]
Donor centering follows by averaging (1) over \(m\in\mathcal D\), because
\(s_p\) is the donor mean.  Let \(\bar Z\) be the \(N_0\times d\) matrix
whose \(m\)-th row is \(\bar z_m^\top\), and define the factor map
\[
 \bar Bx=(\bar b_0^\top x,\bar b_1^\top x)
 \in L^2(\{0,1\}\times\mathcal I).                         \tag{2}
\]
The positive pre-period Gram assumption implies
\[
 x^\top\!\left\{\int_{\mathcal I}\bar b_0(t)\bar b_0(t)^\top dt\right\}x
 =\|\bar b_0^\top x\|_{L^2}^2>0
 \quad(x\ne0),                                             \tag{3}
\]
after removal of any redundant compatible coordinate.  Thus \(\bar B\) is
injective.  For \(a\in\mathbf 1^\perp\), (1) gives
\[
 \mathcal C_Da=\bar B\{N_0^{-1/2}\bar Z^\top a\}.          \tag{4}
\]
The exact centered-rank assumption says that the left side of (4) has rank
\(d\).  Injectivity of \(\bar B\) therefore implies
\(\operatorname{rank}(\bar Z)=d\).  Consequently
\[
 \Sigma=N_0^{-1}\bar Z^\top\bar Z                         \tag{5}
\]
is positive definite.  Put
\[
 b_p^\circ=\Sigma^{1/2}\bar b_p,
 \qquad
 z_m^\circ=\Sigma^{-1/2}\bar z_m.                          \tag{6}
\]
Then (1) is unchanged and
\[
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}z_m^\circ z_m^{\circ\top}=I_d.
                                                                    \tag{7}
\]

Let \(B^\circ x=((b_0^\circ)^\top x,(b_1^\circ)^\top x)\).
Equations (4), (6), and (7) imply that the covariance operator of the
centered donor curves is
\[
 \Gamma=N_0^{-1}\sum_mY_m\otimes Y_m=B^\circ B^{\circ *}. \tag{8}
\]
The donor-SVD factor map \(B\) in the corrected canonical construction is
another full-column-rank square root of the same rank-\(d\) operator:
\(BB^*=\Gamma=B^\circ B^{\circ *}\).  On the range of \(\Gamma\), define
\(R=B^{\dagger}B^\circ\), where \(B^{\dagger}\) is the inverse of \(B\)
on its range.  From (8),
\[
 RR^*=B^{\dagger}B^\circ B^{\circ *}B^{\dagger *}
      =B^{\dagger}BB^*B^{\dagger *}=I_d.                  \tag{9}
\]
Since \(R\) is square, it is orthogonal.  Hence, after choosing the
orientation fixed in the SVD rule,
\[
 B=B^\circ R^\top,
 \qquad
 b_p=R b_p^\circ,
 \qquad
 z_m=Rz_m^\circ.                                           \tag{10}
\]
The last equality follows by substituting the first two identities into
the donor representation and using the injectivity in (3).  This proves
the claimed common orthogonal relation; internal singular-value ties only
change \(R\).

Apply (1) and (6) to the target-pre curve.  With
\(z_\star^\circ=\Sigma^{-1/2}\bar z_\star\), equations (1) and (10)
give
\[
 S^0_{0,\star}-s_0=(b_0^\circ)^\top z_\star^\circ
                   =b_0^\top Rz_\star^\circ.              \tag{11}
\]
Thus compatibility, rather than the donor SVD alone, proves the required
span inclusion.  Moreover (3), (6), and (10) show that
\[
 H_0=\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)b_0(t)^\top dt                 \tag{12}
\]
is positive definite; quantitatively its smallest eigenvalue is at least
\(\kappa_-\).  Multiplying (11) by \(b_0\), integrating, and inverting
(12) yields
\[
 H_0^{-1}\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)
  \{S^0_{0,\star}(t)-s_0(t)\}\,dt=Rz_\star^\circ.          \tag{13}
\]
If two coefficients satisfy (11), their difference \(v\) obeys
\(v^\top H_0v=0\), so positive definiteness forces \(v=0\).  This proves
existence and uniqueness of the corrected target coordinate.  When
\(d=0\), (1) has an empty factor term and directly gives
\(S^0_{p,m}=s_p\) for every \(m\in\mathcal M\). \(\square\)
