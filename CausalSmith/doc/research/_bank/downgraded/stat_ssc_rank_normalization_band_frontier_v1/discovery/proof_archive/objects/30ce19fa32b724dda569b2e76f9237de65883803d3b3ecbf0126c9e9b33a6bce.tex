\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(m=n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\), \(p_{\mathcal G}=2|\mathcal G|\), and abbreviate \(\delta_{r,n}\), \(\chi_{r,n}\), and \(\tau_{r,n}\) by \(\delta_n\), \(\chi_n\), and \(\tau_n\). The patient envelopes in the pooled and stabilized lemmas imply, after division by \(\rho_r\),
\[
 \max_i\|L_i/\rho_r\|_\infty\le Cm^{-1/2}.               \tag{1}
\]

We first couple the oracle process to its Gaussian counterpart. Let \(k=k_{r,n}\), and choose a deterministic subset \(\mathcal T_n\subset\mathcal G\) of at most \(k+1\) knots whose physical-time mesh is at most \(C/k\). Evaluate both signs of every normalized patient vector on \(\mathcal T_n\); the ambient dimension is at most \(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}\). From (1), separately summing the target and donor blocks,
\[
 \sum_iE\|L_i/\rho_r\|_{2,\mathcal T_n}^3
 \le Cp_{\mathrm c,r,n}^{3/2}m^{-1/2}.                    \tag{2}
\]
Apply the corrected Yurinskii lemma with \(a=U_{r,n}^{1/4}\). Since that lemma's dimension factor is retained, its quantity satisfies
\[
 B_0\le C p_{\mathrm c,r,n}^{5/2}m^{-1/2}a^{-3}
 \le U_{r,n}^{1/4}.                                      \tag{3}
\]
Thus a centered Gaussian vector \(G_r\) with the covariance of \(Z_r\) can be coupled on \(\mathcal T_n\) so that the Euclidean error is at most \(C\zeta_{r,n}\) except on an event of probability at most \(C\zeta_{r,n}\). This step permits a singular covariance matrix.

The covariance-modulus lemma transfers this coupling to the canonical grid. Indeed, with \(u=C/k\), its Bernstein bound gives
\[
 \max_{\substack{g,g'\in\mathcal G\\|g-g'|\le u}}
 |Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')|\le C\rho_r\zeta_{r,n}                  \tag{4}
\]
outside an event of probability at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^{-4}\). For the Gaussian process, the same semimetric bound makes each such increment Gaussian with variance at most \(C\rho_r^2u\); a union bound over at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^2\) pairs gives the analogue of (4) with failure at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^{-4}\). The relations \(k\asymp m^{1/9}\), \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\), and \(|\mathcal G|\le C\rho_r^{-1/q_\beta}\) show that both modulus allowances and failures are dominated by \(C\zeta_{r,n}\). Consequently, for every real \(x\),
\[
 P\{\|G_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\}
 \le P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le x+C\rho_r\zeta_{r,n}\}+C\zeta_{r,n}.              \tag{5}
\]

We next compare \(G_r\) with the conditional Gaussian process \(\widehat G_r\) having covariance \(\widehat\Sigma_r\). Put \(a_n=a_{r,n}\) and \(v_n=a_n^{1/2}\). On the event from the covariance-modulus lemma,
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \le C\rho_r^2v_n,qquad
 P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\le Cv_n.                           \tag{6}
\]
Choose an auxiliary subset of the canonical grid with mesh at most \(u_n=v_n^{1/3}\) and cardinality \(K_n\le Cv_n^{-1/3}\). On that subset the two covariance matrices differ in trace norm by at most \(CK_n^2\rho_r^2v_n\). The cited Powers--St\o rmer inequality and a common standard-normal vector therefore give a coupling satisfying
\[
 E\|G_r-\widehat G_r\|_{2,\mathrm{aux}}
 \le C\rho_rK_nv_n^{1/2}le C\rho_r a_n^{1/12}.          \tag{7}
\]
The semimetric bound and a Gaussian union bound transfer (7) to the canonical grid with expected modulus at most
\(C\rho_r a_n^{1/12}\{1+|\log a_n|\}^{1/2}\). Markov's inequality at the larger threshold
\[
 \rho_r a_n^{1/48}\{1+|\log a_n|\}                      \tag{8}
\]
shows, conditionally on the data in \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), that the difference of the two Gaussian suprema exceeds (8) with probability at most the normalized quantity in (8), for every sufficiently large tail. The complement probability \(Cv_n\) is also dominated by (8).

Combining (5)--(8) and the definition of \(\chi_n\) yields, on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), the one-sided comparison
\[
 \widehat F_r(x)
 \le F_{r,P}(x+\rho_r\chi_n)+\chi_n
 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R,                    \tag{9}
\]
where
\[
 F_{r,P}(x)=P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\},
 \quad
 \widehat F_r(x)=P_\xi\{\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\mid O\}.
\]
No anti-concentration or coordinate-variance division is used.

The rates are explicit. Since \(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}=O(m^{1/9})\),
\[
 U_{r,n}=O(m^{-2/9}),qquad
 \zeta_{r,n}=O\{m^{-1/18}(1+\log m)\}.                   \tag{10}
\]
Also \(a_{r,n}\to0\) uniformly on every three-dimensional tail. Hence \(\chi_n\to0\) and \(\tau_n/\rho_r=\chi_n\to0\). Because \(\delta_n\) decreases only at the displayed iterated-logarithm rate, while both terms in \(\chi_n\) decrease at a positive power of the tail index up to logarithms, eventually
\[
 2\chi_n\le\delta_n/2.                                    \tag{11}
\]

Let \(q_n=1-\alpha+\delta_n\), and let
\(c_{r,P}(u)=\inf\{x\ge0:F_{r,P}(x)\ge u\}\). The conditional Gaussian maximum is a Borel function of a finite standard-normal vector and its observed covariance matrix. Taking the infimum over nonnegative rationals proves measurability of \(\widetilde c_r^\xi\), including singular covariance and the identically zero process. Its generalized-inverse property and (9) give on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\)
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi+\rho_r\chi_n
 \ge c_{r,P}(q_n-\chi_n).                                 \tag{12}
\]
The right side is deterministic for fixed \(P\). Therefore, without any independence between \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) and \(Z_r\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le
 \widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_n\}
 &\ge q_n-\chi_n-P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\\
 &\ge1-\alpha+\delta_n/2,
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{13}
\]
after absorbing the event probability into \(\chi_n\) and using (11).

Finally, \(q_n\le1-\alpha/2\). Conditional Markov inequality gives
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi\le
 \left\{\frac2\alpha E_\xi
 \|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{14}
\]
The pooled and stabilized moment bounds, followed by Jensen's inequality, yield
\(\sup_PE_P\widetilde c_r^\xi\le C\rho_r\). Equations (10)--(14) prove every assertion, including zero-variance time zero. \(\square\)
