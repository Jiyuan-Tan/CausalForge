\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(k_D=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\),
\(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\), and
\(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\).  Define
\[
 a_T(K_T)=K_T^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T),
 \qquad
 a_D(n_D)=n_D^{-1/6}\log^2(e+n_D),
\]
\[
 \chi_r=\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)
       +n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)+\rho_r\eta_D(K_D),
\]
and
\[
 v_r=\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}
 \{K_T^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0K_T}\}
 +n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0n_D}.
\]
By the definition of the finite-tail buffer,
\[
 \omega_r=C_\omega\{\varepsilon^{-1}\chi_r
                   +\varepsilon^{-1/2}v_r\}.                 \tag{1}
\]

Let \(\widetilde S^0=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0\), and let
\(\theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}\) be the interpolated population
comparison in \(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\).  By
\(\mathrm{lem:interpolated\mbox{-}population\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}stability}\),
\[
 \|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C h^{q_\beta},
 \qquad
 \lambda_d(\widetilde\Gamma)\ge\gamma_-^2/2,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(\widetilde H_0)\ge\kappa_-/2,              \tag{2}
\]
for all sufficiently large triples when \(d>0\), and
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g)
 \quad\text{for every }g\in\mathcal G.                     \tag{3}
\]
The zero-dimensional versions of (2)--(3) apply when \(d=0\).  The mesh
condition gives \(h^{q_\beta}\le\rho_r\\to0\).  Hence the population point
at which the functional-PCR map is expanded lies in a fixed regular
neighborhood, uniformly over the model class.  In particular, (2), rather
than gridwise equality alone, controls interpolation inside the \(L^2\)
factor map.

Apply
\(\mathrm{lem:kaplan\mbox{-}meier\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}exhaustion}\) to every
full donor cell, both deterministic donor folds, and the target-pre cell.
For a cell containing \(k\) patients, that lemma gives the exact identity
\[
 \widehat S^{\mathrm{KM}}-\widetilde S^0
 =k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_j+b_k+q_k
 \quad\text{in }\ell^\infty(\mathcal I),                   \tag{4}
\]
where the influence processes are independent and mean zero,
\[
 \|b_k\|_\infty\le C_0e^{-c_0k},
 \qquad
 E\|q_k\|_\infty^2\le C_0k^{-2}.                           \tag{5}
\]
The centering in (4) is exact because all four terms on its left and right
are acted on by the same deterministic interpolation operator.  Thus no
off-grid interpolation term is inserted into the internal covariance
operator.

For the full-cell donor means, centering at \(\widetilde s_p\), (4)--(5)
and the cross-donor remainder bound in the same lemma give
\[
 \widehat s_p-\widetilde s_p
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}\varphi^s_{p,m,j}+b^s_p+q^s_p,
 \qquad
 \|b^s_p\|_\infty\le C e^{-c_0K_D},
 \qquad
 E\|q^s_p\|_\infty^2\le C N_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}.                \tag{6}
\]
The target-pre curve has the analogous expansion with deterministic bias
at most \(Ce^{-c_0K_T}\) and centered remainder with squared supremum norm
at most \(CK_T^{-2}\).

For each donor fold write
\[
 \widehat Y_m^{(a)}=\widetilde Y_m+U_m^{(a)}+b_m^{(a)}+q_m^{(a)},
 \qquad a\in\{1,2\}.                                        \tag{7}
\]
The two processes \(U_m^{(1)}\) and \(U_m^{(2)}\) are independent mean-zero
martingale averages because the split uses disjoint patients.  Censoring
overlap, event overlap, the hazard bound, and (4)--(5) imply
\[
 \sup_m E\|U_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-1},
 \qquad
 \sup_m\|b_m^{(a)}\|_2\le Ce^{-c_0k_D},
 \qquad
 \sup_m E\|q_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-2}.                   \tag{8}
\]
Expanding the symmetrized cross-fold operator about
\(\widetilde\Gamma\) gives
\[
 \widehat\Gamma-\widetilde\Gamma=\mathbb L_D+E_D,          \tag{9}
\]
where \(\mathbb L_D\) is the average of the terms
\(\widetilde Y_m\otimes U_m^{(a)}+U_m^{(a)}\otimes
\widetilde Y_m\).  The stability lemma bounds
\(\sup_m\|\widetilde Y_m\|_2\), and patient independence and the martingale
isometry yield
\[
 \|\mathbb L_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=O_P(n_D^{-1/2})              \tag{10}
\]
uniformly over the class.  Cross-fold independence gives
\(E(U_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)})=0\), while independence across donors gives
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mU_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)}
   \right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le C N_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}.                                   \tag{11}
\]
The same calculation for terms containing \(q_m^{(a)}\), together with
(5), gives
\[
 \|E_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le C\eta_D(K_D)+O_P(N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}).                 \tag{12}
\]
This is the point at which cross-folding removes the same-fold
errors-in-variables expectation but does not remove the deterministic
product-limit risk-exhaustion bias.

Represent the fitted curve by the selected spectral projector, its positive
square root, and the inverse pre-period Gram matrix.  The spectral contour
identity used in the stability lemma and the algebraic identity
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}                             \tag{13}
\]
show from (2) that this rotation-invariant composite map is twice
Fr\'echet differentiable throughout a fixed neighborhood of the
interpolated population panel.  Its first two derivative norms are bounded
by a constant depending only on the fixed class constants and \(r\).
Internal selected-eigenspace ties do not alter the composite prediction.
Equations (6)--(12), \(N_0,K_T,K_D\to\infty\), and
\(\eta_D(K_D)\\to0\) also imply that the empirical regular event has
probability tending to one uniformly; on its complement the definition is
the bounded pooled fallback.

Taylor expansion at \(\widetilde S^0\), followed by (3), gives in
\(\ell^\infty(\mathcal G)\)
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =D_T\mathcal F_{\mathcal G,\widetilde S}
   \left[K_T^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_{0,\star,j}\right]
 +D_D\mathcal F_{\mathcal G,\widetilde S}
   \left[n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\varphi_{p,m,j}\right]
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r.                                        \tag{14}
\]
Define \(\phi_{T,j}\) and \(\phi_{D,p,m,j}\) by the two displayed
derivatives, including the deterministic fold weights.  Patient
independence makes the target and donor arrays mutually independent and
mean zero.  When \(r=1\),
\(\mathrm{lem:normalization\mbox{-}collapse}\) and the explicit pooled
branch give \(D_T\mathcal F_{\mathcal G,\widetilde S}=0\), and hence
\(\phi_{T,j}\equiv0\).  Choose the fixed \(C_\eta\) in the definition of
\(\eta_D\) larger than the product of the half-fold bias constant in (5)
and the uniform derivative envelope.  Equations (5)--(12) then give
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D).                        \tag{15}
\]
The integral Taylor remainder, the centered second-order terms in
(6)--(12), and the bounded fallback yield
\[
 \sup_P E_P\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\left[
 \mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\{K_T^{-2}+N_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}
                         +e^{-2c_0K_T}\}
 +n_D^{-2}+e^{-2c_0n_D}
 \right]
 \le C v_r^2.                                                \tag{16}
\]
Since (1) implies \(\omega_r\ge C_\omega v_r/\sqrt\varepsilon\),
Markov's inequality and a sufficiently large fixed \(C_\omega\) give
\[
 \sup_P P\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le\varepsilon/4.                                          \tag{17}
\]

It remains to compare the oracle first-order process with the conditional
patient multiplier.  Censor overlap bounds all counting-process
integrands, and the nested time-indexed martingale class has, after
normalizing each channel by its standard-error scale, an \(L^2\)-net with
cardinality at most \(C\delta^{-2}\).  Construct dyadic nets with mesh
\(2^{-\ell}\), truncate each normalized patient contribution at level
\(k^{1/6}/\log(e+k)\), and replace the truncated patients one at a time by
their Gaussian-multiplier counterparts.  Taylor's formula for a
one-Lipschitz ramp, summed over the nets, bounds the target replacement
error by
\[
 C K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T),
\]
and the donor replacement error by
\[
 C n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D).
\]
The discarded tails are bounded by the same quantities: square-integrable
martingale increments and the stated truncation level give a factor
\(k^{-1/6}\), while summing the dyadic net maxima contributes at most the
two logarithmic factors in \(a_T\) and \(a_D\).  This derivation uses only
the bounded predictable integrands supplied by censor overlap and the
independence of patient records.

The empirical derivative differs from the oracle derivative at
\(\widetilde S^0\) by at most \(C\eta_D(K_D)\) after its centered stochastic
part has been included in (16).  Since the first-order process has scale
\(\rho_r\), independence and the triangle inequality therefore give the
one-sided Lipschitz comparison error
\[
 C\chi_r=C\left\{\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)
 +n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)+\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)\right\}.              \tag{18}
\]
Apply the ramp which is one below the conditional multiplier critical
value and zero one buffer unit above it.  By the definition of the
conditional quantile and (18),
\[
 \sup_P P\!\left\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>
 c_r^\xi+\frac12\omega_r\right\}
 \le1-q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}+\frac{2C\chi_r}{\omega_r}.             \tag{19}
\]
For every sufficiently large triple, \(\varepsilon<\alpha\), and hence
\(q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}=1-\alpha+\varepsilon/2\).  Increasing \(C_\omega\)
makes the last ratio in (19) at most \(\varepsilon/4\), so the right side
is at most \(\alpha-\varepsilon/4\).  The ramp argument requires no uniform
anti-concentration constant; such a constant would fail at the
variance-zero normalization point \(t=0\) and when one information channel
is degenerate.

Finally, \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) grows no faster than an iterated logarithm of
\(a=\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\).  Channel by channel,
\[
 \frac{K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)/\varepsilon}{K_T^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_T^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T)}{\varepsilon}\\to0,
 \qquad
 \frac{n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)/\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{n_D^{-1/6}\log^2(e+n_D)}{\varepsilon}\\to0,          \tag{20}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{K_T^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{K_T^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_T^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\\to0,
 \qquad
 \frac{N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\\to0,
 \qquad
 \frac{n_D^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{n_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\\to0.                 \tag{21}
\]
The exponential terms divided by their corresponding standard-error
channels tend to zero, and
\[
 \frac{\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)/\varepsilon}{\rho_r}
 =\frac{C_\eta e^{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor}}{\varepsilon}
 \\to0.                                                       \tag{22}
\]
The convergence in (20)--(22) is uniform on every three-dimensional tail:
\(a\le K_T\), \(a\le K_D\), and \(a\le n_D\), while a negative power or an
exponential dominates the iterated logarithm uniformly above \(a\).
Equations (1) and (20)--(22) prove \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) without comparing
\(K_T\) with \(n_D\).  Equations (3), (14)--(19) prove all remaining
assertions.  The off-grid difference between \(S^0\) and
\(\widetilde S^0\) never enters (14); it is carried only by the explicit
interpolation allowance in the continuous-time band. \(\square\)
