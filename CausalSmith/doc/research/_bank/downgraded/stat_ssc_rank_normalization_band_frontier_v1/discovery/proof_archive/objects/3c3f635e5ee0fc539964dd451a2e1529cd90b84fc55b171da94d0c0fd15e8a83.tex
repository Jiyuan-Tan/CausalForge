\textbf{Proof.}
The displayed affine identity gives, pointwise in \((p,m,t)\),
\[
 \langle u_p^{\mathrm{HS}}(t),v_m^{\mathrm{HS}}\rangle
 =s_p(t)+b_p(t)^\top z_m=S^0_{p,m}(t).                 \tag{1}
\]
Let \(V_D=[v_m^{\mathrm{HS}}:m\in\mathcal D]\).  Donor centering and the empirical Gram identity give
\[
 \frac1{N_0}V_DV_D^\top
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 1 & N_0^{-1}\sum_m z_m^\top\\
 N_0^{-1}\sum_m z_m & N_0^{-1}\sum_mz_mz_m^\top
 \end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&I_d\end{pmatrix}=I_r.         \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(V_DV_D^\top\) is invertible, so \(V_D\) has row rank \(r\) and its columns span \(\mathbb R^r\).  Since \(v_\star^{\mathrm{HS}}=(1,z_\star^\top)^\top\in\mathbb R^r\), there is a donor weight vector \(a\in\mathbb R^{N_0}\) with \(V_Da=v_\star^{\mathrm{HS}}\); explicitly one may take
\[
 a=V_D^\top(V_DV_D^\top)^{-1}v_\star^{\mathrm{HS}}.    \tag{3}
\]
This proves donor-span inclusion.  For a law in \(\mathfrak P_r\), the affine identity and \(d=r-1\) are the normalized factor and exact-rank conditions, while donor centering and the Gram identity are supplied by the canonical factorization.  Hence (1)--(3) apply to every member.  The conclusion is one-way: none of the extra normalization or quantitative restrictions used to define \(\mathfrak P_r\) has been attributed to the larger published structural model. \(\square\)
