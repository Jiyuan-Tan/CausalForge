\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(m=n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\), and \(p_{\mathcal G}=2|\mathcal G|\).  Write \(a_n=a_{r,n}\), \(\delta_n=\delta_{r,n}(\alpha)\), \(\chi_n=\chi_{r,n}\), and \(\tau_n=\tau_{r,n}\).  The covariance-modulus lemma supplies a measurable event \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), contained in \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) at ranks at least two, such that
\[
 P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\le Ca_n^{1/2},
 \quad
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \le C\rho_r^2a_n^{1/2},                                \tag{1}
\]
and both oracle and empirical canonical semimetrics are bounded by
\(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\).  Thus all empirical coefficients used on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) belong to the single regular branch; no derivative across a cutoff is used.

Let \(k=k_{r,n}\), and choose a deterministic subset \(\mathcal T_n\subset\mathcal G\) of at most \(k+1\) knots with physical-time mesh at most \(C/k\).  Evaluate both signs of each normalized patient vector on \(\mathcal T_n\); its dimension is at most
\[
 p_{\mathrm c,r,n}=2(k_{r,n}+1).                        \tag{2}
\]
The patient envelopes in the covariance-modulus lemma and \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\) give
\[
 \sum_iE\|L_i/\rho_r\|_{2,\mathcal T_n}^3
 \le Cp_{\mathrm c,r,n}^{3/2}m^{-1/2}.                 \tag{3}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:yurinskii\mbox{-}degeneracy\mbox{-}robust\mbox{-}coupling}\) in dimension \(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}\) with \(a=U_{r,n}^{1/4}\).  Its dimensioned quantity satisfies
\[
 B_0\le Cp_{\mathrm c,r,n}^{5/2}m^{-1/2}a^{-3}
 \le U_{r,n}^{1/4}.                                     \tag{4}
\]
Hence the coarse-net oracle vector couples to a centered Gaussian vector \(G_r\) with the same covariance with normalized error and exceptional probability bounded by \(C\zeta_{r,n}\).  The oracle modulus bound from the covariance-modulus lemma with \(u=C/k\), and the Gaussian union bound using the same semimetric, transfer the coupling to the full canonical grid.  Therefore, for every real \(x\),
\[
 P\{\|G_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\}
 \le P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le x+C\rho_r\zeta_{r,n}\}+C\zeta_{r,n}.             \tag{5}
\]
This coupling allows singular covariance.

Conditionally on the data, let \(\widehat G_r\) be Gaussian with covariance \(\widehat\Sigma_r\); this is the law of the explicitly defined multiplier process.  Put \(v_n=a_n^{1/2}\).  On \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), select an auxiliary grid of mesh at most \(u_n=v_n^{1/3}\) and cardinality \(K_n\le Cv_n^{-1/3}\).  The covariance matrices on that grid differ in nuclear norm by at most
\[
 CK_n^2\rho_r^2v_n.                                     \tag{6}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:powers\mbox{-}stormer\mbox{-}square\mbox{-}root}\), a common standard-normal vector couples them so that
\[
 E\|G_r-\widehat G_r\|_{2,\mathrm{aux}}
 \le C\rho_rK_nv_n^{1/2}\le C\rho_ra_n^{1/12}.         \tag{7}
\]
The two semimetric bounds and Gaussian exponential moments transfer (7) to the canonical grid.  Markov's inequality at threshold
\[
 \rho_ra_n^{1/48}\{1+|\log a_n|\}                     \tag{8}
\]
and (5), after enlarging \(C_\chi\), yield on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) the simultaneous one-sided comparison
\[
 \widehat F_r(x)
 \le F_{r,P}(x+\rho_r\chi_n)+\chi_n
 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R,                  \tag{9}
\]
where
\[
 F_{r,P}(x)=P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\},
 \qquad
 \widehat F_r(x)=P_\xi\{\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\mid O\}.
\]
No anti-concentration or coordinate-variance lower bound is used.

The rates are explicit.  Since \(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}=O(m^{1/9})\),
\[
 U_{r,n}=O(m^{-2/9}),
 \qquad \zeta_{r,n}=O\{m^{-1/18}(1+\log m)\}.           \tag{10}
\]
Every term in \(a_n\) tends to zero uniformly on a three-dimensional tail, irrespective of relative growth.  Hence
\[
 \chi_n\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \tau_n/\rho_r=\chi_n\longrightarrow0.          \tag{11}
\]
If \(A=\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\), the terms in \(\chi_n\) decay as positive powers of \(A\), up to logarithms, whereas \(\delta_n\) decreases only at the displayed reciprocal iterated-logarithm rate.  Since \(P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\le Ca_n^{1/2}\), for every sufficiently large triple
\[
 \chi_n+P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\le\delta_n/2.             \tag{12}
\]

Let \(q_n=1-\alpha+\delta_n\), and define the deterministic oracle generalized-inverse quantile
\[
 c_{r,P}(u)=\inf\{x\ge0:F_{r,P}(x)\ge u\}.             \tag{13}
\]
The conditional Gaussian maximum is a Borel function of a finite standard-normal vector and the observed covariance matrix.  Taking the infimum over nonnegative rationals proves measurability of \(\widetilde c_r^\xi\), including when the covariance is singular or zero.  Right-continuity of the conditional distribution gives
\(\widehat F_r(\widetilde c_r^\xi)\ge q_n\).  Applying (9) at this random argument shows on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) that
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_n
 \ge c_{r,P}(q_n-\chi_n).                               \tag{14}
\]
The right side is deterministic for fixed \(P\).  Therefore, without any independence between \(Z_r\) and \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
       \le\widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_n\}
 &\ge F_{r,P}\{c_{r,P}(q_n-\chi_n)\}
       -P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\\
 &\ge q_n-\chi_n-P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\\
 &\ge1-\alpha+\delta_n/2,
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{15}
\]
where the last line uses (12).

Finally \(q_n\le1-\alpha/2\).  Conditional Markov inequality implies
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi
 \le\left\{\frac2\alpha E_\xi
 \|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{16}
\]
The pooled and stabilized branchwise moment bounds, followed by Jensen's inequality, prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P\widetilde c_r^\xi\le C\rho_r. \tag{17}
\]
Equations (11), (15), and (17) prove every assertion, including the zero-variance coordinate at time zero. \(\square\)
