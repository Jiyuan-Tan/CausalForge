\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\), \(J=|\mathcal G|\),
\(k_D=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\), and let
\(\widetilde S^0=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0\).  The corrected grid gives
\[
 J\le2+\bar\tau\rho_r^{-1/q_\beta},
 \qquad h^{q_\beta}\le\rho_r.                             \tag{1}
\]
Because \(\rho_r\ge K_T^{-1/2}\) and
\(\rho_r\ge n_D^{-1/2}\), every logarithm of \(J\) is bounded by a fixed
multiple of both \(\log(e+K_T)\) and \(\log(e+n_D)\).  This observation is
used below without a comparison between \(K_T\) and \(n_D\).

\emph{Step 1: regularity of the prediction map.}
Let \(\mathbb H=L^2(\{0,1\}\times\mathcal I)\).  At an interpolated
panel \(x\), write \(\Gamma(x)\) for the symmetrized cross-fold operator,
\(\Pi(x)\) for its selected rank-\(d\) projector, and
\[
 A(x)=\{\Gamma(x)|_{\operatorname{ran}\Pi(x)}\}^{1/2}.     \tag{2}
\]
The coordinate-free prediction map can be written as
\[
 \mathcal F(x)
 =s_1(x)+B_1(x)^\top
 \left\{\int B_0(x)B_0(x)^\top\right\}^{-1}
 \int B_0(x)\{S_{0,\star}(x)-s_0(x)\},                    \tag{3}
\]
where \(B(x)\) is any orthonormal coordinate frame for the range of
\(A(x)\).  Expression (3) is unchanged under a common orthogonal rotation,
so it is well defined even at a tie among selected positive eigenvalues.

At the population point, the interpolated stability lemma gives
\[
 \lambda_d\{\Gamma(\widetilde S^0)\}\ge\gamma_-^2/2,
 \quad \lambda_{d+1}\{\Gamma(\widetilde S^0)\}=0,
 \quad \lambda_{\min}\{H_0(\widetilde S^0)\}\ge\kappa_-/2. \tag{4}
\]
Take a contour \(\mathscr C\) at fixed distance from zero and the positive
cluster.  For a perturbation \(E\), differentiation of the resolvent
identity gives
\[
 D\Pi_\Gamma[E]
 =\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\mathscr C}
 (z-\Gamma)^{-1}E(z-\Gamma)^{-1}\,dz,                     \tag{5}
\]
and a second differentiation inserts a second copy of \(E\) in the two
possible positions.  The same formula with the analytic function
\(z^{1/2}\) gives the first two derivatives of (2).  Their operator norms
are bounded only by fixed powers of \(\gamma_-^{-1}\).  The identities
\[
 D(H^{-1})[E]=-H^{-1}EH^{-1},                               \tag{6}
\]
\[
 D^2(H^{-1})[E_1,E_2]
 =H^{-1}E_1H^{-1}E_2H^{-1}
  +H^{-1}E_2H^{-1}E_1H^{-1}                               \tag{7}
\]
and (4) bound the first two derivatives of the inverse-Gram term by fixed
powers of \(\kappa_-^{-1}\).  Centering, interpolation, integration, and
the cross-fold map are bounded multilinear operations.  Combining
(3)--(7) proves that, in a fixed sup-norm neighborhood \(\mathcal U\) of
\(\widetilde S^0\),
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal U}\{\|D\mathcal F_x\|+\|D^2\mathcal F_x\|\}
 \le C.                                                      \tag{8}
\]
The codomain is \(\mathbb R^J\) with supremum norm, and the constant in
(8) is independent of \(J\): evaluation at a grid knot is a contraction.

The derivative of a cellwise product-limit curve with respect to one
patient counting-process increment is \(O(k^{-1})\) on the cell's
nonexhausted risk event.  Donor centering contributes \(N_0^{-1}\), while
the target-pre curve enters (3) once.  Equation (8) therefore gives the
individual patient envelopes
\[
 \max_j\|w_{T,j}\|_\infty\le C K_T^{-1},
 \qquad
 \max_{p,m,j}\|w_{D,p,m,j}\|_\infty\le C n_D^{-1}.        \tag{9}
\]
The same calculation after one time increment shows that the part of each
weight process which is indexed by its upper integration limit has
predictable quadratic variation bounded by \(C|t-s|K_T^{-2}\) or
\(C|t-s|n_D^{-2}\).  The remaining terms in (3) are products of a bounded
factor curve with one of finitely many scalar martingale integrals.  Thus
the derivative process is a sum of a fixed number, depending only on
\(r\), of time-ordered martingales and bounded finite-rank scalar terms.
This explicit representation replaces an unverified abstract entropy
assertion.

\emph{Step 2: probability of the regular event.}
Apply the established cellwise product-limit expansion separately to the
two deterministic half-folds.  Write
\[
 \widehat Y_m^{(a)}=\widetilde Y_m+U_m^{(a)}+b_m^{(a)}+q_m^{(a)},
 \qquad a=1,2,                                               \tag{10}
\]
where the two \(U_m^{(a)}\)'s are independent and centered,
\[
 E\|U_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-1},
 \quad \|b_m^{(a)}\|_2\le Ce^{-c_0k_D},
 \quad E\|q_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-2}.                  \tag{11}
\]
Expansion of the cross-fold operator gives
\[
 \widehat\Gamma-\widetilde\Gamma=\mathbb L_D+\mathbb R_D, \tag{12}
\]
where \(\mathbb L_D\) is the donor average of the terms linear in the
\(U_m^{(a)}\)'s.  Independence across donors and folds gives
\[
 E\|\mathbb L_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le Cn_D^{-1},
 \qquad
 E\|\mathbb R_D-E\mathbb R_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le C(N_0K_D^2)^{-1},                                    \tag{13}
\]
and the deterministic part is bounded by \(C\eta_D(K_D)\).

We record the concentration calculation needed for the fallback event.
On the event that every half-fold risk set at \(\bar\tau\) is at least
\(k_Dh_0/2\), changing one patient changes the martingale part of (12) by
at most \(C/(N_0K_D)\) and its degenerate cross-fold part by at most
\(C/(N_0K_D^{3/2})\).  Exponential Markov inequality applied successively
to the independent patients and then to the independent donor blocks gives,
for every fixed \(x>0\),
\[
 P\{\|\widehat\Gamma-\widetilde\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}>x
       +C\eta_D(K_D)\}
 \le C\exp\{-c n_Dx^2\}+C\exp\{-cK_D\}+C\exp\{-cN_0K_D^2x^2\}.
                                                                    \tag{14}
\]
The excluded risk-set event is covered by the same bound because the
Poisson--binomial calculation in the pooled lemma applies to every half
fold, followed by exponential Markov inequality for the number of
exhausted cells.  This two-level argument, rather than a union bound over
cells, is uniform when one sample size grows arbitrarily faster than the
other.  The identical calculation for the empirical pre-Gram, using (6),
gives (14) with its smallest eigenvalue in place of the operator norm.
Taking fixed fractions of the gaps in (4), and enlarging the lower-tail
cutoff so that \(C\eta_D\) is smaller than those fractions, proves
\[
 \sup_PP(\mathcal R^c)\le Cv_r^2,                           \tag{15}
\]
where \(\mathcal R\) is the three-threshold regular event and
\[
 v_r=K_T^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0K_T}
       +n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0n_D}.                               \tag{16}
\]
The target-pre terms in (16) are included because empirical derivatives
also use its product-limit input.  Exponentials in (14) are smaller than
the right side of (15) on every three-dimensional tail.

\emph{Step 3: expansion and nonlinear remainder.}
Insert the cellwise decompositions into \(\mathcal F\) and apply Taylor's
identity with integral remainder inside \(\mathcal U\).  The first
derivative applied to the centered patient martingales is
\[
 Z_r=K_T^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}\phi_{T,j}
     +n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j}.                  \tag{17}
\]
The two arrays are independent and centered by patient independence.  The
deterministic half-fold product-limit biases, after application of (8),
have supremum norm at most \(\eta_D(K_D)\); call their sum
\(\mathfrak b_r\).  Target-pre exhaustion, full-cell donor exhaustion,
centered cellwise remainders, quadratic Taylor terms, and the bounded
fallback are assigned to \(Q_r\).  Equations (8), (11), (13), and (15),
together with independence, give term by term
\[
 \sup_PE\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\{K_T^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0K_T}
          +n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0n_D}\}^2
 =Cv_r^2.                                                    \tag{18}
\]
No class assumption is hidden in (18): inverse and division bounds come
from (4), censor and event overlap, and the fallback contribution is
controlled by (15).  The population centering is exact on the grid because
the interpolated stability lemma and the canonical target-coordinate lemma
give
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta,
 \qquad
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g).         \tag{19}
\]

\emph{Step 4: multiplier moment and covariance.}
Let \(\widehat Z_r^\xi\) be the empirical derivative process.  The
time-ordered martingale representation following (9), conditional Doob
inequality, and the finite number of scalar terms imply
\[
 E_\xi\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\left\{
 K_T^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}V_{T,j}^{\xi}
 +n_D^{-2}\sum_{p,m,j}V_{D,p,m,j}^{\xi}\right\},          \tag{20}
\]
where the conditional optional-variation contributions
\(V_{T,j}^{\xi}\) and \(V_{D,p,m,j}^{\xi}\) have uniformly bounded
expectation.  Hence
\[
 \sup_PE_PE_\xi\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1})\le C\rho_r^2.                   \tag{21}
\]
This establishes the required entropy control without paying a
\(\sqrt{\log J}\) factor in the width: the supremum is controlled before
restriction to the grid by martingale maximal inequalities.

Let \(\Sigma_r\) be the covariance of (17) and
\(\widehat\Sigma_r\) the conditional multiplier covariance.  The
optional-variation calculation in the pooled lemma applies to every
cellwise martingale.  Equations (8), (13), and (18) control replacement of
the population derivative by the empirical derivative.  Exponential
Markov inequality over the finite grid then gives
\[
 E\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_{\max}
 \le C\{K_T^{-3/2}\log(e+J)+n_D^{-3/2}\log(e+J)
          +\rho_r v_r+\rho_r^2\eta_D(K_D)\}.               \tag{22}
\]
The formula remains valid when a row or column of either covariance is
zero.

\emph{Step 5: smooth comparison and the random quantile.}
For a vector \(x\in\mathbb R^J\), duplicate its signs and use the soft
maximum
\[
 F_a(x)=a^{-1}\log\sum_{k=1}^{2J}e^{ax_k},
 \quad
 \max_kx_k\le F_a(x)\le\max_kx_k+a^{-1}\log(2J).           \tag{23}
\]
Direct differentiation gives \(\|D^2F_a\|_1\le Ca\) and
\(\|D^3F_a\|_1\le Ca^2\).  Compose it with a one-Lipschitz ramp of width
\(x/2\), smoothed by a triangular kernel, and choose
\(a=4\log(2J)/x\).  Replace the independent target patients and donor
patients, channel by channel, by centered Gaussian vectors with matching
covariances.  Third-order Taylor's formula and the weight bounds (9) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal E_T(x)&\le
 Cx^{-1}K_T^{-2/3}\log^2(e+K_T),\\
 \mathcal E_D(x)&\le
 Cx^{-1}n_D^{-2/3}\log^2(e+n_D).                           \tag{24}
 \end{split}
\]
One way to see the powers is to truncate standardized patient vectors at
\(k^{1/6}/\log(e+k)\): the Taylor sum of their third moments and the
exponential-Markov bound for the discarded part have the same order.
Equation (1) absorbs every explicit \(\log J\) factor.  This proves the
comparison for non-identically distributed arrays because replacement is
patient by patient and uses only independence and the uniform envelopes.

Gaussian interpolation from the oracle covariance to
\(\widehat\Sigma_r\), with (22), and a second Taylor expansion replacing
the oracle derivative by the empirical derivative add
\[
 Cx^{-1}\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)+Cx^{-2}v_r^2.                  \tag{25}
\]
As in the pooled proof, a ghost sample makes the covariance interpolation
conditional, after which monotone simple-function approximation permits
the ramp center to be the random conditional quantile.  Combining
(24)--(25) yields the additive random-quantile inequality
\[
 \sup_PP\{\|Z_r\|_{infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+x\}
 \le1-q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}+C\{\chi_r/x+v_r^2/x^2\},           \tag{26}
\]
where
\[
 \chi_r=K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)+n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)
          +\rho_r\eta_D(K_D),                              \tag{27}
\]
\[
 a_T(k)=k^{-1/6}\log^2(e+k),
 \qquad a_D(n)=n^{-1/6}\log^2(e+n).                       \tag{28}
\]
The ramp supplies the additive \(x\); no division by a coordinate standard
deviation and no anti-concentration constant occurs.

Put \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\).  By definition,
\[
 \omega_r=C_\omega\{\varepsilon^{-1}\chi_r
                    +\varepsilon^{-1/2}v_r\}.              \tag{29}
\]
Markov's inequality applied to (18), followed by a sufficiently large
fixed \(C_\omega\), gives
\[
 \sup_PP\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le\varepsilon/4.                                         \tag{30}
\]
Taking \(x=\omega_r/2\) in (26), increasing \(C_\omega\) once more, and
using \(q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}=1-\alpha+\varepsilon/2\) for all sufficiently
large triples gives
\[
 \sup_PP\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\omega_r/2\}
 \le\alpha-\varepsilon/4.                                 \tag{31}
\]

Finally, \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) grows at most as an iterated logarithm of
\(a=\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\).  Channel by channel,
\[
 \frac{a_T(K_T)}{\varepsilon}\to0,
 \quad \frac{a_D(n_D)}{\varepsilon}\to0,
 \quad \frac{K_T^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0,
 \quad \frac{K_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0,
 \quad \frac{n_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0,          \tag{32}
\]
and every exponential divided by the corresponding standard-error channel
tends to zero.  Also \(\eta_D(K_D)/\varepsilon\to0\).  Each convergence is
uniform above \(a\), because a negative power or exponential dominates an
iterated logarithm.  Equations (27)--(32) therefore imply
\(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) without any relative-growth restriction.  This
proves all assertions. \(\square\)
