\textbf{Proof.}
Fix an observed control cell and suppress its \((p,m)\) subscripts until the last step.  Write
\[
 N_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\le u,\Delta_j=1\},\qquad
 Y_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\ge u\},\qquad
 Y(u)=\sum_{j=1}^kY_j(u),
\]
and use the predictable left-continuous version of \(Y_j\) at jump times.  By independent censoring, the failure counting process has compensator
\[
 \int_0^uY_j(v)\lambda^0(v)\,dv,
\]
so
\[
 M_j(u)=N_j(u)-\int_0^uY_j(v)\lambda^0(v)\,dv
\]
is a mean-zero square-integrable martingale.  Patient independence makes the \(M_j\)'s independent.  Set
\[
 M=\sum_{j=1}^kM_j,\qquad J(u)=\mathbf 1\{Y(u)>0\},\qquad
 H(u)=E Y_j(u)=S^0(u)G(u).
\]
The equality defining \(H\) follows from independent censoring; the continuous event hazard removes any distinction between \(T^0\ge u\) and \(T^0>u\).  Censor and event overlap give the uniform lower bound
\[
 H(u)\ge h_0:=s_-g_->0,\qquad u\in\mathcal I.
\]
Hazard smoothness also gives
\[
 \Lambda^0(\bar\tau):=\int_0^{\bar\tau}\lambda^0(u)\,du
 \le L\bar\tau.
\]

Let \(\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}\) denote the product-limit step curve and put \(R=\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}/S^0\).  The two product-integral equations are
\[
 d\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}(u)
 =-\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}(u-)\frac{J(u)}{Y(u)}\,dN(u),
 \qquad
 dS^0(u)=-S^0(u)\lambda^0(u)\,du.
\]
Integration by parts, followed by \(dN=dM+Y\lambda^0du\), therefore yields the exact identity
\[
 R(t)-1
 =-\int_0^tR(u-)\frac{J(u)}{Y(u)}\,dM(u)
  +\int_0^tR(u-)\{1-J(u)\}\lambda^0(u)\,du.                 \tag{1}
\]
There is no division on an empty risk set because of the factor \(J\).  Moreover
\[
 0\le R(u)\le s_-^{-1}.                                      \tag{2}
\]

Define the patient influence processes by
\[
 \varphi_j(t)=-S^0(t)\int_0^tH(u)^{-1}\,dM_j(u).             \tag{3}
\]
The integrand in (3) is deterministic and bounded by \(h_0^{-1}\).  Hence each \(\varphi_j\) is mean zero, and the processes are independent across \(j\).  Subtracting \(k^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_j\) from (1) after multiplication by \(S^0(t)\) gives
\[
 \widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}(t)-S^0(t)
 -k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_j(t)=V_k(t)+A_k(t),              \tag{4}
\]
where
\[
 \begin{split}
 V_k(t)&=-S^0(t)\int_0^tD_k(u)\,dM(u),\\
 D_k(u)&=R(u-)\frac{J(u)}{Y(u)}-\frac{1}{kH(u)},\\
 A_k(t)&=S^0(t)\int_0^tR(u-)\{1-J(u)\}\lambda^0(u)\,du.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{5}
\]
All stochastic integrands in (3) and (5) are predictable.

We first isolate the only nonzero bias.  The stochastic integral \(V_k\) is integrable and has mean zero.  For each \(u\), patient independence and overlap imply
\[
 \Pr\{Y(u)=0\}=\{1-H(u)\}^k
 \le e^{-kH(u)}\le e^{-h_0k}.                                \tag{6}
\]
Using (2), Fubini's theorem, and the cumulative-hazard bound,
\[
 \left\|EA_k\right\|_\infty
 \le s_-^{-1}\int_0^{\bar\tau}\Pr\{Y(u)=0\}\lambda^0(u)\,du
 \le s_-^{-1}L\bar\tau e^{-h_0k}.                            \tag{7}
\]
Set
\[
 b_k=EA_k,\qquad q_k=V_k+A_k-EA_k.                             \tag{8}
\]
Equations (4) and (8) give the asserted algebraic decomposition and \(Eq_k=0\).  Since \(c_0=h_0/3<h_0\), enlarging a fixed constant gives
\[
 \|b_k\|_\infty\le C_0e^{-c_0k}.                            \tag{9}
\]

For completeness, we now establish the stated second-order moment uniformly.  The at-risk indicators form a nested class.  Symmetrization followed by the exponential-moment bound for sums of variables in \([-1,1]\) gives, for every \(x>0\),
\[
 \Pr\left\{\sup_{u\in\mathcal I}|Y(u)-kH(u)|>x\right\}
 \le 2\exp(-2x^2/k).                                         \tag{10}
\]
In particular, for
\[
 \mathcal E_k=\left\{\sup_{u\in\mathcal I}|Y(u)/k-H(u)|
 \le h_0/2\right\},
\]
we have \(\Pr(\mathcal E_k^c)\le2e^{-kh_0^2/2}\), and integration of (10) gives
\[
 E\sup_{u\in\mathcal I}|Y(u)-kH(u)|^2\le Ck.                \tag{11}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_k\), \(J=1\), \(Y\ge kh_0/2\), and
\[
 D_k(u)=\frac{R(u-)-1}{Y(u)}
 -\frac{Y(u)-kH(u)}{kH(u)Y(u)}.                               \tag{12}
\]
Stop the processes when \(|Y/k-H|\) first exceeds \(h_0/2\).  Before that stopping time, (1), (2), and the martingale isometry give
\[
 E\sup_{u\in\mathcal I}|R(u)-1|^2\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}
 \le Ck^{-1}.                                                 \tag{13}
\]
Indeed, the predictable quadratic variation is bounded by
\[
 \int_0^{\bar\tau}R(u-)^2Y(u)^{-2}Y(u)\lambda^0(u)\,du
 \le Ck^{-1},
\]
and the maximal second-moment inequality multiplies this bound by at most four.  Combining (11)--(13) with (12) gives
\[
 E\left[\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}
 \int_0^{\bar\tau}D_k(u)^2Y(u)\lambda^0(u)\,du\right]
 \le C\left\{k^{-1}E\|R-1\|_\infty^2
       +k^{-3}E\|Y-kH\|_\infty^2\right\}
 \le Ck^{-2}.                                                 \tag{14}
\]
Applying the same isometry and maximal inequality to (5) yields
\[
 E\|V_k\|_\infty^2\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}\le Ck^{-2}.      \tag{15}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_k^c\), use (4): both survival curves are bounded by one, \(A_k\) is bounded by \(s_-^{-1}L\bar\tau\), and (3) has a uniformly bounded fourth maximal moment.  Cauchy--Schwarz and the exponential bound following (10) then give
\[
 E\|V_k\|_\infty^2\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k^c}
 \le Ce^{-c k}\le Ck^{-2}                                    \tag{16}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(k\), with fixed \(c>0\).  Finally, \(A_k\) can be nonzero only if the nonincreasing risk set is exhausted by \(\bar\tau\).  Thus, by (2) and (6),
\[
 E\|A_k-EA_k\|_\infty^2\le Ce^{-h_0k}\le Ck^{-2}.            \tag{17}
\]
Equations (8) and (15)--(17) prove
\[
 E\|q_k\|_\infty^2\le C_0k^{-2}.                            \tag{18}
\]
All constants in (7)--(18) depend only on the fixed overlap, hazard, and horizon bounds, so the estimates are uniform over the model class.

We next record the donor averaging rate rather than inferring it merely from the Banach-space second moment in (18).  For independent donor cells, sum the stochastic-integral representations in (5) before applying the maximal inequality.  Independence makes the cross predictable covariations zero, and the calculation in (14) gives
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}V_{p,m,k}\right\|_\infty^2
 \le \frac{C}{N_0k^2}.                                      \tag{19}
\]
Multiplication by the deterministic survival curves in (5) is handled by integration by parts; their total variations are bounded by \(L\bar\tau\), so this changes only \(C\).  The centered exhaustion terms are independent bounded-variation processes, supported on events of probability at most \(e^{-h_0k}\).  Symmetrization and summation by parts over their ordered exhaustion times give
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
       \{A_{p,m,k}-EA_{p,m,k}\}\right\|_\infty^2
 \le \frac{Ce^{-h_0k}}{N_0}
 \le \frac{C}{N_0k^2}.                                      \tag{20}
\]
Combining (8), (19), and (20), and then applying Markov's inequality, proves
\[
 \left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}q_{p,m,k}\right\|_\infty
 =O_P(N_0^{-1/2}k^{-1}).                                     \tag{21}
\]
When \(k\to\infty\), division of the right side of (21) by \((N_0k)^{-1/2}\) gives \(k^{-1/2}\to0\).  Biases need not cancel across cells, so (9) is the uniform bound for their average.

It remains to state precisely what linear interpolation does.  Piecewise-linear interpolation is a contraction in the supremum norm.  Applying \(I_{\mathcal G}\) to the step-curve decomposition gives
\[
 I_{\mathcal G}\widehat S^{\mathrm{PL}}-I_{\mathcal G}S^0
 =k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^kI_{\mathcal G}\varphi_j
  +I_{\mathcal G}b_k+I_{\mathcal G}q_k.                     \tag{22}
\]
Because \((S^0)'=-\lambda^0S^0\) and \(\|\lambda^0\|_{C^\beta}\le L\), the survival curve has a uniformly bounded \(C^{\beta+1}\) norm.  Taylor's formula on each grid interval, capped at the order of a linear interpolant, gives, with \(q=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\),
\[
 \|I_{\mathcal G}S^0-S^0\|_\infty
 \le C_{\mathrm{int}}h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{q}.                   \tag{23}
\]
Adding (23) to (22) proves the claimed interpolated decomposition.  Notice that the term in (23) is indispensable: even with no censoring and \(S^0(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\), the chord joining two grid values differs from \(S^0\) at interior points.  Its expectation cannot be absorbed into the exponentially small bias in (9).

Restoring cell subscripts proves every displayed assertion.  For the deterministic odd--even donor split, each half has size at least \(k_D^-:=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\).  Substitution of \(k=k_D^-\) into (9), without replacing the floor by \(K_D/2\), yields the required allowance
\[
 C_0\exp\{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\}.
\]
This completes the proof. \(\square\)
