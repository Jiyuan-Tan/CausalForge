\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\), and \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\). The corrected canonical grid satisfies
\[
 h^{q_\beta}\le\rho_r,
 \qquad |\mathcal G|\le2+\bar\tau\rho_r^{-1/q_\beta}. \tag{1}
\]
The interpolated-population stability lemma gives
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta,
 \qquad
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g)
 \quad(g\in\mathcal G).                                  \tag{2}
\]

If \(r=1\), apply \(\mathrm{lem:pooled\mbox{-}noniid\mbox{-}km\mbox{-}linearization}\). Define \(\phi_{D,1,m,j}=\phi_{m,j}^{\mathrm{pool}}\), put every donor-pre influence equal to zero, set \(\phi_{T,j}=0\) and \(\mathfrak b_1=0\), and include the exponentially small pooled bias in \(Q_1\). The helper gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_1-\theta
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}\phi_{D,1,m,j}+Q_1,
\]
\[
 E\|Q_1\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\{n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}\}^2.             \tag{3}
\]
If \(r\ge2\), \(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}gridwise\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}transfer}\) gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =K_T^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}\phi_{T,j}
 +n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j}
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r,                                      \tag{4}
\]
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le\eta_D(K_D),
 \qquad E\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C\upsilon_r^2. \tag{5}
\]
Disjoint patient samples make all displayed influence processes independent and mean zero. Equations (3)--(5) prove the expansion.

The corrected definition is
\[
 \omega_r=C_\omega\varepsilon^{-1/2}\upsilon_r.        \tag{6}
\]
Choose the fixed \(C_\omega\) so that \(4C/C_\omega^2\le1/4\). Markov's inequality in (3) or (5) then gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}
 P\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le\varepsilon/4.                                       \tag{7}
\]

The pooled and stabilized-transfer helpers, together with the explicit constant registered in the band definition, give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P
 \|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C_Z\rho_r^2.          \tag{8}
\]
The deterministic guard is
\[
 d_r(\alpha)=\left(\frac{2C_Z}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2}\rho_r.
\]
Hence Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>d_r(\alpha)\}
 \le\alpha/2.                                            \tag{9}
\]
By construction \(c_r^\xi\ge d_r(\alpha)\). Since \(\varepsilon\to0\) uniformly on three-dimensional tails, every sufficiently large evaluated triple has \(\varepsilon\le2\alpha\). Therefore
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\omega_r/2\}
 \le\alpha/2\le\alpha-\varepsilon/4.                  \tag{10}
\]
This is a deterministic-oracle moment argument; it does not substitute a random multiplier quantile into an unconditional distribution function.

It remains to prove the uniform small-order assertion. Let \(a=\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\). The reciprocal of \(\varepsilon\) grows only as an iterated logarithm of \(a\). Dividing the terms of \(\upsilon_r\) by the corresponding summands of \(\rho_r\) gives quantities bounded by
\[
 K_T^{-1/2},\quad K_D^{-1/2},\quad (N_0K_D)^{-1/2},\quad
 \eta_D(K_D),                                             \tag{11}
\]
\[
 K_T^{1/2}e^{-c_\dagger K_T/2},\qquad
 (N_0K_D)^{1/2}e^{-c_\dagger N_0K_D/2}.                  \tag{12}
\]
Every quantity in (11)--(12), even after multiplication by \(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\), tends to zero uniformly above the three-dimensional tail cutoff. Thus \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) without comparing \(K_T\) with \(N_0K_D\). Equation (2) explains why interpolation inside the \(L^2\) factor map is used for spectrum and Gram stability, while the off-grid discrepancy is carried by the final interpolation allowance. \(\square\)
