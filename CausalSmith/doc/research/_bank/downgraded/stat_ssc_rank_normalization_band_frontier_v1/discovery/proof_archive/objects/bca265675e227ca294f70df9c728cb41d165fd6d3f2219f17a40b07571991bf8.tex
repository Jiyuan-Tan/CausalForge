\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\) and write \(h=h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\). The hazard identity and endpoint normalization give
\[
 S^0_{p,m}(t)=\exp\left\{-\int_0^t\lambda^0_{p,m}(u)\,du\right\}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:hazard\mbox{-}smoothness}\), the product rule applied to \((S^0_{p,m})'=-\lambda^0_{p,m}S^0_{p,m}\) gives a uniform \(C^{\beta+1}\) bound on every cell survival curve. The piecewise-linear interpolation remainder on each grid interval is therefore bounded by \(C_*h^{q_\beta}\), where \(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\). Interpolation reproduces its input at every knot, so
\[
 \widetilde S^0_{p,m}(g)=S^0_{p,m}(g),
 \qquad
 \|\widetilde S^0_{p,m}-S^0_{p,m}\|_\infty\le C_*h^{q_\beta}. \tag{1}
\]
Because averaging and centering are linear and every cell uses the same grid,
\[
 \widetilde s_p=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}s_p,
 \qquad
 \widetilde Y_m=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}Y_m. \tag{2}
\]
The donor incoherence and factor envelope assumptions give \(\sup_m\|Y_m\|_{L^2}\le C\). Equations (1)--(2), the fixed horizon, and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \sup_m\|\widetilde Y_m-Y_m\|_{L^2}\le C h^{q_\beta}. \tag{3}
\]
For rank-one operators, \(\|x\otimes y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|x\|_2\|y\|_2\). Hence
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le \|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\\
 &\le N_0^{-1}\sum_m
 \{\|\widetilde Y_m-Y_m\|_2\|Y_m\|_2
   +\|\widetilde Y_m\|_2\|\widetilde Y_m-Y_m\|_2\}\\
 &\le C_*h^{q_\beta}.
 \end{split} \tag{4}
\]

Assume \(d>0\). The canonical donor normalization gives
\[
 \Gamma=N_0^{-1}\sum_mY_m\otimes Y_m
       =\sum_{k=1}^d\sigma_k^2u_k\otimes u_k. \tag{5}
\]
Thus \(\lambda_d(\Gamma)\ge\gamma_-^2\) and \(\lambda_{d+1}(\Gamma)=0\). Also (2) and the normalized factor model show that \(\widetilde Y_m=(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}b)^{\top}z_m\), so \(\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde\Gamma)\le d\). The variational characterization of ordered eigenvalues and (4) imply
\[
 |\lambda_k(\widetilde\Gamma)-\lambda_k(\Gamma)|
 \le\|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \tag{6}
\]
For all sufficiently fine grids, (4)--(6) give \(\lambda_d(\widetilde\Gamma)\ge\gamma_-^2/2\); together with the rank bound this proves that \(\widetilde\Gamma\) has exactly \(d\) positive eigenvalues.

Let \(\mathscr C\) be a fixed contour separating \([\gamma_-^2/2,2\gamma_+^2]\) from a neighborhood of zero. Finite-rank spectral calculus and the resolvent identity give
\[
 \widetilde\Pi-\Pi
 =\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathscr C}
 (z-\widetilde\Gamma)^{-1}(\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma)(z-\Gamma)^{-1}\,dz. \tag{7}
\]
The two resolvents on \(\mathscr C\) are bounded only by the fixed spectral gap. Equations (4) and (7) yield
\[
 \|\widetilde\Pi-\Pi\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C h^{q_\beta}. \tag{8}
\]
Applying the same contour identity with the analytic function \(z^{1/2}\) on the positive spectral component shows that the selected positive square roots differ by at most \(Ch^{q_\beta}\) in operator norm. Choose orthonormal coordinate frames for the two selected ranges and align them by the polar factor of their cross-Gram matrix. Equation (8) makes that cross-Gram matrix invertible, and the elementary polar identity then supplies an orthogonal \(R_{\mathcal G}\) for which
\[
 \max_p\|\widetilde b_p-R_{\mathcal G}^{\top}b_p\|_{L^2}
 \le C h^{q_\beta}. \tag{9}
\]
This formulation is insensitive to internal eigenvalue ties.

By the pre-contrast Gram assumption, \(\lambda_{\min}(H_0)\ge\kappa_-\). From (9), the factor envelope, and the identity \(xx^\top-yy^\top=(x-y)x^\top+y(x-y)^\top\),
\[
 \|\widetilde H_0-R_{\mathcal G}^{\top}H_0R_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C h^{q_\beta}. \tag{10}
\]
For a sufficiently fine grid, the right side is at most \(\kappa_-/2\), so \(\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde H_0)\ge\kappa_-/2\). Finally,
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1} \tag{11}
\]
with \(A=\widetilde H_0\) and \(B=R_{\mathcal G}^{\top}H_0R_{\mathcal G}\) proves the inverse bound.

It remains to identify the comparison curve. Linearity of \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) and the normalized factor model give, for the same target loading,
\[
 \widetilde S^0_{p,\star}-\widetilde s_p
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}(b_p^\top z_\star). \tag{12}
\]
The interpolated pre-factor Gram is nonsingular by (10), so the population least-squares step recovers the coordinates of \(z_\star\) in the rotated interpolated factor basis exactly. Substitution in the post-period factor map yields
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}
 =\widetilde s_1+\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}(b_1^\top z_\star)
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}(s_1+b_1^\top z_\star)
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta. \tag{13}
\]
Equation (1) at the knots proves the asserted grid equality. When \(d=0\), every factor calculation is an empty sum and (13) reduces to \(\theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{1,\mathcal G}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}s_1\). \(\square\)
