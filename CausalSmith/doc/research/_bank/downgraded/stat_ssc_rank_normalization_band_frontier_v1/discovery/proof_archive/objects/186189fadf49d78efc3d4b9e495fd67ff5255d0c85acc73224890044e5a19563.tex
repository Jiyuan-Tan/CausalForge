\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\mathrm{lem:normalization\mbox{-}collapse}\), every donor-post event survival curve equals \(s_1\), so every donor-post failure hazard equals a common function \(\lambda_1\), although the censoring survivals \(G_{1,m}\) may differ. For \(i=(m,j)\), define
\[
 N_i(u)=\mathbf1\{X_{1,m,j}\le u,\Delta_{1,m,j}=1\},
 \quad Y_i(u)=\mathbf1\{X_{1,m,j}\ge u\},
\]
\[
 M_i(u)=N_i(u)-\int_0^uY_i(v)\lambda_1(v)\,dv.          \tag{1}
\]
Control consistency and independent censoring identify the compensator in (1), and patient independence makes the \(M_i\)'s independent mean-zero square-integrable martingales. With \(Y=\sum_iY_i\), \(M=\sum_iM_i\), and \(J_Y=\mathbf1\{Y>0\}\), equal donor cell sizes give
\[
 EY(u)=n_DH_D(u),
 \qquad H_D(u)=s_1(u)\bar G_D(u)\ge h_0:=s_-g_->0.      \tag{2}
\]

Let \(\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}\) be the ordinary product-limit curve formed after pooling the donor-post records, and put \(R=\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}/s_1\). Product integration and integration by parts give the exact identity
\[
 R(t)-1=-\int_0^tR(u-)\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)}\,dM(u)
 +\int_0^tR(u-)\{1-J_Y(u)\}\lambda_1(u)\,du.           \tag{3}
\]
The convention \(J_Y/Y=0\) on an empty risk set makes (3) well defined. Define
\[
 \phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}(t)
 =-s_1(t)\int_0^tH_D(u)^{-1}\,dM_i(u).                  \tag{4}
\]
These processes are independent and mean zero. Subtracting \(n_D^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}\) from (3) leaves a martingale term with predictable integrand
\[
 D_n(u)=R(u-)\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)}-\frac1{n_DH_D(u)}       \tag{5}
\]
and the empty-risk drift.

The horizon risk set is a sum of independent, not necessarily identically distributed Bernoulli variables. Equation (2) and exponential Markov inequality give the Poisson--binomial bounds
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)=0\}\le e^{-h_0n_D},
 \qquad
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)<n_Dh_0/2\}\le e^{-cn_D}.              \tag{6}
\]
On the complement of the second event, monotonicity of \(Y\) gives \(Y(u)\ge n_Dh_0/2\) for every \(u\le\bar\tau\), and
\[
 D_n(u)=\frac{R(u-)-1}{Y(u)}
 -\frac{Y(u)-n_DH_D(u)}{n_DH_D(u)Y(u)}.                 \tag{7}
\]
Stop (3) before \(Y<n_Dh_0/2\). The predictable isometry and Doob's \(L^2\) maximal inequality give
\[
 E\{\mathbf1_{\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge n_Dh_0/2\}}
 \|R-1\|_\infty^2\}\le Cn_D^{-1}.                     \tag{8}
\]
Moreover, independence and boundedness give
\[
 E\{Y(u)-n_DH_D(u)\}^2\le n_D.                         \tag{9}
\]
Substitution of (8)--(9) into (7), followed by the predictable isometry, yields a \(Cn_D^{-2}\) bound for the squared supremum of the centered martingale remainder on the regular event. On its complement, the fourth-moment martingale inequality, Cauchy--Schwarz, and (6) give the same polynomial bound. The drift in (3) is uniformly bounded and supported on \(\{Y(\bar\tau)=0\}\), so its expectation is bounded by \(Ce^{-cn_D}\) and its centered squared supremum by \(Ce^{-cn_D}\le Cn_D^{-2}\). Therefore, after setting the deterministic bias equal to the drift expectation and collecting the centered terms into \(q_{D,n_D}^{\mathrm{PL}}\),
\[
 \widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}-s_1
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}
  +b_{D,n_D}^{\mathrm{PL}}+q_{D,n_D}^{\mathrm{PL}},      \tag{10}
\]
\[
 \|b_{D,n_D}^{\mathrm{PL}}\|_\infty\le Ce^{-cn_D},
 \qquad Eq_{D,n_D}^{\mathrm{PL}}=0,
 \qquad E\|q_{D,n_D}^{\mathrm{PL}}\|_\infty^2\le Cn_D^{-2}. \tag{11}
\]
Applying the deterministic linear interpolation operator to (10) preserves the exact identity, the expectations, and the bounds, and it fixes every grid knot. This proves the stated expansion.

The predictable isometry and Doob's inequality applied to (4) give
\[
 E\left\|n_D^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}\right\|_\infty^2
 \le \frac{4L\bar\tau}{h_0^2n_D}
 \le C_Zn_D^{-1}.                                       \tag{12}
\]
Conditionally on the records, the ordered failure increments of the empirical multiplier form a Gaussian martingale with weights \(-\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}J_Y/Y\). On \(\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge n_Dh_0/2\}\), its conditional quadratic variation is at most \(C/n_D\). On the complement, it is at most \(n_D\), while (6) makes that event exponentially rare. Conditional Doob inequality and \(n_De^{-cn_D}\le C/n_D\) yield
\[
 E E_\xi\|\widehat Z_1^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C_Zn_D^{-1}.                                       \tag{13}
\]

It remains to verify covariance consistency, which is not used as a coverage premise. The time-indexed classes \(\{Y_i(u):u\in\mathcal I\}\) and \(\{N_i(u):u\in\mathcal I\}\) are nested. Time-ordered summation by parts and Doob's maximal inequality give, uniformly over the non-identical censoring array,
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal I}|Y(u)/n_D-H_D(u)|=O_P(n_D^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \|\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}-s_1\|_\infty=O_P(n_D^{-1/2}). \tag{14}
\]
On the regular event in (6), substitute (14) in the optional-variation formula for \(\widehat\Sigma_1\). The map
\[
 (S,H)\longmapsto S(s)S(t)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}H(u)^{-1}\lambda_1(u)\,du
\]
is uniformly Lipschitz on \(H\ge h_0/2\). Patient-level Bernstein applied to the bounded ordered increments, followed by the same nested-index maximal calculation, therefore gives
\[
 n_D\|\widehat\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\|_{\infty,\mathcal I^2}
 =o_P(1).                                                  \tag{15}
\]
The complement is exponentially negligible, and interpolation is a supremum-norm contraction. Equations (12)--(15) are uniform because only \(H_D\ge h_0\) and the fixed model constants were used. At time zero every process and covariance is zero. No random critical threshold enters the argument. \(\square\)
