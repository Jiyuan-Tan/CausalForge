\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\), suppress the sample-size indices,
and put \(\mathcal H=L^2(\{0,1\}\times\mathcal I)\).  Let
\((\bar b_p,\bar z_m)\) be the compatible representation in the statement.
Thus
\[
 S^0_{p,m}(t)-s_p(t)=\bar b_p(t)^\top\bar z_m,
 \qquad m\in\mathcal M.                                  \tag{1}
\]
Because \(s_p\) is the donor mean in
\(\mathrm{def:canonical\mbox{-}factorization}\), averaging (1) over
\(m\in\mathcal D\) and using donor centering gives the same centered donor
curves in every compatible representation.

Define \(\bar B:\mathbb R^d\to\mathcal H\) and
\(\bar Z\in\mathbb R^{N_0\times d}\) by
\[
 (\bar Bx)_p(t)=\bar b_p(t)^\top x,
 \qquad \bar Z_{m\cdot}=\bar z_m^\top.                  \tag{2}
\]
For every \(a\in\mathbf 1^\perp\), the definition of the centered donor
operator and (1) give
\[
 \mathcal C_Da
 =\bar B\left(N_0^{-1/2}\bar Z^\top a\right).           \tag{3}
\]
By exact centered rank, \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C_D)=d\).  Since
(3) factors \(\mathcal C_D\) through \(\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 d=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C_D)
 \leq\min\{\operatorname{rank}(\bar B),
             \operatorname{rank}(\bar Z)\}\leq d.       \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\bar B\) and \(\bar Z\) both have rank \(d\).  This conclusion uses
only compatibility and exact centered rank, not the pre-period Gram condition.

The donor loading covariance
\[
 \Sigma=N_0^{-1}\bar Z^\top\bar Z                       \tag{5}
\]
is therefore positive definite.  Covariance-normalize by setting
\[
 \widetilde b_p=\Sigma^{1/2}\bar b_p,
 \qquad \widetilde z_m=\Sigma^{-1/2}\bar z_m.           \tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
 \widetilde b_p(t)^\top\widetilde z_m
 =\bar b_p(t)^\top\bar z_m,
 \qquad
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \widetilde z_m\widetilde z_m^\top=I_d.                 \tag{7}
\]
Let \(\widetilde Bx=(\widetilde b_0^\top x,
\widetilde b_1^\top x)\), and put
\(Y_m=(S^0_{0,m}-s_0,S^0_{1,m}-s_1)\in\mathcal H\).  Equation (7) yields
\[
 \Gamma:=N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}Y_m\otimes Y_m
 =\widetilde B\widetilde B^*.                            \tag{8}
\]

Let \(B:\mathbb R^d\to\mathcal H\) be the canonical donor-SVD factor map,
so \((Bx)_p=b_p^\top x\).  The singular-value construction gives
\[
 \mathcal C_D\mathcal C_D^*=BB^*.                        \tag{9}
\]
Donor centering also gives directly
\[
 \mathcal C_D\mathcal C_D^*
 =N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}Y_m\otimes Y_m=\Gamma.   \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, for \(f\in\mathcal H\), the vector with entries
\(N_0^{-1/2}\langle Y_m,f\rangle_{\mathcal H}\) lies in
\(\mathbf 1^\perp\), and applying \(\mathcal C_D\) to that vector gives the
middle operator in (10).  Combining (8)--(10),
\[
 BB^*=\widetilde B\widetilde B^*.                        \tag{11}
\]
Both maps have rank \(d\), so their ranges coincide.  Let
\(B^\dagger=(B^*B)^{-1}B^*\) be the left inverse of \(B\), and define
\[
 R=B^\dagger\widetilde B.                                \tag{12}
\]
Range equality gives \(BR=\widetilde B\).  Moreover, (11) implies
\[
 RR^*=B^\dagger\widetilde B\widetilde B^*B^{\dagger *}
     =B^\dagger BB^*B^{\dagger *}=I_d.                  \tag{13}
\]
Hence \(R\) is orthogonal and
\[
 b_p=R\widetilde b_p.                                    \tag{14}
\]
For a donor \(m\), injectivity of \(B\), (7), and
\(BR=\widetilde B\) give
\[
 Bz_m=Y_m=\widetilde B\widetilde z_m=BR\widetilde z_m
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z_m=R\widetilde z_m.        \tag{15}
\]
Thus the covariance-normalized compatible factors and loadings equal the
canonical donor-SVD coordinates up to one common orthogonal transformation.
The deterministic sign and tie-breaking rule merely chooses a representative
within this orthogonal equivalence class.

Assume \(d>0\).  Compatibility for the target unit and (6), (14) give
\[
 S^0_{0,\star}-s_0
 =\widetilde b_0^\top\widetilde z_\star
 =b_0^\top R\widetilde z_\star.                          \tag{16}
\]
Thus the target-pre contrast belongs to the span of the canonical pre-period
factors.  Now, and only now, use the positive pre-contrast Gram assumption:
\[
 H_0:=\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)b_0(t)^\top\,dt,
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(H_0)\geq\kappa_->0.              \tag{17}
\]
Therefore \(H_0\) is invertible.  Multiplying (16) by \(b_0(t)\),
integrating, and applying (17) yields
\[
 R\widetilde z_\star
 =H_0^{-1}\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)
 \{S^0_{0,\star}(t)-s_0(t)\}\,dt.                       \tag{18}
\]
If \(x\) and \(x'\) are two compatible canonical target coefficients, then
\(b_0^\top(x-x')=0\) in \(L^2(\mathcal I)\), whence
\[
 0=\int_{\mathcal I}\{b_0(t)^\top(x-x')\}^2\,dt
  =(x-x')^\top H_0(x-x').                                \tag{19}
\]
Positive definiteness forces \(x=x'\).  Hence (18) is the unique compatible
target coordinate and is exactly the \(z_\star\) selected in the canonical
construction.

If \(d=0\), a compatible \(d\)-coordinate representation has an empty factor
term, so (1) reads \(S^0_{p,m}=s_p\) for every period and every unit.  This
proves the zero-dimensional assertion and completes the proof. \(\square\)
