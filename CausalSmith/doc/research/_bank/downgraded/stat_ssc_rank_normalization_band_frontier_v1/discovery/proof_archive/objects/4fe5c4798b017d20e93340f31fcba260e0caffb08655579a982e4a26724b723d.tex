\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(k_D=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\), \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\), and
\[
 a_N=\min\{K_T,n_D\}^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T+n_D).
\]
The cellwise decomposition in \(\mathrm{lem:kaplan\mbox{-}meier\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}exhaustion}\) applies both to every full donor cell and to the two deterministic folds.  Its bias bound on the smaller fold is exactly
\[
 C_0\exp(-c_0k_D)=C_0\exp\{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\}.
\]
This quantity cannot be replaced by \(C\exp(-c_0K_D)\) with a sample-size-independent \(C\).  Choose the fixed constant \(C_\eta\) in \(\eta_D\) larger than \(C_0\) times a uniform bound for the first two derivatives of the functional-PCR map obtained below.  The full-cell donor means then obey
\[
 \widehat s_p-s_p
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}\varphi^s_{p,m,j}+b^s_p+q^s_p,
 \qquad
 \|b^s_p\|_\infty\le\eta_D(K_D),
 \qquad
 E\|q^s_p\|_\infty^2\le Cn_D^{-1}K_D^{-2}.
\]
The target-pre curve has the analogous expansion with deterministic bias at most \(C e^{-c_0K_T}\) and centered second-order remainder having squared supremum norm at most \(CK_T^{-2}\).

Let \(Y_m=(b_0^\top z_m,b_1^\top z_m)\) be the population centered joint-period donor curve.  For fold \(a\in\{1,2\}\), write
\[
 \widehat Y_m^{(a)}=Y_m+U_m^{(a)}+b_m^{(a)}+q_m^{(a)}.
\]
The two \(U_m^{(a)}\)'s are independent, mean-zero martingale averages conditional on the fixed panel, and the overlap and hazard bounds give
\[
 \sup_mE\|U_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-1},\qquad
 \sup_m\|b_m^{(a)}\|_2\le C e^{-c_0k_D},\qquad
 \sup_mE\|q_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-2}.
\]
Expanding the symmetrized cross-fold operator yields
\[
 \widehat\Gamma-\Gamma=\mathbb L_D+E_D,
\]
where \(\mathbb L_D\) is the average of the terms \(Y_m\otimes U_m^{(a)}+U_m^{(a)}\otimes Y_m\).  Donor incoherence, the factor envelope, patient independence, and the martingale isometry imply
\[
 \|\mathbb L_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=O_P(n_D^{-1/2}).
\]
Cross-fold independence gives \(E(U_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)})=0\), and independence across donors gives
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mU_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)}\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le CN_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}.
\]
The same calculation for terms containing \(q_m^{(a)}\), together with the preceding deterministic bias bound, gives
\[
 \|E_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}le C\eta_D(K_D)+O_P(N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}).
\]
Thus cross-folding removes the nonzero same-fold expectation \(N_0^{-1}\sum_mE(U_m\otimes U_m)\), but it does not average away product-limit risk-exhaustion bias.

The population selected spectrum is separated from zero by \(\gamma_-^2\), and the target-loading Gram matrix is bounded below by \(\kappa_-I_d\).  The spectral resolvent identity and
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}
\]
therefore show that, whenever \(\|\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\gamma_-^2/4\) and \(\|\widehat H_0-H_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\kappa_-/2\), the rotation-invariant composite map from the donor means, the selected positive square root, and the target-pre curve to \(s_1+b_1^\top z_\star\) is twice Fr\'echet differentiable.  Its first two derivative norms are bounded by a fixed polynomial in the fixed class constants.  Since \(K_D,N_0,K_T\to\infty\) and \(\eta_D(K_D)\to0\), this regular event has probability tending to one uniformly.  Internal selected-eigenspace ties cause no difficulty because the fitted curve depends on the selected projector and its positive square root, rather than on an individual eigenbasis.

Taylor expansion of the composite map gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =D_T\mathcal F\!\left[K_T^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_{0,\star,j}\right]
 +D_D\mathcal F\!\left[n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\varphi_{p,m,j}\right]
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r.
\]
Define \(\phi_{T,j}=D_T\mathcal F[\varphi_{0,\star,j}]\) and define \(\phi_{D,p,m,j}\) analogously, including the deterministic fold weights.  Patient independence makes the target and donor sums independent and mean zero.  The choice of \(C_\eta\) and the fold-level calculation give
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_\infty\le\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D).
\]
After putting the target-pre and pooled rank-one exponential biases into \(Q_r\), the second-order Taylor formula, the displayed second-moment bounds, and the fallback-event exponential bound imply
\[
 \sup_P E_P\|Q_r\|_\infty^2
 \le C\left[
 \mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\{K_T^{-2}+N_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}+e^{-2c_0K_T}\}
 +n_D^{-2}+e^{-2c_0n_D}
 \right].
\]
The second line in the definition of \(\omega_r\), followed by Markov's inequality, therefore yields, after increasing the fixed \(C_\omega\),
\[
 \sup_P P\{\|Q_r\|_\infty>\omega_r/2\}\le\varepsilon/4.
\]

It remains to justify the random multiplier quantile at the same finite triple.  Divide the first-order process by \(\rho_r\).  Overlap bounds every counting-process integrand, and the nested class \(\{\mathbf 1_{[0,t]}:t\in\mathcal I\}\) has \(L^2\)-covering number at most \(C\delta^{-2}\).  A dyadic-net Lindeberg interpolation, applied separately to the independent target and donor arrays and then to their sum, gives a one-sided coupling between the oracle grid supremum and the empirical-derivative multiplier grid supremum with error
\[
 C\{a_N+\eta_D(K_D)\}.
\]
Here \(a_N\) is the bounded-envelope Lindeberg term, while \(\eta_D\) is the Lipschitz perturbation of the empirical derivative caused by the fold-level drift.  Applying the Lipschitz ramp that equals one below the multiplier critical value and zero one buffer unit above it gives the quantile-transfer inequality
\[
 \sup_P P\!\left\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\frac12\omega_r\right\}
 \le 1-q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}
 +\frac{2C\rho_r\{a_N+\eta_D(K_D)\}}{\omega_r}.
\]
For all sufficiently large triples, \(\varepsilon<\alpha\), so \(q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}=1-\alpha+\varepsilon/2\).  The first line in the definition of \(\omega_r\), with \(C_\omega\) enlarged once more, makes the last ratio at most \(\varepsilon/4\).  Hence the last display is at most \(\alpha-\varepsilon/4\), as claimed.  This explicit enlargement is a quantile-transfer argument; it does not silently assume a uniform anti-concentration constant, which would fail at the variance-zero normalization point \(t=0\) and may fail on easier laws with a degenerate channel.

Finally, \(a_N/\varepsilon\to0\) and \(\eta_D/\varepsilon\to0\) uniformly on three-dimensional tails because \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) grows only as an iterated logarithm.  Moreover,
\[
 \frac{K_T^{-1}}{\rho_r\sqrt\varepsilon}\le\frac{K_T^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon},\qquad
 \frac{n_D^{-1}}{\rho_r\sqrt\varepsilon}\le\frac{n_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon},\qquad
 \frac{N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}}{\rho_r\sqrt\varepsilon}\le\frac{K_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon},
\]
with the first and third terms used only when \(r\ge2\).  Each right-hand side tends to zero, and the exponential terms are smaller still.  Thus \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) uniformly on every three-dimensional tail, without a relative-growth restriction. \(\square\)
