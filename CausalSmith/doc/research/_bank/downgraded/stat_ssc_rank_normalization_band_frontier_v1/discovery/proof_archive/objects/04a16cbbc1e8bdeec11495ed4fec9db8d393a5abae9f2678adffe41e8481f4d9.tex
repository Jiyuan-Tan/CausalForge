\textbf{Proof.}
Put
\[
 c_T=K_T^{-1},
 \qquad
 \bar h=\frac12\min\{h_\circ,R_1,M_\star\}>0,
 \qquad
 q_\alpha=\frac12-\alpha>0,
 \tag{1}
\]
and let \(\widetilde z_m\) denote the unoriented donor scores in the weak-pair construction.  A simultaneous sign change of the factor and all loadings leaves their products unchanged, so the orientation sign \(\varsigma_T\) will be displayed only when it is needed for the canonical sign convention.

First consider an arbitrary \(Q\in\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}(N_0,K_T,K_D)\).  By the definition of this class, its contrast indicator is one and its scalar donor loadings obey
\[
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\zeta_{m,Q}=0,
 \qquad
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\zeta_{m,Q}^2=1.
 \tag{2}
\]
Consequently the two rows of the donor loading matrix
\[
 V_{D,Q}=\bigl[(1,\zeta_{m,Q})^\top:m\in\mathcal D\bigr]
\]
satisfy
\[
 N_0^{-1}V_{D,Q}V_{D,Q}^\top=I_2.
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(V_{D,Q}\) has full row rank two, its columns span \(\mathbb R^2\), and in particular
\[
 (1,\zeta_{\star,Q})^\top
 =V_{D,Q}V_{D,Q}^\top
   (V_{D,Q}V_{D,Q}^\top)^{-1}(1,\zeta_{\star,Q})^\top
 \in\operatorname{span}\{(1,\zeta_{m,Q})^\top:m\in\mathcal D\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The visibility lower bound makes the joint contrast factor nonzero.  Hence every class member has total factor rank two and target loading in the donor span.  By \(\mathrm{lem:han\mbox{-}shah\mbox{-}theorem\mbox{-}one\mbox{-}scope}\), these are precisely the relevant structural factorization-and-span requirements of Han--Shah Theorem~1; no lower pre-period Gram bound is being attributed to that theorem.

We next verify the weak pair.  Direct summation of the centered arithmetic progression gives
\[
 \sum_{m=1}^{N_0}\left\{m-\frac{N_0+1}{2}\right\}=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{m=1}^{N_0}\left\{m-\frac{N_0+1}{2}\right\}^2
 =\frac{N_0(N_0^2-1)}{12}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\widetilde z_m=0,
 \qquad
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\widetilde z_m^2=1.
 \tag{5}
\]
Multiplying both the factors and loadings by \(\varsigma_T\) preserves every survival curve and imposes the sign convention in \(\mathrm{def:rank\mbox{-}at\mbox{-}most\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}closure}\).

For a donor, the pre-period curve equals the witness curve \(\ell_{0,x,0}\) with \(x=c_T\widetilde z_m\), and the post-period curve equals \(\ell_{1,x,0}\) with \(x=\widetilde z_m\).  For the target under \(u\in\{-1,+1\}\), the corresponding coordinates are \(c_Tu\bar h\) and \(u\bar h\).  Since \(c_T\le1\), \(\max_m|\widetilde z_m|\le R_1\), and
\[
 \bar h\le\frac12R_1,
 \qquad
 \bar h\le\frac12M_\star,
 \tag{6}
\]
all these coordinates lie in the range covered by \(\mathrm{ass:explicit\mbox{-}witness\mbox{-}compatibility}\).  That assumption yields, on \(\mathcal I\), survival normalization at zero, values in \([s_-,1]\), monotonicity, the failure-density floor on \(\operatorname{supp}(\psi_\circ')\), the required H\"older hazard bound, and the factor envelope.  Equation~(5) and (6) give the donor and target loading bounds.  The exponential continuation is a survival curve because \(\psi_\circ\), with its smooth zero extension, vanishes at \(\bar\tau\); it agrees under the two signs at and after \(\bar\tau\).  Infinite censoring gives \(G=1\ge g_-\), while consistency, independent censoring, and mutual independence of patient records hold by construction.  These checks prove membership in \(\mathfrak Q_{\leq2}\).

Because the empirical second moment in (5) is one, the invariant joint contrast energy and pre-period Gram are
\[
 \sigma_T
 =\left\{K_T^{-2}a_{\circ,0}^2+a_{\circ,1}^2\right\}^{1/2}
   \|\psi_\circ\|_2,
 \qquad
 H_{0,T}=K_T^{-2}a_{\circ,0}^2\|\psi_\circ\|_2^2.
 \tag{7}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \sigma_T\ge |a_{\circ,1}|\|\psi_\circ\|_2>0,
 \qquad
 H_{0,T}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{8}
\]
The first inequality in (8) is twice the visibility threshold in the definition of \(\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}\).  Combining the preceding law checks with (5)--(8) proves
\[
 \{Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}},Q_{2,+}^{\mathrm{wk}}\}
 \subset\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}(N_0,K_T,K_D).
 \tag{9}
\]

We now compare the pair with the later finite-grid conditions of Han--Shah Theorem~2.  For a fixed finite pre-period grid, write
\[
 a=(e^{-\lambda_\circ t_\ell})_{\ell=1}^{T_0},
 \qquad
 q=(\psi_\circ(t_\ell))_{\ell=1}^{T_0}.
\]
The donor pre-period matrix is
\[
 M_{0,T}=a\mathbf1^\top+c_Ta_{\circ,0}q\widetilde z^\top.
 \tag{10}
\]
The right vectors in (10) are orthogonal by (5).  If \(a\) and \(q\) are linearly independent, then \(M_{0,T}\) has rank two.  Evaluating its operator norm on \(N_0^{-1/2}\mathbf1\), and bounding its second singular value by the norm of the second rank-one summand, gives
\[
 s_1(M_{0,T})\ge\sqrt{N_0}\|a\|_2,
 \qquad
 s_2(M_{0,T})\le c_T|a_{\circ,0}|\sqrt{N_0}\|q\|_2.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus
\[
 \frac{s_1(M_{0,T})}{s_2(M_{0,T})}
 \ge
 K_T\frac{\|a\|_2}{|a_{\circ,0}|\|q\|_2},
 \tag{12}
\]
which is a fixed positive multiple of \(K_T\); hence the uniform conditioning required by Han--Shah Assumption~7 fails eventually.

If \(q=\kappa a\ne0\), all pre-period latent time vectors are multiples of
\[
 (1,\varsigma_Tc_Ta_{\circ,0}\kappa)^\top,
 \tag{13}
\]
whereas the corresponding post-period vectors are multiples of
\[
 (1,\varsigma_Ta_{\circ,1}\kappa)^\top.
 \tag{14}
\]
Since \(a_{\circ,1}\ne0\) and \(c_Ta_{\circ,0}\to0\), the directions in (13)--(14) differ for every sufficiently large \(K_T\).  The post-period latent span is therefore not contained in the pre-period latent span, so Assumption~8 fails eventually.  Finally, if \(q=0\), the grid sees none of the perturbation, although \(\psi_\circ\) is nonzero at some time in the continuous interval and the two target curves remain separated there.  Thus even if Assumptions~7--8 happen to hold on that grid, the finite-grid conclusion does not control this continuous-time separation.  These three cases are exhaustive and show that the lower pair does not contradict Han--Shah Theorem~2.

It remains to establish the information bound.  All observed donor cells have the same distribution under the two signs, and the target-post cell is unobserved.  Hence the observed laws differ only in the \(K_T\) independent target-pre failure times.  The two causal target curves are
\[
 \theta_u(t)=e^{-\lambda_\circ t}+u\bar h a_{\circ,1}\psi_\circ(t),
 \qquad u\in\{-1,+1\},
\]
and consequently
\[
 \Delta_\star:=\|\theta_+-\theta_-\|_\infty
 =2\bar h|a_{\circ,1}|\|\psi_\circ\|_\infty>0.
 \tag{15}
\]
On \([0,\bar\tau]\), the density of one target-pre failure time is
\[
 f_u(t)=\lambda_\circ e^{-\lambda_\circ t}
        -u c_Ta_{\circ,0}\bar h\psi_\circ'(t).
 \tag{16}
\]
Outside \(\operatorname{supp}(\psi_\circ')\), and also after \(\bar\tau\), the two densities agree.  On that support the witness condition gives \(f_-\ge c_f\).  Using \(\log x\le x-1\) and equality of the total masses,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f_+\|f_-)
 &\le \int\frac{(f_+-f_-)^2}{f_-}\,dt \\
 &\le
 \frac{4c_T^2a_{\circ,0}^2\bar h^2}{c_f}
 \int_0^{\bar\tau}\{\psi_\circ'(t)\}^2\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Patient independence and additivity of relative entropy for product laws imply, for a fixed constant \(C_0<\infty\),
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_{2,+}^{\mathrm{wk}}\|Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}})
 \le \frac{C_0}{K_T}.
 \tag{18}
\]
The Pinsker inequality in \(\mathrm{lem:pinsker\mbox{-}kl\mbox{-}tv}\) now yields
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_{2,+}^{\mathrm{wk}},Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}})
 \le\left(\frac{C_0}{2K_T}\right)^{1/2}
 =O(K_T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{19}
\]

Let \(B\) be any continuous band satisfying the honesty constraint in the theorem, and let \(E_u\) be the event that \(B\) contains \(\theta_u\) simultaneously on \(\mathcal I\).  Honesty, the union bound, and total-variation transfer give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}}(E_-\cap E_+)
 &\ge Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}}(E_-)
      +Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}}(E_+)-1 \\
 &\ge 1-2\alpha-2\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)
      -\operatorname{TV}(Q_{2,+}^{\mathrm{wk}},Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
By definition, \(\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\le q_\alpha/4\).  Equation~(19) shows that there is a fixed \(a_0\ge3\), independent of \(N_0,K_T,K_D\), such that the total-variation term is at most \(q_\alpha/2\) whenever \(K_T\ge a_0\).  Because \(1-2\alpha=2q_\alpha\), (20) is then at least \(q_\alpha\).  On \(E_-\cap E_+\), the band contains both target curves at every time and therefore \(w(B)\ge\Delta_\star\).  It follows from (9) that
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}}E_Qw(B)
 \ge E_{Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}}}w(B)
 \ge q_\alpha\Delta_\star=:c_\star>0.
 \tag{21}
\]
Taking the infimum over all honest bands proves the lower bound.  The deterministic continuous band with lower endpoint zero and upper endpoint one covers every survival curve with probability one and has width one, proving the upper bound.

Thus the minimax expected supremum width has order one even though the donor contrast is visibly nonzero, its rank is known to be two, the donor loading matrix has full row rank, and the target loading lies in the donor span.  The conclusion is the generic information-theoretic implication that donor-rank visibility, or a donor-only retained-rank decision, does not by itself license a shrinking honest band: uniform target-relevant pre-period strength is additionally necessary.  It does not assert that any cited PCR, rank-selection, or prediction procedure makes such an honest-band claim.  The positive-pre-Gram functional-PCR upper bound over the smaller class \(\mathfrak P_2\) therefore does not transfer to \(\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}\). \(\square\)
