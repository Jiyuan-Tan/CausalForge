\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\).  By the hazard definition,
\[
 \theta(t)=S^0_{1,\star}(t)
 =\exp\left\{-\int_0^t\lambda^0_{1,\star}(u)\,du\right\}.
\]
The bound \(\|\lambda^0_{1,\star}\|_{C^\beta(\mathcal I)}\le L\), the fixed horizon, and the smoothness of the exponential map imply
\[
 \|\theta\|_{C^{\beta+1}(\mathcal I)}\le C(L,\bar\tau,\beta).
\]
This follows directly by differentiating once, \(\theta'=-\lambda^0_{1,\star}\theta\), and applying the product Hölder inequality; when \(\beta>1\), repeat the same identity for the integer derivatives and use the Hölder bound on the final derivative.

Let \(I_{\mathcal G}\theta\) denote the piecewise-linear interpolant of the grid values of \(\theta\), and put \(q=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\).  On a grid interval \([a,b]\) of length at most \(h=h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\), the first-order Taylor formula with Hölder remainder when \(q<2\), and the second-order Taylor formula when \(q=2\), give
\[
 \sup_{t\in[a,b]}|I_{\mathcal G}\theta(t)-\theta(t)|
 \le C(L,\bar\tau,\beta)|b-a|^q.
\]
Taking the maximum over intervals yields
\[
 \|I_{\mathcal G}\theta-\theta\|_\infty\le c_{\mathrm{int}}h^q.
\]

Because \(\widehat\theta_r\) is itself the linear interpolant of its grid values, for every \(t\) between adjacent grid points,
\[
 |\widehat\theta_r(t)-\theta(t)|
 \le \max_{g\in\mathcal G}|\widehat\theta_r(g)-\theta(g)|
 +|I_{\mathcal G}\theta(t)-\theta(t)|.
\]
The reverse inequality between the grid maximum and the continuous supremum is immediate because \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal I\).  These two inequalities prove the displayed bound.  Finally, the corrected mesh condition is \(h^q\le\rho_r\), hence \(c_{\mathrm{int}}h^q=O(\rho_r)\). \(\square\)
