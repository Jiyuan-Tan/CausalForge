\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(z_m=z_m^{(N_0,1)}\) for the oriented scores in \(\mathrm{def:weak\mbox{-}pre\mbox{-}gram\mbox{-}rank\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}pair}\), let \(\widetilde z_m\) denote their unoriented versions, put \(c_T=K_T^{-1}\), and set \(q_\alpha=1/2-\alpha>0\).

\emph{Step 1: the donor-visible class is genuinely known rank two.}
If \(Q\in\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}\), its defining conditions give \(e_Q=1\) and a nonzero scalar joint factor.  The normalization inherited from \(\mathfrak Q_{\le2}\) is
\[
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\zeta_{m,Q}=0,
 \qquad
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\zeta_{m,Q}^2=1.       \tag{1}
\]
Taking \(d=1\) in \(\mathrm{prop:han\mbox{-}shah\mbox{-}structural\mbox{-}embedding}\), (1) shows that the two-row donor loading matrix with columns \((1,\zeta_{m,Q})^\top\) has full row rank and that \((1,\zeta_{\star,Q})^\top\) lies in its column span.  Endpoint normalization and (1) imply \(s_{p,Q}(0)=1\) and \(b_{p,Q}(0)=0\), while donor visibility implies that \(b_{p,Q}(t)\ne0\) for some \((p,t)\).  Hence the two latent factor coordinates span two dimensions.  This is exact total factor rank two.  The cited scope statement \(\mathrm{lem:han\mbox{-}shah\mbox{-}theorem\mbox{-}one\mbox{-}scope}\) then places these fixed-design laws inside the factorization-and-donor-span model of Han--Shah Theorem~1.  Notice that this argument uses neither a common pre-Gram lower bound nor the particular lower witness.

\emph{Step 2: membership and invariant geometry of the weak pair.}
The centered arithmetic progression satisfies
\[
 \frac1{N_0}\sum_{m=1}^{N_0}\widetilde z_m=0,
 \qquad
 \frac1{N_0}\sum_{m=1}^{N_0}\widetilde z_m^2=1,       \tag{2}
\]
because \(N_0^{-1}\sum_m\{m-(N_0+1)/2\}^2=(N_0^2-1)/12\).  Multiplication by \(\varsigma_T\) preserves (2), and the definition of \(\varsigma_T\) makes the factor satisfy the canonical sign rule of \(\mathfrak Q_{\le2}\).

For a donor pre-period curve, the heterogeneous term before the harmless simultaneous sign change is \(a_{\circ,0}\psi_\circ x\) with \(x=c_T\widetilde z_m\); for the target it has \(x=c_Tu\bar h\).  In the post period the corresponding choices are \(x=\widetilde z_m\) and \(x=u\bar h\).  For all sufficiently large triples, \(c_T\le1\), and the definitions of \(R_1\) and \(\bar h\) give \(|x|\le R_1\) in every case.  The four curve inequalities in \(\mathrm{ass:explicit\mbox{-}witness\mbox{-}compatibility}\) therefore give endpoint normalization, monotonicity, event overlap, the failure-density floor on \(\operatorname{supp}(\psi_\circ')\), and the uniform \(C^\beta\) hazard bound.  Infinite censoring gives \(G\equiv1\), which satisfies censor overlap because \(g_-\le1\).  Patient independence, independent censoring, and control consistency hold by construction.

The donor and target loading bounds follow from (2), \(R_1\le M_z\), and \(\bar h\le M_\star\).  The factor envelope and the two upper Gram bounds follow from \(c_T\le1\) and the corresponding compatibility inequalities.  Thus both laws are in \(\mathfrak Q_{\le2}\).  Since the donor scores have empirical second moment one, their unique nonzero joint contrast singular value and their pre-period Gram are
\[
 \sigma_T
 =\left\{\int_{\mathcal I}(b_{0,T}^2+b_{1,T}^2)\right\}^{1/2}
 =\{K_T^{-2}a_{\circ,0}^2+a_{\circ,1}^2\}^{1/2}\|\psi_\circ\|_2,
 \qquad
 H_{0,T}=K_T^{-2}a_{\circ,0}^2\|\psi_\circ\|_2^2.     \tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \sigma_T\ge |a_{\circ,1}|\|\psi_\circ\|_2
 \ge\frac12|a_{\circ,1}|\|\psi_\circ\|_2,            \tag{4}
\]
so the pair is a subset of \(\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}\), while \(H_{0,T}\to0\).  Equations (1)--(4) and Step~1 also prove exact rank, full donor loading rank, and donor-span inclusion for the pair.

\emph{Step 3: the complete finite-grid audit.}
Fix a finite pre-period grid \(\mathcal T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_{T_0}\}\), and write
\[
 a=(e^{-\lambda_\circ t_\ell})_{\ell=1}^{T_0},
 \qquad q=(\psi_\circ(t_\ell))_{\ell=1}^{T_0}.
\]
The simultaneous orientation of factor and loadings cancels in their product, so the donor survival matrix is
\[
 M_{0,T}=a\mathbf1^\top+c_Ta_{\circ,0}q\widetilde z^\top. \tag{5}
\]
Because \(\mathbf1^\top\widetilde z=0\), (5) has rank two exactly when \(a\) and \(q\) are linearly independent.  In that case, with \(s_1\ge s_2>0\) its two nonzero singular values,
\[
 s_1(M_{0,T})
 \ge\|M_{0,T}\mathbf1/\sqrt{N_0}\|_2
 =\sqrt{N_0}\|a\|_2,
 \qquad
 s_2(M_{0,T})
 \le c_T|a_{\circ,0}|\sqrt{N_0}\|q\|_2.              \tag{6}
\]
The second inequality is the rank-one approximation bound obtained by deleting the second term in (5).  Since
\(
 \|a\|_2\ge e^{-\lambda_\circ\bar\tau}\sqrt{T_0}
\)
and
\(
 \|q\|_2\le\|\psi_\circ\|_\infty\sqrt{T_0},
\)
the condition number based on the smallest nonzero singular value obeys
\[
 \operatorname{cond}(M_{0,T})
 =\frac{s_1(M_{0,T})}{s_2(M_{0,T})}
 \ge
 \frac{e^{-\lambda_\circ\bar\tau}}
 {|a_{\circ,0}|\|\psi_\circ\|_\infty}K_T.             \tag{7}
\]
Thus Han--Shah Assumption~7 fails in this case for all sufficiently large \(K_T\).

Suppose next that \(q=\kappa a\ne0\).  The pre-period factor vectors on the grid are all multiples of
\(
 (1,\varsigma_Tc_Ta_{\circ,0}\kappa)^\top,
\)
so their span is one-dimensional.  The corresponding post-period vectors are multiples of
\(
 (1,\varsigma_Ta_{\circ,1}\kappa)^\top.
\)
Because \(a_{\circ,1}\ne0\) and \(c_Ta_{\circ,0}\to0\), these two directions differ for every sufficiently large \(K_T\).  Han--Shah Assumption~8 therefore fails.  Finally, if \(q=0\), then (5) has rank one and its ratio of largest to smallest nonzero singular value is one; moreover the pre- and post-factor vectors coincide on the grid, so Assumptions~7--8 may hold.  But every grid value of the heterogeneous perturbation is then zero.  Han--Shah Theorem~2 is a finite-grid conclusion and does not address the nonzero continuous-time separation at off-grid points.  These three cases are exhaustive and establish the claimed published-model boundary without treating a rank-one matrix as having infinite condition number.

\emph{Step 4: fixed target separation and full-law likelihood distance.}
All donor laws are identical under the two signs, and no target-post cohort is observed.  Hence the observed laws differ only through the \(K_T\) target-pre failure times.  Their causal targets are
\[
 \theta_u(t)=e^{-\lambda_\circ t}+u\bar h a_{\circ,1}\psi_\circ(t),
\]
and therefore
\[
 \Delta_\star:=\|\theta_+-\theta_-\|_\infty
 =2\bar h|a_{\circ,1}|\|\psi_\circ\|_\infty>0.        \tag{8}
\]
For one target-pre patient, the failure density on \((0,\bar\tau)\) is
\[
 f_u(t)=\lambda_\circ e^{-\lambda_\circ t}
       -u c_Ta_{\circ,0}\bar h\psi_\circ'(t).          \tag{9}
\]
The smooth zero extension gives \(\psi_\circ(0)=\psi_\circ(\bar\tau)=0\) and \(\psi_\circ'(0)=\psi_\circ'(\bar\tau)=0\).  Thus the endpoint survivals agree, there is no unmatched endpoint or tail atom, and the prescribed exponential continuations have identical densities after \(\bar\tau\).  Consequently (9) accounts for the entire difference between the two one-patient laws.  The compatibility density floor gives \(f_-(t)\ge c_f\) wherever \(f_+(t)-f_-(t)\ne0\).  Since the two full densities have equal total mass, \(\log x\le x-1\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f_+\|f_-)
 &\le\int\frac{\{f_+(t)-f_-(t)\}^2}{f_-(t)}\,dt\\
 &\le\frac{4c_T^2a_{\circ,0}^2\bar h^2}{c_f}
       \int_0^{\bar\tau}\{\psi_\circ'(t)\}^2\,dt.
 \end{aligned}                                          \tag{10}
\]
Independence and additivity of relative entropy over the \(K_T\) target-pre patients give a fixed \(C_0<\infty\) such that
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_{2,+}^{\mathrm{wk}}\|Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}})
 \le C_0K_Tc_T^2=C_0K_T^{-1}.                           \tag{11}
\]
The cited Pinsker inequality \(\mathrm{lem:pinsker\mbox{-}kl\mbox{-}tv}\) now gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_{2,+}^{\mathrm{wk}},Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}})
 \le\{C_0/(2K_T)\}^{1/2}.                              \tag{12}
\]
Increase \(a_0\) so that (12) is at most \(q_\alpha/2\) whenever \(K_T\ge a_0\), and so all membership and large-\(K_T\) statements above hold whenever the minimum sample size is at least \(a_0\).

\emph{Step 5: honesty forces constant width.}
For \(u\in\{-1,+1\}\), let
\[
 E_u=\{B^{\mathrm{lo}}(t)\le\theta_u(t)\le B^{\mathrm{up}}(t)
       \text{ for every }t\in\mathcal I\}.
\]
If \(B\) is honest over \(\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}\) at the evaluated triple, pair inclusion from (4) gives
\(
 Q_{2,u}^{\mathrm{wk}}(E_u)\ge1-\alpha-\varepsilon,
\)
where \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\).  Total-variation transfer of \(E_+\) to \(Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}}\), followed by the union bound, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}}(E_-\cap E_+)
 &\ge1-2\alpha-2\varepsilon
      -\operatorname{TV}(Q_{2,+}^{\mathrm{wk}},Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}})\\
 &\ge2q_\alpha-q_\alpha/2-q_\alpha/2=q_\alpha.        \tag{13}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\varepsilon\le q_\alpha/4\) by its definition and (12) supplies the other error term.  On \(E_-\cap E_+\), the same band contains both target curves, so (8) gives \(w(B)\ge\Delta_\star\).  Therefore every honest \(B\) satisfies
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}}E_Qw(B)
 \ge E_{Q_{2,-}^{\mathrm{wk}}}w(B)
 \ge q_\alpha\Delta_\star=:c_\star>0.                 \tag{14}
\]
Taking the infimum proves the lower bound.  Conversely, the deterministic band with endpoints zero and one covers every survival curve and has width one, proving the matching upper bound.  The constants depend only on the fixed primitive constants and \(\alpha\), so the assertion is uniform over all sufficiently large triples without a relative-growth condition. \(\square\)
