\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(m=n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\), \(h=h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\), and let \(Z_r=\sum_iL_i\) be the patient decomposition furnished by the pooled lemma when \(r=1\) and by the stabilized transfer lemma when \(r\ge2\). The deterministic patient envelopes in those constructions give
\[
 \|L_i\|_\infty\le
 \begin{cases}
 Cn_D^{-1},&r=1,\\
 CK_T^{-1},&i\text{ is target-pre},\\
 Cn_D^{-1},&i\text{ is a donor patient}.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{1}
\]
The same bounds hold for the observed multiplier coefficients on every regular branch. The pooled fallback has the donor bound except on the aggregate exhaustion event, whose probability is exponentially small by the risk-set argument in the pooled and stabilized lemmas.

We first compare covariance kernels. In rank one, the explicit formulas in the pooled product-limit calculation are
\[
 \Sigma_1(s,t)=\frac{s_1(s)s_1(t)}{n_D}
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{\lambda_1(u)}{H_D(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_1(s,t)=\widehat S_D(s-)widehat S_D(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)^2}\,dN(u).          \tag{2}
\]
On \(\{\inf_{u\le\bar\tau}Y(u)\ge n_Dh_0/2\}\), substitute \(dN=dM+Y\lambda_1du\) in (2). Predictable isometry, the maximal inequality, and the time-ordered empirical-risk bound give
\[
 E\sup_v\left|
 \int_0^v\frac{n_D}{Y(u)^2}\,dN(u)
 -\int_0^v\frac{\lambda_1(u)}{H_D(u)}\,du
 \right|\le Cn_D^{-1/2}.                                  \tag{3}
\]
The complement has probability at most \(Ce^{-cn_D}\), and the pooled survival error has squared supremum expectation at most \(Cn_D^{-1}\). Hence
\[
 E\{\rho_1^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\|_\infty\}
 \le Cn_D^{-1/2}=Ca_{1,n}.                                \tag{4}
\]

Now let \(r\ge2\). On the aggregate regular event, write the coordinate-free prediction map as \(F\) and its population derivative as \(DF\). The contour formula for the selected projector, the Sylvester equation for the positive square root, and
\(A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}\) imply
\[
 \|D\widehat F-DF\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C\{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)\}              \tag{5}
\]
in mean, after truncation at the fixed spectral and pre-Gram thresholds. For a stabilized cell of size \(k\), predictable isometry applied to its clipped risk denominator gives
\[
 E\sup_{s,t}\left|\widehat V_k(s,t)-V_k(s,t)\right|
 \le Ck^{-1/2},                                           \tag{6}
\]
where \(V_k\) and \(\widehat V_k\) are the oracle and observed covariance kernels after their natural \(k\)-normalization. Equation (6) follows by writing the difference as the martingale term with integrand \(k/Y_k^{\dagger 2}\) plus the Lipschitz inverse-risk term; clipping makes both integrands bounded on every dataset. The deterministic stabilized bias contributes \(C\eta_D(K_D)\), while target and aggregate fallback events contribute \(Ce^{-c_0K_T/2}+Ce^{-c_0n_D/2}\). Composing (5)--(6), using (1), and summing the independent target and donor covariance blocks yields
\[
 E\{\rho_r^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty\}
 \le Ca_{r,n}.                                            \tag{7}
\]
Markov's inequality applied to (4) or (7), together with the exponentially small aggregate fallback event, gives a measurable event \(\mathcal E_{r,n}^{(1)}\) such that
\[
 \sup_PP\{(\mathcal E_{r,n}^{(1)})^c\}\le Ca_{r,n}^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \le C\rho_r^2a_{r,n}^{1/2}                              \tag{8}
\]
on that event.

It remains to control time increments of the realized derivative. For an interval \(J=(g,g']\) with grid endpoints, let \(N_T(J)\) and \(N_D(J)\) count observed failures in the target-pre and pooled donor blocks, respectively; omit \(N_T\) at rank one. Hazard smoothness implies
\[
 EN_T(J)\le LK_T|J|,
 \qquad EN_D(J)\le Ln_D|J|.                               \tag{9}
\]
Bernstein's exponential-moment argument for sums of independent Bernoulli variables, followed by a union bound over at most \(|\mathcal G|^2\) intervals, gives an event \(\mathcal E_{r,n}^{(2)}\) on which
\[
 N_T(J)\le C K_T(|J|+h),
 \qquad N_D(J)\le C n_D(|J|+h)                            \tag{10}
\]
for every grid interval, and
\[
 \sup_PP\{(\mathcal E_{r,n}^{(2)})^c\}
 \le C|\mathcal G|^2e^{-cmh}.                             \tag{11}
\]
Here \(mh\to\infty\) polynomially because \(h\asymp\rho_r^{1/q_\beta}\), \(q_\beta>3/2\), and \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\). Thus (11) is bounded by \(Ca_{r,n}^{1/2}\) on every sufficiently large tail.

The derivative increment between \(g\) and \(g'\) is the sum of a Lipschitz coefficient change and the patient failures in \(J\). Equations (1) and (10), Gaussian isometry, and \(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\le\rho_r^2\) give, for distinct grid points,
\[
 \widehat d_r(g,g')^2
 \le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|^2
 +C\{K_T^{-2}N_T(J)+n_D^{-2}N_D(J)\}
 \le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|.                                    \tag{12}
\]
The last step uses \(|g-g'|\ge h\). Taking expectations in the same calculation gives the oracle bound \(d_r(g,g')^2\le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\). Intersecting the two events in (8) and (11) proves the covariance and semimetric assertions.

Finally, for fixed \(g,g'\), the independent centered increment \(Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')\) has variance at most \(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\) and summand envelope at most \(C\rho_r m^{-1/2}\) by (1) and \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\). The exponential Markov proof of Bernstein's inequality therefore gives
\[
 P\{|Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')|>x\}
 \le2\exp\left[-c\min\left\{
 \frac{x^2}{\rho_r^2|g-g'|},
 \frac{x\sqrt m}{\rho_r}\right\}\right].               \tag{13}
\]
Set
\[
 x=C\rho_r\left[\{u\log(e p_{\mathcal G})\}^{1/2}
 +m^{-1/2}\log(e p_{\mathcal G})\right].
\]
For \(|g-g'|\le u\), (13) is at most \(2p_{\mathcal G}^{-6}\) after enlarging \(C\). A union bound over at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^2\) signed pairs proves the displayed modulus probability. No variance was inverted at any step. \(\square\)
