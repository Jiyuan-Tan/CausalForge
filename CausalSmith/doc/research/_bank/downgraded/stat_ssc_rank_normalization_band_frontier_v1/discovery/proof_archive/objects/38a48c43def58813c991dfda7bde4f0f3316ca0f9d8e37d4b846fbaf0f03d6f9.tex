\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(k_D=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\), \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)\), and \(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\). Define
\[
 a_T(K_T)=K_T^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T),
 \qquad
 a_D(n_D)=n_D^{-1/6}\log^2(e+n_D),
\]
\[
 \chi_r=\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)
       +n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)+\rho_r\eta_D(K_D),
\]
and
\[
 v_r=\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}
 \{K_T^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0K_T}\}
 +n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_0n_D}.
\]
By definition,
\[
 \omega_r=C_\omega\{\varepsilon^{-1}\chi_r+\varepsilon^{-1/2}v_r\}. \tag{1}
\]

Let \(\widetilde S^0=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0\) and let \(\theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}\) be the interpolated population comparison in \(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\). By \(\mathrm{lem:interpolated\mbox{-}population\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}stability}\),
\[
 \|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C h^{q_\beta},
 \qquad
 \lambda_d(\widetilde\Gamma)\ge\gamma_-^2/2,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(\widetilde H_0)\ge\kappa_-/2, \tag{2}
\]
for all sufficiently large triples, and
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g)
 \quad\text{for every }g\in\mathcal G. \tag{3}
\]
The mesh condition gives \(h^{q_\beta}\le\rho_r\to0\), so the point from which the Taylor expansion is taken lies in a fixed regular neighborhood uniformly over the model class. Equation (2), rather than gridwise equality alone, is what controls the internal \(L^2\) factor map.

Apply the corrected interpolation-aware decomposition in \(\mathrm{lem:kaplan\mbox{-}meier\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}exhaustion}\) to every full donor cell, both deterministic donor folds, and the target-pre cell. In a cell whose population survival is \(S^0\), that lemma writes
\[
 \widehat S^{\mathrm{KM}}-S^0
 =k^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_j+b_k+q_k+
   (\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0-S^0).
\]
Since \(\widehat S^{\mathrm{KM}}\) and the first three terms are interpolated by the same operator, subtraction of \(\widetilde S^0=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0\) gives the exact identity
\[
 \widehat S^{\mathrm{KM}}-\widetilde S^0
 =k^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_j+b_k+q_k
 \quad\hbox{in }\ell^\infty(\mathcal I). \tag{4}
\]
Thus the interpolation term is removed before construction of the \(L^2\) covariance operator, not after restriction to the grid.

The exact smaller-fold bias bound is
\[
 C_0e^{-c_0k_D}=C_0\exp\{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor\}. \tag{5}
\]
Choose the fixed \(C_\eta\) in \(\eta_D\) larger than \(C_0\) times the uniform first- and second-derivative envelope obtained below. The full-cell donor means, centered at \(\widetilde s_p\), then obey
\[
 \widehat s_p-\widetilde s_p
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}\varphi^s_{p,m,j}+b^s_p+q^s_p,
 \quad
 \|b^s_p\|_infty\le\eta_D(K_D),
 \quad
 E\|q^s_p\|_infty^2\le Cn_D^{-1}K_D^{-2}. \tag{6}
\]
The target-pre curve has the analogous expansion with deterministic bias at most \(Ce^{-c_0K_T}\) and centered second-order remainder with squared supremum norm at most \(CK_T^{-2}\).

For each donor fold write
\[
 \widehat Y_m^{(a)}=\widetilde Y_m+U_m^{(a)}+b_m^{(a)}+q_m^{(a)},
 \qquad a\in\{1,2\}. \tag{7}
\]
The two \(U_m^{(a)}\)'s are independent mean-zero martingale averages conditional on the fixed panel. Censor overlap, event overlap, hazard boundedness, and (4) yield
\[
 \sup_mE\|U_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-1},
 \quad
 \sup_m\|b_m^{(a)}\|_2\le Ce^{-c_0k_D},
 \quad
 \sup_mE\|q_m^{(a)}\|_2^2\le CK_D^{-2}. \tag{8}
\]
Expanding the symmetrized cross-fold operator about \(\widetilde\Gamma\) gives
\[
 \widehat\Gamma-\widetilde\Gamma=\mathbb L_D+E_D, \tag{9}
\]
where \(\mathbb L_D\) is the average of the terms \(\widetilde Y_m\otimes U_m^{(a)}+U_m^{(a)}\otimes\widetilde Y_m\). The interpolated stability lemma bounds \(\sup_m\|\widetilde Y_m\|_2\), and patient independence plus the martingale isometry imply
\[
 \|\mathbb L_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=O_P(n_D^{-1/2}). \tag{10}
\]
Cross-fold independence gives \(E(U_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)})=0\), and independence across donors gives
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mU_m^{(1)}\otimes U_m^{(2)}\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le CN_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}. \tag{11}
\]
The same calculation for terms containing \(q_m^{(a)}\), together with (5), gives
\[
 \|E_D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le C\eta_D(K_D)+O_P(N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}). \tag{12}
\]
Thus cross-folding removes the same-fold errors-in-variables expectation but does not average away product-limit risk-exhaustion bias.

Use the coordinate-free representation of the fitted curve through the selected projector, selected positive square-root, and pre-period Gram inverse. The spectral contour identity used in the stability lemma and
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1} \tag{13}
\]
show from (2) that this map is twice Fr\'echet differentiable throughout a fixed neighborhood of the interpolated population panel. Its first two derivative norms are bounded by a polynomial in the fixed class constants. Internal selected-eigenspace ties cause no difficulty because the composite prediction is rotation invariant. Equations (6)--(12), together with \(N_0,K_T,K_D\to\infty\) and \(\eta_D(K_D)\to0\), show that the empirical regular event has probability tending to one uniformly; the definition supplies a total pooled fallback on its complement.

Taylor expansion of this map at \(\widetilde S^0\), followed by (3), gives in \(\ell^\infty(\mathcal G)\)
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =D_T\mathcal F_{\mathcal G,\widetilde S}
   \left[K_T^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_{0,\star,j}\right]
 +D_D\mathcal F_{\mathcal G,\widetilde S}
   \left[n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\varphi_{p,m,j}\right]
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r. \tag{14}
\]
Define \(\phi_{T,j}\) and \(\phi_{D,p,m,j}\) by the two displayed derivatives, including the deterministic fold weights. Patient independence makes the target and donor sums independent and mean zero. At rank one, normalization collapse and the explicit pooled branch give \(D_T\mathcal F_{\mathcal G,\widetilde S}=0\), so \(\phi_{T,j}\equiv0\). The choice of \(C_\eta\), (5)--(12), and the bounded derivative envelope give
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D). \tag{15}
\]
The second-order Taylor formula, (6), (8), (11), and the exponentially small fallback contribution imply
\[
 \sup_P E_P\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\left[
 \mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\{K_T^{-2}+N_0^{-1}K_D^{-2}+e^{-2c_0K_T}\}
 +n_D^{-2}+e^{-2c_0n_D}
 \right]
 \le Cv_r^2. \tag{16}
\]
By (1), \(\omega_r\ge C_\omega v_r/\sqrt\varepsilon\). Markov's inequality and a sufficiently large fixed \(C_\omega\) therefore yield
\[
 \sup_P P\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le\varepsilon/4. \tag{17}
\]

It remains to transfer the multiplier quantile at the same finite triple. Divide each channel by its own standard-error scale before discretizing the nested counting-process class. Its \(L^2\)-covering number is at most \(C\delta^{-2}\), and censor overlap bounds every integrand. A dyadic-net Lindeberg replacement of one patient at a time gives absolute Lipschitz-test errors at most \(CK_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)\) in the target array and \(Cn_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)\) in the donor array. The empirical derivative differs from the oracle derivative at \(\widetilde S^0\) by at most \(C\eta_D(K_D)\) after the stochastic derivative error is included in (16). Since the first-order process has scale \(\rho_r\), independence and the triangle inequality give one-sided Lipschitz comparison error at most
\[
 C\chi_r=C\left\{\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)
 +n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)+\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)\right\}. \tag{18}
\]
Apply the ramp that equals one below the conditional multiplier critical value and zero one buffer unit above it. By the definition of the conditional quantile and (18),
\[
 \sup_P P\!\left\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\frac12\omega_r\right\}
 \le1-q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}+\frac{2C\chi_r}{\omega_r}. \tag{19}
\]
For every sufficiently large triple, \(\varepsilon<\alpha\), so \(q_{N_0,K_T,K_D}=1-\alpha+\varepsilon/2\). Increasing \(C_\omega\) makes the last ratio in (19) at most \(\varepsilon/4\), and the right side is then at most \(\alpha-\varepsilon/4\). This smoothing argument does not assume a uniform anti-concentration constant, which would fail at the variance-zero normalization point \(t=0\) and on laws with a degenerate channel.

Finally, \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) grows no faster than an iterated logarithm of \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\). Channel by channel,
\[
 \frac{K_T^{-1/2}a_T(K_T)/\varepsilon}{K_T^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_T^{-1/6}\log^2(e+K_T)}{\varepsilon}\to0,
 \qquad
 \frac{n_D^{-1/2}a_D(n_D)/\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{n_D^{-1/6}\log^2(e+n_D)}{\varepsilon}\to0, \tag{20}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{K_T^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{K_T^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_T^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0,
 \quad
 \frac{N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{K_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0,
 \quad
 \frac{n_D^{-1}/\sqrt\varepsilon}{n_D^{-1/2}}
 =\frac{n_D^{-1/2}}{\sqrt\varepsilon}\to0. \tag{21}
\]
The exponential terms divided by their corresponding standard-error channels tend to zero, and
\[
 \frac{\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)/\varepsilon}{\rho_r}
 =\frac{C_\eta e^{-c_0\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor}}{\varepsilon}\to0. \tag{22}
\]
The convergence in (20)--(22) is uniform on every three-dimensional tail because \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\le K_T\) and \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\le n_D\). Equations (1) and (20)--(22) prove \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) without comparing \(K_T\) and \(n_D\). Equations (14)--(19) prove the remaining assertions. The off-grid difference between \(S^0\) and \(\widetilde S^0\) never enters (14); it is carried only by the deterministic interpolation allowance in the band. \(\square\)
