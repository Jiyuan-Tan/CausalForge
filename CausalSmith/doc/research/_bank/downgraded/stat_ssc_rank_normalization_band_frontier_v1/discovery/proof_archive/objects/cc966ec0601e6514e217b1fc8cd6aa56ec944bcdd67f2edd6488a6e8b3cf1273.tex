Let \(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\), let \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) be piecewise-linear interpolation on \(\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\), and define \(\widetilde S^0\), \(\widetilde s\), \(\widetilde Y_m\), and \(\widetilde\Gamma\) as in \(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\). Put \(Y_m=(S^0_{0,m}-s_0,S^0_{1,m}-s_1)\) and \(\Gamma=N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}Y_m\otimes Y_m\). There is a constant \(C_*<\infty\), depending only on the fixed model constants and \(r\), such that
\[
 \widetilde S^0_{p,m}(g)=S^0_{p,m}(g)\quad(g\in\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}),
 \qquad
 \max_{p,m}\|\widetilde S^0_{p,m}-S^0_{p,m}\|_\infty
 \le C_*h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{q_\beta},
\]
and
\[
 \|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C_*h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{q_\beta}.
\]
When \(d>0\), for every sufficiently fine grid the operator \(\widetilde\Gamma\) has exactly \(d\) positive eigenvalues, its \(d\)-th eigenvalue is at least \(\gamma_-^2/2\), and its selected projector \(\widetilde\Pi\) satisfies
\[
 \|\widetilde\Pi-\Pi\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C_*h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{q_\beta}.
\]
If \(\widetilde b_p\) denotes any selected positive-square-root factor coordinates of \(\widetilde\Gamma\), there is an orthogonal matrix \(R_{\mathcal G}\) such that
\[
 \max_{p\in\{0,1\}}\|\widetilde b_p-R_{\mathcal G}^{\top}b_p\|_{L^2}
 \le C_*h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{q_\beta}.
\]
Consequently, with \(H_0=\int_{\mathcal I}b_0b_0^\top\) and \(\widetilde H_0=\int_{\mathcal I}\widetilde b_0\widetilde b_0^\top\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widetilde H_0)\ge\kappa_-/2,
 \qquad
 \|\widetilde H_0^{-1}-R_{\mathcal G}^{\top}H_0^{-1}R_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C_*h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{q_\beta}.
\]
The population comparison curve is exact for the interpolated panel:
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta
 \quad\hbox{on }\mathcal I,
 \qquad
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g)
 \quad(g\in\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}).
\]
For \(d=0\), the factor, projector, and Gram assertions use the zero-dimensional convention and the last display holds with \(\theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{1,\mathcal G}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}s_1\).
