\textbf{Proof.}
All constants below are uniform over \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\), depend only on the fixed constants in the assumptions and on the fixed rank \(r\), and may change from line to line.  Put
\[
 n_D=N_0K_D,\qquad k_1=\lfloor K_D/2\rfloor,
 \qquad k_2=K_D-k_1,
 \qquad h_0=s_-g_-.
\tag{1}
\]
The assertions are asymptotic uniformly on every three-dimensional tail
\(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\ge a\), with \(a\to\infty\); all subsequent uniform bounds are understood on such a tail.

\emph{Step 1: cell expansions and donor aggregation.}
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}product\mbox{-}limit\mbox{-}cell}\) to the target-pre full cell, every donor full cell, and the two deterministic donor half-folds.  For a cell or half-fold of size \(k\), its interpolated stabilized curve has the decomposition
\[
 \widehat S_k^\dagger
 =\widetilde S^0+k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_{k,j}
   +b_k^\dagger+q_k^\dagger,
\tag{2}
\]
where the patient processes are independent and mean zero,
\[
 \|b_k^\dagger\|_\infty\le Ce^{-c_0k},
 \qquad E\|q_k^\dagger\|_\infty^2\le Ck^{-2},
 \qquad c_0=h_0/3.
\tag{3}
\]
Odd and even half-folds are independent.  The construction in
\(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\) and the cell lemma also give, on every dataset,
\[
 \sup_t|\widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O)-
              \widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O')|\le C/k
\tag{4}
\]
when one patient record is replaced, and the corresponding observed patient multiplier contribution is bounded by \(C/k\).

We need the sharper rate obtained by averaging the centered cell remainders over donors.  In the notation of the proof of the cell lemma, write
\[
 q_{k,m}^\dagger=U_{k,m}+V_{k,m},qquad
 U_{k,m}=A_{k,m}-EA_{k,m},qquad
 V_{k,m}(t)=-S^0_m(t)\int_0^tD_{k,m}(u)\,dM_m(u),
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
 D_{k,m}(u)=\frac{R_m(u-)}{Y_m^\dagger(u)}
             -\frac{1}{kH_m(u)},
 \qquad H_m(u)=S_m^0(u)G_m(u).
\tag{6}
\]
The stopped ratio calculation in that lemma, using \(H_m\ge h_0\), gives
\[
 E\int_0^{\bar\tau}D_{k,m}(u)^2Y_m(u)lambda_m^0(u)\,du
 \le Ck^{-2}.
\tag{7}
\]
For completeness, (7) is the second-order bound: on
\(\{Y_m(\bar\tau)\ge kh_0/2\}\),
\[
 D_{k,m}=\frac{R_m-1}{Y_m}
 -\frac{Y_m-kH_m}{kH_mY_m},
\tag{8}
\]
and the predictable isometry bounds each term by \(Ck^{-2}\); on the complement, clipping gives \(|D_{k,m}|\le C/k\), while the written Chernoff inequality in the cell lemma makes its contribution exponentially small.

Put \(A_m(t)=\int_0^tD_{k,m}\,dM_m\).  Integration by parts gives the exact identity
\[
 V_{k,m}(t)
 =-\int_0^tS_m^0(u)D_{k,m}(u)\,dM_m(u)
 +\int_0^tA_m(u-)\,d\{1-S_m^0(u)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The first terms in (9), averaged over independent donor cells, form a martingale whose terminal expected predictable quadratic variation is at most \(C/(N_0k^2)\) by (7).  Doob's \(L^2\) inequality therefore gives
\[
 E\sup_t\left|N_0^{-1}\sum_m
 \int_0^tS_m^0D_{k,m}\,dM_m\right|^2
 \le \frac{C}{N_0k^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
For the second terms, define
\(F_m(t)=\int_0^tA_m(u-)\,d\{1-S_m^0(u)\}\).  They are independent, pointwise mean zero, and
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(F_m)\le\|A_m\|_\infty.
\tag{11}
\]
The time-ordered bounded-variation inequality proved in the cell lemma says that independent pointwise-centered bounded-variation processes satisfy
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mF_m\right\|_\infty^2
 \le\frac{16}{N_0^2}\sum_mE\{\operatorname{TV}(F_m)^2\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (7), (11), (12), and Doob's inequality imply the same bound \(C/(N_0k^2)\) for the second terms in (9).  The rare-drift part of the cell lemma gives
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mU_{k,m}\right\|_\infty^2
 \le CN_0^{-1}e^{-c_0k}.
\tag{13}
\]
Combining (5), (9)--(13),
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mq_{k,m}^\dagger\right\|_\infty^2
 \le \frac{C}{N_0k^2}+CN_0^{-1}e^{-c_0k}.
\tag{14}
\]
This argument is ordered in continuous time.  It neither takes a maximum over grid knots nor requires all donor cells to have nonexhausted risk sets.

\emph{Step 2: cross-fold population centering and aggregate regularity.}
Let \(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\) denote the interpolated population donor covariance operator from
\(\mathrm{lem:interpolated\mbox{-}population\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}stability}\).  Centering the half-fold versions of (2) across donors and using independence of the two folds shows that every cross-fold product containing a centered first-order or centered remainder term has expectation zero.  The only nonzero displacement of the mean cross-covariance is due to the deterministic stabilized biases.  Hence
\[
 \|E\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le Ce^{-c_0k_1}.
\tag{15}
\]
In particular, there is no same-fold errors-in-variables term of order \(K_D^{-1}\).

Replacing one donor patient record changes one half-fold curve by at most \(C/K_D\) by (4).  Donor centering is linear, all curves take values in \([0,1]\), and
\(\|x\otimes y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|x\|_2\|y\|_2\).  Therefore
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma-widehat\Gamma^{(i)}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le \frac{C}{N_0K_D},
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^{2N_0K_D}
 \|\widehat\Gamma-widehat\Gamma^{(i)}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le \frac{C}{n_D}.
\tag{16}
\]
Hilbert-space Efron--Stein, applied in an orthonormal Hilbert--Schmidt basis and then summed by monotone convergence, yields
\[
 E\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \le Cn_D^{-1}.
\tag{17}
\]
The scalar map
\(O\mapsto\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\) has the bounded differences in (16).  Jensen's inequality, (17), and McDiarmid's inequality thus give
\[
 P\left\{\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 >Cn_D^{-1/2}+x\right\}
 \le 2e^{-cn_Dx^2},\qquad x>0.
\tag{18}
\]
By the interpolated-population stability lemma, for all sufficiently fine canonical grids the \(d\)-th eigenvalue of \(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\) is at least \(\gamma_-^2/2\), its remaining eigenvalues vanish, and the pre-period Gram of its selected positive square root has minimum eigenvalue at least \(\kappa_-/2\).  Since the deterministic displacement in (15) tends to zero as \(K_D\to\infty\), the variational eigenvalue inequality, the spectral-projector resolvent identity, continuity of the positive square root on the selected spectral component, and
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}
\tag{19}
\]
show from (18) that the aggregate regular event \(\mathcal R\) in the band definition satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)}P(\mathcal R^c)
 \le Ce^{-cn_D}.
\tag{20}
\]
This is a single aggregate deviation bound and has no union over the \(N_0\) cells.

\emph{Step 3: coordinate-free derivatives.}
On the fixed-gap neighborhood in Step 2, let \(\Pi(\Gamma)\) be the selected spectral projector, let \(A=(\Pi\Gamma\Pi)_+^{1/2}\) on its selected range, and let \(E_p\) restrict a joint-period function to period \(p\).  Write
\[
 A_p=E_pA,qquad H=A_0^*A_0,qquad
 z=H^{-1}A_0^*x,qquad F(s,A,x)=s_1+A_1z.
\tag{21}
\]
This is the rotation-invariant prediction map implemented by the selected-coordinate construction.  Along a differentiable path,
\[
 \dot H=\dot A_0^*A_0+A_0^*\dot A_0,
 \qquad
 \dot z=H^{-1}\{\dot A_0^*x+A_0^*\dot x-\dot Hz\},
\tag{22}
\]
\[
 \dot F=\dot s_1+\dot A_1z+A_1\dot z.
\tag{23}
\]
The second derivatives are obtained by differentiating (22)--(23):
\[
 \ddot H=\ddot A_0^*A_0+A_0^*\ddot A_0
          +2\dot A_0^*\dot A_0,
\tag{24}
\]
\[
 \ddot z=H^{-1}\{\ddot A_0^*x+2\dot A_0^*\dot x
 +A_0^*\ddot x-\ddot Hz-2\dot H\dot z\},
\tag{25}
\]
\[
 \ddot F=\ddot s_1+\ddot A_1z+2\dot A_1\dot z+A_1\ddot z.
\tag{26}
\]
For a contour \(\mathscr C\) separating the selected spectrum from zero,
\[
 D\Pi_\Gamma[E]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathscr C}
 (w-\Gamma)^{-1}E(w-\Gamma)^{-1}\,dw,
\tag{27}
\]
and \(DA_\Gamma[E]\) is the unique selected-range solution of the Sylvester equation
\[
 A\,DA_\Gamma[E]+DA_\Gamma[E]A
 =D(\Pi\Gamma\Pi)_\Gamma[E].
\tag{28}
\]
The contour distance, the selected-eigenvalue lower bound, and the pre-Gram lower bound are fixed.  Differentiating (27)--(28) once more and using (19) gives
\[
 \|DA_\Gamma[E]\|\le C\|E\|,
 \qquad
 \|D^2A_\Gamma[E_1,E_2]\|
 \le C\|E_1\|\|E_2\|.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (22)--(29), together with the factor and loading envelopes, prove that the first two Fr\'echet derivatives of \(F\) are uniformly bounded on this neighborhood.  Because (21) is coordinate free, internal eigenvalue ties do not affect this conclusion.

Composing the first derivative with the all-dataset sensitivity (4), and observing that a donor enters every donor average with weight \(N_0^{-1}\), gives on every dataset on the regular branch
\[
 \|D_j^TF\|_\infty\le C/K_T,
 \qquad
 \|D_{p,m,j}^DF\|_\infty\le C/(N_0K_D).
\tag{30}
\]
On \(\mathcal R^c\), the definition uses the ordinary pooled donor-post product-limit derivative; thus the derivative remains a total measurable object on every dataset.

\emph{Step 4: the expansion and its remainder.}
Use the deterministic donor-bias inputs in (2) as an intermediate center.  The displacement of the prediction map produced by those inputs is a deterministic curve \(\mathfrak b_r\).  The Lipschitz bound following from (22)--(23) and the choice of \(C_\eta\) in \(\eta_D\) give
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le\eta_D(K_D).
\tag{31}
\]
The target-cell deterministic bias, which is bounded by \(Ce^{-c_0K_T}\), is assigned to the remainder rather than to \(\mathfrak b_r\).

Taylor's formula with integral remainder, applied on \(\mathcal R\) to (21), and (2), (14), (20), and (29) yield at all grid knots
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =K_T^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}\phi_{T,j}
  +n_D^{-1}\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}}
       \sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\sum_{j=1}^{K_D}\phi_{D,p,m,j}
  +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r.
\tag{32}
\]
The two displayed patient blocks are mutually independent by patient independence; within each block, the summands are independent and mean zero because they are the first derivatives in (23) composed with the mean-zero martingale terms in (2).  The use of both a full donor cell and its deterministic half-fold does not destroy independence across patients: it only changes the deterministic linear coefficient attached to that patient's martingale increment.

The population center in (32) is \(\theta\) at every grid point.  Indeed,
\(\mathrm{lem:canonical\mbox{-}target\mbox{-}coordinate}\) identifies the unique compatible target loading, and
\(\mathrm{lem:interpolated\mbox{-}population\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}stability}\) gives
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g),
 \qquad g\in\mathcal G.
\tag{33}
\]

We now account for every term in \(Q_r\).  The target centered cell remainder has \(L^2\)-supremum size \(CK_T^{-1}\) by (3).  The averaged donor centered remainder has size
\(CN_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}\) by (14).  The quadratic Taylor terms have size
\(C\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\}\), and the interaction between a stochastic linear input and the deterministic donor bias has size \(C\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)\).  On \(\mathcal R^c\), all curves are bounded and (20) contributes \(Ce^{-cn_D/2}\) to the \(L^2\) norm.  The analogous target risk-exhaustion contribution is \(Ce^{-cK_T/2}\).  Consequently, after decreasing the fixed exponential constant if necessary,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P
 \|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2
 \le C\upsilon_r^2,
\tag{34}
\]
where, exactly as in the definition of \(\omega_r\) for \(r\ge2\),
\[
 \upsilon_r=
 K_T^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}
 +\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)
 +e^{-c_\dagger K_T/2}+e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}
 +n_D^{-1}+e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}.
\tag{35}
\]
The two identical aggregate exponential allowances in (35) are retained because they are the terms registered in the frozen definition.  This proves the asserted expansion and remainder bound.

\emph{Step 5: continuous-time moment bounds.}
Let \(Z_r\) denote the sum of the two linear blocks in (32).  Composition of (23) with a cell martingale influence is a fixed finite sum, depending only on \(r\), of processes of the form
\[
 a(t)M(t)+\int_0^tK(t,u)\,dM(u),
\tag{36}
\]
where the coefficient curves and their time-ordered total variations are bounded by the overlap, smoothness, spectral-gap, pre-Gram, factor-envelope, and loading-envelope constants.  Stochastic integration by parts, predictable isometry, Doob's inequality, and independence of the patient increments give directly from the weights in (30)
\[
 \sup_PE_P\|Z_r\|_\infty^2
 \le C\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\}.
\tag{37}
\]
This estimate is taken over continuous time before restriction to the grid.

Conditionally on the observations, the multiplier process is the same finite sum with independent standard Gaussian coefficients and the observed patient contributions.  On \(\mathcal R\), (30), Gaussian isometry, and the same time-ordered argument prove the analogue of (37).  On \(\mathcal R^c\), the pooled fallback derivative has conditional squared supremum at most \(Cn_D\).  Equation (20) and
\(n_De^{-cn_D}\le Cn_D^{-1}\) absorb the fallback contribution.  Hence
\[
 \sup_PE_PE_\xi\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le C\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\}.
\tag{38}
\]

We finally record the covariance consistency supplied by the same calculation.  If
\(\Sigma_r(g,g')=E_P\{Z_r(g)Z_r(g')\}\) and
\(\widehat\Sigma_r\) is the conditional multiplier covariance in the band definition, then
\[
 \sup_PE_P\max_{g,g'\in\mathcal G}
 |\widehat\Sigma_r(g,g')-\Sigma_r(g,g')|
 \le C\rho_r^2 a_{N_0,K_T,K_D},
\tag{39}
\]
where one permissible explicit sequence is
\[
 a_{N_0,K_T,K_D}
 =K_T^{-1/2}+K_D^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)
  +e^{-cK_T/2}+n_D^2e^{-cn_D}.
\tag{40}
\]
Indeed, for the target block, predictable isometry reduces the difference of quadratic kernels to the stabilized empirical risk and failure processes; their time-ordered \(L^2\) maximal bounds and (4) give the relative error \(CK_T^{-1/2}\).  For the donor block, the same argument within each cell gives \(CK_D^{-1/2}\), averaging gives the additional \(Cn_D^{-1/2}\), and (22)--(29) show that replacing the population projector, positive square root, pre-Gram inverse, and target coordinate by their empirical versions costs
\(C\{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)\}\) relatively.  Products are controlled by
\(|uv-u'v'|\le |u-u'||v|+|u'||v-v'|\), the all-dataset envelopes (4) and (30), and Cauchy--Schwarz.  The fallback covariance is at most \(Cn_D\); multiplying by (20) and using \(\rho_r^2\ge n_D^{-1}\) produces the last term in (40).  These bounds hold for the continuous supremum, so taking the maximum over \((g,g')\) introduces no grid-cardinality factor.  Every term in (40) tends to zero uniformly on every three-dimensional tail, without a balance condition and without a coordinatewise variance lower bound.

Finally,
\[
 K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\le\rho_r^2
 \le2\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\},
\tag{41}
\]
because \(r\ge2\) and
\(\rho_r=K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}\).  Equations (37), (38), and (41) prove the stated moment conclusion.  Since all maximal estimates were obtained on continuous time before grid restriction, no \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G|}\) factor occurs.  This completes the proof. \(\square\)
