\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}ssc\_\allowbreak{}rank\_\allowbreak{}normalization\_\allowbreak{}band\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The project studies the missing post-control survival trajectory for a treated unit in a right-censored exact-factor panel. The canonical target coordinate follows from target-pre factor compatibility and is unique because the pre-period contrast Gram is positive. Exact rank one eliminates target-loading uncertainty and gives \(W_1^*\asymp(N_0K_D)^{-1/2}\). For every fixed \(r\ge2\), the unrestricted result is the honest bracket \(c\rho_r\le W_r^*\le C\{\rho_r+\eta_D(K_D)\}\); it reduces to \(W_r^*\asymp\rho_r\) only when \(\eta_D(K_D)=O(\rho_r)\), and no unrestricted tightness is claimed. A censoring-aware patient-level functional-PCR martingale-multiplier band supplies the upper envelope, with a fixed-margin treatment of its hard regular/fallback switch and a singular-covariance-safe random quantile. Without a uniform positive pre-period Gram, minimax honest width is of order one even on a donor-visible known-rank-two subclass. This is a generic information-theoretic boundary: donor-rank visibility alone does not imply a shrinking or rank-adaptive honest band.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Han and Shah give the survival-factor and donor-span structure used for Synthetic Survival Control and later impose finite-grid conditioning and latent-span assumptions for their consistency theorem, but they do not provide uniformly honest continuous-time patient-level bands or minimax width converses. Existing PCR, functional principal-component, and martingale-multiplier results separately cover pieces of the problem, not their data-dependent composition under right censoring and weak target-pre identification.

\paragraph{Niche.} Survival normalization separates a homogeneous rank-one regime from positive contrast rank, while target identifiability depends on pre-period factor strength rather than donor contrast visibility alone. This creates a donor-only rank-one rate within the strong exact-rank class and an order-one weak-pre-Gram obstruction even when rank two is known from donors.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project derives canonical coordinates from target-compatible factorization and a positive pre-period Gram, constructs a total measurable censoring-aware functional-PCR multiplier band, validates its branchwise coefficients through an exponentially likely fixed-margin event, proves grid-uniform random-quantile validity and interpolation control, establishes the exact rank-one frontier and honest higher-rank bracket, and gives normalization-preserving Le Cam witnesses for the fixed-rank converse and the generic weak-pre-Gram boundary.

\section{Related work}

Han and Shah's Theorem~1 supplies finite-dimensional survival factorization and treated-unit donor-span inclusion; their Theorem~2 adds finite-grid conditioning and latent time-period span restrictions that the explicit weak-pre-Gram pair is shown not to satisfy, so the order-one lower bound does not contradict that theorem. Agarwal, Shah, and Shen supply minimum-norm PCR, PCR/HSVT retained-rank preprocessing, rank-selection workflows, and fixed-design prediction geometry that inform the functional-PCR map. They make no honest-band claim, and the present lower bound is not a refutation of their method; it is a generic information-theoretic statement that donor-only retained rank cannot by itself certify target-relevant pre-period strength. Dobler, Pauly, and Scheike establish martingale-multiplier survival inference for classical survival functionals; the present result additionally propagates patient-level increments through an estimated projector, matrix square root, inverse pre-Gram, interpolation, and a hard branch with an explicit margin event. Cattaneo, Feng, and Titiunik separate pre-fit and post-prediction uncertainty for scalar synthetic controls, whereas the present survival pairs quantify donor and target-pre channels and yield minimax converses. Imbens and Viviano analyze stochastic channels in a different random-factor synthetic-control model. Chen, Fan, Ma, and Yan study uncertainty quantification for noisy low-rank matrix completion, not a right-censored continuous-time missing survival curve. Athey, Bayati, Doudchenko, Imbens, and Khosravi provide matrix-completion prediction methods rather than this target-loading and band-width frontier. Curth, Poon, Nori, and González motivate caution in survival synthetic-control analysis; the present note supplies an exact-rank construction and a weak-target-pre minimax witness. Robins and Finkelstein mark the dependent-censoring extension deliberately excluded here. Chernozhukov, Wüthrich, and Zhu provide conformal counterfactual synthetic-control inference as a robust alternative, not the censored factor-model continuous-time frontier studied here. Goldsmith, Greven, and Crainiceanu account for decomposition uncertainty in functional principal-component bands but not right censoring or a causal missing curve. Carpentier, Klopp, L\"offler, and Nickl connect honest adaptation to composite testing; the present negative conclusion is instead certified directly by a donor-visible rank-two survival pair. Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato's coupling theorem and the Powers--Størmer inequality are used only as cited classical substrate for the singular-covariance-safe comparison.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:control-consistency]\label{ass:control-consistency}
For every observed control cell, the observed failure time equals the control potential failure time \(T^0_{p,m,j}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency for observed control exposure, \cite{HanShah2025SSC}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:patient-independence]\label{ass:patient-independence}
The records \(O_{p,m,j}\) are mutually independent across observed cells and patient indices.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent patient sampling across survival cohorts, \cite{KaplanMeier1958}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-censoring]\label{ass:independent-censoring}
Within each observed control cell, \(T^0_{p,m,j}\perp C_{p,m,j}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent right censoring, \cite{KaplanMeier1958}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:censor-overlap]\label{ass:censor-overlap}
There is a fixed \(g_->0\) such that \(\inf_{p,m,t\in\mathcal I}G_{p,m}(t)\ge g_-\) over observed control cells.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform censoring overlap, \cite{DoblerPaulyScheike2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:event-overlap]\label{ass:event-overlap}
There is a fixed \(s_->0\) such that \(\inf_{p,m,t\in\mathcal I}S^0_{p,m}(t)\ge s_-\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (At-risk survival positivity on a truncated horizon, \cite{DoblerPaulyScheike2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hazard-smoothness]\label{ass:hazard-smoothness}
For fixed \(\beta>1/2\) and \(L<\infty\), \(\sup_{p,m}\|\lambda^0_{p,m}\|_{C^\beta(\mathcal I)}\le L\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform Hölder hazard regularity, \cite{AndersenBorganGillKeiding1993}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:normalized-factor-model]\label{ass:normalized-factor-model}
The control survival panel satisfies \(S^0_{p,m}(t)=s_p(t)+b_p(t)^\top z_m\) for every \(p\in\{0,1\}\), \(m\in\mathcal M\), and \(t\in\mathcal I\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Canonical affine form of the exact survival factor model, \cite{HanShah2025SSC}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-centered-rank]\label{ass:exact-centered-rank}
The primitive centered donor operator \(\mathcal C_D\) has exactly \(d=r-1\) nonzero singular directions, with rank zero interpreted literally when \(r=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} novel: this is the project's canonical exact centered-donor rank restriction and is not attributed to the broader Han--Shah factor model
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:centered-contrast-spectrum]\label{ass:centered-contrast-spectrum}
For fixed \(0<\gamma_-\le\gamma_+<\infty\), every nonzero singular value of \(\mathcal C_D\) lies in \([\gamma_-,\gamma_+]\) when \(d>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform nonzero contrast-spectrum bounds, \cite{AgarwalShahShen2025PCR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pre-contrast-gram]\label{ass:pre-contrast-gram}
For fixed \(0<\kappa_-\le\kappa_+<\infty\), every eigenvalue of \(\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)b_0(t)^\top\,dt\) lies in \([\kappa_-,\kappa_+]\) when \(d>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Functional factor-strength condition, \cite{AgarwalShahShen2025PCR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-incoherence]\label{ass:donor-incoherence}
For a fixed \(M_z<\infty\), \(\max_{m\in\mathcal D}\|z_m\|_2\le M_z\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded factor-loading incoherence, \cite{AgarwalShahShen2025PCR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:target-loading-bound]\label{ass:target-loading-bound}
For a fixed \(M_\star\in(0,\infty)\), \(\|z_\star\|_2\le M_\star\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded out-of-sample loading, \cite{AgarwalShahShen2025PCR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:factor-envelope]\label{ass:factor-envelope}
For a fixed \(M_b<\infty\), \(\max_{p\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{t\in\mathcal I}\|b_p(t)\|_2\le M_b\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded fixed-rank functional-factor envelope, \cite{GoldsmithGrevenCrainiceanu2013}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:explicit-witness-compatibility]\label{ass:explicit-witness-compatibility}
For \(d=r-1\) and \(d\ge1\), let \(z_m^{(N,d)}\) be obtained, for each \(N\ge2d+1\), by empirical Gram--Schmidt from the explicitly centered columns
\[
 q_{k,m}^{(N)}=\{m-(N+1)/2\}^k-N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\{\ell-(N+1)/2\}^k,
 \qquad 1\le k\le d,
\]
and define \(R_d=\sup_{N\ge2d+1}\max_{1\le m\le N}\|z_m^{(N,d)}\|_2\), with \(R_0=0\), let \(\Phi_d\) be the deterministic \(L^2(\mathcal I)\)-orthonormalization of \(\psi_\circ,t\psi_\circ,\ldots,t^{d-1}\psi_\circ\), with first coordinate \(\psi_\circ/\|\psi_\circ\|_2\), and put \(A_{p,d}=a_{\circ,p}\|\psi_\circ\|_2\Phi_d\), and suppose the fixed primitive constants obey
\[
 g_-\le1,
 \qquad
 R_d\le M_z,
 \qquad
 \gamma_-^2\le(a_{\circ,0}^2+a_{\circ,1}^2)\|\psi_\circ\|_2^2\le\gamma_+^2,
 \qquad
 \kappa_-\le a_{\circ,0}^2\|\psi_\circ\|_2^2\le\kappa_+,
\]
\[
 \max_{p\in\{0,1\}}\sup_{t\in\mathcal I}\|A_{p,d}(t)\|_2\le M_b,
 \qquad
 2|a_{\circ,0}|h_\circ c_f^{-1/2}
 \left\{\int_0^{\bar\tau}\{\psi_\circ'(t)\}^2dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \le\frac34(1/2-\alpha),
\]
and, writing \(v_\circ=|a_{\circ,1}|/4\), for every \(p\in\{0,1\}\), every \(x\in\mathbb R^d\) with \(\|x\|_2\le R_d\), every \(|v|\le v_\circ\), and every \(t\in\mathcal I\), the curve
\[
 \ell_{p,x,v}(t)=e^{-\lambda_\circ t}+A_{p,d}(t)^\top x+\mathbf 1\{p=1\}v\psi_\circ(t)
\]
satisfies
\[
 s_-\le\ell_{p,x,v}(t)\le1,
 \qquad
 -\partial_t\ell_{p,x,v}(t)\ge0,
 \qquad
 \inf_{t\in\operatorname{supp}(\psi_\circ')}[-\partial_t\ell_{p,x,v}(t)]\ge c_f,
 \qquad
 \left\|-\frac{\partial_t\ell_{p,x,v}}{\ell_{p,x,v}}\right\|_{C^\beta(\mathcal I)}\le L;
\]
and for \(d=0\), the same four inequalities hold with the empty factor term, \(x=0\), and every \(|v|\le v_\circ\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} novel: an explicit primitive numerical nonemptiness condition for this worked survival-factor subexperiment
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:canonical-factorization]\label{def:canonical-factorization}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Canonical normalized survival-\allowbreak{}factor coordinates}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set
\[
 s_p(t)=N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}S^0_{p,m}(t).
\]
Start from the model's target-compatible all-unit affine representation, center its donor loadings, covariance-normalize those loadings, and apply the inverse normalization to the factor curves. Write the singular-value decomposition of the resulting primitive centered operator as
\[
 \mathcal C_D=\sum_{k=1}^d\sigma_k u_k\otimes v_k,
\]
ordered by decreasing \(\sigma_k\) with deterministic sign and tie-breaking rules, and define
\[
 z_{m,k}=\sqrt{N_0}v_{k,m},
 \qquad b_{p,k}(t)=\sigma_k u_{k,p}(t).
\]
Thus the construction uses the donor-centered normalization
\[
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}z_m=0,
 \qquad N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}z_mz_m^\top=I_d.
\]
Let
\[
 H_0=\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)b_0(t)^\top\,dt.
\]
Conditional on the model's target-pre factor compatibility
\(S^0_{0,\star}-s_0\in\operatorname{span}\{b_{0,1},\ldots,b_{0,d}\}\), define the shared target coordinate by
\[
 z_\star=H_0^{-1}\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)
 \{S^0_{0,\star}(t)-s_0(t)\}\,dt.
\]
The positive canonical pre-period Gram makes this coordinate unique. Transporting the compatible all-unit representation through the same covariance normalization and orthogonal donor-SVD transform gives
\[
 S^0_{p,m}(t)=s_p(t)+b_p(t)^\top z_m
\]
for both periods and every donor or target unit. Survival normalization gives \(s_p(0)=1\) and \(b_p(0)=0\). When \(d=0\), compatibility forces every contrast to vanish and all vectors, sums, and Gram matrices use the zero-dimensional convention.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{S}; \texttt{r}; \texttt{N0}.
\end{definition}

 \mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}

\begin{definition}[def:fpcr-multiplier-band]\label{def:fpcr-multiplier-band}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Censoring-\allowbreak{}aware functional-\allowbreak{}PCR martingale-\allowbreak{}multiplier band}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) be piecewise-linear interpolation on the canonical grid. For the population comparison, define
\[
 \widetilde S^0_{p,m}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0_{p,m},\qquad
 \widetilde s_p=N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\widetilde S^0_{p,m},
\]
\[
 \widetilde Y_m=(\widetilde S^0_{0,m}-\widetilde s_0,
                  \widetilde S^0_{1,m}-\widetilde s_1),\qquad
 \widetilde\Gamma=N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \widetilde Y_m\otimes\widetilde Y_m.
\]
Apply the selected positive-square-root and pre-period least-squares prediction map below to this panel and call the result \(\theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}\); for \(r=1\), put \(\theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{1,\mathcal G}=\widetilde s_1\). This is a proof comparison and does not redefine \(\theta\).

Put \(h_0=s_-g_-\). For any cell or deterministic half-fold containing \(k\) patients, set
\[
 \ell_k=\max\{1,kh_0/8\},\qquad
 Y_k^\dagger(u)=\max\{Y_k(u),\ell_k\}.
\]
Order tied observed failures lexicographically by failure time and patient index, updating the risk count after each ordered failure, and define
\[
 \widehat S_k^\dagger(t)=
 \prod_{u\le t}\left\{1-\frac{dN_k(u)}{Y_k^\dagger(u)}\right\}.
\]
Every ordered increment is zero or one, so this measurable curve lies in \([0,1]\) on every dataset. Interpolate it by \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\). For every \(r\ge2\), use these stabilized curves for the target-pre full cell, every donor full cell, and both odd--even donor half-folds. For \(r=1\), do not stabilize: pool all \(N_0K_D\) donor-post records first, form the ordinary aggregate-risk product-limit curve with its empty-risk convention, and interpolate it continuously.

For \(r\ge2\), center the two sets of stabilized half-fold donor curves across donors to obtain \(\widehat Y_m^{(1)}\) and \(\widehat Y_m^{(2)}\), and form
\[
 \widehat\Gamma=\frac{1}{2N_0}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \{\widehat Y_m^{(1)}\otimes\widehat Y_m^{(2)}+
   \widehat Y_m^{(2)}\otimes\widehat Y_m^{(1)}\}.
\]
Select its largest \(d\) eigenvalues with deterministic lexicographic basis and sign rules, put \(\widehat\mu_k=\sqrt{\max\{\widehat\lambda_k,0\}}\), and set \(\widehat b_{p,k}=\widehat\mu_k\widehat u_{k,p}\). Let \(\widehat s_p\) be the donor average of the stabilized full-cell curves, and let \(\widehat H_0=\int_{\mathcal I}\widehat b_0\widehat b_0^\top\). On the aggregate regular event
\[
 \widehat\lambda_d\ge\gamma_-^2/4,\qquad
 \widehat\lambda_d-\widehat\lambda_{d+1}\ge\gamma_-^2/4,
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(\widehat H_0)\ge\kappa_-/4,
\]
define
\[
 \widehat z_\star=\widehat H_0^{-1}\int_{\mathcal I}\widehat b_0(t)
 \{\widehat S_{0,\star}^\dagger(t)-\widehat s_0(t)\}\,dt,
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_r=\widehat s_1+\widehat b_1^\top\widehat z_\star.
\]
This is the rotation-invariant selected-projector, positive-square-root, and inverse-pre-Gram prediction map. If a fold is empty or an aggregate threshold fails, set the factor and loading estimates to zero and let \(\widehat\theta_r\) be the continuously interpolated ordinary pooled donor-post product-limit curve. The total hard-switched estimator is measurable and defined on every dataset, and every returned curve is continuous in physical time after interpolation. No continuity or differentiability in the data across a hard cutoff is claimed.

Define the multiplier coefficients branchwise. On a dataset assigned to the regular branch, differentiate the coordinate-free regular prediction map and the stabilized product-limit inputs. The observed contribution of patient \(j\) in a stabilized \(k\)-patient input uses the weight
\[
 -\widehat S_k^\dagger(t-)
 \int_0^t\{Y_k^\dagger(u)\}^{-1}\,dN_{k,j}(u).
\]
On a dataset assigned to the fallback branch, and on the rank-one branch, use instead the ordinary pooled aggregate-risk product-limit coefficients. These two arrays are explicitly defined local branch coefficients; they are not the derivative of the total hard-switched map across a cutoff. Attach independent standard Gaussian multipliers to the selected branch's patient coefficients and denote the resulting interpolated process by \(\widehat Z_r^\xi\). Define its conditional covariance by
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_r(g,g')
 =E_\xi\{\widehat Z_r^\xi(g)\widehat Z_r^\xi(g')\mid O\}.
\]

Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(p_{\mathcal G}=2|\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}|\), and
\[
 n_{\mathrm{eff},r}=
 \begin{cases}
 n_D,&r=1,\\
 \min\{K_T,n_D\},&r\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]
With \(c_0=s_-g_-/3\), define
\[
 a_{r,n}=n_D^{-1/2}+\mathbf1\{r\ge2\}
 \left\{K_T^{-1/2}+K_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)
 +e^{-c_0K_T/2}+e^{-c_0n_D/2}\right\}.
\]
Let
\[
 k_{r,n}=\max\{2,\lceil n_{\mathrm{eff},r}^{1/9}\rceil\},
 \qquad p_{\mathrm c,r,n}=2(k_{r,n}+1),
\]

\begin{lemma}[lem:normalization-collapse]\label{lem:normalization-collapse}
For every \(P\in\mathfrak P_1(N_0,K_T,K_D)\), the canonical contrast dimension is \(d=0\), so \(S^0_{p,m}(t)=s_p(t)\) for every unit and \(\theta(t)=s_1(t)\). Along every regular submodel whose target-pre marginal law is fixed and which perturbs \(\theta\) through the post-control law, the score contribution of the target-pre records is identically zero. For fixed \(r\ge2\), any normalization-preserving loading path \(z_{\star,\eta}=z_\star+\eta v\) that remains in \(\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\) has target derivative \(\left.\partial_\eta\theta_\eta(t)\right|_{\eta=0}=b_1(t)^\top v\), which is nonzero exactly when \(b_1^\top v\not\equiv0\) on \(\mathcal I\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_1(N_0,K_T,K_D)\). Since \(d=r-1\), exact rank one gives \(d=0\). The zero-dimensional clause of the corrected canonical construction turns every factor-loading product into an empty sum. The normalized factor model therefore gives
\[
 S^0_{p,m}(t)=s_p(t),
 \qquad p\in\{0,1\},\quad m\in\mathcal M,\quad t\in\mathcal I. \tag{1}
\]
The target definition yields \(\theta(t)=S^0_{1,\star}(t)=s_1(t)\).

Let \(\{P_\eta:|\eta|<\eta_0\}\) be a regular submodel of the type quantified in the statement, and let \(q_{0,\star,\eta}\) be the density of one target-pre record with respect to a common dominating measure. Its target-pre marginal is fixed, so
\[
 q_{0,\star,\eta}=q_{0,\star,0},
 \qquad
 \left.\partial_\eta\log q_{0,\star,\eta}(O_{0,\star,j})
 \right|_{\eta=0}=0.                                    \tag{2}
\]
Patient independence makes the target-pre sample score the sum of the zero terms in (2). This ancillarity conclusion is only for the fixed-target-pre paths stated in the lemma.

Now fix \(r\ge2\) and a normalization-preserving loading path \(z_{\star,\eta}=z_\star+\eta v\) that remains in the class. Holding the factor curves fixed, the target formula gives
\[
 \left.\partial_\eta\theta_\eta(t)\right|_{\eta=0}
 =\left.\partial_\eta
 \{s_1(t)+b_1(t)^\top(z_\star+\eta v)\}\right|_{\eta=0}
 =b_1(t)^\top v.                                        \tag{3}
\]
The derivative in (3) is a nonzero element of \(C(\mathcal I)\) exactly when \(b_1^\top v\) is not identically zero. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The zero-dimensional collapse and conditional loading derivative isolate the algebraic source of the rank transition. \textit{Gap.} HanShah2025SSC imposes a factor rank but does not state this normalization-induced score degeneracy. \textit{Consumer.} This identifies when target-pre sampling carries no information for the missing post-control curve.

\begin{lemma}[lem:uniform-linearization]\label{lem:uniform-linearization}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), let \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) be piecewise-linear interpolation on \(\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\), and center the functional-PCR map at the interpolated population panel \(\widetilde S^0=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0\) defined in \(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\). Uniformly over \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\) and all sequences with \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\to\infty\), there are independent mean-zero censoring-martingale influence processes \(\phi_{T,j}\) and \(\phi_{D,p,m,j}\), a deterministic grid function \(\mathfrak b_r\), and a remainder \(Q_r\) such that, simultaneously for \(g\in\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\),
\[
 \widehat\theta_r(g)-\theta(g)=Z_r(g)+\mathfrak b_r(g)+Q_r(g),
 \qquad
 Z_r=\mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}K_T^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}\phi_{T,j}
     +n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j},
\]
where \(\phi_{T,j}\equiv0\) when \(r=1\) and
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le \mathbf 1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D).
\]
The interpolated population comparison equals \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta\), and hence equals \(\theta\) at every grid point. For every sufficiently large evaluated triple, the finite-tail buffer and the conditional multiplier critical value in \(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\) obey
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)/4,
\]
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\omega_r/2\}
 \le\alpha-\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)/4.
\]
Moreover \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) uniformly on every three-dimensional tail without a restriction on the relative growth of \(K_T\) and \(N_0K_D\). The expansion and both tail inequalities are gridwise; the interpolation perturbation inside the \(L^2\) factor map is used to establish spectrum and Gram stability, while the discrepancy between \(S^0\) and \(\widetilde S^0\) away from the grid is carried only by \(c_{\mathrm{int}}h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{\min\{\beta+1,2\}}\) in the continuous-time band.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\).  If \(r=1\), normalization collapse makes the target-pre influence block identically zero.  Apply \(\mathrm{lem:pooled\mbox{-}noniid\mbox{-}km\mbox{-}linearization}\), set \(\mathfrak b_1=0\), and absorb its exponentially small deterministic product-limit bias into \(Q_1\).  If \(r\ge2\), apply \(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}gridwise\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}transfer}\).  In both cases the patient blocks are independent and mean zero by patient independence and their censoring-martingale constructions, and we obtain, simultaneously on the grid,
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta=Z_r+\mathfrak b_r+Q_r,
 \qquad
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le\mathbf1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D),                     \tag{1}
\]
with
\[
 E\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\le C\upsilon_r^2.      \tag{2}
\]
The interpolated-population stability lemma proves
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta,
 \qquad
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g)
 \quad(g\in\mathcal G).                                \tag{3}
\]

By definition, \(\omega_r=C_\omega\varepsilon^{-1/2}\upsilon_r\).  Markov's inequality and (2) give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P\{\|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>\omega_r/2\}
 \le4C\upsilon_r^2/\omega_r^2
 \le\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)/4                \tag{4}
\]
once the fixed \(C_\omega\) is chosen sufficiently large.  The random-quantile theorem gives
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le c_r^\xi\}
 \ge1-\alpha+\delta_{r,n}(\alpha)/2.                   \tag{5}
\]
For sufficiently large triples, \(\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)<\alpha\), hence \(\delta_{r,n}(\alpha)=\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)/2\).  Enlarging the threshold in (5) by \(\omega_r/2\) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}>c_r^\xi+\omega_r/2\}
 \le\alpha-\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)/4.         \tag{6}
\]

It remains to check the buffer rate.  At rank one,
\[
 \frac{\upsilon_1}{\rho_1}
 \le C\{n_D^{-1/2}+n_D^{1/2}e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}\}.   \tag{7}
\]
For \(r\ge2\), using \(\rho_r\ge K_T^{-1/2}\) and \(\rho_r\ge n_D^{-1/2}\),
\[
 \frac{\upsilon_r}{\rho_r}
 \le C\left\{K_T^{-1/2}+K_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)
 +n_D^{-1/2}+K_T^{1/2}e^{-c_\dagger K_T/2}
 +n_D^{1/2}e^{-c_\dagger n_D/2}\right\}.               \tag{8}
\]
Multiplication by \(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\), which grows only as a square root of an iterated logarithm, preserves convergence to zero uniformly on every three-dimensional tail.  Thus \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\) without a relative-growth condition.  The expansion is gridwise by construction; \(\mathrm{prop:grid\mbox{-}interpolation}\) carries the only off-grid discrepancy through \(c_{\mathrm{int}}h^{\min\{\beta+1,2\}}\). \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The expansion combines the two patient influence blocks with the proved degeneracy-robust random-quantile tail inequality and the explicit nonlinear buffer. \textit{Gap.} Existing survival and PCR perturbation results do not jointly provide grid-uniform covariance estimation and a valid data-dependent critical-value transfer under singular coordinates. \textit{Consumer.} It is the finite-tail stochastic input for the continuous-time honest-band theorem.

\begin{lemma}[lem:rank-two-pair]\label{lem:rank-two-pair}
For all sufficiently large \(N_0\ge2\), \(K_T\), and \(K_D\), the two laws in \(\mathrm{def:rank-two-lecam-pair}\) belong to \(\mathfrak P_2(N_0,K_T,K_D)\), have \(S_{p,m}^{(u)}(0)=1\), and differ in observed law only through the \(K_T\) target-pre failure times. Their target curves obey \(\|\theta_+-\theta_-\|_\infty=2|a_{\circ,1}|h_\circ\|\psi_\circ\|_\infty K_T^{-1/2}\). Their likelihood divergence satisfies \[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_+\|P_-)\le K_T\int\frac{(f_{0,\star}^{(+)}-f_{0,\star}^{(-)})^2}{f_{0,\star}^{(-)}}\,dt\le\frac{4a_{\circ,0}^2h_\circ^2}{c_f}\int_0^{\bar\tau}\{\psi_\circ'(t)\}^2dt.\] A fixed sufficiently small \(h_\circ\) therefore yields uniformly bounded total variation while preserving exact total rank two, the donor contrast-spectrum bounds, the pre-contrast Gram bounds, and endpoint normalization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Here \(r=2\) and \(d=1\).  Empirical Gram--Schmidt in
\(\mathrm{def:rank\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}lecam\mbox{-}pair}\) gives
\[
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}z_m=0,
 \qquad
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}z_m^2=1,
 \qquad
 \max_m|z_m|<\sqrt3\le R_1\le M_z.                         \tag{1}
\]
Since \(A_{p,1}=a_{\circ,p}\psi_\circ\), the centered donor operator has
exactly one nonzero singular value
\[
 \left\{(a_{\circ,0}^2+a_{\circ,1}^2)
              \|\psi_\circ\|_2^2\right\}^{1/2},             \tag{2}
\]
and the pre-factor Gram is
\[
 a_{\circ,0}^2\|\psi_\circ\|_2^2.                          \tag{3}
\]
The numerical inequalities in
\(\mathrm{ass:explicit\mbox{-}witness\mbox{-}compatibility}\) place (2)
and (3) in the required spectrum and Gram intervals and verify the donor
loading and factor-envelope bounds.

Since \(M_\star>0\), for all sufficiently large \(K_T\),
\[
 h_\circ K_T^{-1/2}\le\min\{M_\star,R_1\}.                  \tag{4}
\]
Apply the four pointwise compatibility inequalities with \(x=z_m\) for
donors and \(x=z_\star^{(u)}\) for the target.  They give valid
nonincreasing survival curves, event overlap, the failure-density floor on
\(\operatorname{supp}(\psi_\circ')\), and the \(C^\beta\) hazard bound.
Every factor curve and \(\psi_\circ\) vanish at zero, so
\(S_{p,m}^{(u)}(0)=1\).  Taking censoring identically infinite gives
\(G=1\), independent censoring, and censor overlap.  The product construction
gives patient independence and control consistency.  Equations (1)--(4)
verify every remaining defining property of
\(\mathfrak P_2(N_0,K_T,K_D)\).  Thus
\(P_-,P_+\in\mathfrak P_2(N_0,K_T,K_D)\).

Only the target loading changes with \(u\), and the target-post control
cohort is not observed.  Hence the observed laws differ only through the
\(K_T\) target-pre records.  The first factor coordinate gives
\[
 \theta_+(t)-\theta_-(t)
 =2a_{\circ,1}h_\circ K_T^{-1/2}\psi_\circ(t),               \tag{5}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \|\theta_+-\theta_-\|_\infty
 =2|a_{\circ,1}|h_\circ\|\psi_\circ\|_\infty K_T^{-1/2}.    \tag{6}
\]
The two target-pre densities satisfy
\[
 f_{0,\star}^{(+)}(t)-f_{0,\star}^{(-)}(t)
 =-2a_{\circ,0}h_\circ K_T^{-1/2}\psi_\circ'(t).             \tag{7}
\]
They agree off \(\operatorname{supp}(\psi_\circ')\), and on that support
\(f_{0,\star}^{(-)}\ge c_f\).  The identity
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f_+\|f_-)=\int f_+\log(f_+/f_-)\), the inequality
\(\log x\le x-1\), and \(\int(f_+-f_-)=0\) imply
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f_+\|f_-)
 \le\int\frac{(f_+-f_-)^2}{f_-}.
\]
Patient independence and (7) consequently give
\[
 \begin{split}
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_+\|P_-)
 &\le K_T\int\frac{(f_{0,\star}^{(+)}-f_{0,\star}^{(-)})^2}
                         {f_{0,\star}^{(-)}}\,dt\\
 &\le\frac{4a_{\circ,0}^2h_\circ^2}{c_f}
       \int_0^{\bar\tau}\{\psi_\circ'(t)\}^2\,dt.
 \end{split}                                                \tag{8}
\]
Moreover,
\((\sqrt{x}-\sqrt y)^2\le(x-y)^2/y\) for \(x,y>0\).  Applying this
pointwise in (7), multiplying the Hellinger affinities of the independent
records, and using total variation bounded by the corresponding Hellinger
distance yields
\[
 \|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le2|a_{\circ,0}|h_\circ c_f^{-1/2}
       \left\{\int_0^{\bar\tau}(\psi_\circ')^2\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{9}
\]
The last inequality in the explicit compatibility assumption makes (9) a
fixed constant strictly below the amount required in the two-point width
argument.  Exact rank, spectrum, Gram, and normalization were established
in (1)--(4) and do not depend on the sign of the target loading. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:grid-interpolation]\label{prop:grid-interpolation}
There is a constant \(c_{\mathrm{int}}=c_{\mathrm{int}}(L,\bar\tau,\beta)<\infty\) such that, for the piecewise-linear interpolant \(\widehat\theta_r\) in \(\mathrm{def:fpcr-multiplier-band}\),
\[
 0\le \|\widehat\theta_r-\theta\|_{\infty,\mathcal I}
 -\max_{g\in\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D}}|\widehat\theta_r(g)-\theta(g)|
 \le c_{\mathrm{int}}h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{\min\{\beta+1,2\}}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\). Consequently the interpolation allowance under the corrected grid definition is \(O(\rho_r)\), so continuous-time honesty is not obtained by replacing \(\mathcal I\) with a fixed finite grid.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\), and set \(q=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\).  By the hazard identity and endpoint normalization,
\[
 \theta(t)=S^0_{1,\star}(t)
 =\exp\left\{-\int_0^t\lambda^0_{1,\star}(u)\,du\right\}.
\]
The bound \(\|\lambda^0_{1,\star}\|_{C^\beta(\mathcal I)}\le L\), the fixed horizon, and the product Hölder inequality applied to \(\theta'=-\lambda^0_{1,\star}\theta\) imply
\[
 \|\theta\|_{C^{\beta+1}(\mathcal I)}\le C(L,\bar\tau,\beta).
\]
Let \(I_{\mathcal G}\theta\) be the piecewise-linear interpolant of the grid values of \(\theta\).  On a grid interval \([a,b]\) of length at most \(h=h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\), the first-order Taylor formula with Hölder remainder when \(q<2\), and the ordinary second-order interpolation remainder when \(q=2\), give
\[
 \sup_{t\in[a,b]}|I_{\mathcal G}\theta(t)-\theta(t)|
 \le C(L,\bar\tau,\beta)|b-a|^q.
\]
Taking the maximum over intervals yields
\[
 \|I_{\mathcal G}\theta-\theta\|_\infty\le c_{\mathrm{int}}h^q.
\]

By construction, \(\widehat\theta_r\) is linear between adjacent grid points.  Hence \(\widehat\theta_r-I_{\mathcal G}\theta\) is also linear there, and the maximum of its absolute value is attained at an endpoint.  Consequently
\[
 \|\widehat\theta_r-\theta\|_{\infty,\mathcal I}
 \le \max_{g\in\mathcal G}|\widehat\theta_r(g)-\theta(g)|
 +\|I_{\mathcal G}\theta-\theta\|_\infty.
\]
The reverse inequality between the grid maximum and the continuous supremum follows from \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal I\).  Combining the last two displays proves the asserted two-sided interpolation inequality.  Finally, the corrected mesh condition is \(h^q\le\rho_r\), so the allowance is \(O(\rho_r)\). \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The corrected exponent \(\min\{\beta+1,2\}\) is the exact regularity available to linear interpolation. \textit{Gap.} Finite-grid SSC analyses do not price the whole-curve interpolation error. \textit{Consumer.} It prevents a gridwise band from being mislabeled continuous-time.

\begin{theorem}[thm:attaining-band]\label{thm:attaining-band}

 \le c_{\mathrm{up}}\bar\rho_r(N_0,K_T,K_D).
\]

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}

\[

 \qquad

\]
The uniform-linearization lemma and a union bound give
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P(\mathcal A_Q\cap\mathcal A_Z)
 \ge1-\alpha.                                           \tag{1}
\]
On this event, at every grid knot,
\[

 \le \widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_{r,n}
 +\mathbf1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D)+\omega_r.              \tag{2}
\]
Projection onto \([0,1]\) is nonexpansive for the true survival value, and both the target and the corrected zero-knot center equal one at zero.  Hence (2) also holds with \(\widetilde\theta_r(g)\) in place of \(\widehat\theta_r(g)\).  Since \(\widetilde\theta_r\) is the piecewise-linear interpolant of these projected grid values, the interpolation argument in \(\mathrm{prop:grid\mbox{-}interpolation}\) yields
\[
 \|\widetilde\theta_r-\theta\|_{\infty,\mathcal I}
 \le \widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_{r,n}
 +\mathbf1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D)+\omega_r
 +c_{\mathrm{int}}h^q=A_r.                              \tag{3}
\]
The corrected definition takes pointwise continuous clipping of the interval \([\widetilde\theta_r-A_r,\widetilde\theta_r+A_r]\) to \([0,1]\). Equation (1) is stronger than the required \(1-\alpha-\varepsilon\) coverage, so \(\widehat B_r\in\mathcal H_r\).

 w(\widehat B_r)\le2A_r.                                \tag{4}

The random-quantile theorem gives \(E\widetilde c_r^\xi\le C\rho_r\) and \(\tau_{r,n}=o(\rho_r)\).  The uniform-linearization lemma gives \(\omega_r=o(\rho_r)\), while the grid definition gives \(h^q\le\rho_r\).  Taking expectations in (4), uniformly over the class, therefore yields

 \le C\{\rho_r+\mathbf1\{r\ge2\}\eta_D(K_D)\}
 =C\bar\rho_r.                                         \tag{5}

All tail cutoffs and constants are uniform and no relative-growth comparison was used.  At rank one \(\bar\rho_1=\rho_1=(N_0K_D)^{-1/2}\); at higher rank (5) is \(O(\rho_r)\) precisely on the stated subregimes. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:interpolated-population-fpcr-stability]\label{lem:interpolated-population-fpcr-stability}

\]

\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widetilde H_0)\ge\kappa_-/2,

 \le C_*h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}^{q_\beta}.
\]
The population comparison curve is exact for the interpolated panel:
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta
 \quad\hbox{on }\mathcal I,
 \qquad
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}(g)=\theta(g)
 \quad(g\in\mathcal G_{N_0,K_T,K_D});
\]
and for \(d=0\), the factor, projector, and Gram assertions use the zero-dimensional convention and the last display holds with \(\theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{1,\mathcal G}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}s_1\).

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\) and write \(h=h_{N_0,K_T,K_D}\). The hazard identity and endpoint normalization give
\[
 S^0_{p,m}(t)=\exp\left\{-\int_0^t\lambda^0_{p,m}(u)\,du\right\}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:hazard\mbox{-}smoothness}\), the product rule applied to \((S^0_{p,m})'=-\lambda^0_{p,m}S^0_{p,m}\) gives a uniform \(C^{\beta+1}\) bound on every cell survival curve. The piecewise-linear interpolation remainder on each grid interval is therefore bounded by \(C_*h^{q_\beta}\), where \(q_\beta=\min\{\beta+1,2\}\). Interpolation reproduces its input at every knot, so
\[
 \widetilde S^0_{p,m}(g)=S^0_{p,m}(g),
 \qquad
 \|\widetilde S^0_{p,m}-S^0_{p,m}\|_\infty\le C_*h^{q_\beta}. \tag{1}
\]
Because averaging and centering are linear and every cell uses the same grid,
\[
 \widetilde s_p=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}s_p,
 \qquad
 \widetilde Y_m=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}Y_m. \tag{2}
\]
The donor incoherence and factor envelope assumptions give \(\sup_m\|Y_m\|_{L^2}\le C\). Equations (1)--(2), the fixed horizon, and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \sup_m\|\widetilde Y_m-Y_m\|_{L^2}\le C h^{q_\beta}. \tag{3}
\]
For rank-one operators, \(\|x\otimes y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|x\|_2\|y\|_2\). Hence
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le \|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\\
 &\le N_0^{-1}\sum_m
 \{\|\widetilde Y_m-Y_m\|_2\|Y_m\|_2
   +\|\widetilde Y_m\|_2\|\widetilde Y_m-Y_m\|_2\}\\
 &\le C_*h^{q_\beta}.
 \end{split} \tag{4}
\]

Assume \(d>0\). The canonical donor normalization gives
\[
 \Gamma=N_0^{-1}\sum_mY_m\otimes Y_m
       =\sum_{k=1}^d\sigma_k^2u_k\otimes u_k. \tag{5}
\]
Thus \(\lambda_d(\Gamma)\ge\gamma_-^2\) and \(\lambda_{d+1}(\Gamma)=0\).  |\lambda_k(\widetilde\Gamma)-\lambda_k(\Gamma)|
 \le\|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \tag{6}
\]

Let \(\mathscr C\) be a fixed contour separating \([\gamma_-^2/2,2\gamma_+^2]\) from a neighborhood of zero. Finite-rank spectral calculus and the resolvent identity give
\[
 \widetilde\Pi-\Pi
 =\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathscr C}
 (z-\widetilde\Gamma)^{-1}(\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma)(z-\Gamma)^{-1}\,dz. \tag{7}
\]
The two resolvents on \(\mathscr C\) are bounded only by the fixed spectral gap. Equations (4) and (7) yield
\[
 \|\widetilde\Pi-\Pi\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C h^{q_\beta}. \tag{8}
\]
Applying the same contour identity with the analytic function \(z^{1/2}\) on the positive spectral component shows that the selected positive square roots differ by at most \(Ch^{q_\beta}\) in operator norm. Choose orthonormal coordinate frames for the two selected ranges and align them by the polar factor of their cross-Gram matrix. Equation (8) makes that cross-Gram matrix invertible, and the elementary polar identity then supplies an orthogonal \(R_{\mathcal G}\) for which
\[
 \max_p\|\widetilde b_p-R_{\mathcal G}^{\top}b_p\|_{L^2}
 \le C h^{q_\beta}. \tag{9}
\]
This formulation is insensitive to internal eigenvalue ties.

By the pre-contrast Gram assumption, \(\lambda_{\min}(H_0)\ge\kappa_-\). From (9), the factor envelope, and the identity \(xx^\top-yy^\top=(x-y)x^\top+y(x-y)^\top\),
\[
 \|\widetilde H_0-R_{\mathcal G}^{\top}H_0R_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C h^{q_\beta}. \tag{10}
\]
For a sufficiently fine grid, the right side is at most \(\kappa_-/2\), so \(\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde H_0)\ge\kappa_-/2\). Finally,
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1} \tag{11}
\]
with \(A=\widetilde H_0\) and \(B=R_{\mathcal G}^{\top}H_0R_{\mathcal G}\) proves the inverse bound.

It remains to identify the comparison curve. Linearity of \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) and the normalized factor model give, for the same target loading,
\[
 \widetilde S^0_{p,\star}-\widetilde s_p
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}(b_p^\top z_\star). \tag{12}
\]
The interpolated pre-factor Gram is nonsingular by (10), so the population least-squares step recovers the coordinates of \(z_\star\) in the rotated interpolated factor basis exactly. Substitution in the post-period factor map yields
\[
 \theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{r,\mathcal G}
 =\widetilde s_1+\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}(b_1^\top z_\star)
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}(s_1+b_1^\top z_\star)
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\theta. \tag{13}
\]
Equation (1) at the knots proves the asserted grid equality. When \(d=0\), every factor calculation is an empty sum and (13) reduces to \(\theta^{\mathrm{pop}}_{1,\mathcal G}=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}s_1\). \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the root interpolation repair: it proves the population factor algorithm remains regular after the curves are interpolated. \textit{Gap.} The output-grid interpolation proposition alone does not control the internal \(L^2\) covariance, selected factor space, or pre-period inverse. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the valid Taylor center for the censored functional-PCR expansion.

\begin{lemma}[lem:canonical-target-coordinate]\label{lem:canonical-target-coordinate}
Let \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\), and let \((\bar b_p,\bar z_m)\) be any donor-centered compatible \(d\)-coordinate representation of the normalized factor model. After covariance normalization of its donor loadings, the donor-SVD factors and loadings in \(\mathrm{def:canonical\mbox{-}factorization}\) equal the compatible factors and loadings up to a common orthogonal transform. If \(d>0\), then \(S^0_{0,\star}-s_0\) belongs to the span of the canonical \(b_0\), the matrix \(H_0=\int_{\mathcal I}b_0b_0^\top\) is positive definite, and
\[
 z_\star=H_0^{-1}\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)
 \{S^0_{0,\star}(t)-s_0(t)\}\,dt
\]
is the unique compatible target coordinate. For \(d=0\), compatibility forces \(S^0_{p,m}=s_p\) for all units.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\), suppress the sample-size indices,
and put \(\mathcal H=L^2(\{0,1\}\times\mathcal I)\).  Let
\((\bar b_p,\bar z_m)\) be the compatible representation in the statement.
Thus
\[
 S^0_{p,m}(t)-s_p(t)=\bar b_p(t)^\top\bar z_m,
 \qquad m\in\mathcal M.                                  \tag{1}
\]
Because \(s_p\) is the donor mean in
\(\mathrm{def:canonical\mbox{-}factorization}\), averaging (1) over
\(m\in\mathcal D\) and using donor centering gives the same centered donor
curves in every compatible representation.

Define \(\bar B:\mathbb R^d\to\mathcal H\) and
\(\bar Z\in\mathbb R^{N_0\times d}\) by
\[
 (\bar Bx)_p(t)=\bar b_p(t)^\top x,
 \qquad \bar Z_{m\cdot}=\bar z_m^\top.                  \tag{2}
\]
For every \(a\in\mathbf 1^\perp\), the definition of the centered donor
operator and (1) give
\[
 \mathcal C_Da
 =\bar B\left(N_0^{-1/2}\bar Z^\top a\right).           \tag{3}
\]
By exact centered rank, \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C_D)=d\).  Since
(3) factors \(\mathcal C_D\) through \(\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 d=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C_D)
 \leq\min\{\operatorname{rank}(\bar B),
             \operatorname{rank}(\bar Z)\}\leq d.       \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\bar B\) and \(\bar Z\) both have rank \(d\).  This conclusion uses
only compatibility and exact centered rank, not the pre-period Gram condition.

The donor loading covariance
\[
 \Sigma=N_0^{-1}\bar Z^\top\bar Z                       \tag{5}
\]
is therefore positive definite.  Covariance-normalize by setting
\[
 \widetilde b_p=\Sigma^{1/2}\bar b_p,
 \qquad \widetilde z_m=\Sigma^{-1/2}\bar z_m.           \tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
 \widetilde b_p(t)^\top\widetilde z_m
 =\bar b_p(t)^\top\bar z_m,
 \qquad
 N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \widetilde z_m\widetilde z_m^\top=I_d.                 \tag{7}
\]
Let \(\widetilde Bx=(\widetilde b_0^\top x,
\widetilde b_1^\top x)\), and put
\(Y_m=(S^0_{0,m}-s_0,S^0_{1,m}-s_1)\in\mathcal H\).  Equation (7) yields
\[
 \Gamma:=N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}Y_m\otimes Y_m
 =\widetilde B\widetilde B^*.                            \tag{8}
\]

Let \(B:\mathbb R^d\to\mathcal H\) be the canonical donor-SVD factor map,
so \((Bx)_p=b_p^\top x\).  The singular-value construction gives
\[
 \mathcal C_D\mathcal C_D^*=BB^*.                        \tag{9}
\]
Donor centering also gives directly
\[
 \mathcal C_D\mathcal C_D^*
 =N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}Y_m\otimes Y_m=\Gamma.   \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, for \(f\in\mathcal H\), the vector with entries
\(N_0^{-1/2}\langle Y_m,f\rangle_{\mathcal H}\) lies in
\(\mathbf 1^\perp\), and applying \(\mathcal C_D\) to that vector gives the
middle operator in (10).  Combining (8)--(10),
\[
 BB^*=\widetilde B\widetilde B^*.                        \tag{11}
\]
Both maps have rank \(d\), so their ranges coincide.  Let
\(B^\dagger=(B^*B)^{-1}B^*\) be the left inverse of \(B\), and define
\[
 R=B^\dagger\widetilde B.                                \tag{12}
\]
Range equality gives \(BR=\widetilde B\).  Moreover, (11) implies
\[
 RR^*=B^\dagger\widetilde B\widetilde B^*B^{\dagger *}
     =B^\dagger BB^*B^{\dagger *}=I_d.                  \tag{13}
\]
Hence \(R\) is orthogonal and
\[
 b_p=R\widetilde b_p.                                    \tag{14}
\]
For a donor \(m\), injectivity of \(B\), (7), and
\(BR=\widetilde B\) give
\[
 Bz_m=Y_m=\widetilde B\widetilde z_m=BR\widetilde z_m
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z_m=R\widetilde z_m.        \tag{15}
\]
Thus the covariance-normalized compatible factors and loadings equal the
canonical donor-SVD coordinates up to one common orthogonal transformation.
The deterministic sign and tie-breaking rule merely chooses a representative
within this orthogonal equivalence class.

Assume \(d>0\).  Compatibility for the target unit and (6), (14) give
\[
 S^0_{0,\star}-s_0
 =\widetilde b_0^\top\widetilde z_\star
 =b_0^\top R\widetilde z_\star.                          \tag{16}
\]
Thus the target-pre contrast belongs to the span of the canonical pre-period
factors.  Now, and only now, use the positive pre-contrast Gram assumption:
\[
 H_0:=\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)b_0(t)^\top\,dt,
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(H_0)\geq\kappa_->0.              \tag{17}
\]
Therefore \(H_0\) is invertible.  Multiplying (16) by \(b_0(t)\),
integrating, and applying (17) yields
\[
 R\widetilde z_\star
 =H_0^{-1}\int_{\mathcal I}b_0(t)
 \{S^0_{0,\star}(t)-s_0(t)\}\,dt.                       \tag{18}
\]
If \(x\) and \(x'\) are two compatible canonical target coefficients, then
\(b_0^\top(x-x')=0\) in \(L^2(\mathcal I)\), whence
\[
 0=\int_{\mathcal I}\{b_0(t)^\top(x-x')\}^2\,dt
  =(x-x')^\top H_0(x-x').                                \tag{19}
\]
Positive definiteness forces \(x=x'\).  Hence (18) is the unique compatible
target coordinate and is exactly the \(z_\star\) selected in the canonical
construction.

If \(d=0\), a compatible \(d\)-coordinate representation has an empty factor
term, so (1) reads \(S^0_{p,m}=s_p\) for every period and every unit.  This
proves the zero-dimensional assertion and completes the proof. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Compatibility and positive canonical pre-Gram, in that order, deliver the unique target coordinate. \textit{Gap.} A coefficient equation cannot define a coordinate until target-pre span membership is established. \textit{Consumer.} It validates the canonical factorization used by every positive result.

\begin{lemma}[lem:stabilized-product-limit-cell]\label{lem:stabilized-product-limit-cell}
Fix an observed control cell or deterministic half-fold containing \(k\) patients, put \(h_0=s_-g_-\), \(\ell_k=\max\{1,kh_0/8\}\), and let \(\widehat S_k^\dagger\) be the stabilized product-limit curve in the corrected \(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\). For all sufficiently large \(k\): (i) \(\widehat S_k^\dagger\) equals the ordinary Kaplan--Meier curve on \(\{Y_k(\bar\tau)\ge\ell_k\}\); (ii)
\[
 P\{Y_k(\bar\tau)<kh_0/8\}
 \le \exp\{-49h_0k/128\}\le e^{-c_0k},
 \qquad c_0=h_0/3;
\]
(iii) changing one patient record changes the entire interpolated stabilized curve by at most \(C/k\), deterministically on every dataset; (iv) every individual observed multiplier contribution has supremum norm at most \(C/k\), deterministically; and (v), with \(\widetilde S^0=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0\), there are independent mean-zero censoring-martingale influence processes \(\varphi_{k,j}\), a deterministic bias \(b_k^\dagger\), and a centered remainder \(q_k^\dagger\) such that
\[
 \widehat S_k^\dagger-\widetilde S^0
 =k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\varphi_{k,j}+b_k^\dagger+q_k^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \|b_k^\dagger\|_\infty\le Ce^{-c_0k},
 \qquad
 E\|q_k^\dagger\|_\infty^2\le Ck^{-2}.
\]
For independent donor cells, their centered rare-cell contributions average with second moment at most \(CN_0^{-1}e^{-c_0k}\le C(N_0k^2)^{-1}\). All assertions allow ties and all realized risk sets; no all-cells-good event is imposed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
\emph{Postimage revalidation.}
The corrected \(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\) leaves unchanged every object used in this cell-local lemma: \(h_0\), \(\ell_k\), the clipped risk process \(Y^\dagger\), the lexicographically ordered stabilized product-limit curve, its piecewise-linear interpolation, and the displayed stabilized patient coefficient. The definition changes only the downstream interpretation of branchwise coefficients, the treatment of the hard regular/fallback switch, and the final band-endpoint construction. Neither that switch, its margin event, nor the pooled fallback enters any assertion or calculation below. Thus the following argument applies unchanged to the corrected definition, with assertion~(iv) understood as a bound for the explicitly defined local stabilized coefficient and not as a derivative of the total hard-switched map.

Suppress the cell label.  For patient \(j\), define
\[
 N_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\leq u,\Delta_j=1\},\qquad
 Y_j(u)=\mathbf 1\{X_j\geq u\},\qquad
 M_j(u)=N_j(u)-\int_0^uY_j(v)\lambda^0(v)\,dv.             \tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:control\mbox{-}consistency}\) and
\(\mathrm{ass:independent\mbox{-}censoring}\), the last integral is the
compensator of \(N_j\).  By
\(\mathrm{ass:patient\mbox{-}independence}\), the processes \(M_j\) are
independent, mean-zero, square-integrable martingales.  Put
\[
 Y=\sum_{j=1}^kY_j,\qquad N=\sum_{j=1}^kN_j,
 \qquad M=\sum_{j=1}^kM_j,
 \qquad H(u)=E Y_j(u)=S^0(u)G(u).
\]
The event- and censor-overlap assumptions give the uniform risk lower bound
\[
 H(u)\geq h_0:=s_-g_->0,
 \qquad u\in\mathcal I.                                  \tag{2}
\]

We first treat ties and clipping.  At a tied failure time, order failures by
patient index and remove each earlier ordered failure from the risk set before
processing the next.  If the pre-tie risk set is \(y\) and there are \(a\)
failures, then
\[
 \prod_{q=0}^{a-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{y-q}\right)=1-\frac{a}{y}. \tag{3}
\]
Thus this measurable ordered convention agrees with the ordinary
product-limit factor.  On the ordered event axis set
\[
 Y^\dagger(u)=\max\{Y(u),\ell_k\},
 \qquad \ell_k=\max\{1,kh_0/8\}.
\]
Every stabilized product factor lies in \([0,1]\).  If
\(Y(\bar\tau)\geq\ell_k\), monotonicity of the risk process gives
\(Y(u)\geq\ell_k\) at every earlier event.  No denominator is then clipped,
and (3) proves assertion (i).

The variables \(Y_j(\bar\tau)\) are independent Bernoulli variables and
\(\mu:=E Y(\bar\tau)\geq kh_0\).  For \(s>0\), independence gives
\[
 E e^{-sY(\bar\tau)}
 \leq \exp\{(e^{-s}-1)\mu\}.
\]
Exponential Markov inequality, optimized at
\(s=-\log(1-\delta)\), together with
\((1-\delta)\log(1-\delta)+\delta\geq\delta^2/2\), yields
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)\leq(1-\delta)\mu\}
 \leq e^{-\delta^2\mu/2},
 \qquad 0<\delta<1.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Because \(kh_0/8\leq\mu/8\), taking \(\delta=7/8\) proves
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)<kh_0/8\}
 \leq e^{-49h_0k/128}
 \leq e^{-h_0k/3}=e^{-c_0k},
 \qquad c_0=h_0/3.                                       \tag{5}
\]

We next prove the deterministic guards.  For
\(f(y)=1-1/\max\{y,\ell_k\}\),
\[
 |f(y+e)-f(y)|\leq |e|\ell_k^{-2},
 \qquad |e|\leq1.                                       \tag{6}
\]
Changing one patient record inserts or deletes at most two ordered failure
factors and changes each remaining ordered risk count by at most one.  The
telescoping identity for products of numbers in \([0,1]\), (6), and the fact
that there are at most \(k\) failure factors give, for all sufficiently large
\(k\),
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathcal I}
 |\widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O)-\widehat S_k^\dagger(t;O')|
 \leq 2\ell_k^{-1}+k\ell_k^{-2}\leq C/k.                \tag{7}
\]
Piecewise-linear interpolation is a contraction in supremum norm.  A patient
has at most one observed failure, so its observed product-limit multiplier
contribution is bounded, on every dataset, by
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathcal I}\widehat S_k^\dagger(t-)
 \int_0^t\{Y^\dagger(u)\}^{-1}\,dN_j(u)
 \leq\ell_k^{-1}\leq C/k.                               \tag{8}
\]
This proves assertions (iii) and (iv), including exhausted-risk datasets.

For the stochastic expansion, put \(R=\widehat S_k^\dagger/S^0\) on the
ordered event-time axis.  The product-integral equations, (1), and integration
by parts give the exact clipped ratio identity
\[
 R(t)-1
 =-\int_0^tR(u-)\{Y^\dagger(u)\}^{-1}\,dM(u)
  +\int_0^tR(u-)\left\{1-\frac{Y(u)}{Y^\dagger(u)}\right\}
             \lambda^0(u)\,du.                           \tag{9}
\]
This is an identity on every dataset, not an approximation on a good-risk-set
event.  Since every stabilized product factor lies in \([0,1]\), event overlap
implies \(0\leq R\leq s_-^{-1}\).  The drift in (9) is supported on
\(\{Y(u)<\ell_k\}\), which by monotonicity is contained in
\(\{Y(\bar\tau)<\ell_k\}\).  Hazard smoothness gives
\(\int_0^{\bar\tau}\lambda^0(u)\,du\leq L\bar\tau\).
If \(A_k\) denotes the drift in (9) after multiplication by \(S^0(t)\),
then (5) yields
\[
 \|EA_k\|_\infty\leq Ce^{-c_0k},
 \qquad
 E\|A_k-EA_k\|_\infty^2\leq Ce^{-c_0k}.                \tag{10}
\]

Define the independent mean-zero influence processes
\[
 \varphi_{k,j}^{\circ}(t)
 =-S^0(t)\int_0^tH(u)^{-1}\,dM_j(u).                    \tag{11}
\]
After subtracting \(k^{-1}\sum_j\varphi_{k,j}^{\circ}\) from (9), the
remaining martingale component is
\[
 V_k(t)=-S^0(t)\int_0^tD_k(u)\,dM(u),
 \qquad
 D_k(u)=\frac{R(u-)}{Y^\dagger(u)}-\frac{1}{kH(u)}.      \tag{12}
\]
Let \(\mathcal E_k=\{Y(\bar\tau)\geq kh_0/2\}\).  On
\(\mathcal E_k\), one has \(Y^\dagger=Y\), and the exact algebraic
decomposition
\[
 D_k
 =\frac{R-1}{Y}-\frac{Y-kH}{kHY}                       \tag{13}
\]
holds.  Stop (9) immediately before \(Y<kh_0/2\).  The predictable
isometry, Doob's \(L^2\) inequality, (2), and patient independence give
\[
 E\{\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}\|R-1\|_\infty^2\}
 \leq Ck^{-1},
 \qquad
 E\{Y(u)-kH(u)\}^2\leq k.                               \tag{14}
\]
Substituting (13)--(14) into the predictable quadratic variation yields
\[
 E\left[\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k}
 \int_0^{\bar\tau}D_k(u)^2Y(u)\lambda^0(u)\,du\right]
 \leq Ck^{-2}.                                           \tag{15}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_k^c\), clipping and (2) give
\(|D_k|\leq C/k\).  The fourth-moment martingale inequality followed by
Cauchy--Schwarz and the Chernoff calculation in (4) gives
\[
 E\left[\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_k^c}
 \int_0^{\bar\tau}D_k(u)^2Y(u)\lambda^0(u)\,du\right]
 \leq Ck^{-1}e^{-ck/2}\leq Ck^{-2}.                     \tag{16}
\]
Doob's inequality applied to (12) now proves
\(E\|V_k\|_\infty^2\leq Ck^{-2}\).  Set
\[
 b_k^\dagger=EA_k,
 \qquad q_k^{\dagger,\circ}=V_k+A_k-EA_k.               \tag{17}
\]
Equations (10), (15)--(17) prove the bias and centered-remainder bounds on
the event-time axis.  Apply the deterministic operator
\(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) to (11) and (17), and denote the resulting
processes by \(\varphi_{k,j}\) and \(q_k^\dagger\).  Interpolation preserves
means and supremum bounds and changes the deterministic center to
\(\widetilde S^0=\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}S^0\).  This proves assertion (v).

It remains to prove the donor rare-cell assertion without a grid union.  We
first record and prove the required ordered bounded-variation inequality.  If
\(F_1,\ldots,F_N\) are independent random c\`adl\`ag functions with
\(F_m(0)=0\), then
\[
 E\left\|N^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^N(F_m-EF_m)\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq \frac{16}{N^2}\sum_{m=1}^N
 E\{\operatorname{TV}(F_m)^2\}.                          \tag{18}
\]
To verify (18), condition first on deterministic bounded-variation paths and
write their Jordan decompositions.  A positive variation component is
\(F_m^+(t)=\mu_m^+([0,t])\), where \(\mu_m^+\) is a positive measure of
mass \(a_m^+\).  Introduce an auxiliary time \(T_m^+\) with conditional law
\(\mu_m^+/a_m^+\).  Conditional Jensen inequality gives
\[
 E_\epsilon\sup_t\left|\sum_m\epsilon_mF_m^+(t)\right|^2
 \leq E_{\epsilon,T}\sup_t
 \left|\sum_m\epsilon_ma_m^+\mathbf 1\{T_m^+\leq t\}\right|^2. \tag{19}
\]
Given the auxiliary times, ordering donors by \(T_m^+\) turns the process on
the right into partial sums of independent mean-zero variables
\(\epsilon_ma_m^+\).  Doob's scalar \(L^2\) inequality bounds (19) by
\(4\sum_m(a_m^+)^2\).  The same argument applies to the negative variation
components.  Conditional symmetrization with an independent copy and
\(a_m^++a_m^-\leq\operatorname{TV}(F_m)\) proves (18), after enlarging the
universal constant.  Step-function approximation in variation norm and Fatou's
lemma remove continuity and atom restrictions.  This argument is ordered in
time and uses a donor-index martingale; it does not involve the number of grid
points.

For donor cell \(m\), let \(A_{k,m}\) be the rare drift from (10).  The
cumulative integral in (9) is increasing, \(S_m^0\) has total variation at
most one, and the cumulative integral is uniformly bounded.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(A_{k,m})
 \leq C\mathbf 1\{Y_m(\bar\tau)<\ell_k\}.                \tag{20}
\]
With \(U_m=A_{k,m}-EA_{k,m}\), one has
\(EU_m(t)=0\) for every \(t\), and Jensen's inequality, (20), and (5) give
\[
 E\{\operatorname{TV}(U_m)^2\}\leq Ce^{-c_0k}.
\]
Apply (18) to the independent centered processes \(U_m\).  For all
sufficiently large \(k\),
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}U_m\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq CN_0^{-1}e^{-c_0k}
 \leq C(N_0k^2)^{-1}.                                    \tag{21}
\]
This proves the last assertion without an all-cells-good event and completes
the proof. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Observable clipping gives deterministic patient stability and exponentially small bias without an all-cells-good event. \textit{Gap.} Ordinary cellwise product-limit derivatives can be order one on rare exhausted-risk datasets. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies globally bounded higher-rank inputs.

\begin{lemma}[lem:pooled-noniid-km-linearization]\label{lem:pooled-noniid-km-linearization}
Suppose \(r=1\), put \(n_D=N_0K_D\),
\[
 \bar G_D(t)=N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}G_{1,m}(t),
 \qquad H_D(t)=s_1(t)\bar G_D(t).
\]
For the continuously interpolated pooled donor-post product-limit estimator in the corrected \(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\), there are independent mean-zero processes
\[
 \phi_{m,j}^{\mathrm{pool}}(t)
 =-s_1(t)\int_0^tH_D(u)^{-1}\,dM_{m,j}(u)
\]
such that, on the inference grid,
\[
 \widehat\theta_1-\theta
 =n_D^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}\sum_{j=1}^{K_D}
 \mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\phi_{m,j}^{\mathrm{pool}}
 +b_{D,n_D}+q_{D,n_D},
\]
where
\[
 \|b_{D,n_D}\|_\infty\le Ce^{-cn_D},
 \qquad Eq_{D,n_D}=0,
 \qquad E\|q_{D,n_D}\|_\infty^2\le Cn_D^{-2},
\]
uniformly over the rank-one class although the censoring laws may differ across donors. If \(\widehat Z_1^\xi\) is the explicit pooled multiplier and \(\Sigma_1\) and \(\widehat\Sigma_1\) are respectively the covariance kernel of the first-order sum and the conditional covariance kernel of that multiplier, then
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_1}E_P
 \left\|n_D^{-1}\sum_{m,j}
 \mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\phi_{m,j}^{\mathrm{pool}}\right\|_
 {\infty,\mathcal I}^2
 +\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_1}E_PE_\xi
 \|\widehat Z_1^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal I}^2
 \le Cn_D^{-1},
\]
and
\[
 n_D\|\widehat\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\|_{\infty,\mathcal I^2}
 \longrightarrow0
\]
in probability uniformly over \(\mathfrak P_1\). These assertions allow variance-zero coordinates, including time zero. No random-quantile coverage assertion is made.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n=n_D=N_0K_D\) and index donor-post patients by \(i=(m,j)\). By normalization collapse, all donor-post failure times have survival \(s_1\) and common hazard \(\lambda_1\), although \(G_{1,m}\) may vary. Define
\[
 N_i(u)=\mathbf1\{X_i\le u,\Delta_i=1\},\quad
 Y_i(u)=\mathbf1\{X_i\ge u\},\quad
 M_i(u)=N_i(u)-\int_0^uY_i(v)\lambda_1(v)\,dv.            \tag{1}
\]
Consistency and independent censoring give the compensator, and patient independence makes the \(M_i\) independent mean-zero martingales. With \(Y=\sum_iY_i\), \(N=\sum_iN_i\), and \(M=\sum_iM_i\),
\[
 EY(u)=nH_D(u),\qquad H_D(u)=s_1(u)\bar G_D(u)\ge h_0:=s_-g_->0. \tag{2}
\]
An exponential lower-tail bound for the independent risk indicators and a time-ordering symmetrization give
\[
 P\{Y(\bar\tau)<nh_0/2\}\le e^{-cn},qquad
 E\sup_u|Y(u)/n-H_D(u)|^2\le Cn^{-1}.                    \tag{3}
\]
For the second display, conditional Rademacher sums indexed by \(u\) are reverse partial sums after ordering the observed times, so the scalar maximal inequality applies without identical censoring laws.

Let \(\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}\) be the pooled product-limit curve, \(J_Y=\mathbf1\{Y>0\}\), and \(R=\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}/s_1\). Product integration gives the exact identity
\[
 R(t)-1=-\int_0^tR(u-)\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)}\,dM(u)
 +\int_0^tR(u-)\{1-J_Y(u)\}\lambda_1(u)\,du.             \tag{4}
\]
The convention \(J_Y/Y=0\) on an empty risk set avoids every boundary division. Stopping before \(Y<nh_0/2\), predictable isometry, \(0\le R\le s_-^{-1}\), and (3) give
\[
 E\sup_{t\le\bar\tau}|R(t)-1|^2\le Cn^{-1}+Ce^{-cn}.     \tag{5}
\]

Define
\[
 \phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}(t)=-s_1(t)\int_0^tH_D(u)^{-1}\,dM_i(u). \tag{6}
\]
These processes are independent and mean zero. Subtracting \(n^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}\) from (4) leaves the martingale integrand
\[
 D_n(u)=R(u-)\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)}-\frac1{nH_D(u)}.          \tag{7}
\]
On \(\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge nh_0/2\}\),
\[
 |D_n(u)|\le \frac{C}{n}|R(u-)-1|
 +\frac{C}{n}|Y(u)/n-H_D(u)|.                             \tag{8}
\]
Equations (3), (5), (8), and predictable isometry yield an \(L^2\)-supremum bound \(Cn^{-2}\) for the resulting martingale remainder. On the complement, the ordered failure risks are distinct positive integers, so
\[
 \int J_Y/Y\,dN\le\sum_{k=1}^nk^{-1}\le1+\log n;         \tag{9}
\]
combining (9) with the exponential probability in (3) gives the same polynomial bound. The drift in (4) is uniformly bounded and vanishes unless \(Y(\bar\tau)=0\). Therefore, with its expectation denoted by \(b_{D,n}^{\mathrm{PL}}\) and the centered remainder by \(q_{D,n}^{\mathrm{PL}}\),
\[
 \widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}-s_1
 =n^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}
 +b_{D,n}^{\mathrm{PL}}+q_{D,n}^{\mathrm{PL}},             \tag{10}
\]
\[
 \|b_{D,n}^{\mathrm{PL}}\|_\infty\le Ce^{-cn},\qquad
 Eq_{D,n}^{\mathrm{PL}}=0,\qquad
 E\|q_{D,n}^{\mathrm{PL}}\|_\infty^2\le Cn^{-2}.         \tag{11}
\]
Applying the deterministic interpolation contraction \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal G}\) proves the asserted grid expansion.

Predictable isometry applied to (6) gives
\[
 E\left\|n^{-1}\sum_i\phi_i^{\mathrm{pool}}\right\|_\infty^2
 \le \frac4n\int_0^{\bar\tau}\frac{\lambda_1(u)}{H_D(u)}\,du
 \le Cn^{-1}.                                             \tag{12}
\]
Conditionally on the observations, the multiplier is a Gaussian partial-sum process in failure-time order. Hence
\[
 E_\xi\|\widehat Z_1^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \le4\int_0^{\bar\tau}\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)^2}\,dN(u).     \tag{13}
\]
On the event in (3) this is \(O(n^{-1})\); on its complement it is bounded by \(4\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-2}\). Averaging and using (3) proves the second moment assertion.

Finally, the oracle and empirical covariance kernels are
\[
 \Sigma_1(s,t)=\frac{s_1(s)s_1(t)}n
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{\lambda_1(u)}{H_D(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_1(s,t)=\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}(s-)
 \widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)^2}\,dN(u).         \tag{14}
\]
Substitute \(dN=dM+Y\lambda_1du\). Equations (3), (5), and predictable isometry imply
\[
 \sup_v\left|\int_0^v\frac{nJ_Y}{Y^2}\,dN
 -\int_0^v\frac{\lambda_1}{H_D}\,du\right|=o_P(1),
 \qquad \|\widehat S_D^{\mathrm{PL}}-s_1\|_\infty=o_P(1), \tag{15}
\]
uniformly over the class. Multiplying (14) by \(n\) and using (15) proves
\(n\|\widehat\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\|_\infty\to0\) uniformly in probability. Bilinear interpolation is a covariance-kernel contraction. At time zero all integrals vanish, and no coordinate variance was inverted. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The rank-one branch pools donor-post records before product-limit estimation while allowing donor-specific censoring. \textit{Gap.} A one-cell identically distributed Kaplan--Meier expansion does not justify this pooled independent triangular array. \textit{Consumer.} This closes the sharp rank-one upper rate and implementation path.

  N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}z_m&
  N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}z_mz_m^\top

\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}

\(n_D=N_0K_D\), and suppress \(P\) from the notation.  Constants below
depend only on the fixed model constants and the fixed rank.  Let

 \mathscr A=\{(p,f):p\in\{0,1\},\
 f\in\{\mathrm{full},\mathrm{odd},\mathrm{even}\}\}

\(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}product\mbox{-}limit\mbox{-}cell}\) to

 =L_{a,m}+b_{a,m}^{\dagger}+q_{a,m},
 \qquad

donor remainder.  Fix one family \(a\), abbreviate \(k=k_a\), and work with

 q_{a,m}=U_{a,m}+V_{a,m},
 \qquad
 U_{a,m}=A_{a,m}-EA_{a,m},
 \qquad

\]

 J_{a,m}(t)=\int_0^tD_{a,m}(u)\,dM_{a,m}(u),

 E\int_0^{\bar\tau}D_{a,m}(u)^2Y_{a,m}(u)

\]

integration by parts gives the exact identity
\[
 \begin{split}
 V_{a,m}(t)
 &=-\int_0^tS^0_{a,m}(u)D_{a,m}(u)\,dM_{a,m}(u)\\
 &\quad+\int_0^tJ_{a,m}(u-)\,d\{1-S^0_{a,m}(u)\}.
 \end{split}                                           \tag{5}
\]
In particular, one may not replace the first integral in (5) by
\(-J_{a,m}\) when \(S^0_{a,m}\) varies with time.

Average the first term of (5) in the joint donor filtration:
\[
 \mathcal A_a(t)=-N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
 \int_0^tS^0_{a,m}(u)D_{a,m}(u)\,dM_{a,m}(u).          \tag{6}
\]
Martingales belonging to distinct donor cells are independent and hence have
zero cross-brackets.  Because \(0\leq S^0_{a,m}\leq1\), the predictable
bracket identity and (4) imply
\[
 \begin{split}
 E\langle\mathcal A_a\rangle(\bar\tau)
 &=N_0^{-2}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}E\int_0^{\bar\tau}
   S^0_{a,m}(u)^2D_{a,m}(u)^2Y_{a,m}(u)
   \lambda^0_{a,m}(u)\,du\\
 &\leq \frac{C}{N_0k^2}.
 \end{split}                                           \tag{7}
\]
Doob's \(L^2\) inequality therefore yields
\[
 E\|\mathcal A_a\|_\infty^2\leq\frac{C}{N_0k^2}.     \tag{8}
\]

For the finite-variation term in (5), define
\[
 F_{a,m}(t)=\int_0^tJ_{a,m}(u-)\,d\{1-S^0_{a,m}(u)\}.
\]
The processes \(F_{a,m}\) are independent over donors.  Moreover,
\(EJ_{a,m}(u)=0\) for every \(u\), so deterministic Stieltjes Fubini gives
\(EF_{a,m}(t)=0\) for every \(t\).  Monotonicity and endpoint normalization
of a survival curve imply
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(F_{a,m})
 \leq\|J_{a,m}\|_\infty
      \operatorname{TV}(1-S^0_{a,m})
 \leq\|J_{a,m}\|_\infty.                              \tag{9}
\]
The time-ordered bounded-variation inequality proved in
\(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}product\mbox{-}limit\mbox{-}cell}\)
states that independent pointwise-centered bounded-variation processes
\(G_m\) satisfy
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_mG_m\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq\frac{16}{N_0^2}\sum_mE\{\operatorname{TV}(G_m)^2\}. \tag{10}
\]
Applying (10), then (9), Doob's inequality, and (4), gives
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}F_{a,m}\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq\frac{C}{N_0^2}\sum_mE\|J_{a,m}\|_\infty^2
 \leq\frac{C}{N_0k^2}.                                \tag{11}
\]
Combining (5), (8), and (11) proves
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}V_{a,m}\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq\frac{C}{N_0k^2}.                                \tag{12}
\]
The centered rare-drift conclusion of the cell lemma gives separately
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}U_{a,m}\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq C N_0^{-1}e^{-c_0k}.                             \tag{13}
\]
Thus the full remainder, rather than only its rare-drift component, obeys
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}q_{a,m}\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq\frac{C}{N_0k^2}+C N_0^{-1}e^{-c_0k}
 \leq\frac{C}{N_0K_D^2}+C N_0^{-1}e^{-cK_D}.           \tag{14}
\]

The same argument permits deterministic scalar or fixed-dimensional weights
\(c_m\) with \(\max_m\|c_m\|\leq C\): insert \(c_m\) in (6), (10), and
the bracket sum.  It follows that
\[
 E\left\|N_0^{-1}\sum_m c_mq_{a,m}\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq\frac{C}{N_0K_D^2}+C N_0^{-1}e^{-cK_D}.           \tag{15}
\]
This weighted form is what is needed after donor centering.  Indeed, the
population donor contrasts have the form
\(\widetilde Y_m=\widetilde b^{\top}z_m\); the fixed rank and donor
incoherence bound the weights \(z_m\).  Expanding a centered perturbation as
\(q_{a,m}-N_0^{-1}\sum_\ell q_{a,\ell}\), every first derivative of the
donor mean or covariance is a fixed finite sum of terms covered by (14) or
(15).  Piecewise-linear interpolation contracts supremum norm.  Treating the
six families separately and using
\(\|\sum_{a\in\mathscr A}x_a\|^2\leq
|\mathscr A|\sum_a\|x_a\|^2\) combines them without assuming independence
between overlapping full and half-fold appearances.  Consequently the
donor-remainder contribution after donor centering and every population
functional-PCR derivative has \(L^2\)-supremum norm at most
\[
 C\{N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}+N_0^{-1/2}e^{-cK_D/2}\}
 \leq C N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1}.                            \tag{16}
\]

We next construct the fixed covariance-margin event.  Put
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathcal G}=\widetilde\Gamma
 =N_0^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathcal D}
   \widetilde Y_m\otimes\widetilde Y_m.
\]
By
\(\mathrm{lem:interpolated\mbox{-}population\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}stability}\),
for every sufficiently large triple,
\[
 \lambda_d(\Gamma_{\mathcal G})\geq\gamma_-^2/2,
 \qquad \lambda_{d+1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal G})=0,
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(H_{0,\mathcal G})\geq\kappa_-/2. \tag{17}
\]
Use the selected projector and positive square root, so internal eigenvalue
ties are immaterial.  The contour-resolvent formula for the selected
projector, the Sylvester equation for the positive-square-root derivative,
and
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}                       \tag{18}
\]
show that the prediction map and its first two derivatives are uniformly
bounded on a fixed Hilbert--Schmidt neighborhood of
\(\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\).  Choose its radius
\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}>0\) small enough that throughout this neighborhood
the selected eigenvalue, selected-to-complement gap, and pre-period Gram are
respectively at least \(3\gamma_-^2/8\), \(3\gamma_-^2/8\), and
\(3\kappa_-/8\), and set \(\epsilon_0=\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}/2\).

The odd and even half-folds within each donor-period cell are independent.
Using (1) for those two folds and their symmetric cross-product therefore
removes the same-fold product-limit variance and gives
\[
 \|E\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma_{\mathcal G}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \leq Ce^{-cK_D}=o(1).                                 \tag{19}
\]
Changing one donor record changes one stabilized half-fold curve by at most
\(C/K_D\).  Donor centering, bounded input curves, and the outer factor
\(N_0^{-1}\) in \(\widehat\Gamma\) hence give, for a one-record replacement,
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma(O)-\widehat\Gamma(O^{(i)})\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \leq\frac{C}{N_0K_D}=\frac{C}{n_D}.                  \tag{20}
\]
Hilbert-space Efron--Stein, obtained by expanding in an orthonormal
Hilbert--Schmidt basis, yields
\[
 E\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \leq\frac12\sum_iE
 \|\widehat\Gamma(O)-\widehat\Gamma(O^{(i)})\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \leq\frac{C}{n_D}.                                   \tag{21}
\]
The scalar map
\(O\mapsto\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\)
has the same bounded differences.  McDiarmid's exponential argument and
(21) therefore give
\[
 P\left\{\|\widehat\Gamma-E\widehat\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 >Cn_D^{-1/2}+x\right\}\leq e^{-cn_Dx^2},
 \qquad x>0.                                           \tag{22}
\]
For all sufficiently large triples, (19)--(22), with a fixed sufficiently
small \(x\), prove that the measurable event
\[
 \mathcal M_{r,n}
 =\{\|\widehat\Gamma-\widetilde\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
       \leq\epsilon_0\}                                \tag{23}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}P(\mathcal M_{r,n}^c)
 \leq Ce^{-cn_D}.                                      \tag{24}
\]
This is one aggregate concentration inequality, not a union over cells.

On \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\), the straight segment from
\(\widetilde\Gamma\) to \(\widehat\Gamma\) lies in the fixed regular
neighborhood.  By (20), once \(C/n_D\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}/2\), the
endpoint produced by any one-donor-record replacement and the segment from
\(\widehat\Gamma\) to that endpoint also remain in the same neighborhood.
Thus the selected eigenvalue, its gap from the complement, and the empirical
pre-period Gram remain above the construction's hard thresholds on all these
segments.  A target-pre replacement does not change \(\widehat\Gamma\), its
selected positive square root, or \(\widehat H_0\), so it cannot change the
branch on this event.  This proves every margin and replacement assertion in
the statement.

For completeness, write the regular coordinate-free prediction map as
\[
 A=\Gamma_+^{1/2},\quad A_p=E_pA,\quad H=A_0^*A_0,
 \quad z=H^{-1}A_0^*x,
 \quad F(s,A,x)=s_1+A_1z.                              \tag{25}
\]
Along a differentiable path,
\[
 \dot H=\dot A_0^*A_0+A_0^*\dot A_0,
 \qquad
 \dot z=H^{-1}\{\dot A_0^*x+A_0^*\dot x-\dot Hz\},  \tag{26}
\]
\[
 \dot F=\dot s_1+\dot A_1z+A_1\dot z.                \tag{27}
\]
The selected-projector derivative is the resolvent integral
\[
 D\Pi_\Gamma[E]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathscr C}
 (w-\Gamma)^{-1}E(w-\Gamma)^{-1}\,dw,                 \tag{28}
\]
and the positive-square-root derivative is the unique selected-range
solution of
\[
 A\,DA_\Gamma[E]+DA_\Gamma[E]A
 =D\{\Pi\Gamma\Pi\}_\Gamma[E].                        \tag{29}
\]
Differentiating (26)--(29) once more, and using the fixed contour distance,
the lower eigenvalue and Gram bounds, and (18), proves
\[
 \|DF\|\leq C,
 \qquad \|D^2F\|\leq C                                \tag{30}
\]
throughout the regular neighborhood, including internal ties.  Composing
(30) with the deterministic one-record bound from the stabilized cell lemma
gives, only on \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) for finite-replacement paths of the total
estimator,
\[
 \|D_j^TF\|_\infty\leq C/K_T,
 \qquad
 \|D_{p,m,j}^DF\|_\infty\leq C/(N_0K_D).              \tag{31}
\]

Both branches return uniformly bounded curves: on the regular branch this
follows from its hard spectral and Gram thresholds and bounded input curves,
and on fallback the pooled curve lies in \([0,1]\).  Hence all contributions
from leaving \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) can be included in a switch remainder
\(Q_r^{\mathrm{sw}}\) satisfying
\[
 \|Q_r^{\mathrm{sw}}\|_\infty
 \leq C\mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\},
 \qquad
 E\|Q_r^{\mathrm{sw}}\|_\infty^2\leq Ce^{-cn_D}.      \tag{32}
\]
No derivative across the hard cutoff is used.

Apply Taylor's formula with integral remainder on
\(\mathcal M_{r,n}\), insert the cell decompositions (1), and use (32)
off that event.  When a patient occurs in both a full input and one half-fold
input, combine its finitely many first-order appearances into one coefficient.
The resulting process \(\phi_{D,p,m,j}\) is therefore a function of that one
patient martingale with deterministic population coefficients; patient
independence makes the combined processes independent and mean zero.  The
target-pre terms are treated identically.  At every grid knot this gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_r-\theta
 =K_T^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_T}\phi_{T,j}
 +n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j}
 +\mathfrak b_r+Q_r.                                   \tag{33}
\]
The population center is \(\theta\) at every knot by
\(\mathrm{lem:canonical\mbox{-}target\mbox{-}coordinate}\) and
\(\mathrm{lem:interpolated\mbox{-}population\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}stability}\).
The fixed derivative bound sends the donor half-fold stabilization means into
\[
 \|\mathfrak b_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\leq\eta_D(K_D). \tag{34}
\]
The target cell's centered remainder has \(L^2\)-supremum norm
\(O(K_T^{-1})\).  The donor centered remainder has norm
\(O(N_0^{-1/2}K_D^{-1})\) by (14)--(16).  The second-order Taylor terms have
norm \(O(K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1})\), the bias--linear interactions have norm
\(O\{\rho_r\eta_D(K_D)\}\), and the target rare-risk and aggregate fallback
terms are bounded by \(Ce^{-cK_T/2}\) and \(Ce^{-cn_D/2}\), respectively.
Choosing the fixed \(c_\dagger\) and \(C_\omega\) in the frozen definition
no larger and no smaller, respectively, than the uniform constants just
obtained, these terms are dominated by the displayed \(\upsilon_r\) in the
definition of \(\omega_r\).  Minkowski's inequality therefore proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P
 \|Q_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\leq C\upsilon_r^2.      \tag{35}
\]
This explicitly propagates the full bound (14), rather than only the
rare-drift part, through donor centering and the functional-PCR map.

It remains to prove the two continuous-time moment bounds.  For the oracle
linear process, stochastic integration by parts applied to each first-order
cell influence, followed by the predictable isometry and Doob's inequality,
gives
\[
 E\left\|K_T^{-1}\sum_j\phi_{T,j}\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq C K_T^{-1},
 \qquad
 E\left\|n_D^{-1}\sum_{p,m,j}\phi_{D,p,m,j}\right\|_\infty^2
 \leq C n_D^{-1}.                                      \tag{36}
\]
For the donor term, the proof is the first-order analogue of (6)--(11): its
cell bracket is \(O(k_a^{-1})\), rather than \(O(k_a^{-2})\), and donor
orthogonality gives \(O((N_0k_a)^{-1})\).  The finite number of input
families is combined by the fixed-dimensional inequality used after (15),
not by an independence assertion.  The bounded deterministic derivatives in
(30) preserve these orders.  Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P\|Z_r\|_\infty^2
 \leq C\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\}.                          \tag{37}
\]

The multiplier coefficients require a separate branchwise argument.  On
every dataset assigned to the regular branch, the hard thresholds themselves,
(25)--(30), and the stabilized patient coefficient bound give the explicitly
defined local coefficients the envelopes \(C/K_T\) and \(C/n_D\).  Combining
repeated appearances of a patient before attaching its one multiplier changes
only the fixed constant.  Their time-ordered total variations obey the same
bounds.  Conditional Gaussian isometry and the time-ordered maximal inequality
therefore give
\[
 E E_\xi\{\|\widehat Z_{r,\mathrm{reg}}^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \mathbf1(\text{regular branch})\}
 \leq C\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\}.                          \tag{38}
\]
On fallback, the definition uses the ordinary pooled donor-post coefficients.
The aggregate at-risk count at \(\bar\tau\) is a sum of independent, possibly
non-identically distributed Bernoulli variables with means at least
\(s_-g_-\).  The Chernoff calculation used in the cell lemma therefore makes
the event of aggregate risk below \(n_Ds_-g_-/2\) have probability at most
\(Ce^{-cn_D}\).  On its complement, ordered integration by parts and
conditional Gaussian Doob inequality give \(C/n_D\); on the rare event the
same ordered calculation is at most \(Cn_D\).  Since
\(n_De^{-cn_D}\leq C/n_D\),
\[
 E E_\xi\{\|\widehat Z_{r,\mathrm{pool}}^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \mathbf1(\text{fallback branch})\}\leq Cn_D^{-1}.     \tag{39}
\]
Equations (38)--(39) prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_PE_\xi
 \|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_\infty^2
 \leq C\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\}.                         \tag{40}
\]
All maximal inequalities in (36)--(40) were applied in physical time before
restriction to the grid.  Thus no \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G|}\) factor occurs.
Finally,
\[
 K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\leq\rho_r^2
 \leq2\{K_T^{-1}+n_D^{-1}\},
\]
so (37) and (40) give the asserted \(C\rho_r^2\) bound.  The proof used local
regular-branch derivatives and an explicit fallback calculation, never a
derivative of the total hard-switched map across its cutoff. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The expansion now respects the hard estimator switch: total-map replacement and Taylor bounds hold on a uniformly exponentially likely fixed-margin event, and switching is an explicit remainder off that event. \textit{Gap.} A branchwise Fréchet derivative is not a global derivative of a hard-switched estimator; the margin-event argument closes that distinction without changing the estimator or model class. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the linear process, remainder, and branchwise multiplier moments used by covariance transfer and the attaining band.

\begin{theorem}[thm:grid-uniform-random-quantile-validity]\label{thm:grid-uniform-random-quantile-validity}
Fix \(r\ge1\), and let \(n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\), \(\delta_{r,n}(\alpha)\), \(\chi_{r,n}\), and \(\tau_{r,n}\) be the deterministic sequences in \(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\). Let \(Z_r\) be the oracle patient linear process in \(\mathrm{lem:pooled\mbox{-}noniid\mbox{-}km\mbox{-}linearization}\) when \(r=1\) and in \(\mathrm{lem:stabilized\mbox{-}gridwise\mbox{-}fpcr\mbox{-}transfer}\) when \(r\ge2\). Uniformly over \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\) and every three-dimensional tail,
\[
 \chi_{r,n}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\tau_{r,n}}{\rho_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)}=\chi_{r,n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For every sufficiently large evaluated triple,
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}
 P\left\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le \widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_{r,n}\right\}
 \ge 1-\alpha+\frac12\delta_{r,n}(\alpha)
 \ge1-\alpha-o(1),
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P\widetilde c_r^\xi
 \le C\rho_r(N_0,K_T,K_D).
\]
The result permits singular covariance matrices and zero-variance coordinates, including the coordinate at time zero; \(\widetilde c_r^\xi\) is the measurable left-continuous generalized-inverse conditional \(1-\alpha+\delta_{r,n}(\alpha)\) quantile.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(m=n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\), and \(p_{\mathcal G}=2|\mathcal G|\).  Write \(a_n=a_{r,n}\), \(\delta_n=\delta_{r,n}(\alpha)\), \(\chi_n=\chi_{r,n}\), and \(\tau_n=\tau_{r,n}\).  The covariance-modulus lemma supplies a measurable event \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), contained in \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) at ranks at least two, such that
\[
 P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\le Ca_n^{1/2},
 \quad
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \le C\rho_r^2a_n^{1/2},                                \tag{1}
\]
and both oracle and empirical canonical semimetrics are bounded by
\(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\).  Thus all empirical coefficients used on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) belong to the single regular branch; no derivative across a cutoff is used.

Let \(k=k_{r,n}\), and choose a deterministic subset \(\mathcal T_n\subset\mathcal G\) of at most \(k+1\) knots with physical-time mesh at most \(C/k\).  Evaluate both signs of each normalized patient vector on \(\mathcal T_n\); its dimension is at most
\[
 p_{\mathrm c,r,n}=2(k_{r,n}+1).                        \tag{2}
\]
The patient envelopes in the covariance-modulus lemma and \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\) give
\[
 \sum_iE\|L_i/\rho_r\|_{2,\mathcal T_n}^3
 \le Cp_{\mathrm c,r,n}^{3/2}m^{-1/2}.                 \tag{3}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:yurinskii\mbox{-}degeneracy\mbox{-}robust\mbox{-}coupling}\) in dimension \(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}\) with \(a=U_{r,n}^{1/4}\).  Its dimensioned quantity satisfies
\[
 B_0\le Cp_{\mathrm c,r,n}^{5/2}m^{-1/2}a^{-3}
 \le U_{r,n}^{1/4}.                                     \tag{4}
\]
Hence the coarse-net oracle vector couples to a centered Gaussian vector \(G_r\) with the same covariance with normalized error and exceptional probability bounded by \(C\zeta_{r,n}\).  The oracle modulus bound from the covariance-modulus lemma with \(u=C/k\), and the Gaussian union bound using the same semimetric, transfer the coupling to the full canonical grid.  Therefore, for every real \(x\),
\[
 P\{\|G_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\}
 \le P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
 \le x+C\rho_r\zeta_{r,n}\}+C\zeta_{r,n}.             \tag{5}
\]
This coupling allows singular covariance.

Conditionally on the data, let \(\widehat G_r\) be Gaussian with covariance \(\widehat\Sigma_r\); this is the law of the explicitly defined multiplier process.  Put \(v_n=a_n^{1/2}\).  On \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\), select an auxiliary grid of mesh at most \(u_n=v_n^{1/3}\) and cardinality \(K_n\le Cv_n^{-1/3}\).  The covariance matrices on that grid differ in nuclear norm by at most
\[
 CK_n^2\rho_r^2v_n.                                     \tag{6}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:powers\mbox{-}stormer\mbox{-}square\mbox{-}root}\), a common standard-normal vector couples them so that
\[
 E\|G_r-\widehat G_r\|_{2,\mathrm{aux}}
 \le C\rho_rK_nv_n^{1/2}\le C\rho_ra_n^{1/12}.         \tag{7}
\]
The two semimetric bounds and Gaussian exponential moments transfer (7) to the canonical grid.  Markov's inequality at threshold
\[
 \rho_ra_n^{1/48}\{1+|\log a_n|\}                     \tag{8}
\]
and (5), after enlarging \(C_\chi\), yield on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) the simultaneous one-sided comparison
\[
 \widehat F_r(x)
 \le F_{r,P}(x+\rho_r\chi_n)+\chi_n
 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R,                  \tag{9}
\]
where
\[
 F_{r,P}(x)=P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\},
 \qquad
 \widehat F_r(x)=P_\xi\{\|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}\le x\mid O\}.
\]
No anti-concentration or coordinate-variance lower bound is used.

The rates are explicit.  Since \(p_{\mathrm c,r,n}=O(m^{1/9})\),
\[
 U_{r,n}=O(m^{-2/9}),
 \qquad \zeta_{r,n}=O\{m^{-1/18}(1+\log m)\}.           \tag{10}
\]
Every term in \(a_n\) tends to zero uniformly on a three-dimensional tail, irrespective of relative growth.  Hence
\[
 \chi_n\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \tau_n/\rho_r=\chi_n\longrightarrow0.          \tag{11}
\]
If \(A=\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\), the terms in \(\chi_n\) decay as positive powers of \(A\), up to logarithms, whereas \(\delta_n\) decreases only at the displayed reciprocal iterated-logarithm rate.  Since \(P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\le Ca_n^{1/2}\), for every sufficiently large triple
\[
 \chi_n+P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\le\delta_n/2.             \tag{12}
\]

Let \(q_n=1-\alpha+\delta_n\), and define the deterministic oracle generalized-inverse quantile
\[
 c_{r,P}(u)=\inf\{x\ge0:F_{r,P}(x)\ge u\}.             \tag{13}
\]
The conditional Gaussian maximum is a Borel function of a finite standard-normal vector and the observed covariance matrix.  Taking the infimum over nonnegative rationals proves measurability of \(\widetilde c_r^\xi\), including when the covariance is singular or zero.  Right-continuity of the conditional distribution gives
\(\widehat F_r(\widetilde c_r^\xi)\ge q_n\).  Applying (9) at this random argument shows on \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\) that
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_n
 \ge c_{r,P}(q_n-\chi_n).                               \tag{14}
\]
The right side is deterministic for fixed \(P\).  Therefore, without any independence between \(Z_r\) and \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\{\|Z_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}
       \le\widetilde c_r^\xi+\tau_n\}
 &\ge F_{r,P}\{c_{r,P}(q_n-\chi_n)\}
       -P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\\
 &\ge q_n-\chi_n-P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\\
 &\ge1-\alpha+\delta_n/2,
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{15}
\]
where the last line uses (12).

Finally \(q_n\le1-\alpha/2\).  Conditional Markov inequality implies
\[
 \widetilde c_r^\xi
 \le\left\{\frac2\alpha E_\xi
 \|\widehat Z_r^\xi\|_{\infty,\mathcal G}^2\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{16}
\]
The pooled and stabilized branchwise moment bounds, followed by Jensen's inequality, prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_r}E_P\widetilde c_r^\xi\le C\rho_r. \tag{17}
\]
Equations (11), (15), and (17) prove every assertion, including the zero-variance coordinate at time zero. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem validates the singular-covariance-safe generalized-inverse multiplier quantile through a coarse-net coupling and eventwise covariance transfer. \textit{Gap.} Standard bootstrap validity does not automatically cover an estimated projector, inverse pre-Gram, hard fallback, and zero-variance coordinates. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the random critical radius in the honest functional-PCR band.

\begin{theorem}[thm:no-uniform-unknown-rank-adaptation]\label{thm:no-uniform-unknown-rank-adaptation}
Let \(\mathfrak Q_{\leq2}(N_0,K_T,K_D)\) be the separately normalized
rank-at-most-two closure in
\(\mathrm{def:rank\mbox{-}at\mbox{-}most\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}closure}\).  There are
constants \(a_0<\infty\) and \(c_\star>0\), independent of
\(N_0,K_T,K_D\), such that, whenever
\(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\geq a_0\), every random continuous-time band \(B\)
satisfying
\[
\inf_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{\leq2}(N_0,K_T,K_D)}
Q\left\{B^{\mathrm{lo}}(t)\leq\theta_Q(t)\leq B^{\mathrm{up}}(t)
\text{ for every }t\in\mathcal I\right\}
\geq1-\alpha-\varepsilon_{N_0,K_T,K_D}(\alpha)
\]
also satisfies
\[
\sup_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{\leq2}(N_0,K_T,K_D)}E_Q\{w(B)\}\geq c_\star.
\]
The constant band with endpoints zero and one has width one and is honest, so the
corresponding minimax maximal expected width is between \(c_\star\) and \(1\).
Hence its sharp order is one.  In particular, over the no-common-pre-Gram closure
there is no single rank-selection band attaining the vanishing known-rank oracle
widths \(\rho_1\) and \(\rho_2\).  This remains true on a rank-two subfamily whose
joint donor contrast singular value is bounded away from zero: donor-rank
detectability alone does not make the target loading estimable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
By definition,
\(\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}(N_0,K_T,K_D)\subset
\mathfrak Q_{\le2}(N_0,K_T,K_D)\).  Hence any band honest over the latter class is honest over the donor-visible rank-two subclass.  The donor-visible boundary theorem therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{\le2}}E_Qw(B)
 \ge\sup_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}}E_Qw(B)
 \ge c_\star.                                           \tag{1}
\]
The deterministic band \([0,1]\) covers every survival curve and has width one, proving the matching upper bound.  Thus the minimax maximal expected width over the closure is \(\Theta(1)\).  In contrast, the known-rank widths over the strong positive-pre-Gram classes vanish by the sharp-rank theorem because \(\rho_1,\rho_2\to0\) and the stated upper envelope vanishes.  The explicit weak pair inside \(\mathfrak Q_{2,\mathrm{vis}}\) has joint donor contrast bounded away from zero, so the obstruction persists when donor rank is visible: the missing ingredient is target-relevant pre-period strength. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Subclass monotonicity transfers the donor-visible rank-two order-one converse to the full no-common-pre-Gram rank-at-most-two closure. \textit{Gap.} Generic testing reductions do not establish that observed donor rank can remain strong while the target loading is weakly identified in this survival panel. \textit{Consumer.} It rules out a uniformly shrinking rank-selection band over the stated closure and identifies target-pre strength as necessary for a smaller adaptive model.

\begin{lemma}[lem:fpcr-covariance-modulus-transfer]\label{lem:fpcr-covariance-modulus-transfer}
Fix \(r\ge1\), put \(n_D=N_0K_D\), \(m=n_{\mathrm{eff},r}\), and let
\(a_{r,n}\) be the deterministic covariance-transfer schedule in
\(\mathrm{def:fpcr\mbox{-}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\).  Let \(\Sigma_r\) be
the covariance kernel of the oracle patient linear process \(Z_r\), let
\(\widehat\Sigma_r\) be the conditional covariance kernel of
\(\widehat Z_r^\xi\), and let \(d_r\) and \(\widehat d_r\) be their canonical
Gaussian semimetrics.  There is a measurable event \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\),
contained in the regular-margin event \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) when \(r\ge2\),
such that, uniformly over \(P\in\mathfrak P_r(N_0,K_T,K_D)\),
\[
 P(\mathcal E_{r,n}^c)\le C a_{r,n}^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_{\infty,\mathcal I^2}
 \le C\rho_r^2a_{r,n}^{1/2}\quad\hbox{on }\mathcal E_{r,n},
\]
and, for all grid points \(g,g'\),
\[
 d_r(g,g')^2+\widehat d_r(g,g')^2
 \le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\quad\hbox{on }\mathcal E_{r,n}.
\]
Moreover the oracle summands have deterministic envelopes \(Cn_D^{-1}\) for
rank one, and \(CK_T^{-1}\) and \(Cn_D^{-1}\) for target-pre and donor
patients when \(r\ge2\).  If \(p_{\mathcal G}=2|\mathcal G|\), then for every
\(u\in[h_{N_0,K_T,K_D},\bar\tau]\),
\[
 P\left\{
 \max_{\substack{g,g'\in\mathcal G\\|g-g'|\le u}}
 |Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')|>
 C\rho_r\left[\{u\log(ep_{\mathcal G})\}^{1/2}
 +m^{-1/2}\log(ep_{\mathcal G})\right]\right\}
 \le 2p_{\mathcal G}^{-4}.
\]
The empirical semimetric assertion uses the explicitly defined branchwise
multiplier coefficients; it does not assert differentiability of the total
hard-switched map across a cutoff, and it permits singular covariance and
zero-variance coordinates.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(Z_r=\sum_iL_i\).  The pooled linearization gives \(\|L_i\|_\infty\le Cn_D^{-1}\) for \(r=1\).  For \(r\ge2\), the stabilized transfer gives
\[
 \|L_i\|_\infty\le
 \begin{cases}
 CK_T^{-1},&i\text{ is target-pre},\\
 Cn_D^{-1},&i\text{ is a donor patient}.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{1}
\]

For rank one the pooled formulas are
\[
 \Sigma_1(s,t)=\frac{s_1(s)s_1(t)}{n_D}
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{\lambda_1(u)}{H_D(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_1(s,t)=\widehat S_D(s-)\widehat S_D(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\frac{J_Y(u)}{Y(u)^2}\,dN(u).       \tag{2}
\]
On \(\{Y(\bar\tau)\ge n_Ds_-g_-/2\}\), substitute \(dN=dM+Y\lambda_1du\).  Predictable isometry, the ordered risk-process maximal inequality, and the pooled expansion imply
\[
 E\{\rho_1^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\|_\infty\}
 \le Cn_D^{-1/2}.                                       \tag{3}
\]
The complement is exponentially small and the empty-risk convention bounds (2), so (3) is uniform under non-identical donor censoring.

Now suppose \(r\ge2\), and use the measurable event \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) constructed in the stabilized-transfer proof.  On that event, all population-to-observed and one-replacement covariance segments lie in one fixed-gap neighborhood.  For the coordinate-free regular prediction map \(F\), the projector resolvent identity, the positive-square-root Sylvester equation, and the inverse-Gram identity give, without selecting a Lipschitz eigenvector frame,
\[
 E\bigl[\|D\widehat F-DF\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}\}\bigr]
 \le C\{K_T^{-1/2}+n_D^{-1/2}+\eta_D(K_D)\}.           \tag{4}
\]
For a stabilized input of size \(k\), put \(H_k=S^0G\) and
\[
 V_k(s,t)=S^0(s)S^0(t)\int_0^{s\wedge t}
 \frac{\lambda^0(u)}{H_k(u)}\,du,
\]
\[
 \widehat V_k(s,t)=k\widehat S_k^\dagger(s-)
 \widehat S_k^\dagger(t-)
 \int_0^{s\wedge t}\{Y_k^\dagger(u)\}^{-2}\,dN_k(u). \tag{5}
\]
After substituting \(dN_k=dM_k+Y_k\lambda^0du\), clipping bounds every inverse risk on every dataset.  Predictable isometry and the stabilized cell expansion give
\[
 E\|\widehat V_k-V_k\|_{\infty,\mathcal I^2}\le Ck^{-1/2}. \tag{6}
\]
Composing (4)--(6), summing the target and donor covariance blocks, and using the deterministic bias bound yields
\[
 E\left[\rho_r^{-2}
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}\}\right]
 \le Ca_{r,n}.                                          \tag{7}
\]

We next control the complement pointwise, rather than multiplying its probability by an unconditional moment.  On every dataset assigned to the regular branch, the hard thresholds and stabilized coefficients give
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r\|_\infty\le C\rho_r^2.            \tag{8}
\]
On the pooled fallback each of the \(n_D\) coefficients has supremum norm at most one, hence
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_r\|_\infty\le n_D.                  \tag{9}
\]
Equation (1) also gives \(\|\Sigma_r\|_\infty\le C\rho_r^2\), and \(\rho_r^2\ge n_D^{-1}\).  Therefore, on every dataset,
\[
 \rho_r^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty
 \mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\}
 \le C(1+n_D^2)\mathbf1\{\mathcal M_{r,n}^c\}.        \tag{10}
\]
The exponential margin bound implies
\[
 E[\text{left side of (10)}]
 \le Cn_D^2e^{-cn_D}=o(n_D^{-1/2}).                    \tag{11}
\]
Thus (11) is absorbed by the existing \(n_D^{-1/2}\) term in \(a_{r,n}\).  Combining (3), (7), and (11) proves
\[
 E\{\rho_r^{-2}\|\widehat\Sigma_r-\Sigma_r\|_\infty\}
 \le Ca_{r,n}.                                          \tag{12}
\]
Markov's inequality at level \(a_{r,n}^{1/2}\), intersected with \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\) for \(r\ge2\), supplies a measurable covariance event with complement probability at most \(Ca_{r,n}^{1/2}\) and covariance error at most \(C\rho_r^2a_{r,n}^{1/2}\).

For the semimetric, let \(J=(g,g']\) be a grid interval, and let \(N_T(J)\) and \(N_D(J)\) count target-pre and donor failures, omitting \(N_T\) at rank one.  Hazard smoothness gives
\[
 EN_T(J)\le LK_T|J|,
 \qquad EN_D(J)\le Ln_D|J|.                             \tag{13}
\]
The Bernoulli exponential-moment inequality and a union bound over the finite grid give a measurable event on which, simultaneously over all such intervals,
\[
 N_T(J)\le CK_T(|J|+h),
 \qquad N_D(J)\le Cn_D(|J|+h),                           \tag{14}
\]
and whose complement is at most \(C|\mathcal G|^2e^{-cmh}\).  Since \(h\asymp\rho_r^{1/q_\beta}\), \(q_\beta>3/2\), and \(\rho_r\asymp m^{-1/2}\), this probability is bounded by \(Ca_{r,n}^{1/2}\) on every sufficiently large three-dimensional tail.

On the intersection with the covariance event and \(\mathcal M_{r,n}\), the empirical coefficients remain on the regular branch.  Smooth coefficient changes contribute \(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|^2\); the failures in \(J\), (1), and (14) contribute
\[
 C\{K_T^{-2}N_T(J)+n_D^{-2}N_D(J)\}.
\]
For distinct knots \(|g-g'|\ge h\), so
\[
 \widehat d_r(g,g')^2\le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|.              \tag{15}
\]
Taking expectations in the same calculation yields \(d_r(g,g')^2\le C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\).  This intersection is the asserted measurable \(\mathcal E_{r,n}\).

Finally, every oracle increment is a sum of independent centered variables, has variance at most \(C\rho_r^2|g-g'|\), and has summand envelope at most \(C\rho_rm^{-1/2}\).  Bernstein's exponential-moment calculation gives
\[
 P\{|Z_r(g)-Z_r(g')|>x\}
 \le2\exp\left[-c\min\left\{
 \frac{x^2}{\rho_r^2|g-g'|},
 \frac{x\sqrt m}{\rho_r}\right\}\right].             \tag{16}
\]
Insert
\[
 x=C\rho_r\left[\{u\log(ep_{\mathcal G})\}^{1/2}
 +m^{-1/2}\log(ep_{\mathcal G})\right]
\]
and take a union bound over at most \(p_{\mathcal G}^2\) signed pairs.  Enlarging \(C\) gives the stated \(2p_{\mathcal G}^{-4}\) bound.  No variance was inverted. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The measurable margin event and pointwise fallback covariance bound make the conditional covariance and semimetric transfer valid on every dataset branch. \textit{Gap.} Unconditional multiplier moments alone cannot be multiplied by a rare-event probability; a pointwise polynomial envelope is needed on the margin complement. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the covariance, entropy, and modulus event used in the conditional Gaussian comparison.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal target is the missing post-control curve, while the functional-PCR curve and multiplier band are observed-data procedures. Within the positive-pre-Gram exact-rank class, rank one removes the loading channel and higher rank retains it. Across the larger closure, a strong donor contrast singular value can coexist with a vanishing target-pre Gram, making opposite target loadings nearly observationally indistinguishable while their post-control curves remain separated. Therefore donor-rank detection is not a substitute for target-relevant pre-period strength, but this information-theoretic statement does not attribute an invalid band claim to any PCR or HSVT method.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

For fixed \(r\ge2\), the proved unrestricted conclusion is only \(c\rho_r\le W_r^*\le C\{\rho_r+\eta_D(K_D)\}\). The exponential risk-exhaustion term is an estimator-specific upper allowance; no matching \(\eta_D\) converse and no estimator that removes it uniformly over every relative-growth sequence are proved. Thus tightness outside \(\eta_D(K_D)=O(\rho_r)\) remains unresolved. The hard-switch analysis gives total-map derivative and finite-replacement bounds only on the exponentially likely regular-margin event; off that event it uses separately controlled branchwise coefficients and a bounded switching remainder. Exact fixed rank, independent censoring, a fixed horizon, hazard smoothness, bounded loadings and contrast spectrum, and a positive pre-period Gram remain assumptions of the positive result.

\section{Honest scope}

Honesty and expected width are evaluated at the same sample-size triple, with a deterministic coverage-error allowance vanishing uniformly whenever \(\min\{N_0,K_T,K_D\}\to\infty\), and no balanced-growth restriction. Over the strong exact-rank class, the rank-one frontier is sharp and the higher-rank theorem gives the unrestricted bracket, becoming sharp only in the declared \(\eta_D=O(\rho_r)\) subregimes. Over the separately normalized rank-at-most-two closure without a common positive pre-period Gram, minimax maximal expected width is \(\Theta(1)\), including on a donor-visible known-rank-two subclass inside the Han--Shah Theorem~1 factorization-and-span model. This is a generic boundary on what donor-rank information can establish, not a claim that Agarwal--Shah--Shen or another cited prediction method licenses or is refuted by shrinking bands. Adaptation may still be possible on a smaller class with an explicit target-pre strength separation.
