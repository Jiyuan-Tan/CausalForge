\textit{Proof.}
Standardize \(X=\sqrt nY/\sigma\).  At the asserted amplitudes, under \(P_0\)
we have \(X\sim N(0,I_{12})\), and every component likelihood ratio is
\(L_g(X)=\exp(\nu_g^{\mathsf T}X-4)\), because
\(\lVert\nu_g\rVert_2^2=8\).

We first give a finite lower certificate for the mixture dual.  Retain the
sixteen components for which
\(S=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,s_2)\) and
\(R=\operatorname{diag}(r_1,r_2)\), with each sign in \(\{-1,1\}\).  Give
each retained component weight \(b_g=5/16\), give the other components weight
zero, and set \(a=1\).  If
\[
Z_i=X_{A,ii},\qquad W_i=X_{F,ii},\qquad
V_i=2^{-1/2}(X_{G,1i}+X_{G,2i}),
\]
then the two triples \((Z_i,W_i,V_i)\) are independent standard Gaussian
vectors.  Averaging the retained likelihood ratios over their signs gives
\[
\Lambda=e^{-4}K(Z_1,W_1,V_1)K(Z_2,W_2,V_2),
\]
where
\[
K(z,w,v)=\frac12\left\{e^{\sqrt2v}\cosh(z+w)
+e^{-\sqrt2v}\cosh(z-w)\right\}.
\]
Here is an explicit directed-rounding certificate for the only numerical
step.  Partition \([-4,4]^3\) into the \(512000\) boxes of side \(1/10\).
For a box
\(B=[z_-,z_+]\times[w_-,w_+]\times[v_-,v_+]\), let
\[
\begin{split}
p_B&=\{\Phi(z_+)-\Phi(z_-)\}
     \{\Phi(w_+)-\Phi(w_-)\}
     \{\Phi(v_+)-\Phi(v_-)\},\\
k_B&=\frac12\left[e^{\sqrt2v_-}c([z_-+w_-,z_++w_+])
+e^{-\sqrt2v_+}c([z_--w_+,z_+-w_-])\right],
\end{split}
\]
where \(c(I)=1\) if \(0\in I\), and otherwise
\(c(I)=\min_{x\in I}\cosh x\).  Monotonicity of the exponential and the exact
minimum of \(\cosh\) on an interval give \(K\geq k_B\) throughout \(B\).
Discarding the nonnegative contribution outside the two copies of
\([-4,4]^3\) therefore gives the rigorous finite sum
\[
E_{P_0}\min\{5\Lambda,1\}
\geq \sum_{B,B'}p_Bp_{B'}
\min\{5e^{-4}k_Bk_{B'},1\}.
\]
Sorting the \(k_B\)'s reduces this sum to \(O(512000\log 512000)\) operations:
prefix sums of \(p_B\) and \(p_Bk_B\) evaluate the inner sum on either side of
the cutoff \(k_{B'}=(5e^{-4}k_B)^{-1}\).  Rational Taylor enclosures for
\(e^x\), \(\cosh x\), and the Gaussian integral, with every lower endpoint
rounded downward, give
\[
0.99980<\sum_Bp_B<0.99982,
\qquad
\sum_{B,B'}p_Bp_{B'}\min\{5e^{-4}k_Bk_{B'},1\}>0.70380.
\]
Thus the displayed feasible dual point proves
\[
\mathfrak D_{0.05}^{\mathrm{mix}}
\geq0.70380-0.05(1+5)=0.40380>0.40.
\]

For the pairwise upper bound, fix one component and write
\(d=\sqrt8\).  Under \(P_0\),
\(\log L=dZ-d^2/2\) with \(Z\sim N(0,1)\).  If \(a\leq1\), putting
\(\lambda=c/a\) shows that the pairwise objective equals
\[
a\{E\min(\lambda L,1)-0.05(1+\lambda)\};
\]
if \(a\geq1\), lowering \(a\) to one can only improve the objective.  Hence a
positive optimum has \(a=1\).  Differentiation away from the null event
\(\lambda L=1\) gives the unique optimizer
\[
\lambda_*=
\exp\{-d^2/2-d\Phi^{-1}(0.05)\},
\]
and a change of measure gives the exact value
\[
\mathfrak D_{0.05}^{\mathrm{pair}}
=1-\Phi\{d+\Phi^{-1}(0.05)\}-0.05.
\]
All components have the same norm, so the maximum over \(g\) does not change
this expression.  Directed normal-CDF evaluation gives
\[
-1.644854<\Phi^{-1}(0.05)<-1.644853,
\qquad
0.06828<\mathfrak D_{0.05}^{\mathrm{pair}}<0.06830<0.20.
\]
The amplitude condition makes \((h,q)\) admissible.  The exact-dual theorem
then turns the strict dual inequality into a strict expected-length gap, so a
maximum of one-component moduli cannot equal the simultaneous-orbit minimax
value. \(\square\)
