\textit{Proof.}
Every positive-rank member of \(\mathcal U\) satisfies \(\lVert M\rVert_\infty\leq B_{\max}\), and the only rank-zero matrix is zero.  Consequently every observed coordinate and every target value in the generating set for \(\overline{\mathcal K}\) lies in \([-B_{\max},B_{\max}]\).  Its closure is therefore compact and satisfies
\[
 \overline{\mathcal K}\subset[-B_{\max},B_{\max}]^{12}\times[-B_{\max},B_{\max}].
\tag{1}
\]
It follows that, whenever \(T(y)\neq\varnothing\), the infimum and supremum defining \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}(y)\) are finite and attained.  Equation (1) also gives
\(\operatorname{len}C_{\mathrm{prof}}(y)\leq2B_{\max}\).

We next verify measurability without imposing any unproved regularity on the union.  Let \(K_\mu\) be the projection of \(\overline{\mathcal K}\) onto its first twelve coordinates.  It is compact, and
\[
 \{y:T(y)\neq\varnothing\}=\{y:\operatorname{dist}(y,K_\mu)\leq\rho_\alpha\}
\tag{2}
\]
is closed.  For each real \(c\), the projections onto \(\mu\)-space of
\[
 \overline{\mathcal K}\cap\{(\mu,t):t\geq c\},
 \qquad
 \overline{\mathcal K}\cap\{(\mu,t):t\leq c\}
\]
are compact.  The events \(\{\sup T(y)\geq c\}\) and \(\{\inf T(y)\leq c\}\) are their respective closed \(\rho_\alpha\)-neighborhoods.  Together with (2) and the fixed value zero off (2), this proves that both endpoints are Borel measurable.  Thus \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\) is a measurable nonempty connected closed interval rule.

Fix any \(M\in\mathcal U\), and put \(\mu=P_\Omega M\) and \(t=\tau(M)\).  The pair \((\mu,t)\) belongs to the generating set and hence to \(\overline{\mathcal K}\).  By the Gaussian sequence assumption,
\[
 \frac{n}{\sigma^2}\lVert Y-\mu\rVert_2^2\sim\chi^2_{12}.
\tag{3}
\]
The definition of \(q_{12,1-\alpha}\) and continuity of the chi-square distribution give
\[
 P_M\{\lVert Y-\mu\rVert_2\leq\rho_\alpha\}=1-\alpha.
\tag{4}
\]
On the event in (4), \(t\in T(Y)\), so \(t\in C_{\mathrm{prof}}(Y)\).  Therefore
\[
 \inf_{M\in\mathcal U}P_M\{\tau(M)\in C_{\mathrm{prof}}(Y)\}\geq1-\alpha,
\]
which proves \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\in\mathfrak C_\alpha(\mathcal U)\).  Notice that no rank, strength, or orbit amplitude is supplied to the rule.

The pointwise length bound gives
\[
 E_{P_0}\operatorname{len}C_{\mathrm{prof}}(Y)\leq2B_{\max}.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\) is union-honest, the definition of the rank-zero frontier gives the left inequality in the claimed ratio.  Theorem~\ref{thm:union-zero-lower} gives
\(\mathfrak L_0^{\mathcal U}(n)\geq B_{\max}(1-2\alpha)>0\); combining this with (5) proves the upper inequality.

Finally, the membership relation used by the endpoint programs is explicitly
\[
 (\mu,t)\in\overline{\mathcal K}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists M\in\mathcal U:\
 \lVert P_\Omega M-\mu\rVert_2^2+|\tau(M)-t|^2<\varepsilon^2.
\tag{6}
\]
For the fixed four-by-four mask, membership in \(\mathcal U\) is a finite Boolean combination of entry bounds, vanishing and nonvanishing minors for ranks zero, one, or two, and the condition that the smallest positive singular value of the two-by-two anchor does not exceed \(B_{\max}\).  The last condition can itself be written with an auxiliary two-dimensional unit vector and polynomial inequalities.  Hence (6), together with
\(\lVert y-\mu\rVert_2^2\leq\rho_\alpha^2\), is a first-order formula over the reals in a fixed finite number of variables.  Quantifier elimination decides each endpoint sublevel query; bisection on the compact interval \([-B_{\max},B_{\max}]\) evaluates the two extrema to any prescribed accuracy. \(\square\)
