\textit{Proof.}
Let \(\delta=q^2/h>0\).  An arbitrary feasible connected interval can be
shortened pointwise as follows: replace it by \([0,\delta]\) if it contains
both target values, by the appropriate singleton if it contains exactly one,
and by \(\{0\}\) if it contains neither.  This operation is measurable, cannot
increase length, cannot reduce coverage of either target, and leaves only the
three actions
\[
e_0=\{0\},\qquad e_\delta=\{\delta\},\qquad
e_B=[0,\delta].
\]
For convex analysis, temporarily permit a randomized kernel
\((p_0,p_\delta,p_B)\) on these actions.  The normalized primal value
\(V=\mathfrak L_{0,n}^{\mathrm{orb}}(h,q)/\delta\) is
\[
\begin{split}
\inf_p\quad &E_{P_0}p_B(Y),\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&E_{P_0}p_\delta(Y)\leq\alpha,\\
&E_{P_0}[L_g^{h,q}(Y)p_0(Y)]\leq\alpha
\quad(g\in\mathcal G),\\
&p_0(y)+p_\delta(y)+p_B(y)=1,\qquad p_j(y)\geq0.
\end{split}
\]
The first inequality is null noncoverage and the next sixty-four inequalities
are alternative noncoverage.  All sixty-five Gaussian measures are atomless.
The finite-action purification lemma therefore replaces any randomized kernel
by a measurable pure action while preserving its objective and all sixty-five
constraint integrals.  Consequently the randomized and original deterministic
primal values agree, and primal attainment for the compact finite-dimensional
risk image gives an attaining data-only interval.

Introduce nonnegative multipliers \(a\) and \(b_g\), and put
\(B_b(y)=\sum_gb_gL_g^{h,q}(y)\).  The Lagrangian, divided by \(\delta\), is
\[
-\alpha\!\left(a+\sum_gb_g\right)
+E_{P_0}\!\left[p_0B_b+p_\delta a+p_B\right].
\]
Minimization over the pointwise probability simplex gives
\(\min\{B_b(y),a,1\}\).  Weak duality therefore gives exactly the functional
in \(\mathrm{def:orbit-duals}\).  For completeness, strong duality follows
directly by separating the compact convex risk image of the three-action
kernels from the open set consisting of vectors with all noncoverage
coordinates below \(\alpha\) and objective below \(V\).  The separating
coefficients of the noncoverage coordinates are nonnegative; normalizing the
objective coefficient gives the displayed \((a,b)\)-dual.  The always-covering
action \(e_B\) is strictly feasible because \(\alpha>0\), so the objective
coefficient cannot vanish.  This proves equality of the primal and dual values.

It remains to verify dual attainment.  Its objective is continuous on the
nonnegative orthant: the integrand is Lipschitz in \((a,b)\), with integrable
Lipschitz envelope \(1+\sum_gL_g\), and \(E_{P_0}L_g=1\).  Moreover,
\[
E_{P_0}\min\{B_b,a,1\}-\alpha\!\left(a+\sum_gb_g\right)
\leq1-\alpha\!\left(a+\sum_gb_g\right).
\]
The value at the origin is zero, so no maximizer with
\(a+\sum_gb_g>1/\alpha\) is needed.  Continuity on the resulting compact
simplex proves that the supremum is attained.  Purifying an attaining primal
kernel yields a measurable rule with no auxiliary randomization.  Its action
is a function only of the likelihood ratios, hence of
\(Y,n,\sigma,h,q\) and the fixed public orbit and confidence level.  Multiplying
the normalized value by \(\delta=q^2/h\) completes the proof. \(\square\)
