\textit{Proof.}
Fix a normalization-legal grid.  All target values are finite and \(\mathcal A_N\) is finite.  Write
\[
 \Delta_N=\max_i t_i-\min_i t_i.
\]
Then every action loss lies in \([0,\Delta_N]\), while all Gaussian measures with densities \(f_i\) are atomless and mutually absolutely continuous.

First consider the risk vector consisting of the \(N\) coordinates \(Q_i(p)\) and the \(N\) coordinates \(R_i(p)\).  The set of such vectors is convex.  It is also compact: the kernel simplex is a weak-star closed subset of the finite product of the unit balls of \(L^\infty(\sum_iP_i)\), and integration against the finitely many bounded \(L^1(\sum_iP_i)\) action losses is continuous for that topology.  Thus each \(L_{k,N}\) is achieved, and the continuous function
\[
 (Q,R)\longmapsto\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\frac{R_i}{L_{k(i),N}}
\]
achieves its minimum on the closed subset \(Q_i\geq1-\alpha\).  This proves primal attainment.  The positivity in the definition of normalization-legality is exactly the boundary condition needed for these divisions.

Introduce multipliers \(\lambda_i\geq0\) for
\((1-\alpha)-Q_i(p)\leq0\) and \(w_i\geq0\) for
\(R_i(p)-\kappa L_{k(i),N}\leq0\).  The Lagrangian is
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal L(p,\kappa;\lambda,w)
 ={}&\kappa\left\{1-\sum_iw_iL_{k(i),N}\right\}
 +(1-\alpha)\sum_i\lambda_i\\
 &+\int\sum_{A\in\mathcal A_N}p_A(y)
 \sum_i f_i(y)\{w_i\operatorname{len}(A)-\lambda_i\mathbf1(t_i\in A)\}\,dy.
\end{split}
\tag{1}
\]
For fixed multipliers, minimization over the pointwise probability simplex replaces the last line by
\[
 \int\min_{A\in\mathcal A_N}\sum_i f_i(y)
 \{w_i\operatorname{len}(A)-\lambda_i\mathbf1(t_i\in A)\}\,dy.
\tag{2}
\]
Minimization over \(\kappa\geq0\) gives the preliminary dual constraint
\(\sum_iw_iL_{k(i),N}\leq1\).  The objective in (2), together with the first term in \(\lambda\), is positively homogeneous in \((\lambda,w)\).  If a feasible point with strict inequality has positive objective, scaling it until equality only increases that objective.  If its objective is nonpositive, choose any \(w\geq0\) with \(\sum_iw_iL_{k(i),N}=1\) and put \(\lambda=0\); because \(\mathcal A_N\) contains a singleton of length zero, this choice has objective zero.  Finally, if \(w=0\), comparison in (2) with the interval containing all target values shows that the objective is at most \(-\alpha\sum_i\lambda_i\).  Hence replacing the preliminary inequality by
\[
 \sum_iw_iL_{k(i),N}=1
\tag{3}
\]
does not change the dual value.  Equations (1)--(3) give exactly the displayed dual.

Strong duality follows in finite-dimensional risk space.  Indeed, the always-covering action
\(A_{\mathrm{all}}=[\min_i t_i,\max_i t_i]\) has \(Q_i=1>1-\alpha\), and choosing
\[
 \kappa>\max_i\frac{\Delta_N}{L_{k(i),N}}
\]
makes every risk constraint strict.  Separation of the compact convex risk image from a strictly lower objective half-space therefore gives no duality gap, with (1) as its separating functional.

It remains to prove dual attainment.  From (3), positivity of the finitely many \(L_{k,N}\) bounds every \(w_i\).  Evaluating the pointwise minimum at \(A_{\mathrm{all}}\), integrating \(f_i\), and using (3) gives the upper bound
\[
 (1-\alpha)\sum_i\lambda_i+\int\min_A(\cdots)
 \leq \Delta_N\sum_iw_i-\alpha\sum_i\lambda_i.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus no maximizing sequence with \(\sum_i\lambda_i\to\infty\) is needed.  On the resulting compact multiplier set the objective is continuous, because the pointwise minimum is Lipschitz in the finitely many multipliers with integrable envelope proportional to \(\sum_i f_i\).  The supremum is attained.

An attaining randomized kernel has only the finite action set \(\mathcal A_N\).  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-action-purification} to the atomless measures \(P_1,\ldots,P_N\) and to the two action losses \(\mathbf1\{t_i\in A\}\) and \(\operatorname{len}(A)\) for every \(i\).  The resulting measurable pure action preserves all \(2N\) integrals, so it is an attaining nonrandomized data-only connected interval.

For the orbit specialization, the distinct targets are \(0\) and \(\delta=q^2/h\).  Therefore \(\mathcal A_N\) consists exactly of \(\{0\}\), \(\{\delta\}\), and \([0,\delta]\).  If the linear objective is the null risk alone, pointwise shortening gives precisely these three actions; assigning a multiplier to null noncoverage and one to each of the sixty-four alternative noncoverage constraints turns (1)--(2), after division by \(\delta\), into
\[
 \sup_{a\geq0,\,b_g\geq0}
 \left(E_{P_0}\min\left\{\sum_gb_gL_g^{h,q},a,1\right\}
 -\alpha\left(a+\sum_gb_g\right)\right).
\]
This is the dual in Definition~\ref{def:orbit-duals} and Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit-exact-dual}.  The general grid permits more than two target values and simultaneously constrains normalized risk at every component, so the extension is strict. \(\square\)
