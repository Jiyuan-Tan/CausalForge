\textit{Proof.}
Every interval honest over \(\mathcal U\) covers the null and, by orbit
membership, all sixty-four alternatives for every
\((h,q)\in\mathcal A_B\).  It is therefore feasible for every orbit confidence
problem.  The exact-dual theorem implies, simultaneously for all admissible
\((h,q)\),
\[
E_{P_0}\operatorname{len}C(Y)
\geq\frac{q^2}{h}\mathfrak D_\alpha^{\mathrm{mix}}(h,q).
\]
Taking first the infimum over union-honest \(C\), then the supremum over
\((h,q)\), proves the first inequality.

For the second inequality, take \(q=\sqrt{B_{\max}h}\) and let
\(h\downarrow0\) through \(0<h\leq B_{\max}\).  Then
\((h,q)\in\mathcal A_B\) and \(q^2/h=B_{\max}\).  The three observed blocks
have amplitudes \(h,q,q\), so each \(\mu_g(h,q)\to0\).  The Gaussian
likelihood formula consequently gives \(L_g^{h,q}(Y)\to1\) in
\(P_0\)-probability, and bounded convergence applies after the outer minimum.
In the mixture dual choose \(a=1\) and \(b_g=1/64\).  Then
\[
\begin{split}
\liminf_{h\downarrow0}
\mathfrak D_\alpha^{\mathrm{mix}}(h,\sqrt{B_{\max}h})
&\geq
\lim_{h\downarrow0}E_{P_0}\min\left\{64^{-1}\sum_gL_g^{h,q},1\right\}
-2\alpha\\
&=1-2\alpha.
\end{split}
\]
Multiplication by the constant target separation \(B_{\max}\) and the first
inequality complete the proof.  The boundary is legitimate because
\(\alpha<1/2\) and the limiting argument uses only positive \(h,q\), never an
inadmissible zero-strength member. \(\square\)
