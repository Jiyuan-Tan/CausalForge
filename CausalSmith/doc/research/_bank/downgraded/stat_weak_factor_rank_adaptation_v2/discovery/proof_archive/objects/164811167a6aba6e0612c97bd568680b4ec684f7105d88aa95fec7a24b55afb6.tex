\textit{Proof.}
Fix \((h,q)\in\mathcal A_B\) and \(g=(S,R)\in\mathcal G\).  Signed-permutation
matrices are orthogonal, so \(S^{-1}=S^{\mathsf T}\), and the anchor
\(A=hS\) has rank two with both singular values equal to \(h\).  Direct
multiplication gives
\[
GA^{-1}F
=q\mathbf J R^{\mathsf T}S\,(hS)^{-1}qR
=\frac{q^2}{h}\mathbf J R^{\mathsf T}SS^{\mathsf T}R
=\frac{q^2}{h}\mathbf J=D.
\]
Applying the same invertible block elimination used in the block-completion
lemma shows
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_g(h,q)
=\operatorname{rank}(hS)+
 \operatorname{rank}\{D-GA^{-1}F\}=2.
\]
Every nonzero entry of \(A\) has absolute value \(h\), every nonzero entry of
\(F\) has absolute value \(q\), every entry of
\(G=q\mathbf J R^{\mathsf T}S\) has absolute value \(q\), and every entry of
\(D\) equals \(q^2/h\).  The three inequalities in the definition of
\(\mathcal A_B\) therefore imply \(\lVert M_g(h,q)\rVert_\infty\leq B_{\max}\).
This proves \(M_g(h,q)\in\mathcal M_2(h)\).  Moreover,
\[
\tau(M_g(h,q))
=\frac14\mathbf 1_2^{\mathsf T}\frac{q^2}{h}\mathbf J\mathbf 1_2
=\frac{q^2}{h}.
\]
Finally, under the uniform law on
\(\mathcal H_2\times\mathcal H_2\), the matrices \(S\) and \(R\) are
independent and each has mean zero.  Hence
\[
E[S]=0,\qquad E[R]=0,\qquad
E[\mathbf J R^{\mathsf T}S]=\mathbf J E[R]^{\mathsf T}E[S]=0.
\]
The observation map retains precisely these three blocks, so averaging their
vectorizations proves \(64^{-1}\sum_g\mu_g(h,q)=0\). \(\square\)
