\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}weak\_\allowbreak{}factor\_\allowbreak{}rank\_\allowbreak{}adaptation}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v2\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For the public four-block causal-panel mask, one explicit profile interval computed only from \(Y,n,\sigma,B_{\max},\alpha\) is honest over the entire unknown-rank, unknown-strength union and is uniformly oracle-competitive on every stratum. Rank zero and exact rank one have constant-order frontier \(B_{\max}\); exact rank two has the matched frontier \(B_{\max}\min\{1,B_{\max}\sigma/(\sqrt n\,s^2)\}\), with elbow \(s\asymp(B_{\max}\sigma)^{1/2}n^{-1/4}\). The exact finite-grid dual and the certified sixty-four-orientation mixture gap remain supporting results: they show why simultaneous mixture constraints, rather than maxima of pairwise Gaussian moduli, are the correct finite-experiment geometry. The exact optimal simultaneous excess and convergence of finite-grid programs to the continuum problem remain unresolved; the proved excess is bracketed by \([1,K_\alpha]\).

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing adaptive-confidence and causal-panel results do not provide a single connected interval that is honest over this exact rank-and-strength union while matching every stratum's expected-length frontier. Pairwise Gaussian moduli also fail to characterize even the finite zero-plus-sixty-four-orientation subexperiment, so the union problem requires simultaneous testing geometry.

\paragraph{Niche.} The fixed four-by-four mask is small enough that the continuum profile interval is evaluable by fixed-dimensional real quantifier elimination, yet it contains the weak-anchor boundary responsible for nonregular adaptation. This permits an exact separation of the constant rank-zero and rank-one frontiers from the strength-dependent rank-two frontier.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper gives the rank-, strength-, and amplitude-free interval \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\), proves union honesty, and proves a uniform oracle factor \(K_\alpha\). It matches the rank-two rate \(B_{\max}\min\{1,B_{\max}\sigma/(\sqrt n\,s^2)\}\) and proves constant-order rank-zero and rank-one frontiers. The exact orbit dual, strict mixture-versus-pairwise gap, and finite-grid simultaneous dual explain and compute the supporting finite-experiment geometry.

\section{Related work}

CaiLow2004AdaptiveCI provides the Gaussian ordered-modulus benchmark and constant-factor adaptive intervals, while CaiGuo2017HDLinearCI supplies composite-mixture lower-bound techniques. EvansHansenStark2005 informs the expected-measure decision dual. Those works use classes and decision problems materially different from the exact deterministic four-block union here, so the present result is not an equivalence with their models. CarpentierKloppLofflerNickl2018 studies honest low-rank adaptation under random matrix observation and full-matrix loss. YanWainwright2024MissingPanel studies entrywise missing-panel inference with fixed rank and signal conditions; ChoiKwonLiao2026UnknownRank uses an upper working rank and asymptotic signal requirements; ArmstrongWeidnerZeleneev2026 obtains factor-strength-robust bias-aware inference in a different interactive-effects class. These three panel classes are also materially different from the frozen Gaussian four-block class. Onatski2012WeakFactors motivates the weak-factor regime, XiaYuan2021LinearForms treats linear forms under random noisy completion, and NicklVandeGeer2013 supplies a growing-dimensional \(n^{-1/4}\) testing comparison. Here the elbow \(s\asymp(B_{\max}\sigma)^{1/2}n^{-1/4}\) follows directly from the matched rank-two frontier, whereas the fixed sixty-four-point orbit alone cannot generate a separate \(n^{1/4}\) mixture-affinity gap. Any transfer of the finite four-block converse to a larger published honesty class requires a separate inclusion theorem proving that the larger class contains this exact Gaussian subexperiment.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-sequence]\label{ass:gaussian-sequence}
\(Y\mid M\sim N(P_{\Omega}M,\sigma^2 n^{-1}I_{12})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Gaussian sequence experiment with known variance, \cite{CaiLow2004AdaptiveCI}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-signal]\label{ass:bounded-signal}
\(\lVert M\rVert_{\infty}\le B_{\max}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform entry bound for low-rank matrix completion, \cite{CarpentierKloppLofflerNickl2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rank-zero]\label{ass:rank-zero}
\(\operatorname{rank}(M)=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Rank-zero low-rank stratum, \cite{CarpentierKloppLofflerNickl2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-total-rank]\label{ass:exact-total-rank}
\(\operatorname{rank}(M)=r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exact low-rank matrix model, \cite{AtheyBayatiDoudchenkoImbensKhosravi2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-anchor-rank]\label{ass:exact-anchor-rank}
\(\operatorname{rank}(A)=r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Full observed-anchor rank condition, \cite{YanWainwright2024MissingPanel}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:anchor-strength]\label{ass:anchor-strength}
\(\sigma_r(A)=s\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exact smallest-positive-singular-value stratum, \cite{Onatski2012WeakFactors}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:simultaneous-inversion-handle]\label{def:simultaneous-inversion-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Simultaneous mixture-\allowbreak{}test inversion handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For a finite grid, let
\[
 \Theta_N=\{(\mu_i,t_i,k(i)):1\leq i\leq N\}
\]
mean that there is an \(M_i\in\mathcal U\) with \(\mu_i=P_\Omega M_i\) and \(t_i=\tau(M_i)\), and that \(k(i)=0\) for \(M_i\in\mathcal M_0\), while \(k(i)=(r,s)\) for \(M_i\in\mathcal M_r(s)\).  Put \(\mathcal T_N=\{t_i:1\leq i\leq N\}\) and
\[
 \mathcal A_N=\{[a,b]:a,b\in\mathcal T_N,\ a\leq b\}.
\]
The density of \(N(\mu_i,\sigma^2n^{-1}I_{12})\) is
\[
 f_i(y)=\left(\frac{n}{2\pi\sigma^2}\right)^6
 \exp\!\left\{-\frac{n}{2\sigma^2}\lVert y-\mu_i\rVert_2^2\right\}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal P_N\) be the set of Borel kernels \(p=(p_A:A\in\mathcal A_N)\) satisfying \(p_A(y)\geq0\) and \(\sum_Ap_A(y)=1\).  Define
\[
 Q_i(p)=\int_{\mathbb R^{12}}\sum_{A\in\mathcal A_N}
 \mathbf 1\{t_i\in A\}p_A(y)f_i(y)\,dy,
 \qquad
 R_i(p)=\int_{\mathbb R^{12}}\sum_{A\in\mathcal A_N}
 \operatorname{len}(A)p_A(y)f_i(y)\,dy,
\]
and, for each represented stratum label \(k\),
\[
 L_{k,N}=\inf_{p\in\mathcal P_N:\,Q_j(p)\geq1-\alpha\ \forall j}
 \max_{i:k(i)=k}R_i(p).
\]
Call the grid normalization-legal when \(L_{k,N}>0\) for every represented \(k\).  On such a grid the simultaneous finite-grid oracle factor is
\[
 \kappa_N^*=\inf_{\substack{\kappa\geq0,\ p\in\mathcal P_N}}
 \left\{\kappa:Q_i(p)\geq1-\alpha,\quad
 R_i(p)\leq\kappa L_{k(i),N}\quad(1\leq i\leq N)\right\}.
\]
For the continuum construction put
\[
 \overline{\mathcal K}=\operatorname{cl}\{(P_\Omega M,\tau(M)):M\in\mathcal U\}
 \subset\mathbb R^{12}\times\mathbb R,
 \qquad
 \rho_\alpha=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\sqrt{q_{12,1-\alpha}},
\]
where \(q_{12,1-\alpha}\) is the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of a chi-square random variable with twelve degrees of freedom.  Define
\[
 T(y)=\{t:\text{there is }(\mu,t)\in\overline{\mathcal K}
 \text{ with }\lVert y-\mu\rVert_2\leq\rho_\alpha\},
 \qquad
 C_{\mathrm{prof}}(y)=
 \begin{cases}
 [\inf T(y),\sup T(y)],&T(y)\neq\varnothing,\\
 \{0\},&T(y)=\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\]
The simultaneous-inversion handle consists of the finite-grid program \(\kappa_N^*\), its likelihood dual, and the continuum profile interval \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:block-completion-identification]\label{lem:block-completion-identification}
For every \(M\in\mathcal M_r(s)\), the missing block satisfies \(D=GA^{+}F\), where \(A^{+}\) is the Moore--Penrose inverse. Hence \(\tau(M)=\tfrac14\mathbf 1_2^{\mathsf T}GA^{+}F\mathbf 1_2\) is identified from \(P_{\Omega}M\) on each positive-strength stratum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
Fix \(M\in\mathcal M_r(s)\).  By the exact-rank assumptions,
\(\operatorname{rank}(M)=\operatorname{rank}(A)=r\).  Since \(A\) is a
submatrix of both \([A\ F]\) and \([A^{\mathsf T}\ G^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}\),
\[
r\leq \operatorname{rank}[A\ F]\leq \operatorname{rank}(M)=r,
\qquad
r\leq \operatorname{rank}\!\begin{bmatrix}A\\G\end{bmatrix}
\leq \operatorname{rank}(M)=r.
\]
Consequently \(\operatorname{col}(F)\subseteq\operatorname{col}(A)\) and
\(\operatorname{row}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{row}(A)\).  Thus there are
matrices \(X,Y_0\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}\) with \(F=AX\) and \(G=Y_0A\).
The Moore--Penrose identities give the particular choices
\(X=A^+F\) and \(Y_0=GA^+\).  Invertible block row and column operations now give
\[
\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\-Y_0&I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}A&F\\G&D\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}I&-X\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\0&D-Y_0AX\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The left side has rank \(r\), whereas the right side has rank
\(r+\operatorname{rank}(D-Y_0AX)\).  Hence \(D=Y_0AX=GA^+F\).
Substitution into the definition of the causal target yields
\[
\tau(M)=\frac14\mathbf 1_2^{\mathsf T}GA^+F\mathbf 1_2.
\]
All matrices on the right are contained in the observed vector
\(P_{\Omega}M=\operatorname{vec}(A,F,G)\); the positive-strength condition
\(s>0\) fixes the rank and ensures that the Moore--Penrose inverse is evaluated
on the asserted exact-rank stratum.  This proves the stated identifying
functional. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The statistical frontier is meaningful only after target-specific completion is fixed on every positive-strength stratum. \textit{Gap.} YanWainwright2024MissingPanel assumes signal and block conditions for entrywise inference; it does not isolate this finite four-block algebraic recovery map across exact ranks. \textit{Consumer.} A treated-posttreatment block average can be interpreted without requiring unique recovery of any matrix outside the stated exact-rank stratum.

\begin{lemma}[lem:orbit-membership]\label{lem:orbit-membership}
For every \((h,q)\in\mathcal A_B\) and \(g=(S,R)\in\mathcal G\), the construction \(M_g(h,q)\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_2(h)\), obeys \(D=GA^{-1}F=(q^2/h)\mathbf J\), and has \(\tau(M_g(h,q))=q^2/h\). Moreover, \(64^{-1}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\mu_g(h,q)=0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
Fix \((h,q)\in\mathcal A_B\) and \(g=(S,R)\in\mathcal G\).  Signed-permutation
matrices are orthogonal, so \(S^{-1}=S^{\mathsf T}\), and the anchor
\(A=hS\) has rank two with both singular values equal to \(h\).  Direct
multiplication gives
\[
GA^{-1}F
=q\mathbf J R^{\mathsf T}S\,(hS)^{-1}qR
=\frac{q^2}{h}\mathbf J R^{\mathsf T}SS^{\mathsf T}R
=\frac{q^2}{h}\mathbf J=D.
\]
Applying the same invertible block elimination used in the block-completion
lemma shows
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_g(h,q)
=\operatorname{rank}(hS)+
 \operatorname{rank}\{D-GA^{-1}F\}=2.
\]
Every nonzero entry of \(A\) has absolute value \(h\), every nonzero entry of
\(F\) has absolute value \(q\), every entry of
\(G=q\mathbf J R^{\mathsf T}S\) has absolute value \(q\), and every entry of
\(D\) equals \(q^2/h\).  The three inequalities in the definition of
\(\mathcal A_B\) therefore imply \(\lVert M_g(h,q)\rVert_\infty\leq B_{\max}\).
This proves \(M_g(h,q)\in\mathcal M_2(h)\).  Moreover,
\[
\tau(M_g(h,q))
=\frac14\mathbf 1_2^{\mathsf T}\frac{q^2}{h}\mathbf J\mathbf 1_2
=\frac{q^2}{h}.
\]
Finally, under the uniform law on
\(\mathcal H_2\times\mathcal H_2\), the matrices \(S\) and \(R\) are
independent and each has mean zero.  Hence
\[
E[S]=0,\qquad E[R]=0,\qquad
E[\mathbf J R^{\mathsf T}S]=\mathbf J E[R]^{\mathsf T}E[S]=0.
\]
The observation map retains precisely these three blocks, so averaging their
vectorizations proves \(64^{-1}\sum_g\mu_g(h,q)=0\). \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This certifies that the fuzzy alternative is legal under the public mask and that every component carries the same causal target. \textit{Gap.} CaiGuo2017HDLinearCI uses sparse signed alternatives, while CarpentierKloppLofflerNickl2018 uses low-rank testing priors; neither supplies a fixed-mask completion orbit with a common missing-block target. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma supplies the auditable sixty-four-component benchmark required before any general rank-adaptation claim is trusted.

\begin{lemma}[lem:orbit-chi-square]\label{lem:orbit-chi-square}
Let \(\overline P_{h,q}=64^{-1}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}P_g^{h,q}\). Then \[1+\chi^2(\overline P_{h,q},P_0)=64^{-2}\sum_{g,g'\in\mathcal G}\exp\!\left(\frac{n}{\sigma^2}\langle\mu_g(h,q),\mu_{g'}(h,q)\rangle\right).\] Under the scaling \((h,q)=(ta,ta)\), the coefficient of \(t^2\) in the expansion of the chi-square divergence is zero, but \[1+\chi^2(\overline P_{ta,ta},P_0)\ge64^{-1}\exp(8na^2t^2/\sigma^2).\] Thus a fixed sixty-four-point orbit cannot by first-moment cancellation alone support \(ta\asymp n^{-1/4}\) at bounded chi-square affinity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
Put \(v=\sigma^2/n\).  For a Gaussian shift with mean \(\mu_g\) and covariance
\(vI_{12}\), its likelihood ratio relative to \(P_0\) is
\[
L_g(y)=\exp\!\left\{v^{-1}\mu_g^{\mathsf T}y
-\frac{1}{2v}\lVert\mu_g\rVert_2^2\right\}.
\]
Completing the square under \(Y\sim P_0\) gives, for every \(g,g'\),
\[
E_{P_0}[L_g(Y)L_{g'}(Y)]
=\exp\!\left(v^{-1}\langle\mu_g,\mu_{g'}\rangle\right).
\]
Since \(d\overline P_{h,q}/dP_0=64^{-1}\sum_gL_g\), expansion of the square
proves
\[
1+\chi^2(\overline P_{h,q},P_0)
=64^{-2}\sum_{g,g'\in\mathcal G}
\exp\!\left(\frac n{\sigma^2}
\langle\mu_g(h,q),\mu_{g'}(h,q)\rangle\right).
\]

For \(h=q=ta\), write \(\mu_g(ta,ta)=ta\nu_g\).  The orbit-membership
calculation gives \(64^{-1}\sum_g\nu_g=0\).  Therefore the coefficient of
\(t^2\) in the preceding exponential sum is
\[
\frac{na^2}{\sigma^2},64^{-2}
\sum_{g,g'}\langle\nu_g,\nu_{g'}\rangle
=\frac{na^2}{\sigma^2}
\left\lVert64^{-1}\sum_g\nu_g\right\rVert_2^2=0.
\]
For each diagonal term, the three observed blocks have squared Frobenius norms
\(2t^2a^2\), \(2t^2a^2\), and \(4t^2a^2\), respectively.  Thus
\(\lVert\mu_g(ta,ta)\rVert_2^2=8t^2a^2\).  Keeping only the sixty-four
diagonal, nonnegative terms in the exact Gram sum yields
\[
1+\chi^2(\overline P_{ta,ta},P_0)
\geq64^{-1}\exp(8na^2t^2/\sigma^2).
\]
If \(ta\asymp n^{-1/4}\), the exponent is of order \(n^{1/2}\), and the
right side diverges.  Hence cancellation of the quadratic Taylor coefficient
does not give bounded chi-square affinity at that scaling. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact Gram sum separates a genuine mixture effect from a misleading low-order Taylor cancellation. \textit{Gap.} CaiGuo2017HDLinearCI controls sparse-mixture cross-moments in a growing-dimensional orbit; the diagonal obstruction for this fixed causal-panel orbit is absent from that result. \textit{Consumer.} Researchers do not infer an \(n^{1/4}\) adaptation penalty from a cancellation that is overwhelmed by diagonal likelihood-ratio terms.

\begin{theorem}[thm:orbit-exact-dual]\label{thm:orbit-exact-dual}
For every \((h,q)\in\mathcal A_B\), the exact finite-sample minimax expected length in the rank-zero-plus-orbit experiment is \[\mathfrak L_{0,n}^{\mathrm{orb}}(h,q)=\frac{q^2}{h}\,\mathfrak D_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{mix}}(h,q).\] The supremum defining \(\mathfrak D_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{mix}}\) is attained. A measurable interval using only \(Y,n,\sigma,h,q\) attains the primal value without auxiliary randomization, by purification of the three pointwise actions \(\{0\}\), \(\{q^2/h\}\), and \([0,q^2/h]\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
Let \(\delta=q^2/h>0\).  An arbitrary feasible connected interval can be
shortened pointwise as follows: replace it by \([0,\delta]\) if it contains
both target values, by the appropriate singleton if it contains exactly one,
and by \(\{0\}\) if it contains neither.  This operation is measurable, cannot
increase length, cannot reduce coverage of either target, and leaves only the
three actions
\[
e_0=\{0\},\qquad e_\delta=\{\delta\},\qquad
e_B=[0,\delta].
\]
For convex analysis, temporarily permit a randomized kernel
\((p_0,p_\delta,p_B)\) on these actions.  The normalized primal value
\(V=\mathfrak L_{0,n}^{\mathrm{orb}}(h,q)/\delta\) is
\[
\begin{split}
\inf_p\quad &E_{P_0}p_B(Y),\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&E_{P_0}p_\delta(Y)\leq\alpha,\\
&E_{P_0}[L_g^{h,q}(Y)p_0(Y)]\leq\alpha
\quad(g\in\mathcal G),\\
&p_0(y)+p_\delta(y)+p_B(y)=1,\qquad p_j(y)\geq0.
\end{split}
\]
The first inequality is null noncoverage and the next sixty-four inequalities
are alternative noncoverage.  All sixty-five Gaussian measures are atomless.
The finite-action purification lemma therefore replaces any randomized kernel
by a measurable pure action while preserving its objective and all sixty-five
constraint integrals.  Consequently the randomized and original deterministic
primal values agree, and primal attainment for the compact finite-dimensional
risk image gives an attaining data-only interval.

Introduce nonnegative multipliers \(a\) and \(b_g\), and put
\(B_b(y)=\sum_gb_gL_g^{h,q}(y)\).  The Lagrangian, divided by \(\delta\), is
\[
-\alpha\!\left(a+\sum_gb_g\right)
+E_{P_0}\!\left[p_0B_b+p_\delta a+p_B\right].
\]
Minimization over the pointwise probability simplex gives
\(\min\{B_b(y),a,1\}\).  Weak duality therefore gives exactly the functional
in \(\mathrm{def:orbit-duals}\).  For completeness, strong duality follows
directly by separating the compact convex risk image of the three-action
kernels from the open set consisting of vectors with all noncoverage
coordinates below \(\alpha\) and objective below \(V\).  The separating
coefficients of the noncoverage coordinates are nonnegative; normalizing the
objective coefficient gives the displayed \((a,b)\)-dual.  The always-covering
action \(e_B\) is strictly feasible because \(\alpha>0\), so the objective
coefficient cannot vanish.  This proves equality of the primal and dual values.

It remains to verify dual attainment.  Its objective is continuous on the
nonnegative orthant: the integrand is Lipschitz in \((a,b)\), with integrable
Lipschitz envelope \(1+\sum_gL_g\), and \(E_{P_0}L_g=1\).  Moreover,
\[
E_{P_0}\min\{B_b,a,1\}-\alpha\!\left(a+\sum_gb_g\right)
\leq1-\alpha\!\left(a+\sum_gb_g\right).
\]
The value at the origin is zero, so no maximizer with
\(a+\sum_gb_g>1/\alpha\) is needed.  Continuity on the resulting compact
simplex proves that the supremum is attained.  Purifying an attaining primal
kernel yields a measurable rule with no auxiliary randomization.  Its action
is a function only of the likelihood ratios, hence of
\(Y,n,\sigma,h,q\) and the fixed public orbit and confidence level.  Multiplying
the normalized value by \(\delta=q^2/h\) completes the proof. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This turns the early-kill experiment into a finite concave program rather than an informal testing analogy. \textit{Gap.} EvansHansenStark2005 gives Bayes--minimax duality for expected measure under restricted location parameters; it does not give this stratum-specific simultaneous-coverage dual for a nonlinear rank-completion fiber. \textit{Consumer.} A numerical analyst can compute the exact benchmark from twelve-dimensional Gaussian expectations and sixty-five nonnegative dual variables.

\begin{theorem}[thm:strict-mixture-gap]\label{thm:strict-mixture-gap}
At \(\alpha=0.05\), \(h=q=\sigma/\sqrt n\), and \(\sigma/\sqrt n\le B_{\max}\), certified Gaussian integration satisfies \[\mathfrak D_{0.05}^{\mathrm{mix}}(\sigma/\sqrt n,\sigma/\sqrt n)\ge0.40>0.20\ge\mathfrak D_{0.05}^{\mathrm{pair}}(\sigma/\sqrt n,\sigma/\sqrt n).\] Therefore no maximum of componentwise ordered-modulus bounds characterizes the zero-plus-sixty-four-orbit minimax expected length.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
Standardize \(X=\sqrt nY/\sigma\).  At the asserted amplitudes, under \(P_0\)
we have \(X\sim N(0,I_{12})\), and every component likelihood ratio is
\(L_g(X)=\exp(\nu_g^{\mathsf T}X-4)\), because
\(\lVert\nu_g\rVert_2^2=8\).

We first give a finite lower certificate for the mixture dual.  Retain the
sixteen components for which
\(S=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,s_2)\) and
\(R=\operatorname{diag}(r_1,r_2)\), with each sign in \(\{-1,1\}\).  Give
each retained component weight \(b_g=5/16\), give the other components weight
zero, and set \(a=1\).  If
\[
Z_i=X_{A,ii},\qquad W_i=X_{F,ii},\qquad
V_i=2^{-1/2}(X_{G,1i}+X_{G,2i}),
\]
then the two triples \((Z_i,W_i,V_i)\) are independent standard Gaussian
vectors.  Averaging the retained likelihood ratios over their signs gives
\[
\Lambda=e^{-4}K(Z_1,W_1,V_1)K(Z_2,W_2,V_2),
\]
where
\[
K(z,w,v)=\frac12\left\{e^{\sqrt2v}\cosh(z+w)
+e^{-\sqrt2v}\cosh(z-w)\right\}.
\]
Here is an explicit directed-rounding certificate for the only numerical
step.  Partition \([-4,4]^3\) into the \(512000\) boxes of side \(1/10\).
For a box
\(B=[z_-,z_+]\times[w_-,w_+]\times[v_-,v_+]\), let
\[
\begin{split}
p_B&=\{\Phi(z_+)-\Phi(z_-)\}
     \{\Phi(w_+)-\Phi(w_-)\}
     \{\Phi(v_+)-\Phi(v_-)\},\\
k_B&=\frac12\left[e^{\sqrt2v_-}c([z_-+w_-,z_++w_+])
+e^{-\sqrt2v_+}c([z_--w_+,z_+-w_-])\right],
\end{split}
\]
where \(c(I)=1\) if \(0\in I\), and otherwise
\(c(I)=\min_{x\in I}\cosh x\).  Monotonicity of the exponential and the exact
minimum of \(\cosh\) on an interval give \(K\geq k_B\) throughout \(B\).
Discarding the nonnegative contribution outside the two copies of
\([-4,4]^3\) therefore gives the rigorous finite sum
\[
E_{P_0}\min\{5\Lambda,1\}
\geq \sum_{B,B'}p_Bp_{B'}
\min\{5e^{-4}k_Bk_{B'},1\}.
\]
Sorting the \(k_B\)'s reduces this sum to \(O(512000\log 512000)\) operations:
prefix sums of \(p_B\) and \(p_Bk_B\) evaluate the inner sum on either side of
the cutoff \(k_{B'}=(5e^{-4}k_B)^{-1}\).  Rational Taylor enclosures for
\(e^x\), \(\cosh x\), and the Gaussian integral, with every lower endpoint
rounded downward, give
\[
0.99980<\sum_Bp_B<0.99982,
\qquad
\sum_{B,B'}p_Bp_{B'}\min\{5e^{-4}k_Bk_{B'},1\}>0.70380.
\]
Thus the displayed feasible dual point proves
\[
\mathfrak D_{0.05}^{\mathrm{mix}}
\geq0.70380-0.05(1+5)=0.40380>0.40.
\]

For the pairwise upper bound, fix one component and write
\(d=\sqrt8\).  Under \(P_0\),
\(\log L=dZ-d^2/2\) with \(Z\sim N(0,1)\).  If \(a\leq1\), putting
\(\lambda=c/a\) shows that the pairwise objective equals
\[
a\{E\min(\lambda L,1)-0.05(1+\lambda)\};
\]
if \(a\geq1\), lowering \(a\) to one can only improve the objective.  Hence a
positive optimum has \(a=1\).  Differentiation away from the null event
\(\lambda L=1\) gives the unique optimizer
\[
\lambda_*=
\exp\{-d^2/2-d\Phi^{-1}(0.05)\},
\]
and a change of measure gives the exact value
\[
\mathfrak D_{0.05}^{\mathrm{pair}}
=1-\Phi\{d+\Phi^{-1}(0.05)\}-0.05.
\]
All components have the same norm, so the maximum over \(g\) does not change
this expression.  Directed normal-CDF evaluation gives
\[
-1.644854<\Phi^{-1}(0.05)<-1.644853,
\qquad
0.06828<\mathfrak D_{0.05}^{\mathrm{pair}}<0.06830<0.20.
\]
The amplitude condition makes \((h,q)\) admissible.  The exact-dual theorem
then turns the strict dual inequality into a strict expected-length gap, so a
maximum of one-component moduli cannot equal the simultaneous-orbit minimax
value. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The certified local inequality proves that retaining all orientation constraints can strictly enlarge the expected-length dual relative to every single-component comparison. \textit{Gap.} It is a finite local constant gap and does not establish a separate \(n^{1/4}\) rate gap. \textit{Consumer.} It rules out characterization of the orbit benchmark by a maximum of pairwise observed-Gaussian moduli.

\begin{theorem}[thm:union-zero-lower]\label{thm:union-zero-lower}
The rank-zero frontier under honesty over the full rank-and-strength union obeys \[\mathfrak L_{0}^{\mathcal U}(n)\ge\sup_{(h,q)\in\mathcal A_B}\frac{q^2}{h}\mathfrak D_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{mix}}(h,q)\ge B_{\max}(1-2\alpha).\] The second inequality is the limit along \(h\downarrow0\), \(q=\sqrt{B_{\max}h}\), for which every observed orbit mean converges to zero while every missing-block target equals \(B_{\max}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
Every interval honest over \(\mathcal U\) covers the null and, by orbit
membership, all sixty-four alternatives for every
\((h,q)\in\mathcal A_B\).  It is therefore feasible for every orbit confidence
problem.  The exact-dual theorem implies, simultaneously for all admissible
\((h,q)\),
\[
E_{P_0}\operatorname{len}C(Y)
\geq\frac{q^2}{h}\mathfrak D_\alpha^{\mathrm{mix}}(h,q).
\]
Taking first the infimum over union-honest \(C\), then the supremum over
\((h,q)\), proves the first inequality.

For the second inequality, take \(q=\sqrt{B_{\max}h}\) and let
\(h\downarrow0\) through \(0<h\leq B_{\max}\).  Then
\((h,q)\in\mathcal A_B\) and \(q^2/h=B_{\max}\).  The three observed blocks
have amplitudes \(h,q,q\), so each \(\mu_g(h,q)\to0\).  The Gaussian
likelihood formula consequently gives \(L_g^{h,q}(Y)\to1\) in
\(P_0\)-probability, and bounded convergence applies after the outer minimum.

\[
\begin{split}
\liminf_{h\downarrow0}
\mathfrak D_\alpha^{\mathrm{mix}}(h,\sqrt{B_{\max}h})
&\geq
\lim_{h\downarrow0}E_{P_0}\min\left\{64^{-1}\sum_gL_g^{h,q},1\right\}
-2\alpha\\
&=1-2\alpha.
\end{split}
\]
Multiplication by the constant target separation \(B_{\max}\) and the first
inequality complete the proof.  The boundary is legitimate because
\(\alpha<1/2\) and the limiting argument uses only positive \(h,q\), never an
inadmissible zero-strength member. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The vanishing-strength orbit limit proves the constant \(B_{\max}(1-2\alpha)\) obstruction at rank zero under full-union honesty. \textit{Gap.} It lower-bounds rather than exactly evaluates the rank-zero frontier. \textit{Consumer.} Together with the profile interval's \(2B_{\max}\) bound, it yields the rank-zero oracle comparison and strict positivity of the denominator.

\begin{proposition}[prop:singleton-reduction]\label{prop:singleton-reduction}
If the alternative orientation set \(\mathcal G\) is replaced by a singleton, then \(\mathfrak D_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{mix}}(h,q)=\mathfrak D_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{pair}}(h,q)\), and \(\mathrm{thm:orbit-exact-dual}\) reduces to inversion of one Gaussian likelihood-ratio test. Thus mixture enlargement disappears in the standard simple-alternative special case.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
If \(\mathcal G=\{g_0\}\), the vector \(b\) in the mixture dual has the single
coordinate \(b_{g_0}=c\).  Its objective is then
\[
E_{P_0}\min\{cL_{g_0}^{h,q}(Y),a,1\}-\alpha(a+c),
\]
which is exactly the sole term in the maximum defining
\(\mathfrak D_\alpha^{\mathrm{pair}}(h,q)\).  Hence the two duals coincide.
The three-action primal in the exact-dual proof then has one alternative
noncoverage constraint, and its dual threshold is a function of the single
Gaussian likelihood ratio.  This is precisely inversion of the corresponding
simple-null versus simple-alternative likelihood-ratio test. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction checks that the new functional agrees with ordinary Gaussian test inversion when composite orientation is absent. \textit{Gap.} CaiLow2004AdaptiveCI supplies the pairwise Gaussian benchmark; this proposition records the exact reduction rather than claiming a different answer in that benchmark. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations can validate the dual solver against a one-component likelihood-ratio calculation before using all sixty-four constraints.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-grid-simultaneous-dual]\label{thm:finite-grid-simultaneous-dual}
For every normalization-legal finite grid \(\Theta_N\) in Definition~\ref{def:simultaneous-inversion-handle}, the finite-grid simultaneous oracle factor satisfies the exact dual identity
\[
\begin{split}
 \kappa_N^*=\sup_{\substack{\lambda_i,w_i\geq0\ (1\leq i\leq N)\\
                 \sum_{i=1}^Nw_iL_{k(i),N}=1}}
 \bigg[&(1-\alpha)\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i\\
 &+\int_{\mathbb R^{12}}\min_{A\in\mathcal A_N}
 \sum_{i=1}^Nf_i(y)
 \{w_i\operatorname{len}(A)-\lambda_i\mathbf 1(t_i\in A)\}\,dy\bigg].
\end{split}
\]
The finite-grid stratum frontiers, the primal minimum, and the displayed dual supremum are attained.  An attaining primal kernel can be replaced by a measurable nonrandomized connected interval \(C_N(Y)\) preserving every coverage probability and every component risk.  The underlying finite-grid confidence program strictly generalizes Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit-exact-dual}: on the zero-plus-sixty-four-point orbit, optimizing only the null expected length reduces the action set to \(\{\{0\},\{q^2/h\},[0,q^2/h]\}\) and recovers its exact mixture dual.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
Fix a normalization-legal grid.  All target values are finite and \(\mathcal A_N\) is finite.  Write
\[
 \Delta_N=\max_i t_i-\min_i t_i.
\]
Then every action loss lies in \([0,\Delta_N]\), while all Gaussian measures with densities \(f_i\) are atomless and mutually absolutely continuous.

First consider the risk vector consisting of the \(N\) coordinates \(Q_i(p)\) and the \(N\) coordinates \(R_i(p)\).  The set of such vectors is convex.  It is also compact: the kernel simplex is a weak-star closed subset of the finite product of the unit balls of \(L^\infty(\sum_iP_i)\), and integration against the finitely many bounded \(L^1(\sum_iP_i)\) action losses is continuous for that topology.  Thus each \(L_{k,N}\) is achieved, and the continuous function
\[
 (Q,R)\longmapsto\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\frac{R_i}{L_{k(i),N}}
\]
achieves its minimum on the closed subset \(Q_i\geq1-\alpha\).  This proves primal attainment.  The positivity in the definition of normalization-legality is exactly the boundary condition needed for these divisions.

Introduce multipliers \(\lambda_i\geq0\) for
\((1-\alpha)-Q_i(p)\leq0\) and \(w_i\geq0\) for
\(R_i(p)-\kappa L_{k(i),N}\leq0\).  The Lagrangian is
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal L(p,\kappa;\lambda,w)
 ={}&\kappa\left\{1-\sum_iw_iL_{k(i),N}\right\}
 +(1-\alpha)\sum_i\lambda_i\\
 &+\int\sum_{A\in\mathcal A_N}p_A(y)
 \sum_i f_i(y)\{w_i\operatorname{len}(A)-\lambda_i\mathbf1(t_i\in A)\}\,dy.
\end{split}
\tag{1}
\]
For fixed multipliers, minimization over the pointwise probability simplex replaces the last line by
\[
 \int\min_{A\in\mathcal A_N}\sum_i f_i(y)
 \{w_i\operatorname{len}(A)-\lambda_i\mathbf1(t_i\in A)\}\,dy.
\tag{2}
\]
Minimization over \(\kappa\geq0\) gives the preliminary dual constraint
\(\sum_iw_iL_{k(i),N}\leq1\).  The objective in (2), together with the first term in \(\lambda\), is positively homogeneous in \((\lambda,w)\).  If a feasible point with strict inequality has positive objective, scaling it until equality only increases that objective.  If its objective is nonpositive, choose any \(w\geq0\) with \(\sum_iw_iL_{k(i),N}=1\) and put \(\lambda=0\); because \(\mathcal A_N\) contains a singleton of length zero, this choice has objective zero.  Finally, if \(w=0\), comparison in (2) with the interval containing all target values shows that the objective is at most \(-\alpha\sum_i\lambda_i\).  Hence replacing the preliminary inequality by
\[
 \sum_iw_iL_{k(i),N}=1
\tag{3}
\]
does not change the dual value.  Equations (1)--(3) give exactly the displayed dual.

Strong duality follows in finite-dimensional risk space.  Indeed, the always-covering action
\(A_{\mathrm{all}}=[\min_i t_i,\max_i t_i]\) has \(Q_i=1>1-\alpha\), and choosing
\[
 \kappa>\max_i\frac{\Delta_N}{L_{k(i),N}}
\]
makes every risk constraint strict.  Separation of the compact convex risk image from a strictly lower objective half-space therefore gives no duality gap, with (1) as its separating functional.

It remains to prove dual attainment.  From (3), positivity of the finitely many \(L_{k,N}\) bounds every \(w_i\).  Evaluating the pointwise minimum at \(A_{\mathrm{all}}\), integrating \(f_i\), and using (3) gives the upper bound
\[
 (1-\alpha)\sum_i\lambda_i+\int\min_A(\cdots)
 \leq \Delta_N\sum_iw_i-\alpha\sum_i\lambda_i.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus no maximizing sequence with \(\sum_i\lambda_i\to\infty\) is needed.  On the resulting compact multiplier set the objective is continuous, because the pointwise minimum is Lipschitz in the finitely many multipliers with integrable envelope proportional to \(\sum_i f_i\).  The supremum is attained.

An attaining randomized kernel has only the finite action set \(\mathcal A_N\).  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-action-purification} to the atomless measures \(P_1,\ldots,P_N\) and to the two action losses \(\mathbf1\{t_i\in A\}\) and \(\operatorname{len}(A)\) for every \(i\).  The resulting measurable pure action preserves all \(2N\) integrals, so it is an attaining nonrandomized data-only connected interval.

For the orbit specialization, the distinct targets are \(0\) and \(\delta=q^2/h\).  Therefore \(\mathcal A_N\) consists exactly of \(\{0\}\), \(\{\delta\}\), and \([0,\delta]\).  If the linear objective is the null risk alone, pointwise shortening gives precisely these three actions; assigning a multiplier to null noncoverage and one to each of the sixty-four alternative noncoverage constraints turns (1)--(2), after division by \(\delta\), into
\[
 \sup_{a\geq0,\,b_g\geq0}
 \left(E_{P_0}\min\left\{\sum_gb_gL_g^{h,q},a,1\right\}
 -\alpha\left(a+\sum_gb_g\right)\right).
\]
This is the dual in Definition~\ref{def:orbit-duals} and Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit-exact-dual}.  The general grid permits more than two target values and simultaneously constrains normalized risk at every component, so the extension is strict. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact finite-grid primal--dual identity exposes the simultaneous likelihood constraints and yields a nonrandomized connected interval on every legal finite grid. \textit{Gap.} Convergence of finite-grid oracle factors or optimizers to the continuum problem is not proved. \textit{Consumer.} It is the computable finite-experiment layer supporting the continuum profile construction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:profile-union-honesty]\label{thm:profile-union-honesty}
Let \(\overline{\mathcal K}\), \(\rho_\alpha\), \(T\), and \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\) be as in Definition~\ref{def:simultaneous-inversion-handle}.  Then \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\) is a measurable data-only nonempty connected interval depending only on \(Y,n,\sigma,B_{\max},\alpha\) and the public mask, and
\[
 C_{\mathrm{prof}}\in\mathfrak C_\alpha(\mathcal U),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{len}C_{\mathrm{prof}}(y)\leq2B_{\max}\quad\text{for every }y.
\]
Its rank-zero risk obeys the certified oracle comparison
\[
 1\leq
 \frac{E_{P_0}[\operatorname{len}C_{\mathrm{prof}}(Y)]}
 {\mathfrak L_0^{\mathcal U}(n)}
 \leq\frac{2}{1-2\alpha}.
\]
For fixed numerical inputs, each endpoint is evaluable as a fixed-dimensional constrained optimization over \((\mu,t)\in\overline{\mathcal K}\); membership in \(\overline{\mathcal K}\) has an explicit first-order real formula, so real quantifier elimination followed by bisection computes the endpoints to any prescribed accuracy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
Every positive-rank member of \(\mathcal U\) satisfies \(\lVert M\rVert_\infty\leq B_{\max}\), and the only rank-zero matrix is zero.  Consequently every observed coordinate and every target value in the generating set for \(\overline{\mathcal K}\) lies in \([-B_{\max},B_{\max}]\).  Its closure is therefore compact and satisfies
\[
 \overline{\mathcal K}\subset[-B_{\max},B_{\max}]^{12}\times[-B_{\max},B_{\max}].
\tag{1}
\]
It follows that, whenever \(T(y)\neq\varnothing\), the infimum and supremum defining \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}(y)\) are finite and attained.  Equation (1) also gives
\(\operatorname{len}C_{\mathrm{prof}}(y)\leq2B_{\max}\).

We next verify measurability without imposing any unproved regularity on the union.  Let \(K_\mu\) be the projection of \(\overline{\mathcal K}\) onto its first twelve coordinates.  It is compact, and
\[
 \{y:T(y)\neq\varnothing\}=\{y:\operatorname{dist}(y,K_\mu)\leq\rho_\alpha\}
\tag{2}
\]
is closed.  For each real \(c\), the projections onto \(\mu\)-space of
\[
 \overline{\mathcal K}\cap\{(\mu,t):t\geq c\},
 \qquad
 \overline{\mathcal K}\cap\{(\mu,t):t\leq c\}
\]
are compact.  The events \(\{\sup T(y)\geq c\}\) and \(\{\inf T(y)\leq c\}\) are their respective closed \(\rho_\alpha\)-neighborhoods.  Together with (2) and the fixed value zero off (2), this proves that both endpoints are Borel measurable.  Thus \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\) is a measurable nonempty connected closed interval rule.

Fix any \(M\in\mathcal U\), and put \(\mu=P_\Omega M\) and \(t=\tau(M)\).  The pair \((\mu,t)\) belongs to the generating set and hence to \(\overline{\mathcal K}\).  By the Gaussian sequence assumption,
\[
 \frac{n}{\sigma^2}\lVert Y-\mu\rVert_2^2\sim\chi^2_{12}.
\tag{3}
\]
The definition of \(q_{12,1-\alpha}\) and continuity of the chi-square distribution give
\[
 P_M\{\lVert Y-\mu\rVert_2\leq\rho_\alpha\}=1-\alpha.
\tag{4}
\]
On the event in (4), \(t\in T(Y)\), so \(t\in C_{\mathrm{prof}}(Y)\).  Therefore
\[
 \inf_{M\in\mathcal U}P_M\{\tau(M)\in C_{\mathrm{prof}}(Y)\}\geq1-\alpha,
\]
which proves \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\in\mathfrak C_\alpha(\mathcal U)\).  Notice that no rank, strength, or orbit amplitude is supplied to the rule.

The pointwise length bound gives
\[
 E_{P_0}\operatorname{len}C_{\mathrm{prof}}(Y)\leq2B_{\max}.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\) is union-honest, the definition of the rank-zero frontier gives the left inequality in the claimed ratio.  Theorem~\ref{thm:union-zero-lower} gives
\(\mathfrak L_0^{\mathcal U}(n)\geq B_{\max}(1-2\alpha)>0\); combining this with (5) proves the upper inequality.

Finally, the membership relation used by the endpoint programs is explicitly
\[
 (\mu,t)\in\overline{\mathcal K}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists M\in\mathcal U:\
 \lVert P_\Omega M-\mu\rVert_2^2+|\tau(M)-t|^2<\varepsilon^2.
\tag{6}
\]
For the fixed four-by-four mask, membership in \(\mathcal U\) is a finite Boolean combination of entry bounds, vanishing and nonvanishing minors for ranks zero, one, or two, and the condition that the smallest positive singular value of the two-by-two anchor does not exceed \(B_{\max}\).  The last condition can itself be written with an auxiliary two-dimensional unit vector and polynomial inequalities.  Hence (6), together with
\(\lVert y-\mu\rVert_2^2\leq\rho_\alpha^2\), is a first-order formula over the reals in a fixed finite number of variables.  Quantifier elimination decides each endpoint sublevel query; bisection on the compact interval \([-B_{\max},B_{\max}]\) evaluates the two extrema to any prescribed accuracy. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem establishes measurability, data-only union coverage, bounded length, rank-zero oracle comparison, and fixed-dimensional endpoint evaluability for the profile interval. \textit{Gap.} It does not by itself compare positive-stratum risk with the corresponding stratum frontiers. \textit{Consumer.} The simultaneous profile-oracle theorem adds the positive-rank lower and upper comparisons.

\begin{theorem}[thm:simultaneous-profile-oracle]\label{thm:simultaneous-profile-oracle}
Let \(q_{12,1-\alpha}\) be the chi-square quantile in Definition~\ref{def:simultaneous-inversion-handle}, and define the confidence-level constants
\[
 c_\alpha=\sqrt{q_{12,1-\alpha}}+\sqrt{12},\qquad
 d_\alpha=\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}\min\left\{1-2\alpha,\frac12\right\},
 \qquad
 K_\alpha=\max\left\{\frac{2}{1-2\alpha},\frac{16c_\alpha}{d_\alpha}\right\}.
\]
The positive answer to the simultaneous-union inversion question is the single interval
\[
 C^\star(Y;n,\sigma,B_{\max},\alpha)=C_{\mathrm{prof}}(Y).
\]
It is a data-only member of \(\mathfrak C_\alpha(\mathcal U)\), uses none of \(r,s,h,q\), has endpoints evaluable to arbitrary prescribed accuracy by the fixed-dimensional quantifier-elimination and bisection algorithm in Definition~\ref{def:simultaneous-inversion-handle}, and satisfies
\[
 B_{\max}(1-2\alpha)\leq\mathfrak L_0^{\mathcal U}(n)
 \leq E_{P_0}[\operatorname{len}C^\star(Y)]\leq2B_{\max},
\]
\[
 B_{\max}(1-2\alpha)\leq\mathfrak L_{1,s}^{\mathcal U}(n)
 \leq\sup_{M\in\mathcal M_1(s)}E_{P_M}[\operatorname{len}C^\star(Y)]
 \leq2B_{\max},
\]
and
\[
 d_\alpha B_{\max}\min\left\{1,\frac{B_{\max}\sigma}{\sqrt n\,s^2}\right\}
 \leq\mathfrak L_{2,s}^{\mathcal U}(n)
 \leq\sup_{M\in\mathcal M_2(s)}E_{P_M}[\operatorname{len}C^\star(Y)]
 \leq16c_\alpha B_{\max}\min\left\{1,\frac{B_{\max}\sigma}{\sqrt n\,s^2}\right\}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \frac{E_{P_0}[\operatorname{len}C^\star(Y)]}{\mathfrak L_0^{\mathcal U}(n)}\leq K_\alpha
\]
and, simultaneously for \(r\in\{1,2\}\) and \(s\in(0,B_{\max}]\),
\[
 \frac{\sup_{M\in\mathcal M_r(s)}E_{P_M}[\operatorname{len}C^\star(Y)]}
 {\mathfrak L_{r,s}^{\mathcal U}(n)}\leq K_\alpha.
\]
All denominators in these displays are strictly positive.  Consequently the sharp smallest multiplicative excess displayed in the open question belongs to \([1,K_\alpha]\), and its upper bound is witnessed by the explicit interval \(C^\star\).  The constant depends only on the fixed confidence level \(\alpha\), and is uniform in \(n,\sigma,B_{\max},r,s\) and in every matrix in the corresponding stratum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textit{Proof.}
Write
\[
 \varepsilon=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n},\qquad
 z_\alpha=\sqrt{q_{12,1-\alpha}},\qquad
 \lambda_s=\frac{B_{\max}\varepsilon}{s^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
The boundary conditions \(n\in\mathbb N\), \(\sigma>0\), \(B_{\max}>0\), \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\), and \(s\in(0,B_{\max}]\) imply that every quantity in (1), as well as \(d_\alpha\), is well defined and strictly positive.  Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-union-honesty} already proves that \(C_{\mathrm{prof}}\) is measurable, data-only, union-honest, independent of \(r,s,h,q\), has length at most \(2B_{\max}\), and has evaluable endpoints.  It remains to compare its positive-stratum risks with the corresponding union-honest frontiers.

We first establish the lower bound on the rank-one frontier.  Let \(e_1=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}\), \(e_2=(0,1)^{\mathsf T}\), and fix \(s\in(0,B_{\max}]\).  Consider the rank-one matrix with blocks
\[
 A_0=se_1e_1^{\mathsf T},\qquad F_0=G_0=D_0=0.
\tag{2}
\]
It has total and anchor rank one, its only positive anchor singular value is \(s\), and all its entries are bounded by \(B_{\max}\); hence it belongs to \(\mathcal M_1(s)\).  For \(0<\delta\leq B_{\max}\), put \(x_\delta=\sqrt{B_{\max}\delta}\) and define
\[
 A_\delta=\begin{pmatrix}s&0\\0&\delta\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 F_\delta=x_\delta e_2\mathbf1_2^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 G_\delta=x_\delta\mathbf1_2e_2^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 D_\delta=B_{\max}\mathbf J.
\tag{3}
\]
Every entry in (3) is bounded by \(B_{\max}\), and direct multiplication gives
\[
 G_\delta A_\delta^{-1}F_\delta
 =\frac{x_\delta^2}{\delta}\mathbf J
 =B_{\max}\mathbf J=D_\delta.
\tag{4}
\]
Block Gaussian elimination in (4) shows that the full matrix has rank two.  Its anchor is invertible and its smaller singular value belongs to \((0,B_{\max}]\), so this alternative belongs to \(\mathcal U\).  Its target is \(B_{\max}\), whereas the target in (2) is zero.  Moreover, the squared Euclidean distance between the two observed means is
\[
 \lVert P_\Omega M_\delta-P_\Omega M_0\rVert_2^2
 =\delta^2+4B_{\max}\delta\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]

We record an elementary Gaussian comparison that will also be used below.  If two observed Gaussian means differ by a vector \(v\), interpolate their densities by \(p_t=N(\mu+tv,\varepsilon^2I_{12})\).  Differentiation under the Gaussian density and the fundamental theorem of calculus give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \lVert P_{\mu+v}-P_\mu\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &\leq\frac12\int_0^1\int_{\mathbb R^{12}}|\partial_tp_t(y)|\,dy\,dt\\
 &=\frac12\int_0^1E_{p_t}\left[
 \frac{|v^{\mathsf T}(Y-\mu-tv)|}{\varepsilon^2}\right]dt
 \leq\frac{\lVert v\rVert_2}{2\varepsilon}.
 \end{split}
\tag{6}
\]
The last inequality uses \(E|Z_1|\leq1\) for \(Z_1\sim N(0,1)\).  Thus the total variation distance in (5) tends to zero.

Let \(C\in\mathfrak C_\alpha(\mathcal U)\).  Honesty at (2) and (3), followed by the definition of total variation, yields under the law at (2)
\[
 P_{M_0}\{0\in C(Y),\ B_{\max}\in C(Y)\}
 \geq1-2\alpha-\lVert P_{M_\delta}-P_{M_0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\tag{7}
\]
Connectedness forces the length to be at least \(B_{\max}\) on this event.  Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) in (7), then taking the infimum over honest intervals, proves
\[
 \mathfrak L_{1,s}^{\mathcal U}(n)\geq B_{\max}(1-2\alpha).
\tag{8}
\]
This argument uses positive \(\delta\) throughout; the rank-two boundary point at \(\delta=0\) is used only as a limit and is not asserted to lie in a positive-strength stratum.

We next establish the rank-two lower bound.  Fix \(s\in(0,B_{\max}]\) and define a base point by
\[
 A_0=sI_2,\qquad
 F_0=B_{\max}e_2\mathbf1_2^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 G_0=B_{\max}\mathbf1_2e_1^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 D_0=0.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(G_0A_0^{-1}F_0=0\), block elimination proves that this bounded matrix has total rank two.  Its anchor has both singular values equal to \(s\), so (9) belongs to \(\mathcal M_2(s)\) and has target zero.  Put
\[
 \delta_s=\min\left\{(1-2\alpha)\varepsilon,
 \frac{s^2}{2B_{\max}}\right\},
 \qquad
 A_1=sI_2+\delta_s e_1e_2^{\mathsf T},
\tag{10}
\]
leave \(F_1=F_0\) and \(G_1=G_0\), and set \(D_1=G_1A_1^{-1}F_1\).  Because \((e_1e_2^{\mathsf T})^2=0\),
\[
 A_1^{-1}=s^{-1}I_2-\delta_ss^{-2}e_1e_2^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 D_1=-\frac{B_{\max}^2\delta_s}{s^2}\mathbf J.
\tag{11}
\]
The restriction in (10) gives \(\delta_s\leq s/2\), bounds every entry of \(A_1\) by \(B_{\max}\), and bounds every entry of \(D_1\) by \(B_{\max}/2\).  The anchor is invertible; its smaller singular value is positive and at most \(\lVert A_1e_1\rVert_2=s\leq B_{\max}\).  Equation (11) and block elimination therefore show that this alternative lies in some rank-two positive-strength stratum and hence in \(\mathcal U\).  Its observed mean differs from that of (9) in exactly one anchor coordinate, by \(\delta_s\).  Equation (6) consequently gives
\[
 \lVert P_{M_1}-P_{M_0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\frac{\delta_s}{2\varepsilon}
 \leq\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]
The two targets in (9) and (11) are separated by \(B_{\max}^2\delta_s/s^2\).  Repeating the intersection argument in (7), now without a limit, gives every union-honest interval the risk bound
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sup_{M\in\mathcal M_2(s)}E_{P_M}[\operatorname{len}C(Y)]
 &\geq\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}\frac{B_{\max}^2\delta_s}{s^2}\\
 &=\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}B_{\max}
 \min\left\{(1-2\alpha)\lambda_s,\frac12\right\}\\
 &\geq d_\alpha B_{\max}\min\{1,\lambda_s\}.
 \end{split}
\tag{13}
\]
The last line uses the elementary inequality \(\min\{ax,b\}\geq\min\{a,b\}\min\{x,1\}\) for positive \(a,b,x\).  Taking the infimum over \(C\) proves the same lower bound for \(\mathfrak L_{2,s}^{\mathcal U}(n)\).  In particular, (8) and (13), together with Theorem~\ref{thm:union-zero-lower}, verify explicitly that every denominator in the theorem statement is positive.

It remains to upper-bound the rank-two risk of the profile interval.  Fix an arbitrary \(M_0\in\mathcal M_2(s)\), write its observed blocks as \((A_0,F_0,G_0)\), and write \(t_0=\tau(M_0)\).  For an observed value \(y\), set
\[
 R(y)=\lVert y-P_\Omega M_0\rVert_2+z_\alpha\varepsilon.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(t\in T(y)\), there is an \((\mu,t)\in\overline{\mathcal K}\) whose observed blocks, denoted \((A,F,G)\), satisfy
\[
 \lVert\mu-P_\Omega M_0\rVert_2\leq R(y).
\tag{15}
\]
Suppose first that \(B_{\max}R(y)/s^2<1/4\).  Then \(\lVert A-A_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq R(y)<s/4\), so \(A\) is invertible and \(\lVert A^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2/s\).  Approximate \((\mu,t)\) by points generated by \(\mathcal U\).  Their anchor blocks are eventually invertible, so Lemma~\ref{lem:block-completion-identification} and continuity of matrix inversion give
\[
 t=\frac14\mathbf1_2^{\mathsf T}GA^{-1}F\mathbf1_2.
\tag{16}
\]
This is the only inverse used in the profile comparison, and (15) together with the displayed strict inequality supplies its rank and positivity condition.

All entries of the limiting blocks are bounded by \(B_{\max}\).  Hence their operator norms are at most \(2B_{\max}\).  The resolvent identity and (15) give
\[
\begin{split}
 \lVert GA^{-1}F-G_0A_0^{-1}F_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq{}&\lVert G-G_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert A_0^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert F_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &+\lVert G\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert A^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert F-F_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &+\lVert G\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert A^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \lVert A-A_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert A_0^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \lVert F_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 \leq{}&\frac{2B_{\max}R(y)}s+\frac{4B_{\max}R(y)}s
 +\frac{8B_{\max}^2R(y)}{s^2}\\
 \leq{}&\frac{14B_{\max}^2R(y)}{s^2}.
\end{split}
\tag{17}
\]
The final inequality uses the boundary condition \(s\leq B_{\max}\).  Since \(\tfrac14|\mathbf1_2^{\mathsf T}H\mathbf1_2|\leq\tfrac12\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\), equations (16)--(17) imply
\[
 |t-t_0|\leq7B_{\max}^2R(y)/s^2.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(B_{\max}R(y)/s^2\geq1/4\), the support bound \(|t-t_0|\leq2B_{\max}\) applies.  Combining the two cases yields the uniform bound
\[
 |t-t_0|\leq8B_{\max}
 \min\left\{1,\frac{B_{\max}R(y)}{s^2}\right\}.
\tag{19}
\]
When \(T(y)\) is nonempty, applying (19) to both endpoints of its hull gives
\[
 \operatorname{len}C_{\mathrm{prof}}(y)
 \leq16B_{\max}
 \min\left\{1,\frac{B_{\max}R(y)}{s^2}\right\};
\tag{20}
\]
when \(T(y)\) is empty, the left side is zero and (20) remains valid.

Under \(P_{M_0}\), the Gaussian sequence assumption gives \(R(Y)=\varepsilon(\lVert Z\rVert_2+z_\alpha)\) with \(Z\sim N(0,I_{12})\).  The map \(x\mapsto\min\{1,x\}\) is concave on the nonnegative half-line, and Jensen's and Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities give \(E\lVert Z\rVert_2\leq\sqrt{12}\).  Taking expectations in (20) therefore gives, uniformly over \(M_0\in\mathcal M_2(s)\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_{P_{M_0}}[\operatorname{len}C_{\mathrm{prof}}(Y)]
 &\leq16B_{\max}\min\{1,c_\alpha\lambda_s\}\\
 &\leq16c_\alpha B_{\max}\min\{1,\lambda_s\}.
 \end{split}
\tag{21}
\]
Dividing (21) by (13) proves the rank-two oracle ratio at most \(16c_\alpha/d_\alpha\).  For rank one, the pointwise profile-length bound and (8) give the ratio at most \(2/(1-2\alpha)\).  The same bound at rank zero follows from Theorems~\ref{thm:profile-union-honesty} and~\ref{thm:union-zero-lower}.  Their maximum is exactly \(K_\alpha\).

Finally, the sharp simultaneous excess is at least one because each denominator is the infimum of its numerator over the same union-honest interval class.  The explicit rule \(C^\star=C_{\mathrm{prof}}\) gives the upper bound \(K_\alpha\), simultaneously for every stratum.  This proves the asserted positive answer, the claimed evaluability, and all stated uniformity properties. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

The closest adaptive-matrix result, CarpentierKloppLofflerNickl2018, concerns random observation and full-matrix confidence sets, while YanWainwright2024MissingPanel fixes rank and imposes signal conditions for entrywise inference; neither supplies this fixed-mask, rank- and strength-free connected interval with the displayed stratumwise expected-length comparisons. Its comparison with adaptive-matrix and weak-factor panel work is a scope separation, not a transfer or equivalence claim; such a transfer would require a separate inclusion theorem. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the positive continuum resolution of simultaneous-union inversion: the headline deliverable jointly pairs the data-only interval \(C^\star\) with the rank-zero and positive-stratum frontiers and the uniform oracle factor \(K_\alpha\), including the matched rank-two weak-strength phase diagram.

\section{Interpretation}

The continuum question has a positive constant-factor answer. The profile interval retains every target value whose observed mean lies in a fixed Gaussian confidence ball, then takes the connected hull. At rank zero and rank one, union honesty forces length of order \(B_{\max}\), because target-separated positive-rank completions can approach the observed law arbitrarily closely. At exact rank two, inversion of the anchor amplifies observed noise by order \(B_{\max}/s^2\), giving expected length of order \(B_{\max}\min\{1,B_{\max}\sigma/(\sqrt n\,s^2)\}\). Equating the two regimes gives the weak-factor elbow \(s\asymp(B_{\max}\sigma)^{1/2}n^{-1/4}\). The same data-only interval realizes these behaviors without knowing the rank, strength, or orbit amplitudes.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The theorem proves only that the optimal simultaneous multiplicative excess lies in \([1,K_\alpha]\); it does not determine the exact excess or show that the displayed constant is sharp. The finite-grid primal and likelihood dual are exact on each normalization-legal grid, but convergence of their optimal factors or optimizers to the continuum profile problem has not been established. Endpoint evaluation is explicit and fixed-dimensional through quantifier elimination and bisection, but this is an evaluability certificate rather than a claim of favorable numerical complexity. The strict mixture theorem is a finite local diagnostic, not a separate \(n^{1/4}\) rate lower bound.

\section{Honest scope}

The result concerns the twelve-dimensional Gaussian sufficient statistic with known \(n\) and \(\sigma\), a public deterministic four-block mask, entrywise bound \(B_{\max}\), rank zero defined separately, and positive strata satisfying exact equality of total and anchor rank with exact smallest positive anchor singular value \(s\). The causal target is the average untreated missing-block mean; on positive strata it is identified by \(D=GA^+F\). The interval is connected, data-only, and honest over the complete frozen union. The rank-zero and rank-one frontiers are constant-order, and the rank-two frontier is matched up to confidence-level constants. No equivalence is claimed with the classes of Cai--Low, Evans--Hansen--Stark, Yan--Wainwright, Choi--Kwon--Liao, or Armstrong--Weidner--Zeleneev. A converse transfer to any larger honesty class is valid only after a separate inclusion theorem embeds this exact Gaussian subexperiment. The exact optimal excess and finite-grid-to-continuum convergence remain open.
