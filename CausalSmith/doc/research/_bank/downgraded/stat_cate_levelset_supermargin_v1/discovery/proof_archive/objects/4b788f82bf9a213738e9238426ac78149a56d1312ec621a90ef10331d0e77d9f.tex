A tensor B-spline of fixed order has at most \(r_B^d\) nonzero tensor factors at any point. The Gram matrix of the unnormalized tensor basis has eigenvalues of order \(m^{-d}=k^{-1}\), with constants depending only on \(d\) and \(r_B\); hence its inverse-square-root whitening operator has norm of order \(k^{1/2}\). Consequently,
\[
\sup_{z\in[0,1]^d}\|B_k(z)\|_2^2\le Ck.
\tag{1}
\]
The definition of the localized basis then gives
\[
\|b_{x,h,k}(u)\|_2^2
=V_{x,h}^{-1}\|B_k(T_{x,h}(u))\|_2^2
\mathbf 1\{u\in W_{x,h}\}
\le\frac{Ck}{V_{x,h}}.
\tag{2}
\]

The finite-dimensional Legendre vector and the evaluation factors in \(q_1\) and \(s_1\) are bounded by a constant depending only on \(d\) and \(r_B\). Under calibration, \(|A-1/2|\le1/2\), \(|Y|\le1\), and the reference nuisance functions are bounded. Since \(K_{x,h}=V_{x,h}^{-1}\mathbf 1_{W_{x,h}}\), every scalar entry of \(q_1\) and \(s_1\) is bounded by \(C/V_{x,h}\). Furthermore, by uniform design,
\[
\mathbb E[K_{x,h}(X)^2]
=2^{-d}V_{x,h}^{-1}.
\]
Using \(V_{x,h}\ge2^{-d}h^d\) from \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\), the envelopes are at most \(Ch^{-d}\) and the variances are at most \(Ch^{-d}\).

For the second-order scores, the deterministic factors other than the localized spline inner product are again bounded, while \(V_{x,h}K_{x,h}(X_1)K_{x,h}(X_2)=V_{x,h}^{-1}\) on \(W_{x,h}^2\). By \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\), \(\widehat\Omega^{-1}=2^dI_k\). Thus (2) and Cauchy--Schwarz give the pointwise bound
\[
|b_{x,h,k}(X_1)^\top\widehat\Omega^{-1}b_{x,h,k}(X_2)|
\le Ck/V_{x,h},
\]
so every scalar raw-kernel entry is bounded by \(Ck/V_{x,h}^2\le Ckh^{-2d}\).

For the second moment, condition first on \(X_1\). The population identity \(\mathbb E[b(X)b(X)^\top]=2^{-d}I_k\) yields
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\{b(X_1)^\top\widehat\Omega^{-1}b(X_2)\}^2
\mid X_1\right]
=b(X_1)^\top\widehat\Omega^{-1}b(X_1).
\]
After averaging over \(X_1\), the right side has expectation \(k\). Including the squared factor \(V_{x,h}^{-2}\) shows that every scalar entry of either raw second-order kernel has second moment at most
\[
CkV_{x,h}^{-2}\le Ckh^{-2d}.
\]
Hoeffding centering subtracts conditional expectations and the grand mean. Conditional expectation is an \(L_2\) contraction, and the triangle inequality changes the displayed second-moment and envelope bounds only by a universal constant factor. Hence the same asserted orders hold for the centered kernels induced by \(q_2\) and \(s_2\), uniformly over the stated indices.
