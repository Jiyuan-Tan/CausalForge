Put \(r=r_B\), and let \(S_{m,r}\) denote the tensor-product spline space spanned by the coordinates of \(B_k\). Orthonormalizing the tensor-product B-splines changes their coordinates but not their linear span, so
\[
S_{m,r}=\{v^\top B_k:v\in\mathbb R^k\},\qquad k=m^d.
\]
We first record directly the approximation property of this space. On the one-dimensional regular grid of resolution \(m\), let \(N_i\) be the unnormalized B-splines of order \(r\). They are nonnegative, form a partition of unity, have supports of diameter at most \(C_r m^{-1}\), and at most \(r\) of them are nonzero at any point. There are local linear coefficient functionals \(\lambda_i\) such that the operator
\[
Q_m^{(1)}f=\sum_i\lambda_i(f)N_i
\]
reproduces every polynomial of degree at most \(r-1\) and satisfies
\[
|\lambda_i(f)|\le C_r\sup_{y\in I_i}|f(y)|,
\]
where \(I_i\) is an interval of diameter at most \(C_r m^{-1}\) containing \(\operatorname{supp}(N_i)\). For completeness, these functionals can be constructed by taking \(r\) fixed relative evaluation points in each reference knot patch and solving for the coefficients that reproduce \(1,z,\ldots,z^{r-1}\). After translating and rescaling a patch to the fixed reference patch, the coefficient matrix is the same nonsingular Vandermonde matrix for every interior patch. Its inverse therefore has a norm depending only on \(r\). One-sided reference points give the boundary functionals; there are only finitely many boundary patterns. This proves both polynomial reproduction and the displayed uniform stability bound.

Tensorizing the one-dimensional construction gives a map \(Q_m:C([0,1]^d)\to S_{m,r}\) which reproduces every coordinatewise polynomial of degree at most \(r-1\), and hence every total-degree polynomial of degree at most \(r-1\). The tensor-product partition of unity, the local stability just proved, and the fact that at most \(r^d\) tensor B-splines meet any point imply
\[
|Q_m f(x)|\le C_{d,r}\sup_{\|y-x\|_\infty\le C_{d,r}m^{-1}}|f(y)|
\tag{1}
\]
for every \(x\in[0,1]^d\), with the supremum restricted to \([0,1]^d\).

Fix \(q\in\{\alpha,\beta,\bar s\}\) and \(g\in\mathsf H_q(1;[0,1]^d)\). By \(\mathrm{def:average-smoothness}\), \(q\le\max\{\alpha,\beta\}\). The order choice in \(\mathrm{def:spline-basis}\) therefore gives \(r>q\). Let \(T_xg\) be the Taylor polynomial at \(x\) of the degree prescribed by the Taylor-remainder Hölder norm in \(\mathrm{def:holder-ball}\). Its degree is strictly smaller than \(r\), and the unit Hölder-radius condition gives
\[
|g(y)-T_xg(y)|\le C_{d,q}\|y-x\|_\infty^q,\qquad x,y\in[0,1]^d.
\tag{2}
\]
Because \(Q_m\) reproduces \(T_xg\), equations \((1)\) and \((2)\) yield, pointwise,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|g(x)-Q_mg(x)|
&=|Q_m(T_xg-g)(x)|\\
&\le C_{d,r}\sup_{\|y-x\|_\infty\le C_{d,r}m^{-1}}|T_xg(y)-g(y)|\\
&\le C_{d,r,q}m^{-q}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The unit cube has Lebesgue measure one, hence
\[
\|g-Q_mg\|_{L_2([0,1]^d)}
\le \|g-Q_mg\|_\infty
\le C_{d,r,q}m^{-q}
=C_{d,r,q}k^{-q/d}.
\]
Since \(Q_mg\in S_{m,r}\), there is a coefficient vector \(v_g\in\mathbb R^k\) with \(Q_mg=v_g^\top B_k\). Taking the infimum over \(v\), and then taking the largest constant over the three-element set \(\{\alpha,\beta,\bar s\}\), gives the asserted inequality with \(C_{\mathrm{app}}\).

Applying the inequality separately at orders \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), the product of the two approximation errors is bounded by a constant times
\[
k^{-\alpha/d}k^{-\beta/d}=k^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}.
\]
Finally, \(\mathrm{def:average-smoothness}\) states \(\alpha+\beta=2\bar s\), so this order is exactly \(k^{-2\bar s/d}\), as claimed.
