Let \(B_0\) bound the degree of the support-intersection graph. A greedy coloring partitions \(\{1,\ldots,K\}\) into \(L\le B_0+1\) color classes, and supports having the same color are disjoint. Assign every centered quadratic term in \(C_\lambda\) to one of the colors of its two sign indices. Starting from \(p_\lambda\), introduce the terms in \(B_\lambda+C_\lambda\) one color at a time; call the resulting density families \(p_\lambda^{(0)},\ldots,p_\lambda^{(L)}\), where \(p_\lambda^{(0)}=p_\lambda\) and \(p_\lambda^{(L)}=q_\lambda\).

Fix a color \(r\) and condition on every sign whose index does not have color \(r\). Each term added at this step contains exactly one sign of color \(r\): a quadratic term whose two indices had the same color vanishes because the corresponding supports are disjoint. Hence averaging over the color-\(r\) signs makes the added one-observation term zero. The two conditional one-observation mixtures at this step are therefore identical. Moreover, on a support \(S_l\) of color \(r\), the conditional density depends on no other color-\(r\) sign, because same-color supports are disjoint.

We now calculate the product-mixture distance at this one color step. Partition the sample space into the disjoint sets \(S_l\) of color \(r\), after assigning their null boundaries arbitrarily, and their complement. Conditional on the vector of occupancy counts \((N_l)\), the likelihood and the Rademacher prior factor over \(l\). For a cell containing \(m\) observations, divide both conditional likelihoods by their common one-observation mixture. Their factors have the form
\[
 1+\lambda_l a_i
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 1+\lambda_l(a_i+b_i)+c_i,
\]
where \(|a_i|\le CR\), \(|b_i|\le CD\), the centered term \(c_i\) has conditional mean zero and magnitude at most \(CDR\), and all denominators are bounded below by the assumed ratio constant. Expanding the two products and averaging over \(\lambda_l\), the terms involving zero or one observation agree exactly. Every surviving difference therefore contains a pair of distinct observations. Its leading coefficient is bounded by \(C(DR+D^2)\). Every additional pair of common coefficients contributes at most \(CR^2\). Thus, when \(mR^2\le c_1\), direct summation of the even Rademacher expansion gives
\[
 H_l^2\le C m(m-1)(D^2R^2+D^4)
 \sum_{j=0}^\infty(CmR^2)^j
 \le C m(m-1)(D^2R^2+D^4).
\]
This calculation uses the likelihood normalized within the cell. Consequently, arbitrary common likelihood factors in other cells have Hellinger affinity one and do not create a global second-moment multiplier.

The condition \(Nw^qR^2\le c_0\), with \(c_0\) small relative to the fixed support constant, controls the same geometric expansion after averaging over occupancies. Hellinger distance tensorizes conditionally over the disjoint cells, so
\[
 H^2\!\left(\mathbb E_{\lambda_r}(P_{\lambda}^{(r-1)})^{\otimes N},
 \mathbb E_{\lambda_r}(P_{\lambda}^{(r)})^{\otimes N}
 \mathrel{\big|}\lambda_{-r}\right)
 \le C(D^2R^2+D^4)\sum_{l:\,\operatorname{col}(l)=r}
 \mathbb E[N_l(N_l-1)].
\]
For multinomial occupancies, \(\mathbb E[N_l(N_l-1)]=N(N-1)p_l^2\), and \(p_l\le Cw^q\). Hence the last display is at most
\[
 C N^2K_r w^{2q}(D^2R^2+D^4),
\]
where \(K_r\) is the number of supports of color \(r\). Convexity of squared Hellinger distance permits averaging over the conditioned signs. The triangle inequality for Hellinger distance across the \(L\) color steps, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz and \(\sum_rK_r=K\), yields
\[
 H^2(\overline P_N,\overline Q_N)
 \le C N^2K w^{2q}(D^2R^2+D^4).
\]
Finally, \(\operatorname{TV}^2(P,Q)\le H^2(P,Q)\) under the convention \(H^2=\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\), by Cauchy--Schwarz applied to \(|p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\). Absorbing convention-dependent numerical factors into \(C\) proves the lemma.
