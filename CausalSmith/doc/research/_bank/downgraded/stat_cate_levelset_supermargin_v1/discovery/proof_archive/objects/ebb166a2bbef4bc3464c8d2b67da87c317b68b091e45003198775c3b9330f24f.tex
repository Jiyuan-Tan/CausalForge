Write \(\bar s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\). By the corrected construction,
\[
 \widehat\pi^{(-\ell)}\equiv\frac12,
 \qquad
 \widehat\mu_0^{(-\ell)}\equiv0,
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega^{(-\ell)}_{x,h,k}=2^{-d}I_k.
\]
These functions are deterministic and hence independent of every evaluation fold. The error \(\pi-1/2\) has Hölder order \(\alpha\), because subtracting a constant preserves every derivative and Hölder remainder in \(\mathrm{ass:propensity-holder}\). Likewise, \(\mu_0-0=\mu_0\) has Hölder order \(\beta\) by \(\mathrm{ass:control-holder}\). Their envelopes are uniform over the calibrated class by strict overlap, bounded outcomes, and the fixed Hölder radii. Balanced fixed folds have sizes comparable to \(n\), and the fallback in \(\mathrm{def:boundary-adapted-estimator}\) makes the statistic measurable for every sample.

By \(\mathrm{lem:uniform-design}\), the deterministic input \(F_0\) equals \(f_P\) almost everywhere. Consequently the density ratio is one and its error is zero. By \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\), the population Gram matrices have uniform spectral bounds, the spline Gram is inserted exactly, the stabilized local Gram is always invertible, and its inverse obeys the stated deterministic resolvent bound. The outcome and the final CATE estimate are bounded by construction, while strict overlap bounds every treatment weight.

The polynomial order \(p=\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\) reproduces the Taylor polynomial allowed by \(\mathrm{ass:near-band-holder}\); the defining Taylor remainder therefore gives the local deterministic bias
\[
 O(h^\gamma).
\]
The normalized spline approximation in \(\mathrm{lem:sieve-approximation}\), after rescaling a window of diameter proportional to \(h\), gives nuisance errors of orders \((h/k^{1/d})^\alpha\) and \((h/k^{1/d})^\beta\). Their product is
\[
 O\!\left((h/k^{1/d})^{\alpha+\beta}\right)
 =O\!\left((h/k^{1/d})^{2\bar s}\right).
\]
For completeness, the first-order empirical average and the ordered second-order average in the displayed construction have, by direct expansion of their variances and the Hoeffding decomposition, respective root-mean-square scales
\[
 (nh^d)^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \frac{\sqrt{k}}{nh^d}.
\]
Indeed, the kernel normalization makes the first score have second moment \(O(h^{-d})\). For the second score, orthonormality of the localized \(k\)-dimensional basis and the exact bounded inverse Gram give a degenerate component with second moment \(O(kh^{-2d})\); division by the number of ordered pairs yields \(O(k/(n^2h^{2d}))\). Its nondegenerate projection is of the first-order scale and is already included in \((nh^d)^{-1/2}\). These calculations also apply entrywise to the fixed-dimensional local Gram, and the deterministic spectral clipping converts them to the inverse-matrix condition through \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\). Thus the density, matrix, approximation, fixed-fold independence, and boundedness hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:bkk-level-set-upper-cited}\) are verified.

It remains only to check the declared tuning. In the high-smoothness branch,
\[
 h_n=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)},
 \qquad
 k_n\asymp nh_n^d.
\]
Hence
\[
 h_n^\gamma\asymp(nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
 \asymp\frac{\sqrt{k_n}}{nh_n^d}
 =n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}=r_n^*.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 (h_n/k_n^{1/d})^{2\bar s}\asymp n^{-2\bar s/d}\le r_n^*,
\]
where the last inequality is equivalent to
\(
\bar s\ge d/[4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}].
\)
In the low-smoothness branch, put
\[
 T=1+\frac d{4\bar s}+\frac d{2\gamma}.
\]
Then \(r_n^*=n^{-1/T}\), \(h_n=(r_n^*)^{1/\gamma}\), and
\[
 k_n\asymp h_n^d(r_n^*)^{-d/(2\bar s)}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 h_n^\gamma=r_n^*,
 \qquad
 (h_n/k_n^{1/d})^{2\bar s}\asymp r_n^*,
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{k_n}}{nh_n^d}
 \asymp n^{-1}(r_n^*)^{-d/(2\gamma)-d/(4\bar s)}
 =r_n^*.
\]
The strict low-branch inequality is equivalent to \(T>2+d/\gamma\), and therefore
\[
 (nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\le r_n^*.
\]
Ceilings in \(m_n\) alter only fixed comparison constants. Thus every required term is \(O(r_n^*)\), uniformly over the calibrated transverse class, in both branches.
