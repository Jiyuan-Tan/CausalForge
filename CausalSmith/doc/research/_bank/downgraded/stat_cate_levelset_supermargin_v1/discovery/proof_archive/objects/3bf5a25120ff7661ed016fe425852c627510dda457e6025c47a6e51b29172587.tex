Write \(N=|\mathcal I_\ell|\); balanced fixed folding gives \(N\asymp n\). We first prove the inequality for one fold. All constants below are uniform in the fold, \(x\), and the member law.

\textbf{Deterministic target and bias.} Let \(\Pi_k\) denote localized \(L_2(P_X)\) projection on the span of \(b_{x,h,k}\), with the normalizing factors in \(\mathrm{def:boundary-adapted-estimator}\), and let \(t_x(u)=q_x^\top r_{x,h,p}(u)\) be the Taylor polynomial of \(\tau_P\) at \(x\). Put \(\overline Q_{x,h,k}=\mathbb E\widetilde Q_{x,h}^{(\ell)}\) and \(\overline R_{x,h,k}=\mathbb E\widetilde R_{x,h}^{(\ell)}\). Conditioning the first-order scores on \(X\), factoring the expectation of each order-two score over its two independent arguments, and using \(\widehat\Omega=\Omega\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline Q_{x,h,k}q_x
 &=\mathbb E\!\left[K_{x,h}r_{x,h,p}
 \left\{\pi(1-1/2)t_x-(\pi-1/2)(\Pi_k\pi)t_x\right\}\right],\\
\overline R_{x,h,k}
 &=\mathbb E\!\left[K_{x,h}r_{x,h,p}
 \left\{\pi(1-1/2)\tau_P+(\pi-1/2)
 \left(\mu_0-\Pi_k(\mu_0+\pi\tau_P)\right)\right\}\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Subtract the first display from the second. Terms involving \(\tau_P-t_x\) are bounded by its Taylor remainder. For the projection commutator, subdivide the rescaled cube into spline cells and Taylor-expand \(t_x\) there. Polynomial reproduction up to order smaller than \(r_B\), which exceeds \(\gamma\), cancels every lower-order cell polynomial; stability of \(\Pi_k\) bounds the remainder by \(Ch^\gamma\). For the remaining nuisance term, orthogonality of \(\mu_0-\Pi_k\mu_0\) to the spline span permits replacing \(K_{x,h}r_{x,h,p}(\pi-1/2)\) by its spline-projection residual. Thus Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\left\|\mathbb E\!\left[K_{x,h}r_{x,h,p}(\pi-1/2)
 (\mu_0-\Pi_k\mu_0)\right]\right\|
 \le C\|\pi-\Pi_k\pi\|_2\|\mu_0-\Pi_k\mu_0\|_2.
\]
Consequently \(\mathrm{ass:near-band-holder}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:sieve-approximation}\) yield the joint higher-order normal-equation bound
\[
\|\overline R_{x,h,k}-\overline Q_{x,h,k}q_x\|
 \le C\left\{h^\gamma+(h/k^{1/d})^{\alpha+\beta}\right\}
 =C\left\{h^\gamma+(h/k^{1/d})^{2\bar s}\right\}.
\]
Separately, the same projection expansion without multiplying by the response residual gives \(\|\overline Q_{x,h,k}-Q_{x,h}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C(h/k^{1/d})^\alpha=o(1)\). The strict spectral slack between the bounds for \(Q_{x,h}\) in \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\) and the clipping thresholds therefore places \(\overline Q_{x,h,k}\) inside the clipping interval and makes its inverse uniformly bounded for all sufficiently large \(n\). Left multiplication of the joint normal equation by \(\overline Q_{x,h,k}^{-1}\), followed by evaluation at \(x\), gives pointwise deterministic bias of the displayed product order. No rate claim for \(\overline Q-Q\) alone is used.

If \(|\tau_P(x)-\theta|\le\eta/2\), then \(W_{x,h}\subset\mathcal D_P(\eta)\) for every sufficiently small uniform \(h\). Otherwise a segment from \(x\) to a point outside the band has a first point \(y\) with \(|\tau_P(y)-\theta|=\eta\), while the near-band Hölder condition gives \(|\tau_P(y)-\tau_P(x)|\le C\|y-x\|^{\min(1,\gamma)}\), contradicting the gap \(\eta/2\) when \(h\) is small.

For later use away from the threshold, put \(\zeta=\min\{1,\gamma,\gamma'\}>0\). Continuity and the two Hölder conditions imply a uniform modulus \(|\tau_P(u)-\tau_P(v)|\le C\|u-v\|^\zeta\) for nearby points: if the segment crosses the boundary of \(\mathcal D_P(\eta)\), split it at the first crossing and apply the two within-region inequalities. Thus a constant local approximation gives a uniform \(O(h^\zeta)\) CATE bias away from the near-threshold calculation. Together with the spline approximation, the deterministic score target converges uniformly to \(\tau_P(x)\). This is the required off-band \(\gamma'\) transfer; it need not have the near-band rate, because off-band classification has the fixed gap \(\eta/2\).

\textbf{Exponential score bounds.} For a centered first-order score entry \(S_i\), \(\mathrm{lem:spline-score-envelope}\) gives \(\mathbb E S_i^2\le Ch^{-d}\) and \(|S_i|\le Ch^{-d}\). Expanding the exponential series, using \(\mathbb E S_i=0\), and bounding every moment of order \(q\ge2\) by \((Ch^{-d})^{q-2}\mathbb E S_i^2\), yields the Bernstein bound
\[
P\!\left\{\left|N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^NS_i\right|>
 C\left(\sqrt{u/(Nh^d)}+u/(Nh^d)\right)\right\}\le2e^{-u}.
\]
For an order-two score, use its Hoeffding decomposition into two first-order projections and a canonical kernel \(H_{ij}\). The projections obey the preceding display. For completeness, the canonical part follows from the same moment method. In the expansion of the \(q\)-th moment of \(\sum_{i\ne j}H_{ij}\), a term vanishes if an observation index occurs once. Associate to each surviving term its multigraph on observation indices. Successively remove double edges, cycles, vertices of degree at least three, and the remaining isolated heavy edges. Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz at these four removals, followed by the bounds in \(\mathrm{lem:spline-score-envelope}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
P\!\left[\left|U_\ell H\right|>C\left\{
 &\frac{\sqrt{ku}}{Nh^d}+\frac{u}{Nh^d}
 +\frac{\sqrt{k}u^{3/2}}{N^{3/2}h^d}
 +\frac{ku^2}{N^2h^{2d}}
 \right\}\right]\le2e^{-u}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the four displayed terms correspond respectively to paired edges, the conditional integral operator, a degree-three star, and the kernel envelope. The multigraph count contributes at most \(C^q q^{q/2}\), \(C^q q^q\), \(C^q q^{3q/2}\), and \(C^q q^{2q}\) in those four cases; Markov's inequality with the \(q\)-th moment and integer \(q=\lceil u\rceil\) gives the display. This derivation applies entrywise to both raw matrices and response vectors. Their dimensions \(J_p\) are fixed, so a union bound changes only \(C\).

Spectral clipping is the Frobenius projection onto \(\{M:q_-I\preceq M\preceq q_+I\}\). The preceding deterministic argument places \(\overline Q_{x,h,k}\) in this set for all sufficiently large \(n\). Projection nonexpansiveness gives
\[
\|\widehat Q_{x,h}^{(\ell)}-\overline Q_{x,h,k}\|_F
 \le\|\{\widetilde Q_{x,h}^{(\ell)}+\widetilde Q_{x,h}^{(\ell)\top}\}/2-\overline Q_{x,h,k}\|_F,
\]
while both inverses are uniformly bounded. The resolvent identity therefore transfers the same exponential bound to the inverse and to the ratio \(r_x^\top\widehat Q^{-1}\widetilde R\), centered at \(r_x^\top\overline Q^{-1}\overline R\). Clipping the final scalar estimate cannot increase its distance from \(\tau_P(x)\), because \(|\tau_P(x)|\le2M_Y\).

\textbf{Insertion of the tuning.} Define
\[
b_n=h_n^\gamma+(h_n/k_n^{1/d})^{2\bar s},
\qquad
v_n=(nh_n^d)^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k_n}}{nh_n^d}.
\]
The calculations in \(\mathrm{lem:estimator-construction}\) give \(b_n+v_n\le Cr_n^*\) in both branches. They also give \((nh_n^d)^{-1}\le C(r_n^*)^2\) and \(k_n/(n^2h_n^{2d})\le C(r_n^*)^2\). Hence, for \(1\le u\le c/r_n^*\), every term in the two exponential displays is at most \(Cur_n^*\): for example,
\[
\frac{\sqrt{k_n}u^{3/2}}{n^{3/2}h_n^d}
 \le Cv_nu\sqrt{u/n}\le Cur_n^*,
\qquad
\frac{k_nu^2}{n^2h_n^{2d}}
 \le C(u r_n^*)^2\le Cur_n^*.
\]
Combining the fixed number of folds shows
\[
P\{|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|>Cur_n^*\}\le Ce^{-cu}
\]
throughout the near band, after enlarging \(C\) to absorb \(b_n\). This is the first assertion with \(u=z\).

Finally suppose \(|\tau_P(x)-\theta|>\eta/2\). The uniform off-band bias just proved is smaller than \(\eta/8\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Misclassification then forces the stochastic score deviation to exceed \(\eta/8\). Apply the preceding score inequalities with \(u=c_0/r_n^*\), where the fixed \(c_0>0\) is chosen so that every deviation threshold is at most \(\eta/8\). This gives \(C\exp\{-c/r_n^*\}\), uniformly over the off-band region, and proves the second assertion.
