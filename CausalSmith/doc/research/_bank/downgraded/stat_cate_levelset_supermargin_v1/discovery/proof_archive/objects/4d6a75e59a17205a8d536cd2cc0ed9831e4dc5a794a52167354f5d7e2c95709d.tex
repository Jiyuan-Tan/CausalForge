Start from the nonorthogonal tensor-product B-spline vector \(N_k\) on the regular grid. At each \(z\in[0,1]^d\), at most \(r_B^d\) entries of \(N_k(z)\) are nonzero, and each entry is bounded by one. If \(G_k=\int_{[0,1]^d}N_k(z)N_k(z)^\top\,dz\), the usual cellwise rescaling gives constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on \(d\) and \(r_B\), such that
\[
 c k^{-1}I_k\preceq G_k\preceq C k^{-1}I_k.
\]
Indeed, on each grid cell the relevant quadratic form reduces after rescaling to one of finitely many fixed polynomial Gram matrices, and summation over the cells gives the display. Orthonormalization may therefore be written, up to an orthogonal change of coordinates, as \(B_k=G_k^{-1/2}N_k\). Orthogonal changes preserve Euclidean norms, so
\[
 \sup_{z\in[0,1]^d}\|B_k(z)\|_2^2
 \le \|G_k^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\sup_z\|N_k(z)\|_2^2
 \le Ck.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:spline-basis}\),
\[
 \|b_{x,h,k}(u)\|_2^2
 =V_{x,h}^{-1}\|B_k(T_{x,h}(u))\|_2^2
 \mathbf 1\{u\in W_{x,h}\}
 \le CkV_{x,h}^{-1}\mathbf 1\{u\in W_{x,h}\}.
\]
This proves the first two assertions.

The dimension \(J_p\) is fixed once \(d\) and \(p\) are fixed, so the Legendre vectors in \(\mathrm{def:window-basis}\) are uniformly bounded. Moreover, \(K_{x,h}=V_{x,h}^{-1}\mathbf 1_{W_{x,h}}\), and \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\) gives \(V_{x,h}\asymp h^d\). All treatment factors are bounded by one, all response factors are bounded by a constant depending only on the calibrated outcome envelope, and \(\mathrm{def:boundary-adapted-estimator}\) sets \(\widehat\Omega_{x,h,k}^{(-\ell)}=2^{-d}I_k\). Hence every scalar entry of \(q_1\) and \(s_1\) is bounded in absolute value by \(CV_{x,h}^{-1}\le Ch^{-d}\). By \(\mathrm{lem:uniform-design}\), \(P_X(W_{x,h})=2^{-d}V_{x,h}\), and thus its second moment, and therefore its variance, is at most
\[
 C V_{x,h}^{-2}P_X(W_{x,h})
 \le C V_{x,h}^{-1}
 \le Ch^{-d}.
\]

For either second-order score, the factor \(V_{x,h}K_{x,h}(X_1)K_{x,h}(X_2)\) equals \(V_{x,h}^{-1}\) on \(W_{x,h}^2\) and vanishes otherwise. Put \(\Omega=\Omega_{x,h,k}=2^{-d}I_k\), where the equality follows from \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\). Independence of \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) and the definition of \(\Omega\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\!\left[\{b_{x,h,k}(X_1)^\top\Omega^{-1}b_{x,h,k}(X_2)\}^2\right]
 &=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Omega^{-1}\Omega\Omega^{-1}\Omega\right)\\
 &=k.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The remaining scalar factors in \(q_2\) and \(s_2\), including the fixed-dimensional Legendre factors, are uniformly bounded. Consequently every raw scalar kernel entry has second moment at most
\[
 CkV_{x,h}^{-2}\le Ckh^{-2d}.
\]
For the pointwise envelope, the already proved basis bound and \(\|\Omega^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=2^d\) imply
\[
 |b_{x,h,k}(u_1)^\top\Omega^{-1}b_{x,h,k}(u_2)|
 \le CkV_{x,h}^{-1}
\]
on \(W_{x,h}^2\). Multiplication by the outer factor \(V_{x,h}^{-1}\) therefore gives the envelope \(CkV_{x,h}^{-2}\le Ckh^{-2d}\). Finally, subtracting either Hoeffding projection and adding the mean changes the envelope only by a fixed factor, while conditional Jensen's inequality changes the second-moment bound only by a fixed factor. The same argument applies entrywise to both \(q_2\) and \(s_2\), uniformly over \(x\), \(h\), and every calibrated member law.
