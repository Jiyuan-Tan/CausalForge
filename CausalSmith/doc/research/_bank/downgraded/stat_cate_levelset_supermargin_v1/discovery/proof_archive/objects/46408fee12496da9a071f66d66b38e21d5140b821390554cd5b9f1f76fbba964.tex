The law in \(\mathrm{thm:calibrated-witness}\) proves that \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\) is nonempty for every \(\gamma>d\). The identification theorem distinguishes the causal upper-level set from its observed-law representative and shows that the latter and its weighted loss are well defined modulo irrelevant null sets.

For the upper bound, \(\widehat G_n^{\mathrm{LP}}\) is one law-independent measurable estimator. The uniform-design, local-Gram, spline-approximation, and estimator-construction lemmas verify all hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:bkk-level-set-upper-cited}\). Applying that cited result with \(\xi=1\) gives, in both branches of \(r_n^*\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)}
 \mathbb E_P[d_H(\widehat G_n^{\mathrm{LP}},G_P;P)]
 \le C_1(r_n^*)^2
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). Taking the infimum over all measurable set estimators proves the minimax upper inequality and also proves the asserted attainment by \(\widehat G_n^{\mathrm{LP}}\).

For the converse, \(\mathrm{lem:transverse-assouad-lower-valid}\) supplies an explicit exact-margin graph packing. Its direct adjacent experiments give the high-smoothness lower bound, and its uniform-design fuzzy adjacent experiments, controlled by \(\mathrm{lem:uniform-bounded-overlap-fuzzy-testing}\), give the low-smoothness lower bound. Thus, in either branch,
\[
 \inf_{\widehat G_n}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)}
 \mathbb E_P[d_H(\widehat G_n,G_P;P)]
 \ge c_1(r_n^*)^2.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\), the middle quantity is \(\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\). Decrease \(c_1\) and increase \(C_1\), if necessary, so that \(0<c_1<C_1<\infty\). The construction and all estimates use only the fixed calibrated model constants, so these constants do not depend on \(n\).
