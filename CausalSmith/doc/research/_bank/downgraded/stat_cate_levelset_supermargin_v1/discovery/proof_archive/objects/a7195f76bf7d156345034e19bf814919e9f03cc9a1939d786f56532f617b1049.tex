The folds and the three reference objects are deterministic by \(\mathrm{def:boundary-adapted-estimator}\). Therefore they are independent of every evaluation-fold sample. The window maps, polynomial basis, spline basis, and kernels appearing in the definition are Borel measurable in their sample and evaluation-point arguments. The empirical and ordered \(U\)-statistic operators are finite sums of those measurable functions. Matrix symmetrization is continuous, spectral clipping is a continuous map on symmetric finite-dimensional matrices, and \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\) places every clipped eigenvalue in \([q_-,q_+]\) with \(q_->0\). Matrix inversion is continuous on this set and satisfies
\[
\|[\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le q_-^{-1}.
\]
The scalar clipping map is continuous. Hence, whenever a fold has at least two observations and the raw entries are finite, every operation defining \(\widehat\tau_n(x)\) is jointly measurable in the sample and \(x\). The explicit fallback returns \(\theta\) whenever either exceptional condition occurs, so the map is defined and measurable for every sample. The final set
\[
\widehat G_n^{\mathrm{LP}}=\{x\in\mathcal X:\widehat\tau_n(x)>\theta\}
\]
is therefore a measurable set estimator. Its scalar output satisfies the clipping bound in the definition. No probabilistic tail estimate has been used, so the proof establishes only the construction-level assertions stated in the lemma.
