Fix a calibrated \(\kappa\) and \(\gamma>d\). By \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated-direct-assouad-lower}\), there exist \(c_{\mathrm{lb}}>0\) and \(N_{\mathrm{lb}}<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that for every \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{lb}}\),
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\ge c_{\mathrm{lb}}n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated-direct-regression-upper}\), there exist \(C_{\mathrm{ub}}<\infty\) and \(N_{\mathrm{ub}}<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that for every \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{ub}}\),
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C_{\mathrm{ub}}n^{-2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}/\{2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}+d\}}.
\]
Set \(N_0=\max\{N_{\mathrm{lb}},N_{\mathrm{ub}}\}\), \(c=c_{\mathrm{lb}}\), and \(C=\max\{C_{\mathrm{ub}},c_{\mathrm{lb}}\}\). Then \(0<c\le C<\infty\), and the two preceding displays give the asserted bracket simultaneously for every \(n\ge N_0\).

Suppose now that \(\beta\ge\gamma\). Then \(\min\{\beta,\gamma\}=\gamma\), so the lower and upper powers in the bracket both equal \(2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\). Moreover, \(\mathrm{def:average-smoothness}\) gives
\[
\bar s=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\ge\frac{\beta}{2}\ge\frac{\gamma}{2}.
\]
Because \(\gamma>d\),
\[
\frac{d}{4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}}<\frac d4<\frac\gamma2\le\bar s.
\]
Thus the high-smoothness branch in \(\mathrm{def:rate}\) applies, and hence
\[
r_n^*=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Substituting this identity and \(\min\{\beta,\gamma\}=\gamma\) into the bracket yields, for every \(n\ge N_0\),
\[
c(r_n^*)^2
\le\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C(r_n^*)^2.
\]
The constants are positive, finite, and independent of \(n\), which is precisely \(\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\asymp(r_n^*)^2\).
