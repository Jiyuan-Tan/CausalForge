Write \(r(x,a,y)=\mathbb E_\lambda p_\lambda(a,y\mid x)\). Since the signs are centered and independent,
\[
\mathbb E_\lambda u_\lambda=\mathbb E_\lambda v_\lambda=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\lambda\{u_\lambda v_\lambda\}=\frac{t_1-t_0}{4}.
\]
Substitution in either pair of displays gives the pointwise identity
\[
\mathbb E_\lambda p_\lambda(a,y\mid x)
=\mathbb E_\lambda q_\lambda(a,y\mid x)
=r(x,a,y).
\]
Thus the fixed uniform density of \(X\) is the same under every component law and under both mixtures; it contributes the common factor \(2^{-d}\) to every likelihood. The cell lower bound gives \(r\ge\varepsilon\).

Let
\[
L^P_\lambda(x,a,y)=\frac{p_\lambda(a,y\mid x)}{r(x,a,y)}-1,
\qquad
L^Q_\lambda(x,a,y)=\frac{q_\lambda(a,y\mid x)}{r(x,a,y)}-1.
\]
These are polynomials of degree at most two in the Rademacher signs. Their coefficients vanish off the fine supports and are bounded, up to a constant depending only on \(\varepsilon\), by \(a_0\), \(b_0\), and \(a_0b_0\). The preceding pointwise mixture identity says that their constant Walsh coefficients are both zero.

We record the coefficient calculation that distinguishes the two product mixtures. In the case \(a_0\le b_0\), direct multiplication of the four displayed cells and use of \((a-1/2)^2=(y-1/2)^2=1/4\) show that every two-observation Walsh coefficient which is different for \(P\) and \(Q\) contains either one \(u\)-coefficient and one \(v\)-coefficient, or two \(u\)-coefficients. Its squared magnitude is therefore bounded by
\[
C\{a_0^2b_0^2+a_0^4\}.
\]
All terms containing only \(v\)-coefficients are identical and cancel. In the case \(b_0\le a_0\), the same calculation with the second display for \(p_\lambda\) interchanges the roles of \(u\) and \(v\), giving
\[
C\{a_0^2b_0^2+b_0^4\}.
\]
Consequently, in both cases the squared size of a distinguishing pair is at most
\[
B_0=C\{a_0^2b_0^2+\min(a_0^4,b_0^4)\}.
\]
This calculation also explains why the larger one of the two nuisance perturbations does not appear alone in the bound.

For completeness, expand the two mixed product likelihoods relative to the common product law with one-observation density \(2^{-d}r\):
\[
\overline L_P=\mathbb E_\lambda\prod_{i=1}^n\{1+L^P_\lambda(Z_i)\},
\qquad
\overline L_Q=\mathbb E_\lambda\prod_{i=1}^n\{1+L^Q_\lambda(Z_i)\}.
\]
Rademacher orthogonality removes every monomial in which some sign has odd multiplicity. In the difference \(\overline L_P-\overline L_Q\), connected components containing no distinguishing pair cancel exactly. Root each remaining component at its first distinguishing pair. There are at most \(n(n-1)K\) choices of its two ordered observations and first fine support. Integration over the uniform design contributes at most \(Cw^{2d}\) for those two observations. Every further observation in the same component must lie in a support meeting one already selected. The overlap bound gives at most \(D_0\) choices, its design integral is at most \(C_0w^d\), and there are at most \(n\) choices of its sample label. Hence the sum of all rooted components with \(r\) additional observations is bounded by
\[
Cn^2Kw^{2d}B_0\,(CD_0nw^d)^r.
\]
The rooting map has multiplicity bounded by an exponential constant, absorbed into \(C^r\): order the vertices by first appearance in the product expansion, and attach each new vertex to the least previously exposed overlapping support. This gives an injection into ordered rooted support chains together with at most a fixed number of four-cell Walsh labels per vertex. Disconnected common components cancel before this rooting; equivalently, dividing both likelihoods by their common-component expansion leaves the same rooted series. Positivity bounds that common factor and its inverse by the identical geometric series. It follows that
\[
H^2\!\left(\mathbb E_\lambda P_\lambda^{\otimes n},\mathbb E_\lambda Q_\lambda^{\otimes n}\right)
\le Cn^2Kw^{2d}B_0
\sum_{r=0}^{n-2}(Cnw^d)^r.
\]
Here we used \((\sqrt s-\sqrt t)^2\le(s-t)^2/(s+t)\) and the positive common-component likelihood as the dominating denominator. If \(Cnw^d\le1/2\), the series is at most two. Absorbing constants into \(C\) proves the claimed inequality. The calculation explicitly retains the fixed uniform design and uses no change of covariate density.
