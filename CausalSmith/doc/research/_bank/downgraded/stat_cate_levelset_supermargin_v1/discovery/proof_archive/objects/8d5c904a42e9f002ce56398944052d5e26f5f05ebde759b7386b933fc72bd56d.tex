Put \(s=\bar s\). We use the graph packing in \(\mathrm{lem:exact-margin-graph-packing}\), with \(\delta=h^\gamma\), \(M\asymp h^{-(d-1)}\), and local separation \(s_j\ge c\delta^2h^{d-1}\).

\textbf{High-smoothness branch.} For every outer vertex \(\omega\), let \(X\) be uniform on \([-1,1]^d\), let \(A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), let \(Y\mid(A=0,X)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and let
\[
 Y\mid(A=1,X=x)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+\tau_\omega(x)).
\]
Because \(|\tau_\omega|\le c<1/4\), all conditional probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\). Here \(\pi=1/2\), \(\mu_0=1/2\), and the CATE is \(\tau_\omega\). The graph lemma verifies the exact two-sided margin condition, the \(\gamma\)-Hölder condition, and interior crossing; the near band is all of \(\mathcal X\), so the off-band condition is vacuous. Thus every law belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\).

For adjacent vertices, binary relative entropy on \([1/4,3/4]\) is bounded by a constant times the squared difference of the success probabilities. The support and amplitude bounds in the graph lemma therefore give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{\otimes n},P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{\otimes n})
 \le Cn\delta^2h^d.
\]
Choose \(h=(\varepsilon/n)^{1/(2\gamma+d)}\). For sufficiently small fixed \(\varepsilon\), the adjacent total variation distance is bounded by a constant strictly smaller than one, because
\[
 \operatorname{TV}^2(P,Q)\le \frac12\operatorname{KL}(P,Q),
\]
which follows directly from \((\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\le(p-q)\log(p/q)\) and Cauchy--Schwarz. For any sample-dependent set \(H\), integration against the minimum of the two adjacent sample densities and the local separation inequality give
\[
 \mathbb E_\omega d_{H,j}(H,G_\omega)
 +\mathbb E_{\omega^{(j)}}d_{H,j}(H,G_{\omega^{(j)}})
 \ge s_j\{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_\omega^{\otimes n},P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{\otimes n})\}.
\]
Average this inequality over all paired vertices and sum over \(j\). Since the full loss dominates the sum of the disjoint local losses,
\[
 \inf_{\widehat G_n}\max_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega d_H(\widehat G_n,G_\omega;P_\omega)
 \ge cM\delta^2h^{d-1}\ge c\delta^2.
\]
Here \(\delta=h^\gamma\asymp n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}=r_n^*\), proving the high-branch lower bound.

\textbf{Low-smoothness branch.} The strict low-branch inequality implies
\[
 s<\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2\gamma)},
 \qquad
 2s<\frac{d\gamma}{2\gamma+d}<\gamma.
\]
Fix an adjacent graph patch and orient it so that its two CATEs satisfy \(t_1\ge t_0\). Implicit differentiation of the normal-coordinate construction gives
\[
 \Delta=t_1-t_0=\delta\phi^2 A_h,
\]
where \(A_h\ge0\), the support of \(A_h\phi\) has volume \(O(h^d)\), and \(\|D^mA_h\|_\infty\le C_mh^{-m}\).

Let
\[
 w=\delta^{1/(2s)}=h^{\gamma/(2s)}<h.
\]
A normalized smooth lattice partition gives functions \(\chi_l\), \(1\le l\le K\), with
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^K\chi_l^2=1
\]
on the coarse support, support volume \(O(w^d)\), uniformly bounded overlap and intersection degree, derivative bounds \(\|D^m\chi_l\|_\infty\le C_mw^{-m}\), and \(K\le Ch^dw^{-d}\). To see this directly, translate a nonnegative compactly supported smooth bump over a \(w\)-lattice, divide each translate by the square root of the sum of squared translates, and retain only translates meeting the coarse support.

For independent Rademacher signs \(\lambda_l\), choose fixed small constants and set
\[
 u_\lambda=a_*w^\alpha\sum_l\lambda_l\phi\chi_l,
 \qquad
 v_\lambda=\frac{w^\beta}{4a_*}
 \sum_l\lambda_lA_h\phi\chi_l.
\]
Since \(w^{\alpha+\beta}=w^{2s}=\delta\), independence and the squared partition identity give
\[
 4\mathbb E_\lambda(u_\lambda v_\lambda)
 =\delta A_h\phi^2=\Delta.
\]
Product differentiation, \(w<h\), and the displayed derivative bounds show that \(u_\lambda\) and \(v_\lambda\) have uniformly bounded \(\alpha\)- and \(\beta\)-Hölder norms. Notice also that \(\max(\alpha,\beta)\le2s<\gamma\), so adding either \(t_i\) to a nuisance regression preserves the required nuisance smoothness.

For \((a_0,y_0)\in\{0,1\}^2\), suppose first that \(\alpha\ge\beta\), and define on this coarse patch
\[
 p_\lambda(a_0,y_0\mid x)=\frac14+(y_0-\tfrac12)v_\lambda
 +(2a_0-1)(2y_0-1)\frac{t_1}{4},
\]
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q_\lambda(a_0,y_0\mid x)
 &=\frac14+(a_0-\tfrac12)u_\lambda
 +(y_0-\tfrac12)(v_\lambda+t_0u_\lambda)\\
 &\quad +(2a_0-1)(2y_0-1)
 \left(\frac{t_0}{4}+u_\lambda v_\lambda\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Direct summation over \(y_0\) and division by the arm probabilities give
\[
 (\pi,\mu_0,\tau)_{p_\lambda}
 =(1/2,1/2+v_\lambda-t_1/2,t_1),
\]
\[
 (\pi,\mu_0,\tau)_{q_\lambda}
 =(1/2+u_\lambda,1/2+v_\lambda-t_0/2,t_0).
\]
If \(\beta\ge\alpha\), retain \(q_\lambda\) and instead define
\[
 p_\lambda(a_0,y_0\mid x)=\frac14+(a_0-\tfrac12)u_\lambda
 +(y_0-\tfrac12)t_1u_\lambda
 +(2a_0-1)(2y_0-1)\frac{t_1}{4}.
\]
Then \((\pi,\mu_0,\tau)_{p_\lambda}=(1/2+u_\lambda,1/2-t_1/2,t_1)\). In either ordering, averaging the four-cell probabilities over the signs and using \(4\mathbb E(u_\lambda v_\lambda)=t_1-t_0\) shows that the two one-observation mixtures are identical.

Choose the fixed amplitudes successively small. Every cell probability, including every colorwise partial interpolation used below, is then bounded above and below by fixed positive constants; also \(1/4\le\pi\le3/4\), and the nuisance Hölder radii are at most one. Paste these formulas smoothly to the common Bernoulli law outside the patch and repeat them independently on the disjoint outer patches. For each outer vertex and each full sign vector, this produces a law in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\): the covariate law is uniform, the CATE is the corresponding exact-margin graph function, the outcome is binary, and all class conditions have just been checked.

For one outer patch, divide the conditional probabilities by their common one-observation mixture. In the notation of \(\mathrm{lem:bounded-overlap-fuzzy-testing}\), the larger common linear coefficient has size
\[
 R\asymp\max(w^\alpha,w^\beta),
\]
and the residual linear coefficient has size
\[
 D\asymp\min(w^\alpha,w^\beta).
\]
The centered quadratic coefficient is bounded by \(C DR\). Therefore
\[
 D^2R^2+D^4
 \le Cw^{2\alpha+2\beta}=C\delta^2.
\]
Since \(K\le Ch^dw^{-d}\), that lemma yields
\[
 \operatorname{TV}^2(\overline P_{j,n},\overline Q_{j,n})
 \le Cn^2h^dw^d\delta^2,
\]
provided \(nw^dR^2\le c_0\).

Let
\[
 T=1+\frac d{4s}+\frac d{2\gamma},
 \qquad
 h=(\varepsilon/n)^{1/(\gamma T)}.
\]
Because \(w=h^{\gamma/(2s)}\), \(\delta=h^\gamma\), and
\[
 2\gamma+d+\frac{d\gamma}{2s}=2\gamma T,
\]
the preceding total-variation square is at most \(C\varepsilon^2\). Moreover, the low-branch inequality is equivalent to \(d/(2s)>T\), so
\[
 nw^d=n h^{d\gamma/(2s)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the testing lemma's condition holds for all sufficiently large \(n\), and a sufficiently small fixed \(\varepsilon\) makes every adjacent mixture total variation bounded away from one.

Average the risk over the nuisance signs and the outer hypercube. Conditional on the covariates falling outside the one outer patch being compared, the two adjacent mixtures have identical normalized laws; these common factors integrate to one. The local loss separation is independent of all nuisance signs. The same pairing calculation as in the high branch consequently gives
\[
 \inf_{\widehat G_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)}
 \mathbb E_Pd_H(\widehat G_n,G_P;P)
 \ge cM\delta^2h^{d-1}\ge c\delta^2.
\]
Finally,
\[
 \delta=h^\gamma\asymp n^{-1/T}=r_n^*.
\]
For \(d=1\), \(M=1\), and the identical calculation is a fuzzy two-mixture argument rather than a multi-coordinate Assouad pairing. This proves the low-branch lower bound and completes the lemma.
