Fix \(\gamma>d\). Nonemptiness of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\) follows from \(\mathrm{thm:calibrated-witness}\). Every loss in \(\mathrm{def:weighted-loss}\) is nonnegative, so \(\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\ge0\).

For the reverse inequality, use the deterministic estimator \(\widehat G_n=\varnothing\), which is a single measurable sample-independent estimator for every law in the class. Under \(M_Y=1\), both conditional outcome means lie in \([-1,1]\); hence \(|\tau_P(x)|=|\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)|\le2\). Since \(\theta=0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_H(\varnothing,G_P;P)
&=\int_{G_P}|\tau_P(x)-\theta|f_P(x)\,dx\\
&\le2\int_{\mathcal X}f_P(x)\,dx
=2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking expectation, the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\), and then the infimum over all estimators gives
\[
0\le\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\le2
\]
for every \(n\).
