\textbf{Identification almost surely.} For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), conditional exchangeability and strict overlap give
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y^a\mid X,A=a]=\mathbb E_P[Y^a\mid X]
\]
for \(P_X\)-almost every covariate value. Consistency gives \(Y=Y^a\) on \(\{A=a\}\), so
\[
\mu_a(X)=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X]=\mathbb E_P[Y^a\mid X]
\]
almost surely. Subtracting the two arm identities and using \(\mathrm{def:cate}\) yields
\[
\tau_P(X)=\mu_1(X)-\mu_0(X)=\mathbb E_P[Y^1-Y^0\mid X]
\]
almost surely.

\textbf{Uniqueness on the statistical support.} Let \(g_1,g_2\) be two continuous representatives of the same \(P_X\)-almost-sure function. If \(g_1(x_0)\ne g_2(x_0)\) at some \(x_0\in\operatorname{supp}(P_X)\), continuity supplies a relatively open neighborhood \(O\) of \(x_0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) such that \(|g_1-g_2|>\varepsilon\) on \(O\). The definition of support gives \(P_X(O)>0\), contradicting almost-sure equality. Hence the continuous representative is unique on \(\operatorname{supp}(P_X)\).

If two observed-law versions of \(\tau_P\) agree almost surely, their upper sets from \(\mathrm{def:upper-set}\) differ only on a \(P_X\)-null set. Likewise, replacing \(H\) or \(G_P\) by a set differing on a \(P_X\)-null set does not change
\[
d_H(H,G_P;P)=\int_{H\triangle G_P}|\tau_P(x)-\theta|f_P(x)\,dx,
\]
because the integrand is integrated with respect to \(P_X\). Thus \(G_P\) is identified modulo \(P_X\)-null sets and the weighted loss is invariant under those changes.
