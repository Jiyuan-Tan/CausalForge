First let \((\xi,\gamma)\in\mathcal F(\kappa)\). By \(\mathrm{def:feasible-frontier}\), one has \(\xi\gamma>d\) and \(\mathcal M(\xi,\gamma;\kappa)\ne\varnothing\). Choose \(P\) in this class. Then \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated-margin-compatibility}\) gives \(\xi=1\) and \(\gamma>d\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal F(\kappa)\subseteq\{(1,\gamma):\gamma>d\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Conversely, fix \(\gamma>d\). By \(\mathrm{thm:calibrated-witness}\), the observed marginal induced by \(P^{\mathrm{tr}}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\). By \(\mathrm{def:transverse-model}\),
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)=\mathcal M(1,\gamma;\kappa).
\]
Thus \(\mathcal M(1,\gamma;\kappa)\ne\varnothing\), and \(1\cdot\gamma>d\). The definition of the feasible frontier therefore gives \((1,\gamma)\in\mathcal F(\kappa)\). Together with (1), this proves
\[
\mathcal F(\kappa)=\{(1,\gamma):\gamma>d\}.
\]
Finally, if \((\xi,\gamma)\) belongs to this frontier, then \(\xi=1\) and \(\gamma>d\); substituting \(\xi=1\) into \(\mathrm{def:transverse-model}\) gives
\[
\mathcal M(\xi,\gamma;\kappa)=\mathcal M(1,\gamma;\kappa)=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa),
\]
which proves the remaining assertion.
