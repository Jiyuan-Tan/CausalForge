Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\), \(x\in\mathcal X=[-1,1]^d\), and \(0<h\le1\). For each \(j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), \(\mathrm{def:window-basis}\) gives
\[
[L_j,R_j]=[-1,1]\cap[x_j-h/2,x_j+h/2].
\]
Truncation gives \(\ell_j=R_j-L_j\le h\). At least one of \(x_j+1\) and \(1-x_j\) is at least one, hence at least \(h/2\); the corresponding half of the untruncated interval remains in the cube. Thus \(\ell_j\ge h/2\). Multiplication over coordinates yields
\[
2^{-d}h^d\le V_{x,h}=\prod_{j=1}^d\ell_j\le h^d.
\]

By \(\mathrm{lem:uniform-design}\), \(f_P=2^{-d}\) almost everywhere on \(\mathcal X\). The affine map \(z=T_{x,h}(u)\) sends \(W_{x,h}\) bijectively to \([0,1]^d\) and has Jacobian \(du=V_{x,h}\,dz\). Hence \(\mathrm{def:spline-basis}\), \(\mathrm{def:local-matrices}\), and orthonormality of \(B_k\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_{x,h,k}
&=2^{-d}\int_{W_{x,h}}V_{x,h}^{-1}B_k(T_{x,h}(u))B_k(T_{x,h}(u))^\top\,du\\
&=2^{-d}\int_{[0,1]^d}B_k(z)B_k(z)^\top\,dz
=2^{-d}I_k.
\end{aligned}
\]
The known-density construction in \(\mathrm{def:boundary-adapted-estimator}\) sets \(\widehat\Omega^{(-\ell)}_{x,h,k}=2^{-d}I_k\), so \(\widehat\Omega^{(-\ell)}_{x,h,k}=\Omega_{x,h,k}\).

For \(w\in\mathbb R^{J_p}\), the same change of variables gives
\[
w^\top Q_{x,h}w
=2^{-d}\int_{[0,1]^d}\{w^\top\rho_p(z)\}^2
\pi(T_{x,h}^{-1}(z))\{1-\pi(T_{x,h}^{-1}(z))\}\,dz.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:strict-overlap}\) gives
\[
c_\pi(1-c_\pi)\le\pi(u)\{1-\pi(u)\}\le\frac14,
\qquad u\in\mathcal X.
\]
Since the shifted-Legendre basis is orthonormal,
\[
\int_{[0,1]^d}\{w^\top\rho_p(z)\}^2\,dz=\lVert w\rVert_2^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
2^{-d}c_\pi(1-c_\pi)I_{J_p}
\preceq Q_{x,h}
\preceq2^{-d}I_{J_p}/4.
\]

Put
\[
S=\frac{\widetilde Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}+\widetilde Q^{(\ell)\top}_{x,h}}2,
\qquad a=q_-=2^{-d-1}c_\pi(1-c_\pi),
\qquad b=q_+=2^{-d-1}.
\]
Whenever the fold matrix is formed, \(\mathrm{def:boundary-adapted-estimator}\) gives \(\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}=T_{[a,b]}(S)\). Thus every eigenvalue belongs to \([a,b]\), the matrix is invertible, and
\[
\bigl\lVert[\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}\bigr\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le a^{-1}=q_-^{-1}.
\]
All entries used to form a raw matrix are finite because \(h>0\), the bases have finite dimension, the observations are bounded, and \(\widehat\Omega^{(-\ell)}_{x,h,k}=2^{-d}I_k\). If a balanced fold has fewer than two observations, the construction invokes its stated scalar fallback and evaluates no matrix inverse; equivalently one may assign \(aI_{J_p}\) to the unused matrix without changing the estimator.

The population bounds show \(2aI_{J_p}\preceq Q_{x,h}\preceq(b/2)I_{J_p}\), so in particular the spectrum of \(Q_{x,h}\) lies in \([a,b]\) and \(\lVert Q_{x,h}^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le a^{-1}\). Spectral clipping cannot increase Frobenius distance from such a matrix. Indeed, write
\[
S=V\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{J_p})V^\top,
\qquad Q'=V^\top Q_{x,h}V.
\]
Each diagonal entry \(Q'_{rr}\), being a Rayleigh quotient, lies in \([a,b]\). For the scalar projection \(p_{[a,b]}(s)=\min\{b,\max\{a,s\}\}\),
\[
|p_{[a,b]}(\lambda_r)-Q'_{rr}|\le|\lambda_r-Q'_{rr}|.
\]
In the basis \(V\), the off-diagonal contributions of \(Q'\) to the two squared Frobenius distances are identical. Summing entries and using orthogonal invariance yields
\[
\lVert\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}-Q_{x,h}\rVert_F
\le\lVert S-Q_{x,h}\rVert_F.
\]
Finally, the resolvent identity gives
\[
[\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}-Q_{x,h}^{-1}
=[\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}
(Q_{x,h}-\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h})Q_{x,h}^{-1}.
\]
Submultiplicativity, the two inverse bounds, \(\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lVert M\rVert_F\), and the clipping contraction imply
\[
\bigl\lVert[\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}-Q_{x,h}^{-1}\bigr\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le q_-^{-2}\lVert S-Q_{x,h}\rVert_F.
\]
This proves every asserted deterministic bound.
