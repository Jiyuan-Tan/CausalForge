By \(\mathrm{thm:calibrated-frontier-feasibility}\), the calibrated feasible frontier is
\[
\mathcal F(\kappa)=\{(1,\gamma):\gamma>d\}.
\]
The same theorem gives, for every \((\xi,\gamma)\in\mathcal F(\kappa)\),
\[
\xi=1,\qquad \gamma>d,\qquad
\mathcal M(\xi,\gamma;\kappa)=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa).
\]
In particular, \(1+\xi=2\). Applying \(\mathrm{thm:transverse-minimax-rate}\) at this \(\gamma\) yields constants \(0<c_1<C_1<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
c_1(r_n^*)^2
\le \mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C_1(r_n^*)^2.
\]
Because \(\xi=1\), the two powers in this display satisfy \((r_n^*)^2=(r_n^*)^{1+\xi}\), while \(\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)=\mathfrak R_n(\xi,\gamma;\kappa)\). Substitution proves
\[
c_1(r_n^*)^{1+\xi}
\le \mathfrak R_n(\xi,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C_1(r_n^*)^{1+\xi}.
\]
Finally, \(\mathrm{thm:transverse-minimax-rate}\) states that \(\widehat G_n^{\mathrm{LP}}\) attains its upper inequality in each branch of \(r_n^*\). Since every calibrated feasible pair has just been reduced to that transverse case, the same estimator attains the displayed upper bound in both branches. This also shows that the apparent nontransverse part of the calibrated super-margin question is empty: nonemptiness and the tight two-sided margin force \(\xi=1\).
