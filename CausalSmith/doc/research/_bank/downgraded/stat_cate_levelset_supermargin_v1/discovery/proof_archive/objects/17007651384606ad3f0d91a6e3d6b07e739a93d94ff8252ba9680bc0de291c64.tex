Let \(r\) be the product of the uniform covariate density and the uniform law on \(\{0,1\}^2\). Divide every conditional density by the corresponding conditional density of the common one-observation mixture. The lower bound \(\varepsilon\) makes this change of reference bounded in both directions by constants depending only on \(\varepsilon\).

Direct averaging over the independent signs gives
\[
\mathbb E_\lambda p_\lambda(a,y\mid x)
=\frac14+(2a-1)(2y-1)\frac{t_1(x)}4
=\mathbb E_\lambda q_\lambda(a,y\mid x),
\]
because \(t_1-t_0=4\mathbb E_\lambda(u_\lambda v_\lambda)\). Thus the order-one terms in the likelihood expansion of either product mixture agree exactly.

We record the remaining expansion. Write \(g_{P,\lambda}=p_\lambda/\bar p-1\) and \(g_{Q,\lambda}=q_\lambda/\bar p-1\), where \(\bar p=\mathbb E_\lambda p_\lambda=\mathbb E_\lambda q_\lambda\). For observations \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\),
\[
\frac{d\mathbb E_\lambda P_\lambda^{\otimes n}}{d\bar P^{\otimes n}}
=1+\sum_{r=2}^n\ \sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_r\le n}
\mathbb E_\lambda\prod_{s=1}^r g_{P,\lambda}(z_{i_s}),
\]
and the analogous identity holds for \(Q\). The sum starts at two because every sign is centered and the one-observation mixtures agree.

Each summand is a polynomial in the Rademacher signs of degree at most two per observation. Its sign average vanishes unless every sign label occurs an even number of times. Moreover, terms involving only the component common to the two displays cancel when the two product-mixture likelihoods are subtracted. Hence every surviving connected diagram contains an anchor of one of two types: either two occurrences of an \(u\)-coefficient and two occurrences of a \(v\)-coefficient, contributing at most \(C a_0^2b_0^2\), or four occurrences of the coefficient belonging to the smoother, smaller-amplitude component, contributing at most \(C\min(a_0^4,b_0^4)\). This assertion follows by inspecting the displays: in the first case the component common to both experiments is the \(v\)-linear term and the unmatched pure term is \(u\); in the second case the common component is the \(u\)-linear term and the unmatched pure term is \(v\). The bilinear term is always paired with the difference \(t_1-t_0=4\mathbb E(uv)\).

There are at most \(K\) choices for the anchor label. Both observations carrying an anchor must fall in its support or in one of at most \(D_0\) overlapping supports, so integration of the squared anchor contributes at most
\[
CKw^{2d}\left\{a_0^2b_0^2+\min(a_0^4,b_0^4)\right\}.
\]
There are fewer than \(n^2/2\) choices for the two anchor observations. Once an anchor is fixed, every additional observation in the same connected component must fall in a support overlapping a support already present. Bounded overlap gives at most \(CD_0\) choices of a new sign label and integrated covariate mass at most \(Cw^d\); choosing its observation index gives the factor \(Cnw^d\). Thus the sum of connected diagrams containing a specified anchor is multiplied by at most \(\sum_{r\ge0}(Cnw^d)^r\). A disconnected component must contain another anchor. If
\[
B=n^2Kw^{2d}\{a_0^2b_0^2+\min(a_0^4,b_0^4)\},
\]
the exponential formula for unordered components therefore contributes at most \(\exp(CB)\). When \(B\le1\), this is bounded by a constant; when \(B>1\), the desired conclusion follows directly from \(H^2\le2\le2B\). We may consequently restrict attention to \(B\le1\).

The assumed inequality \(Cnw^d\le1/2\) bounds the connected geometric series by two. The same component expansion, now applied to \((x+y)^{-1}\), is absolutely convergent because all cell probabilities are bounded below by \(\varepsilon\). Using
\[
(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y})^2\le\frac{(x-y)^2}{x+y}
\]
and integrating term by term against \(\bar P^{\otimes n}\) gives
\[
H^2\!\left(\mathbb E_\lambda P_\lambda^{\otimes n},\mathbb E_\lambda Q_\lambda^{\otimes n}\right)
\le CB.
\]
All constants depend only on \(C_0,D_0,\varepsilon\). This proves the claim.
