Put
\[
F_P(t)=P_X\{0<|\tau_P(X)-\theta|\le t\},
\qquad
\delta=\min\{\gamma,1\}.
\]
Under calibration, \(c_m=C_m=c^{-1}\) and \(t_0=c\). The lower and upper margin inequalities therefore coincide:
\[
F_P(t)=c^{-1}t^\xi,
\qquad 0<t\le c.
\]
At \(t=c\), the probability bound \(F_P(c)\le1\) gives \(c^{\xi-1}\le1\). Since \(0<c<1\), this implies \(\xi\ge1\).

We next derive the crossing restriction. Membership in the near-band Hölder ball supplies \(L_*<\infty\) such that
\[
|\tau_P(z)-\tau_P(z')|\le L_*\|z-z'\|^\delta
\]
for sufficiently close \(z,z'\in\mathcal D_P(c)\). For \(\gamma\le1\), this is the defining Hölder remainder; for \(\gamma>1\), it follows from the bounded first derivative.

By the interior-crossing condition and continuity, there are two small interior balls on which \(\tau_P-\theta\) has opposite signs. Because the cube is convex, a sufficiently thin cylinder of parallel line segments joining sub-balls is contained in the interior. Parameterize each segment from its positive endpoint toward its negative endpoint. For every cross-sectional coordinate \(y\), let \(b(y)\) be the first parameter at which \(\tau_P-\theta\le0\). Continuity gives \(\tau_P(b(y))=\theta\), and every point immediately before \(b(y)\) has positive contrast.

For all sufficiently small \(u>0\), both the zero and the point at parameter \(b(y)-u\) lie in \(\mathcal D_P(c)\). Indeed, otherwise continuity would place a point at contrast \(c\) within distance \(u\) of the zero, contradicting \(c\le L_*u^\delta\) for small \(u\). Hence
\[
0<\tau_P(b(y)-u)-\theta\le L_*u^\delta.
\]
Taking \(0<u<(t/L_*)^{1/\delta}\), integrating over the cylinder's positive-volume cross-section, and using \(f_P=2^{-d}\) almost everywhere gives
\[
F_P(t)\ge C_*t^{1/\delta}
\]
for all sufficiently small \(t\), with \(C_*>0\). Comparison with \(F_P(t)=c^{-1}t^\xi\) as \(t\downarrow0\) forces \(\xi\delta\le1\).

If \(\gamma\le1\), then \(\xi\gamma\le1\le d\), contrary to \(\xi\gamma>d\). Thus \(\gamma>1\), so \(\delta=1\) and \(\xi\le1\). Combining this with \(\xi\ge1\) yields \(\xi=1\), and then \(\xi\gamma>d\) reduces to \(\gamma>d\).
