Membership in \(\mathcal M(\xi,\gamma;\kappa)\), together with \(c_m=C_m=c^{-1}\) and \(t_0=c\) from \(\mathrm{def:calibration}\), makes the two margin inequalities equalities:
\[
P_X\{0<|\tau_P(X)-\theta|\le t\}=c^{-1}t^\xi,
\qquad 0<t\le c.
\tag{1}
\]
At \(t=c\), the left side is at most one, so \(c^{\xi-1}\le1\). Since \(0<c<1\), this implies
\[
\xi\ge1.
\tag{2}
\]

We next derive the opposite inequality from an interior crossing. Let \(x^-\) and \(x^+\) be the points supplied by \(\mathrm{ass:interior-crossing}\). Under calibration, \(\mathcal X=[-1,1]^d\). By continuity and by shrinking small neighborhoods of the two endpoints, one can choose a solid cylinder
\[
\{x^-_z+s e: z\in B, 0\le s\le\ell_0\}\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal X),
\]
whose cross-section \(B\subset\mathbb R^{d-1}\) has positive \((d-1)\)-dimensional measure, such that \(\tau_P(x^-_z)-\theta\le-\varepsilon\) and \(\tau_P(x^+_z)-\theta\ge\varepsilon\) at its two end faces, uniformly over \(z\in B\). Here \(e\) is the cylinder direction and \(x^+_z=x^-_z+\ell_0e\).

Put \(a=\min\{\gamma,1\}\). The Taylor-remainder Hölder condition on the threshold band implies, after possibly decreasing \(\varepsilon\), that along the portion of each cylinder line where \(|\tau_P-\theta|\le\eta\),
\[
|\tau_P(x)-\tau_P(y)|\le L_*\|x-y\|^a
\tag{3}
\]
for a constant \(L_*<\infty\) independent of the line. For \(\gamma\le1\), this is the defining Hölder inequality. For \(\gamma>1\), the Hölder norm bounds the first derivatives, and the mean-value theorem gives (3) with \(a=1\). Uniform continuity on compact \(\mathcal X\) also supplies a fixed neighborhood of the zero set contained in \(\mathcal D_P(\eta)\), so (3) applies on all sufficiently short line segments ending at a zero.

Fix a line indexed by \(z\). Starting from its negative end, let \(s_z\) be the first location at which \(\tau_P-\theta=0\). Before \(s_z\) the function is nonzero and negative. For all sufficiently small \(t>0\), the interval immediately to the left of \(s_z\) of length \((t/L_*)^{1/a}\) stays inside the cylinder and, by (3), satisfies
\[
0<|\tau_P-\theta|\le t.
\]
The endpoint separation by \(\varepsilon\) and the fixed zero-set neighborhood make the allowable range of \(t\) uniform in \(z\). Fubini's theorem and the density lower bound \(f_P\ge2^{-d}\) therefore give
\[
P_X\{0<|\tau_P(X)-\theta|\le t\}
\ge C t^{1/a}
\tag{4}
\]
for all sufficiently small \(t>0\), where \(C>0\) is independent of \(t\). Combining (1) and (4) and sending \(t\downarrow0\) yields
\[
\xi\le\frac1a=\frac1{\min\{\gamma,1\}}.
\tag{5}
\]

If \(\gamma\le1\), then (5) implies \(\xi\gamma\le1\le d\), contradicting the assumed strict inequality \(\xi\gamma>d\). Hence \(\gamma>1\), so (5) becomes \(\xi\le1\). Together with (2), this proves \(\xi=1\). The assumed inequality then reads \(\gamma>d\), as required.
