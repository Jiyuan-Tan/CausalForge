Fix a calibrated \(\kappa\) and \(\gamma>d\). By the calibrated direct Assouad lower bound, there are constants \(c_{\mathrm{lb}}>0\) and \(N_{\mathrm{lb}}<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\ge c_{\mathrm{lb}}n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\]
for every \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{lb}}\). By the calibrated direct regression upper bound, there are constants \(C_{\mathrm{ub}}<\infty\) and \(N_{\mathrm{ub}}<\infty\), also independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C_{\mathrm{ub}}n^{-2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}/\{2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}+d\}}
\]
for every \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{ub}}\). Set
\[
N_0=\max\{N_{\mathrm{lb}},N_{\mathrm{ub}}\},\qquad
c=c_{\mathrm{lb}},\qquad
C=\max\{c_{\mathrm{lb}},C_{\mathrm{ub}}\}.
\]
Then \(0<c\le C<\infty\), and the two preceding inequalities hold simultaneously for every \(n\ge N_0\). This proves the displayed two-sided bracket.

It remains to verify the matched subregime and the applicable branch of \(r_n^*\). Suppose \(\beta\ge\gamma\). Then \(\min\{\beta,\gamma\}=\gamma\), so the lower and upper exponents in the bracket are both \(2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\). Moreover, by the definition of average smoothness,
\[
\bar s=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\ge\frac{\beta}{2}\ge\frac{\gamma}{2}.
\]
Because \(\gamma>d\),
\[
\frac{d}{4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}}
<\frac d4
<\frac\gamma2
\le\bar s.
\]
Hence the high-smoothness branch in the definition of \(r_n^*\) applies, and
\[
r_n^*=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Substituting this identity and \(\min\{\beta,\gamma\}=\gamma\) into the bracket yields
\[
c(r_n^*)^2
\le\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C(r_n^*)^2
\]
for every \(n\ge N_0\). Since \(c\) and \(C\) are positive, finite, and independent of \(n\), this is precisely \(\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\asymp(r_n^*)^2\).
