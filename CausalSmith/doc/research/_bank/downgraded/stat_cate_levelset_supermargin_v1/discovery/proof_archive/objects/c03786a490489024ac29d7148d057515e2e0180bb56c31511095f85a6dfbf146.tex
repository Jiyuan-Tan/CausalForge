Let \(q\in\{\alpha,\beta,\bar s\}\). By \(\mathrm{def:average-smoothness}\), \(\bar s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\le\max\{\alpha,\beta\}\), while \(\mathrm{def:spline-basis}\) chooses the spline order strictly larger than \(\lceil\max\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\gamma'\}\rceil\). Thus the tensor spline space reproduces all coordinatewise polynomials needed for a Taylor approximation of order \(q\).

Partition \([0,1]^d\) into the tensor grid of side length \(m^{-1}\). On each grid cell, Taylor's formula in the Hölder ball \(\mathsf H_q(1;[0,1]^d)\) gives a polynomial of degree less than the spline order whose pointwise remainder is bounded by \(C m^{-q}\). The standard local B-spline partition of unity glues these polynomial pieces into a spline \(s_m\) in the span of the tensor-product basis, with
\[
\|g-s_m\|_{L_\infty([0,1]^d)}\le C_{\mathrm{app}}m^{-q}.
\]
The domain has unit volume, so the same bound holds in \(L_2([0,1]^d)\). Orthonormalizing the B-splines changes only the coordinates and not their span. Hence there is \(v\in\mathbb R^k\) such that \(s_m=v^\top B_k\), and, because \(k=m^d\),
\[
\inf_v\|g-v^\top B_k\|_{L_2([0,1]^d)}
\le C_{\mathrm{app}}m^{-q}
=C_{\mathrm{app}}k^{-q/d}.
\]
Applying this display with \(q=\alpha\) and \(q=\beta\) and multiplying the two approximation orders yields
\[
k^{-\alpha/d}k^{-\beta/d}
=k^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}
=k^{-2\bar s/d},
\]
where the last equality again uses \(\bar s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\).
