Fix a calibrated \(\kappa\) and \(\gamma>d\). By \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated\text{-}direct\text{-}assouad\text{-}lower}\), there exist constants \(c_{\mathrm{lb}}>0\) and \(N_{\mathrm{lb}}<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\ge c_{\mathrm{lb}}n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\]
for every \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{lb}}\). By \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated\text{-}direct\text{-}regression\text{-}upper}\), there exist constants \(C_{\mathrm{ub}}<\infty\) and \(N_{\mathrm{ub}}<\infty\), also independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C_{\mathrm{ub}}n^{-2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}/\{2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}+d\}}
\]
for every \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{ub}}\). Set
\[
N_0=\max\{N_{\mathrm{lb}},N_{\mathrm{ub}}\},
\qquad c=c_{\mathrm{lb}},
\qquad C=\max\{c_{\mathrm{lb}},C_{\mathrm{ub}}\}.
\]
Then \(0<c\le C<\infty\), and both preceding inequalities hold simultaneously for every \(n\ge N_0\). This proves the displayed two-sided bracket.

Now suppose \(\beta\ge\gamma\). Then \(\min\{\beta,\gamma\}=\gamma\), so the lower and upper exponents in the bracket both equal \(2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\). By \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}smoothness}\),
\[
\bar s=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\ge\frac{\beta}{2}\ge\frac{\gamma}{2}.
\]
Because \(\gamma>d\),
\[
\frac{d}{4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}}<\frac d4<\frac\gamma2\le\bar s.
\]
Thus the high-smoothness branch in \(\mathrm{def:rate}\) applies, and
\[
r_n^*=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Substitution into the bracket gives
\[
c(r_n^*)^2
\le\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C(r_n^*)^2
\]
for every \(n\ge N_0\). Since \(c\) and \(C\) are positive, finite, and independent of \(n\), this is precisely \(\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\asymp(r_n^*)^2\).
