Fix a calibrated \(\kappa\) and an exponent \(\gamma>d\). By \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated\text{-}direct\text{-}assouad\text{-}lower}\), there are constants \(c_{\mathrm{lb}}>0\) and \(N_{\mathrm{lb}}<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\ge c_{\mathrm{lb}}n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge N_{\mathrm{lb}}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated\text{-}direct\text{-}regression\text{-}upper}\), there are constants \(C_{\mathrm{ub}}<\infty\) and \(N_{\mathrm{ub}}<\infty\), also independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C_{\mathrm{ub}}n^{-2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}/\{2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}+d\}}
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge N_{\mathrm{ub}}.
\]
Set
\[
N_0=\max\{N_{\mathrm{lb}},N_{\mathrm{ub}}\},
\qquad c=c_{\mathrm{lb}},
\qquad C=\max\{c_{\mathrm{lb}},C_{\mathrm{ub}}\}.
\]
Then \(0<c\le C<\infty\), and the two preceding inequalities hold simultaneously for every \(n\ge N_0\). This proves the displayed two-sided bracket.

It remains to verify the asserted matched subregime and the applicable branch of \(r_n^*\). Suppose \(\beta\ge\gamma\). Then
\[
\min\{\beta,\gamma\}=\gamma,
\]
so the lower and upper exponents in the bracket are both \(2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\). Moreover, \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}smoothness}\) gives
\[
\bar s=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\ge\frac{\beta}{2}\ge\frac{\gamma}{2}.
\]
Since \(\gamma>d\),
\[
\frac{d}{4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}}
<\frac d4
<\frac\gamma2
\le\bar s.
\]
Therefore the high-smoothness branch in \(\mathrm{def:rate}\) applies, and its defining formula is
\[
r_n^*=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Substituting this identity and \(\min\{\beta,\gamma\}=\gamma\) into the already proved bracket yields
\[
c(r_n^*)^2
\le\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C(r_n^*)^2
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge N_0.
\]
The comparison constants are positive, finite, and independent of \(n\), which is exactly \(\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\asymp(r_n^*)^2\).
