Define
\[
F_P(t)=P_X\{0<|\tau_P(X)-\theta|\le t\},
\qquad
\delta=\min\{\gamma,1\}.
\]
Under \(\mathrm{def:calibration}\), \(c_m=C_m=c^{-1}\) and \(t_0=c\). The lower and upper margin inequalities therefore coincide, so
\[
F_P(t)=c^{-1}t^\xi,
\qquad 0<t\le c.
\]
At \(t=c\), the probability bound \(F_P(c)\le1\) gives \(c^{\xi-1}\le1\). Since \(0<c<1\), this implies \(\xi\ge1\).

We next use the interior crossing. Membership in the near-band Hölder ball supplies a finite constant \(L_*\), depending only on the fixed radius, such that
\[
|\tau_P(z)-\tau_P(z')|\le L_*\|z-z'\|^\delta
\]
for sufficiently close \(z,z'\in\mathcal D_P(c)\). For \(\gamma\le1\), this is the defining Hölder inequality; for \(\gamma>1\), it follows from the bounded first derivatives and the Taylor-remainder inequality in the Hölder norm.

By \(\mathrm{ass:interior-crossing}\) and continuity, there are two sufficiently small interior balls on which \(\tau_P-\theta\) is uniformly positive and uniformly negative, respectively. Because the cube is convex, after shrinking the balls there is a cylinder of parallel line segments, with a cross-section of positive \((d-1)\)-dimensional measure, joining the positive ball to the negative ball and lying in the interior. Parameterize each segment from its positive endpoint toward its negative endpoint. For a cross-sectional coordinate \(y\), let \(b(y)\) be the first parameter at which \(\tau_P-\theta\le0\). Continuity gives \(\tau_P(b(y))=\theta\), every point before \(b(y)\) has positive contrast, and uniform separation on the endpoint balls keeps \(b(y)\) a fixed positive distance from both endpoints.

There is a constant \(C_0<\infty\), determined by the cylinder geometry, such that points whose parameters differ by \(u\) have Euclidean distance at most \(C_0u\). For all sufficiently small \(u>0\), the point at parameter \(b(y)-u\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_P(c)\), uniformly in \(y\). Indeed, otherwise the intermediate value theorem would produce, between that point and the zero, a point \(z\in\mathcal D_P(c)\) satisfying \(|\tau_P(z)-\theta|=c\) and \(\|z-b(y)\|\le C_0u\). The Hölder inequality applied to \(z\) and the zero would then give \(c\le L_*C_0^\delta u^\delta\), which is impossible for all sufficiently small \(u\). Hence
\[
0<\tau_P(b(y)-u)-\theta\le L_*C_0^\delta u^\delta.
\]
For every sufficiently small \(t\), the one-dimensional section on each segment therefore has length at least
\[
\left\{\frac{t}{L_*C_0^\delta}\right\}^{1/\delta}
\]
on which \(0<\tau_P-\theta\le t\). The set being integrated is Borel because \(\tau_P\) is continuous. Tonelli's theorem, the positive measure of the cylinder cross-section, and \(f_P=2^{-d}\) almost everywhere now yield
\[
F_P(t)\ge C_*t^{1/\delta}
\]
for all sufficiently small \(t\), where \(C_*>0\). Comparing this inequality with \(F_P(t)=c^{-1}t^\xi\) as \(t\downarrow0\) forces \(\xi\delta\le1\).

If \(\gamma\le1\), then \(\delta=\gamma\), so \(\xi\gamma\le1\le d\), contradicting \(\xi\gamma>d\). Therefore \(\gamma>1\), hence \(\delta=1\) and \(\xi\le1\). Together with \(\xi\ge1\), this gives \(\xi=1\). The assumed inequality \(\xi\gamma>d\) then reduces to \(\gamma>d\), as claimed.
