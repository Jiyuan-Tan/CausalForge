\textbf{1. Exact-margin graph family.} Write \(z=(x_2,\ldots,x_d)\) and \(D=d-1\); omit \(z\) when \(d=1\). Choose a nonincreasing \(C^\infty\) function \(\chi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\) which equals one on \([0,1/4]\) and zero on \([3/4,\infty)\). Put \(\psi=-2\chi\chi'\), so \(\psi\ge0\). For a smooth graph \(b\) satisfying \(\|b\|_\infty\|\psi\|_\infty<h/2\), define
\[
 x_\sigma(v,z)=\sigma v+b(z)\chi(v/h)^2,
 \qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\},\quad0\le v\le1,
\]
and define \(\tau_b\) implicitly by
\[
 \tau_b(x_\sigma(v,z),z)=\sigma cv.
\]
Since
\[
 \partial_vx_+=1-\frac{b(z)}h\psi(v/h)>\frac12,
 \qquad
 \partial_vx_-=-1-\frac{b(z)}h\psi(v/h)<-\frac12,
\]
the two branches are bijections from \([0,1]\) onto \([b(z),1]\) and \([b(z),-1]\). Near \(v=0\), \(\tau_b=c\{x_1-b(z)\}\), and for \(v\ge3h/4\), \(\tau_b=cx_1\). Flatness of the cutoff at its constant pieces makes the implicit function smooth at the joins.

Under the uniform covariate law, conditional on \(z\), the density of \(V=|\tau_b(X)|/c\) is
\[
 f_{V\mid z}(v)=\frac12\{|\partial_vx_+|+|\partial_vx_-|\}
 =\frac12\left(1-\frac bh\psi+1+\frac bh\psi\right)=1.
\]
The zero set is a graph and has Lebesgue measure zero. Therefore
\[
 P_X\{0<|\tau_b(X)|\le t\}=\frac tc,
 \qquad0<t\le c,
\]
exactly, and \(G_b=\{(x_1,z):x_1>b(z)\}\).

Choose \(\phi\in C_c^\infty((-1/2,1/2)^D)\), with \(0\le\phi\le1\) and \(\phi=1\) on \([-1/4,1/4]^D\). Pack disjoint tangential cubes of side \(h\) with centers \(z_j\in[-1/2,1/2]^D\). Their number \(M\) satisfies \(M\ge c_Dh^{-(d-1)}\). Put
\[
 b_\omega(z)=a h^\gamma\sum_{j=1}^M(2\omega_j-1)\phi((z-z_j)/h)^2,
 \qquad \omega\in\{0,1\}^M,
\]
where \(a>0\) is fixed and small. For \(d=1\), take \(M=1\) and \(b_\omega=ah^\gamma(2\omega_1-1)\). Since \(\gamma>d\ge1\), \(h^\gamma=o(h)\), so the branch maps are valid for all small \(h\).

Implicit differentiation shows that a derivative of order \(m\) of \(\tau_{b_\omega}-cx_1\) is bounded by \(Ca h^{\gamma-m}\), and the order-\(\gamma\) Taylor remainder is bounded by \(Ca\). The base linear function has Hölder norm strictly below one because \(c<1/4\). Choosing \(a\) small, independently of \(h\), puts every \(\tau_{b_\omega}\) in \(\mathsf H_\gamma(1;[-1,1]^d)\). Its range is \([-c,c]\), so the near band is the full cube and the off-band condition is vacuous. Each graph is interior and is crossed transversely. The exact display above verifies both calibrated margin inequalities with equality.

\textbf{2. Loss separation.} For adjacent vertices, the two local graphs on cell \(j\) are \(b_+=a\delta\phi_j^2\) and \(b_-=-a\delta\phi_j^2\), where \(\delta=h^\gamma\). On the core \(Q_j\), where \(\phi_j=1\), and for \(|x_1|\le a\delta<h/4\),
\[
 \tau_+(x)=c(x_1-a\delta),
 \qquad
 \tau_-(x)=c(x_1+a\delta).
\]
At each point between the graphs, an arbitrary measurable set \(H\) disagrees with at least one truth. With uniform density,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 d_H(H,G_+;P_+)+d_H(H,G_-;P_-)
 &\ge2^{-d}c\int_{Q_j}\int_{-a\delta}^{a\delta}
 \min\{x_1+a\delta,a\delta-x_1\}\,dx_1\,dz\\
 &\ge c_0\delta^2h^{d-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The bound is independent of every nuisance randomization below.

\textbf{3. High-smoothness branch.} Set \(A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), \(Y\mid(A=0,X)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and \(Y\mid(A=1,X)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+\tau_{b_\omega}(X))\). All probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\), while \(\pi=1/2\), \(\mu_0=1/2\), and the CATE is \(\tau_{b_\omega}\). Hence every law is in the calibrated transverse class. Adjacent CATEs differ by at most \(C\delta\) on a set of volume at most \(Ch^d\). The elementary inequality
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\{\operatorname{Bern}(p),\operatorname{Bern}(q)\}
 \le C(p-q)^2,
 \qquad p,q\in[1/4,3/4],
\]
therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{\otimes n},P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{\otimes n})
 \le Cn\delta^2h^d.
\]
Choose \(h=(\varepsilon_0/n)^{1/(2\gamma+d)}\). A sufficiently small fixed \(\varepsilon_0\) makes adjacent total variation at most a constant \(q<1\). Averaging over the hypercube, pairing every vertex with its neighbor in coordinate \(j\), and using that the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-q\), the loss-separation display yields
\[
 \inf_{\widehat G_n}\max_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega d_H(\widehat G_n,G_\omega;P_\omega)
 \ge cM\delta^2h^{d-1}\ge c\delta^2.
\]
Since \(\delta=h^\gamma\asymp n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}=r_n^*\), this is the high-branch bound.

\textbf{4. Low-smoothness branch.} Put \(s=\bar s\). The strict low-branch inequality implies
\[
 2s<d/2<\gamma.
\]
Orient an adjacent edge so that \(t_1=\tau_{-q}\ge t_0=\tau_q\), where \(q(z)=a\delta\phi_j(z)^2\), and put \(\Delta_j=t_1-t_0\). Differentiating the branch equations with respect to \(b\) shows that \(\partial_b\tau_b<0\). The divided-difference identity consequently gives
\[
 \Delta_j(x,z)=\delta\phi_j(z)^2A_j(x,z),
\]
where \(A_j\ge0\), \(A_j\) is supported in a region of volume \(O(h^d)\), and
\[
 |D^mA_j|\le C_mh^{-m}.
\]
Set
\[
 w=\delta^{1/(2s)}=h^{\gamma/(2s)}<h.
\]
Take a smooth squared partition of unity \(\{\chi_l\}\) at scale \(w\) on the support of \(A_j\phi_j^2\): it can be obtained by normalizing the integer translates of a fixed compactly supported bump. It has uniformly bounded overlap, each support has volume \(O(w^d)\), \(|D^m\chi_l|\le C_mw^{-m}\), and only
\[
 K\le Ch^dw^{-d}
\]
terms meet the coarse cell.

For a fixed small \(a_*>0\) and independent Rademacher signs \(\lambda_l\), define
\[
 u_\lambda=a_*w^\alpha\sum_l\lambda_l\phi_j\chi_l,
 \qquad
 v_\lambda=\frac{w^\beta}{4a_*}
 \sum_l\lambda_lA_j\phi_j\chi_l.
\]
Because \(w^{\alpha+\beta}=w^{2s}=\delta\) and \(\sum_l\chi_l^2=1\),
\[
 4\mathbb E_\lambda(u_\lambda v_\lambda)=\Delta_j.
\]
The product rule, \(w<h\), and the derivative bounds above show that \(u_\lambda\) and \(v_\lambda\) have uniformly bounded \(\alpha\)- and \(\beta\)-Hölder norms. Choose \(a\) and \(a_*\) successively small so these norms, together with their constant baselines, fit within the calibrated radius-one balls.

If \(\alpha\ge\beta\), use the first pair of four-cell laws in \(\mathrm{lem:uniform-bounded-overlap-fuzzy-testing}\). Direct summation over \(y\) and division by the treatment marginal give
\[
 (\pi,\mu_0,\tau)_{p_\lambda}
 =(1/2,1/2+v_\lambda-t_1/2,t_1),
\]
\[
 (\pi,\mu_0,\tau)_{q_\lambda}
 =(1/2+u_\lambda,1/2+v_\lambda-t_0/2,t_0).
\]
If \(\beta\ge\alpha\), use its second pair, which gives
\[
 (\pi,\mu_0,\tau)_{p_\lambda}
 =(1/2+u_\lambda,1/2-t_1/2,t_1)
\]
and the same parameters for \(q_\lambda\) as above. Since
\[
 \max(\alpha,\beta)\le\alpha+\beta=2s<\gamma,
\]
the terms involving \(t_i\) satisfy the required nuisance smoothness. The fixed small amplitudes make every four-cell probability bounded below, preserve \(1/4\le\pi\le3/4\), and keep \(Y\) binary. The covariate marginal is uniform. Thus all realized laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\), and their exact margins are unchanged.

The testing lemma applies with amplitudes of orders \(w^\alpha\) and \(w^\beta\). Since \(\max(\alpha,\beta)\ge s\),
\[
 \min(w^{4\alpha},w^{4\beta})\le w^{4s}=\delta^2.
\]
Consequently the squared total variation between adjacent mixtures is bounded by a constant times
\[
 n^2Kw^{2d}\delta^2
 \le Cn^2h^dw^d\delta^2
 =Cn^2h^{2\gamma+d+d\gamma/(2s)}.
\]
Let
\[
 T=1+\frac d{4s}+\frac d{2\gamma},
 \qquad
 h=(\varepsilon_0/n)^{1/(\gamma T)}.
\]
The identity
\[
 2\gamma+d+\frac{d\gamma}{2s}=2\gamma T
\]
makes the last display at most \(C\varepsilon_0^2\). Also, the strict low-branch inequality is exactly
\[
 \frac d{2s}>T,
\]
so \(nw^d=nh^{d\gamma/(2s)}\to0\). Thus both smallness hypotheses of the testing lemma hold for large \(n\), and choosing \(\varepsilon_0\) small makes adjacent mixture total variation at most some \(q<1\).

Apply the same elementary edge-pairing argument as in the direct case, now averaging also over the nuisance priors. The loss separation is uniform in those priors, so
\[
 \inf_{\widehat G_n}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)}
 \mathbb E_Pd_H(\widehat G_n,G_P;P)
 \ge cM\delta^2h^{d-1}\ge c\delta^2.
\]
Finally, \(\delta=h^\gamma\asymp n^{-1/T}=r_n^*\). When \(d=1\), take \(M=1\); the same direct or fuzzy edge argument applies. This proves both branches.
