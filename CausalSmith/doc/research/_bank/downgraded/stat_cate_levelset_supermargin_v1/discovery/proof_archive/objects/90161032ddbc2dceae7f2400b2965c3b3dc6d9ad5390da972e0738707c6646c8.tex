Let \(S_{m,r_B}\) be the tensor-product spline space spanned by \(B_k\), with \(k=m^d\). Orthonormalizing a B-spline basis changes the coordinates but not this span. We recall the elementary quasi-interpolation argument. On the univariate grid, choose on each fixed-size knot stencil dual coefficient functionals that reproduce polynomials of degree smaller than \(r_B\). The relevant moment matrix has fixed finite dimension and is invertible; one-sided stencils at the two endpoints give the same uniform bound. Tensorizing these functionals produces an operator \(Q_m\) into \(S_{m,r_B}\) which reproduces coordinatewise polynomials of degree smaller than \(r_B\) and whose local operator norm is bounded independently of \(m\).

For \(g\in\mathsf H_q(1;[0,1]^d)\), expand \(g\) at a point of each grid cell to order strictly smaller than \(q\). Because \(r_B>q\) for \(q\in\{\alpha,\beta,\bar s\}\), polynomial reproduction cancels the Taylor polynomial, while the defining Hölder remainder is at most \(C m^{-q}\) on the fixed neighboring stencil. Bounded overlap and the uniform operator norm give
\[
\|g-Q_mg\|_{L_\infty([0,1]^d)}\le C(d,r_B,q)m^{-q}.
\]
Since the cube has unit volume,
\[
\inf_v\|g-v^\top B_k\|_{L_2([0,1]^d)}
\le \|g-Q_mg\|_{L_2([0,1]^d)}
\le C(d,r_B,q)m^{-q}
=C(d,r_B,q)k^{-q/d}.
\]
Choose \(C_{\mathrm{app}}\) as the maximum of these three finite constants. Applying the inequality once with \(q=\alpha\) and once with \(q=\beta\), and multiplying, gives
\[
k^{-\alpha/d}k^{-\beta/d}=k^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}=k^{-2\bar s/d},
\]
where the last equality is \(\mathrm{def:average-smoothness}\). On a window of diameter proportional to \(h\), the same rescaling gives the product \(C(h/k^{1/d})^{2\bar s}\), which is the form used by the estimator.
