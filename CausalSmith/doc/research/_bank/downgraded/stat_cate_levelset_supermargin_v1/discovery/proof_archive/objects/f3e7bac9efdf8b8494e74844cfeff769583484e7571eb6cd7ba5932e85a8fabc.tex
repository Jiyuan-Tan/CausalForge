Put
\[
F_P(t)=P_X\{0<|\tau_P(X)-\theta|\le t\},
\qquad \delta=\min\{\gamma,1\}.
\]
Calibration makes the lower and upper margin constants equal, so
\[
F_P(t)=c^{-1}t^\xi,\qquad 0<t\le c.
\]
At \(t=c\), \(F_P(c)=c^{\xi-1}\le1\). Since \(0<c<1\), this implies \(\xi\ge1\).

We next derive the crossing compatibility inequality. The Taylor-remainder Hölder norm supplies \(L_*<\infty\) such that
\[
|\tau_P(z)-\tau_P(z')|\le L_*\|z-z'\|^\delta
\]
whenever \(z,z'\in\mathcal D_P(c)\) are sufficiently close. For \(\gamma\le1\), this is the defining remainder inequality; for \(\gamma>1\), it follows from the uniformly bounded first derivative.

Choose small balls around the two interior points in \(\mathrm{ass:interior-crossing}\), one on which \(\tau_P-\theta>0\) and the other on which it is negative. A sufficiently thin cylinder of parallel segments joins subsets of these balls and remains in the cube. Parameterize a segment from its positive endpoint toward its negative endpoint. For each cross-sectional coordinate \(y\), let \(b(y)\) be the first parameter at which \(\tau_P-\theta\le0\). Continuity gives \(\tau_P(b(y))=\theta\), while the immediately preceding points have positive contrast. The hitting-time map is measurable because it is defined from a continuous function.

For all sufficiently small \(u>0\), the point just before the hit has contrast at most \(c\). Otherwise continuity would give a point at level \(c\) within distance \(u\) of the zero; both points lie in \(\mathcal D_P(c)\), and the Hölder modulus would imply \(c\le L_*u^\delta\), a contradiction for small \(u\). Hence both the zero and the preceding point are in the band, and
\[
0<\tau_P(b(y)-u)-\theta\le L_*u^\delta.
\]
Taking \(0<u<(t/L_*)^{1/\delta}\), integrating over the positive-volume cross-section, and using the calibrated density \(f_P=2^{-d}\), gives
\[
F_P(t)\ge C_*t^{1/\delta}
\]
for all sufficiently small \(t\). Comparing this with \(F_P(t)=c^{-1}t^\xi\) as \(t\downarrow0\) forces
\[
\xi\delta\le1.
\]
If \(\gamma\le1\), this says \(\xi\gamma\le1\le d\), contradicting \(\xi\gamma>d\). Therefore \(\gamma>1\), so \(\delta=1\) and \(\xi\le1\). Together with \(\xi\ge1\), this gives \(\xi=1\), and the super-margin inequality becomes \(\gamma>d\).
