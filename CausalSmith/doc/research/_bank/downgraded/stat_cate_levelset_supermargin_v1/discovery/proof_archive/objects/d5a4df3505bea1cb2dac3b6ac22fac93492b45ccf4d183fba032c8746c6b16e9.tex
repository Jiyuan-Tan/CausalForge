The construction fixes \(\widehat\pi^{(-\ell)}\equiv1/2\), \(\widehat\mu_0^{(-\ell)}\equiv0\), and \(\widehat\Omega^{(-\ell)}_{x,h,k}=2^{-d}I_k\), so these objects are deterministic. The raw fold averages and ordered second-order averages are measurable functions of the sample. Symmetrization is continuous, and the spectral map \(T_{[q_-,q_+]}\) is continuous on symmetric matrices. Therefore every \(\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}\) is measurable.

By \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\), its spectrum is contained in \([q_-,q_+]\) with \(q_->0\). It is thus invertible for every sample, and
\[
\|[\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le q_-^{-1}.
\]
Matrix inversion is continuous on this uniformly positive-definite set. The foldwise fitted value, its prescribed fallback for a nonfinite raw input or a fold of size smaller than two, the finite fold average, and the scalar clipping operation are therefore measurable. The final clipping step gives, pointwise,
\[
|\widehat\tau_n(x)|\le2M_Y.
\]
The local windows, spline functions, polynomial functions, matrix operations, and fallback are measurable in \(x\), so \(x\mapsto\widehat\tau_n(x)\) is measurable. Hence
\[
\widehat G_n^{\mathrm{LP}}=\{x\in\mathcal X:\widehat\tau_n(x)>\theta\}
\]
is a measurable set-valued statistic for every sample. These conclusions are deterministic and make no stochastic-rate assertion.
