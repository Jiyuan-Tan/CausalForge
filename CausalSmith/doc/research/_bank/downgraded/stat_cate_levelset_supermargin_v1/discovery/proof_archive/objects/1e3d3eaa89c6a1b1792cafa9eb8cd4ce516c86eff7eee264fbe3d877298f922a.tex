Start with the usual nonorthogonal tensor B-splines on the regular grid. At any point at most \(r_B^d\) basis functions are nonzero, each is bounded by one, and their Gram matrix, after multiplication by \(m^d=k\), has eigenvalues bounded above and below by positive constants depending only on \(d\) and \(r_B\). Indeed, on each grid cell rescaling to the unit cell reduces the quadratic form to one of finitely many fixed polynomial Gram matrices; summing the cell inequalities proves the global bounds. If \(G_k\) is this Gram matrix and \(N_k(z)\) is the nonorthogonal basis vector, the orthonormalized basis is \(B_k(z)=G_k^{-1/2}N_k(z)\). Consequently,
\[
\|B_k(z)\|_2^2
 \le \|G_k^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|N_k(z)\|_2^2
 \le Ck.
\]
The definition of the localized basis then gives
\[
\|b_{x,h,k}(u)\|_2^2
 =V_{x,h}^{-1}\|B_k(T_{x,h}(u))\|_2^2
 \mathbf 1\{u\in W_{x,h}\}
 \le CkV_{x,h}^{-1}\mathbf 1\{u\in W_{x,h}\}.
\]
The total-degree Legendre vector has fixed dimension \(J_p\), because \(d\) and \(p\) are fixed, and hence its norm is uniformly bounded. Also \(K_{x,h}=V_{x,h}^{-1}\mathbf 1_{W_{x,h}}\), \(V_{x,h}\asymp h^d\) by \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\), all treatment and outcome factors are bounded, and the stabilized inverse spline Gram is the fixed matrix \(2^dI_k\). Thus a first-order score entry has absolute value at most \(Ch^{-d}\), and its square integrates over a window of probability \(O(h^d)\), giving variance at most \(Ch^{-d}\).

For a second-order score, the factor \(V_{x,h}K_{x,h}(X_1)K_{x,h}(X_2)\) is \(V_{x,h}^{-1}\) on \(W_{x,h}^2\). Orthonormality and \(\Omega_{x,h,k}=2^{-d}I_k\) give
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\{b_{x,h,k}(X_1)^\top\Omega_{x,h,k}^{-1}b_{x,h,k}(X_2)\}^2
 \mathbf 1\{X_1,X_2\in W_{x,h}\}\right]\le Ck.
\]
After the outer factor \(V_{x,h}^{-2}\), this yields second moment at most \(CkV_{x,h}^{-2}\le Ckh^{-2d}\). The pointwise basis bound gives the corresponding envelope \(CkV_{x,h}^{-2}\le Ckh^{-2d}\). Subtracting either Hoeffding projection changes these bounds only by a fixed factor, by conditional Jensen. The same calculation applies to the matrix and response kernels and is uniform over the calibrated class.
