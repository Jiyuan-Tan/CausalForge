Fix the calibrated \(\kappa\) and \(\gamma>d\). By \(\mathrm{thm:calibrated-frontier-feasibility}\), the class entering \(\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\) is the nonempty class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\). The two inequalities in the first display are exactly \(\mathrm{prop:calibrated-direct-rate-bracket}\), after renaming its positive comparison constants as \(c_1\) and \(C_1\).

Suppose first that \(\beta\ge\gamma\). Then \(\min\{\beta,\gamma\}=\gamma\), so the lower and upper powers in the bracket coincide. Moreover, \(\mathrm{def:average-smoothness}\) gives
\[
 \bar s=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\ge\frac{\gamma}{2}.
\]
Since \(\gamma>d\),
\[
 \frac{\gamma}{2}>\frac d4>
 \frac{d}{4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}}.
\]
Consequently the high-smoothness branch in \(\mathrm{def:rate}\) applies and
\[
 r_n^*=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Substitution into the bracket yields
\[
 c_1(r_n^*)^2
 \le \mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
 \le C_1(r_n^*)^2,
\]
which proves the asserted exact order.

Now suppose that \(\beta<\gamma\). Strict monotonicity of \(q\mapsto 2q/(2q+d)\) on \((0,\infty)\) gives
\[
 \frac{2\beta}{2\beta+d}<
 \frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma+d},
\]
so the endpoints of the proved bracket do not match. If the high-smoothness branch applies, \(\mathrm{def:rate}\) again gives \((r_n^*)^2=n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\). Hence the lower endpoint has the desired order, whereas the proved upper endpoint has the strictly smaller exponent \(2\beta/(2\beta+d)\).

It remains to locate the low-smoothness branch. Its defining strict inequality is
\[
 \bar s<\frac{d}{4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}}
 =\frac{d\gamma}{2(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \beta<\alpha+\beta=2\bar s
 <\frac{d\gamma}{2\gamma+d}<\gamma,
\]
so every low-smoothness point indeed satisfies \(\beta<\gamma\). Put \(T=1+d/(4\bar s)+d/(2\gamma)\). The low-branch inequality is equivalent to
\[
 T>2+\frac d\gamma,
\]
and hence
\[
 \frac{2}{T}<\frac{2}{2+d/\gamma}
 =\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma+d}.
\]
For the other comparison, positivity of \(\alpha\) and the inequality \(\gamma>d\) imply
\[
 T
 =1+\frac{d}{2(\alpha+\beta)}+\frac{d}{2\gamma}
 <1+\frac{d}{2\beta}+\frac12
 <2+\frac d\beta.
\]
Taking reciprocals of positive quantities gives
\[
 \frac{2}{T}>\frac{2}{2+d/\beta}
 =\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+d}.
\]
Combining the last two comparisons proves the displayed strict ordering of the three exponents and completes the partial answer.
