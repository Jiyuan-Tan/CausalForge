The covariate marginal is identical under the two mixtures. Therefore conditional Hellinger distance gives the exact identity
\[
H^2(\overline P_N,\overline Q_N)
 =\mathbb E_{X^N}H^2\!\left(\overline P_N(\,\cdot\mid X^N),
 \overline Q_N(\,\cdot\mid X^N)\right).
\]
Fix \(x^N\). Form the bipartite incidence graph whose left vertices are observations and whose right vertices are Rademacher labels, joining observation \(i\) to label \(l\) when \(x_i\) lies in the support carrying that label. Independence of the signs and conditional independence of the responses imply that each conditional mixture is a product over the connected components \(C\) of this graph:
\[
\overline P_N(\,\cdot\mid x^N)=\bigotimes_C P_C,
\qquad
\overline Q_N(\,\cdot\mid x^N)=\bigotimes_C Q_C.
\]
This is a factorization of normalized conditional probability laws, not of likelihood perturbation series. Consequently,
\[
H^2\!\left(\bigotimes_CP_C,\bigotimes_CQ_C\right)
\le\sum_CH^2(P_C,Q_C).
\]
In particular, any component on which the two normalized laws agree contributes exactly zero, irrespective of the size of its common likelihood factor. This is the denominator-aware cancellation missing from an unnormalized disconnected-diagram argument.

We bound a component containing \(r\) observations. When \(r=1\), its two laws agree by the assumed equality of the one-observation mixtures. For \(r\ge2\), expand its finite four-cell probabilities in the Rademacher Walsh basis. The constant coefficients and the common linear coefficients are identical. Rademacher parity implies that a nonzero term in \(P_C-Q_C\) contains either a residual linear coefficient paired with another occurrence of the same sign, or a residual quadratic coefficient. Its magnitude is therefore at most
\[
C^r(DR+D^2).
\]
Each component cell probability is at least \(\varepsilon^r\). Using \((\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\le(p-q)^2/(p+q)\) and summing over the \(4^r\) response configurations gives
\[
H^2(P_C,Q_C)\le C^r(D^2R^2+D^4).
\]
Notice that the positive denominator is the actual normalized component probability. No inverse of an \(N\)-sample mixture likelihood is introduced.

It remains to average the component count over the uniform design. Root a component of size \(r\ge2\) at its least Rademacher label and the first two observation indices connected through that label. There are at most \(K N(N-1)\) choices. Both rooted covariates must fall in the corresponding fine support, which has probability at most \(Cw^d\), giving \(Cw^{2d}\). Order the other observations by first graph distance from the root. Each new observation meets a support already exposed; bounded overlap gives at most \(CD_0\) support choices, at most \(N\) index choices, and probability at most \(Cw^d\). Thus the expected sum of the bounds over rooted components of size \(r\) is at most
\[
CN^2Kw^{2d}(D^2R^2+D^4)(CNw^d)^{r-2}.
\]
The least-label and first-distance conventions make this a bounded-multiplicity encoding of every component. Summing over \(r\ge2\) and using \(CNw^d\le1/2\) proves the stated inequality. The proof explicitly includes all components made from the common coefficient: when such a component is identical it contributes zero by normalized product affinity, and when it is attached to a residual pair its common vertices are counted by the displayed geometric series.
