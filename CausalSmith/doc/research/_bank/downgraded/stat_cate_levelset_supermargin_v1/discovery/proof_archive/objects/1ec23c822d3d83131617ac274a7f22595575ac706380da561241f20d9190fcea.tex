Under calibration, \(\mathcal X=[-1,1]^d\), whose Lebesgue volume is \(2^d\), and \(c_f=C_f=2^{-d}\). Every member of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\) satisfies the bounded-density condition, so
\[
2^{-d}\le f_P(x)\le 2^{-d}
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\in\mathcal X\). Thus \(f_P(x)=2^{-d}=F_0(x)\) almost everywhere. Extending both densities by zero outside \(\mathcal X\) preserves equality. Consequently \(f_P/F_0=1\) wherever \(F_0>0\), so the density-ratio error is zero and its essential supremum is one.
