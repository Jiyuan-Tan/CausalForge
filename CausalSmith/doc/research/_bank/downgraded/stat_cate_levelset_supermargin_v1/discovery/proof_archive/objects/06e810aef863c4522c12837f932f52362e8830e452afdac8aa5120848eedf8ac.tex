Write \(z=(x_2,\ldots,x_d)\) and \(D=d-1\); when \(d=1\), omit \(z\). Choose a nonincreasing function \(\chi\in C^\infty([0,\infty);[0,1])\) that equals one on \([0,1/4]\) and zero on \([3/4,\infty)\), and set \(\psi=-2\chi\chi'\ge0\). For a smooth function \(b\) satisfying \(\|b\|_\infty\|\psi\|_\infty<h/2\), define, for \(0\le v\le1\) and \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 x_\sigma(v,z)=\sigma v+b(z)\chi(v/h)^2,
 \qquad
 \tau_b(x_\sigma(v,z),z)=\sigma cv.
\]
The derivatives of the two coordinate maps are
\[
 \partial_vx_+=1-(b/h)\psi>1/2,
 \qquad
 \partial_vx_-=-1-(b/h)\psi<-1/2.
\]
They are therefore bijections from \([0,1]\) onto \([b(z),1]\) and \([b(z),-1]\), respectively. The two definitions agree smoothly at \(x_1=b(z)\), because \(\chi=1\) near zero and hence \(\tau_b(x,z)=c\{x_1-b(z)\}\) there. They also agree with \(cx_1\) outside an \(O(h)\)-neighborhood of the graph.

Conditional on \(z\), uniformity of \(X_1\) and change of variables give the density of \(V=|\tau_b(X)|/c\):
\[
 f_{V\mid z}(v)
 =\frac12\left(|\partial_vx_+(v,z)|+|\partial_vx_-(v,z)|\right)
 =\frac12\left(1-(b/h)\psi+1+(b/h)\psi\right)=1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 P_X\{0<|\tau_b(X)|\le t\}=P(0<V\le t/c)=t/c,
 \qquad 0<t\le c.
\]
Also \(\{\tau_b>0\}=\{(x_1,z):x_1>b(z)\}\), and the range of \(\tau_b\) is \([-c,c]\).

Choose \(\phi\in C_c^\infty((-1/2,1/2)^D)\), with \(0\le\phi\le1\), equal to one on \([-1/4,1/4]^D\). Pack disjoint tangential cubes of side \(h\) with centers \(z_j\), \(1\le j\le M\), where \(M\ge c_0h^{-D}\). For a fixed sufficiently small constant \(a>0\), define
\[
 b_\omega(z)=a\delta\sum_{j=1}^M(2\omega_j-1)
 \phi\!\left(\frac{z-z_j}{h}\right)^2.
\]
When \(d=1\), take \(M=1\) and \(b_\omega=a\delta(2\omega_1-1)\). Because \(\delta/h=h^{\gamma-1}\to0\), the monotonicity requirement above holds for small \(h\). Repeated implicit differentiation of the two coordinate maps shows, for every integer \(m\le\lceil\gamma\rceil\),
\[
 \|D^m(\tau_{b_\omega}-cx_1)\|_\infty\le C_ma h^{\gamma-m}.
\]
Taylor's formula at order \(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\) gives a top remainder bounded by \(C a\|x-y\|^\gamma\). Since \(c<1/4\), choosing \(a\) sufficiently small places every \(\tau_{b_\omega}\) in \(\mathsf H_\gamma(1;\mathcal X)\). The points with \(x_1=1/2\) and \(x_1=-1/2\) have opposite signs for small \(h\), so every member crosses zero in the interior.

It remains to verify separation. Across an edge in coordinate \(j\), the two graphs equal \(a\delta\) and \(-a\delta\) on the core of the \(j\)-th tangential cube. On this core and for \(|x_1|\le a\delta\),
\[
 \tau_+(x)=c(x_1-a\delta),
 \qquad
 \tau_-(x)=c(x_1+a\delta).
\]
At every point between the two graphs, an arbitrary set \(H\) disagrees with at least one of the two upper sets. Using the calibrated density \(2^{-d}\), the sum of the two local losses is therefore at least
\[
 2^{-d}c\int_{[-h/4,h/4]^D}
 \int_{-a\delta}^{a\delta}
 \min\{x_1+a\delta,a\delta-x_1\}\,dx_1\,dz
 \ge c_1\delta^2h^{d-1}.
\]
Finally, the two adjacent functions coincide outside the product of a tangential cube of volume \(O(h^{d-1})\) and a normal interval of length \(O(h)\), and their pointwise difference is \(O(\delta)\). This proves all assertions.
