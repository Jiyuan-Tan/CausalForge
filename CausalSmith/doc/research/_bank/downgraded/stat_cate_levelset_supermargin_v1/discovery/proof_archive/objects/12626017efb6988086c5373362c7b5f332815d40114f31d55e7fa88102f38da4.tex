By \(\mathrm{thm:calibrated-frontier-feasibility}\), the exact lower and upper margin constants imposed by \(\mathrm{def:calibration}\), together with the interior crossing and Hölder regularity, give
\[
\mathcal F(\kappa)=\{(1,\gamma):\gamma>d\}.
\]
The same theorem gives \(\mathcal M(\xi,\gamma;\kappa)=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\) for every feasible pair. Nonemptiness is witnessed by the calibrated transverse law used in that theorem, so this is a genuine frontier and not an empty-class statement. The causal meaning of its CATE and upper set is supplied by \(\mathrm{thm:identified-version}\).

Fix a feasible pair. Then \(\xi=1\). For the upper bound, \(\mathrm{def:boundary-adapted-estimator}\) and \(\mathrm{def:tuning}\) define one sample-measurable estimator, independent of the unknown member law. By \(\mathrm{lem:estimator-construction}\), its deterministic reference nuisances, exact uniform-density input, stabilized matrices, spline approximation, fixed-fold independence, and bounded scores satisfy the hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:bkk-level-set-upper-cited}\) in each branch of \(\mathrm{def:rate}\). Hence
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[d_H(\widehat G_n^{\mathrm{LP}},G_P;P)\right]
\le C_1(r_n^*)^2.
\]
Taking the infimum over all measurable set estimators proves
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\le C_1(r_n^*)^2.
\]
No margin--smoothness restriction is used by the cited upper result.

For the converse, \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated-transverse-assouad-lower}\) constructs laws inside this same calibrated class, not inside a larger bounded-density class. Its direct Assouad experiment gives the high-smoothness branch, and its uniform-design fuzzy Assouad experiment gives the low-smoothness branch. Therefore
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
=\inf_{\widehat G_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[d_H(\widehat G_n,G_P;P)\right]
\ge c_1(r_n^*)^2.
\]
Since \(\xi=1\), \((r_n^*)^2=(r_n^*)^{1+\xi}\). Enlarging \(C_1\) and decreasing \(c_1\), if necessary, makes \(0<c_1<C_1<\infty\) without introducing dependence on \(n\). This proves the displayed matched rate and the asserted attainment in both branches.
