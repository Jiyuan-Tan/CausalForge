Work with the corrected construction and put \(\bar s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\). The reference functions \(\widehat\pi^{(-\ell)}\equiv1/2\) and \(\widehat\mu_0^{(-\ell)}\equiv0\) are deterministic, bounded, and independent of every evaluation fold. Their errors \(\pi-1/2\) and \(\mu_0\) inherit Hölder orders \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), respectively, with constants controlled uniformly by the model radii. The exact identity \(F_0=f_P\) makes the density-ratio remainder identically zero, so no nuisance convergence rate is required. Balanced fixed folds have sizes comparable to \(n\).

The local-polynomial order \(p=\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\) reproduces the Taylor polynomial allowed by the \(\gamma\)-Hölder condition. Consequently its deterministic local bias is \(O(h^\gamma)\). The corrected Gram choice is exact, \(\widehat\Omega=2^{-d}I_k\), and \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\) supplies the uniform population and stabilized inverse bounds. The normalized spline projection and \(\mathrm{lem:sieve-approximation}\) give product bias
\[
O\!\left(\left(\frac{h}{k^{1/d}}\right)^{2\bar s}\right).
\]
For bounded first-order scores and bounded second-order kernels, their Hoeffding decomposition has stochastic scales
\[
(nh^d)^{-1/2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{\sqrt{k}}{nh^d},
\]
respectively. These are precisely the boundedness, matrix, approximation, and fixed-fold independence inputs in the setup of \(\mathrm{lem:bkk-level-set-upper-cited}\).

It remains to verify the two tunings. In the high-smoothness branch,
\[
h=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)},\qquad k\asymp nh^d,
\]
so
\[
h^\gamma\asymp(nh^d)^{-1/2}\asymp\frac{\sqrt{k}}{nh^d}=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}=r_n^*.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\left(\frac h{k^{1/d}}\right)^{2\bar s}\asymp n^{-2\bar s/d}\le r_n^*,
\]
where the inequality is exactly \(\bar s\ge d/[4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}]\). In the low-smoothness branch, \(h=(r_n^*)^{1/\gamma}\) and
\[
k\asymp h^d(r_n^*)^{-d/(2\bar s)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
h^\gamma=r_n^*,
\qquad
\left(\frac h{k^{1/d}}\right)^{2\bar s}\asymp r_n^*,
\]
and, using \(T=1+d/(4\bar s)+d/(2\gamma)\) and \(r_n^*=n^{-1/T}\),
\[
\frac{\sqrt{k}}{nh^d}
\asymp n^{-1}(r_n^*)^{-d/(2\gamma)-d/(4\bar s)}
=r_n^*.
\]
The strict low-branch inequality implies \(T>2+d/\gamma\), hence \((nh^d)^{-1/2}\le r_n^*\). Thus every term is \(O(r_n^*)\) in both branches, uniformly over the calibrated class.
