\textbf{1. A graph family with exact margin.} Write \(z=(x_2,\ldots,x_d)\) and \(D=d-1\); omit \(z\) when \(d=1\). Choose a nonincreasing \(C^\infty\) function \(\chi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\) equal to one on \([0,1/4]\) and zero on \([3/4,\infty)\), and put \(\psi=-2\chi\chi'\ge0\). For a smooth graph \(b\) with \(\|b\|_\infty\|\psi\|_\infty<h/2\), define
\[
x_\sigma(v,z)=\sigma v+b(z)\chi(v/h)^2,
\qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\},\quad0\le v\le1,
\]
and define \(\tau_b\) by \(\tau_b(x_\sigma(v,z),z)=\sigma cv\). Since
\[
\partial_vx_+=1-(b/h)\psi>1/2,
\qquad
\partial_vx_-=-1-(b/h)\psi<-1/2,
\]
the branches are bijections onto \([b(z),1]\) and \([b(z),-1]\). Near zero, \(\tau_b(x,z)=c\{x_1-b(z)\}\), and outside the cutoff region \(\tau_b=cx_1\).

Under the uniform design, conditional on \(z\), the density of \(V=|\tau_b(X)|/c\) is
\[
 f_{V\mid z}(v)=\frac12\{|\partial_vx_+|+|\partial_vx_-|\}=1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
P_X\{0<|\tau_b(X)|\le t\}=t/c,
\qquad0<t\le c,
\]
and \(G_b=\{(x_1,z):x_1>b(z)\}\). This verifies both calibrated margin inequalities with equality.

Choose \(\phi\in C_c^\infty((-1/2,1/2)^D)\), \(0\le\phi\le1\), equal to one on \([-1/4,1/4]^D\). Pack disjoint tangential cubes of side \(h\), with centers \(z_j\), so their number satisfies \(M\ge c_Dh^{-(d-1)}\). Put
\[
b_\omega(z)=a h^\gamma\sum_{j=1}^M(2\omega_j-1)
 \phi((z-z_j)/h)^2,
\qquad \omega\in\{0,1\}^M.
\]
For \(d=1\), take \(M=1\). Let \(\delta=h^\gamma\). Derivatives of order \(m\) of \(\tau_{b_\omega}-cx_1\) are bounded by \(Cah^{\gamma-m}\), and the top Taylor remainder is bounded by \(Ca\). A fixed sufficiently small \(a\) therefore places every CATE in \(\mathsf H_\gamma(1;[-1,1]^d)\). Its range is \([-c,c]\), so the near band is the whole cube and the off-band condition is vacuous. Each zero graph crosses in the interior.

\textbf{2. Loss separation.} Across an edge in coordinate \(j\), the two local graphs are \(b_+=a\delta\phi_j^2\) and \(b_-=-a\delta\phi_j^2\). On the core \(Q_j\), where \(\phi_j=1\),
\[
\tau_+(x)=c(x_1-a\delta),
\qquad
\tau_-(x)=c(x_1+a\delta)
\]
for \(|x_1|\le a\delta\). For every measurable \(H\), at each point between the graphs it disagrees with at least one truth. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_H(H,G_+;P_+)+d_H(H,G_-;P_-)
&\ge2^{-d}c\int_{Q_j}\int_{-a\delta}^{a\delta}
 \min\{x_1+a\delta,a\delta-x_1\}\,dx_1\,dz\\
&\ge c_0\delta^2h^{d-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The separation is independent of all nuisance signs introduced below.

\textbf{3. High-smoothness branch.} Let \(A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), \(Y\mid(A=0,X)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and \(Y\mid(A=1,X)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+\tau_{b_\omega}(X))\). Then \(\pi=1/2\), \(\mu_0=1/2\), and the CATE is \(\tau_{b_\omega}\); all probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\). Adjacent laws differ by \(O(\delta)\) on a set of volume \(O(h^d)\). Taylor expansion of binary relative entropy on \([1/4,3/4]\) gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{\otimes n},P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{\otimes n})
 \le Cn\delta^2h^d.
\]
Take \(h=(\varepsilon/n)^{1/(2\gamma+d)}\). For fixed sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to \((\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\le(p-q)\log(p/q)\) bounds adjacent total variation by a constant smaller than one. Pairing all cube vertices across coordinate \(j\), using the loss separation, and averaging over \(j\) yields
\[
\inf_{\widehat G_n}\max_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega d_H(\widehat G_n,G_\omega;P_\omega)
 \ge cM\delta^2h^{d-1}\ge c\delta^2.
\]
Here \(\delta=h^\gamma\asymp n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}=r_n^*\).

\textbf{4. Low-smoothness branch: fuzzy laws and membership.} Put \(s=\bar s\). The strict low-branch inequality implies
\[
2s<d/2<\gamma.
\]
Orient an adjacent edge so that \(t_1=\tau_{-q}\ge t_0=\tau_q\), where \(q(z)=a\delta\phi_j(z)^2\), and put \(\Delta_j=t_1-t_0\). Implicit differentiation in \(b\) gives
\[
\Delta_j(x,z)=\delta\phi_j(z)^2A_j(x,z),
\]
where \(A_j\ge0\), its support has volume \(O(h^d)\), and \(|D^mA_j|\le C_mh^{-m}\).

Let \(w=\delta^{1/(2s)}=h^{\gamma/(2s)}<h\). A normalized family of translates of a fixed smooth bump gives a squared partition of unity \(\sum_l\chi_l^2=1\) on the coarse support, with overlap bounded by a constant, support volume \(O(w^d)\), derivative bounds \(|D^m\chi_l|\le C_mw^{-m}\), and
\[
K\le Ch^dw^{-d}.
\]
For independent Rademacher signs, define
\[
u_\lambda=a_*w^\alpha\sum_l\lambda_l\phi_j\chi_l,
\qquad
v_\lambda=\frac{w^\beta}{4a_*}
 \sum_l\lambda_lA_j\phi_j\chi_l.
\]
Since \(w^{\alpha+\beta}=w^{2s}=\delta\),
\[
4\mathbb E_\lambda(u_\lambda v_\lambda)=\Delta_j.
\]
Product differentiation, \(w<h\), and the derivative bounds show that \(u_\lambda\) and \(v_\lambda\) have uniformly bounded \(\alpha\)- and \(\beta\)-Hölder norms.

If \(\alpha\ge\beta\), define, on the coarse cell,
\[
 p_\lambda(a_0,y_0\mid x)=\frac14+(y_0-\tfrac12)v_\lambda
 +(2a_0-1)(2y_0-1)\frac{t_1}{4},
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_\lambda(a_0,y_0\mid x)
 &=\frac14+(a_0-\tfrac12)u_\lambda
 +(y_0-\tfrac12)(v_\lambda+t_0u_\lambda)\\
 &\quad +(2a_0-1)(2y_0-1)
 \left(\frac{t_0}{4}+u_\lambda v_\lambda\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then
\[
(\pi,\mu_0,\tau)_{p_\lambda}
 =(1/2,1/2+v_\lambda-t_1/2,t_1),
\]
\[
(\pi,\mu_0,\tau)_{q_\lambda}
 =(1/2+u_\lambda,1/2+v_\lambda-t_0/2,t_0).
\]
If \(\beta\ge\alpha\), keep \(q_\lambda\) and instead put
\[
 p_\lambda(a_0,y_0\mid x)=\frac14+(a_0-\tfrac12)u_\lambda
 +(y_0-\tfrac12)t_1u_\lambda
 +(2a_0-1)(2y_0-1)\frac{t_1}{4}.
\]
This gives \((\pi,\mu_0,\tau)_{p_\lambda}=(1/2+u_\lambda,1/2-t_1/2,t_1)\). Direct Rademacher averaging and \(t_1-t_0=4\mathbb E(u_\lambda v_\lambda)\) show that the one-observation mixtures of the two displayed families are identical.

Because \(\max(\alpha,\beta)\le2s<\gamma\), all terms involving \(t_i\) satisfy the required nuisance smoothness. Choose \(a\) and \(a_*\) successively small. Then every cell probability is bounded below by a fixed \(\varepsilon\), \(1/4\le\pi\le3/4\), the nuisance Hölder radii are at most one, and \(Y\) is binary. Pasting to the common law outside the coarse cells is smooth. The covariate law is uniform, the CATE is one of the graph functions, and the exact margin is unchanged. Applying the construction independently in every disjoint coarse cell gives component laws in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\).

\textbf{5. Low-smoothness mixture distance and Assouad reduction.} In the notation of \(\mathrm{lem:conditional-component-fuzzy-hellinger}\), \(R\asymp\max(w^\alpha,w^\beta)\) and \(D\asymp\min(w^\alpha,w^\beta)\). Hence
\[
D^2R^2+D^4
 \le Cw^{2\alpha+2\beta}+Cw^{4\max(\alpha,\beta)}
 \le C\delta^2.
\]
The conditional-component lemma yields for every adjacent pair
\[
H^2(\overline P_\omega,\overline P_{\omega^{(j)}})
 \le Cn^2Kw^{2d}\delta^2
 \le Cn^2h^dw^d\delta^2.
\]
Let
\[
T=1+\frac d{4s}+\frac d{2\gamma},
\qquad
h=(\varepsilon/n)^{1/(\gamma T)}.
\]
Because \(2\gamma+d+d\gamma/(2s)=2\gamma T\), the last Hellinger bound is at most \(C\varepsilon^2\). The low-branch inequality is equivalent to \(d/(2s)>T\), so
\[
nw^d=nh^{d\gamma/(2s)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the component lemma's collision condition holds for all sufficiently large \(n\). Choose \(\varepsilon\) so adjacent mixture total variation is bounded away from one.

Average over all nuisance signs and pair outer vertices across each coordinate. Other coarse-cell factors are identical normalized conditional laws and therefore have affinity one. The loss separation from Step 2 is uniform over the signs, so the elementary two-mixture testing inequality gives
\[
\inf_{\widehat G_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)}
 \mathbb E_Pd_H(\widehat G_n,G_P;P)
 \ge cM\delta^2h^{d-1}\ge c\delta^2.
\]
Finally, \(\delta=h^\gamma\asymp n^{-1/T}=r_n^*\). For \(d=1\), \(M=1\), and the same calculation is the corresponding direct or fuzzy two-hypothesis argument. This proves the claimed lower bound in both branches.
