Nonemptiness follows from \(\mathrm{thm:calibrated-witness}\). For any observed law in the class, \(Y\in[-M_Y,M_Y]\) implies \(|\mu_a(X)|\le M_Y\) almost surely for each arm. By \(\mathrm{def:cate}\),
\[
|\tau_P(X)|\le2M_Y
\]
almost surely. Calibration gives \(M_Y=1\) and \(\theta=0\). Let the estimator be the empty set for every sample. By \(\mathrm{def:weighted-loss}\), its risk under any member law is
\[
d_H(\varnothing,G_P;P)
=\int_{G_P}|\tau_P(x)|f_P(x)\,dx
\le2P_X(G_P)
\le2.
\]
Taking the supremum over the nonempty class and then the infimum over all measurable set estimators proves \(\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\le2\). Nonnegativity of the loss gives the lower inequality.
