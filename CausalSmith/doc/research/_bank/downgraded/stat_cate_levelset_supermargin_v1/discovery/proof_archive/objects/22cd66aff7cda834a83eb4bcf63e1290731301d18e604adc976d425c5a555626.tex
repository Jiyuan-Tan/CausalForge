Fix \(x\in\mathcal X=[-1,1]^d\), \(h\in(0,1]\), and a coordinate \(j\). Put \(a_j=x_j+1\) and \(b_j=1-x_j\), so \(a_j,b_j\ge0\) and \(a_j+b_j=2\). From \(\mathrm{def:window-basis}\),
\[
 \ell_j=\min\{h/2,a_j\}+\min\{h/2,b_j\}.
\]
Both summands are at most \(h/2\), giving \(\ell_j\le h\). At least one of \(a_j,b_j\) is at least one, and hence at least \(h/2\), because \(h\le1\). Therefore \(\ell_j\ge h/2\). Multiplication over the \(d\) coordinates yields
\[
 2^{-d}h^d\le V_{x,h}=\prod_{j=1}^d\ell_j\le h^d.
\]

By \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\text{-}design}\), \(X\) has density \(2^{-d}\) on \(\mathcal X\). The affine map \(T_{x,h}\) sends \(W_{x,h}\) bijectively onto \([0,1]^d\) and has Jacobian \(V_{x,h}^{-1}\), so the change of variables \(z=T_{x,h}(u)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Omega_{x,h,k}
 &=2^{-d}\int_{W_{x,h}}V_{x,h}^{-1}
 B_k(T_{x,h}(u))B_k(T_{x,h}(u))^\top\,du\\
 &=2^{-d}\int_{[0,1]^d}B_k(z)B_k(z)^\top\,dz
 =2^{-d}I_k,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality is the orthonormality in \(\mathrm{def:spline\text{-}basis}\). The construction \(\mathrm{def:boundary\text{-}adapted\text{-}estimator}\) sets \(\widehat\Omega^{(-\ell)}_{x,h,k}=2^{-d}I_k\) on every training complement. Consequently
\[
 \widehat\Omega^{(-\ell)}_{x,h,k}=\Omega_{x,h,k}.
\]

For any \(v\in\mathbb R^{J_p}\), the same change of variables and the definition of \(Q_{x,h}\) imply
\[
 v^\top Q_{x,h}v
 =2^{-d}\int_{[0,1]^d}
 \{v^\top\rho_p(z)\}^2
 \pi(T_{x,h}^{-1}(z))\{1-\pi(T_{x,h}^{-1}(z))\}\,dz.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:strict\text{-}overlap}\) gives
\[
 c_\pi(1-c_\pi)
 \le \pi(u)\{1-\pi(u)\}\le\frac14
 \qquad(u\in\mathcal X).
\]
Because \(\rho_p\) is orthonormal on \([0,1]^d\),
\[
 \int_{[0,1]^d}\{v^\top\rho_p(z)\}^2\,dz=\|v\|_2^2.
\]
Thus, for every \(v\),
\[
 2^{-d}c_\pi(1-c_\pi)\|v\|_2^2
 \le v^\top Q_{x,h}v
 \le 2^{-d}\|v\|_2^2/4,
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
 2^{-d}c_\pi(1-c_\pi)I_{J_p}
 \preceq Q_{x,h}
 \preceq 2^{-d}I_{J_p}/4.
\]

Now let
\[
 S=\frac{\widetilde Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}+\widetilde Q^{(\ell)\top}_{x,h}}2,
 \qquad q_-=2^{-d-1}c_\pi(1-c_\pi),
 \qquad q_+=2^{-d-1}.
\]
By construction, \(\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}=T_{[q_-,q_+]}(S)\). Eigenvalue clipping therefore puts every eigenvalue of \(\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}\) in \([q_-,q_+]\), and, since \(q_->0\),
\[
 \bigl\|[\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le q_-^{-1}.
\]
The population bounds already proved give
\[
 2q_-I_{J_p}\preceq Q_{x,h}\preceq(q_+/2)I_{J_p};
\]
in particular, \(Q_{x,h}\) lies in the closed convex spectral box
\[
 \mathcal C=\{M=M^\top:q_-I_{J_p}\preceq M\preceq q_+I_{J_p}\}.
\]
Spectral clipping is the Frobenius-metric projection onto \(\mathcal C\): after diagonalizing \(S\), minimizing the squared Frobenius distance over \(\mathcal C\) clips each eigenvalue to \([q_-,q_+]\). For completeness, the metric-projection variational inequality gives, for every \(M\in\mathcal C\),
\[
 \langle S-\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h},M-\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}\rangle_F\le0.
\]
Taking \(M=Q_{x,h}\) and expanding \(\|S-Q_{x,h}\|_F^2\) therefore yields
\[
 \|\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}-Q_{x,h}\|_F
 \le \|S-Q_{x,h}\|_F.
\]
Finally, the resolvent identity gives
\[
 [\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}-Q_{x,h}^{-1}
 = [\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}
 (Q_{x,h}-\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h})Q_{x,h}^{-1}.
\]
Since \(\|Q_{x,h}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le(2q_-)^{-1}\le q_-^{-1}\) and \(\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|M\|_F\), it follows that
\[
 \bigl\|[\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}-Q_{x,h}^{-1}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le q_-^{-2}\|S-Q_{x,h}\|_F.
\]
All conclusions are deterministic once the displayed matrices are formed; the fold convention in \(\mathrm{def:boundary\text{-}adapted\text{-}estimator}\) handles any fold of size smaller than two. This proves the lemma.
