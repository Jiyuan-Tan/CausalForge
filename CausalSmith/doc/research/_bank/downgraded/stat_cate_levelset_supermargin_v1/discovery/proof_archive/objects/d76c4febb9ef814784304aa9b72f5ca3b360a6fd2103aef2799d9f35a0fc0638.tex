By \(\mathrm{def:transverse-witness}\), conditional on \(X=x\), the treatment variable is generated from \(U_A\), whereas \((Y^0,Y^1)\) is generated from \((U_0,U_1)\). The three uniforms are conditionally independent, so
\[
(Y^0,Y^1)\perp A\mid X.
\]
The same definition sets \(Y=Y^A\). Since \(A=\mathbf 1\{U_A\le 1/2\}\),
\[
\pi(x)=P(A=1\mid X=x)=\frac12.
\]
Similarly, the two Bernoulli constructions give
\[
\mu_0(x)=\mathbb E[Y\mid A=0,X=x]=\frac12,
\qquad
\mu_1(x)=\mathbb E[Y\mid A=1,X=x]=\frac12+cx_1.
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{def:cate}\) yields the continuous representative
\[
\tau_{P^{\mathrm{tr}}}(x)=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)=cx_1.
\]
Because \(0<c<1/4\) and \(x_1\in[-1,1]\), all Bernoulli probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\), and the observed outcome lies in \([0,1]\subseteq[-M_Y,M_Y]\) under \(M_Y=1\). The propensity \(1/2\) satisfies strict overlap with \(c_\pi=1/4\). The uniform distribution on \([-1,1]^d\) has density \(2^{-d}=c_f=C_f\).

The functions \(\pi\) and \(\mu_0\) are constant, and \(\tau_{P^{\mathrm{tr}}}(x)=cx_1\) is affine. Under the Taylor-remainder norm in \(\mathrm{def:holder-ball}\), their relevant Hölder norms are at most one because \(c<1/4\); all derivatives above the affine order vanish. Moreover,
\[
|\tau_{P^{\mathrm{tr}}}(x)-\theta|=|cx_1|\le c=\eta,
\]
so \(\mathcal D_{P^{\mathrm{tr}}}(\eta)=\mathcal X\). The near-band Hölder condition therefore holds for every \(\gamma>0\), and the off-band condition holds vacuously for every \(\gamma'>0\).

For \(0<t\le c\), uniformity of the first coordinate and \(\theta=0\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_X\{0<|\tau_{P^{\mathrm{tr}}}(X)-\theta|\le t\}
&=P\{0<|cX_1|\le t\}\\
&=P\{0<|X_1|\le t/c\}
=\frac{t}{c}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(c_m=C_m=c^{-1}\), this is exactly the required lower and upper margin relation with \(\xi=1\) on \((0,t_0]\), where \(t_0=c\). Finally, the interior points \((-1/2,0,\ldots,0)\) and \((1/2,0,\ldots,0)\) give CATE values \(-c/2\) and \(c/2\), so the threshold is crossed in the interior. Every member condition in \(\mathrm{def:smooth-model}\) is satisfied. Hence the observed marginal belongs to \(\mathcal M(1,\gamma;\kappa)=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\) for every \(\gamma>d\), as claimed.
