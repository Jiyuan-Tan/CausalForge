Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\). We verify the hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:bkk-level-set-upper-cited}\) uniformly over this law-level class.

First, \(\mathrm{lem:uniform-design}\) gives \(f_P=F_0=2^{-d}\) almost everywhere. Hence the density ratio is identically one and its estimation error is zero. Second, \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\) gives uniform positive lower and finite upper eigenvalue bounds for the population local matrices. It also gives the exact spline Gram matrix and the deterministic inverse bound
\[
 \|[\widehat Q^{(\ell)}_{x,h}]^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le q_-^{-1}.
\]
Since the inverse of \(Q_{x,h}\) is also uniformly bounded, the required inverse-difference bound follows from the triangle inequality. The spectral clipping in \(\mathrm{def:boundary-adapted-estimator}\) therefore verifies the matrix conditions for every sample, including boundary windows.

Third, the reference functions \(\widehat\pi^{(-\ell)}\equiv1/2\) and \(\widehat\mu_0^{(-\ell)}\equiv0\) are deterministic and independent of every evaluation fold. Subtracting these constants does not change the \(\alpha\)- and \(\beta\)-Hölder seminorms of \(\pi\) and \(\mu_0\). The required nuisance approximation product is thus bounded by \(Ck^{-2\bar s/d}\) by \(\mathrm{lem:sieve-approximation}\). The density-ratio remainder multiplying these errors is exactly zero. Boundedness of \(Y\), the references, the stabilized inverses, and the score envelopes follows from calibration, \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\), and \(\mathrm{lem:spline-score-envelope}\). Measurability and the output-clipping bound follow from \(\mathrm{lem:displayed-estimator-well-defined}\).

The affine map \(T_{x,h}\) sends every truncated boundary window to \([0,1]^d\), while \(h/2\le\ell_j\le h\). Thus the usual local-polynomial Taylor calculation has constants uniform in \(x\): on the near-threshold region its approximation term is \(Ch^\gamma\) by \(\mathrm{ass:near-band-holder}\); away from that region the corresponding positive-order polynomial scale follows from \(\mathrm{ass:off-band-holder}\). The latter scale tends to zero polynomially and is therefore more than sufficient for the off-band tail condition in the cited level-set upper result. All approximation and score constants are uniform because the Hölder radii and the support geometry are fixed by calibration.

It remains to evaluate the tuning. In the high-smoothness branch, \(h=h_n=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)}\) and \(k\asymp nh^d\), so the local-polynomial bias and stochastic scale are both bounded by
\[
 h^\gamma=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}=r_n^*.
\]
In the low-smoothness branch, \(h=h_n=n^{-1/(\gamma T)}\), \(r_n^*=h^\gamma\), and
\[
 k\asymp h^d(r_n^*)^{-d/(2\bar s)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 (h/k^{1/d})^{2\bar s}\asymp r_n^*,
 \qquad
 \left\{\frac1{nh^d}\left(1+\frac{k}{nh^d}\right)\right\}^{1/2}\lesssim r_n^*;
\]
the second identity is exactly \(n h^{\gamma+d/2+\gamma d/(4\bar s)}=1\), which follows from the definition of \(T\). Therefore the near-threshold exponential scale in \(\mathrm{lem:bkk-level-set-upper-cited}\) is \(r_n^*\) in both cases. Applying that result with the calibrated margin exponent \(\xi=1\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[d_H(\widehat G_n^{\mathrm{LP}},G_P;P)]
 \le C(r_n^*)^{1+1}.
\]
All constants used above are fixed over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\), so taking the supremum over \(P\) proves the lemma.
