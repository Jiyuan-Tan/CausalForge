Fix the calibrated \(\kappa\) and \(\gamma>d\). The exponent \(\beta\) is a fixed component of \(\kappa\), not a sample-dependent quantity. By \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated-direct-assouad-lower}\), there is a constant \(c_{\mathrm{lb}}>0\), independent of \(n\), such that for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)\ge c_{\mathrm{lb}}n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:calibrated-direct-regression-upper}\), there is a constant \(C_{\mathrm{ub}}<\infty\), also independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C_{\mathrm{ub}}n^{-2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}/\{2\min\{\beta,\gamma\}+d\}}.
\]
Taking a common sufficiently large lower threshold for \(n\) proves the displayed bracket with the nonshadowing constants \(c_{\mathrm{lb}}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{ub}}\).

Suppose now that \(\beta\ge\gamma\). Then \(\min\{\beta,\gamma\}=\gamma\), so the two powers of \(n\) coincide. Furthermore, by \(\mathrm{def:average-smoothness}\),
\[
\bar s=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\ge\frac\gamma2.
\]
Because \(\gamma>d\),
\[
\frac\gamma2>\frac d4>
\frac{d}{4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}}.
\]
Hence the high-smoothness branch in \(\mathrm{def:rate}\) applies and
\[
r_n^*=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Substitution into the two matching endpoints gives
\[
c_{\mathrm{lb}}(r_n^*)^2
\le\mathfrak R_n(1,\gamma;\kappa)
\le C_{\mathrm{ub}}(r_n^*)^2,
\]
which is the claimed exact order, only in the stated subregime.
