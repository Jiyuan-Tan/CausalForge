Write \(\bar s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\). By \(\mathrm{def:boundary-adapted-estimator}\),
\[
\widehat\pi^{(-\ell)}\equiv\frac12,
\qquad
\widehat\mu_0^{(-\ell)}\equiv0,
\qquad
\widehat\Omega^{(-\ell)}_{x,h,k}=2^{-d}I_k.
\]
These references are deterministic and hence independent of every evaluation fold. Subtracting a constant preserves derivatives and Taylor remainders, so \(\pi-1/2\) has the \(\alpha\)-Hölder bound in \(\mathrm{ass:propensity-holder}\). Likewise, \(\mu_0-0=\mu_0\) has the \(\beta\)-Hölder bound in \(\mathrm{ass:control-holder}\). Strict overlap and bounded outcomes give uniform envelopes. Fixed balanced folds have sizes comparable to \(n\), and the fallback clause makes the statistic measurable for every sample.

By \(\mathrm{lem:uniform-design}\), \(F_0=f_P\) almost everywhere, so the density ratio is one and its error is zero. By \(\mathrm{lem:local-gram}\), the population matrices have uniform spectral bounds, the spline Gram is inserted exactly, and the stabilized local Gram is always invertible with a deterministic inverse bound. The final scalar estimate is clipped to \([-2M_Y,2M_Y]\).

The order \(p=\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\) reproduces the Taylor polynomial allowed by \(\mathrm{ass:near-band-holder}\), leaving local deterministic bias \(O(h^\gamma)\). After rescaling a window of diameter proportional to \(h\), \(\mathrm{lem:sieve-approximation}\) gives nuisance approximation errors of orders \((h/k^{1/d})^\alpha\) and \((h/k^{1/d})^\beta\). Their product is
\[
O\!\left((h/k^{1/d})^{\alpha+\beta}\right)
=O\!\left((h/k^{1/d})^{2\bar s}\right).
\]
Direct variance expansion of the first-order empirical average gives scale \((nh^d)^{-1/2}\). For the ordered second-order average, orthonormality of the localized \(k\)-dimensional basis and the bounded exact inverse Gram give scale \(\sqrt{k}/(nh^d)\); its nondegenerate projection is already of first-order scale. These are precisely the stochastic scales required by the setup underlying \(\mathrm{lem:bkk-level-set-upper-cited}\).

It remains to check tuning. In the high-smoothness branch,
\[
h_n=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)},
\qquad
k_n\asymp nh_n^d.
\]
Consequently
\[
h_n^\gamma\asymp(nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
\asymp\frac{\sqrt{k_n}}{nh_n^d}
=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}=r_n^*.
\]
Moreover,
\[
(h_n/k_n^{1/d})^{2\bar s}\asymp n^{-2\bar s/d}\le r_n^*,
\]
where the inequality is equivalent to \(\bar s\ge d/[4\{1+d/(2\gamma)\}]\).

In the low-smoothness branch, let \(T=1+d/(4\bar s)+d/(2\gamma)\). Then \(r_n^*=n^{-1/T}\), \(h_n=(r_n^*)^{1/\gamma}\), and
\[
k_n\asymp h_n^d(r_n^*)^{-d/(2\bar s)}.
\]
It follows that
\[
h_n^\gamma=r_n^*,
\qquad
(h_n/k_n^{1/d})^{2\bar s}\asymp r_n^*,
\qquad
\frac{\sqrt{k_n}}{nh_n^d}\asymp r_n^*.
\]
The strict low-branch inequality is equivalent to \(T>2+d/\gamma\), which also gives \((nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\le r_n^*\). Ceilings in \(m_n\) change only fixed constants. Thus the density, matrix, approximation, independence, boundedness, and tuning requirements of the cited upper theorem hold uniformly in both branches.
