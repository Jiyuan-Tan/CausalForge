\textbf{Step 1: an exact-margin graph family.} Write \(z=(x_2,\ldots,x_d)\) and \(D=d-1\); when \(d=1\), omit \(z\). Choose a nonincreasing \(C^\infty\) function \(\chi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\) equal to one on \([0,1/4]\) and zero on \([3/4,\infty)\). Put \(\psi=-2\chi\chi'\), so \(\psi\ge0\) and \(\int_0^r\psi(s)\,ds=1-\chi(r)^2\). For a smooth graph \(b\) satisfying \(\|b\|_\infty\|\psi\|_\infty<h/2\), define
\[
x_\sigma(v,z)=\sigma v+b(z)\chi(v/h)^2,
\qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\},\quad 0\le v\le1,
\]
and define \(\tau_b\) implicitly by
\[
\tau_b(x_\sigma(v,z),z)=\sigma cv.
\]
The derivatives are
\[
\partial_vx_+=1-\frac{b(z)}h\psi(v/h)>\frac12,
\qquad
\partial_vx_-=-1-\frac{b(z)}h\psi(v/h)<-\frac12.
\]
Thus the two branches biject \([0,1]\) onto \([b(z),1]\) and \([b(z),-1]\). Near \(v=0\), \(\chi=1\), so \(\tau_b=c\{x_1-b(z)\}\); after \(v=3h/4\), \(\tau_b=cx_1\). Flatness of the cutoff at both transitions makes \(\tau_b\) smooth.

Under the uniform covariate law, conditional on \(z\), the density of \(V=|\tau_b(X)|/c\) is
\[
f_{V\mid z}(v)=\frac12\left(|\partial_vx_+|+|\partial_vx_-|\right)
=\frac12\left(1-\frac b h\psi+1+\frac b h\psi\right)=1.
\]
Consequently
\[
P_X\{0<|\tau_b(X)|\le t\}=\frac tc,\qquad 0<t\le c,
\]
exactly, and \(G_b=\{(x_1,z):x_1>b(z)\}\).

Choose \(\phi\in C_c^\infty((-1/2,1/2)^D)\), with \(0\le\phi\le1\) and \(\phi=1\) on \([-1/4,1/4]^D\). Pack disjoint tangential cubes of side \(h\) with centers \(z_j\in[-1/2,1/2]^D\); their number satisfies \(M\ge c_Dh^{-D}\). Put
\[
b_\omega(z)=a h^\gamma\sum_{j=1}^M(2\omega_j-1)\phi((z-z_j)/h)^2,
\qquad \omega\in\{0,1\}^M.
\]
For \(d=1\), take \(M=1\) and \(b_\omega=a h^\gamma(2\omega_1-1)\). Since \(\gamma>d\ge1\), \(\|b_\omega\|_\infty/h\le ah^{\gamma-1}\to0\). In \((v,z)\)-coordinates the perturbation from \(cx_1\) is \(-cb_\omega(z)\chi(v/h)^2\). Every derivative of order \(m\) is bounded by \(Ca h^{\gamma-m}\), and the top Hölder remainder is bounded by \(Ca\). Smooth inversion of the branch maps has the same scaling. Since the linear base has Hölder norm strictly below one when \(c<1/4\), a fixed sufficiently small \(a\) puts every \(\tau_{b_\omega}\) in the radius-one \(\gamma\)-Hölder ball. The exact margin identity makes the near band the whole cube; the graph crosses in its interior.

\textbf{Step 2: loss separation.} For adjacent vertices, suppress the common graph terms and write their local graphs as \(b_+=a\delta\phi_j^2\) and \(b_-=-a\delta\phi_j^2\), where \(\delta=h^\gamma\). On the tangential core \(Q_j\), where \(\phi_j=1\), and for \(|x_1|\le a\delta<h/4\),
\[
\tau_+(x)=c(x_1-a\delta),\qquad \tau_-(x)=c(x_1+a\delta).
\]
For every measurable \(H\), at each point between the two graphs, \(H\) disagrees with at least one truth. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_H(H,G_+;P_+)+d_H(H,G_-;P_-)
&\ge 2^{-d}c\int_{Q_j}\int_{-a\delta}^{a\delta}
\min\{x_1+a\delta,a\delta-x_1\}\,dx_1\,dz\\
&\ge c_0\delta^2h^{d-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\textbf{Step 3: the high-smoothness experiment.} Take \(A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), \(Y\mid(A=0,X)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and \(Y\mid(A=1,X)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+\tau_{b_\omega}(X))\). The conditional probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\). Thus \(\pi=1/2\), \(\mu_0=1/2\), and the CATE is \(\tau_{b_\omega}\); all laws belong to the calibrated transverse model. Adjacent targets differ by at most \(C\delta\) on a set of volume \(Ch^d\). The elementary Bernoulli inequality \(\operatorname{KL}(\operatorname{Bern}(p),\operatorname{Bern}(q))\le C(p-q)^2\) on \([1/4,3/4]\) yields
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^n,P_{\omega^{(j)}}^n)\le Cn\delta^2h^d.
\]
Set \(h=(\varepsilon/n)^{1/(2\gamma+d)}\). For sufficiently small fixed \(\varepsilon\), adjacent total variation is bounded away from one. Averaging the loss-separation inequality over the hypercube and pairing each vertex with its \(j\)-th neighbor gives the elementary Assouad testing bound
\[
\inf_{\widehat G_n}\max_\omega \mathbb E_\omega d_H(\widehat G_n,G_\omega;P_\omega)
\ge cM\delta^2h^{d-1}\ge c\delta^2.
\]
Here the last inequality uses \(M\ge c_Dh^{-(d-1)}\). Since \(\delta=h^\gamma\asymp n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}=r_n^*\), this is the required high-branch bound.

\textbf{Step 4: the fuzzy low-smoothness experiment.} Put \(s=\bar s\). The strict low-branch condition implies
\[
2s<\frac{d}{2}<\gamma.
\]
Let \(w=\delta^{1/(2s)}=h^{\gamma/(2s)}<h\). Orient an adjacent edge so that \(t_1=\tau_{-q}\ge t_0=\tau_q\), where \(q(z)=a\delta\phi_j(z)^2\), and put \(\Delta_j=t_1-t_0\). Implicit differentiation gives
\[
\partial_b\tau_b=-\frac{c\chi(v/h)^2}{1-(b/h)\psi(v/h)}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\partial_b\tau_b=-\frac{c\chi(v/h)^2}{1+(b/h)\psi(v/h)},
\]
according to the branch, so \(\Delta_j\ge0\). Hadamard's divided-difference identity gives the exact factorization
\[
\Delta_j(x,z)=\delta\phi_j(z)^2A_j(x,z),
\qquad
A_j=-2a\int_0^1\left.\partial_b\tau_b\right|_{b=(2r-1)q}\,dr.
\]
The function \(A_j\) is nonnegative, smooth, supported in the same \(h\)-cell, and satisfies \(|D^mA_j|\le C_mh^{-m}\).

Choose a smooth squared partition of unity \(\sum_{l=1}^K\chi_l^2=1\) on that support, with \(K\le Ch^d/w^d\), support diameter \(Cw\), uniformly bounded overlap, and \(|D^m\chi_l|\le C_mw^{-m}\). For independent Rademacher signs \(\lambda_l\), define
\[
u_\lambda=w^\alpha\sum_l\lambda_l\phi_j\chi_l,
\qquad
v_\lambda=\frac{w^\beta}{4}\sum_l\lambda_lA_j\phi_j\chi_l.
\]
Since \(w^{\alpha+\beta}=w^{2s}=\delta\),
\[
\mathbb E_\lambda u_\lambda=\mathbb E_\lambda v_\lambda=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\lambda(u_\lambda v_\lambda)=\frac{\Delta_j}{4}.
\]
Because \(w<h\), the product rule shows that \(u_\lambda\) has uniformly bounded \(\alpha\)-Hölder norm and \(v_\lambda\) has uniformly bounded \(\beta\)-Hölder norm. Multiplying the construction by the already free small constant \(a\) ensures the calibrated radii and positivity.

Suppose first that \(\alpha\ge\beta\). For \(a_0,y_0\in\{0,1\}\), define on the edge cell
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_\lambda(a_0,y_0\mid x)
&=\frac14+(y_0-\tfrac12)v_\lambda+(2a_0-1)(2y_0-1)\frac{t_1}{4},\\
q_\lambda(a_0,y_0\mid x)
&=\frac14+(a_0-\tfrac12)u_\lambda+(y_0-\tfrac12)(v_\lambda+t_0u_\lambda)
 +(2a_0-1)(2y_0-1)\left(\frac{t_0}{4}+u_\lambda v_\lambda\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Direct summation over \(y_0\) and division by the treatment marginal show that \(p_\lambda\) has \((\pi,\mu_0,\tau)=(1/2,1/2+v_\lambda-t_1/2,t_1)\), while \(q_\lambda\) has \((\pi,\mu_0,\tau)=(1/2+u_\lambda,1/2+v_\lambda-t_0/2,t_0)\). Their one-observation mixtures agree because \(\mathbb E(u_\lambda v_\lambda)=(t_1-t_0)/4\). If \(\beta\ge\alpha\), interchange the roles by taking
\[
p_\lambda(a_0,y_0\mid x)=\frac14+(a_0-\tfrac12)u_\lambda+(y_0-\tfrac12)t_1u_\lambda+(2a_0-1)(2y_0-1)\frac{t_1}{4}
\]
with the same \(q_\lambda\). The same calculation gives the advertised CATEs and equality of the one-observation mixtures. Since \(\beta\le2s<\gamma\), the centered terms involving \(t_i\) satisfy the control-regression smoothness condition. Small \(a\) keeps every four-cell probability bounded away from zero and gives strict overlap. Pasting independent copies over disjoint graph cells produces nuisance-indexed laws in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\) for every vertex.

We now bound the adjacent mixture distance. Relative to their common one-observation mixture, expand the two \(n\)-fold likelihoods and average over the Rademacher signs. Every monomial in which a sign occurs an odd number of times vanishes. Because the one-observation mixtures agree, the first surviving difference pairs two observations whose covariates fall in the same or two overlapping fine supports. There are \(O(K)\) such support pairs, each has joint probability \(O(w^{2d})\), and bounded overlap changes only the constant. For \(\alpha\ge\beta\), direct squaring of the displayed four-cell probabilities gives the second-order bound
\[
C n(n-1)K w^{2d}\{w^{2\alpha+2\beta}+w^{4\alpha}\};
\]
for \(\beta\ge\alpha\), the last term is \(w^{4\beta}\). Higher collision diagrams add successive factors bounded by \(Cn w^d\), so their geometric sum is bounded whenever \(nw^d\) is sufficiently small. Since \(\max\{\alpha,\beta\}\ge s\) and \(w<1\), in either case
\[
H^2(\overline P_\omega^n,\overline P_{\omega^{(j)}}^n)
\le Cn^2h^dw^dw^{4s}
=Cn^2\delta^2h^dw^d
=Cn^2h^{2\gamma+d+d\gamma/(2s)}.
\]
This collision calculation is purely the preceding likelihood expansion; it does not assume a divergence property of the construction.

Let \(T=1+d/(4s)+d/(2\gamma)\) and choose \(h=(\varepsilon/n)^{1/(\gamma T)}\). The identity
\[
2\gamma+d+\frac{d\gamma}{2s}=2\gamma T
\]
makes the last Hellinger bound at most \(C\varepsilon^2\). Also the low-branch inequality gives \(d/(2s)>T\), so
\[
nw^d=n h^{d\gamma/(2s)}\longrightarrow0,
\]
validating the geometric bound. Pairing adjacent fuzzy mixtures in the same loss-separation argument, and using \(1-\operatorname{TV}\ge c>0\), yields
\[
\inf_{\widehat G_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)}
\mathbb E_P d_H(\widehat G_n,G_P;P)
\ge cM\delta^2h^{d-1}\ge c\delta^2.
\]
Finally, \(\delta=h^\gamma\asymp n^{-1/T}=r_n^*\). For \(d=1\), \(M=1\) and both arguments are the corresponding direct or fuzzy two-hypothesis bounds. This proves the lower rate in both branches.
