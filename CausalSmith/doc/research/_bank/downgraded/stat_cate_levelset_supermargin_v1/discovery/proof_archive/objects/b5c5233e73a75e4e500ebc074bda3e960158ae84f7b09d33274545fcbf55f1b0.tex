Fix a sufficiently small \(h\), put \(\delta=h^\gamma\), and take the hypercube \(\{\tau_\omega:\omega\in\{0,1\}^M\}\) from \(\mathrm{lem:exact-margin-graph-packing}\). Its \(M\) tangential patches are disjoint, \(M\ge c_0h^{-(d-1)}\) when \(d>1\), and \(M=1\) when \(d=1\). For each \(\omega\), define an observed law as follows. Let \(X\) be uniform on \([-1,1]^d\), let \(A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and set
\[
 Y\mid(A=0,X)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),
 \qquad
 Y\mid(A=1,X=x)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+\tau_\omega(x)).
\]
The exact margin identity at \(t=c\), continuity, and the positive uniform density imply \(|\tau_\omega|\le c<1/4\) everywhere. Thus all displayed conditional probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\). The constructed law has \(\pi=1/2\), \(\mu_0=1/2\), and CATE \(\tau_\omega\). Constants belong to every required nuisance Hölder ball. The graph-packing lemma supplies global \(\gamma\)-Hölder smoothness, exact lower and upper margins, and interior crossing. Its range is contained in the calibrated band, so the off-band condition is vacuous. Therefore every constructed law belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma;\kappa)\).

Let \(\omega^{(j)}\) differ from \(\omega\) only in coordinate \(j\). For \(p,q\in[1/4,3/4]\), two differentiations of binary relative entropy give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\{\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p),\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\}
 \le C(p-q)^2.
\]
The adjacent amplitude and support assertions in \(\mathrm{lem:exact-margin-graph-packing}\) consequently yield
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\omega^{\otimes n},P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{\otimes n})
 \le Cn\delta^2h^d.
\]
Choose \(h=(\varepsilon/n)^{1/(2\gamma+d)}\), with a fixed sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\). Then the last display is bounded by a sufficiently small constant. To control total variation without invoking an external testing result, use the scalar inequality
\[
 u\log u-u+1\ge\frac{(u-1)^2}{2(u+1)},
 \qquad u>0.
\]
After integration with \(u=dP/dQ\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, it gives \(\operatorname{TV}^2(P,Q)\le\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)\). Hence every adjacent product-law total variation is at most some fixed \(v_0<1\).

For a measurable set \(H\), let \(L_{\omega,j}(H)\) be the weighted loss restricted to the \(j\)-th patch. The patches are disjoint, so
\[
 d_H(H,G_{P_\omega};P_\omega)\ge\sum_{j=1}^M L_{\omega,j}(H).
\]
The local separation in the graph-packing lemma states that, pointwise in \(H\),
\[
 L_{\omega,j}(H)+L_{\omega^{(j)},j}(H)
 \ge s_j,
 \qquad
 s_j\ge c_1\delta^2h^{d-1}.
\]
For any sample-dependent \(H\), integration against the minimum of the two adjacent sample densities therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\omega L_{\omega,j}(H)
 +\mathbb E_{\omega^{(j)}}L_{\omega^{(j)},j}(H)
 \ge s_j\{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_\omega^{\otimes n},P_{\omega^{(j)}}^{\otimes n})\}
 \ge s_j(1-v_0).
\]
Average this inequality over all paired vertices for each \(j\), sum over the disjoint patches, and use that the maximum risk is no smaller than the uniform average risk. This gives
\[
 \inf_{\widehat G_n}\max_\omega
 \mathbb E_\omega d_H(\widehat G_n,G_{P_\omega};P_\omega)
 \ge \frac{1-v_0}{2}\sum_{j=1}^M s_j
 \ge cM\delta^2h^{d-1}
 \ge c\delta^2.
\]
Because the finite family is contained in the calibrated transverse class, the left-hand side is bounded above by the minimax risk over that class. Finally,
\[
 \delta^2=h^{2\gamma}
 =\varepsilon^{2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Absorbing the fixed power of \(\varepsilon\) into the constant proves the lemma.
