Let \(a_n=d\log(en)+\log(2n^2)\). The fast alternative in \(\text{thm:erm-plugin-upper}\) supplies the measurable learner \(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{ERM}}\), whose worst-case expected regularized regret over the fixed nonempty shell is at most
\[
600\kappa_\eta D\frac{a_n}{n}+b_\eta n^{-2}.
\]
Indeed, the complement term in that theorem is
\[
\frac{\delta_n}{2}\min\{2,\eta/2\}=b_\eta n^{-2}
\]
because \(\delta_n=n^{-2}\). By the infimum over learners in \(\text{def:minimax-risk}\), the same expression is an upper bound on
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,\mathfrak E}\bigl(\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)\bigr).
\]

For every integer \(n\ge1\),
\[
a_n=d+(d+2)\log n+\log2
\le(d+2)\log(2en).
\]
Write \(Q=Q_{d,D,\eta}\), fix \(0<\epsilon<1\), and put
\[
L=\log\!\left(\frac{8eQ}{\epsilon}\right),
\qquad
n_\epsilon=\left\lceil\frac{8QL}{\epsilon}\right\rceil.
\]
Because \(Q\ge1\), \(0<\epsilon<1\), and \(L\ge\log(8e)>1\),
\[
\frac{8QL}{\epsilon}\le n_\epsilon\le\frac{9QL}{\epsilon}.
\]
Moreover, \(L\ge\log(8e)\) implies \(\log(9L/4)\le L\). To verify this, the function \(x\mapsto x-\log(9x/4)\) is increasing for \(x>1\) and is positive at \(x=\log(8e)\). Therefore
\[
\log(2en_\epsilon)
\le\log\!\left(\frac{18eQL}{\epsilon}\right)
=L+\log(9L/4)
\le2L.
\]
Using \(600\kappa_\eta D(d+2)\le Q\), \(b_\eta\le Q\), and the lower bound on \(n_\epsilon\), we obtain
\[
600\kappa_\eta D\frac{a_{n_\epsilon}}{n_\epsilon}
\le
600\kappa_\eta D(d+2)\frac{2L}{8QL/\epsilon}
\le\frac\epsilon4,
\]
and
\[
b_\eta n_\epsilon^{-2}
\le\frac{b_\eta\epsilon^2}{64Q^2L^2}
\le\frac\epsilon{64},
\]
where the last inequality uses \(b_\eta\le Q\), \(Q\ge1\), \(L>1\), and \(\epsilon<1\). Consequently,
\[
\mathfrak R_{n_\epsilon,\mathfrak E}
\bigl(\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)\bigr)
\le\frac{17}{64}\epsilon<\epsilon.
\]
The definition in \(\text{def:sample-complexity}\) now gives
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le n_\epsilon
=
\left\lceil\frac{8Q_{d,D,\eta}}{\epsilon}
\log\!\left(\frac{8eQ_{d,D,\eta}}{\epsilon}\right)\right\rceil,
\]
which is the asserted explicit bound.

Finally, the upper estimate \(n_\epsilon\le9QL/\epsilon\) yields
\[
0\le
\frac{\epsilon^2}{d}N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le\frac{9Q}{d}\epsilon
\log\!\left(\frac{8eQ}{\epsilon}\right)
\longrightarrow0
\qquad(\epsilon\downarrow0),
\]
because \(d\), \(D\), and \(\eta\), and hence \(Q\), are fixed. If constants \(c(C,D,\eta)>0\) and \(\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)>0\) gave the proposed lower bound for every \(0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)\), then the left-hand side of the last display would be at least \(c(C,D,\eta)\) throughout that interval, a contradiction. The argument applies to every finite public experiment with a nonempty shell, and therefore in particular to every genuinely shared-feature off-diagonal experiment.
