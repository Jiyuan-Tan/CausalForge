Fix a context \(x\), and restrict attention to the common support on which \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)>0\). Write \(p=\pi_r(\cdot\mid x)\) and \(q=\pi_g(\cdot\mid x)\). The Gibbs formulas give
\[
\log\frac{q(a)}{p(a)}
=\eta\Delta(x,a)-\log\frac{Z_g(x)}{Z_r(x)}.
\]
Multiplication by \(q-p\), summation over actions, and \(\sum_a(q(a)-p(a))=0\) yield
\[
\operatorname{KL}(q\Vert p)+\operatorname{KL}(p\Vert q)
=\eta\sum_a\{q(a)-p(a)\}\Delta(x,a).
\]
After averaging over \(x\), put
\[
m=\mathbb E_\rho\sum_a\{\pi_g(a\mid X)-\pi_r(a\mid X)\}\Delta(X,a).
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E_\rho\{\operatorname{KL}(\pi_g\Vert\pi_r)+\operatorname{KL}(\pi_r\Vert\pi_g)\}=\eta m,
\]
so \(m\ge0\). Direct substitution of the Gibbs variational identity also gives
\[
J_{\eta,r}(\pi_r)-J_{\eta,r}(\pi_g)
=\eta^{-1}\mathbb E_\rho\operatorname{KL}(\pi_g\Vert\pi_r)
\le m.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz separately under the two endpoint policies gives
\[
m\le|\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_g}\Delta|+|\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_r}\Delta|
\le\sqrt{V_g}+\sqrt{V_r}.
\]

For the quadratic bound, define
\[
T=\mathbb E_\rho\sum_a\frac{\{\pi_g(a\mid X)-\pi_r(a\mid X)\}^2}{\pi_g(a\mid X)+\pi_r(a\mid X)}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz with denominator \(\pi_g+\pi_r\) yields
\[
m^2\le(V_g+V_r)T.
\]
For every \(s>0\),
\[
(s-1)\log s\ge\frac{2(s-1)^2}{s+1}.
\]
Indeed, \(\ell(s)=\log s-2(s-1)/(s+1)\) has derivative \(\ell'(s)=(s-1)^2/[s(s+1)^2]\ge0\) and \(\ell(1)=0\); multiplying by \(s-1\) gives the inequality on both sides of one. Applying it with \(s=\pi_g/\pi_r\) shows
\[
2T\le\mathbb E_\rho\{\operatorname{KL}(\pi_g\Vert\pi_r)+\operatorname{KL}(\pi_r\Vert\pi_g)\}=\eta m.
\]
If \(m>0\), the last three displays imply
\[
m^2\le\frac\eta2(V_g+V_r)m,
\qquad
m\le\frac\eta2(V_g+V_r).
\]
The same conclusion is immediate when \(m=0\). This proves the two error-dependent bounds.

For the remaining deterministic bounds, the two Gibbs normalizers for potentials in \([0,1]\) lie in \([1,e^\eta]\), so \(\log(q/p)\le2\eta\) and hence \(\eta^{-1}\operatorname{KL}(q\Vert p)\le2\). Finally, along the Gibbs path generated by \(r+t\Delta\), differentiation of the log normalizer twice gives
\[
J_{\eta,r}(\pi_r)-J_{\eta,r}(\pi_g)
=\eta\int_0^1t\,\mathbb E_\rho\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{r+t\Delta}(\cdot\mid X)}(\Delta(X,\cdot))\,dt.
\]
Since \(\Delta\in[-1,1]\), its variance is at most one, and the integral is at most \(\eta/2\). Taking the minimum of the four valid bounds proves the lemma.
