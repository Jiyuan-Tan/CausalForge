View \(\mathcal F\) as a subset of the linear space of prediction vectors in \(\mathbb R^S\), equipped with the supremum norm. This space has dimension at most \(d\), and \(\mathcal F\) is contained in its unit ball because every prediction lies in \([0,1]\). A maximal \(u\)-separated subset of that unit ball has cardinality at most \((1+2/u)^d\): the disjoint balls of radius \(u/2\) centered at its points lie in the ball of radius \(1+u/2\), and comparison of Lebesgue volumes on the prediction subspace gives the claim. Hence \(\mathcal F\) has a supremum-norm \(u\)-net of size at most \((1+2/u)^d\).

For fixed \(x\), write \(H_{f,g}(x)=\sum_a\pi_f(a\mid x)(f(x,a)-g(x,a))^2\). Suppose \(\|f-f'\|_\infty\le u\) and \(\|g-g'\|_\infty\le u\). The two squared contrasts differ pointwise by at most \(4u\). Along the path \(f_t=f'+t(f-f')\), differentiation of the finite Gibbs sum gives, for every \(q:\mathcal A\to[0,1]\),
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\sum_a\pi_{f_t}(a\mid x)q(a)
=\eta\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi_{f_t}(\cdot\mid x)}(q,f-f').
\]
The absolute value of the covariance is at most \(2u\). Applying this identity to the fixed squared contrast generated by \((f',g')\) shows
\[
\|H_{f,g}-H_{f',g'}\|_\infty\le(4+2\eta)u.
\]
Choose \(u=\{n(4+2\eta)\}^{-1}\). The class \(\{H_{f,g}:f,g\in\mathcal F\}\) therefore has a \(1/n\)-net of cardinality at most
\[
M=\{1+2n(4+2\eta)\}^{2d}=\{1+4n(2+\eta)\}^{2d}.
\]

We next record the required scalar concentration calculation. If \(0\le H\le1\), exponential Markov's inequality together with
\[
e^u\le1+u+\frac{u^2}{2(1-u/3)}\qquad(0\le u<3)
\]
gives, for every \(t>0\), with probability at least \(1-2e^{-t}\),
\[
|\mathbb P_nH-\mathbb EH|
\le\sqrt{\frac{2(\mathbb EH)t}{n}}+\frac{t}{3n}.
\]
Since
\[
\sqrt{\frac{2(\mathbb EH)t}{n}}
\le\frac12\mathbb EH+\frac tn,
\]
this implies both
\[
\mathbb EH\le2\mathbb P_nH+\frac{8t}{3n},
\qquad
\mathbb P_nH\le2\mathbb EH+\frac{4t}{3n}.
\]
Apply these inequalities to every member of the finite net with \(t=\log(4M/\zeta)\), and take a union bound. The resulting event has probability at least \(1-\zeta\). For arbitrary \(H_{f,g}\), choose a net member within \(1/n\) in supremum norm. Replacing its empirical and population means by those of \(H_{f,g}\) adds at most \(3/n\) to either displayed comparison. Because
\[
\log M=2d\log\{1+4n(2+\eta)\},
\]
the larger quantity \(\alpha_n(\zeta)\) absorbs all these terms. Finally, \(\mathbb EH_{f,g}=V(f,g)\) and \(\mathbb P_nH_{f,g}=\widehat V_n(f,g)\), which proves both simultaneous inequalities.
