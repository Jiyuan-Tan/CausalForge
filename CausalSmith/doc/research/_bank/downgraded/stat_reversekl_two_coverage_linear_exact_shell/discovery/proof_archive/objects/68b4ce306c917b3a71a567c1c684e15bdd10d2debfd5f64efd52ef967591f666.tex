We first establish existence and measurability of the learner. Regard \(\mathcal F\) as a compact subset of the finite-dimensional Euclidean space \(\mathbb R^S\), and equip the sample space
\[
\mathscr Z_n=(S\times[0,1])^n
\]
with its standard Borel sigma-field. The empirical loss \(L_n(z,f)\) is Borel in \(z\) and continuous in \(f\). Its minimum over the fixed compact set \(\mathcal F\) is therefore Borel. One direct verification is to take a fixed countable dense subset of \(\mathcal F\) and express the minimum as the infimum over that subset. The minimizer correspondence is nonempty and compact-valued and has Borel graph.

For completeness, the measurable-maximum step used below can be verified in the same finite-dimensional manner. If a nonempty compact-valued correspondence is described by finitely many Borel-in-\(z\), continuous-in-\(f\) inequalities, then the minimum or maximum of a Borel-in-\(z\), continuous-in-\(f\) criterion over its values is Borel. Indeed, fix a countable dense subset of the ambient compact set, relax every defining inequality by \(1/m\), optimize over the dense points satisfying the relaxed inequalities, and let \(m\to\infty\). Compactness and continuity identify the limit with the desired optimum. The same representation shows that the optimizer graph is Borel. Applying this measurable-maximum argument recursively to the ordered coordinates of \(S\) shows that the successive coordinate minima over the empirical-risk minimizer correspondence are Borel. After all coordinates have been minimized, the correspondence is a singleton, so the resulting sequential lexicographic selector \(\widehat f(z)\) is Borel measurable.

The map
\[
(z,f)\longmapsto
\|f-\widehat f(z)\|_{n,z}^2
\]
is Borel in \(z\) and continuous in \(f\). Hence \(\mathcal Q_n(z)\) is a Borel compact-valued correspondence, and it is nonempty because it contains \(\widehat f(z)\). Moreover,
\[
(z,g,f)\longmapsto
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nH_{g,f}(X_i)
\]
is Borel in \(z\) and continuous in \((g,f)\), because the supports are finite and every Gibbs normalizer is strictly positive. The measurable-maximum argument gives a Borel function
\[
(z,g)\longmapsto
\widehat U_n(z,g)
=\max_{f\in\mathcal Q_n(z)}
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nH_{g,f}(X_i).
\]
For fixed \(z\), this function is continuous in \(g\), by uniform continuity of the displayed criterion on the compact set \(\mathcal F\times\mathcal F\). Applying the same argument to minimization over \(g\in\mathcal Q_n(z)\) gives a nonempty compact-valued Borel minimizing correspondence. Recursive measurable coordinate minimization in the fixed order of \(S\) produces the Borel selector \(\widetilde g_n\). The Gibbs formula is continuous in its finite vector of reward inputs and has a positive denominator. Thus \(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}}\) is measurable in \((\mathcal D_n,\mathfrak E)\), belongs to \(\mathcal L_n(\mathfrak E)\), and is defined at observed and unobserved contexts. Its construction uses the realized sample and the public objects \((\mathcal X,\mathcal A,\phi,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}},\eta)\), but not the unknown context law \(\rho\) or either shell index \(C,D\).

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)\), write \(r=r_\theta\), and set
\[
R_n=600\frac{a_n}{n}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:bounded-linear-localization}\), there is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\) with probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\) on which
\[
r\in\mathcal Q_n,
\qquad
\sup_{f\in\mathcal Q_n}
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(f-r)^2
\le R_n.
\]
Apply \(\text{lem:uniform-gibbs-radius-comparison}\) with \(\zeta=\delta_n/2\). Since \(\delta_n=n^{-2}\),
\[
\log\frac2\zeta=\log(4n^2).
\]
Thus there is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{cmp}}\), of probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\), on which, simultaneously for every \(g,f\in\mathcal F\),
\[
P_\rho H_{g,f}\le2P_nH_{g,f}+b_n,
\qquad
P_nH_{g,f}\le2P_\rho H_{g,f}+b_n.
\]
These inequalities hold over the deterministic class indexed by all of \(\mathcal F^2\), so they may be applied to the sample-dependent set \(\mathcal Q_n\) and selector \(\widetilde g_n\).

Work on
\[
\mathcal E_n=\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\cap\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{cmp}},
\]
whose probability is at least \(1-\delta_n\). For every \(f\in\mathcal Q_n\), the contrast \(f-r\) lies in \(\operatorname{span}(\phi)\). By \(\text{lem:feature-coverage-domination}\) and the shell equality \(D_P=D\),
\[
P_\rho H_{r,f}
=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(r-f)^2
\le D\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(r-f)^2
\le DR_n.
\]
Because \(r\in\mathcal Q_n\), the population-to-empirical comparison gives
\[
\widehat U_n(r)
\le2\sup_{f\in\mathcal Q_n}P_\rho H_{r,f}+b_n
\le2DR_n+b_n.
\]
The defining minimality of \(\widetilde g_n\) yields
\[
\widehat U_n(\widetilde g_n)\le\widehat U_n(r).
\]
Since \(r\in\mathcal Q_n\), the reverse comparison now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\widetilde g_n}}
(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
&=P_\rho H_{\widetilde g_n,r}\\
&\le2P_nH_{\widetilde g_n,r}+b_n\\
&\le2\widehat U_n(\widetilde g_n)+b_n\\
&\le4DR_n+3b_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
At the true Gibbs endpoint, feature-coverage domination and localization give
\[
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}
(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
\le
D\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}
(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
\le DR_n.
\]
Define
\[
A_n=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}
(\widetilde g_n-r)^2,
\qquad
B_n=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\widetilde g_n}}
(\widetilde g_n-r)^2.
\]
Every nonempty shell has \(D\ge1\) by \(\text{thm:feasible-index-region}\). Therefore
\[
A_n+B_n
\le5DR_n+3b_n
\le D(5R_n+3b_n)
=V_n.
\]
Apply \(\text{lem:two-endpoint-gibbs-conversion}\) with \(g=\widetilde g_n\). On \(\mathcal E_n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)
-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}})
&\le
\min\{\eta(A_n+B_n),\sqrt{A_n}+\sqrt{B_n}\}\\
&\le
\min\{\eta V_n,\sqrt{2V_n}\},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the final slow inequality uses
\[
\sqrt{A_n}+\sqrt{B_n}
\le\sqrt{2(A_n+B_n)}.
\]

We next bound the regret on the complement without any probabilistic condition. For arbitrary \(r,g:S\to[0,1]\), put \(\Delta=g-r\), and let \(\pi_t\) be the Gibbs policy generated by \(r+t\Delta\). Define
\[
\Phi(t)=
\eta^{-1}\mathbb E_\rho
\log\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid X)
e^{\eta(r+t\Delta)(X,a)}.
\]
Finite support permits direct differentiation, giving
\[
\Phi''(t)
=\eta\mathbb E_\rho
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta).
\]
The Gibbs variational identity and integration by parts yield
\[
J_{\eta,r}(\pi_r)-J_{\eta,r}(\pi_g)
=\eta\int_0^1t\,
\mathbb E_\rho\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta)\,dt
\le\frac\eta2,
\]
because \(|\Delta|\le1\). The slow part of \(\text{lem:two-endpoint-gibbs-conversion}\), again using \(|\Delta|\le1\), also bounds the regret by \(2\). Since \(P^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_n^c)\le\delta_n\), averaging the good- and bad-event bounds proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[
J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)
-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}})
\right]
\le
\min\{\eta V_n,\sqrt{2V_n}\}
+\delta_n\min\{2,\eta/2\}.
\]

It remains to prove the explicit inversion. For \(d\ge4\) and \(n\ge1\),
\[
a_n\le2d(1+\log n),
\qquad
\log(4n^2)\le d(1+\log n).
\]
Using also \(9\le(9/4)d(1+\log n)\) in the definition of \(3b_n\), one obtains the universal deterministic bound
\[
V_n
\le6100\frac{dD}{n}
\left[
1+\log n+\log\{1+4n(\eta+2)\}
\right].
\]
If \(\eta\le2\epsilon\), the deterministic regret bound \(\eta/2\le\epsilon\) applies already at \(n=1\). Hence the minimax definitions give
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)=1.
\]

Suppose now that \(\eta>2\epsilon\), and define \(q_\epsilon,L_\epsilon,n_\epsilon\) as in the statement. Since \(dD\ge4\), \(\epsilon<1\), and \(\eta/\epsilon>2\), one has \(q_\epsilon>4\), \(L_\epsilon>1\), and \(n_\epsilon\ge2\). Put \(K_0=10^7\). Integer rounding gives
\[
K_0q_\epsilon L_\epsilon
\le n_\epsilon
\le2K_0q_\epsilon L_\epsilon.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
1+\log n_\epsilon
&+\log\{1+4n_\epsilon(\eta+2)\}\\
&\le
1+\log(18K_0^2)
+2\log q_\epsilon
+2\log L_\epsilon
+\log(\eta+2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(q_\epsilon>1\),
\[
\log(\eta+2)
\le\log2+\log\{1+(1+\eta)q_\epsilon\}.
\]
Moreover, \(\log q_\epsilon\le L_\epsilon\), \(\log L_\epsilon\le L_\epsilon\), and \(L_\epsilon\ge1\). Thus
\[
1+\log n_\epsilon
+\log\{1+4n_\epsilon(\eta+2)\}
\le c_{K_0}L_\epsilon,
\qquad
c_{K_0}=6+\log(36K_0^2).
\]
The numerical choice is sufficient because
\[
K_0\ge8\cdot6100\,c_{K_0}.
\]
Indeed, \(36\cdot10^{14}<10^{16}\) and \(\log10<3\), so \(c_{K_0}<54\), while
\[
8\cdot6100\cdot54=2635200<10^7.
\]

There are two cases. If
\[
q_\epsilon=dD\eta/\epsilon,
\]
then the last three displays imply
\[
\eta V_{n_\epsilon}
\le
\frac{6100c_{K_0}}{K_0}\epsilon
\le\frac\epsilon8.
\]
Also \(n_\epsilon\ge q_\epsilon\ge\eta/\epsilon\), and therefore
\[
\delta_{n_\epsilon}\min\{2,\eta/2\}
\le\frac{\eta}{2n_\epsilon^2}
\le\frac\epsilon2.
\]
If instead
\[
q_\epsilon=dD\epsilon^{-2},
\]
then
\[
V_{n_\epsilon}
\le
\frac{6100c_{K_0}}{K_0}\epsilon^2
\le\frac{\epsilon^2}{8},
\]
so
\[
\sqrt{2V_{n_\epsilon}}\le\frac\epsilon2.
\]
Furthermore \(n_\epsilon\ge4\epsilon^{-2}\), and hence
\[
\delta_{n_\epsilon}\min\{2,\eta/2\}
\le\frac2{n_\epsilon^2}
\le\frac{\epsilon^4}{8}
\le\frac\epsilon2.
\]
At equality of the two branches either calculation applies. Thus the risk of the same data-only learner at \(n_\epsilon\) is at most \(\epsilon\) in every case. By \(\text{def:minimax-risk}\) and \(\text{def:sample-complexity}\),
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le n_\epsilon.
\]
The stated \(\widetilde O\) bound follows. Its suppressed factors are the explicitly displayed logarithms \(\log(e q_\epsilon)\) and \(\log\{1+(1+\eta)q_\epsilon\}\), plus integer rounding; all multiplicative constants are universal. The exact \(N^\star_{\mathfrak E}=1\) conclusion covers the regime below the displayed-order-at-least-two proviso.
