Put \(t=\eta\gamma\) and let \(H\) be the sum of the raw context masses in the construction. Since \(\tau(1+A)=1/4\), \(H=5/4\) for odd \(d\) and \(H=3/2\) for even \(d\). In particular, every hard context has probability \(\rho(x_j)=1/(Hk)\).

First consider admissibility. At \(\gamma=0\),
\[
T_0=\frac{D(1-2p)}{1-2pD}=2D-1.
\]
The hard-region inequalities give \(1<T_0<e^\eta\). Therefore \(\beta_0=\eta^{-1}\log(2D-1)\) lies strictly between zero and one. The denominator defining \(T_\gamma\), the map \(T_\gamma\), and \(\beta_\gamma=\eta^{-1}\log T_\gamma\) are continuous near zero. Hence there is \(\gamma_1>0\), depending only on \(D,\eta\), such that, whenever \(0\le\gamma\le\gamma_1\), the denominator is positive, \(0<\gamma\le\beta_\gamma\le1-\gamma\), and \(t\le1\). This also proves \(g_0(D,\eta)>0\), and consequently \(\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)>0\). By continuity and strict monotonicity of \(\epsilon\mapsto\eta^{-1}\operatorname{arcosh}(e^{64\eta\epsilon})\), choose \(\epsilon_1\in(0,\epsilon_0]\) so that \(\gamma(\epsilon,d,C,D,\eta)\le\min\{\gamma_1,1/4\}\) for \(0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_1\).

We next verify the shell equalities. At \(x_C\), the Gibbs normalizer is
\[
q e^\eta+1-q=e^\eta/C,
\]
so the likelihood ratio of action \(+\) is exactly \(C\), while the ratios of the other actions equal \(C/e^\eta<1\). At each hard context,
\[
Z=1-2p+2pT_\gamma\cosh t.
\]
The defining equation for \(T_\gamma\) is equivalent to
\[
T_\gamma e^t=DZ.
\]
Thus the favored and unfavored informative actions have likelihood ratios \(D\) and \(De^{-2t}\), respectively. Moreover \(T_\gamma e^t\ge1\) because \(\beta_\gamma\ge\gamma\), so the zero-action ratio \(1/Z\) is at most \(D\). At \(x_A\), and at \(x_z\) when present, every action has the same reward and the likelihood ratio is one. Since \(D\le C\), the global essential supremum is exactly \(C\).

All covariance blocks are diagonal except for the two-dimensional hard blocks. In the \(e_0\) direction, the target-to-logging variance ratio is
\[
\frac{qC+A}{q+A}=D,
\qquad
A=\frac{q(C-D)}{D-1}.
\]
In the basis \((u_j,w_j)\), the logging block is a positive multiple of \(pI_2\). After whitening, the target block has eigenvalues \(D\) and \(De^{-2t}\). The optional \(z\) block has ratio one. Every basis direction receives positive logging mass, so \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}\succ0\), and the largest generalized eigenvalue is exactly \(D\). The reward means are in \([0,1]\) by the choice of \(\gamma_1\), and the features have norm at most one. Taking the conditional reward to be Bernoulli with the displayed mean verifies every remaining condition of the exact shell. Thus \(\mathcal H_{d,C,D,\eta,\epsilon}\subset\mathcal M(d,C,D,\eta)\).

It remains to prove the local lower bound. At a hard context, the two Gibbs action laws corresponding to the two signs are
\[
s_+=(a,b,c),\qquad s_-=(b,a,c),\qquad a=pD=\frac14,\quad b=ae^{-2t},\quad c=1-a-b.
\]
For any action distribution \(u\), the Gibbs variational identity gives the sum of the two regularized regrets as
\[
\eta^{-1}\{\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_+)+\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_-)\}.
\]
Minimizing over \(u\) yields minus twice the logarithm of the Bhattacharyya affinity, and hence
\[
\inf_u\eta^{-1}\{\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_+)+\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_-)\}
=-\frac2\eta\log\{1-a(1-e^{-t})^2\}
\ge \frac{t^2}{8\eta},
\]
where the last inequality uses \(t\le1\), \(1-e^{-t}\ge t/2\), and \(-\log(1-z)\ge z\).

Neighboring sign vectors differ only at one hard context. Shrinking \(\gamma_1\) if necessary, continuity of \(\beta_\gamma\) ensures that all four Bernoulli means \(\beta_\gamma\pm\gamma\) lie in a fixed compact subinterval of \((0,1)\). The elementary Bernoulli inequality
\[
\operatorname{kl}(u\Vert v)\le\frac{(u-v)^2}{v(1-v)}
\]
therefore shows that the Kullback--Leibler divergence between the \(n\)-sample laws of two neighbors is at most
\[
K(D,\eta)\,n\rho(x_j)p\gamma^2
=K(D,\eta)\frac{np\gamma^2}{Hk}.
\]
If \(n\le c_1(D,\eta)k/(p\gamma^2)\), Pinsker's inequality, which follows from the scalar inequality \((1+z)\log(1+z)+(1-z)\log(1-z)\ge z^2\), makes the total variation distance between every pair of neighbors at most \(1/2\).

For nonnegative losses \(L_+,L_-\) satisfying \(L_+(u)+L_-(u)\ge s\) pointwise, integration against the minimum of the two neighboring data densities gives
\[
\mathbb E_+L_++\mathbb E_-L_-\ge s\{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_+^{\otimes n},P_-^{\otimes n})\}.
\]
Pair the vertices of the sign cube along each coordinate and average this inequality. Since \(\sum_j\rho(x_j)=1/H\), the Bayes risk under the uniform prior on the cube is at least a fixed positive multiple of \(t^2/\eta\). For \(0\le t\le1\),
\[
\frac{t^2}{3}\le\log\cosh t\le\frac{t^2}{2}.
\]
The calibration \(\log\cosh t=64\eta\epsilon\) therefore makes this Bayes risk strictly larger than \(\epsilon\), after decreasing \(c_1\) if needed. The same inequalities give \(\gamma^2=t^2/\eta^2\le192\epsilon/\eta\). Since \(p=(4D)^{-1}\) and \(k\ge d/4\), the testing condition includes
\[
n\le c_2(C,D,\eta)\frac{dD\eta}{\epsilon}.
\]
The minimax risk over the shell is no smaller than the Bayes risk over its finite subfamily. Hence it exceeds \(\epsilon\) throughout the last range of sample sizes, which proves
\[
N^\star(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)\ge c_2(C,D,\eta)dD\eta/\epsilon
\]
for every \(0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_1\).
