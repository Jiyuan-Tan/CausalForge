Let \(e_0,\ldots,e_{d-1}\) be an orthonormal basis, use two actions \(\{1,0\}\), and put
\[
q=\frac{e^\eta/C-1}{e^\eta-1}\in(0,1).
\]
At a context \(x_C\), set
\[
\phi(x_C,1)=e_0,\qquad \phi(x_C,0)=0,\qquad
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(1\mid x_C)=q.
\]
If \(D<C\), add a context \(x_A\) at which both action features equal \(e_0\); if \(D=C\), omit it. For each \(j=1,\ldots,d-1\), add a context \(x_j\) at which both action features equal \(e_j\). At every action-invariant context give both actions positive reference probability. This fixes a finite public experiment, and all its features have norm at most one.

We first show that its exact shell is nonempty. When \(D<C\), put
\[
a_{\mathrm{anc}}=q\frac{C-D}{D-1}>0,
\]
give \(x_C\) raw context mass one, \(x_A\) raw mass \(a_{\mathrm{anc}}\), and every \(x_j\) raw mass one, and normalize. When \(D=C\), omit \(x_A\) and normalize the other positive raw masses. Take \(\theta=e_0\) and deterministic reward \(Y=\phi(X,A)^\top\theta\). At \(x_C\), the Gibbs normalizer is
\[
qe^\eta+1-q=e^\eta/C,
\]
so the target-to-reference ratios of actions \(1\) and \(0\) are respectively \(C\) and \(C/e^\eta<1\). All action-invariant contexts have ratio one, whence \(C_P=C\). The covariance matrices are diagonal. In direction \(e_0\), their generalized ratio is \(C=D\) when the anchor is omitted and, when it is present, is
\[
\frac{qC+a_{\mathrm{anc}}}{q+a_{\mathrm{anc}}}=D.
\]
Every other generalized ratio equals one. All context masses and the relevant logging action probabilities are positive, so \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}\succ0\), and consequently \(D_P=D\). This proves nonemptiness.

Now take an arbitrary \(P\) in this exact shell and write \(t=\theta^\top e_0\). Nonsingularity forces positive mass on every \(x_j\). Moreover \(x_C\) must have positive mass: otherwise every positive-mass context would be action-invariant and \(C_P\) would equal one. Bounded rewards at \(x_C\) give \(0\le t\le1\). The only nontrivial target-to-reference ratio is the ratio of action \(1\) at \(x_C\), namely
\[
K(t)=\frac{e^{\eta t}}{qe^{\eta t}+1-q}.
\]
The other ratio at that context is at most one. A direct derivative gives
\[
K'(t)=\frac{\eta(1-q)e^{\eta t}}{(qe^{\eta t}+1-q)^2}>0,
\]
and the calibration gives \(K(1)=C\). Hence exact pointwise coverage \(C_P=C\) forces \(t=1\). It follows that every shell member has the same Gibbs policy: at \(x_C\) it is the Gibbs tilt generated by the publicly forced reward pair \((1,0)\), and at every other context it equals \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\). Call this common policy \(\pi^\dagger\). Its formula uses only the public experiment.

The constant learner returning \(\pi^\dagger\) belongs to \(\mathcal L_n(\mathfrak E)\). By \(\text{lem:gibbs-regret-identity}\), its regret is zero for every shell member. Regret is nonnegative because \(\pi_P^\star\) maximizes \(J_{\eta,P}\), so the minimax risk is exactly zero. Since sample sizes begin at one, \(\text{def:sample-complexity}\) yields \(N^\star(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)=1\) for every \(\epsilon>0\).
