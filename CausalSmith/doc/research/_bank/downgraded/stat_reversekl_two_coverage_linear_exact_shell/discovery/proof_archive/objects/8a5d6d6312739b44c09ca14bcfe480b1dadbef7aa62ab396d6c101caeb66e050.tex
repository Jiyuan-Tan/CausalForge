For a fixed public experiment \(\mathfrak E\), the equivalence between nonemptiness of \(\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)\) and the displayed finite exponential nonlinear-semidefinite system is exactly \(\text{lem:fixed-experiment-shell-certificate}\), with the unindexed shell notation there interpreted as the fixed-\(\mathfrak E\) abbreviation established by \(\text{def:common-experiment}\).

Fix \(d\ge4\), \(\eta>0\), and \(1<D\le C<e^\eta\). By \(\text{prop:zero-risk-nontrivial-shell}\), there is a finite public experiment \(\mathfrak E_0\) with a nonempty exact shell such that
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,\mathfrak E_0}\bigl(\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E_0}(d,C,D,\eta)\bigr)=0
\]
for every \(n\in\mathbb N\). By \(\text{def:sample-complexity}\) and the fact that sample sizes begin at one,
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E_0}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)=1
\]
for every \(\epsilon>0\). By \(\text{prop:informative-fast-shell-all-indices}\), there is another finite public experiment \(\mathfrak E_1\), at the same values of \((d,C,D,\eta)\), and positive constants \(c(C,D,\eta)\) and \(\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)\), such that
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E_1}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\ge c(C,D,\eta)\frac{dD\eta}{\epsilon}
\qquad(0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)).
\]
Both constructions apply throughout \(1<D\le C<e^\eta\), including pairs for which \(2D-1\ge e^\eta\).

The two experiments have identical numerical indices but different sample-complexity functions: the first is identically one, whereas the second diverges at least at the displayed pointwise local rate as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). Therefore \((d,C,D,\eta)\) alone cannot determine the fixed-experiment minimax frontier.

Finally, let \(B(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)\) be any lower bound asserted to hold for every fixed public experiment having a nonempty exact shell at the given indices. Applying it to \(\mathfrak E_0\) gives
\[
B(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le N^\star_{\mathfrak E_0}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)=1.
\]
Thus no such uniform lower bound can exceed the unavoidable bound \(N^\star_{\mathfrak E}\ge1\), and none can diverge as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). In particular, a uniform \(\Omega(dD\epsilon^{-2})\) lower branch is impossible. This argument does not decide whether a slow branch holds for a specified informative experiment, nor whether an all-learner upper bound with \(dD\) rather than \(d^2D\) dependence is attainable.
