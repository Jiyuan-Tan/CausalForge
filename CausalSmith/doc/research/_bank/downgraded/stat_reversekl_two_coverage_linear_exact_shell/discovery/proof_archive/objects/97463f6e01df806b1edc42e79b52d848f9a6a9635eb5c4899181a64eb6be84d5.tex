Fix \((C,D)\in\mathfrak F_{\mathrm{hard}}^\circ(d,\eta)\), and write \(t=\eta\gamma\). Let \(H\) denote the sum of the raw context masses in the construction. Since \(\tau(1+A)=1/4\), the calibration contexts have total raw mass \(1/4\), while the hard contexts have total raw mass one. Thus
\[
H=\frac54
\quad\text{if \(d\) is odd},
\qquad
H=\frac32
\quad\text{if \(d\) is even},
\]
the second equality accounting for the optional \(z\)-context. In either case,
\[
\rho(x_j)=\frac{1}{Hk},
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^k\rho(x_j)=\frac1H,
\qquad
\frac54\le H\le\frac32.
\]

We first establish admissibility. Since \(p=(4D)^{-1}\), at \(\gamma=0\) the hard-context calibration satisfies
\[
T_0=\frac{D(1-2p)}{1-2pD}=2D-1.
\]
The defining inequalities for \(\mathfrak F_{\mathrm{hard}}^\circ(d,\eta)\) imply
\[
1<2D-1<e^\eta.
\]
Consequently \(\beta_0=\eta^{-1}\log(2D-1)\) lies strictly between zero and one. The denominator \(e^{\eta s}-2pD\cosh(\eta s)\) equals \(1/2\) at \(s=0\). By continuity of this denominator and of
\[
T_s=\frac{D(1-2p)}{e^{\eta s}-2pD\cosh(\eta s)},
\qquad
\beta_s=\eta^{-1}\log T_s,
\]
there is \(\bar\gamma>0\), depending only on \((D,\eta)\), such that for every \(s\in[0,\bar\gamma]\) the denominator is positive,
\[
s\le\beta_s\le1-s,
\qquad
\eta s\le1,
\qquad
s\le\frac14.
\]
It follows directly from the definition of \(g_0(D,\eta)\) that \(g_0(D,\eta)\ge\bar\gamma>0\). Hence \(\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)>0\). Because
\[
\gamma(\epsilon,d,C,D,\eta)
=\eta^{-1}\operatorname{arcosh}(e^{64\eta\epsilon})
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\)},
\]
we may choose \(\epsilon_1(C,D,\eta)\in(0,\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)]\) so that \(\gamma\le\bar\gamma\) whenever \(0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_1(C,D,\eta)\). In particular, the hard rewards \(\beta\pm\gamma\) belong to \([0,1]\), and \(0\le t\le1\).

We next verify the two exact shell equalities. At \(x_C\), the Gibbs normalizer is
\[
q e^\eta+1-q=\frac{e^\eta}{C}.
\]
Therefore the target-to-reference likelihood ratio of action \(+\) is exactly \(C\), whereas the ratio of each other action in the reference support is \(C/e^\eta<1\). At a hard context \(x_j\), the normalizer is
\[
Z=1-2p+2pT_\gamma\cosh t.
\]
The defining formula for \(T_\gamma\) is algebraically equivalent to
\[
T_\gamma e^t=DZ.
\]
Thus the likelihood ratios of the favored and unfavored informative actions are, respectively,
\[
D
\quad\text{and}\quad
De^{-2t}.
\]
Moreover, \(\beta\ge\gamma\) gives \(T_\gamma e^t\ge1\), so the zero-action ratio \(1/Z=D/(T_\gamma e^t)\) is at most \(D\). At \(x_A\), and at \(x_z\) when the latter is present, every action has the same reward, so every likelihood ratio on the reference support is one. Since \(D\le C\), the global essential supremum is attained at \((x_C,+)\) and equals \(C\). Hence \(C_P=C\).

The logging and target covariance matrices are block diagonal in the orthonormal basis used by the construction. In the \(e_0\)-direction their generalized variance ratio is
\[
\frac{qC+A}{q+A}=D,
\qquad
A=\frac{q(C-D)}{D-1},
\]
where the same identity holds when \(C=D\) because then \(A=0\). In the \((u_j,w_j)\)-block, the logging covariance is a positive scalar multiple of \(pI_2\). The two action-feature directions diagonalize the corresponding target block, and after whitening its eigenvalues are
\[
D
\quad\text{and}\quad
De^{-2t}.
\]
The optional \(z\)-block has generalized ratio one. Every basis direction receives positive logging mass, so \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}\succ0\), and the largest generalized eigenvalue is exactly \(D\). Thus \(D_P=D\). The displayed features have norm at most one, and taking the conditional reward at every cell to be Bernoulli with the displayed mean gives linear realizability and rewards in \([0,1]\). All members use the stipulated reference policy as their logging policy. We have therefore proved
\[
\mathcal H_{d,C,D,\eta,\epsilon}
\subseteq\mathcal M(d,C,D,\eta)
\]
throughout the stated positive accuracy interval; in particular, the shell is nonempty.

It remains to prove the testing lower bound. At any hard context, the two target Gibbs action laws corresponding to the two possible signs are
\[
s_+=(a,b,c),
\qquad
s_-=(b,a,c),
\qquad
a=pD=\frac14,
\qquad
b=ae^{-2t},
\qquad
c=1-a-b.
\]
For any action distribution \(u\), let
\[
A_t=\sum_{a'\in\mathcal A}\sqrt{s_+(a')s_-(a')}
=1-a(1-e^{-t})^2
\]
and let \(\bar s(a')=\sqrt{s_+(a')s_-(a')}/A_t\). Direct expansion of the three relative entropies gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_+)+\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_-)
=2\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert\bar s)-2\log A_t.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\inf_u\frac1\eta\left\{
\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_+)+\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_-)
\right\}
=-\frac2\eta\log\{1-a(1-e^{-t})^2\}.
\]
For \(0\le t\le1\), the inequalities \(1-e^{-t}\ge t/2\) and \(-\log(1-z)\ge z\) yield
\[
\inf_u\frac1\eta\left\{
\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_+)+\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_-)
\right\}
\ge\frac{t^2}{8\eta}=:\Delta.
\]

Let \(v^{(j)}\) be obtained from \(v\in\{-1,1\}^k\) by flipping its \(j\)-th coordinate. Neighboring data laws differ only in the two Bernoulli reward distributions attached to the informative actions at \(x_j\). By decreasing \(\bar\gamma\), and hence \(\epsilon_1\), if necessary, there is \(m=m(D,\eta)\in(0,1/2)\) such that every hard Bernoulli mean belongs to \([m,1-m]\). The elementary inequality \(\log y\le y-1\) implies
\[
\operatorname{kl}(u\Vert v')
\le\frac{(u-v')^2}{v'(1-v')}
\qquad(0<u,v'<1).
\]
Since the two neighboring means differ by \(2\gamma\), tensorization of relative entropy therefore gives a constant \(K(D,\eta)<\infty\) for which
\[
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(P_v^{\otimes n}\middle\Vert P_{v^{(j)}}^{\otimes n}\right)
\le K(D,\eta)n\rho(x_j)p\gamma^2
=K(D,\eta)\frac{np\gamma^2}{Hk}.
\]
Choose \(c_1(D,\eta)>0\) sufficiently small. If
\[
n\le c_1(D,\eta)\frac{k}{p\gamma^2},
\]
then the last relative entropy is at most \(1/2\). Applying the scalar inequality
\[
(1+z)\log(1+z)+(1-z)\log(1-z)\ge z^2
\]
to the binary partition attaining total variation gives
\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(P_v^{\otimes n},P_{v^{(j)}}^{\otimes n}\right)^2
\le\frac12\operatorname{KL}\!\left(P_v^{\otimes n}\middle\Vert P_{v^{(j)}}^{\otimes n}\right),
\]
and hence every neighboring total variation distance is at most \(1/2\).

We now perform the coordinatewise Assouad summation. Fix an arbitrary learner and suppress its fixed public-design argument. If its regularized regret is infinite on some instance, the desired lower bound is immediate, so consider the finite-regret case. For a realized sample \(z\), define
\[
R_j(v,z)=\frac{\rho(x_j)}{\eta}
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(\widehat\pi_z(\cdot\mid x_j)\middle\Vert s_{v_j}\right).
\]
Direct expansion of the regularized-welfare definition using the displayed Gibbs probabilities shows that the total regularized regret is the sum over contexts of their context mass times the relative entropy from the learned action law to the corresponding target Gibbs law, divided by the inverse temperature. All these context terms are nonnegative. Therefore
\[
R(v,z)\ge\sum_{j=1}^kR_j(v,z).
\]
For every \(v,j,z\), the preceding two-sign calculation, applied to \(u=\widehat\pi_z(\cdot\mid x_j)\), gives the pointwise inequality
\[
R_j(v,z)+R_j(v^{(j)},z)
\ge\rho(x_j)\Delta.
\]
Let the two neighboring sample laws have densities \(f_v\) and \(f_{v^{(j)}}\) with respect to a common dominating measure. Nonnegativity of the two losses and integration against the smaller density imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_v R_j(v,Z)+\mathbb E_{v^{(j)}}R_j(v^{(j)},Z)\\
&\quad\ge
\int\min\{f_v,f_{v^{(j)}}\}
\{R_j(v,z)+R_j(v^{(j)},z)\}\,dz\\
&\quad\ge
\rho(x_j)\Delta
\left\{1-\operatorname{TV}\!\left(P_v^{\otimes n},P_{v^{(j)}}^{\otimes n}\right)\right\}
\ge\frac{\rho(x_j)\Delta}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Define the Bayes risk under the uniform prior on the sign cube by
\[
B=2^{-k}\sum_v\mathbb E_vR(v,Z).
\]
For each fixed \(j\), the cube has \(2^{k-1}\) disjoint neighboring pairs along coordinate \(j\). Hence the preceding inequalities may be averaged separately for each coordinate and then summed:
\[
\begin{aligned}
B
&\ge\sum_{j=1}^k2^{-k}\sum_v\mathbb E_vR_j(v,Z)\\
&\ge\sum_{j=1}^k2^{-k}2^{k-1}\frac{\rho(x_j)\Delta}{2}
=\frac{\Delta}{4H}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Pairing the full regret for each \(j\) would not justify this summation, because it would count the same full loss \(k\) times; the coordinatewise decomposition above is what makes the sum valid. Since \(\Delta=t^2/(8\eta)\) and \(H\le3/2\),
\[
B\ge\frac{t^2}{32H\eta}
\ge\frac{t^2}{48\eta}.
\]

The calibration of the hard family gives
\[
\log\cosh t=64\eta\epsilon.
\]
For \(0\le t\le1\), integration of the derivative bounds for \(\log\cosh t\) gives
\[
\frac{t^2}{3}\le\log\cosh t\le\frac{t^2}{2}.
\]
The upper inequality yields \(t^2\ge128\eta\epsilon\), and therefore
\[
B\ge\frac{8}{3}\epsilon>\epsilon.
\]
The lower inequality yields
\[
\gamma^2=\frac{t^2}{\eta^2}
\le\frac{192\epsilon}{\eta}.
\]
Since \(k=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor\ge d/4\) for \(d\ge4\) and \(p=(4D)^{-1}\),
\[
\frac{k}{p\gamma^2}
\ge\frac{dD\eta}{192\epsilon}.
\]
Thus, with \(c_2(C,D,\eta)>0\) chosen no larger than \(c_1(D,\eta)/192\), every positive integer
\[
n\le c_2(C,D,\eta)\frac{dD\eta}{\epsilon}
\]
satisfies the neighboring-testing condition and has minimax risk at least the Bayes risk \(B>\epsilon\). By the definition of exact-shell sample complexity,
\[
N^\star(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\ge c_2(C,D,\eta)\frac{dD\eta}{\epsilon}
\]
for every \(0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_1(C,D,\eta)\), after shrinking \(\epsilon_1\) once more if needed. This proves the pointwise local fast branch. The argument uses the quadratic local separation \(t^2/\eta\) and supplies no \(\epsilon^{-2}\) branch.
