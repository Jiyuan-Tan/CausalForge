Fix a positive integer \(n\). We first verify measurability. Regard \(\mathcal F\) as a compact subset of the finite-dimensional Euclidean space \(\mathbb R^S\), with coordinates ordered as in \(\text{def:prediction-polytope-learner}\), and let \(z\) denote a realized sample. The map \((z,f)\mapsto L_n(z,f)\) is Borel measurable in \(z\) and continuous in \(f\). Applying \(\text{lem:measurable-maximum-theorem}\) to the constant correspondence \(z\mapsto\mathcal F\) and the objective \(-L_n\) shows that the empirical-risk minimizing correspondence is measurable, nonempty, and compact valued. Recursively minimizing the first coordinate, then the second, and so on, applies the same lemma at each of the finitely many steps. The final correspondence is a singleton, so \(z\mapsto\widehat f_z\) is Borel measurable.

Put
\[
\Gamma(z)=\mathcal Q_n(z)=\left\{f\in\mathcal F:\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{f(X_i,A_i)-\widehat f_z(X_i,A_i)\}^2\le64\frac{a_n}{n}\right\}.
\]
This correspondence is nonempty and compact valued. It is weakly measurable. Indeed, for every open \(O\subseteq\mathcal F\), compact metrizability supplies compact sets \(K_m\subset O\) with \(O=\bigcup_mK_m\). Then \(\{z:\Gamma(z)\cap O\ne\varnothing\}\) is the union over \(m\) of
\[
\left\{z:\min_{f\in K_m}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{f(X_i,A_i)-\widehat f_z(X_i,A_i)\}^2\le64\frac{a_n}{n}\right\},
\]
and each displayed minimum is measurable by \(\text{lem:measurable-maximum-theorem}\).

For fixed \(z\), the map
\[
(g,f)\longmapsto P_nH_{g,f}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nH_{g,f}(X_i)
\]
is continuous on \(\mathcal F^2\), because the action support is finite and every Gibbs normalizer is strictly positive. The measurable maximum theorem, with base point \((z,g)\), correspondence \(\Gamma(z)\), and objective \(P_nH_{g,f}\), shows that
\[
(z,g)\longmapsto\widehat U_n(z,g)=\max_{f\in\Gamma(z)}P_nH_{g,f}
\]
is Borel measurable. For fixed \(z\), uniform continuity on the compact set \(\mathcal F^2\) shows that this supremum is continuous in \(g\). Applying the same theorem to \(-\widehat U_n\) over \(g\in\Gamma(z)\), followed by recursive coordinate minimization, proves that the stipulated lexicographic selector \(z\mapsto\widetilde g_n(z)\) is Borel measurable. The finite Gibbs formula is continuous in its reward-vector input. Hence \(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}}=\pi_{\widetilde g_n}\) is measurable and belongs to \(\mathcal L_n(\mathfrak E)\). Its construction uses only \((\mathcal D_n,\mathfrak E)\), and therefore uses neither \(\rho\), \(C\), nor \(D\).

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)\), write \(r=r_\theta\), and set
\[
R_n=600\frac{a_n}{n}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:bounded-linear-localization}\), there is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\) of probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\) on which
\[
r\in\mathcal Q_n,
\qquad
\sup_{f\in\mathcal Q_n}\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(f-r)^2\le R_n.
\]
Apply \(\text{lem:uniform-gibbs-radius-comparison}\) with \(\zeta=\delta_n/2\). Since \(\delta_n=n^{-2}\), there is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{cmp}}\), of probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\), on which, simultaneously for every \(g,f\in\mathcal F\),
\[
P_\rho H_{g,f}\le2P_nH_{g,f}+b_n,
\qquad
P_nH_{g,f}\le2P_\rho H_{g,f}+b_n,
\]
where
\[
b_n=\frac{8}{3n}\left[\log(4n^2)+2d\log\{1+4n(\eta+2)\}\right]+\frac3n.
\]
The comparison is simultaneous over the deterministic class \(\mathcal F^2\), so it remains valid for the data-dependent set \(\mathcal Q_n\) and selector \(\widetilde g_n\).

Work on \(\mathcal E_n=\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\cap\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{cmp}}\), which has probability at least \(1-\delta_n\). For every \(f\in\mathcal Q_n\), the contrast \(f-r\) belongs to \(\operatorname{span}(\phi)\). By \(\text{lem:feature-coverage-domination}\) and the shell equality \(D_P=D\),
\[
P_\rho H_{r,f}
=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(r-f)^2
\le D\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(r-f)^2
\le DR_n.
\]
Since \(r\in\mathcal Q_n\), the empirical comparison gives
\[
\widehat U_n(r)
\le2\sup_{f\in\mathcal Q_n}P_\rho H_{r,f}+b_n
\le2DR_n+b_n.
\]
The defining minimality of \(\widetilde g_n\) yields \(\widehat U_n(\widetilde g_n)\le\widehat U_n(r)\). Again using \(r\in\mathcal Q_n\) and the reverse comparison,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\widetilde g_n}}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
&=P_\rho H_{\widetilde g_n,r}\\
&\le2P_nH_{\widetilde g_n,r}+b_n\\
&\le2\widehat U_n(\widetilde g_n)+b_n\\
&\le4DR_n+3b_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
At the true Gibbs endpoint, feature domination and localization also give
\[
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
\le D\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
\le DR_n.
\]
Define
\[
A_n=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2,
\qquad
B_n=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\widetilde g_n}}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2.
\]
Every nonempty shell has \(D\ge1\) by \(\text{thm:feasible-index-region}\). Therefore
\[
A_n+B_n\le5DR_n+3b_n
\le D(5R_n+3b_n)
=D\left(3000\frac{a_n}{n}+3b_n\right)=V_n.
\]
Apply \(\text{lem:two-endpoint-gibbs-conversion}\) with \(g=\widetilde g_n\). On \(\mathcal E_n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}})
&\le\min\{\eta(A_n+B_n),\sqrt{A_n}+\sqrt{B_n}\}\\
&\le\min\{\eta V_n,\sqrt{2V_n}\},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last step uses \((\sqrt{A_n}+\sqrt{B_n})^2\le2(A_n+B_n)\).

We next bound regret off \(\mathcal E_n\). For arbitrary \(r,g:S\to[0,1]\), put \(\Delta=g-r\) and let \(\pi_t\) be the Gibbs policy generated by \(r+t\Delta\). Define
\[
F(t)=\eta^{-1}\mathbb E_\rho\log\sum_a\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid X)e^{\eta(r+t\Delta)(X,a)}.
\]
Finite support permits differentiation under the sum and expectation, and gives
\[
F''(t)=\eta\mathbb E_\rho\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta).
\]
The Gibbs variational identity followed by integration by parts yields
\[
J_{\eta,r}(\pi_r)-J_{\eta,r}(\pi_g)
=F'(1)-\{F(1)-F(0)\}
=\eta\int_0^1t\,\mathbb E_\rho\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta)\,dt
\le\eta/2,
\]
because \(\Delta\in[-1,1]\) implies \(\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta)\le1\). The slow part of \(\text{lem:two-endpoint-gibbs-conversion}\), together with \(|\Delta|\le1\), also bounds the regret by \(2\). Since \(P^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_n^c)\le\delta_n\), averaging the good- and bad-event bounds proves, uniformly over the exact shell,
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}})\right]
\le\min\{\eta V_n,\sqrt{2V_n}\}
+\delta_n\min\{2,\eta/2\}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(P\) proves the finite-sample assertion.

Fix \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\). We now invert the bound. For \(d\ge4\) and \(n\ge1\),
\[
a_n\le2d\log(en),
\qquad
\log(4n^2)\le d\log(en),
\]
\[
\log\{1+4n(\eta+2)\}
\le\log8+\log\{1+n(1+\eta)\},
\qquad
9\le3d\log(en).
\]
Substitution into the definitions of \(b_n\) and \(V_n\) gives
\[
V_n
\le6100\frac{dD}{n}
\left[\log(en)+\log\{1+n(1+\eta)\}\right].
\tag{1}
\]
If \(\eta\le2\epsilon\), the deterministic bound \(J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}})\le\eta/2\le\epsilon\) holds already at \(n=1\). Because the sample-size domain is \(\mathbb N\), \(N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)=1\).

Suppose \(\eta>2\epsilon\), and define
\[
q_\epsilon=dD\min\{\eta/\epsilon,\epsilon^{-2}\},
\qquad
\ell_\epsilon=1+\log(eq_\epsilon)+\log\{1+(1+\eta)q_\epsilon\}.
\]
Since \(D\ge1\), \(d\ge4\), \(\epsilon<1\), and \(\eta>2\epsilon\), one has \(q_\epsilon>4\) and \(\ell_\epsilon>1\). Set \(K_0=10^8\) and
\[
n_0=\left\lceil K_0q_\epsilon\ell_\epsilon\right\rceil.
\]
Integer rounding gives
\[
K_0q_\epsilon\ell_\epsilon\le n_0\le2K_0q_\epsilon\ell_\epsilon.
\]
Writing \(s=(1+\eta)q_\epsilon\), the definition of \(\ell_\epsilon\), the inequality \(\log\ell_\epsilon\le\ell_\epsilon\), and \(s\ge1\) imply
\[
\log(en_0)\le\{\log(2eK_0)+2\}\ell_\epsilon,
\]
\[
\log\{1+n_0(1+\eta)\}
\le\{\log(3K_0)+2\}\ell_\epsilon.
\]
Consequently
\[
\log(en_0)+\log\{1+n_0(1+\eta)\}
\le c_{K_0}\ell_\epsilon,
\qquad
c_{K_0}=4+\log(6eK_0^2)<44.
\]
Applying \((1)\) at \(n_0\) gives
\[
V_{n_0}\le\alpha\frac{dD}{q_\epsilon},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{6100c_{K_0}}{K_0}<\frac18.
\]
If the minimum defining \(q_\epsilon\) is \(\eta/\epsilon\), then \(\eta V_{n_0}\le\alpha\epsilon\le\epsilon/8\). If it is \(\epsilon^{-2}\), then \(\sqrt{2V_{n_0}}\le\sqrt{2\alpha}\,\epsilon\le\epsilon/2\). Thus the good-event term is at most \(\epsilon/2\) in either case.

The complement term is also bounded explicitly. In the first case, \(n_0\ge q_\epsilon\ge4\eta/\epsilon\), so
\[
n_0^{-2}\min\{2,\eta/2\}
\le\frac{\epsilon^2}{32\eta}<\frac\epsilon{64}.
\]
In the second case, \(n_0\ge q_\epsilon\ge4\epsilon^{-2}\), so
\[
n_0^{-2}\min\{2,\eta/2\}
\le\frac{\epsilon^4}{8}\le\frac\epsilon8.
\]
Therefore the risk of the stabilized learner at \(n_0\) is at most \(\epsilon\). By \(\text{def:minimax-risk}\) and \(\text{def:sample-complexity}\),
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le n_0
=\left\lceil10^8q_\epsilon
\left[1+\log(eq_\epsilon)+\log\{1+(1+\eta)q_\epsilon\}\right]\right\rceil.
\]
The two logarithms are precisely the factors suppressed by \(\widetilde O\). Hence, whenever the displayed order is at least two,
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le\widetilde O\left(dD\min\{\eta/\epsilon,\epsilon^{-2}\}\right).
\]
The learner and every bound above are independent of \(C\), which completes the proof.
