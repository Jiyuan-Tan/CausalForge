The learner, its measurability, and its independence from \(C\) and \(D\) are exactly those established in \(\text{thm:erm-plugin-upper}\). For \(0<\eta\le1\),
\[
\kappa_\eta
=\eta\int_0^1 t e^{2\eta t}\,dt
\le \eta e^2\int_0^1t\,dt
=\frac{e^2}{2}\eta,
\]
and
\[
(1+e^{2\eta})^2\le(1+e^2)^2.
\]
For nonnegative \(x,y\) and positive \(a,b\),
\[
\min\{ax,by\}\le\max\{a,b\}\min\{x,y\}.
\]
Apply this inequality with
\[
x=\frac\eta\epsilon,\qquad y=\frac1{\epsilon^2},\qquad a=\frac{e^2}{2},\qquad b=(1+e^2)^2
\]
to the sample-complexity conclusion of \(\text{thm:erm-plugin-upper}\). The multiplicative constant \(\max\{e^2/2,(1+e^2)^2\}\) is universal, and the logarithmic factors and the stated sample-size range are unchanged. This proves the claim.
