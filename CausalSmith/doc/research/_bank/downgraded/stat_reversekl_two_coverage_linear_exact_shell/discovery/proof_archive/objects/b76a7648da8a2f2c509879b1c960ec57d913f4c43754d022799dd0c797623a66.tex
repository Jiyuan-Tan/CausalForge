Fix \(d\ge4\), \(\eta>0\), and the finite public experiment \(\mathfrak E\). By \(\text{thm:feasible-index-region}\), every nonempty shell has either \((C,D)=(1,1)\) or \(1<D\le C<e^\eta\). By \(\text{lem:fixed-experiment-shell-certificate}\), within that region the shell \(\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)\) is nonempty if and only if at least one branch of the displayed finite exponential nonlinear-semidefinite system is feasible. This proves the fixed-experiment feasibility characterization. If the boundary shell at \((C,D)=(1,1)\) is nonempty, \(\text{thm:zero-risk-boundary}\) gives
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,1,1,\eta)=1
\]
for every \(\epsilon>0\).

Suppose that a nontrivial shell is nonempty. The learner constructed in \(\text{thm:clean-temperature-feature-upper}\) is the Gibbs policy generated by the data-selected stabilizer \(\widetilde g_n\). That theorem proves that the selector is measurable and belongs to \(\mathcal L_n(\mathfrak E)\). Its construction uses the sample and the public experiment only. By \(\text{def:common-experiment}\), \(\rho\) is law-specific and is not a component of \(\mathfrak E\); the learner also does not receive \(C\) or \(D\). The same theorem proves, with universal constants and with only logarithmic factors suppressed, that
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le\widetilde O\!\left(dD\min\{\eta/\epsilon,\epsilon^{-2}\}\right)
\]
whenever the displayed sample-size order is at least two. Since \(C\) occurs neither in the learner nor on the right-hand side, it is irrelevant to this general achievability guarantee.

We next show why these indices cannot determine the exact fixed-design frontier. Fix any \(1<D\le C<e^\eta\). By \(\text{thm:fixed-design-index-insufficiency}\), there are finite public experiments \(\mathfrak E_0\) and \(\mathfrak E_1\), both with nonempty exact shells at the same \((d,C,D,\eta)\), such that
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E_0}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)=1
\]
for every \(\epsilon>0\), while for some \(c(C,D,\eta)>0\) and \(\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)>0\),
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E_1}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\ge c(C,D,\eta)\frac{dD\eta}{\epsilon}
\]
for every \(0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)\). The latter construction is valid throughout \(1<D\le C<e^\eta\), with no condition \(2D-1<e^\eta\), so it includes every feasible index pair satisfying \(2D-1\ge e^\eta\). For fixed \((C,D,\eta)\), its lower bound has the same \(dD\eta/\epsilon\) fast-branch dependence as the clean upper bound, apart from logarithms and the stated pointwise constants. The two experiments have identical numerical indices but incompatible sample-complexity behavior. Therefore no function of \((d,C,D,\eta)\) alone can equal the fixed-experiment minimax frontier.

It remains to settle the proposed quadratic lower branch. Define \(b_\eta\) and \(Q_{d,D,\eta}\) as in the statement. The hypotheses of \(\text{lem:fixed-experiment-fast-branch-inversion}\) hold for the fixed finite experiment and its nonempty shell. Hence, for every \(0<\epsilon<1\),
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le
\left\lceil\frac{8Q_{d,D,\eta}}{\epsilon}
\log\!\left(\frac{8eQ_{d,D,\eta}}{\epsilon}\right)\right\rceil
\]
and
\[
\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{\epsilon^2}{d}N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)=0.
\]
If there were constants \(c(C,D,\eta)>0\) and \(\epsilon_1(C,D,\eta)>0\) such that \(N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)\ge c(C,D,\eta)d/\epsilon^2\) for every \(0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_1(C,D,\eta)\), then the last normalized limit would be bounded below by the positive constant \(c(C,D,\eta)\), a contradiction. Since \(D>0\) is fixed on the shell, the same argument rules out a \(dD/\epsilon^2\) lower branch. Finally, any lower bound asserted uniformly over all public experiments at the given indices must apply to \(\mathfrak E_0\) and therefore cannot exceed one. These conclusions prove the claimed positive achievability result and the negative frontier and quadratic-branch assertions.
