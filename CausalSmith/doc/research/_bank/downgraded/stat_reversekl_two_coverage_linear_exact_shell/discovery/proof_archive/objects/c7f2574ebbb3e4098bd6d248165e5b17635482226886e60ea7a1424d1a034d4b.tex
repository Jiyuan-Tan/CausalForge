\paragraph{Proof.} For each integer \(d\ge 4\), let \(\mathcal X_d=\{x_1,\ldots,x_d\}\), let \(\mathcal A=\{1,0\}\), and put \(\rho(x_j)=1/d\).  With \((e_j)_{j=1}^d\) the canonical basis of \(\mathbb R^d\), define
\[
\phi(x_j,1)=e_j,\qquad \phi(x_j,0)=\tfrac12e_j,
\qquad
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(1\mid x_j)=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(0\mid x_j)=\tfrac12.
\]
Take \(\theta=\tfrac12\sum_{j=1}^d e_j\), set \(\eta_0=4\log 3\), and let \(P_d\) have deterministic reward \(Y=r_\theta(X,A)\).  Thus
\[
r_\theta(x_j,1)=\tfrac12,\qquad r_\theta(x_j,0)=\tfrac14.
\]
The supports are finite, the feature norms are at most one, the reward belongs to \([0,1]\), linear realizability holds, and the action law is the reference policy.  Moreover,
\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}
 =\frac1d\left(\frac12+\frac18\right)I_d
 =\frac{5}{8d}I_d\succ0.
\]
Because \(\eta_0\{r_\theta(x_j,1)-r_\theta(x_j,0)\}=\log 3\), the Gibbs odds of action \(1\) against action \(0\) equal three, whence
\[
\pi_{P_d}^\star(1\mid x_j)=\frac34,
\qquad
\pi_{P_d}^\star(0\mid x_j)=\frac14.
\]
It follows directly that
\[
C_{P_d}=\max\left\{\frac{3/4}{1/2},\frac{1/4}{1/2}\right\}=\frac32=:C_0.
\]
The target feature covariance is
\[
\Lambda_{P_d}^\star
 =\frac1d\left(\frac34+\frac1{16}\right)I_d
 =\frac{13}{16d}I_d.
\]
Consequently every eigenvalue of \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1/2}\Lambda_{P_d}^\star\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1/2}\) is
\[
\frac{13/16}{5/8}=\frac{13}{10}=:D_0.
\]
All defining conditions of \(\mathcal M(d,C_0,D_0,\eta_0)\) therefore hold, so \(P_d\in\mathcal M(d,C_0,D_0,\eta_0)\).

Now take \(n_d=\lceil d^2\rceil=d^2\), and let \(N_j=\sum_{i=1}^{n_d}\mathbf 1\{X_i=x_j\}\).  By \(\text{lem:multinomial-max-count}\), the event
\[
\mathcal B_d=\left\{\max_{1\le j\le d}N_j\le 3d\right\}
\]
satisfies
\[
P_d^{\otimes n_d}(\mathcal B_d)
\ge 1-d\exp\{-d(3\log3-2)\}=1-o(1).
\]
The true reward function belongs to \(\mathcal F\) and has empirical loss zero.  Hence every empirical-risk minimizer, including the selected \(\widehat f\), also has empirical loss zero.  Write \(\widehat f(x_j,1)=\widehat\theta_j\) and \(\widehat f(x_j,0)=\widehat\theta_j/2\).  If \(N_j>0\), zero empirical loss and either possible logged action imply \(\widehat\theta_j=1/2\).  If \(N_j=0\), the empirical loss and empirical seminorm contain no term from context \(x_j\).

For each \(j\), let \(f^{(j)}\) be the prediction function obtained from \(\widehat f\) by setting coordinate \(j\) of its coefficient vector to zero and leaving all other coordinates unchanged.  Because the resulting predictions at \(x_j\) are zero and all other predictions equal those of \(\widehat f\), the function \(f^{(j)}\) belongs to \(\mathcal F\).  No claim that \(f^{(j)}\) is an empirical-risk minimizer is needed.  When \(N_j>0\), the preceding identification of \(\widehat\theta_j\) gives
\[
\|f^{(j)}-\widehat f\|_{n_d}^2
\le \frac{N_j}{4n_d};
\]
when \(N_j=0\), the left-hand side is zero, so the same inequality remains valid.  On \(\mathcal B_d\), therefore,
\[
\|f^{(j)}-\widehat f\|_{n_d}^2
\le \frac{3d}{4n_d}
\le \frac{3}{4d}.
\]
The squared radius defining \(\mathcal Q_{n_d}\) is
\[
R_d=c_0\frac{d\log(en_d)+\log(2n_d^2)}{n_d}.
\]
Since \(dR_d\to\infty\), one has \(R_d\ge3/(4d)\) for all sufficiently large \(d\).  Thus \(f^{(j)}\in\mathcal Q_{n_d}\) simultaneously for every \(j\) on \(\mathcal B_d\).  As every member of \(\mathcal F\) is pointwise nonnegative and \(f^{(j)}(x_j,1)=f^{(j)}(x_j,0)=0\), coordinatewise minimization over \(\mathcal Q_{n_d}\) yields
\[
\underline r_{n_d}(x_j,1)=\underline r_{n_d}(x_j,0)=0
\qquad (j=1,\ldots,d)
\]
on \(\mathcal B_d\).  The lower-envelope Gibbs formula in \(\text{def:prediction-polytope-learner}\) then gives \(\widehat\pi=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\) at every context on this event.

Applying \(\text{lem:gibbs-regret-identity}\) on \(\mathcal B_d\), and using that all contexts have the same conditional policies, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\eta_0,P_d}(\pi_{P_d}^\star)-J_{\eta_0,P_d}(\widehat\pi)
&=\frac1{\eta_0}\operatorname{KL}\left((1/2,1/2)\,\middle\Vert\,(3/4,1/4)\right)\\
&=\frac1{4\log3}\left\{\frac12\log\frac23+\frac12\log2\right\}\\
&=\frac{\log(4/3)}{8\log3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Regularized regret is nonnegative everywhere because \(\pi_{P_d}^\star\) maximizes \(J_{\eta_0,P_d}\).  Hence
\[
\mathbb E_{P_d^{\otimes n_d}}\!\left[J_{\eta_0,P_d}(\pi_{P_d}^\star)-J_{\eta_0,P_d}(\widehat\pi)\right]
\ge \frac{\log(4/3)}{8\log3}\,P_d^{\otimes n_d}(\mathcal B_d)
=\frac{\log(4/3)}{8\log3}\{1-o(1)\}.
\]
Finally,
\[
\frac{dD_0}{n_d}=\frac{D_0}{d}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\sqrt{\frac{dD_0}{n_d}}=\sqrt{\frac{D_0}{d}}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and either convergence remains valid after multiplication by any fixed power of \(\log d\).  Thus neither displayed \(\widetilde O(dD/n)\) conversion can upper-bound the regret of this empirical-ball coordinatewise-envelope learner on these exact shells. \(\square\)
