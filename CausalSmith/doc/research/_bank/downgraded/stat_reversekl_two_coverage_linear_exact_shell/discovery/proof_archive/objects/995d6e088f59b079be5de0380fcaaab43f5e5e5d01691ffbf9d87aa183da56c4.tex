Let \(V=\operatorname{span}(\mathcal F)\), viewed as a linear subspace of \(\mathbb R^S\) with the supremum norm, and put \(r_0=\dim(V)\le d\). Since every member of \(\mathcal F\) takes values in \([0,1]\), \(\mathcal F\) lies in the unit ball of \(V\). For \(0<v<1\), a maximal \(v\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal F\) is a \(v\)-net. The open supremum-norm balls of radius \(v/2\) centered at its elements are disjoint and lie in the ball of radius \(1+v/2\). Comparing \(r_0\)-dimensional Lebesgue volumes on \(V\) gives
\[
N_{\mathcal F}(v)
\le\left(1+\frac2v\right)^{r_0}
\le\left(1+\frac2v\right)^d.
\]

We next control the dependence of \(H_{g,f}\) on its two arguments. Suppose \(\|g-g'\|_\infty\le v\), and define \(g_t=(1-t)g+tg'\). Differentiating the finite Gibbs formula gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\pi_{g_t}(a\mid x)
=\eta\pi_{g_t}(a\mid x)
\left[
(g'-g)(x,a)
-\sum_{b\in\mathcal A}\pi_{g_t}(b\mid x)(g'-g)(x,b)
\right].
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
\left|\frac{d}{dt}\pi_{g_t}(a\mid x)\right|
\le2\eta v,
\]
and integration over \(t\in[0,1]\) yields
\[
\|\pi_g(\cdot\mid x)-\pi_{g'}(\cdot\mid x)\|_1
\le2\eta v.
\]
If also \(\|f-f'\|_\infty\le v\), then \(g-f\) and \(g'-f'\) take values in \([-1,1]\), so
\[
\left|(g-f)^2-(g'-f')^2\right|
\le2\left|(g-f)-(g'-f')\right|
\le4v
\]
pointwise. Splitting the change in Gibbs weights from the change in squared contrasts consequently gives
\[
\|H_{g,f}-H_{g',f'}\|_\infty
\le(2\eta+4)v.
\]
Choose
\[
v=\{n(2\eta+4)\}^{-1}.
\]
The Cartesian square of a \(v\)-net of \(\mathcal F\) induces a \(1/n\)-net for
\[
\{H_{g,f}:g,f\in\mathcal F\},
\]
and its cardinality \(M\) satisfies
\[
M\le\{1+4n(\eta+2)\}^{2d}.
\]

It remains to prove a fixed-function deviation inequality. Let \(W_1,\ldots,W_n\) be independent random variables in \([0,1]\) with common mean \(\mu\). For \(|\lambda|<3\) and \(|w|\le1\), expansion of the exponential and the inequality \(k!\ge2\cdot3^{k-2}\) for \(k\ge2\) give
\[
e^{\lambda w}
\le1+\lambda w+
\frac{\lambda^2w^2}{2(1-|\lambda|/3)}.
\]
Apply this inequality to \(w=W_i-\mu\). Since
\[
\mathbb E(W_i-\mu)=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E(W_i-\mu)^2\le\mu,
\]
independence and \(1+s\le e^s\) give the corresponding Bernstein moment-generating-function bounds for both signs. Exponential Markov's inequality with the optimizing parameter then yields, for every \(t>0\),
\[
\Pr\left\{
\left|P_nW-\mu\right|
>\sqrt{\frac{2\mu t}{n}}+\frac{t}{3n}
\right\}
\le2e^{-t}.
\]
The inequality
\[
\sqrt{\frac{2\mu t}{n}}
\le\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac tn
\]
shows that, outside this one two-sided event,
\[
\mu\le2P_nW+\frac{8t}{3n},
\qquad
P_nW\le2\mu+\frac{8t}{3n}.
\]

Apply this event to each of the \(M\) net functions and set
\[
t=\log\frac{2M}{\zeta}.
\]
For each fixed net function the event containing both comparisons fails with probability at most \(2e^{-t}\). Thus the union bound gives all comparisons on the net simultaneously with probability at least
\[
1-2Me^{-t}=1-\zeta.
\]
For an arbitrary \(H_{g,f}\), choose a net function \(H_0\) satisfying
\[
\|H_{g,f}-H_0\|_\infty\le\frac1n.
\]
Replacing both the population and empirical means of \(H_0\) by those of \(H_{g,f}\) costs at most \(3/n\) in either comparison. Finally,
\[
t
\le\log\frac2\zeta
+2d\log\{1+4n(\eta+2)\}.
\]
Substitution proves, on one event of probability at least \(1-\zeta\), both asserted inequalities simultaneously for every \(g,f\in\mathcal F\).
