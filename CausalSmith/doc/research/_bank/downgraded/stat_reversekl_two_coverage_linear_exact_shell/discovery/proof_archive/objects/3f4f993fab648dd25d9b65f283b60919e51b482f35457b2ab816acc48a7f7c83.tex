Put \(E=e^\eta\), \(k=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor\), and define
\[
\alpha=\frac{E-D}{8(E-1)},\qquad p=\frac{\alpha}{D}.
\]
Because \(1<D<E\), one has \(0<\alpha<1/8\) and \(0<p<1/8\). Choose an orthonormal system \(e_0,(u_j,w_j)_{j=1}^k\), completed by a vector \(z\) when \(d\) is even, and use the action set \(\{+,-,0\}\). At each hard context \(x_j\), set
\[
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(+\mid x_j)=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(-\mid x_j)=p,
\qquad \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(0\mid x_j)=1-2p,
\]
\[
\phi(x_j,+)=\frac{u_j+w_j}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\phi(x_j,-)=\frac{u_j-w_j}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\phi(x_j,0)=0,
\]
and give each hard context raw mass \(1/k\). Add a calibration context \(x_C\) of raw mass one and put
\[
q=\frac{E/C-1}{E-1},\qquad
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(+\mid x_C)=q,\qquad
\phi(x_C,+)=e_0,\qquad
\phi(x_C,-)=\phi(x_C,0)=0,
\]
with the remaining reference probability distributed positively over \(-\) and \(0\). Since \(1<C<E\), \(q\in(0,1)\). If \(C>D\), add an anchor context \(x_A\), of raw mass
\[
a_{\mathrm{anc}}=q\frac{C-D}{D-1}>0,
\]
at which every action feature is \(e_0\), and give all actions positive reference probability. If \(C=D\), omit \(x_A\). If \(z\) is present, add a context of raw mass one at which every action feature is \(z\), again with positive reference probabilities. Normalize the raw masses and denote their sum by \(H\). Thus \(H\) is bounded above and below by positive constants depending only on \((C,D,\eta)\), uniformly in \(d\). The supports, feature map, reference policy, and \(\eta\) define a fixed public experiment \(\mathfrak E_1\); the normalized context law \(\rho\) is a law-specific component of the instances constructed below, not a component of \(\mathfrak E_1\).

For \(t=\eta\gamma\), define
\[
T_\gamma=\frac{D(1-2p)}{e^t-2\alpha\cosh t},
\qquad \beta_\gamma=\eta^{-1}\log T_\gamma.
\]
At \(\gamma=0\),
\[
T_0=\frac{D-2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}.
\]
The inequality \(T_0>1\) is equivalent to \(D>1\). Moreover \(T_0<E\), since
\[
2\alpha(E-1)=\frac{E-D}{4}<E-D.
\]
By continuity, there exist \(\gamma_0>0\) and \(m\in(0,1/2)\), depending only on \((D,\eta)\), such that whenever \(0\le\gamma\le\gamma_0\), the denominator defining \(T_\gamma\) is positive and
\[
0\le t\le1,\qquad
m\le\beta_\gamma-\gamma\le\beta_\gamma+\gamma\le1-m.
\]
For each \(v\in\{-1,1\}^k\), set the coefficient in the \(e_0\) direction equal to one, set
\[
\theta_{u_j}=\sqrt2\beta_\gamma,\qquad
\theta_{w_j}=\sqrt2\gamma v_j,
\]
and set the optional \(z\)-coefficient equal to zero. The hard-context reward means are then \(\beta_\gamma\pm\gamma v_j\), the reward means at \(x_C\) are one for action \(+\) and zero for the other actions, and all action-invariant contexts have a common reward across actions. Use Bernoulli rewards with these means. The preceding display puts every mean in \([0,1]\), and every feature has norm at most one.

We next verify exact-shell membership. At \(x_C\), the Gibbs normalizer equals
\[
qE+1-q=E/C.
\]
Thus the target-to-reference ratios of action \(+\) and of each zero-reward action are respectively \(C\) and \(C/E<1\). At a hard context, the Gibbs normalizer is
\[
Z=1-2p+2pT_\gamma\cosh t.
\]
Using \(pD=\alpha\), the defining equation for \(T_\gamma\) rearranges to
\[
T_\gamma e^t=DZ.
\]
Consequently the favored and unfavored informative actions have target-to-reference ratios \(D\) and \(De^{-2t}\). Since \(\beta_\gamma\ge\gamma\), one has \(T_\gamma e^t=e^{\eta(\beta_\gamma+\gamma)}\ge1\), and hence the zero-action ratio \(1/Z\le D\). All ratios at the action-invariant contexts equal one. Because \(D\le C\), the essential maximum is exactly \(C\), so \(C_P=C\).

In the basis \((u_j,w_j)\), the logging covariance block at \(x_j\) is a positive scalar multiple of \(pI_2\). The target probabilities of the favored and unfavored informative actions are
\[
pD=\alpha,\qquad pDe^{-2t}=\alpha e^{-2t}.
\]
Therefore the two generalized eigenvalues on this block are \(D\) and \(De^{-2t}\). On the \(e_0\) block, the generalized ratio is \(D\): it is \(C=D\) when the anchor is absent, while when the anchor is present it is
\[
\frac{qC+a_{\mathrm{anc}}}{q+a_{\mathrm{anc}}}=D.
\]
The optional \(z\) block has ratio one. Every basis direction receives positive logging mass, so \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}\succ0\), and the largest generalized eigenvalue is exactly \(D\). Hence every sign-indexed law belongs to the same nonempty shell \(\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E_1}(d,C,D,\eta)\).

It remains to establish the testing lower bound. Conditional on a hard context, the target action laws under opposite signs in coordinate \(j\) are
\[
s_+=(\alpha,\alpha e^{-2t},1-\alpha-\alpha e^{-2t}),
\qquad
s_-=(\alpha e^{-2t},\alpha,1-\alpha-\alpha e^{-2t}).
\]
For any action law \(u\), normalizing the coordinatewise geometric mean of \(s_+\) and \(s_-\) gives the identity
\[
\inf_u\{\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_+)+\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_-)\}
=-2\log\sum_a\sqrt{s_+(a)s_-(a)}.
\]
Here
\[
\sum_a\sqrt{s_+(a)s_-(a)}=1-\alpha(1-e^{-t})^2.
\]
For \(0\le t\le1\), the inequalities \(1-e^{-t}\ge t/2\) and \(-\log(1-y)\ge y\) imply
\[
\inf_u\eta^{-1}\{\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_+)+\operatorname{KL}(u\Vert s_-)\}
\ge\frac{\alpha t^2}{2\eta}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:gibbs-regret-identity}\), this is a two-regret separation for any policy selected from the data.

Neighboring sign vectors differ only in the two Bernoulli means at one hard context. For \(r,s\in[m,1-m]\), \(\log x\le x-1\) yields
\[
\operatorname{kl}(r\Vert s)\le\frac{(r-s)^2}{s(1-s)}.
\]
Because the context probability is \(1/(Hk)\) and each informative action has logging probability \(p\), the one-observation Kullback--Leibler divergence between neighboring laws is at most
\[
\frac{8p\gamma^2}{Hk\,m(1-m)}.
\]
Product additivity and Pinsker's inequality therefore give total variation at most \(1/2\) whenever
\[
n\le c_1(C,D,\eta)\frac{k}{p\gamma^2}
\]
for a positive constant \(c_1(C,D,\eta)\).

If nonnegative losses \(L_+,L_-\) satisfy \(L_+(a)+L_-(a)\ge s\) pointwise, integration against the smaller of the two data densities gives
\[
\mathbb E_+L_++\mathbb E_-L_-
\ge s\{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_+,P_-)\}.
\]
Apply this inequality to every neighboring pair and average over the uniform prior on \(\{-1,1\}^k\). Each hard context has probability \(1/(Hk)\); summing over the \(k\) coordinates and using total variation at most \(1/2\) shows that the Bayes regularized regret of every learner is at least
\[
\frac{1}{4H}\frac{\alpha t^2}{2\eta}
=\frac{\alpha t^2}{8H\eta}.
\]
Choose
\[
t^2=16H\eta\epsilon/\alpha.
\]
There is a positive \(\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)\) such that \(t\le1\) and \(\gamma=t/\eta\le\gamma_0\) for every \(0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)\). The Bayes regret is then at least \(2\epsilon\), while
\[
p\gamma^2
=\frac{\alpha}{D}\frac{t^2}{\eta^2}
=\frac{16H\epsilon}{D\eta}.
\]
Since \(k=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor\ge d/4\) for \(d\ge4\), the testing condition holds throughout
\[
n\le c_2(C,D,\eta)\frac{dD\eta}{\epsilon}
\]
for a positive constant \(c_2(C,D,\eta)\). The minimax risk over the full shell is at least the Bayes risk over this finite subfamily. Thus it exceeds \(\epsilon\) throughout the displayed sample-size range. After decreasing the constant and \(\epsilon_0(C,D,\eta)\) to absorb integer rounding, \(\text{def:sample-complexity}\) gives
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E_1}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\ge c(C,D,\eta)\frac{dD\eta}{\epsilon}.
\]
The only coverage restriction used is \(D<e^\eta\); in particular, no inequality \(2D-1<e^\eta\) was used. The constants depend on \((C,D,\eta)\), so the conclusion is pointwise local in those indices.
