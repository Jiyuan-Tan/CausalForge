The displayed construction is explicit. Since \(\mathcal Q_n\) is nonempty and compact, \((f,g)\mapsto\widehat V_n(f,g)\) is continuous, and Gibbs normalization has a strictly positive denominator, the inner maximum and outer minimum are attained. The stipulated sequential lexicographic rule makes the selected prediction vector measurable in \((\mathcal D_n,\mathfrak E)\), exactly as for the selections in \(\text{def:prediction-polytope-learner}\). Equivalently, one may carry out the inner and outer continuous optimizations to successively vanishing rational tolerances and take the first lexicographic limit point. Thus \(\widehat\pi_n^\dagger\in\mathcal L_n(\mathfrak E)\). Its formula contains \(\mathcal D_n\), \(\mathfrak E\), and the fixed constant \(c_0=64\), but not \(C\) or \(D\).

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)\), and abbreviate the true reward by \(r=r_\theta\). By \(\text{lem:bounded-linear-localization}\), there is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\), of probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\), on which
\[
r\in\mathcal Q_n,
\qquad
\sup_{g\in\mathcal Q_n}\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(g-r)^2\le R_n.
\]
Apply \(\text{lem:uniform-gibbs-square-comparison}\) with \(\zeta=\delta_n/2\). There is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{ctx}}\), of probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\), on which, simultaneously for every \(f,g\in\mathcal F\),
\[
V(f,g):=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_f}(f-g)^2
\le2\widehat V_n(f,g)+\alpha_n,
\qquad
\widehat V_n(f,g)\le2V(f,g)+\alpha_n.
\]
No independence between these two events is required. Their intersection \(\mathcal E_n=\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\cap\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{ctx}}\) has probability at least \(1-\delta_n\).

Work on \(\mathcal E_n\). For every \(g\in\mathcal Q_n\), the contrast \(g-r\) lies in \(\operatorname{span}(\phi)\). Since \(\pi_r=\pi_P^\star\), \(D_P=D\), and \(\text{lem:feature-coverage-domination}\) applies,
\[
V(r,g)=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(g-r)^2
\le D\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(g-r)^2
\le DR_n.
\]
The reverse half of the uniform comparison therefore gives
\[
\widehat s_n(r)=\max_{g\in\mathcal Q_n}\widehat V_n(r,g)
\le2DR_n+\alpha_n.
\]
Because \(r\in\mathcal Q_n\) and \(f_n^\dagger\) minimizes \(\widehat s_n\) over \(\mathcal Q_n\),
\[
\widehat V_n(f_n^\dagger,r)
\le\widehat s_n(f_n^\dagger)
\le\widehat s_n(r)
\le2DR_n+\alpha_n.
\]
The forward half of the uniform comparison now yields
\[
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{f_n^\dagger}}(f_n^\dagger-r)^2
\le2\widehat V_n(f_n^\dagger,r)+\alpha_n
\le4DR_n+3\alpha_n
\le D B_n,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(D\ge1\), as supplied for every nonempty shell by \(\text{thm:feasible-index-region}\). The true-policy square error also obeys
\[
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(f_n^\dagger-r)^2
\le DR_n\le DB_n.
\]
Thus the adaptive center has controlled error under both endpoint Gibbs policies, although neither coverage index was given to the learner.

Invoke \(\text{lem:symmetric-gibbs-feature-conversion}\) with \(g=f_n^\dagger\). The two preceding displays give, on \(\mathcal E_n\),
\[
J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_n^\dagger)
\le\min\{\eta DB_n,2\sqrt{DB_n}\}.
\]
The same lemma gives the deterministic bound \(J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_n^\dagger)\le\min\{2,\eta/2\}\) on the whole sample space. Averaging over \(\mathcal E_n\) and its complement proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_n^\dagger)\right]
\le\min\{\eta DB_n,2\sqrt{DB_n}\}+2\delta_n.
\]
The deterministic bound also holds after expectation, so taking the smaller of the two bounds proves the asserted finite-sample inequality. All constants are uniform in the member \(P\).

It remains to invert the bound. Put
\[
b_n=d\log\{12en(1+\eta)\}+\log(8en^2).
\]
The elementary inequalities
\[
a_n\le b_n,
\qquad
1+\log(8n^2)+2d\log\{1+4n(2+\eta)\}\le2b_n
\]
imply
\[
B_n\le2500\frac{b_n}{n}.
\]
If \(\eta\le2\epsilon\), the deterministic \(\eta/2\) bound already makes the risk at most \(\epsilon\). Suppose \(\eta>2\epsilon\), and write
\[
M=dD\min\{\eta/\epsilon,\epsilon^{-2}\}.
\]
If \(\eta\le\epsilon^{-1}\), choose \(n\), up to a sufficiently large universal constant and integer rounding, of order
\[
dD\frac\eta\epsilon
\]
times \(\log\{e+(1+\eta)dD\eta/\epsilon^2\}\). Then \(b_n=O(d\log\{e+(1+\eta)n\})\), so \(2500\eta D b_n/n\le\epsilon/2\). If \(\eta>\epsilon^{-1}\), choose \(n\), again up to a universal constant and the same type of logarithmic factor, of order
\[
dD\epsilon^{-2}.
\]
Then \(2\sqrt{2500D b_n/n}\le\epsilon/2\). In the first case \(\eta>2\epsilon\) implies \(M\ge2dD\), and in the second case \(M\ge dD/\epsilon^2\); hence, after increasing the same universal constant, \(2n^{-2}\le\epsilon/2\) in both cases. Therefore \(\text{def:minimax-risk}\) and \(\text{def:sample-complexity}\) give
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le\widetilde O\!\left(dD\min\{\eta/\epsilon,\epsilon^{-2}\}\right)
\]
whenever the underlying displayed order is at least two. The logarithmic factor just displayed is absorbed by \(\widetilde O\), and its multiplicative constant is universal. This proves the affirmative resolution.
