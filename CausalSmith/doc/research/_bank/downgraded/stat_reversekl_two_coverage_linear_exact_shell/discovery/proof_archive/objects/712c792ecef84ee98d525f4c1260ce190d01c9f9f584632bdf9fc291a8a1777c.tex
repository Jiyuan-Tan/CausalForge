Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\), and restrict the action distributions to
\[
\mathcal A_x^+=\{a\in\mathcal A:\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid x)>0\}.
\]
This set is nonempty. Write \(p=\pi_r(\cdot\mid x)\), \(q=\pi_g(\cdot\mid x)\), and \(\Delta(a)=g(x,a)-r(x,a)\). Both \(p\) and \(q\) are strictly positive on \(\mathcal A_x^+\). Their Gibbs formulas imply
\[
\frac{q(a)}{p(a)}
=\frac{e^{\eta\Delta(a)}}{\mathbb E_p e^{\eta\Delta}},
\qquad
\log\frac{q(a)}{p(a)}
=\eta\{\Delta(a)-c\},
\qquad
c=\eta^{-1}\log\mathbb E_p e^{\eta\Delta}.
\]
Let \(m_p=\mathbb E_p\Delta\) and \(m_q=\mathbb E_q\Delta\). For
\[
\psi(t)=\log\mathbb E_p e^{t\Delta},
\]
finite support makes \(\psi\) differentiable and convex, while exponential tilting gives \(\psi'(0)=m_p\) and \(\psi'(\eta)=m_q\). The secant-slope inequalities for a differentiable convex function therefore give
\[
m_p=\psi'(0)
\le\frac{\psi(\eta)-\psi(0)}{\eta}
=c
\le\psi'(\eta)=m_q.
\]
Substitution of the Gibbs formula into the conditional regularized welfare gives the conditional Gibbs identity
\[
J_{\eta,r,x}(p)-J_{\eta,r,x}(q)
=\eta^{-1}\operatorname{KL}(q\Vert p)
=m_q-c.
\]
Consequently,
\[
J_{\eta,r,x}(p)-J_{\eta,r,x}(q)
\le m_q-m_p
\le |m_q|+|m_p|
\le\sqrt{\mathbb E_q\Delta^2}+\sqrt{\mathbb E_p\Delta^2}.
\]
This is the conditional slow inequality.

For the fast inequality, first observe that, for all \(u,v>0\),
\[
(u-v)\log(u/v)
\le\frac{u+v}{2}\{\log(u/v)\}^2.
\]
Indeed, after writing \(u/v=e^t\), this is equivalent to
\[
2\tanh(|t|/2)\le |t|.
\]
The difference between the right- and left-hand sides vanishes at zero and has derivative \(\tanh^2(s/2)\ge0\) at \(s=|t|\). Apply the scalar inequality action by action with \(u=q(a)\) and \(v=p(a)\), and use the displayed log-likelihood ratio. The conditional Jeffreys divergence then satisfies
\[
\operatorname{KL}(q\Vert p)+\operatorname{KL}(p\Vert q)
\le\frac{\eta^2}{2}
\left\{\mathbb E_p(\Delta-c)^2+\mathbb E_q(\Delta-c)^2\right\}.
\]
Define
\[
S(t)=\mathbb E_p(\Delta-t)^2+\mathbb E_q(\Delta-t)^2.
\]
The function \(S\) is convex. Since \(c\in[m_p,m_q]\), its maximum on this interval is attained at an endpoint, and hence
\[
S(c)\le\max\{S(m_p),S(m_q)\}.
\]
Direct expansion at either endpoint gives
\[
S(m_p)=S(m_q)
=\mathbb E_p\Delta^2+\mathbb E_q\Delta^2-2m_pm_q.
\]
The elementary inequality
\[
-2m_pm_q\le m_p^2+m_q^2
\]
and Jensen's inequality under \(p\) and \(q\) imply
\[
S(c)
\le2\left\{\mathbb E_p\Delta^2+\mathbb E_q\Delta^2\right\}.
\]
Since \(\operatorname{KL}(p\Vert q)\ge0\), the conditional Gibbs identity and the last two displays yield
\[
J_{\eta,r,x}(p)-J_{\eta,r,x}(q)
\le\eta\left\{\mathbb E_p\Delta^2+\mathbb E_q\Delta^2\right\}.
\]
Average both conditional inequalities over \(X\sim\rho\). The fast inequality becomes
\[
J_{\eta,r}(\pi_r)-J_{\eta,r}(\pi_g)\le\eta(A+B).
\]
For the slow inequality, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\mathbb E_\rho
\sqrt{\mathbb E_{\pi_r(\cdot\mid X)}\Delta^2}
\le\sqrt A,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\rho
\sqrt{\mathbb E_{\pi_g(\cdot\mid X)}\Delta^2}
\le\sqrt B.
\]
Thus the regret is also at most \(\sqrt A+\sqrt B\). Taking the smaller of the two valid bounds proves the sharpened statement.
