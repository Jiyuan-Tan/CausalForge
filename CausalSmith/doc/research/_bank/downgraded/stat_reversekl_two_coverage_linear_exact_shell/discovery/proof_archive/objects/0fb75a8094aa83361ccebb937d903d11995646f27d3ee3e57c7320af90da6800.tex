The learner is exactly the one in \(\text{def:erm-gibbs-plugin-learner}\), so its measurability and its independence from \(C\) and \(D\) follow from \(\text{thm:erm-plugin-upper}\). For \(0<\eta\le1\),
\[
\kappa_\eta
=\eta\int_0^1 t e^{2\eta t}\,dt
\le \eta e^2\int_0^1t\,dt
=\frac{e^2}{2}\eta,
\]
and
\[
(1+e^{2\eta})^2\le(1+e^2)^2.
\]
For nonnegative \(x,y\) and positive \(a,b\),
\[
\min\{ax,by\}\le\max\{a,b\}\min\{x,y\}.
\]
Apply this elementary inequality with \(x=\eta/\epsilon\), \(y=\epsilon^{-2}\), \(a=e^2/2\), and \(b=(1+e^2)^2\) to the sample-complexity conclusion of \(\text{thm:erm-plugin-upper}\). The logarithmic factors are unchanged, and \(\max\{e^2/2,(1+e^2)^2\}\) is a universal numerical constant. This proves the claim in the sample-size range stated there.
