We first verify that the learner is a measurable, data-only map. Regard \(\mathcal F\) as a compact subset of the Euclidean space \(\mathbb R^S\), with the coordinates ordered as fixed in \(\text{def:prediction-polytope-learner}\), and let \(z\) denote a realized sample. The loss \((z,f)\mapsto L_n(z,f)\) is Borel in \(z\) and continuous in \(f\). Apply \(\text{lem:measurable-maximum-theorem}\) to the constant correspondence \(z\mapsto\mathcal F\) and the objective \(-L_n\). Its argmax correspondence is nonempty, compact valued, and measurable. Recursively minimizing the first coordinate, then the second, and so on, applies the same theorem at each step to a continuous coordinate functional on the preceding measurable compact-valued correspondence. After the finitely many coordinates in \(S\) have been fixed, the correspondence is a singleton. Thus the sequentially lexicographic empirical-risk minimizer \(z\mapsto\widehat f_z\) is Borel measurable.

Put
\[
\Gamma(z)=\mathcal Q_n(z)
=\left\{f\in\mathcal F:\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{f(X_i,A_i)-\widehat f_z(X_i,A_i)\}^2\le64\frac{a_n}{n}\right\}.
\]
This correspondence is nonempty and compact valued. It is weakly measurable. Indeed, if \(O\) is open in \(\mathcal F\), compactness and metrizability give compact sets \(K_m\subset O\) with \(O=\bigcup_mK_m\). The event \(\Gamma(z)\cap O\ne\varnothing\) is the union over \(m\) of
\[
\left\{z:\min_{f\in K_m}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{f(X_i,A_i)-\widehat f_z(X_i,A_i)\}^2\le64\frac{a_n}{n}\right\}.
\]
Each displayed minimum is measurable by \(\text{lem:measurable-maximum-theorem}\) applied to the fixed compact set \(K_m\); empty \(K_m\) may simply be omitted. Hence every lower inverse of an open set is Borel.

For fixed \(z\), the map
\[
(g,f)\longmapsto P_nH_{g,f}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nH_{g,f}(X_i)
\]
is continuous on \(\mathcal F^2\), because the action support is finite and every Gibbs normalizer is strictly positive. The measurable maximum theorem, now with base point \((z,g)\), correspondence \(\Gamma(z)\), and objective \(P_nH_{g,f}\), shows that
\[
(z,g)\longmapsto\widehat U_n(z,g)=\max_{f\in\Gamma(z)}P_nH_{g,f}
\]
is Borel measurable. For each fixed \(z\), it is continuous in \(g\): uniform continuity of \((g,f)\mapsto P_nH_{g,f}\) on compact \(\mathcal F^2\) implies that the supremum over \(f\in\Gamma(z)\) preserves continuity. A further application of the theorem to \(-\widehat U_n\) over \(g\in\Gamma(z)\) makes the minimizing correspondence nonempty, compact valued, and measurable. Recursive measurable coordinate minimization therefore produces the stipulated Borel lexicographic selector \(z\mapsto\widetilde g_n(z)\). The finite Gibbs formula is continuous in its reward-vector input, so \(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}}=\pi_{\widetilde g_n}\) is measurable. Its construction uses only \((\mathcal D_n,\mathfrak E)\); in particular, it uses neither \(\rho\), \(C\), nor \(D\). Thus \(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}}\in\mathcal L_n(\mathfrak E)\).

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)\), write \(r=r_\theta\), and set
\[
R_n=600\frac{a_n}{n}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:bounded-linear-localization}\), there is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\) of probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\) on which
\[
r\in\mathcal Q_n,
\qquad
\sup_{f\in\mathcal Q_n}\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(f-r)^2\le R_n.
\]
Apply the proposed sharpened \(\text{lem:uniform-gibbs-radius-comparison}\) with \(\zeta=\delta_n/2\). Since \(\delta_n=n^{-2}\), there is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{cmp}}\), of probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\), on which, simultaneously for all \(g,f\in\mathcal F\),
\[
P_\rho H_{g,f}\le2P_nH_{g,f}+b_n,
\qquad
P_nH_{g,f}\le2P_\rho H_{g,f}+b_n,
\]
where
\[
b_n=\frac{8}{3n}\left[\log(4n^2)+2d\log\{1+4n(\eta+2)\}\right]+\frac3n.
\]
These inequalities hold over the deterministic class indexed by all of \(\mathcal F^2\); hence their simultaneous use remains valid although \(\mathcal Q_n\) and \(\widetilde g_n\) use the same sample.

Work on \(\mathcal E_n=\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\cap\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{cmp}}\), whose probability is at least \(1-\delta_n\). For every \(f\in\mathcal Q_n\), the contrast \(f-r\) belongs to \(\operatorname{span}(\phi)\). The feature-coverage domination lemma and \(D_P=D\) give
\[
P_\rho H_{r,f}
=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(r-f)^2
\le D\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(r-f)^2
\le DR_n.
\]
Thus, because \(r\in\mathcal Q_n\),
\[
\widehat U_n(r)
\le2\sup_{f\in\mathcal Q_n}P_\rho H_{r,f}+b_n
\le2DR_n+b_n.
\]
The defining minimality of \(\widetilde g_n\) yields \(\widehat U_n(\widetilde g_n)\le\widehat U_n(r)\). Since \(r\in\mathcal Q_n\), the reverse comparison then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\widetilde g_n}}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
&=P_\rho H_{\widetilde g_n,r}\\
&\le2P_nH_{\widetilde g_n,r}+b_n\\
&\le2\widehat U_n(\widetilde g_n)+b_n\\
&\le4DR_n+3b_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
At the true Gibbs endpoint, another application of feature domination and localization gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
\le D\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
\le DR_n.
\]
Define
\[
A_n=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2,
\qquad
B_n=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\widetilde g_n}}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2.
\]
Every nonempty shell has \(D\ge1\) by \(\text{thm:feasible-index-region}\), and hence
\[
A_n+B_n\le5DR_n+3b_n
\le D(5R_n+3b_n)=V_n.
\]
Apply the proposed sharpened \(\text{lem:two-endpoint-gibbs-conversion}\) with \(g=\widetilde g_n\). On \(\mathcal E_n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}})
&\le\min\{\eta(A_n+B_n),\sqrt{A_n}+\sqrt{B_n}\}\\
&\le\min\{\eta V_n,\sqrt{2V_n}\},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last step uses \((\sqrt{A_n}+\sqrt{B_n})^2\le2(A_n+B_n)\).

For the complement, take arbitrary \(r,g:S\to[0,1]\), put \(\Delta=g-r\), and let \(\pi_t\) be the Gibbs policy generated by \(r+t\Delta\). If
\[
F(t)=\eta^{-1}\mathbb E_\rho\log\sum_a\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid X)e^{\eta(r+t\Delta)(X,a)},
\]
direct differentiation gives \(F''(t)=\eta\mathbb E_\rho\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta)\). The Gibbs variational identity and integration by parts therefore yield
\[
J_{\eta,r}(\pi_r)-J_{\eta,r}(\pi_g)
=F'(1)-\{F(1)-F(0)\}
=\eta\int_0^1t\,\mathbb E_\rho\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta)\,dt
\le\eta/2,
\]
because \(\Delta\in[-1,1]\) implies \(\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta)\le1\). The slow part of \(\text{lem:two-endpoint-gibbs-conversion}\), together with \(|\Delta|\le1\), also bounds this regret by two. Since \(P^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_n^c)\le\delta_n\), averaging the good- and bad-event bounds proves, uniformly over the exact shell,
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left[J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}})\right]
\le\min\{\eta V_n,\sqrt{2V_n}\}
+\delta_n\min\{2,\eta/2\}.
\]

We finish with an explicit inversion. For \(d\ge4\) and \(n\ge1\), elementary estimates give
\[
a_n\le2d\log(en),
\qquad
\log(4n^2)\le d\log(en),
\]
\[
\log\{1+4n(\eta+2)\}
\le\log8+\log\{1+n(1+\eta)\},
\qquad
9\le3d\log(en).
\]
Substitution in the definitions of \(b_n\) and \(V_n\) yields the fully numerical bound
\[
V_n
\le6100\frac{dD}{n}
\left[\log(en)+\log\{1+n(1+\eta)\}\right].
\tag{1}
\]
If \(\eta\le2\epsilon\), the deterministic regret bound \(\eta/2\le\epsilon\) applies at \(n=1\). Thus \(N^\star_{\mathfrak E}=1\).

Suppose now that \(\eta>2\epsilon\), and define
\[
q_\epsilon=dD\min\{\eta/\epsilon,\epsilon^{-2}\},
\qquad
\ell_\epsilon=1+\log(eq_\epsilon)+\log\{1+(1+\eta)q_\epsilon\}.
\]
By \(D\ge1\), \(d\ge4\), \(\epsilon<1\), and \(\eta>2\epsilon\), one has \(q_\epsilon>4\) and \(\ell_\epsilon>1\). Put \(K_0=10^8\) and
\[
n_0=\left\lceil K_0q_\epsilon\ell_\epsilon\right\rceil.
\]
Integer rounding gives
\[
K_0q_\epsilon\ell_\epsilon\le n_0\le2K_0q_\epsilon\ell_\epsilon.
\]
Writing \(s=(1+\eta)q_\epsilon\), the definitions of \(\ell_\epsilon\) and the inequalities \(\log\ell_\epsilon\le\ell_\epsilon\) and \(s\ge1\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log(en_0)
&\le\{\log(2eK_0)+2\}\ell_\epsilon,\\
\log\{1+n_0(1+\eta)\}
&\le\{\log(3K_0)+2\}\ell_\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
\log(en_0)+\log\{1+n_0(1+\eta)\}
\le c_{K_0}\ell_\epsilon,
\qquad
c_{K_0}=4+\log(6eK_0^2)<44.
\]
Applying \((1)\) at \(n_0\) gives
\[
V_{n_0}\le\alpha\frac{dD}{q_\epsilon},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{6100c_{K_0}}{K_0}<\frac18.
\]
If the minimum defining \(q_\epsilon\) is \(\eta/\epsilon\), then \(\eta V_{n_0}\le\alpha\epsilon\le\epsilon/8\). If it is \(\epsilon^{-2}\), then \(\sqrt{2V_{n_0}}\le\sqrt{2\alpha}\,\epsilon\le\epsilon/2\). Thus the good-event term is at most \(\epsilon/2\) in either case.

The complement term is also explicit. In the first case, \(n_0\ge q_\epsilon\ge4\eta/\epsilon\), so
\[
n_0^{-2}\min\{2,\eta/2\}
\le\frac{\epsilon^2}{32\eta}<\frac\epsilon{64}.
\]
In the second case, \(n_0\ge q_\epsilon\ge4\epsilon^{-2}\), so
\[
n_0^{-2}\min\{2,\eta/2\}
\le\frac{\epsilon^4}{8}\le\frac\epsilon8.
\]
Therefore the risk of the same stabilized learner at \(n_0\) is at most \(\epsilon\), including integer rounding and the \(n^{-2}\) complement. By \(\text{def:minimax-risk}\) and \(\text{def:sample-complexity}\),
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le n_0
=\left\lceil10^8q_\epsilon
\left[1+\log(eq_\epsilon)+\log\{1+(1+\eta)q_\epsilon\}\right]\right\rceil.
\]
The two displayed logarithms are precisely the factors suppressed by \(\widetilde O\). Hence, whenever the displayed order is at least two,
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le\widetilde O\left(dD\min\{\eta/\epsilon,\epsilon^{-2}\}\right)
\]
with universal multiplicative constants, for every \(\eta>0\). This proves the proposed sharpened theorem.
