Order the coordinates of \(\mathbb R^{|S|}\) by the cells \((x,a)\in S\). Under \(\phi(x,a)=e_{(x,a)}\), the covariance definitions give
\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}
=\operatorname{diag}_{(x,a)\in S}\!\left\{\rho(x)\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid x)\right\},
\qquad
\Lambda_P^\star
=\operatorname{diag}_{(x,a)\in S}\!\left\{\rho(x)\pi_P^\star(a\mid x)\right\}.
\]
By \(\text{ass:nonsingular-logging-geometry}\), \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}\succ0\). Since this matrix is diagonal, \(\rho(x)\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid x)>0\) for every \((x,a)\in S\). Its inverse square root is therefore defined coordinatewise, and direct multiplication yields
\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1/2}\Lambda_P^\star\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1/2}
=\operatorname{diag}_{(x,a)\in S}\!\left\{
\frac{\rho(x)\pi_P^\star(a\mid x)}{\rho(x)\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid x)}
\right\}
=\operatorname{diag}_{(x,a)\in S}\!\left\{
\frac{\pi_P^\star(a\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid x)}
\right\}.
\]
The largest eigenvalue of a real diagonal matrix is its largest diagonal entry. Moreover, because \(S\) is finite and every cell has strictly positive probability under \(\rho\times\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\), the essential supremum in the definition of \(C_P\) is the ordinary maximum over \(S\). Consequently,
\[
D_P
=\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1/2}\Lambda_P^\star\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1/2}\right)
=\max_{(x,a)\in S}\frac{\pi_P^\star(a\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid x)}
=C_P.
\]
Thus, in \(\text{def:exact-shell}\), the restrictions \(C_P=C\) and \(D_P=C\) coincide under canonical tabular features. Hence \(\mathcal M(d,C,C,\eta)\) is exactly the canonical tabular reverse-KL shell expressed in the shared-linear notation.
