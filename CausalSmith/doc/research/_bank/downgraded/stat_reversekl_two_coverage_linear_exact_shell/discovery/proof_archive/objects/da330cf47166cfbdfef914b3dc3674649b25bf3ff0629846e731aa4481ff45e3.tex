The construction is data driven. The set \(\mathcal Q_n\) is a nonempty compact subset of the finite-dimensional compact polytope \(\mathcal F\). Because the action support is finite, \((g,f)\mapsto n^{-1}\sum_iH_{g,f}(X_i)\) is continuous. Maximization over \(f\in\mathcal Q_n\) therefore makes \(\widehat U_n\) continuous on \(\mathcal Q_n\), so its minimum is attained. Regard functions in \(\mathcal F\) as vectors in \(\mathbb R^S\). The graph of \(\mathcal Q_n\), the graph of the minimizing correspondence, and the successive coordinate-minimizing sets used by the fixed lexicographic rule are Borel: each is described by finitely many continuous inequalities and minima over compact sets, and each minimum can equivalently be tested on a countable dense subset. The resulting selector \(\widetilde g_n\), its finite collection of exponentials, and its strictly positive Gibbs normalizers are measurable in \((\mathcal D_n,\mathfrak E)\). Hence \(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}}\in\mathcal L_n(\mathfrak E)\). Its formula contains neither shell index.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathfrak E}(d,C,D,\eta)\), write \(r=r_\theta\), and set
\[
R_n=600\frac{a_n}{n}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:bounded-linear-localization}\), there is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\) of probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\) on which
\[
r\in\mathcal Q_n,
\qquad
\sup_{f\in\mathcal Q_n}\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(f-r)^2\le R_n.
\]
Apply \(\text{lem:uniform-gibbs-radius-comparison}\) with \(\zeta=\delta_n/2\). There is an event \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{cmp}}\), of probability at least \(1-\delta_n/2\), on which, simultaneously for all \(g,f\in\mathcal F\),
\[
P_\rho H_{g,f}\le2P_nH_{g,f}+b_n,
\qquad
P_nH_{g,f}\le2P_\rho H_{g,f}+b_n,
\]
where \(P_\rho h=\mathbb E_{X\sim\rho}h(X)\) and \(P_nh=n^{-1}\sum_ih(X_i)\). These inequalities hold over the deterministic class indexed by all of \(\mathcal F^2\); hence their simultaneous use remains valid although \(\mathcal Q_n\) and \(\widetilde g_n\) use the same sample.

Work on \(\mathcal E_n=\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\cap\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{cmp}}\), whose probability is at least \(1-\delta_n\). For every \(f\in\mathcal Q_n\), the contrast \(f-r\) belongs to \(\operatorname{span}(\phi)\). The feature-coverage domination lemma and \(D_P=D\) give
\[
P_\rho H_{r,f}
=\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(r-f)^2
\le D\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(r-f)^2
\le DR_n.
\]
Thus, because \(r\in\mathcal Q_n\),
\[
\widehat U_n(r)
\le2\sup_{f\in\mathcal Q_n}P_\rho H_{r,f}+b_n
\le2DR_n+b_n.
\]
The defining minimality of \(\widetilde g_n\) yields \(\widehat U_n(\widetilde g_n)\le\widehat U_n(r)\). Since \(r\in\mathcal Q_n\), the reverse comparison then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\widetilde g_n}}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
&=P_\rho H_{\widetilde g_n,r}\\
&\le2P_nH_{\widetilde g_n,r}+b_n\\
&\le2\widehat U_n(\widetilde g_n)+b_n\\
&\le4DR_n+3b_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
At the true Gibbs endpoint, another application of feature domination and localization gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_P^\star}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
\le D\mathbb E_{\rho,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(\widetilde g_n-r)^2
\le DR_n.
\]
Let \(A_n\) and \(B_n\) denote the last two endpoint squared errors. Since every nonempty shell has \(D\ge1\) by \(\text{thm:feasible-index-region}\),
\[
A_n+B_n\le5DR_n+3b_n
\le D(5R_n+3b_n)=V_n.
\]
Apply \(\text{lem:two-endpoint-gibbs-conversion}\) with \(g=\widetilde g_n\). On \(\mathcal E_n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\eta,P}(\pi_P^\star)-J_{\eta,P}(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{stab}})
&\le\min\{3\eta(A_n+B_n),\sqrt{A_n}+\sqrt{B_n}\}\\
&\le\min\{3\eta V_n,\sqrt{2V_n}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the complement, take arbitrary \(r,g:S\to[0,1]\), put \(\Delta=g-r\), and let \(\pi_t\) be the Gibbs policy generated by \(r+t\Delta\). If \(A(t)=\eta^{-1}\mathbb E_\rho\log\sum_a\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a\mid X)e^{\eta(r+t\Delta)(X,a)}\), direct differentiation gives \(A''(t)=\eta\mathbb E_\rho\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta)\). The Gibbs variational identity therefore yields
\[
J_{\eta,r}(\pi_r)-J_{\eta,r}(\pi_g)
=\eta\int_0^1t\,\mathbb E_\rho\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}(\Delta)\,dt
\le\eta/2.
\]
The slow part of \(\text{lem:two-endpoint-gibbs-conversion}\), together with \(|\Delta|\le1\), also bounds this regret by two. Since \(P^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_n^c)\le\delta_n\), averaging the good- and bad-event bounds proves the displayed uniform risk inequality.

It remains to invert the risk bound. From the definitions of \(a_n\) and \(b_n\),
\[
V_n\le K\frac{dD}{n}\left[\log(en)+\log\{1+n(1+\eta)\}\right]
\]
for a universal \(K\). If \(\eta\le2\epsilon\), the deterministic \(\eta/2\) bound already gives risk at most \(\epsilon\) at sample size one. Otherwise, choosing \(n\), up to the displayed logarithms and integer rounding, of order \(\eta dD/\epsilon\) makes \(3\eta V_n\) a fixed fraction of \(\epsilon\); choosing it of order \(dD/\epsilon^2\) instead makes \(\sqrt{2V_n}\) a fixed fraction of \(\epsilon\). In either case, enlarging the universal constant absorbs \(\delta_n\min\{2,\eta/2\}\): on the fast branch use \(n\gtrsim\eta/\epsilon\), and on the slow branch use \(n\gtrsim\epsilon^{-2}\). The same learner is used on both branches, so one may take the smaller sufficient order. By \(\text{def:minimax-risk}\) and \(\text{def:sample-complexity}\),
\[
N^\star_{\mathfrak E}(\epsilon;d,C,D,\eta)
\le\widetilde O\left(dD\min\{\eta/\epsilon,\epsilon^{-2}\}\right)
\]
whenever that order is at least two. All multiplicative constants are universal; the suppressed factors are logarithmic. This proves the positive answer.
