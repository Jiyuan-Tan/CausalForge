By the three-mode lemma, the worst-case risk of every \(\delta_{p,A,D}\) is the value of the two-multiplier epigraph at its rank-one moment matrices.  Conversely, rank-one purification converts every feasible SDP optimum into such coefficients without increasing risk.  Taking the infimum over the orbit simplex and the coefficients therefore proves
\[
V_{b,s}=inf_{p,A,D}\sup_{u\in\Theta_{b,s}}
\mathbb E\!\left[\left\{\delta_{p,A,D}-\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}(Y)\right\}^2\right].
\]
The randomization in the definition of \(\delta_{p,A,D}\) is a probability law on binary assignments: it draws \(c\), then \(C\), then one label in every block outside \(C\), and applies the corresponding blockwise assignment.  The conflict calibration shows that these are lawful realizations of the maximal target-exposure sets.  Hence a purified optimizer is an admissible member of \(\mathcal D_n\times\Gamma_n\).  Since the unrestricted infimum includes this subclass,
\[
R_2(G_{b,s})\le V_{b,s}.
\]

We next verify what the corrected lower construction supplies.  Orthogonality of \(P_c\) gives, separately in each layer,
\[
\|P_cv\pm(I-P_c)v\|_2^2
=\|P_cv\|_2^2+\|(I-P_c)v\|_2^2
=\|v\|_2^2.
\]
The prescribed normalization therefore puts every collision atom in \(\Theta_{b,s}\); group closure preserves both layer norms.  There are at most two constant-mode columns, two between-block columns, and one within-block column.  Collision doubles these five columns, there are at most \(b+1\) active indices, and group closure multiplies cardinality by at most \(|\mathfrak G_{b,s}|\).  Thus
\[
L_{b,s}\le10(b+1)|\mathfrak G_{b,s}|.
\]
The weight simplex is compact.  For a fixed posterior cell, its contribution can be written as
\[
\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q}q_h\tau_h^2
-\frac{\left(\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q}q_h\tau_h\right)^2}
{\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q}q_h},
\]
with value zero when the denominator is zero.  This expression is continuous at zero cell mass, so every \(B_c\), their finite minimum, and hence the lexicographically selected maximizer are well defined.

Fix any orbit \(c\).  Conditional on the representative observation, atoms in the same cell \(\mathcal Q\) have identical revealed coordinates.  Squared loss is minimized by their posterior mean \(m_{\mathcal Q}\), and the minimized conditional Bayes risk is exactly \(B_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star})\).  Orbit-constant weights and group closure give the same value for every member of \(\mathcal S_c\).  Thus every measurable estimator under every design supported on maximal patterns has Bayes risk at least
\(\min_cB_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star})\).  A nonmaximal observation contains no more information than any maximal extension, so the same lower bound holds for arbitrary assignment laws.  Since worst-case risk dominates Bayes risk for every prior supported in the parameter set,
\[
\min_{0\le c\le b}B_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star})\le R_2(G_{b,s}).
\]
Combining the last inequality with the realizable upper bound proves the sandwich.  If its two endpoints agree, both inequalities are equalities and the procedure and prior are an exact saddle.  The proof makes no claim that this finite equality test always succeeds.
