The randomization defining \(\delta_{p,A,D}\) draws \(c\), then a uniformly random \(c\)-subset \(C\), then one uniformly random label in every block outside \(C\), and applies the corresponding binary assignment. \]

We next analyze the corrected finite lower construction. The normalization in \(\text{def:posterior-collision-lift}\) puts every embedded vector \(v\) inside the two layer balls. Since \(P_c\) is an orthogonal coordinate projection and preserves the layer decomposition, separately for \(k=0,1\),
\[
\|P_cv_k\pm(I-P_c)v_k\|_2^2
=\|P_cv_k\|_2^2+\|(I-P_c)v_k\|_2^2
=\|v_k\|_2^2.
\]
Thus every collision atom belongs to \(\Theta_{b,s}\), and closure under \(\mathfrak G_{b,s}\) preserves both layer norms. A two-dimensional positive-semidefinite constant-mode matrix and a two-dimensional positive-semidefinite between-block matrix each have at most two spectral columns, while the treated within-block scalar contributes at most one. Collision doubles these at most five columns, there are at most \(b+1\) active indices, and group closure multiplies cardinality by at most \(|\mathfrak G_{b,s}|\). Therefore
\[
L_{b,s}\le10(b+1)|\mathfrak G_{b,s}|.
\]

The permitted weight vectors form a nonempty compact simplex; the zero atom covers the degenerate dictionary. For a fixed posterior cell \(\mathcal Q\), write \(\tau_h=n^{-1}\sum_i(u^{(h)}_{i,1}-u^{(h)}_{i,0})\). Its contribution to posterior risk is
\[
\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q}q_h\tau_h^2
-\frac{\left(\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q}q_h\tau_h\right)^2}
{\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q}q_h},
\]
with value zero when the cell mass is zero. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality bounds the magnitude of the quotient by a constant times the cell mass, so this extension is continuous at zero mass. Every \(B_c\), their finite minimum, and hence the lexicographically selected maximizer in the corrected construction are therefore well defined.

Fix an orbit index \(c\). Conditional on the representative observation, atoms in the same cell \(\mathcal Q\) have identical revealed coordinates. For squared loss, completing the square shows that the posterior mean \(m_{\mathcal Q}\) is the Bayes action and that the minimized conditional Bayes risk is exactly \(B_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star})\). Orbit-constant weights and group closure give the same value for every pattern in \(\mathcal S_c\). Hence every measurable estimator under a design supported on maximal patterns has Bayes risk at least
\[
\min_{0\le c\le b}B_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star}).
\]
For an arbitrary assignment, extend its revealed compatible target set to a maximal one. The extension reveals weakly more coordinates, so conditional variance, and therefore optimal conditional squared-error risk, can only decrease. The published conflict calibration realizes that maximal extension by a binary assignment. It follows that the same lower bound holds for every assignment law, including laws supported on nonmaximal observations. Finally, worst-case risk over \(\Theta_{b,s}\) dominates Bayes risk under every prior supported on \(\Theta_{b,s}\). Thus
\[
\min_{0\le c\le b}B_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star})
\le R_2(G_{b,s}).
\]
Combining the lower and upper inequalities proves the sandwich. If the finite endpoint check gives \(\min_cB_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star})=V_{b,s}\), both inequalities are equalities; the purified procedure is then minimax against all measurable estimators and the finite prior is least favorable.
