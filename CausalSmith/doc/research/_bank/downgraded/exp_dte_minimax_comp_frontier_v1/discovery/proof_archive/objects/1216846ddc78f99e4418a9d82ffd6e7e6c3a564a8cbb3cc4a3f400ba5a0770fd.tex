\textbf{Error coefficients.}  Fix \(c\), \(C\), and the sampled labels \((J_r:r\notin C)\).  Put
\[
a_{0,c,C}=n^{-1}\mathbf 1_{V_n}+D_c\sum_{r\in C}\mathbf 1_{B_r},
\qquad
a_{1,c,C,J}=-n^{-1}\mathbf 1_{V_n}+A_c\sum_{r\notin C}\mathbf e_{J_r}.
\]
The estimation error is exactly
\[
\delta_{p,A,D}-\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}
=\langle a_{0,c,C},u_0\rangle+\langle a_{1,c,C,J},u_1\rangle.
\]
Therefore its design risk is a positive-semidefinite quadratic form in \((u_0,u_1)\).

\textbf{Orthogonal decomposition.}  Decompose \(\mathbb R^{V_n}\) into the orthogonal sum of the constant vector, the \((b-1)\)-dimensional space of vectors constant on each block with block constants summing to zero, and the \(b(s-1)\)-dimensional space of within-block zero-sum vectors.  Uniformity of \(C\) and of the labels \(J_r\) makes the risk operator equivariant under block permutations and within-block permutations.  Hence these three spaces are invariant, the between-block copies have a common two-layer matrix, and the treated within-block copies have a common scalar eigenvalue.  The control coefficient is constant inside each observed control block, so every control within-block contrast has eigenvalue zero.

For the normalized constant vector \(g_0\), the two error coefficients, after multiplying modal coordinates by \(\sqrt n\), are
\[
1+scD_c,
\qquad
-1+(b-c)A_c.
\]
Thus the conditional constant-mode matrix is the outer product of this vector.  With
\(W_c=p_c(1,A_c,D_c)^{\mathsf T}(1,A_c,D_c)\), summing its entries gives exactly
\(K_0(W)\) in the definition.

For a normalized between-block contrast, use two distinct blocks and the identities
\[
\Pr(1\in C,,2\notin C)=\Pr(2\in C,,1\notin C)
=\rho_c=\frac{c(b-c)}{b(b-1)}.
\]
On the event that exactly one of the two blocks is in \(C\), the scaled coefficient vector is, up to a common sign, \((sD_c,-A_c)\).  The normalization by \(n=bs\) and the two possible orientations give the multiplier \(b\rho_c\).  Consequently the common between-block matrix is
\[
\sum_{c=0}^b b\rho_c
\begin{pmatrix}
s^2p_cD_c^2&-sp_cA_cD_c\\
-sp_cA_cD_c&p_cA_c^2
\end{pmatrix}
=K_{\mathrm B}(W).
\]
For a normalized treated within-block contrast, uniform sampling of \(J_r\) gives mean squared sampled coordinate \(1/s\), and the block is outside \(C\) with probability \((b-c)/b\).  Multiplication by \(n=bs\) therefore gives
\[
k_{\mathrm W}(W)=\sum_{c=0}^b(b-c)p_cA_c^2.
\]
These calculations also show that cross terms between distinct orthogonal modes vanish.

\textbf{Maximization over the two balls.}  Scale every modal coordinate so that the control and treated energy constraints are each at most one.  Let \(Q(W)\) be the block diagonal matrix containing one copy of \(K_0(W)\), \(b-1\) copies of \(K_{\mathrm B}(W)\), the treated scalars \(k_{\mathrm W}(W)\), and the zero control within-block scalars.  The worst-case risk is
\[
\sup_{\|x_0\|_2\le1,\,\|x_1\|_2\le1}x^{\mathsf T}Q(W)x.
\]
Lift \(xx^{\mathsf T}\) to a positive-semidefinite matrix \(X\), with the two layer-trace constraints at most one.  This semidefinite relaxation is exact.  Indeed, if an extreme feasible \(X\) had rank \(r\ge2\), perturbations within its range would be parameterized by a symmetric \(r\times r\) matrix, a space of dimension \(r(r+1)/2\ge3\).  A nonzero perturbation can annihilate the at most two active layer-trace functionals; sufficiently small perturbations of both signs preserve positive semidefiniteness and all inactive inequalities, contradicting extremality.  Thus every extreme point relevant to the maximum has rank at most one.  Compactness gives an extreme maximizer.

Semidefinite duality, with strict feasibility obtained by taking both multipliers sufficiently large, now yields
\[
\sup x^{\mathsf T}Q(W)x
=\min_{\lambda_0,\lambda_1\ge0}
\left\{\lambda_0+\lambda_1:
\lambda_0E_0+\lambda_1E_1-Q(W)\succeq0\right\}.
\]
Because \(Q(W)\) is block diagonal, the last matrix inequality is equivalent to
\[
\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_0,\lambda_1)-K_0(W)\succeq0,
\quad
\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_0,\lambda_1)-K_{\mathrm B}(W)\succeq0,
\quad
\lambda_1\ge k_{\mathrm W}(W).
\]
This is exactly the two-multiplier epigraph in the mode SDP.

\textbf{Rank-one purification.}  Consider a relaxed block \(W_c\succeq0\) and put \(p_c=w^c_{00}\).  If \(p_c>0\), define
\[
\bar A_c=\frac{w^c_{01}}{p_c},
\qquad
\bar D_c=\frac{w^c_{02}}{p_c},
\qquad
\bar W_c=p_c(1,\bar A_c,\bar D_c)^{\mathsf T}(1,\bar A_c,\bar D_c).
\]
The Schur complement gives \(W_c-\bar W_c\succeq0\).  If \(p_c=0\), positive semidefiniteness forces the first row and column of \(W_c\) to vanish; set \(\bar W_c=0\).  Each conditional mode matrix is a congruence image of \(W_c\), and the within-block scalar is a nonnegative coordinate image.  Hence
\[
K_0(\bar W)\preceq K_0(W),
\qquad
K_{\mathrm B}(\bar W)\preceq K_{\mathrm B}(W),
\qquad
k_{\mathrm W}(\bar W)\le k_{\mathrm W}(W).
\]
The normalization \(\sum_cp_c=1\) is unchanged, so every feasible relaxed point is purified without increasing either multiplier.  Applying this to an optimum proves the claimed rank-one realization and completes the mode-SDP calculation.
