\textbf{Feasibility and size of the finite posterior certificate.}
The normalization in the posterior-collision construction puts every embedded vector \(v\) inside the two layer balls.  Since \(P_c\) is an orthogonal coordinate projection that preserves the layer decomposition, for \(k=0,1\),
\[
\bigl\|P_cv_k\mathbin{\pm}(I-P_c)v_k\bigr\|_2^2
=\|P_cv_k\|_2^2+\|(I-P_c)v_k\|_2^2
=\|v_k\|_2^2.
\]
Every collision atom therefore lies in \(\Theta_{b,s}\), and closure under \(\mathfrak G_{b,s}\) preserves both layer norms.  A two-dimensional positive-semidefinite constant-mode matrix and a two-dimensional positive-semidefinite between-block matrix each have at most two spectral columns, while the treated within-block scalar contributes at most one.  Collision doubles these at most five columns, there are at most \(b+1\) active orbit indices, and group closure multiplies cardinality by at most \(|\mathfrak G_{b,s}|\).  Consequently
\[
L_{b,s}\leq10(b+1)|\mathfrak G_{b,s}|.
\]

The permitted weight vectors form a nonempty compact simplex; the zero atom handles the degenerate dictionary.  For a fixed posterior cell \(\mathcal Q\), write
\[
\tau_h=\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}(u^{(h)}_{i,1}-u^{(h)}_{i,0}).

Its contribution to posterior risk is
\[
\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q}q_h\tau_h^2
-\frac{\left(\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q}q_h\tau_h\right)^2}
{\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q}q_h},

with value zero when the cell mass is zero.  Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the quotient in absolute value by a constant multiple of the cell mass, so this expression extends continuously to zero cell mass.  Each \(B_c\), their finite minimum, and the lexicographically selected maximizer are therefore well defined.

\textbf{Bayes lower endpoint.}
Fix an orbit index \(c\).  Conditional on its representative observation, atoms in the same cell \(\mathcal Q\) have identical revealed coordinates.  Completing the square shows that the posterior mean \(m_{\mathcal Q}\) minimizes squared loss and that the resulting Bayes residual risk is exactly \(B_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star})\).  Orbit-constant weights and group closure give the same value for every pattern in \(\mathcal S_c\).  Thus every measurable estimator under every design supported on maximal patterns has Bayes risk at least
\[
\min_{0\leq c\leq b}B_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star}).
\]
For an arbitrary binary assignment, extend its revealed compatible target set to a maximal one.  The extension reveals weakly more target coordinates, so its optimal conditional squared-error risk cannot exceed that of the original observation.  The published conflict calibration realizes the extension by a binary assignment.  Hence the same lower bound applies to every assignment law, including laws supported on nonmaximal observations.  Finally, worst-case risk over \(\Theta_{b,s}\) dominates Bayes risk under a prior supported on \(\Theta_{b,s}\).  Therefore
\[
\min_{0\leq c\leq b}B_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star})
\leq R_2(G_{b,s}).
\]
Combining this inequality with the upper bound proves the displayed sandwich.  If the finite posterior-cell calculation verifies
