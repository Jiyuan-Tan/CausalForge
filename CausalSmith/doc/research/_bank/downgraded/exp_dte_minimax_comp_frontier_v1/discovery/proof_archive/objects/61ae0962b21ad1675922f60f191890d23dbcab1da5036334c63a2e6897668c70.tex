\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(G\) and a clique partition \(\mathcal P\), and abbreviate
\[
a_B=\frac{|B|}{n},\qquad B\in\mathcal P.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Construction and atomwise support.}
For each sign vector \(\xi=(\xi_{B,k})\), define a complete schedule on the spanning clique graph \(H_{\mathcal P}\) by, for \(i\in B\),
\[
Y_i^{\xi}(z)=
\begin{cases}
\xi_{B,0},&z|_B\equiv0,\\
\xi_{B,1},&z_i=1\text{ and }z_j=0\text{ for every }j\in B\setminus\{i\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
Because the right side depends only on \(z|_B=z|_{\widetilde N_i(H_{\mathcal P})}\), equation (2) satisfies arbitrary neighborhood interference on \(H_{\mathcal P}\). Its all-control and isolated-treatment target values are respectively
\[
Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf0)=\xi_{B,0},
\qquad
Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf e_i)=\xi_{B,1}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus, for either \(k\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}\{Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf e_i^{\,k})\}^2
=\frac1n\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}|B|=1,
\tag{4}
\]
where \(\mathbf e_i^{\,0}=\mathbf0\) and \(\mathbf e_i^{\,1}=\mathbf e_i\). This proves atomwise membership in both separate normalized Euclidean balls and hence in the published class \(\mathcal M_2(H_{\mathcal P})\).

\textbf{Exact posterior calculation.}
By (3), the target under atom \(\xi\) is
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^{H_{\mathcal P}}(Y^{\xi})
=\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}a_B(\xi_{B,1}-\xi_{B,0}).
\tag{5}
\]
Consider any fixed binary assignment \(z\). Within a block \(B\), equation (2) has exactly three possibilities. If \(z|_B\equiv0\), all observations in the block repeat \(\xi_{B,0}\). If exactly one unit is treated, its observation is \(\xi_{B,1}\), and all other observations in the block are the fixed value zero. If at least two units are treated, every observation in the block is the fixed value zero. Therefore the data reveal respectively the control sign only, the treated sign only, or neither sign. In particular, no assignment reveals both signs from a block.

The signs are mutually independent, and every non-target value in (2) is fixed independently of them. Conditional on \(z\) and the observed outcomes, the unrevealed signs therefore remain independent Rademacher variables. It follows from (5) that
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\xi}\!\left(
\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^{H_{\mathcal P}}(Y^{\xi})
\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z)
\right)
=\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}a_B^2N_B(z),
\tag{6}
\]
where \(N_B(z)\in\{1,2\}\) is the number of unrevealed signs in block \(B\). Hence (6) is at least \(\sum_Ba_B^2\). A maximal target-exposure observation chooses in each clique block either all control or exactly one isolated treatment. It therefore has \(N_B(z)=1\) for every block, so its conditional variance is exactly \(\sum_Ba_B^2\). This also verifies directly the maximal-independent-set observation structure invoked by \(\mathrm{lem{:}published\text{-}conflict\text{-}graph\text{-}characterization}\).

For squared loss, conditional completion of the square gives, for every estimator value \(t\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\xi}\!\left[(\tau-t)^2\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z)\right]
=\operatorname{Var}_{\xi}(\tau\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z))
+\left\{\mathbb E_{\xi}(\tau\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z))-t\right\}^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) show that every assignment law and every measurable estimator have Bayes risk at least \(\sum_Ba_B^2\) under the uniform prior on the \(4^{|\mathcal P|}\) schedules. Worst-case risk dominates Bayes risk, so
\[
R_2(H_{\mathcal P})\geq\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}a_B^2.
\tag{8}
\]

\textbf{Transfer to the original graph.}
Because \(H_{\mathcal P}\subseteq G\) is a spanning subgraph, \(\mathrm{lem{:}published\text{-}network\text{-}risk\text{-}monotonicity}\) gives
\[
R_2(G)\geq R_2(H_{\mathcal P}).
\tag{9}
\]
For completeness, the direction is also visible directly from the model classes. For every \(i\in V_n\),
\[
\widetilde N_i(H_{\mathcal P})\subseteq\widetilde N_i(G).
\tag{10}
\]
Thus a schedule depending only on the smaller closed neighborhood also satisfies arbitrary neighborhood interference relative to the larger one. The two layerwise moment restrictions and the DTE use the same all-control and singleton-treatment assignments, so the schedule class on \(H_{\mathcal P}\) embeds in that on \(G\) without changing the target or the observed pair \((z,Y(z))\). For each fixed design and estimator, the supremum over the class for \(G\) is therefore at least the supremum over this embedded subclass; taking the common infimum yields (9).

Combining (8)--(9) proves the certificate for each \(\mathcal P\). The population is finite, so there are finitely many set partitions of \(V_n\); maximizing over those whose blocks induce cliques proves the final display. The argument enumerates this finite family and makes no polynomial-time optimization claim. \(\square\)
