\textbf{Counting and orbit structure.} By \(\text{lem:published-conflict-calibration}\), a maximal compatible exposure set makes exactly one of \(s+1\) choices in each block: it contains either all \(s\) control-layer vertices of that block or one of its \(s\) treated-layer vertices. Choices in distinct blocks are independent. Hence the number of maximal compatible sets is
\[
(s+1)^b.
\]
Block permutations and within-block label permutations preserve the number \(c\) of all-control blocks. Conversely, if two maximal compatible sets have the same value of \(c\), a block permutation matches their all-control blocks and within-block permutations match their selected treated labels. Therefore the orbits are exactly \(\mathcal S_0,\ldots,\mathcal S_b\).

\textbf{Joint symmetrization.} Let \(\mathfrak P_{b,s}\) be the finite wreath-product group of block and within-block permutations. It acts on assignments, observations, and the two outcome layers by relabeling coordinates. Both \(\Theta_{b,s}\) and \(\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}\) are invariant under this action. Given any design-estimator pair, first draw \(g\) uniformly from \(\mathfrak P_{b,s}\), apply the relabeled design, undo the relabeling before evaluating the original estimator, and then condition on the actually observed assignment and outcomes. Replacing the latent randomized estimate by its conditional mean cannot increase squared loss, by Jensen's inequality. At a parameter \(u\), the resulting risk is at most the average of the original risks at \(g^{-1}u\), and hence is at most the original worst-case risk. Applying the same argument to simultaneous sign reversal, or equivalently replacing an estimator \(T\) by
\[
T_{\mathrm{odd}}(x)=\frac{T(x)-T(-x)}2,
\]
does not increase worst-case risk because \(\Theta_{b,s}\) is centrally symmetric and the target changes sign. Thus joint symmetrization does not increase the minimax criterion. An invariant law assigns equal mass inside each orbit, so it is described by the orbit masses \(p_0,\ldots,p_b\).

\textbf{A nonlinear invariant rule.} Take \(b=1\), \(s=2\), and the all-control orbit. The measurable rule
\[
T(u_{1,0},u_{2,0})=(u_{1,0}+u_{2,0})^3
\]
is unchanged by exchanging the two labels and changes sign under simultaneous sign reversal. On this orbit, however, every rule of the form \(\delta_{p,A,D}\) is \(D_1(u_{1,0}+u_{2,0})\). Equality with \(T\) at \((u_{1,0},u_{2,0})=(1,0)\) would force \(D_1=1\), whereas equality at \((1/2,0)\) would require \(D_1/2=1/8\). Both observations occur for vectors satisfying the layer-ball constraint, and the two requirements are inconsistent. Hence invariance does not imply linearity. This example refutes only the symmetry-to-linearity step; it makes no assertion that this cubic rule is minimax.
