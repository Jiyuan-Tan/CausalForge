\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(G\) and a clique partition \(\mathcal P\), and abbreviate
\[
a_B=\frac{|B|}{n},\qquad B\in\mathcal P.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Construction and atomwise support.}
For each sign vector \(\xi=(\xi_{B,k})\), define a complete schedule on the spanning clique graph \(H_{\mathcal P}\) by, for \(i\in B\),
\[
Y_i^{\xi}(z)=
\begin{cases}
\xi_{B,0},&z|_B\equiv0,\\
\xi_{B,1},&z_i=1\text{ and }z_j=0\text{ for every }j\in B\setminus\{i\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
Because the right side depends only on \(z|_B=z|_{\widetilde N_i(H_{\mathcal P})}\), equation (2) satisfies arbitrary neighborhood interference on \(H_{\mathcal P}\). Its all-control and isolated-treatment target values are respectively
\[
Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf0)=\xi_{B,0},
\qquad
Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf e_i)=\xi_{B,1}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus for either \(k\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}\{Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf e_i^{\,k})\}^2
=\frac1n\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}|B|=1,
\tag{4}
\]
where \(\mathbf e_i^{\,0}=\mathbf0\) and \(\mathbf e_i^{\,1}=\mathbf e_i\). This proves atomwise membership in both separate moment balls and hence in \(\mathcal M_2(H_{\mathcal P})\).

\textbf{Exact posterior calculation.}
By (3), the target under atom \(\xi\) is
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^{H_{\mathcal P}}(Y^{\xi})
=\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}a_B(\xi_{B,1}-\xi_{B,0}).
\tag{5}
\]
Consider any fixed binary assignment \(z\). Within a block \(B\), equation (2) has exactly three possibilities. If \(z|_B\equiv0\), all observations in the block repeat \(\xi_{B,0}\). If exactly one unit is treated, its observation is \(\xi_{B,1}\) and all other observations in the block are the fixed value zero. If at least two units are treated, every observation in the block is the fixed value zero. Therefore the data reveal respectively the control sign only, the treated sign only, or neither sign. They never reveal both signs from a block.

The signs are mutually independent and every non-target value in (2) is fixed independently of them. Conditional on \(z\) and the observed outcomes, the unrevealed signs remain independent Rademacher variables. It follows from (5) that
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\xi}\!\left(
\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^{H_{\mathcal P}}(Y^{\xi})
\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z)
\right)
=\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}a_B^2N_B(z),
\tag{6}
\]
where \(N_B(z)\in\{1,2\}\) is the number of unrevealed signs in block \(B\). Hence (6) is at least \(\sum_Ba_B^2\). A maximal target-exposure observation chooses, in each clique block, either all control or exactly one isolated treatment. It therefore has \(N_B(z)=1\) for every block, so its conditional variance is exactly \(\sum_Ba_B^2\). This also verifies directly the maximal-independent-set observation structure invoked by the published conflict-graph characterization \(\mathrm{lem{:}published\text{-}conflict\text{-}graph\text{-}characterization}\), whose source is Proposition~2.2 and Appendix~A.5 of Kandiros, Harshaw, and S\"avje.

For squared loss, conditional completion of the square gives, for every estimator value \(t\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\xi}\!\left[(\tau-t)^2\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z)\right]
=\operatorname{Var}_{\xi}(\tau\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z))
+\left\{\mathbb E_{\xi}(\tau\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z))-t\right\}^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) show that every assignment law and every measurable estimator have Bayes risk at least \(\sum_Ba_B^2\) under the uniform \(4^{|\mathcal P|}\)-atom prior. Worst-case risk dominates Bayes risk, so
\[
R_2(H_{\mathcal P})\geq\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}a_B^2.
\tag{8}
\]

\textbf{Exact subgraph monotonicity.}
We now verify, rather than merely assert, the direction needed to transfer (8). Since \(H_{\mathcal P}\subseteq G\),
\[
\widetilde N_i(H_{\mathcal P})\subseteq\widetilde N_i(G)
\qquad(i\in V_n).
\tag{9}
\]
Take any \(Y\in\mathcal M_2(H_{\mathcal P})\). If \(z\) and \(z'\) agree on \(\widetilde N_i(G)\), then by (9) they agree on \(\widetilde N_i(H_{\mathcal P})\), so the ANI property for \(H_{\mathcal P}\) gives \(Y_i(z)=Y_i(z')\). The two moment restrictions and the DTE use the same physical assignments \(\mathbf0\) and \(\mathbf e_i\) for both graphs. Therefore
\[
\mathcal M_2(H_{\mathcal P})\subseteq\mathcal M_2(G),
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^{H_{\mathcal P}}(Y)
=\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^G(Y)
\quad\text{on }\mathcal M_2(H_{\mathcal P}).
\tag{10}
\]
Moreover, for a fixed schedule in this embedded subclass, the observation \((z,Y(z))\) is literally the same under the two graph labels; the assignment space, design laws, and measurable estimator space are also identical. Hence, for every fixed \((P,\widehat\tau)\),
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal M_2(G)}\mathbb E_P(\widehat\tau-\tau^G)^2
\geq
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal M_2(H_{\mathcal P})}
\mathbb E_P(\widehat\tau-\tau^{H_{\mathcal P}})^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Taking the common infimum over \((P,\widehat\tau)\) in (11) yields
\[
R_2(G)\geq R_2(H_{\mathcal P}),
\tag{12}
\]
which is exactly the \(q=2\) DTE specialization of the cited published network-risk monotonicity proposition \(\mathrm{lem{:}published\text{-}network\text{-}risk\text{-}monotonicity}\). Combining (8) and (12), and maximizing over the finite set of clique partitions of \(V_n\), proves the proposition. \(\square\)
