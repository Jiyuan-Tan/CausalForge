\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a nonempty clique \(Q\), put \(q=|Q|\), and note the support conditions \(1\leq q\leq n\).  Thus the scale
\[
a=\sqrt{\frac{n}{q}}
\tag{1}
\]
is finite and positive.  Let \(\xi_0\) and \(\xi_1\) be independent Rademacher signs.  For each sign pair \(\xi=(\xi_0,\xi_1)\), define a complete potential-outcome schedule by setting \(Y_i^{\xi}(z)=0\) for \(i\notin Q\), and, for \(i\in Q\), setting
\[
Y_i^{\xi}(z)=
\begin{cases}
a\xi_0,
&z|_{\widetilde N_i(G)}\equiv0,\\
a\xi_1,
&z_i=1\text{ and }z_j=0\text{ for every }j\in\widetilde N_i(G)\setminus\{i\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
The first two cases are disjoint.  Formula (2) depends on \(z\) only through \(z|_{\widetilde N_i(G)}\), while the off-clique schedule is constant.  Hence every atom satisfies arbitrary neighborhood interference in \(\text{def:general-published-q2-class}\).  It also gives
\[
Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf0)=a\xi_0\mathbf1\{i\in Q\},
\qquad
Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf e_i)=a\xi_1\mathbf1\{i\in Q\}.
\tag{3}
\]
By (1), each separate global layer moment constraint holds with equality:
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf0)^2
=\frac{qa^2}{n}=1,
\qquad
\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}Y_i^{\xi}(\mathbf e_i)^2
=\frac{qa^2}{n}=1.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus all four schedules belong atomwise to the unchanged published class \(\mathcal M_2(G)\).  Equations (3) and \(\text{def:general-dte-primitives}\) yield the causal estimand
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^G(Y^{\xi})
=\frac{qa}{n}(\xi_1-\xi_0)
=\sqrt{\frac qn}(\xi_1-\xi_0).
\tag{5}
\]

We next establish the exact observation obstruction.  Fix an arbitrary binary assignment \(z\).  An observation from a unit of \(Q\) can depend on \(\xi_0\) only through the first line of (2), and can depend on \(\xi_1\) only through the second line.  If the first line holds for some \(i\in Q\), then \(z_j=0\) for every \(j\in Q\), because \(Q\) is a clique and hence \(Q\subseteq\widetilde N_i(G)\).  If the second line holds for some \(j\in Q\), then \(z_j=1\).  These two events cannot occur under the same assignment.  All other observed outcomes are the fixed value zero.  Therefore \((z,Y^{\xi}(z))\) reveals at most one of the two independent signs.  The unrevealed signs retain their independent Rademacher laws conditional on the observation.

Put \(r_q=\sqrt{q/n}\).  From (5), each unrevealed sign contributes \(r_q^2=q/n\) to the conditional variance.  Since at least one sign is unrevealed,
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\xi}\!\left(
\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^G(Y^{\xi})
\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z)
\right)\geq\frac qn.
\tag{6}
\]
Equality holds when exactly one sign is revealed, and the variance is \(2q/n\) when neither is revealed.  For every measurable estimator value \(t=t(z,Y^{\xi}(z))\), conditional completion of the square gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\xi}\!\left[
\{\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^G(Y^{\xi})-t\}^2
\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z)
\right]
=\operatorname{Var}_{\xi}(\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^G(Y^{\xi})\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z))
+\{\mathbb E_{\xi}(\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^G(Y^{\xi})\mid z,Y^{\xi}(z))-t\}^2
\geq\frac qn.
\tag{7}
\]
Integrating (7) over any assignment law \(P\in\mathcal D_n\) proves that every design--estimator pair has Bayes mean squared error at least \(q/n\) under this four-atom prior.  Its worst-case risk over \(\mathcal M_2(G)\) is no smaller than its finite-prior average risk.  Taking the joint infimum over \(P\in\mathcal D_n\) and \(\widehat\tau\in\Gamma_n\) in \(\text{def:general-dte-minimax-risk}\) proves
\[
R_2(G)\geq\frac qn.
\tag{8}
\]

Because \(V_n\) is finite and nonempty, a maximum clique exists and has size \(\omega(G)\geq1\).  Substituting such a clique in (8) proves \(R_2(G)\geq\omega(G)/n\); a singleton supplies the boundary value \(1/n\).

Finally, every block \(B\) of a clique partition is a clique, so \(|B|\leq\omega(G)\).  Since the blocks partition \(V_n\),
\[
\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\left(\frac{|B|}{n}\right)^2
\leq\frac{\omega(G)}{n^2}\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}|B|
=\frac{\omega(G)}n.
\tag{9}
\]
Together with \(\text{prop:clique-partition-rademacher-converse}\), (9) proves the claimed domination.  For the equal block-clique graph, each block has size \(s\), no clique meets two components, and hence \(\omega(G_{b,s})=s\).  Since \(n=bs\), (8) becomes \(s/(bs)=1/b\).  \(\square\)
