\textbf{Established partial result and boundary checks.}
For a nonempty finite population, define the cross-layer balanced-biclique parameter by
\[
\beta_{\mathrm{DTE}}(G)
=
\max\left\{
\min\{|A|,|B|\}:
\varnothing\ne A,B\subseteq V_n,
\quad
B\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in A}\widetilde N_i(G)
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{prop{:}rescaled\text{-}clique\text{-}four\text{-}atom\text{-}certificate}\), every supplied feasible pair \((A,B)\) in (1) yields a uniform four-atom prior.  Its control layer equals a Rademacher sign of amplitude \(\sqrt{n/|A|}\) on \(A\), its treated layer equals an independent Rademacher sign of amplitude \(\sqrt{n/|B|}\) on \(B\), and all other target and non-target outcomes needed to complete the schedules are fixed at zero.  Every atom is a complete arbitrary-neighborhood-interference schedule in the unchanged published class, and its two separate normalized layer moments are exactly
\[
\frac1n|A|\frac n{|A|}=1,
\qquad
\frac1n|B|\frac n{|B|}=1.
\tag{2}
\]
The corresponding DTE is
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{DTE}}^G
=
\sqrt{\frac{|B|}{n}}\,\xi_1
-
\sqrt{\frac{|A|}{n}}\,\xi_0.
\tag{3}
\]
If an observation reveals \(\xi_0\) through an all-control exposure at some \(i\in A\), then every vertex in \(B\) is assigned control because \(B\subseteq\widetilde N_i(G)\).  Revealing \(\xi_1\), however, requires an isolated-treated exposure at a vertex of \(B\), and hence requires a treated vertex in \(B\).  Thus no assignment reveals both signs.  At least one independent sign in (3) remains posterior-unrevealed, so conditional DTE variance is at least
\[
\min\left\{\frac{|A|}{n},\frac{|B|}{n}\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Conditional completion of the square, integration over any assignment law, and domination of finite-prior Bayes risk by worst-case risk therefore give
\[
R_2(G)\geq\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{DTE}}(G)}n.
\tag{5}
\]
The prior has exactly four atoms once \((A,B)\) is supplied.

If \(Q\) is any nonempty clique, then \(A=B=Q\) is feasible in (1), because \(Q\subseteq\widetilde N_i(G)\) for every \(i\in Q\).  Hence
\[
\beta_{\mathrm{DTE}}(G)\geq\omega(G),
\qquad
R_2(G)\geq\frac{\omega(G)}n.
\tag{6}
\]
For every clique partition \(\mathcal P\), each block \(Q'\in\mathcal P\) satisfies \(|Q'|\leq\omega(G)\), and consequently
\[
\sum_{Q'\in\mathcal P}\left(\frac{|Q'|}{n}\right)^2
\leq
\frac{\omega(G)}{n^2}\sum_{Q'\in\mathcal P}|Q'|
=\frac{\omega(G)}n
\leq\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{DTE}}(G)}n.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus every clique-partition certificate is subordinate to (5).  On the equal block-clique graph, a feasible pair must lie in one component unless one side is a singleton, and each component has size \(s\); taking both sides equal to a block shows \(\beta_{\mathrm{DTE}}(G_{b,s})=s\).  Therefore the previously established computable sandwich becomes
\[
\max\left\{
\frac1b,
\min_{0\leq c\leq b}B_c(\Pi_{b,s}^{\star})
\right\}
\leq R_2(G_{b,s})\leq V_{b,s},
\tag{8}
\]
with the invariant-linear upper endpoint and its realizable procedure unchanged.

For the complete-bipartite calibration, let
\[
G_{n,m}=K_{m,m}\sqcup(n-2m)K_1,
\qquad
0\leq m\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor,
\tag{9}
\]
where \(V_n\ne\varnothing\).  When \(m\geq1\), let \(L\) and \(R\) be the two shores.  The pair \(A=L\), \(B=R\) is feasible: if \(m\geq2\), then
\[
\bigcap_{i\in L}\widetilde N_i(G_{n,m})=R,
\tag{10}
\]
whereas for \(m=1\) that intersection is \(L\cup R\), which still contains \(R\).  Hence
\[
\beta_{\mathrm{DTE}}(G_{n,m})\geq m,
\qquad
R_2(G_{n,m})\geq\frac mn
\quad(m\geq1).
\tag{11}
\]
The exact adjacency spectral radius is \(\lambda(G_{n,m})=m\) for \(m\geq1\), and it is zero for \(m=0\).  The cited spectral upper functional therefore gives
\[
R_2(G_{n,m})\lesssim\frac{m+1}{n}.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (11)--(12) yield \(R_2(G_{n,m})=\Theta(m/n)\) uniformly for \(m\geq1\).  For \(m\geq2\), the clique number is only two, so the clique certificate is only \(2/n\), while the balanced-biclique certificate is \(m/n\).  At \(m=1\), the shore construction gives \(1/n\), and the clique edge strengthens it to \(2/n\).  At \(m=0\), there is no nonempty shore, but any isolated vertex gives the feasible singleton pair \(A=B\), so (5) gives \(1/n\), while (12) also has order \(1/n\).  In particular, if \(m_n=\lfloor\rho n^{\gamma}\rfloor\), then the feasibility condition \(2m_n\leq n\) holds eventually for every fixed \(\gamma\in(0,1)\) and \(\rho>0\), and for \(\gamma=1\) whenever \(0<\rho\leq1/2\); on those regimes the matching rate is
\[
R_2(G_{n,m_n})=\Theta(n^{\gamma-1}).
\tag{13}
\]

Finally, every explicitly listed finite prior supported on \(\Theta_2(G)\) has an exactly enumerable finite collection of posterior cells and hence an auditable Bayes lower certificate.  Every explicitly listed realizable randomization and measurable estimator likewise gives an auditable upper certificate.  The four-atom certificate above has constant support for a supplied feasible pair.  What remains unproved is a randomized polynomial-time method that, on every arbitrary DTE conflict graph, performs the necessary separation, returns polynomial support on both sides, and produces a realizable upper action within a universal factor of the lower certificate.  The present lower-only advance neither supplies that action nor resolves the open-ended question.
