\paragraph{Locked parameters.}
The target is the point-identified monotone probability of necessity or sufficiency.  The two directions are PN and PS, with separately indexed factual-probability schedules \(r_n\).  Fixed-fold cross-fitting learns the control regression for PN and the treated regression after treatment--outcome swapping for PS.

\begin{abstract}
This paper formulates two not-yet-proved directional Gaussian-shell conjectures when the factual probability vanishes but its effective count diverges.  The upper conjecture asks whether a cross-fitted score has uniform Gaussian inversion and inverse-square-root effective-count diameter under primitive spline-learning and pointwise score-information conditions.  The lower conjecture asks for the same order as an honest-diameter converse on the identical shell.  Identification and regular PN/PS variance-inference benchmarks are known support.  Finite-sample continuous-law coverage, bounded-count calibration, an all-regime frontier, and post-selection event inference are not asserted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Probabilities of causation give a causal interpretation to an observed factual event, but their information can be concentrated in a rare factual stratum.  Tian and Wu provide regular semiparametric PN and PS inference, while Zhang, Wang, and Miao study generally partially identified PN bounds under a margin condition.  The open object is point-identified monotone inference on a triangular rare-event shell with learned nuisances and an effective-count honest-diameter scale.

Hannart and Naveau construct and select an event from a trajectory by maximizing causal evidence.  The PN conjecture instead concerns an externally fixed event definition and applies only if the resulting continuous-data law belongs to the PN shell; it does not analyze event selection or post-selection validity.  The PS conjecture applies separately to a Tian--Wu-style stroke attribution analysis only on its PS shell.  The lower conjecture would make directional factual effective count, rather than nominal sample size alone, relevant to Li--Mao--Pearl-style planning.

\section{Related work}
Tian and Pearl are the foundational identification reference.  Tian and Wu's Lemma 1 on p.~3 gives a modern observed-law PN and PS formulation.  For PN, their Section~3.1 and Theorem~1 on p.~4 give the regular efficient influence function and efficiency bound; Section~3.2 on pp.~5--7 gives cross-fitted estimators; and Section~4.2 and Theorem~3 on p.~8 give regular asymptotic normality, efficiency, and plug-in variance estimation.  For PS, their Section~7.1, Lemma~2 on p.~15, and Theorem~5 on pp.~15--16 give identification and efficiency bounds, while Section~7.2 and Theorem~6 on pp.~16--17 give asymptotic normality and efficiency.  These are known regular benchmarks, not triangular rare-shell diameter results.  Cai and Kuroki provide regular variance estimators for PN, PS, and PNS, but not rare-shell studentization.

In the current cited arXiv version, Zhang, Wang, and Miao's Section~2.1 and Propositions~1--2 on pp.~5--6 derive sharp generally partially identified PN bounds without monotonicity.  Their Section~2.2 on pp.~6--7 formulates bound-based confidence intervals, and their Section~3 develops margin-regular inference through Assumption~2 on p.~8, Theorems~1--2 on pp.~8--10, and Proposition~3 with intervals on pp.~11--12.  This is inference for nonsmooth sharp bounds, not point-identified monotone PN on a triangular shell, PS, uniform rare-shell score inversion or relative studentization, or an identical-shell causal converse.  Chernozhukov et al. supply cross-fitting, Chen supplies sieve tools, and Chernozhukov, Newey, and Singh study debiased global and point-local regression functionals with regularized Riesz representers.  Gleser--Hwang's Theorem~1, in \emph{Annals of Statistics} 15(4):1351--1362, is a general nonexistence result when a limiting law loses an unbounded target.  It is only an adjacent weak-information diameter precedent, not a bounded PN or PS, monotone causal, rare-shell-rate, or shell-membership result.  Khan--Tamer are an adjacent thin-information reference, and Cuellar--Kennedy target a projected conditional probability rather than this marginal monotone ratio.

\section{Formal setup}
Let the observed row be independent and identically distributed, with propensity score and treatment-specific outcome regressions.  Under consistency, conditional exchangeability, and monotonicity,
\[
\Theta_{\mathrm{PN}}(P_n)=1-\frac{E[e_n(X)m_{0,n}(X)]}{E[e_n(X)m_{1,n}(X)]},\qquad
\Theta_{\mathrm{PS}}(P_n)=1-\frac{E[(1-e_n(X))(1-m_{1,n}(X))]}{E[(1-e_n(X))(1-m_{0,n}(X))]}.
\]
PN uses the treated-success factual probability, and PS uses the control-failure factual probability.

\section{Assumptions}
The row is independent and identically distributed within each triangular-array row.  The covariate density and population propensity score are bounded, and the propensity, control regression, and treated regression belong to fixed Hölder balls.  Fixed-fold spline learners clip fitted propensities to \([\varepsilon/2,1-\varepsilon/2]\) and fitted outcome regressions to \([0,1]\).  Thus, for either direction and \(t\in[0,1]\),
\[
 |\psi_t^d(O;\widehat\eta)|\le B_\varepsilon:=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}-1,
 \qquad |\psi_t^d(O;\widehat\eta)-E\psi_t^d(O;\widehat\eta)|\le2B_\varepsilon.
\]
An admissible schedule has diverging effective count and the two primitive rare-learning inequalities.  Pointwise score information is comparable to the directional factual probability.  The construction-specific cap \(0<\bar d\le1/2\) ensures that the least-favorable type mass \(1-2r\) is nonnegative; it is not a generic rare-event assumption.

\section{Main results}
\begin{conjecture}[Directional upper frontier (NOT YET PROVED)]
On each direction-specific LF-compatible Gaussian shell, score inversion is uniformly asymptotically Gaussian, has its nominal coverage, and has inverse-square-root effective-count expected diameter.
\end{conjecture}
\begin{conjecture}[Identical-shell lower frontier (NOT YET PROVED)]
Every uniformly honest confidence set over that same directional shell has an inverse-square-root effective-count lower bound on expected diameter.
\end{conjecture}
The route to these conjectures derives rare-scale nuisance remainders and empirical score-variance consistency.  The deterministic envelope gives the Lindeberg control \(B_\varepsilon/\sqrt{nr_n}\to0\), bounds the centered score in the variance argument, and combines with affine score geometry for the expected-diameter route.  The lower route verifies a constant monotone two-point construction in the exact same shell.

\paragraph{Score inversion convention.}
For direction \(d\), let \(\widehat g_{d,n}(t)\) and \(\widehat\sigma^2_{d,n}(t)\) be the cross-fitted score mean and held-out empirical score variance.  Define
\[
\mathcal A_{n,\alpha}^d=
\left\{t\in[0,1]:n\widehat g_{d,n}(t)^2
\le z_{1-\alpha/2}^2\widehat\sigma_{d,n}^2(t)\right\},
\qquad
\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}^{\mathrm{score},d}=
\begin{cases}
\mathcal A_{n,\alpha}^d,&\mathcal A_{n,\alpha}^d\ne\varnothing,\\
\{0\},&\mathcal A_{n,\alpha}^d=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\]
This accepts a zero-variance point only when its empirical score is zero.  Affinity of the score and continuity of its empirical variance make \(\mathcal A_{n,\alpha}^d\) closed; the fallback makes the reported set measurable, nonempty, compact-valued, and always of defined diameter.  It cannot weaken acceptance of the true value or enlarge diameter.

\section{Sanity-check corollaries}
When the factual probability is bounded away from zero, the rate conditions reduce to ordinary product-rate conditions and the proposed score formulas agree with Tian--Wu's PN Sections~3.1--4.2 and Theorems~1 and 3, their PS Sections~7.1--7.2 and Theorems~5--6, and the Cai--Kuroki regular variance-inference context.  This is a known sanity reduction, not a new result.

\section{Proof-sketch route}
The proposed upper proof combines the cross-fit bias identity, monotone localization, the fitted-score envelope, a rare-scale triangular-array central limit theorem, derived studentization, and affine score geometry.  The preliminary inversion is closed and the singleton fallback preserves true-value acceptance and cannot enlarge its diameter.  The proposed lower proof verifies the capped monotone construction, computes its information ratio, controls two-point product Kullback--Leibler divergence, and applies an honest-confidence-set testing argument.

\section{Honest scope flags}
The kernel contains only the two conjectures on separate directional Gaussian shells with diverging effective count.  Identification and the regular reduction are known.  Necessity of rare-learning inequalities and bounded-count diameter are open questions.  No finite-sample continuous-law coverage, Poisson limit, all-regime upper frontier, event-selection or selection-honest result, or likelihood-ratio-transfer theorem is asserted.

\section{Iteration trail}
The current revision adds verified comparator anchors for Tian--Wu, Zhang--Wang--Miao, and Gleser--Hwang, and replaces ambiguous zero-denominator score inversion with a measurable nonempty closed-set inversion and fixed-singleton fallback.
