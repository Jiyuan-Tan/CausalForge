\section{Targets and shells}
Under consistency, conditional exchangeability, monotonicity, and overlap,
\[
 \Theta_{\rm PN}(P)=1-
 \frac{E[e(X)m_0(X)]}{E[e(X)m_1(X)]},
 \qquad
 \Theta_{\rm PS}(P)=1-
 \frac{E[(1-e(X))(1-m_1(X))]}
 {E[(1-e(X))(1-m_0(X))]}.
\]
The two targets use separate factual probabilities, $E[AY]$ and
$E[(1-A)(1-Y)]$.  The structural class imposes no joint rarity.  Directional
shells fix one probability at $d\in(0,\bar d]$, where
$0<\bar d\leq 1/2$.

\section{Abstract finite experiments}
For each direction, a separate compact profile on deterministic support
$\{x_1,\ldots,x_J\}$ generates a genuine random-label four-cell law
\[
 Q^d_\xi(L=i,C=c)=w_iq^d_{i,c},
 \qquad S^{\rm fin,d}\sim(Q^d_\xi)^{\otimes n}.
\]
The PN profile uses $(e,m_0,u)$ and the PS profile uses its own
$(r,v,u^{\rm PS})$ spline boxes.  Each null is the joint closure of the
positive-denominator graph.  With the unrestricted-versus-null profile LR
$\Lambda_J^d(t;s)$, define
\[
 p_t^{d,\rm LF}(s,u)=\sup_{\xi\in H_t^d}
 \left[(Q_\xi^d)^{\otimes n}\{\Lambda_J^d(t;S)>
 \Lambda_J^d(t;s)\}+u(Q_\xi^d)^{\otimes n}\{\Lambda_J^d(t;S)=
 \Lambda_J^d(t;s)\}\right],
\]
and invert $p_t^{d,\rm LF}>\alpha$.  Compactness, the closed graph, and a
lexicographic maximum make this Borel.  For a positive-denominator true
profile, randomized coverage is at least $1-\alpha$ under its own abstract
product law.  A zero directional factual-cell count reports $[0,1]$.
This is not a claim of exact coverage for continuous random covariates.

Along a specified finite profile family, a marked-Poisson LR limit requires
convergence of $nd$, normalized rare-cell mark measures, tight masses, unit
normalized true factual-cell mass, and a common-cell compensator remainder.
Bounded $nd$ alone does not establish such a limit.  At the limit $\lambda=0$,
the point process is empty and the reported set is $[0,1]$.

\section{Learning and the continuous score theorem}
Fixed-fold clipped tensor-product splines estimate $e$ by logistic loss,
$m_0$ on controls, and $m_1$ on treated observations.  The generic learning
lemma uses only the observed law.  Monotonicity subsequently gives
\[
 E[m_0(X)]\leq E[m_1(X)]\leq d^{\rm PN}/\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 E[1-m_1(X)]\leq E[1-m_0(X)]\leq d^{\rm PS}/\varepsilon.
\]
For PN the resulting remainder is negligible at the rare score scale when
\[
 d\gg\max\{a_{0,n}^2,na_{e,n}^2a_{0,n}^2\},
\]
and PS has the identical requirement with $a_{0,n}$ replaced by $a_{1,n}$.
The Gaussian sub-shell also requires $nd\to\infty$ and score information
bounded above and below after division by $d$.

For the real sample $Z_n\sim P_n^{\otimes n}$, invert the studentized
cross-fitted directional score.  The empirical variance consistency is a
derived lemma: validation-fold conditioning gives
\[
 \mathbb P_n\Delta_{\rm PN}^2=O_P(a_{0,n}^2+da_{e,n}^2)=o_P(d),
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_n\Delta_{\rm PS}^2=O_P(a_{1,n}^2+da_{e,n}^2)=o_P(d).
\]
Bounded scores and the information bound give triangular-array Lindeberg
without an extra rare-score moment assumption.  Therefore each directional
score set has uniform asymptotic coverage and expected diameter
$O((nd)^{-1/2})$ on its own Gaussian sub-shell.  The diameter follows from
the affine identity
\[
 \widehat g_d(t_d+u)-\widehat g_d(t_d)=-u\widehat d_d
\]
and factual-count concentration.  It is not an all-regime upper result.

For dominated, class-preserving PN paths that retain a monotone full-data
extension, the induced experiments satisfy
\[
 \log\frac{dP_{n,h}^{\otimes n}}{dP_n^{\otimes n}}
 =\frac{h}{v_n}\Delta_n-\frac{h^2}{2v_n}+o_P(1).
\]
Thus LAN is an induced-experiment statement, not a property of an interval.

\section{Lower bound}
The capped constant submodel has $e=1/2$, $m_0=2(1-t)d$, and $m_1=2d$.
Its monotone type probabilities are $2(1-t)d$, $2td$, and $1-2d$.  On
$t\in[1/4,3/4]$, its score-information ratio lies in $[3/16,21/16]$.
It proves, shell by shell and in both directions, that every uniformly honest
set has expected diameter at least a constant times
\[
 \min\{1,(nd)^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Hence bounded effective counts force nonshrinking honest intervals.  The same
pair also supplies the lower-bound corollary on the Gaussian sub-shell; this
does not claim a matching upper bound outside that sub-shell.

\section{Two open likelihood-ratio problems}
First, an independent pilot must construct compact continuous directional
sieve densities, closed positive-denominator nulls, measurable
least-favourable conditional calibration, and target approximation for a
continuous profile LR.  Second, only after that statistic exists, a
pilot-selected finite profile may be compared with its null and bounded-local
LR law, with stable conditional unconditioning and rare-scale diameter
integrability.  Neither question is an assumption of the score theorem.

\section{Positioning and consumers}
Tian and Wu (2025) are the regular point-identified PN/PS comparator.
Cuellar and Kennedy (2020) study regular projection inference for a
conditional probability of causation.  Zhang, Wang, and Miao (2025,
arXiv:2504.08294v2) study generally partially identified PN bounds.  Li,
Mao, and Pearl (2022) study sample size for specified PN, PS, and PNS-bound
accuracy.  The finite rare-count result is a direct consumer for
Hannart--Naveau climate PN/FAR analyses; the PS score result speaks to the
Tian--Wu stroke-attribution setting.  Selected climate-event honesty remains
an open consumer.  No unverified theorem, section, page, or equation anchor
is asserted.
