\textbf{Proof.} Put \(r=b/(b+1)\), \(s=1/(b+1)\), \(q_b=r^{-b}=(1+1/b)^b\), and \(\lambda=r-s=(b-1)/(b+1)\). The assignment process is the stationary symmetric two-state Markov chain with transition matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\s&r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is outcome-independent and therefore belongs to \(\mathcal D_T\). Stationarity and the Markov property give
\[
\upsilon_{t,b,a}=\frac12r^{k_t}>0.
\]
In particular, the displayed Horvitz--Thompson estimator is an observed-data statistic with positive denominators.

Fix \(Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\), put \(y_{t,a}=Y_t(\mathbf a_{1:t})\), and define
\[
Z_t=\frac{H_{t,b,1}y_{t,1}}{\upsilon_{t,b,1}}-\frac{H_{t,b,0}y_{t,0}}{\upsilon_{t,b,0}},
\qquad
\widetilde\theta_b=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T Z_t.
\]
The two exposure events at a fixed time are disjoint. The inclusion-probability identity therefore yields
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}Z_t=y_{t,1}-y_{t,0},
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}\widetilde\theta_b=\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y).
\]
Moreover, since \(k_t\le b\), boundedness and \(r^{-k_t}\le q_b\) imply
\[
\mathbb E Z_t^2
=\frac{y_{t,1}^2}{\upsilon_{t,b,1}}+\frac{y_{t,0}^2}{\upsilon_{t,b,0}}
\le 4q_bB^2,
\qquad
\mathbb E|Z_t|=|y_{t,1}|+|y_{t,0}|\le2B.
\]
If \(1\le u-t\le b\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)|\le4q_bB^2.
\]
There are exactly
\[
\sum_{h=1}^b(T-h)=bT-\frac{b(b+1)}2
\]
such pairs because \(b\le T\).

Now suppose \(u-t>b\) and put \(d=u-b-t\ge1\). Conditional on \(W_{u-b}\), the remaining \(b\) coordinates of the homogeneous window stay equal to that coordinate with probability \(r^b\). Since \(\upsilon_{u,b,a}=r^b/2\),
\[
\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{u-b}=1)=2y_{u,1},
\qquad
\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{u-b}=0)=-2y_{u,0}.
\]
The symmetric two-state chain satisfies the exact transition identity
\[
\Pr(W_{t+d}=j\mid W_t=i)
=\frac12\{1+(2i-1)(2j-1)\lambda^d\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\left|\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{1:t})-\mathbb EZ_u\right|
=\lambda^d|y_{u,1}+y_{u,0}|
\le2B\lambda^d.
\]
Because \(Z_t\) is measurable with respect to \(W_{1:t}\),
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)|
\le \mathbb E|Z_t|\,2B\lambda^d
\le4B^2\lambda^d.
\]
For a fixed \(d\), there are exactly \(T-b-d\) pairs, provided this number is positive. Adding the diagonal and twice the two covariance sums proves
\[
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t\right)
\le B^2V_{T,b},
\]
where
\[
V_{T,b}=4q_bT+8q_b\left(bT-\frac{b(b+1)}2\right)
+8\sum_{d=1}^{(T-b-1)_+}(T-b-d)\lambda^d.
\]
Jensen's inequality and unbiasedness now give the exact finite-pair variance guarantee
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}
\left|\widetilde\theta_b-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le\frac BT\sqrt{V_{T,b}}.
\]

We next prove the stated simplification. The elementary inequality \(q_b=(1+1/b)^b\le e\) holds for every \(b\ge1\). If \(b=1\), then \(q_b=2\), \(\lambda=0\), and
\[
V_{T,1}=8T+16(T-1)=24T-16\le(10e+2)T,
\]
where the last inequality follows, for example, from \(e>1+1+1/2=5/2\). If \(b\ge2\), then
\[
\sum_{d=1}^{(T-b-1)_+}(T-b-d)\lambda^d
\le (T-b)\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\lambda^d
=(T-b)\frac{b-1}{2}.
\]
It follows that
\[
V_{T,b}
\le4eT+8e\left(bT-\frac{b(b+1)}2\right)+4(T-b)(b-1).
\]
The difference between \((10e+2)Tb\) and the right-hand side is
\[
2(e-1)(b-2)T+4b\{e(b+1)+b-1\}\ge0.
\]
Thus \(V_{T,b}\le(10e+2)Tb\) in all cases.

It remains to replace the oracle homogeneous outcomes by observed outcomes. If \(t\le b+1\), then \(k_t=t-1\); on \(H_{t,b,a}=1\), the complete realized history through time \(t\) is \(\mathbf a_{1:t}\), so the replacement error is exactly zero. If \(t\ge b+2\), then on \(H_{t,b,a}=1\) the current assignment and its preceding \(b\) assignments all equal \(a\). The historical-envelope assumption gives
\[
|Y_t(W_{1:t})-y_{t,a}|
\le C\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{t-1}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}.
\]
For each arm and time, the exact exposure-weight identity is
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}\left[\frac{H_{t,b,a}}{\upsilon_{t,b,a}}\right]=1.
\]
The triangle inequality, expectation, this identity, and an interchange of two finite sums therefore give the endpoint-aware expected replacement-error upper bound
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}|\widehat\vartheta_b-\widetilde\theta_b|
&\le\frac{2C}{T}\sum_{t=b+2}^T\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{t-1}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\\
&=\frac{2C}{T}\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both sides are zero when the index set is empty. Since \(x\mapsto x^{-(1+\beta)}\) is decreasing and \(\beta>0\),
\[
\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{\infty}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\le\int_{b+1}^{\infty}x^{-(1+\beta)}\,dx
=\frac{(b+1)^{-\beta}}{\beta}
\le\frac{b^{-\beta}}{\beta}.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}|\widehat\vartheta_b-\widetilde\theta_b|
\le\frac{2C}{\beta}\frac{(T-b-1)_+}{T}b^{-\beta}.
\]
The preceding derivation shows separately that replacement error is zero through \(t=b+1\); it covers \(T=1\), \(b=1\), and \(b=T\), with empty sums interpreted as zero. Combining the oracle-statistic and replacement inequalities proves
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}|\widehat\vartheta_b-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|
\le\frac BT\sqrt{V_{T,b}}
+\frac{2C}{T}\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)},
\]
and the two displayed corollaries follow from \(V_{T,b}\le(10e+2)Tb\) and the endpoint-aware tail bound. The carryover expression is an expected replacement-error upper bound obtained using the exact exposure-weight identity; it is not asserted to equal the estimator's bias. \(\square\)
