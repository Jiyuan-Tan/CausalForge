\textbf{Proof.} Put \(r=b/(b+1)\), \(s=1/(b+1)\), \(q_b=r^{-b}=(1+1/b)^b\), and \(\lambda=r-s=(b-1)/(b+1)\). The assignment process is the stationary symmetric two-state Markov chain with transition matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\s&r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is outcome-independent and hence belongs to \(\mathcal D_T\). Stationarity and the Markov property give
\[
\upsilon_{t,b,a}=\frac12r^{k_t}>0.
\]
Thus \(\widehat\vartheta_b\) is an observed-data statistic and every denominator in it is positive.

Fix \(Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\), put \(y_{t,a}=Y_t(\mathbf a_{1:t})\), and define
\[
Z_t=\frac{H_{t,b,1}y_{t,1}}{\upsilon_{t,b,1}}-\frac{H_{t,b,0}y_{t,0}}{\upsilon_{t,b,0}},
\qquad
\widetilde\theta_b=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T Z_t.
\]
The two exposure events at a fixed time are disjoint. The inclusion-probability identity therefore yields
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}Z_t=y_{t,1}-y_{t,0},
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}\widetilde\theta_b=\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y).
\]
Since \(k_t\le b\), Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:bounded-array}\) and \(r^{-k_t}\le q_b\) imply
\[
\mathbb E Z_t^2
=\frac{y_{t,1}^2}{\upsilon_{t,b,1}}+\frac{y_{t,0}^2}{\upsilon_{t,b,0}}
\le4q_bB^2,
\qquad
\mathbb E|Z_t|=|y_{t,1}|+|y_{t,0}|\le2B.
\]
If \(1\le u-t\le b\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)|\le4q_bB^2.
\]
There are exactly
\[
\sum_{h=1}^b(T-h)=bT-\frac{b(b+1)}2
\]
such pairs, interpreting the term with \(h=T\) as zero when \(b=T\).

Now suppose \(u-t>b\) and put \(d=u-b-t\ge1\). Conditional on \(W_{u-b}\), the remaining \(b\) coordinates in the homogeneous window stay equal to that coordinate with probability \(r^b\). Since \(\upsilon_{u,b,a}=r^b/2\),
\[
\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{u-b}=1)=2y_{u,1},
\qquad
\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{u-b}=0)=-2y_{u,0}.
\]
The symmetric chain has the exact transition identity
\[
\Pr(W_{t+d}=j\mid W_t=i)
=\frac12\{1+(2i-1)(2j-1)\lambda^d\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\left|\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{1:t})-\mathbb EZ_u\right|
=\lambda^d|y_{u,1}+y_{u,0}|
\le2B\lambda^d.
\]
Because \(Z_t\) is measurable with respect to \(W_{1:t}\),
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)|
\le \mathbb E|Z_t|\,2B\lambda^d
\le4B^2\lambda^d.
\]
For fixed \(d\), there are \((T-b-d)_+\) such pairs. Adding the diagonal and twice the two covariance sums proves
\[
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{t=1}^T Z_t\right)\le B^2V_{T,b}.
\]
Jensen's inequality and unbiasedness then give
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}\left|\widetilde\theta_b-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le\frac BT\sqrt{V_{T,b}}.
\]

We next establish the uniform simplification of \(V_{T,b}\). For \(b=1\), direct substitution gives \(q_b=2\), \(\lambda=0\), and
\[
V_{T,1}=24T-16\le(8e+4)T,
\]
because \(e>5/2\). For \(b=2\), \(q_b=9/4\), \(\lambda=1/3\), and
\[
V_{T,2}=45T-54+8\sum_{d=1}^{(T-3)_+}(T-2-d)3^{-d}
\le45T-54+4(T-2)\le49T.
\]
Since \(e>8/3\), \(49T<(16e+8)T=(8e+4)Tb\).

Suppose \(b\ge3\). The geometric tail satisfies
\[
\sum_{d=1}^{(T-b-1)_+}(T-b-d)\lambda^d
\le(T-b)\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\lambda^d
=(T-b)\frac{b-1}{2}.
\]
It follows that
\[
V_{T,b}\le4q_bT+8q_b\left(bT-\frac{b(b+1)}2\right)+4(T-b)(b-1).
\]
The difference between \((8e+4)Tb\) and the right-hand side equals
\[
T\{8b(e-q_b)-4(q_b-1)\}+4b\{q_b(b+1)+b-1\}.
\]
To bound the first brace, expand
\[
q_b=\sum_{k=0}^b\frac1{k!}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac jb\right).
\]
The deficits of the \(k=2\) and \(k=3\) terms alone imply
\[
e-q_b\ge\frac1{2b}+\left(\frac1{2b}-\frac1{3b^2}\right)=\frac1b-\frac1{3b^2}.
\]
Also \(q_b\le e\). The exponential series and the ratio bound \(1/(k+1)\le1/4\) for \(k\ge3\) give
\[
e=\frac52+\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
\le\frac52+\frac16\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}4^{-j}
=\frac{49}{18}<\frac{11}{4}.
\]
Therefore
\[
8b(e-q_b)-4(q_b-1)
\ge8-\frac8{3b}-4(e-1)
\ge\frac{100}{9}-4e>\frac19>0.
\]
The second brace is positive, so \(V_{T,b}\le(8e+4)Tb\) also for \(b\ge3\).

The coefficient is best possible for a uniform inequality of the asserted form. For fixed \(b\), division of the exact formula by \(Tb\) and passage to \(T\to\infty\) give
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{V_{T,b}}{Tb}
=\frac{4q_b}{b}+8q_b+\frac8b\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}
=\frac{4q_b}{b}+8q_b+4\frac{b-1}{b}.
\]
Since \(q_b\to e\), taking \(b\to\infty\) gives \(8e+4\). Thus no smaller constant works uniformly over all admissible \(T\) and \(b\).

It remains to compare observed outcomes with their homogeneous-path counterparts. If \(t\le b+1\), then \(k_t=t-1\), and on \(H_{t,b,a}=1\) the entire history through \(t\) is \(\mathbf a_{1:t}\); hence the replacement error is zero. If \(t\ge b+2\), then on \(H_{t,b,a}=1\), the current assignment and its preceding \(b\) assignments all equal \(a\). Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:historical-polynomial-envelope}\) gives
\[
|Y_t(W_{1:t})-y_{t,a}|
\le C\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{t-1}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}.
\]
For each arm and time, the exact exposure-weight identity is
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}\left[\frac{H_{t,b,a}}{\upsilon_{t,b,a}}\right]=1.
\]
The triangle inequality and interchange of two finite sums therefore yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}|\widehat\vartheta_b-\widetilde\theta_b|
&\le\frac{2C}{T}\sum_{t=b+2}^T\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{t-1}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\\
&=\frac{2C}{T}\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both sides vanish when the index set is empty. Since \(x\mapsto x^{-(1+\beta)}\) is decreasing and \(\beta>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
&\le (T-b-1)_+\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{\infty}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\\
&\le\frac{(T-b-1)_+}{\beta}(b+1)^{-\beta}\\
&\le\frac{(T-b-1)_+}{\beta}b^{-\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This also proves directly that replacement error is zero through \(t=b+1\), including \(T=1\), \(b=1\), and \(b=T\). Combining the sampling and replacement bounds proves the finite envelope, and using \(V_{T,b}\le(8e+4)Tb\) gives
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}|\widehat\vartheta_b-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|
\le \sqrt{8e+4}\,B\sqrt{\frac bT}
+\frac{2C}{\beta}\frac{(T-b-1)_+}{T}(b+1)^{-\beta}.
\]
The second summand was obtained as an expected replacement-error upper bound; no equality with the estimator's bias has been used or claimed. \(\square\)
