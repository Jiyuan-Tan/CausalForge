\textbf{Proof.} For every design-estimator pair and every \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\), its worst-case risk over \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) is at least \(R_T(\beta;C)\). Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) makes this denominator strictly positive. Thus every ratio in the definition of \(A_T(C)\) is at least one, and
\[
A_T(C)\ge1.
\tag{1}
\]

At \(T=1\), Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\) gives \(R_1(\beta;C)=B\) for every \(\beta\). The common fixed-before-assignment rule \(W_1\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), with
\[
\widehat\theta_1=(2W_1-1)Y_1^{\mathrm{obs}},
\]
has worst-case risk at most \(B\) simultaneously over all \(\beta\). Its simultaneous ratio is therefore at most one; (1) makes it exactly one. Hence
\[
A_1(C)=1.
\tag{2}
\]
The piecewise definitions also give \(N_1^\star=N_1^{\mathrm{fin}}=D_1^{\mathrm{bin}}=B\).

Suppose \(T\ge2\). For \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\) and every lag \(\ell\ge1\),
\[
C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\le C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta_-)}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\subseteq\mathcal P_{T,\beta_-}(C).
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(b^\star\) be the smallest minimizer of \(U_{T,\beta_-}^{\dagger}(b)\) over the finite set \(\{1,\ldots,T\}\). Before assignment, compare \(2B\) with \(U_{T,\beta_-}^{\dagger}(b^\star)\). If \(2B\) is no larger, use the zero estimator under any fixed assignment rule; otherwise use the stationary Markov switchback and Horvitz--Thompson estimator of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) at \(b=b^\star\). This procedure depends only on \(T,B,C,\beta_-\), not on the realized \(\beta\). By (3), its risk on every admissible class is at most \(N_T^\star\). In the Markov branch,
\[
\upsilon_{t,b^\star,a}\ge\frac{1}{2q_{b^\star}}>\frac{1}{2e},
\]
with unattained uniform infimum \(1/(2e)\).

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) gives
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\ge D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}>0
\tag{4}
\]
for every \(\beta\). Taking the simultaneous ratio for the common procedure and then the infimum defining \(A_T(C)\) proves
\[
A_T(C)\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}.
\tag{5}
\]
The denominator in (5) is the exact binomial Bayes lower bound on oracle risk, not the unknown oracle risk itself.

Put \(b_-=\lceil T^{1/(2\beta_-+1)}\rceil\). This is one candidate in the finite minimum, so
\[
N_T^\star\le U_{T,\beta_-}^{\dagger}(b_-)=N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}.
\tag{6}
\]
The pointwise ceiling calculation in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\), specialized to \(\beta_-\), gives
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
\le
\left[\sqrt{(8e-4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right]
T^{-\beta_-/(2\beta_-+1)}.
\tag{7}
\]
This also holds at \(T=1\), where \(N_1^{\mathrm{fin}}=B\). Finally,
\[
D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}.
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (1), (5)--(8) yields
\[
1\le A_T(C)
\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}
\le\frac{N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}
\le\frac{\sqrt2}{B}
\left[\sqrt{(8e-4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right]
T^{1/(4\beta_-+2)}.
\]
The final coefficient is endpoint-specific because the common rule may use the known endpoint \(\beta_-\). This remains a polynomial common-design certificate based on a universal oracle-risk floor; it proves neither divergent adaptation cost, subpolynomial adaptation, nor a sharp adaptation order. \(\square\)
