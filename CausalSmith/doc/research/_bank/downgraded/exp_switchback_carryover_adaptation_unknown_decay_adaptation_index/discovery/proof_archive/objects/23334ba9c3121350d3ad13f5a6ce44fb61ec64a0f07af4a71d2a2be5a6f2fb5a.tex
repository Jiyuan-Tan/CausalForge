\textbf{Proof.} Put \(r=r_b=b/(b+1)\), \(s=1/(b+1)\), \(q_b=r^{-b}=(1+1/b)^b\), and \(\lambda=r-s=(b-1)/(b+1)\). The assignment process is the stationary symmetric two-state Markov chain with transition matrix
\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\s&r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is outcome-independent and belongs to \(\mathcal D_T\). Stationarity and the Markov property give
\[
\upsilon_{t,b,a}=\frac12r^{k_t}\ge\frac12r^b=\frac{1}{2q_b}>\frac{1}{2e}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, \(b\log(1+1/b)<1\). Equality in the weak inequality in \((1)\) holds whenever \(k_t=b\). Since \(q_b\to e\), the uniform infimum is \(1/(2e)\), approached along indices with \(k_t=b\), and it is not attained for finite \(b\).

Fix \(Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\), write \(y_{t,a}=Y_t(\mathbf a_{1:t})\), and define \(Z_t\) and \(\widetilde\theta_b\) as in the statement. The exposure events for the two arms are disjoint, and inverse-probability weighting gives
\[
\mathbb EZ_t=y_{t,1}-y_{t,0},\qquad \mathbb E\widetilde\theta_b=\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y).
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\mathbb EZ_t^2=2r^{-k_t}(y_{t,1}^2+y_{t,0}^2)\le4B^2r^{-k_t}.
\tag{3}
\]
For the constant endpoint values \(y_{t,0}=y_{t,1}=B\), the mean in \((2)\) is zero and equality holds in the diagonal variance bound.

Let \(u=t+h\) with \(1\le h\le b\). Opposite-arm exposure events are disjoint, whereas
\[
\Pr(H_{t,b,a}=H_{u,b,a}=1)=\frac12r^{k_t+h}.
\]
Since \(k_u\ge h\), the number \(\rho=2r^{h-k_u}\) satisfies \(\rho\ge2\). Expanding the covariance exactly yields
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)=(\rho-1)(y_{t,1}y_{u,1}+y_{t,0}y_{u,0})+y_{t,1}y_{u,0}+y_{t,0}y_{u,1}.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently,
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)|\le2(\rho-1)B^2+2B^2=4B^2r^{h-k_u},
\tag{5}
\]
with equality when all four endpoint values equal \(B\).

If \(u-t>b\), put \(d=u-b-t\ge1\). Here \(k_u=b\). Conditional on \(W_{u-b}\), the final \(b\) transitions in the exposure window remain at that state with probability \(r^b\), and hence
\[
\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{u-b}=1)=2y_{u,1},\qquad \mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{u-b}=0)=-2y_{u,0}.
\]
The transition identity
\[
P^d(i,j)=\frac12\{1+(2i-1)(2j-1)\lambda^d\}
\]
therefore implies
\[
\left|\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{1:t})-\mathbb EZ_u\right|\le2B\lambda^d.
\]
Because \(Z_t\) is measurable with respect to \(W_{1:t}\) and \(\mathbb E|Z_t|=|y_{t,1}|+|y_{t,0}|\le2B\),
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)|\le4B^2\lambda^d.
\tag{6}
\]
Equality again holds for the constant endpoint array. Summing \((3)\), twice the near-pair bounds \((5)\), and twice the far-pair bounds \((6)\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t\right)\le B^2V_{T,b}^{\dagger}.
\tag{7}
\]
The constant potential-outcome array \(Y_t(\cdot)=B\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) and attains equality simultaneously in every term of \((7)\). Thus
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{(b)}}\!\left(\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t\right)=B^2V_{T,b}^{\dagger}.
\tag{8}
\]
By \((2)\), Jensen's inequality, and \((8)\),
\[
\mathbb E|\widetilde\theta_b-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|\le\frac BT\sqrt{V_{T,b}^{\dagger}}.
\tag{9}
\]

We next control the exact variance expression. Since \(r^{-k_t}\le q_b\),
\[
4\sum_{t=1}^Tr^{-k_t}\le4q_bT.
\tag{10}
\]
Also,
\[
\sum_{h=1}^b\sum_{t=1}^{T-h}r^{h-k_{t+h}}
\le T\sum_{h=1}^br^{h-b}=Tb(q_b-1),
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
8\sum_{d=1}^{(T-b-1)_+}(T-b-d)\lambda^d
\le8(T-b)\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}=4(T-b)(b-1).
\tag{12}
\]
This proves the first displayed upper bound for \(V_{T,b}^{\dagger}\). To prove its strict sharpening, set
\[
\delta_b=1-b\log(1+1/b)=b\int_0^{1/b}\frac{x}{1+x}\,dx>\frac{1}{2(b+1)}.
\]
Since \(e=q_be^{\delta_b}\),
\[
2b(e-q_b)=2bq_b(e^{\delta_b}-1)>\frac{bq_b}{b+1}=r q_b.
\]
Furthermore \(q_b\le b+1\): this is equality for \(b=1\), while for \(b\ge2\), \(q_b<e<3\le b+1\). Hence \(rq_b\ge q_b-1\), so
\[
8b(e-q_b)-4(q_b-1)>0.
\tag{13}
\]
The difference between \((8e-4)Tb\) and the right-hand side of the bound obtained from \((10)\)--\((12)\) is
\[
T\{8b(e-q_b)-4(q_b-1)\}+4b(b-1)>0.
\]
Therefore
\[
V_{T,b}^{\dagger}<(8e-4)Tb.
\tag{14}
\]
For fixed \(b\), direct geometric summation of the three exact terms gives
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{V_{T,b}^{\dagger}}{Tb}=\frac{4q_b}{b}+8(q_b-1)+4\frac{b-1}{b}.
\]
Letting \(b\to\infty\) gives \(8e-4\). Together with \((14)\), this proves that \(8e-4\) is the smallest coefficient valid uniformly in \(T\) and \(b\).

It remains to control replacement by observed outcomes. For \(t\le b+1\), the event \(H_{t,b,a}=1\) fixes the entire treatment history through time \(t\) at arm \(a\), so replacement error is zero. Now fix indices in the exact domain
\[
t\ge b+2,\qquad b+1\le\ell\le t-1,\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{15}
\]
On \(H_{t,b,a}=1\), one has \(W_{t-b}=a\). The Markov property makes \(W_{1:t-b-1}\) conditionally independent of \(W_{t-b+1:t}\) given \(W_{t-b}\). Since the stationary symmetric chain is reversible, its \((\ell-b)\)-step backward transition equals its forward transition. All coordinates in \((15)\) are defined, because \(1\le t-\ell\le t-b-1\). Consequently,
\[
\Pr_{\pi^{(b)}}(W_{t-\ell}\ne a\mid H_{t,b,a}=1)
=\Pr_{\pi^{(b)}}(W_{t-\ell}\ne a\mid W_{t-b}=a)
=\frac{1-\lambda^{\ell-b}}2.
\tag{16}
\]
On the same exposure event, boundedness and the historical polynomial envelope give
\[
|Y_t(W_{1:t})-y_{t,a}|
\le \min\!\left\{2B,\ C\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{t-1}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\mathbf1\{W_{t-\ell}\ne a\}\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The map \(x\mapsto\min\{2B,x\}\) is concave and nondecreasing on \([0,\infty)\). Conditioning in \((17)\), applying Jensen's inequality, and using \((16)\) for every summand in its exact domain yield
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[|Y_t(W_{1:t})-y_{t,a}|\mid H_{t,b,a}=1\right]
\le\min\!\left\{2B,\frac C2\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{t-1}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}(1-\lambda^{\ell-b})\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Because \(\mathbb E[H_{t,b,a}/\upsilon_{t,b,a}]=1\), the triangle inequality applied to the two arms and \((18)\) proves
\[
\mathbb E|\widehat\vartheta_b-\widetilde\theta_b|\le E_{T,\beta}^{\mathrm{cond}}(b).
\tag{19}
\]
Dropping the cap and interchanging the two finite sums gives
\[
E_{T,\beta}^{\mathrm{cond}}(b)
\le\frac CT\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}(1-\lambda^{\ell-b}).
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(0\le1-\lambda^{\ell-b}\le1\), monotonicity and integral comparison imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{T,\beta}^{\mathrm{cond}}(b)
&\le\frac CT(T-b-1)_+\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{\infty}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\\
&\le\frac C\beta\frac{(T-b-1)_+}{T}(b+1)^{-\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
All sums in \((19)\)--\((21)\) are zero when empty, so these formulas cover \(T=1\), \(b=1\), and \(b=T\). Finally, combine \((9)\), \((14)\), and \((19)\)--\((21)\) with the triangle inequality. This gives both the finite certificate \(U_{T,\beta}^{\mathrm{cond}}(b)\) and its coarse upper bound with coefficient \(C/\beta\). The exactness in \((8)\) concerns only the auxiliary homogeneous-history variance; none of the replacement or risk inequalities is an asserted exact bias, maximal carryover error, exact observed-data risk, or minimax risk. \(\square\)
