\textbf{Proof.} Fix \((\pi,\widehat\theta)\). For \(\sigma\in\{-,+\}\), abbreviate \(\beta_\sigma\) by the corresponding endpoint and put
\[
L_\sigma(Y)=\mathbb E_\pi
\left|\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|,
\qquad
S_\sigma=\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta_\sigma}(C)}L_\sigma(Y).
\]
By the definition of the oracle minimax risk,
\[
S_\sigma\ge R_T(\beta_\sigma;C).
\]
The denominator is strictly positive by \(\mathrm{lem:positive-oracle-risk}\). By the defining property of a supremum, for every \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) there is a fixed array \(Y_\sigma\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta_\sigma}(C)\) such that
\[
L_\sigma(Y_\sigma)
\ge S_\sigma-\varepsilon R_T(\beta_\sigma;C)
\ge(1-\varepsilon)R_T(\beta_\sigma;C).
\]
If \(S_\sigma=\infty\), choose instead any array with loss at least \((1-\varepsilon)R_T(\beta_\sigma;C)\), which exists by the same definition of the supremum. Let \(\Pi_\sigma=\delta_{Y_\sigma}\). This prior is sampled entirely before assignment; its support contains one fixed potential-outcome array and therefore cannot depend on the realized assignment path. Its Bayes risk equals \(L_\sigma(Y_\sigma)\), proving the two displayed inequalities.

For the final conclusion, the same minimax-definition argument without \(\varepsilon\) gives, for every fixed procedure and either endpoint,
\[
\frac{\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta_\sigma}(C)}L_\sigma(Y)}
{R_T(\beta_\sigma;C)}\ge1.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\) and then the infimum over procedures yields \(A_T(C)\ge1\). No cadence incompatibility has entered this argument, so it cannot imply any factor larger than one. \(\square\)
