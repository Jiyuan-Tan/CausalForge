\textbf{Proof.} Fix an arbitrary \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and an arbitrary observed-data estimator \(\widehat\theta\). Let \((\xi_{t,0},\xi_{t,1})_{t=1}^T\) be independent Rademacher signs, and define the fixed array
\[
Y_t^\xi(z_{1:t})=B\xi_{t,z_t}.
\]
Each realization is bounded and has no historical dependence, so it belongs to \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) for every \(C\ge0\). Under the prior followed by the sequential design, the likelihood contribution at time \(t\) is
\[
\pi_t(w_t\mid w_{1:t-1},y_{1:t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}})
\mathbf1\{y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}=B\xi_{t,w_t}\}.
\]
It contains no unobserved sign \(\xi_{t,1-w_t}\). Conditional on all observed data, the variables \(\varepsilon_t=(1-2W_t)\xi_{t,1-W_t}\) therefore remain independent Rademacher signs, including for deterministic or outcome-adaptive \(\pi\). Moreover,
\[
\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y^\xi)
=M(W,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})+\frac BT\sum_{t=1}^T\varepsilon_t
\]
for an observed-data measurable \(M\). By symmetry and convexity, zero is a median of \(\sum_t\varepsilon_t\), so every conditional absolute-error risk is at least \((B/T)\mathbb E|\sum_t\varepsilon_t|\). If \(S=\sum_t\varepsilon_t\), then \(\mathbb ES^2=T\) and \(\mathbb ES^4=3T^2-2T\le3T^2\). Hölder's inequality implies
\[
\mathbb E|S|\ge
\frac{(\mathbb ES^2)^{3/2}}{(\mathbb ES^4)^{1/2}}
\ge\sqrt{T/3}.
\]
The supremum over fixed arrays dominates this prior average. Taking the two infima proves \(R_T(\beta;C)\ge B/\sqrt{3T}\).

For any fixed pair \((\pi,\widehat\theta)\), its worst-case risk on \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) is at least the infimum defining \(R_T(\beta;C)\). Since the denominator is positive, the corresponding ratio is at least one for every \(\beta\). Taking the supremum over \(\beta\) and then the infimum over procedures gives \(A_T(C)\ge1\). \(\square\)
