\textbf{Proof.} Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:positive-oracle-risk}\) gives, uniformly over \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\),
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\ge B2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}
\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}>0.
\]
The supported prior behind this inequality lies in the no-carryover subclass of every \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\), is fixed before assignment, and leaves an independent counterfactual sign hidden at each time. The lemma applies separately to every \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and every observed-data estimator, so the bound includes deterministic, history-dependent, and outcome-adaptive kernels and requires no positivity condition.

For every \(b\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) constructs a stationary Markov design and an observed-data estimator whose worst-case risk is at most \(U_{T,\beta}(b)\). Independently, Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:bounded-array}\) gives
\[
|\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|
\le\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\{|Y_t(\mathbf1_{1:t})|+|Y_t(\mathbf0_{1:t})|\}
\le2B.
\]
Thus the identically zero estimator, under any fixed assignment rule, has worst-case risk at most \(2B\). These procedures are admissible in the definition of \(R_T(\beta;C)\), and hence
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\le\min\left\{2B,\min_{1\le b\le T}U_{T,\beta}(b)\right\}.
\]
The inner minimum is attained because its domain is a nonempty finite set. A smallest minimizing scale can therefore be selected before assignment from \(T,B,C,\beta\).

Put \(K=8e+4\), \(x=T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), and \(b_\beta=\lceil x\rceil\). If \(T\ge2\), then \(x\ge2^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), so
\[
\frac{b_\beta}{x}\le1+\frac1x\le1+2^{-1/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
Also \(b_\beta\ge x\). The final bound in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\), together with \((T-b_\beta-1)_+/T\le1\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{T,\beta}(b_\beta)
&\le \sqrt K\,B\sqrt{\frac{b_\beta}{T}}+\frac{2C}{\beta}b_\beta^{-\beta}\\
&=\sqrt K\,B\sqrt{\frac{b_\beta}{x}}\,T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}
 +\frac{2C}{\beta}b_\beta^{-\beta}\\
&\le\left[\sqrt{K\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta}\right]T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(T=1\), one has \(b_\beta=1\), \(V_{1,1}=8\), and an empty replacement sum. Therefore \(U_{1,\beta}(1)=\sqrt8\,B\), which is no larger than the same displayed right-hand side because \(K=8e+4>8\).

Finally, \(\beta\le\beta_+\) implies
\[
2^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\le2^{-1/(2\beta_++1)},
\]
whereas \(\beta\ge\beta_->0\) implies \(1/\beta\le1/\beta_-\). Consequently
\[
\sqrt{K\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta}
\le
\sqrt{K\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_++1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}
=C_0^\star.
\]
This proves the complete lower and upper chains. The Markov lemma supplies positive homogeneous-exposure probabilities and an estimator depending only on \((W,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\); its carryover term is the stated expected replacement-error bound derived from the exact exposure-weight identity, rather than an asserted exact bias. The proof supplies no equality between the optimized certificate and oracle risk, and the lower exponent \(1/2\) remains unmatched with the upper exponent \(\beta/(2\beta+1)\). \(\square\)
