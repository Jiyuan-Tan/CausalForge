\textbf{Proof.} Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:positive-oracle-risk}\) gives, uniformly over \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\),
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\ge B2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}
\ge \frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}>0.
\]
The supported prior used for this inequality is drawn before assignment, lies in the no-carryover subclass of every \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\), and leaves one independent counterfactual sign hidden at every time. Its conditional Bayes-risk argument applies separately to every \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and every observed-data estimator. Thus the displayed lower bound holds under deterministic, history-dependent, and outcome-adaptive kernels, without a positivity condition.

For the upper bound, define
\[
b_\beta=\left\lceil T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\right\rceil.
\]
When \(T=1\), one has \(b_\beta=1\). When \(T\ge2\), writing \(x=T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\ge1\),
\[
1\le b_\beta\le x+1\le2x\le T.
\]
Apply Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) with \(b=b_\beta\). Its stationary Markov design belongs to \(\mathcal D_T\), every homogeneous-exposure probability in its Horvitz--Thompson estimator is strictly positive, and
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b_\beta)}}
\left|\widehat\vartheta_{b_\beta}-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le
\sqrt{12e}\,B\sqrt{\frac{b_\beta}{T}}
+\frac{2C}{\beta}\frac{(T-b_\beta-1)_+}{T}b_\beta^{-\beta}.
\]
The ceiling inequalities, \((T-b_\beta-1)_+/T\le1\), and \(b_\beta\ge x\) imply
\[
\sqrt{\frac{b_\beta}{T}}
\le \sqrt2\,T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)},
\qquad
\frac{(T-b_\beta-1)_+}{T}b_\beta^{-\beta}
\le T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
These inequalities also hold directly when \(T=1\). Since \(\beta\ge\beta_-\),
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b_\beta)}}
\left|\widehat\vartheta_{b_\beta}-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le
\left(\sqrt{24e}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right)
T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
Because \(e>1\), the right-hand side is at most
\[
\left(\sqrt{24e}\,B+\frac{2eC}{\beta_-}\right)
T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}
=C_0T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
Taking the infima over estimators and designs proves the asserted oracle upper bound. The proved lower exponent is \(1/2\), whereas the upper exponent is \(\beta/(2\beta+1)\); no step above supplies a lower bound with the latter exponent. \(\square\)
