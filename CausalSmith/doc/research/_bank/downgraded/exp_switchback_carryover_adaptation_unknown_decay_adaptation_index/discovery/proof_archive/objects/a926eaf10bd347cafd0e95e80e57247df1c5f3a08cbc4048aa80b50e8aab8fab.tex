\textbf{Proof.} Fix \(C>0\), \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\), and \(m<T-1\). For the first witness, define
\[
a_m=\frac12\min\{B,C(2+m)^{-(1+\beta)}\}.
\]
Because \(B>0\) and \(C>0\), one has \(a_m>0\), and also \(a_m\le B/2\). Hence the array
\[
Y_t(z_{1:t})=
\begin{cases}
a_m z_{t-m-1},&t\ge m+2,\\
0,&t<m+2
\end{cases}
\]
is bounded in absolute value by \(B\), as required by Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:bounded-array}\).

Consider two histories \(z_{1:t}\) and \(z'_{1:t}\) with \(z_t=z'_t\). When \(t<m+2\), the corresponding outcomes are both zero. When \(t\ge m+2\),
\[
\lvert Y_t(z_{1:t})-Y_t(z'_{1:t})\rvert
=a_m\mathbf 1\{z_{t-m-1}\ne z'_{t-m-1}\}.
\]
Since
\[
a_m\le C(2+m)^{-(1+\beta)},
\]
the last display is at most the lag-\((m+1)\) summand in
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\mathbf 1\{z_{t-\ell}\ne z'_{t-\ell}\}.
\]
Thus Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:historical-polynomial-envelope}\) holds, and Definition~\(\mathrm{def:poly-class}\) gives \(Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\).

At time \(t=m+2\), choose histories that differ in coordinate one and agree in coordinates \(2,\ldots,m+2\). They agree on
\[
z_{(t-m)\vee1:t}=z_{2:m+2},
\]
but their outcomes differ by \(a_m>0\). Therefore Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:finite-order-carryover}\) fails for this array, so Definition~\(\mathrm{def:finite-order-class}\) gives \(Y\notin\mathcal F_{T,m}\). Since the zero array belongs to both classes, while this witness belongs to \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) and not to \(\mathcal F_{T,m}\),
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\cap\mathcal F_{T,m}
\subsetneq\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C).
\]

For the infinite-tail witness, put
\[
S_T=\sum_{u=1}^{T-1}(1+u)^{-(1+\beta)},
\qquad
D_T=2\max\{1,(C/B)S_T\},
\]
and define
\[
\gamma_\ell=\frac{C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}}{D_T},
\qquad
Y_t(z_{1:t})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}\gamma_\ell z_{t-\ell}.
\]
Every \(\gamma_\ell\) is strictly positive. Moreover,
\[
0\le Y_t(z_{1:t})
\le\sum_{\ell=1}^{T-1}\gamma_\ell
=\frac{CS_T}{2\max\{1,(C/B)S_T\}}
\le\frac B2,
\]
so the array satisfies Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:bounded-array}\). For histories having the same current treatment, the triangle inequality yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lvert Y_t(z_{1:t})-Y_t(z'_{1:t})\rvert
&\le\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}\gamma_\ell
\mathbf 1\{z_{t-\ell}\ne z'_{t-\ell}\}\\
&\le\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\mathbf 1\{z_{t-\ell}\ne z'_{t-\ell}\},
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(D_T\ge2\). Hence this array also lies in \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\). Changing only the treatment at lag \(\ell\) changes the outcome by exactly \(\gamma_\ell>0\), which verifies a positive effect at every available lag.

Finally, for any prespecified \(m<T-1\), take \(t=m+2\) and two histories that differ only in coordinate one. They agree on their last \(m+1\) coordinates, but the corresponding outcomes differ by \(\gamma_{m+1}>0\). Thus the same infinite-tail array is outside \(\mathcal F_{T,m}\) for every such prespecified \(m\). No common-design or minimax-optimality conclusion is used. \(\square\)
