\textbf{Proof.} Fix \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\), an estimator \(\widehat\theta\), and \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\). For \(\sigma\in\{-,+\}\), let \(\beta_\sigma\) denote the corresponding endpoint and define
\[
L_\sigma(Y)=\mathbb E_\pi\left|\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|,
\qquad
S_\sigma=\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta_\sigma}(C)}L_\sigma(Y).
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:positive-oracle-risk}\),
\[
R_T(\beta_\sigma;C)>0.
\]
The risk is also finite: every array in \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta_\sigma}(C)\) is bounded by \(B\), hence \(\left|\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|\le 2B\), and the constant estimator zero shows \(R_T(\beta_\sigma;C)\le2B\). By the definition of \(R_T(\beta_\sigma;C)\), the worst-case risk of the fixed pair \((\pi,\widehat\theta)\) satisfies
\[
S_\sigma\ge R_T(\beta_\sigma;C).
\]
Because \((1-\varepsilon)R_T(\beta_\sigma;C)<R_T(\beta_\sigma;C)\le S_\sigma\), the defining property of the supremum yields an array \(Y_\sigma\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta_\sigma}(C)\) for which
\[
L_\sigma(Y_\sigma)\ge (1-\varepsilon)R_T(\beta_\sigma;C).
\]
This conclusion also holds when \(S_\sigma=+\infty\), since the displayed threshold is finite. Set \(\Pi_\sigma=\delta_{Y_\sigma}\). The prior is a point mass on a fixed potential-outcome array selected after the procedure is fixed but before any assignment is drawn; in particular, it does not depend on the realized assignment or observed outcomes. Its Bayes risk is
\[
\mathfrak B_{\Pi_\sigma}(\pi,\widehat\theta)
=\int L_\sigma(Y)\,d\Pi_\sigma(Y)
=L_\sigma(Y_\sigma),
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{\mathfrak B_{\Pi_\sigma}(\pi,\widehat\theta)}{R_T(\beta_\sigma;C)}\ge1-\varepsilon
\qquad(\sigma\in\{-,+\}).
\]
Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:positive-oracle-risk}\) gives, for every fixed procedure and every \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\),
\[
\frac{\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}\mathbb E_\pi\left|\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|}{R_T(\beta;C)}\ge1.
\]
Taking first the supremum over \(\beta\) and then the infimum over \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and \(\widehat\theta\), exactly as in \(\mathrm{def:adaptation-index}\), proves \(A_T(C)\ge1\). The argument uses only separate near-maximizers of the two endpoint risks and supplies no lower bound larger than one. \(\square\)
