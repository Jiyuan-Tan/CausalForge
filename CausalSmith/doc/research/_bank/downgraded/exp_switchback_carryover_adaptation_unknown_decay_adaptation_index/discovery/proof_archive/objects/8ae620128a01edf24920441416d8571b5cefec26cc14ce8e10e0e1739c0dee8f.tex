The complete policy-uniform statement currently proved is
\[
1\le A_T(C)\le \frac{\sqrt{2}\,C_0}{B}T^{1/(4\beta_-+2)},
\qquad
C_0=\sqrt{24e}\,B+\frac{2eC}{\beta_-}.
\]
The upper endpoint is attained by one stationary Markov switchback and observed-data Horvitz--Thompson estimator tuned to \(\beta_-\), and the lower endpoint holds for all sequential and outcome-adaptive kernels. More locally, whenever the rough-prior energy satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_{P_{1,\pi}}\sum_{t=2b}^Tq_t(W_{1:t})^2\le\frac{3a^2}{64},
\]
every estimator has worst-case risk at least \(s_b/25\). If \(\eta=1/2\) is allowed by the amplitude condition, then
\[
\frac{C}{2^{2+\beta}}b^{-\beta}\le s_b\le\frac{C}{2}b^{-\beta},
\]
so this is the desired tail-order lower bound on the low-energy subregime. Independently, for every arm \(\alpha\) and persistence length \(K\),
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}\mathbb E_\pi\left|\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\ge
\frac{(T-K)\delta_K}{2T}\left\{1-Q_\pi(E_{\alpha,K})\right\}.
\]
These certified pieces do not imply a divergent \(g_T\), a subpolynomial common-design ratio, or a sharp order. Accordingly the strongest certified adaptation lower factor remains \(g_T=1\).
