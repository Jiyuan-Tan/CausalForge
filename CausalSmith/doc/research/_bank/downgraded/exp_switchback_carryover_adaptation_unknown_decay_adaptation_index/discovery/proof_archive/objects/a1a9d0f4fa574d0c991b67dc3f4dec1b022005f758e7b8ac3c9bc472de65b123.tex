\textbf{Proof.} We prove the proposed one-sided oracle-rate statement, including the universal root-\(T\) lower bound.

\emph{Upper bound.} Fix \(T\ge2\), \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\), and an integer \(b\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\). Put \(\alpha=(2b)^{-1}\). Draw \(W_1\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and, independently for \(t\ge2\), switch the current arm with probability \(\alpha\). This non-adaptive Markov rule is an element of \(\mathcal D_T\). For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define the homogeneous potential outcome \(y_{t,a}=Y_t(\mathbf a_{1:t})\), let \(L_t=\min\{b,t-1\}\), and set
\[
H_{t,a}=\mathbf1\{W_{t-L_t:t}=a\},
\qquad
p_{t,a}=\mathbb P(H_{t,a}=1)=\frac12(1-\alpha)^{L_t}.
\]
Because \((1-(2b)^{-1})^b\ge1/2\), every denominator satisfies \(p_{t,a}\ge1/4\). Define the explicit observed-data estimator
\[
\widehat\theta_b^{\mathrm M}
=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
\left\{
\frac{H_{t,1}Y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}}{p_{t,1}}
-
\frac{H_{t,0}Y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}}{p_{t,0}}
\right\}.
\]
Also define its ideal homogeneous counterpart
\[
\widetilde\theta_b^{\mathrm M}
=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T X_t,
\qquad
X_t=\frac{H_{t,1}y_{t,1}}{p_{t,1}}-\frac{H_{t,0}y_{t,0}}{p_{t,0}}.
\]
The exposure-probability identity gives
\[
\mathbb E X_t=y_{t,1}-y_{t,0},
\qquad
\mathbb E\widetilde\theta_b^{\mathrm M}=\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y).
\]
On \(H_{t,a}=1\), the realized history agrees with the homogeneous arm-\(a\) history at all available lags when \(t\le b\), and at lags \(1,\ldots,b\) when \(t>b\). The historical envelope and the integral bound therefore give
\[
|Y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}-y_{t,a}|
\le C\sum_{\ell>b}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\le \frac{C}{\beta_-}b^{-\beta}.
\]
At most one of \(H_{t,0},H_{t,1}\) is nonzero. Since \(p_{t,a}^{-1}\le4\), this yields the deterministic comparison
\[
|\widehat\theta_b^{\mathrm M}-\widetilde\theta_b^{\mathrm M}|
\le \frac{4C}{\beta_-}b^{-\beta}.
\]
It remains to control the ideal randomization fluctuation. We have \(|X_t|\le4B\). Write \(\lambda=1-2\alpha=1-b^{-1}\). If \(t-s\le b\), then \(|\operatorname{Cov}(X_s,X_t)|\le16B^2\). If \(t-s>b\), then \(X_t\) is a function of \(W_{t-b:t}\), while \(X_s\) is measurable with respect to assignments through time \(s\). The two-state transition matrix has nonconstant eigenvalue \(\lambda\), so the total-variation distance between the conditional law of \(W_{t-b}\) given the past through \(s\) and its stationary law is at most \(\lambda^{t-b-s}/2\). Consequently,
\[
\left|\mathbb E(X_t\mid W_{1:s})-\mathbb EX_t\right|
\le4B\lambda^{t-b-s},
\]
and hence
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(X_s,X_t)|
\le32B^2\lambda^{t-b-s}.
\]
For \(b=1\), \(\lambda=0\); for \(b>1\), \(\sum_{j\ge0}\lambda^j=(1-\lambda)^{-1}=b\). Summing the covariance bounds gives a universal constant \(c\) such that
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\widetilde\theta_b^{\mathrm M}\right)
\le cB^2\frac bT.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and the deterministic comparison now imply
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E\left|\widehat\theta_b^{\mathrm M}-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le \sqrt c\,B\sqrt{\frac bT}+\frac{4C}{\beta_-}b^{-\beta}.
\]
Take \(b=\lceil T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\rceil\). If \(x=T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), then \(x\le b\le2x\), and therefore
\[
\sqrt{b/T}\le\sqrt2\,T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)},
\qquad
b^{-\beta}\le T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
The definition of \(R_T(\beta;C)\) then gives
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\le C_0T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)},
\]
where \(C_0\) depends only on \(B,C,\beta_-\) and hence may be chosen uniformly on \([\beta_-,\beta_+]\). Enlarging \(C_0\) covers \(T=1\).

\emph{Universal lower bound.} Fix any \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and any observed-data estimator \(\widehat\theta\). Before assignment, draw independent Rademacher variables \((\xi_{t,0},\xi_{t,1})_{t=1}^T\) and define the fixed array
\[
Y_t^\xi(z_{1:t})=B\xi_{t,z_t}.
\]
Every realization has zero historical oscillation and belongs to \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\). Conditional on the observed assignment and outcomes, the unobserved signs \(\xi_{t,1-W_t}\) remain independent Rademacher variables: in the sequential likelihood, every policy factor is measurable before the current outcome is revealed and contains no unobserved current-arm counterfactual. Thus
\[
\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y^\xi)
=M(W,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})+\frac BT\sum_{t=1}^T\varepsilon_t,
\]
where \(M\) is observed-data measurable and the conditional variables \(\varepsilon_t=(1-2W_t)\xi_{t,1-W_t}\) are independent Rademacher variables. Symmetry and convexity show that every conditional absolute-error risk is at least \((B/T)\mathbb E|S_T|\), where \(S_T=\sum_t\varepsilon_t\). Since
\[
\mathbb ES_T^2=T,
\qquad
\mathbb ES_T^4=3T^2-2T\le3T^2,
\]
Hölder's inequality yields \(\mathbb E|S_T|\ge\sqrt{T/3}\). A supremum over fixed arrays dominates this prior average, so
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{3T}}.
\]
Both bounds hold without a positivity condition on the competing sequential kernels. \(\square\)
