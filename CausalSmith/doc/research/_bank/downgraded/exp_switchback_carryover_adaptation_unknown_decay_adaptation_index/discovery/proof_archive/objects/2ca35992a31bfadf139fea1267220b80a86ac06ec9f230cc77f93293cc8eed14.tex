\textbf{Proof.} First consider the classes.  When \(C=0\), the right-hand side of the historical-envelope inequality is zero.  Hence every \(Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(0)\) satisfies
\[
Y_t(z_{1:t})=Y_t(z'_{1:t})\qquad\text{whenever }z_t=z'_t.
\]
Together with the common bound \(\lvert Y_t(z_{1:t})\rvert\le B\), this is exactly the defining condition for \(Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\).  Conversely, an array in \(\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\) has zero difference for every pair with the same contemporaneous treatment, so it satisfies the historical-envelope inequality with \(C=0\).  Thus
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(0)=\mathcal P_T^{(0)}.
\]
The premise of the envelope comparison requires \(z_t=z'_t\), so this substitution imposes no comparison between a path ending in treatment one and a path ending in treatment zero.  In particular, contemporaneous treatment effects remain unrestricted apart from boundedness.  Since the two risk definitions then have the same design class, estimator class, loss, target, and adversarial array class, they give
\[
R_T(\beta;0)=R_T^{(0)}.
\]

We next prove the upper bound.  Use the non-adaptive design under which \(W_1,\ldots,W_T\) are independent \(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) variables.  For a no-carryover array, write \(y_{t,a}\) for the common value of \(Y_t(z_{1:t})\) over paths with \(z_t=a\).  Define the observed-data Horvitz--Thompson estimator
\[
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}
=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\left\{\frac{W_tY_t^{\mathrm{obs}}}{1/2}-\frac{(1-W_t)Y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}}{1/2}\right\}
=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\{2W_ty_{t,1}-2(1-W_t)y_{t,0}\}.
\]
Each summand has expectation \(y_{t,1}-y_{t,0}\), so the estimator is unbiased for \(\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\).  Its centered \(t\)-th summand is
\[
(2W_t-1)(y_{t,1}+y_{t,0}).
\]
Independence of the assignments and \(\lvert y_{t,a}\rvert\le B\) therefore yield
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}})
=\frac1{T^2}\sum_{t=1}^T(y_{t,1}+y_{t,0})^2
\le \frac{4B^2}{T}.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}}
\mathbb E_\pi\left\lvert\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right\rvert
\le \frac{2B}{\sqrt T},
\]
and consequently \(R_T^{(0)}\le 2B T^{-1/2}\).

For the matching lower bound, fix an arbitrary sequential kernel \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and an arbitrary data-measurable estimator \(\widehat\theta\).  Introduce a finite prior solely as a minimax proof device: let \(X_{t,0}\) and \(X_{t,1}\), independently over \(t\) and \(a\), take the values \(-B\) and \(B\) with equal probabilities, and set
\[
Y_t(z_{1:t})=X_{t,z_t}.
\]
Every realized array in the support of this prior is fixed during randomization, is bounded by \(B\), and belongs to \(\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\).  Thus the prior is supported on the design-based adversarial class and introduces no outcome noise into any fixed-array experiment.

Let \(D=(W,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\) denote the full observed data.  Conditional on any data value \(D=d\) having positive probability, the unobserved coordinates \(X_{t,1-W_t}\), \(t=1,\ldots,T\), remain independent and uniform on \(\{-B,B\}\).  Indeed, the joint likelihood factors into the independent prior masses, the indicators fixing the observed coordinates, and
\[
\prod_{t=1}^T\pi_t(W_t\mid W_{1:t-1},Y_{1:t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}),
\]
and the last factor is a function only of the observed data, not of any unobserved coordinate.  This factorization is valid for randomized, deterministic, history-dependent, and outcome-adaptive kernels alike.

Define the data-measurable quantity
\[
K(D)=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T(2W_t-1)Y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
The preceding conditional independence gives
\[
\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\mid D
\ \overset{d}{=}\ K(D)+\frac BT S_T,
\qquad
S_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\varepsilon_t,
\]
where \(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_T\) are conditionally independent Rademacher variables.  For any real \(a\), symmetry and the triangle inequality imply
\[
\mathbb E\lvert a-S_T\rvert
=\frac12\mathbb E\{\lvert a-S_T\rvert+\lvert a+S_T\rvert\}
\ge \mathbb E\lvert S_T\rvert.
\]
Applying this conditionally with \(a=T\{\widehat\theta-K(D)\}/B\) shows that every estimator has conditional absolute risk at least \(B\mathbb E\lvert S_T\rvert/T\).

It remains to bound this elementary Rademacher moment.  Direct expansion gives
\[
\mathbb E S_T^2=T,
\qquad
\mathbb E S_T^4=T+6\binom T2=3T^2-2T\le 3T^2.
\]
Hölder's inequality, applied to \(\lvert S_T\rvert^{2/3}\lvert S_T\rvert^{4/3}\), gives
\[
T=\mathbb E S_T^2
\le (\mathbb E\lvert S_T\rvert)^{2/3}(\mathbb E S_T^4)^{1/3},
\]
and hence
\[
\mathbb E\lvert S_T\rvert
\ge \frac{T^{3/2}}{(\mathbb E S_T^4)^{1/2}}
\ge \sqrt{\frac T3}.
\]
After averaging the conditional risk over \(D\), the Bayes risk under this supported prior is therefore at least \(B/\sqrt{3T}\).  The supremum risk over \(\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\) is no smaller than its prior average, so, for the arbitrary pair \((\pi,\widehat\theta)\),
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}}
\mathbb E_\pi\left\lvert\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right\rvert
\ge \frac{B}{\sqrt{3T}}.
\]
Taking both infima preserves this lower bound.  Combining the bounds proves
\[
\frac{B}{\sqrt{3T}}
\le R_T(\beta;0)=R_T^{(0)}
\le \frac{2B}{\sqrt T},
\]
which is \(R_T(\beta;0)=R_T^{(0)}\asymp_B T^{-1/2}\). \(\square\)
