\textbf{Proof.} Fix \(T\ge 1\), \(B>0\), and \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\). We first verify the class identity. If \(Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(0)\) and two treatment histories have the same current treatment, the historical-envelope inequality gives
\[
\left|Y_t(z_{1:t})-Y_t(z'_{1:t})\right|
\le \sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}0\cdot(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\mathbf 1\{z_{t-\ell}\ne z'_{t-\ell}\}=0.
\]
Thus \(Y_t(z_{1:t})=Y_t(z'_{1:t})\) whenever \(z_t=z'_t\), and boundedness then gives \(Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\). Conversely, if \(Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\), the left-hand side of the same envelope inequality is zero whenever \(z_t=z'_t\), so the inequality with \(C=0\) holds and \(Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(0)\). Hence
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(0)=\mathcal P_T^{(0)}.
\]
The restriction compares only histories having the same current treatment. It therefore imposes no equality between a path ending in treatment one and a path ending in treatment zero; the two contemporaneous potential outcomes remain arbitrary elements of \([-B,B]\).

Because the two risk definitions have the same design class, estimator class, loss, and target, substitution of this class identity yields the exact equality
\[
R_T(\beta;0)=R_T^{(0)}.
\]
It remains to establish the asserted order.

For the upper bound, use the non-adaptive design \(\pi^{\mathrm{iid}}\in\mathcal D_T\) under which \(W_1,\ldots,W_T\) are independent and \(\Pr(W_t=1)=1/2\). For \(Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\), let \(y_{t,a}\) be the common value of \(Y_t(z_{1:t})\) over histories with \(z_t=a\). Then \(Y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}=y_{t,W_t}\), and define the Horvitz--Thompson estimator
\[
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}
=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\left\{\frac{W_tY_t^{\mathrm{obs}}}{1/2}-\frac{(1-W_t)Y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}}{1/2}\right\}.
\]
Writing \(X_t=2W_ty_{t,1}-2(1-W_t)y_{t,0}\), direct calculation gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{\mathrm{iid}}}X_t=y_{t,1}-y_{t,0},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{\mathrm{iid}}}(X_t)=(y_{t,1}+y_{t,0})^2\le 4B^2.
\]
The \(X_t\)'s are independent, so
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{\mathrm{iid}}}\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}
=\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y),
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{\mathrm{iid}}}(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}})\le\frac{4B^2}{T}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz therefore implies, uniformly over \(Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{\mathrm{iid}}}\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le \frac{2B}{\sqrt T},
\]
and consequently \(R_T^{(0)}\le 2B T^{-1/2}\). All inverse-probability denominators in this construction equal \(1/2\).

For the lower bound, fix an arbitrary sequential kernel \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\), with no positivity condition, and an arbitrary data-measurable estimator \(\widehat\theta\). Put a product prior on fixed arrays as follows. Let \((\xi_{t,0},\xi_{t,1})_{t=1}^T\) be \(2T\) independent Rademacher signs and define
\[
Y_t^{\xi}(z_{1:t})=B\xi_{t,z_t}.
\]
Every realization \(Y^\xi\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\). Under the joint law obtained by first drawing \(\xi\) and then applying \(\pi\), the likelihood of an observed path \((w,y^{\mathrm{obs}})\), apart from its prior factor, is
\[
\prod_{t=1}^T
\pi_t\!\left(w_t\mid w_{1:t-1},y_{1:t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}\right)
\mathbf 1\!\left\{y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}=B\xi_{t,w_t}\right\}.
\]
This expression contains no unobserved sign \(\xi_{t,1-w_t}\). Therefore, conditional on the complete observed data, the variables
\[
\varepsilon_t=(1-2W_t)\xi_{t,1-W_t},\qquad t=1,\ldots,T,
\]
remain independent Rademacher signs, even when \(\pi\) is deterministic, history-dependent, or outcome-adaptive. Moreover,
\[
\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y^\xi)
=\frac BT\sum_{t=1}^T(\xi_{t,1}-\xi_{t,0})
=M(W,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})+\frac BT\sum_{t=1}^T\varepsilon_t,
\]
where
\[
M(W,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})=\frac BT\sum_{t=1}^T(2W_t-1)\xi_{t,W_t}
\]
is observed-data measurable. Let \(S_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\varepsilon_t\). The map \(a\mapsto\mathbb E|S_T-a|\) is convex and even, hence is minimized at zero. Conditional on the observed data, every estimator thus has absolute risk at least \((B/T)\mathbb E|S_T|\).

For completeness, this last expectation has a uniform elementary lower bound. Independence gives
\[
\mathbb E S_T^2=T,
\qquad
\mathbb E S_T^4=T+6\binom T2=3T^2-2T\le 3T^2.
\]
Hölder's inequality yields
\[
T=\mathbb E|S_T|^2
=\mathbb E\!\left(|S_T|^{2/3}|S_T|^{4/3}\right)
\le (\mathbb E|S_T|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|S_T|^4)^{1/3},
\]
so
\[
\mathbb E|S_T|\ge
\frac{(\mathbb E S_T^2)^{3/2}}{(\mathbb E S_T^4)^{1/2}}
\ge \sqrt{\frac T3}.
\]
Averaging the conditional lower bound over the observed data and then using that a supremum dominates the prior average gives
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}}
\mathbb E_\pi\left|\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\ge \frac{B}{\sqrt{3T}}.
\]
Since \(\pi\) and \(\widehat\theta\) were arbitrary, \(R_T^{(0)}\ge B/\sqrt{3T}\). Combining the two bounds proves, for every \(T\ge1\),
\[
\frac{B}{\sqrt{3T}}
\le R_T(\beta;0)=R_T^{(0)}
\le \frac{2B}{\sqrt T},
\]
which is \(R_T(\beta;0)=R_T^{(0)}\asymp_B T^{-1/2}\). \(\square\)
