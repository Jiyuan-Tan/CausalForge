\textbf{Proof.} Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:positive-oracle-risk}\) gives, uniformly over \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\),
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\ge B2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}
\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}>0.
\]
The supported prior behind this inequality is fixed before assignment, lies in the no-carryover subclass of every \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\), and leaves one independent counterfactual sign hidden at every time. Its conditional Bayes-risk argument applies separately to every \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and every observed-data estimator. Thus the lower chain includes deterministic, history-dependent, and outcome-adaptive kernels without imposing positivity.

For every \(b\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) constructs an outcome-independent stationary Markov design and observed-data estimator whose worst-case risk is at most \(U_{T,\beta}(b)\). Independently, boundedness gives
\[
|\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|
\le\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\{|Y_t(\mathbf1_{1:t})|+|Y_t(\mathbf0_{1:t})|\}
\le2B.
\]
Consequently the identically zero estimator, under any fixed assignment rule, has worst-case risk at most \(2B\). All these procedures belong to the decision classes in the definition of \(R_T(\beta;C)\), so
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\le\min\left\{2B,\min_{1\le b\le T}U_{T,\beta}(b)\right\}.
\]
The inner minimum is attained because its domain is a nonempty finite set. A smallest minimizing \(b\), if the Markov branch beats the zero estimator, can be selected before assignment from \(T,B,C,\beta\).

Put \(x=T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\) and \(b_\beta=\lceil x\rceil\). For \(T=1\), \(b_\beta=1\). For \(T\ge2\), \(1\le x\le T\), hence \(1\le b_\beta\le T\), and \(b_\beta\le2x\). Therefore
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\le\min\left\{2B,\min_{1\le b\le T}U_{T,\beta}(b)\right\}
\le U_{T,\beta}(b_\beta).
\]
Using \(V_{T,b}\le(8e+4)Tb\), \((T-b-1)_+/T\le1\), \((b+1)^{-\beta}\le b^{-\beta}\), \(b_\beta\le2x\), and \(b_\beta\ge x\), the Markov lemma yields, for \(T\ge2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{T,\beta}(b_\beta)
&\le\sqrt{8e+4}\,B\sqrt{\frac{b_\beta}{T}}
+\frac{2C}{\beta}b_\beta^{-\beta}\\
&\le\left(\sqrt{16e+8}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta}\right)
T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(T=1\), the finite replacement sum is empty and \(V_{1,1}=8\), so the same inequality holds directly. Finally, \(\beta\ge\beta_->0\) gives
\[
\sqrt{16e+8}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta}
\le \sqrt{16e+8}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}=C_0^\star.
\]
This proves the complete lower and upper chains. The beta-aware Markov branches have positive homogeneous-exposure probabilities and use only \((W,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\). Their carryover terms are endpoint-aware expected replacement-error upper bounds obtained using the exact exposure-weight identity, not exact biases. The lower exponent \(1/2\) and upper exponent \(\beta/(2\beta+1)\) remain unmatched. \(\square\)
