\textbf{Proof.} Set \(r=b/(b+1)\), \(s=1/(b+1)\), and \(\lambda=r-s=(b-1)/(b+1)\). The proposed assignment is the stationary symmetric two-state Markov chain with transition matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\s&r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is a non-adaptive member of \(\mathcal D_T\). For each \(t\) and \(a\), stationarity and the Markov property give
\[
\upsilon_{t,b,a}=\frac12r^{k_t}.
\]
Since \((1+1/b)^b<4\), one has \(r^b>1/4\), and therefore
\[
\upsilon_{t,b,a}\ge\frac18.
\]
Thus every denominator is positive, including at every boundary time.

For a fixed array \(Y\), write
\[
y_{t,a}=Y_t(\mathbf a_{1:t})
\]
and introduce the oracle homogeneous-outcome statistic
\[
\widetilde\theta_b=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t,
\qquad
Z_t=\frac{H_{t,b,1}y_{t,1}}{\upsilon_{t,b,1}}-\frac{H_{t,b,0}y_{t,0}}{\upsilon_{t,b,0}}.
\]
The inclusion-probability identity gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}Z_t=y_{t,1}-y_{t,0},
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}\widetilde\theta_b=\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y).
\]
Moreover, \(\lvert Z_t\rvert\le8B\).

We next derive the variance directly. If \(u-t\le b\), boundedness gives
\[
\lvert\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)\rvert\le128B^2.
\]
If \(u-t>b\), then \(Z_t\) is measurable with respect to assignments through time \(t\), while \(Z_u\) is a function of \(W_{u-b:u}\). The two-state transition matrix has the exact power formula
\[
\Pr(W_{t+d}=j\mid W_t=i)=\frac12\left\{1+(2i-1)(2j-1)\lambda^d\right\}.
\]
Conditioning first on \(W_{u-b}\) and then on the assignments through time \(t\), this identity and \(\lvert Z_u\rvert\le8B\) imply
\[
\left\lvert\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{1:t})-\mathbb EZ_u\right\rvert\le8B\lambda^{u-b-t}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\lvert\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)\rvert\le64B^2\lambda^{u-b-t}.
\]
Summing the diagonal, the at most \(b\) overlapping-window covariances on either side, and the geometric tail yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t\right)
\le64B^2T+256B^2Tb+128B^2T\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\lambda^d
\le448B^2Tb,
\]
where \((1-\lambda)^{-1}=(b+1)/2\) and \(b\ge1\) were used in the last step. Jensen's inequality therefore gives the unrelaxed bound
\[
\mathbb E\left\lvert\widetilde\theta_b-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right\rvert
\le\sqrt{448}\,B\sqrt{\frac bT}
=8\sqrt7\,B\sqrt{\frac bT}.
\]

It remains to replace homogeneous potential outcomes by observations. On \(H_{t,b,a}=1\), the current and preceding \(k_t\) assignments all equal \(a\). When \(t\le b\), the entire realized history is homogeneous and the replacement error is zero. When \(t>b\), the historical-envelope assumption and the integral comparison for the polynomial tail give
\[
\left\lvert Y_t(W_{1:t})-y_{t,a}\right\rvert
\le C\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{t-1}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\le\frac{C}{\beta_-}b^{-\beta}.
\]
At most one of \(H_{t,b,0}\) and \(H_{t,b,1}\) is nonzero, and every reciprocal denominator is at most eight. Therefore, pathwise,
\[
\left\lvert\widehat\vartheta_b-\widetilde\theta_b\right\rvert
\le\frac{8C}{\beta_-}b^{-\beta}.
\]
The triangle inequality proves
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}\left\lvert\widehat\vartheta_b-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right\rvert
\le8\sqrt7\,B\sqrt{\frac bT}+\frac{8C}{\beta_-}b^{-\beta}.
\]
As a finite-horizon stress check, for every \(T\le4\), direct summation over the \(2^T\) assignment paths gives \(\mathbb E\{H_{t,b,a}/\upsilon_{t,b,a}\}=1\) at every \(t,a\); hence no time point is omitted and the oracle statistic is exactly unbiased before the displayed envelope error. \(\square\)
