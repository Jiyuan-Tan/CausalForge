\textbf{Proof.} Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:positive-oracle-risk}\) gives, uniformly over \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\),
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\ge B2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}
\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}>0.
\]
The supported prior used there belongs to the no-carryover subclass of every \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\). Because the likelihood cancellation holds separately for every sequential kernel, the lower bound covers deterministic, history-dependent, and outcome-adaptive designs without a positivity restriction.

At \(T=1\), take \(W_1\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) and
\[
\widehat\theta_1=(2W_1-1)Y_1^{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
Since \(\operatorname{GATE}_1(Y)=Y_1(1)-Y_1(0)\), the absolute error is \(|Y_1(0)|\) when \(W_1=1\) and \(|Y_1(1)|\) when \(W_1=0\). Hence Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:bounded-array}\) gives
\[
\mathbb E|\widehat\theta_1-\operatorname{GATE}_1(Y)|
=\frac12\{|Y_1(0)|+|Y_1(1)|\}\le B.
\]
The exact-binomial lower floor equals \(B\) at \(T=1\), so \(R_1(\beta;C)=B\).

For each \(b\), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) constructs an observed-data estimator with worst-case risk at most \(U_{T,\beta}^{\sharp}(b)\). Also, boundedness implies \(|\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|\le2B\), so the zero estimator has worst-case risk at most \(2B\). It follows that, for \(T\ge2\),
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\le\min\left\{2B,\min_{1\le b\le T}U_{T,\beta}^{\sharp}(b)\right\}.
\]
Together with the preceding one-period procedure, this proves
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\le\mathfrak U_{T,\beta}^{\sharp}.
\]
Every minimum for \(T\ge2\) is over a nonempty finite set, so a smallest minimizing scale is selectable before assignment from \(T,B,C,\beta\). The Markov lemma also gives \(\upsilon_{t,b,a}\ge1/(2q_b)>1/(2e)\), with unattained uniform infimum \(1/(2e)\).

Put \(K=8e+4\), \(x=T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), and \(b_\beta=\lceil x\rceil\). For \(T\ge2\),
\[
\frac{b_\beta}{x}\le1+\frac1x\le1+2^{-1/(2\beta+1)},
\qquad b_\beta\ge x.
\]
The rate corollary of the Markov lemma therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{T,\beta}^{\sharp}(b_\beta)
&\le \sqrt K\,B\sqrt{\frac{b_\beta}{T}}+\frac{2C}{\beta}b_\beta^{-\beta}\\
&\le\left[\sqrt{K\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta}\right]T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
At \(T=1\), \(b_\beta=1\), \(k_1=0\), \(V_{1,1}^{\sharp}=4\), and \(E_{1,\beta}^{\sharp}(1)=0\). Thus \(U_{1,\beta}^{\sharp}(1)=2B\), whereas the piecewise finite oracle certificate is \(\mathfrak U_{1,\beta}^{\sharp}=B\) because it uses the separate one-period estimator. Since \(K>4\),
\[
B=\mathfrak U_{1,\beta}^{\sharp}\le U_{1,\beta}^{\sharp}(1)
\le\left[\sqrt{K\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta}\right].
\]
Hence the entire finite-certificate and power-rate chain holds for every \(T\ge1\), without identifying \(U_{1,\beta}^{\sharp}(1)\) with \(B\).

Finally, \(\beta\le\beta_+\) implies
\[
2^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\le2^{-1/(2\beta_++1)},
\]
and \(\beta\ge\beta_->0\) implies \(1/\beta\le1/\beta_-\). Therefore the pointwise coefficient is at most \(C_0^\star\). This proves all displayed inequalities. The finite variance certificate uses exact pair counts, while the carryover term is a bounded expected replacement-error upper bound; neither is asserted to be an exact risk or exact bias. The lower and upper exponents remain unmatched. \(\square\)
