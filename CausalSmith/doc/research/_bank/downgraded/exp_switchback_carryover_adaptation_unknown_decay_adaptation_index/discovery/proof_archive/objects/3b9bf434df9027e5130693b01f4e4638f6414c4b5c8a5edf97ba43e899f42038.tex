\textbf{Proof.} Fix \(\beta\). We first identify the two classes. Substituting \(C=0\) in the definition of \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\), for every \(t\) and every pair of histories satisfying \(z_t=z'_t\), gives
\[
\left|Y_t(z_{1:t})-Y_t(z'_{1:t})\right|
\le
\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}0\cdot(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\mathbf 1\{z_{t-\ell}\ne z'_{t-\ell}\}=0.
\]
Hence \(Y_t(z_{1:t})=Y_t(z'_{1:t})\) whenever \(z_t=z'_t\). Together with \(\lvert Y_t(z_{1:t})\rvert\le B\), this is exactly the defining condition for \(\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\). Conversely, if \(Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\), then the left-hand side of the preceding envelope inequality is zero whenever \(z_t=z'_t\), so the inequality with \(C=0\) holds and \(Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(0)\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(0)=\mathcal P_T^{(0)}.
\]
The restriction was imposed only on pairs with equal contemporaneous assignments. It therefore does not equate \(Y_t(1,z_{1:t-1})\) with \(Y_t(0,z'_{1:t-1})\); these two values remain unrestricted apart from the bound by \(B\). Because the definitions of \(R_T(\beta;0)\) and \(R_T^{(0)}\) then have the same design class, estimator class, loss, target, and adversarial class, the class equality implies
\[
R_T(\beta;0)=R_T^{(0)}.
\]

We next prove the upper risk bound by an explicit design and estimator. Assign \(W_1,\ldots,W_T\) independently as \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\). This non-adaptive rule belongs to \(\mathcal D_T\). For \(Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\), let \(y_{t,a}\) be the common value of \(Y_t(z_{1:t})\) over all paths with \(z_t=a\). Define the timewise Horvitz--Thompson estimator
\[
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}
=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
\left\{
\frac{W_tY_t^{\mathrm{obs}}}{1/2}
-\frac{(1-W_t)Y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}}{1/2}
\right\}
=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
\{2W_ty_{t,1}-2(1-W_t)y_{t,0}\}.
\]
The expectation of the \(t\)-th unscaled summand is \(y_{t,1}-y_{t,0}\), so \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}\) is unbiased for \(\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\). Its centered \(t\)-th unscaled summand is
\[
(2W_t-1)(y_{t,1}+y_{t,0}).
\]
Independence across times and \(\lvert y_{t,a}\rvert\le B\) yield
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}})
=\frac1{T^2}\sum_{t=1}^T(y_{t,1}+y_{t,0})^2
\le\frac{4B^2}{T}.
\]
Thus Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}}
\mathbb E_\pi\left|\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le\frac{2B}{\sqrt T},
\]
and consequently \(R_T^{(0)}\le 2B T^{-1/2}\).

For the matching-order lower bound, fix an arbitrary \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and an arbitrary observed-data estimator \(\widehat\theta\). Draw mutually independent variables \(X_{t,a}\), for \(t=1,\ldots,T\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), each uniformly distributed on \(\{-B,B\}\), before assignment, and define
\[
Y_t(z_{1:t})=X_{t,z_t}.
\]
Every realization is a fixed bounded array in \(\mathcal P_T^{(0)}\); the prior is only a minimax proof device and introduces no outcome noise conditional on the realized array.

Let \(D=(W,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\). Conditional on any value of \(D\) having positive probability, the unobserved coordinates \(X_{t,1-W_t}\), \(t=1,\ldots,T\), remain mutually independent and uniform on \(\{-B,B\}\). Indeed, the joint mass of the latent coordinates and the observed data factors into the independent prior masses, indicators fixing the observed coordinates, and
\[
\prod_{t=1}^T
\pi_t\bigl(W_t\mid W_{1:t-1},Y_{1:t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}\bigr).
\]
The last factor is measurable with respect to \(D\) and contains no unobserved coordinate. This factorization applies to deterministic, randomized, history-dependent, and outcome-adaptive kernels and requires no positivity assumption.

Define
\[
K(D)=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T(2W_t-1)Y_t^{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
The preceding conditional factorization gives
\[
\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\mid D
\ \overset{d}{=}\
K(D)+\frac BT S_T,
\qquad
S_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\varepsilon_t,
\]
where, conditionally on \(D\), \(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_T\) are independent Rademacher variables. For every real number \(a\), symmetry of \(S_T\) and the triangle inequality imply
\[
\mathbb E|a-S_T|
=\frac12\mathbb E\{|a-S_T|+|a+S_T|\}
\ge\mathbb E|S_T|.
\]
Applying this inequality conditionally with \(a=T\{\widehat\theta-K(D)\}/B\) yields
\[
\mathbb E\left[
\left|\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\mathrel{\big|}D
\right]
\ge\frac BT\mathbb E|S_T|.
\]

We now evaluate the Rademacher absolute moment. If \(J\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(T,1/2)\), then \(S_T\) has the distribution of \(2J-T\). With \(r=\lfloor T/2\rfloor+1\), symmetry and the identity
\[
(2j-T)\binom Tj
=T\left\{\binom{T-1}{j-1}-\binom{T-1}{j}\right\}
\]
give the telescoping formula
\[
\mathbb E|S_T|
=2^{1-T}\sum_{j=r}^T(2j-T)\binom Tj
=T2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}.
\]
This exact expression is at least \(\sqrt{T/2}\). To verify the inequality, put
\[
p_k=4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k},\qquad k\ge1.
\]
Since \(p_1=1/2\) and
\[
\frac{p_{k+1}}{p_k}=\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}
\ge\sqrt{\frac{k}{k+1}},
\]
induction gives \(p_k\ge(2\sqrt{k})^{-1}\). If \(n=2k\), then
\[
2^{-n}\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
=p_k\ge\frac1{\sqrt{2n}}
\ge\frac1{\sqrt{2(n+1)}};
\]
if \(n=2k+1\), then
\[
2^{-n}\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
=p_{k+1}\ge\frac1{2\sqrt{k+1}}
=\frac1{\sqrt{2(n+1)}}.
\]
The same inequality is immediate for \(n=0\). Taking \(n=T-1\) in the exact formula proves \(\mathbb E|S_T|\ge\sqrt{T/2}\).

After averaging over \(D\), the Bayes risk under this supported prior is at least \(B/\sqrt{2T}\). Since supremum risk is no smaller than prior-average risk, the arbitrary pair \((\pi,\widehat\theta)\) obeys
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_T^{(0)}}
\mathbb E_\pi\left|\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}.
\]
Taking the two infima gives \(R_T^{(0)}\ge B/\sqrt{2T}\). Combining the bounds,
\[
\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}
\le R_T(\beta;0)=R_T^{(0)}
\le\frac{2B}{\sqrt T}.
\]
Therefore \(R_T(\beta;0)=R_T^{(0)}\asymp_B T^{-1/2}\), with the claimed independent Bernoulli design and Horvitz--Thompson estimator. \(\square\)
