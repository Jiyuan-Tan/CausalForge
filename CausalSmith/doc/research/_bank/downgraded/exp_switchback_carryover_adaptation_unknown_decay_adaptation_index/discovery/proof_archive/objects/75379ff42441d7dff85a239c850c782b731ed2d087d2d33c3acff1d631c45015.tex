\textbf{Proof.} The claim is precisely \(\mathrm{lem:finite-order-witnesses}\). For completeness, the first witness has amplitude
\[
a_m=\frac12\min\{B,C(2+m)^{-(1+\beta)}\}>0.
\]
It is bounded by \(B/2\), and changing its only active historical coordinate changes the outcome by at most the lag-\((m+1)\) term \(C(2+m)^{-(1+\beta)}\) in the envelope. At time \(m+2\), two histories can agree on their last \(m+1\) coordinates and differ at coordinate one, so the resulting outcomes differ by \(a_m\); hence this array is not in \(\mathcal F_{T,m}\).

For the second witness, write
\[
S=\sum_{u=1}^{T-1}(1+u)^{-(1+\beta)},
\qquad
D=2\max\{1,(C/B)S\}.
\]
Then \(\gamma_\ell=C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}/D>0\) and
\[
0\le \sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}\gamma_\ell z_{t-\ell}
\le \frac{CS}{2\max\{1,(C/B)S\}}
\le \frac B2.
\]
The triangle inequality verifies the historical envelope term by term because \(D\ge2\). Since \(\gamma_{m+1}>0\), the same pair of histories at time \(m+2\) violates finite order \(m\). The zero array belongs to the intersection, whereas either displayed witness belongs to \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) and not to \(\mathcal F_{T,m}\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\cap\mathcal F_{T,m}
\subsetneq \mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C).
\]
No consequence involving a common adaptive design is used or asserted. \(\square\)
