\textbf{Proof.} For every design-estimator pair and every \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\), its worst-case risk over \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) is at least \(R_T(\beta;C)\). Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) makes this denominator strictly positive. Every ratio in the definition of \(A_T(C)\) is therefore at least one, whence \(A_T(C)\ge1\).

At \(T=1\), Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\) gives \(R_1(\beta;C)=B\) for every \(\beta\). Use the common fixed-before-assignment rule \(W_1\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) with
\[
\widehat\theta_1=(2W_1-1)Y_1^{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
The calculation in that theorem shows that this rule has worst-case risk at most \(B\) simultaneously over all \(\beta\). Thus its simultaneous ratio is at most one, and the universal lower argument makes it exactly one. Hence \(A_1(C)=1\). The piecewise definitions give \(N_1^\star=N_1^{\mathrm{fin}}=D_1^{\mathrm{bin}}=B\), so every displayed ratio is also exactly one at this boundary.

Suppose henceforth that \(T\ge2\). For \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\) and \(\ell\ge1\),
\[
C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\le C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta_-)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\subseteq\mathcal P_{T,\beta_-}(C).
\]
The finite set \(\{1,\ldots,T\}\) contains a smallest minimizer \(b^\star\) of \(U_{T,\beta_-}^{\sharp}(b)\). Before assignment, compare \(2B\) with \(U_{T,\beta_-}^{\sharp}(b^\star)\). If \(2B\) is no larger, use the zero estimator under any fixed assignment rule. Otherwise use the Markov switchback and Horvitz--Thompson estimator of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) at \(b=b^\star\). This common procedure depends only on \(T,B,C,\beta_-\), not on the realized \(\beta\). Its risk on every admissible class is at most \(N_T^\star\). In its Markov branch,
\[
\upsilon_{t,b^\star,a}\ge\frac{1}{2q_{b^\star}}>\frac{1}{2e},
\]
with unattained uniform infimum \(1/(2e)\).

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) gives
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\ge D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}>0
\]
for every \(\beta\). Taking the simultaneous ratio for the common procedure and then the infimum defining \(A_T(C)\) proves
\[
A_T(C)\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}.
\]
The denominator is the exact binomial Bayes lower bound on oracle risk, not the unknown exact oracle risk.

Put \(b_-=\lceil T^{1/(2\beta_-+1)}\rceil\). For \(T\ge2\), this scale is one candidate in the finite minimum, so
\[
N_T^\star\le U_{T,\beta_-}^{\sharp}(b_-)=N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}.
\]
The pointwise ceiling calculation in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\), specialized to \(\beta_-\), gives
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
\le
\left[\sqrt{(8e+4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right]
T^{-\beta_-/(2\beta_-+1)}.
\]
This inequality also holds at \(T=1\) because then \(N_1^{\mathrm{fin}}=B\). Finally,
\[
D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}.
\]
Division by this positive denominator yields
\[
1\le A_T(C)
\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}
\le\frac{N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}
\le\frac{\sqrt2}{B}
\left[\sqrt{(8e+4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right]
T^{1/(4\beta_-+2)}.
\]
The coefficient is endpoint-specific because the common procedure may use the known endpoint \(\beta_-\); the larger interval-uniform constant \(C_0^\star\) is unnecessary. This remains a polynomial common-design certificate and proves neither K1, K2, nor sharpness. \(\square\)
