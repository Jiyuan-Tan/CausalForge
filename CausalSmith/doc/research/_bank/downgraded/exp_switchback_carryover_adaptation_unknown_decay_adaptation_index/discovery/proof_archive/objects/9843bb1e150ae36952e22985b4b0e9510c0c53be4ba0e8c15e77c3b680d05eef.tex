\textbf{Proof.} We first record the nesting that makes a common endpoint-tuned design valid. If \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\), then for every lag \(\ell\ge1\),
\[
C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\le C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta_-)}.
\]
Comparing the member conditions in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:poly-class}\) therefore gives
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\subseteq\mathcal P_{T,\beta_-}(C).
\tag{1}
\]

For any design-estimator pair \((\pi,\widehat\theta)\), write
\[
\mathcal R_\beta(\pi,\widehat\theta)
=\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E_\pi\left\lvert\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right\rvert.
\]
By the definition of \(R_T(\beta;C)\),
\[
\mathcal R_\beta(\pi,\widehat\theta)\ge R_T(\beta;C).
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) makes the denominator strictly positive. Thus every ratio \(\mathcal R_\beta(\pi,\widehat\theta)/R_T(\beta;C)\) is at least one. Taking first the supremum over \(\beta\) and then the infimum over \((\pi,\widehat\theta)\) proves
\[
A_T(C)\ge1.
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose first that \(T\ge2\). Since the scale set is finite, choose the smallest
\[
b^\star\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{1\le b\le T}U_{T,\beta_-}^{\dagger}(b).
\]
Compare the Markov switchback and Horvitz--Thompson estimator at scale \(b^\star\) with the zero estimator, and select the branch having the smaller displayed certificate. This selection depends only on \(T,B,C,\beta_-\) and is made before assignment. By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\), the selected procedure has worst-case risk over \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta_-}(C)\) at most
\[
N_T^\star=\min\left\{2B,\min_{1\le b\le T}U_{T,\beta_-}^{\dagger}(b)\right\}.
\]
By (1), the same single procedure satisfies
\[
\mathcal R_\beta(\pi^\star,\widehat\theta^\star)\le N_T^\star
\qquad\text{for every }\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+].
\tag{3}
\]
The exact lower bound in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) gives
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\ge D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}>0
\qquad\text{for every }\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+].
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (3)--(4), then using this procedure as a witness in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:adaptation-index}\), yields
\[
A_T(C)\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(b_-=\lceil T^{1/(2\beta_-+1)}\rceil\) is an admissible scale,
\[
N_T^\star\le U_{T,\beta_-}^{\dagger}(b_-)=N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}.
\tag{6}
\]

Apply Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\) at \(\beta=\beta_-\) to its explicit scale \(b_-\). For \(T\ge2\), it gives
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
\le\left[\sqrt{(8e-4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right]
T^{-\beta_-/(2\beta_-+1)}.
\tag{7}
\]
The second inequality in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) gives
\[
D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}.
\tag{8}
\]
Dividing (7) by (8) is legitimate because \(B>0\). Since
\[
\frac12-\frac{\beta_-}{2\beta_-+1}=\frac{1}{4\beta_-+2},
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}
\le\frac{\sqrt2}{B}
\left[\sqrt{(8e-4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right]
T^{1/(4\beta_-+2)}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (2), (5), (6), and (9) prove the asserted chain for \(T\ge2\).

At \(T=1\), Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\) gives \(R_1(\beta;C)=B\) for every \(\beta\), and its fair-Bernoulli one-period procedure has worst-case risk at most \(B\) for every \(\beta\). Hence its simultaneous ratio is at most one; (2) forces
\[
A_1(C)=1.
\]
The definitions give
\[
N_1^\star=N_1^{\mathrm{fin}}=D_1^{\mathrm{bin}}=B.
\]
The right side of (9), evaluated at \(T=1\), is larger than one, so the complete chain also holds at this horizon.

In the Markov branch, the exposure lower bound and its unattained uniform infimum are exactly those established in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\). The coefficient in (9) is endpoint-specific because the common procedure is permitted to use the known endpoint \(\beta_-\), though it does not use the realized \(\beta\). The denominators in (4)--(9) are the exact binomial Bayes lower certificate, not the unknown exact oracle risk except when \(T=1\). Since the exponent in (9) is positive, this argument supplies only the stated polynomial certificate and proves neither divergent adaptation loss, subpolynomial adaptation, nor sharpness. \(\square\)
