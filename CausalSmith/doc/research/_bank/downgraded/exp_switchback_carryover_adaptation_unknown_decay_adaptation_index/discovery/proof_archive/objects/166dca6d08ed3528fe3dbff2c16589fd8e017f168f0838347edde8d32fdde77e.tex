\textbf{Proof.} Fix \(C>0\), \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\), and \(m<T-1\). For the first witness, define
\[
a_m=\frac12\min\{B,C(2+m)^{-(1+\beta)}\}>0
\]
and set \(Y_t(z_{1:t})=a_m z_{t-m-1}\) when \(t\ge m+2\), with \(Y_t(z_{1:t})=0\) otherwise. Since \(0\le Y_t(z_{1:t})\le a_m\le B/2\), the bounded-array condition holds. If \(z_t=z'_t\), then for \(t<m+2\) both outcomes are zero, while for \(t\ge m+2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|Y_t(z_{1:t})-Y_t(z'_{1:t})\right|
&=a_m\left|z_{t-m-1}-z'_{t-m-1}\right|\\
&\le C(2+m)^{-(1+\beta)}\mathbf 1\{z_{t-m-1}\ne z'_{t-m-1}\}\\
&\le \sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\mathbf 1\{z_{t-\ell}\ne z'_{t-\ell}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality retains the term indexed by \(\ell=m+1\). Hence this witness belongs to \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\).

At time \(t=m+2\), take two histories that differ in coordinate \(1\) and agree in coordinates \(2,\ldots,m+2\). Their suffixes \(z_{(t-m)\vee1:t}=z_{2:m+2}\) coincide, but their outcomes differ by \(a_m>0\). The defining finite-order restriction therefore fails, so this witness does not belong to \(\mathcal F_{T,m}\). Since intersection with \(\mathcal F_{T,m}\) is automatically a subset of \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\), this witness proves
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\cap\mathcal F_{T,m}\subsetneq\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C).
\]

For the infinite-tail witness, put
\[
S_T=\sum_{u=1}^{T-1}(1+u)^{-(1+\beta)},
\qquad
D_T=2\max\{1,(C/B)S_T\},
\]
so that \(\gamma_\ell=C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}/D_T>0\) for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,T-1\}\). Define
\[
Y_t(z_{1:t})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}\gamma_\ell z_{t-\ell}.
\]
All summands are nonnegative, and
\[
0\le Y_t(z_{1:t})\le\sum_{\ell=1}^{T-1}\gamma_\ell
=\frac{CS_T}{2\max\{1,(C/B)S_T\}}\le\frac B2.
\]
Indeed, if the maximum equals \(1\), then \(CS_T\le B\), while if it equals \((C/B)S_T\), the displayed ratio equals \(B/2\). Thus the array is bounded. For histories with the same current treatment, the triangle inequality and \(D_T\ge1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|Y_t(z_{1:t})-Y_t(z'_{1:t})\right|
&\le\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}\gamma_\ell\mathbf 1\{z_{t-\ell}\ne z'_{t-\ell}\}\\
&\le\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\mathbf 1\{z_{t-\ell}\ne z'_{t-\ell}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore this second witness also belongs to \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\). For each lag \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,T-1\}\), at time \(t=\ell+1\), changing only the coordinate \(z_{t-\ell}\) from zero to one changes the outcome by \(\gamma_\ell>0\); hence the witness has a positive effect at every available historical lag. In particular, for any prespecified \(m<T-1\), choose \(t=m+2\) and two histories that differ only in coordinate \(1\). They agree on coordinates \(2,\ldots,m+2\), hence on the suffix required by the order-\(m\) restriction, but their outcomes differ by \(\gamma_{m+1}>0\). Consequently this single infinite-tail array lies outside every prespecified \(\mathcal F_{T,m}\) with \(m<T-1\). \(\square\)
