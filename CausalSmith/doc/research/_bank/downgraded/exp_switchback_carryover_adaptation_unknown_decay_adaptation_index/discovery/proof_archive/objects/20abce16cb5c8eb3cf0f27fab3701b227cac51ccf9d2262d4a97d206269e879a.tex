\textbf{Proof.} For any design-estimator pair and any \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\), its worst-case risk over \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) is at least \(R_T(\beta;C)\). Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) makes this denominator strictly positive. Hence every ratio in the definition of \(A_T(C)\) is at least one, and taking the supremum over \(\beta\) and then the infimum over procedures proves \(A_T(C)\ge1\).

For every \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\) and every \(\ell\ge1\),
\[
C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\le C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta_-)}.
\]
Thus the class definitions imply
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\subseteq\mathcal P_{T,\beta_-}(C).
\]
The finite set \(\{1,\ldots,T\}\) contains a minimizer \(b^\star\) of \(U_{T,\beta_-}(b)\). Before assignment, compare \(2B\) with \(U_{T,\beta_-}(b^\star)\). If the former is no larger, use the zero estimator under any fixed assignment rule; otherwise use the stationary Markov switchback and Horvitz--Thompson estimator from Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) at \(b=b^\star\). Ties may be resolved deterministically. This selected procedure depends only on \(T,B,C,\beta_-\), is fixed before assignment, and is independent of the realized \(\beta\).

The zero-estimator branch has risk at most \(2B\) on every class. In the Markov branch, nestedness and the Markov lemma at \(\beta_-\) bound the risk simultaneously on every admissible class by \(U_{T,\beta_-}(b^\star)\). Hence the one selected procedure satisfies
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E\left|\widehat\theta^\star-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le N_T^\star
\qquad\text{for every }\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+].
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) gives
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\ge D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}>0
\qquad\text{for every }\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+].
\]
Taking the simultaneous ratio for this common procedure and then the infimum defining \(A_T(C)\) proves
\[
A_T(C)\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}.
\]
The denominator here is an exact Bayes lower bound on oracle risk, not the unknown exact oracle risk.

Put \(b_-=\lceil T^{1/(2\beta_-+1)}\rceil\). Since \(b_-\) is one candidate in the finite minimum,
\[
N_T^\star
\le U_{T,\beta_-}(b_-)
=N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}.
\]
The finite-sum and variance bounds of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) yield
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
\le \sqrt{10e+2}\,B\sqrt{\frac{b_-}{T}} +\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\frac{(T-b_--1)_+}{T}b_-^{-\beta_-}.
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\), specialized to \(\beta_-\), or equivalently its ceiling calculation, gives
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}\le C_0T^{-\beta_-/(2\beta_-+1)}.
\]
Finally, \(D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}\ge B/\sqrt{2T}\). Dividing the preceding inequalities by the positive denominator proves
\[
1\le A_T(C)
\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}
\le\frac{N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}
\le\frac{\sqrt2\,C_0}{B}T^{1/(4\beta_-+2)}.
\]
This is a common-design polynomial upper certificate. It proves neither divergence, subpolynomial adaptation, nor sharpness. \(\square\)
