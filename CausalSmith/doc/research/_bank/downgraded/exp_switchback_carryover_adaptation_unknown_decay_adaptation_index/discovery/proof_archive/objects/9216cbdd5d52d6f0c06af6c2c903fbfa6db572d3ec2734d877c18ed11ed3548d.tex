\textbf{Proof.} Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:positive-oracle-risk}\) gives, uniformly over \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\),
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\ge B2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}
\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}>0.
\]
The supported prior behind this inequality is fixed before assignment, lies in the no-carryover subclass of every \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\), and leaves one independent counterfactual sign hidden at every time. Its conditional Bayes-risk argument applies separately to every \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and every observed-data estimator. Thus the lower chain includes deterministic, history-dependent, and outcome-adaptive kernels without imposing positivity.

For the upper bound, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) constructs, for every \(b\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), an outcome-independent stationary Markov design and observed-data estimator whose risk is at most \(U_{T,\beta}(b)\). The same lemma shows that the zero estimator has risk at most \(2B\). These are all legitimate elements of the two decision classes in the definition of \(R_T(\beta;C)\). Therefore
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\le\min\left\{2B,\min_{1\le b\le T}U_{T,\beta}(b)\right\}.
\]
The inner minimum is attained because its domain is the nonempty finite set \(\{1,\ldots,T\}\). When this branch is used, a minimizing \(b\) can be selected before assignment from the known quantities \(T,B,C,\beta\); it does not depend on the realized assignment or outcomes.

Put
\[
b_\beta=\left\lceil T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\right\rceil.
\]
If \(T=1\), then \(b_\beta=1\). If \(T\ge2\), setting \(x=T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\) gives \(1\le x\le T\), hence \(1\le b_\beta\le T\). It follows directly from the minimum that
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\le\min\left\{2B,\min_{1\le b\le T}U_{T,\beta}(b)\right\}
\le U_{T,\beta}(b_\beta).
\]
The pointwise corollary of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) now gives
\[
U_{T,\beta}(b_\beta)
\le\left(\sqrt{20e+4}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta}\right)T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
Since \(\beta\ge\beta_->0\),
\[
\sqrt{20e+4}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta}
\le\sqrt{20e+4}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}=C_0.
\]
This proves the entire displayed upper chain. The beta-aware Markov branch has positive homogeneous-exposure probabilities and uses only \((W,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\). Its carryover term is the endpoint-aware expected replacement-error upper bound obtained using the exact exposure-weight identity, not an exact bias. The lower exponent \(1/2\) and upper exponent \(\beta/(2\beta+1)\) remain unmatched. \(\square\)
