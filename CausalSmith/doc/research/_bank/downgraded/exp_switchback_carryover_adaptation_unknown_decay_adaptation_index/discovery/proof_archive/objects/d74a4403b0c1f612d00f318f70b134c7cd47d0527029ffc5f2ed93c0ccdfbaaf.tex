\textbf{Proof.} For every \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\) and \(\ell\ge1\),
\[
C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\le C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta_-)}.
\]
The defining envelope and the common boundedness condition therefore give
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\subseteq\mathcal P_{T,\beta_-}(C).
\tag{1}
\]

First let \(T\ge2\). Compare the zero estimator with the finitely many Markov procedures of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\), and select before assignment a procedure certified at \(\beta_-\) with bound \(N_T^\star\). If the selected branch is Markov, use the smallest minimizing scale. By \((1)\), this single procedure, which depends only on \(T,B,C,\beta_-\), satisfies
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E_\pi\left|\widehat\theta-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le N_T^\star
\quad\text{for all }\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+].
\tag{2}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) gives
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\ge D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}>0
\quad\text{for all }\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+].
\tag{3}
\]
Divide \((2)\) by \((3)\), take the supremum over \(\beta\), and use the selected procedure as a candidate in the infimum defining \(A_T(C)\). This proves
\[
A_T(C)\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}.
\tag{4}
\]
Conversely, for any fixed design and estimator, its worst-case risk at each \(\beta\) is at least the infimum \(R_T(\beta;C)\). Division is valid by \((3)\), so its ratio is at least one for each \(\beta\). Taking the supremum and then the infimum proves \(A_T(C)\ge1\).

The admissibility of \(b_-=\lceil T^{1/(2\beta_-+1)}\rceil\) and the definitions imply
\[
N_T^\star\le U_{T,\beta_-}^{\mathrm{cond}}(b_-)=N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}.
\tag{5}
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\), evaluated at \(\beta_-\), gives
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
\le\left[\sqrt{(8e-4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac C{\beta_-}\right]T^{-\beta_-/(2\beta_-+1)}.
\tag{6}
\]
Also, \((3)\) gives \(D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}\ge B/\sqrt{2T}\). Dividing \((6)\) by this lower bound and using
\[
\frac12-\frac{\beta_-}{2\beta_-+1}=\frac1{4\beta_-+2}
\]
proves the final polynomial inequality.

For \(T=1\), Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\) gives \(R_1(\beta;C)=B\). Under the common fair-Bernoulli rule and stated estimator, the two assignment-specific errors are \(|Y_1(0)|\) and \(|Y_1(1)|\), so its worst-case risk is exactly \(B\). Hence its ratio is one for every \(\beta\), while the preceding general lower-bound argument gives \(A_1(C)\ge1\). Thus \(A_1(C)=1\), and the definitions give \(N_1^\star=N_1^{\mathrm{fin}}=D_1^{\mathrm{bin}}=B\). The last inequality at \(T=1\) follows from the corresponding \(T=1\) coefficient bound in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\).

Any selected Markov branch inherits the exposure floor \(\upsilon_{t,b,a}\ge1/(2q_b)>1/(2e)\) and its unattained uniform infimum from Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\). The numerator coefficient is endpoint-specific because the common procedure may use the known \(\beta_-\). The denominator in \((4)\) is only the exact binomial lower bound on oracle risk, except at \(T=1\); consequently the result is a polynomial certificate and asserts neither K1, K2, nor sharpness. \(\square\)
