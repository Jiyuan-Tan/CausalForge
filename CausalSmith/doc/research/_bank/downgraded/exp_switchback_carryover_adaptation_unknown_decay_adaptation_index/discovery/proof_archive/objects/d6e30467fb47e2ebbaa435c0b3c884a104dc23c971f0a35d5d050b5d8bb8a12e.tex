\textbf{Proof.} For any fixed design-estimator pair and any \(\beta\), its worst-case risk over \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) is at least \(R_T(\beta;C)\). Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) makes this denominator strictly positive. Thus every ratio in the definition of \(A_T(C)\) is at least one, and hence \(A_T(C)\ge1\).

For the upper bound, put
\[
b_-=\left\lceil T^{1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right\rceil
\]
and use the single stationary Markov design and observed-data estimator of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) with this value of \(b\). For every \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\) and every \(\ell\ge1\),
\[
C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\le C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta_-)}.
\]
The class definitions therefore give
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\subseteq\mathcal P_{T,\beta_-}(C).
\]
Applying the maximal replacement-bias inequality in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) at \(\beta_-\), simultaneously for all \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\), yields
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b_-)}}|\widehat\vartheta_{b_-}-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|
\le N_T,
\]
where
\[
N_T=\sqrt{12e}\,B\sqrt{\frac{b_-}{T}}
+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\frac{(T-b_--1)_+}{T}b_-^{-\beta_-}.
\]
On the other hand, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) gives, for every such \(\beta\),
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\ge D_T,
\qquad
D_T=B2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}.
\]
The risk ratio of this one common procedure is therefore at most \(N_T/D_T\). Taking the supremum over \(\beta\) and then the infimum defining \(A_T(C)\) proves
\[
A_T(C)\le
\frac{\sqrt{12e}\,B\sqrt{b_-/T}+(2C/\beta_-)((T-b_--1)_+/T)b_-^{-\beta_-}}
{B2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}}.
\]
For the simpler corollary, the ceiling inequalities give
\[
N_T\le C_0T^{-\beta_-/(2\beta_-+1)},
\qquad
C_0=\sqrt{24e}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-},
\]
while \(D_T\ge B/\sqrt{2T}\). Consequently
\[
A_T(C)
\le\frac{\sqrt2\,C_0}{B}T^{1/(4\beta_-+2)}.
\]
This proves both proposed upper bounds for the same common procedure; neither calculation asserts sharpness or resolves K1 or K2. \(\square\)
