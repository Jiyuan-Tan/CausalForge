\textbf{Proof.} Put \(r=b/(b+1)\), \(s=1/(b+1)\), and \(\lambda=r-s=(b-1)/(b+1)\). The assignment process is the stationary symmetric two-state Markov chain with transition matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\s&r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is outcome-independent and hence belongs to \(\mathcal D_T\). Stationarity and the Markov property give
\[
\upsilon_{t,b,a}=\frac12r^{k_t}.
\]
Because \((1+1/b)^b<e\),
\[
\upsilon_{t,b,a}\ge\frac12r^b>\frac1{2e}.
\]
Thus every denominator is strictly positive and every reciprocal is less than \(2e\).

Fix \(Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) and write \(y_{t,a}=Y_t(\mathbf a_{1:t})\). Define the oracle homogeneous-outcome statistic
\[
\widetilde\theta_b=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T Z_t,
\qquad
Z_t=\frac{H_{t,b,1}y_{t,1}}{\upsilon_{t,b,1}}-
\frac{H_{t,b,0}y_{t,0}}{\upsilon_{t,b,0}}.
\]
The inclusion-probability identity yields
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}Z_t=y_{t,1}-y_{t,0},
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}\widetilde\theta_b=\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y).
\]
The events \(H_{t,b,0}\) and \(H_{t,b,1}\) are disjoint. The bounded-array assumption and the exposure lower bound therefore give
\[
\mathbb E Z_t^2
=\frac{y_{t,1}^2}{\upsilon_{t,b,1}}+
\frac{y_{t,0}^2}{\upsilon_{t,b,0}}
\le4eB^2,
\qquad
\mathbb E|Z_t|=|y_{t,1}|+|y_{t,0}|\le2B.
\]

We next control the covariance sum. If \(1\le u-t\le b\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)|
\le\{\mathbb E Z_t^2\,\mathbb E Z_u^2\}^{1/2}
\le4eB^2.
\]
Suppose \(u-t>b\) and put \(d=u-b-t\ge1\). Then \(k_u=b\). Conditional on the first coordinate of the homogeneous window,
\[
\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{u-b}=1)=2y_{u,1},
\qquad
\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{u-b}=0)=-2y_{u,0}.
\]
The symmetric chain has the exact transition formula
\[
\Pr(W_{t+d}=j\mid W_t=i)
=\frac12\{1+(2i-1)(2j-1)\lambda^d\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\left|\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{1:t})-\mathbb EZ_u\right|
=\lambda^d|y_{u,1}+y_{u,0}|
\le2B\lambda^d.
\]
Since \(Z_t\) is measurable with respect to \(W_{1:t}\),
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_u)|
=\left|\mathbb E\left[Z_t\{\mathbb E(Z_u\mid W_{1:t})-\mathbb EZ_u\}\right]\right|
\le4B^2\lambda^d.
\]
There are at most \(Tb\) ordered pairs with \(t<u\) and \(u-t\le b\). For each \(t\), the remaining geometric tail is bounded by \(\sum_{d\ge1}\lambda^d\), and \(\lambda/(1-\lambda)=(b-1)/2\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{t=1}^T Z_t\right)
&\le4eB^2T+8eB^2Tb+8B^2T\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\lambda^d\\
&=4eB^2T+8eB^2Tb+4B^2T(b-1)\\
&\le12eB^2Tb.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the last inequality, the right side minus the preceding expression is \(4B^2T(e-1)(b-1)\ge0\). This also covers \(b=1\), for which \(\lambda=0\). Jensen's inequality now gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}
\left|\widetilde\theta_b-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right|
\le\sqrt{12e}\,B\sqrt{\frac bT}.
\]

It remains to compare the oracle statistic with the observed-data estimator. If \(t\le b+1\), then \(k_t=t-1\), so on \(H_{t,b,a}=1\) the complete realized history through time \(t\) is \(\mathbf a_{1:t}\); the replacement error is zero. If \(t\ge b+2\), then on \(H_{t,b,a}=1\) the current assignment and its preceding \(b\) assignments all equal \(a\). The historical-envelope assumption gives
\[
\left|Y_t(W_{1:t})-y_{t,a}\right|
\le C\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{t-1}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}.
\]
For each arm, the exact exposure-weight identity is
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}\left[\frac{H_{t,b,a}}{\upsilon_{t,b,a}}\right]=1.
\]
The triangle inequality, followed by expectation and this exact identity, therefore yields the expected replacement-error upper bound
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}|\widehat\vartheta_b-\widetilde\theta_b|
\le \frac{2C}{T}\sum_{t=b+2}^{T}\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{t-1}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}.
\]
An empty sum is zero. Since \(x\mapsto x^{-(1+\beta)}\) is decreasing and \(\beta>0\),
\[
\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{\infty}(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\le\int_{b+1}^{\infty}x^{-(1+\beta)}\,dx
=\frac1\beta(b+1)^{-\beta}
\le\frac1\beta b^{-\beta}.
\]
There are \((T-b-1)_+\) possibly nonzero time indices, so
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}|\widehat\vartheta_b-\widetilde\theta_b|
\le\frac{2C}{\beta}\frac{(T-b-1)_+}{T}b^{-\beta}.
\]
This covers \(b=1\); it is zero when \(b=T\), and for \(T=1\) the only admissible choice is \(b=1\), so it is again zero. The triangle inequality combined with the oracle-statistic bound proves
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b)}}
|\widehat\vartheta_b-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|
\le\sqrt{12e}\,B\sqrt{\frac bT}
+\frac{2C}{\beta}\frac{(T-b-1)_+}{T}b^{-\beta}.
\]
The second summand is an expected replacement-error upper bound obtained using the exact exposure-weight identity; it is not asserted to equal the estimator's bias. \(\square\)
