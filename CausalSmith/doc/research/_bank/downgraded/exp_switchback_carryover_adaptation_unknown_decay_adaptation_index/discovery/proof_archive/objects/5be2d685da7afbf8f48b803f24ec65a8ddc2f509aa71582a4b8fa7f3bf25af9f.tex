\textbf{Proof.} For any design-estimator pair and any \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\), its worst-case risk over \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) is at least the infimum \(R_T(\beta;C)\). Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) makes this denominator strictly positive. Hence every ratio in the definition of \(A_T(C)\) is at least one, and taking the supremum over \(\beta\) and then the infimum over procedures proves \(A_T(C)\ge1\).

Put
\[
b_-=\left\lceil T^{1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right\rceil,
\qquad
q_-=\left(1+\frac1{b_-}\right)^{b_-},
\qquad
\lambda_-=\frac{b_--1}{b_-+1},
\]
and define
\[
V_{T,b_-}=4q_-T+8q_-\left(b_-T-\frac{b_-(b_-+1)}2\right)
+8\sum_{d=1}^{(T-b_--1)_+}(T-b_--d)\lambda_-^d.
\]
The same ceiling argument as in Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\) shows \(1\le b_-\le T\). Use the one stationary Markov design and observed-data Horvitz--Thompson estimator of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) with \(b=b_-\). For every \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\) and \(\ell\ge1\),
\[
C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}
\le C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta_-)}.
\]
The class definitions therefore give
\[
\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\subseteq\mathcal P_{T,\beta_-}(C).
\]
Applying the exact finite-pair variance and endpoint-aware expected replacement-error bound of the Markov lemma at \(\beta_-\) gives, simultaneously for all admissible \(\beta\),
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E_{\pi^{(b_-)}}
|\widehat\vartheta_{b_-}-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|
\le N_T^{\mathrm{fin}},
\]
where
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
=\frac BT\sqrt{V_{T,b_-}}
+\frac{2C}{T}\sum_{\ell=b_-+1}^{T-1}(T-\ell)(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta_-)}.
\]
On the other hand, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) gives for every such \(\beta\)
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\ge D_T^{\mathrm{bin}},
\qquad
D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}
=B2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}>0.
\]
Thus the simultaneous risk ratio of this one common procedure is at most \(N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}/D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}\). Taking the supremum over \(\beta\) and then the infimum defining \(A_T(C)\) proves the exact-binomial-lower-denominator bound
\[
A_T(C)\le\frac{N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}.
\]
The word ``lower'' is essential here: \(D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}\) is an exact Bayes lower bound, not the unknown exact oracle risk.

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) also yields
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
\le \sqrt{10e+2}\,B\sqrt{\frac{b_-}{T}}
+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\frac{(T-b_--1)_+}{T}b_-^{-\beta_-}.
\]
For \(T\ge2\), put \(x=T^{1/(2\beta_-+1)}\). The separate inequalities \(b_-\le x+1\le2x\) and \(b_-\ge x\) imply
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
\le\left(\sqrt{20e+4}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right)
T^{-\beta_-/(2\beta_-+1)}
=C_0T^{-\beta_-/(2\beta_-+1)}.
\]
For \(T=1\), the finite replacement sum is zero, \(V_{1,1}=8\), and this inequality also holds. Finally, \(D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}\ge B/\sqrt{2T}\), whence
\[
A_T(C)\le\frac{\sqrt2\,C_0}{B}T^{1/(4\beta_-+2)}.
\]
Both upper bounds use the same procedure, independent of the realized \(\beta\). This polynomial certificate proves neither divergence, subpolynomial adaptation, nor sharpness. \(\square\)
