\textbf{Proof.} Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:positive-oracle-risk}\) gives, uniformly over \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\),
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\ge B2^{-(T-1)}\binom{T-1}{\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}\ge \frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
The supported prior used for this bound lies in the no-carryover subclass of every \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\). Since the bound is proved for each \(\pi\in\mathcal D_T\) and each observed-data estimator separately, it includes deterministic, history-dependent, and outcome-adaptive kernels without any positivity restriction.

For \(T=1\), take \(W_1\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) and
\[
\widehat\theta_1=(2W_1-1)Y_1^{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
Because \(\operatorname{GATE}_1(Y)=Y_1(1)-Y_1(0)\), the absolute error is \(\lvert Y_1(0)\rvert\) when \(W_1=1\) and \(\lvert Y_1(1)\rvert\) when \(W_1=0\). Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass:bounded-array}\) therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E\left\lvert\widehat\theta_1-\operatorname{GATE}_1(Y)\right\rvert
=\frac12\left\{\lvert Y_1(0)\rvert+\lvert Y_1(1)\rvert\right\}\le B.
\]
Thus \(R_1(\beta;C)\le B\), whereas (1) equals \(B\) at \(T=1\). Hence
\[
R_1(\beta;C)=B.
\tag{2}
\]

Now suppose \(T\ge2\). For every \(b\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) constructs a stationary Markov switchback design and an observed-data Horvitz--Thompson estimator whose worst-case risk over \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) is at most \(U_{T,\beta}^{\dagger}(b)\). The same lemma proves
\[
\upsilon_{t,b,a}\ge\frac{1}{2q_b}>\frac{1}{2e},
\]
where \(q_b=(1+1/b)^b\), and shows that the uniform infimum \(1/(2e)\) is approached but not attained. Independently, boundedness implies
\[
\left\lvert\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)\right\rvert
\le \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\left\{\lvert Y_t(\mathbf1_{1:t})\rvert+\lvert Y_t(\mathbf0_{1:t})\rvert\right\}
\le2B,
\]
so the zero estimator has worst-case risk at most \(2B\). It follows that
\[
R_T(\beta;C)
\le \min\left\{2B,\min_{1\le b\le T}U_{T,\beta}^{\dagger}(b)\right\}
\qquad(T\ge2).
\tag{3}
\]
The scale set is finite, so its minimum is attained. Choosing the smallest minimizing scale is a deterministic function of \(T,B,C,\beta\), and hence can be done before assignment. Equations (2)--(3) prove
\[
R_T(\beta;C)\le\mathfrak U_{T,\beta}^{\dagger}.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, the \(T=1\) branch in (4) is supplied by the explicit fair-Bernoulli procedure, not by any assertion about \(U_{1,\beta}^{\dagger}(1)\).

Put \(K=8e-4\), \(x=T^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), and \(b_\beta=\lceil x\rceil\). If \(T\ge2\), then \(1<x<T\), so \(b_\beta\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), and
\[
\frac{b_\beta}{x}\le1+\frac1x\le1+2^{-1/(2\beta+1)},
\qquad b_\beta\ge x.
\tag{5}
\]
The final risk bound of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\), the inequalities \((T-b_\beta-1)_+/T\le1\) and \((b_\beta+1)^{-\beta}\le b_\beta^{-\beta}\), and (5) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{T,\beta}^{\dagger}(b_\beta)
&\le \sqrt K\,B\sqrt{\frac{b_\beta}{T}}+\frac{2C}{\beta}b_\beta^{-\beta}\\
&\le\left[\sqrt{K\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta}\right]T^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
For \(T\ge2\), the definition of \(\mathfrak U_{T,\beta}^{\dagger}\) also gives \(\mathfrak U_{T,\beta}^{\dagger}\le U_{T,\beta}^{\dagger}(b_\beta)\). At \(T=1\), the piecewise certificate equals \(B\), and the coefficient on the right of (6) exceeds \(B\), so the same displayed upper chain remains valid.

Finally, \(\beta\le\beta_+\) implies
\[
2^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\le2^{-1/(2\beta_++1)},
\]
while \(\beta\ge\beta_->0\) implies \(1/\beta\le1/\beta_-\). Hence the coefficient in (6) is at most
\[
\sqrt{(8e-4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_++1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}=C_0^\star.
\]
This proves the complete lower and upper chains.

For completeness, Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) proves that \(B^2V_{T,b}^{\dagger}\) is the exact worst-case auxiliary homogeneous-history variance and that \(8e-4\) is the smallest coefficient in a uniform inequality \(V_{T,b}^{\dagger}\le KTb\). That identity concerns the auxiliary process only. The carryover summand is a capped, endpoint-aware expected replacement-error upper bound obtained from the exposure-weight identity. Consequently neither summand, their sum, nor the optimized certificate is asserted to equal the exact estimator risk, an exact bias, a maximal carryover error, or the oracle risk. The exponents in (1) and (6) remain unmatched. \(\square\)
