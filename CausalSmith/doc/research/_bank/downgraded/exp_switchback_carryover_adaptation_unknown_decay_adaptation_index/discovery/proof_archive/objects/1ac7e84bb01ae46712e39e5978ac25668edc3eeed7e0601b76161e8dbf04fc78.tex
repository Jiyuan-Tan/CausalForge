\textbf{Proof.} For any design-estimator pair and every \(\beta\), its worst-case risk over \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\) is at least \(R_T(\beta;C)\). Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:exact-positive-oracle-risk}\) makes this denominator strictly positive. Thus every ratio defining \(A_T(C)\) is at least one, and \(A_T(C)\ge1\).

At \(T=1\), Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\) gives \(R_1(\beta;C)=B\) for every \(\beta\). The common rule \(W_1\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), \(\widehat\theta_1=(2W_1-1)Y_1^{\mathrm{obs}}\), has worst-case risk at most \(B\) for every \(\beta\). Hence \(A_1(C)=1\). By definition, \(N_1^\star=N_1^{\mathrm{fin}}=D_1^{\mathrm{bin}}=B\), so every finite ratio in the claimed chain is also exactly one at this boundary.

Suppose \(T\ge2\). For \(\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\) and every \(\ell\ge1\),
\[
C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta)}\le C(1+\ell)^{-(1+\beta_-)}.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)\subseteq\mathcal P_{T,\beta_-}(C)\). The finite set of scales contains a smallest minimizer \(b^\star\) of \(U_{T,\beta_-}^{\sharp}(b)\). Before assignment, compare \(2B\) with \(U_{T,\beta_-}^{\sharp}(b^\star)\). Use the zero estimator if the former is no larger; otherwise use the stationary Markov rule and estimator from Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:markov-washout-oracle-upper}\) at \(b^\star\). This procedure depends only on \(T,B,C,\beta_-\), not on the realized \(\beta\). Nestedness and the lemma imply
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal P_{T,\beta}(C)}
\mathbb E|\widehat\theta^\star-\operatorname{GATE}_T(Y)|
\le N_T^\star
\]
for every admissible \(\beta\). In the Markov branch, \(\upsilon_{t,b^\star,a}\ge1/(2q_{b^\star})>1/(2e)\), and the uniform infimum is \(1/(2e)\).

The exact-positive-risk lemma gives \(R_T(\beta;C)\ge D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}>0\). Taking the simultaneous ratio for the displayed common procedure and then the infimum defining \(A_T(C)\) gives
\[
A_T(C)\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}.
\]
The denominator is the exact binomial Bayes lower floor, not the unknown oracle risk. Since \(b_-\) is a candidate in the finite minimum,
\[
N_T^\star\le U_{T,\beta_-}^{\sharp}(b_-)=N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}.
\]
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:oracle-rate}\), specialized to \(\beta_-\), gives
\[
N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}
\le\left[\sqrt{(8e+4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right]T^{-\beta_-/(2\beta_-+1)}.
\]
At \(T=1\), the same inequality holds because its left side is \(B\) and the bracketed coefficient exceeds \(B\). Finally,
\[
D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}\ge\frac{B}{\sqrt{2T}}.
\]
Division proves
\[
1\le A_T(C)\le\frac{N_T^\star}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}
\le\frac{N_T^{\mathrm{fin}}}{D_T^{\mathrm{bin}}}
\le\frac{\sqrt2}{B}
\left[\sqrt{(8e+4)\left(1+2^{-1/(2\beta_-+1)}\right)}\,B+\frac{2C}{\beta_-}\right]T^{1/(4\beta_-+2)}.
\]
The coefficient is endpoint-specific because the common procedure may use the known lower endpoint \(\beta_-\); the larger interval-uniform coefficient \(C_0^\star\) is unnecessary. The result supplies a polynomial common-design certificate only and proves neither K1, K2, nor sharpness. \(\square\)
