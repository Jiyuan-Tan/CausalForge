Fix a compact set \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  The density-coupled lower-bound theorem supplies constants \(c_K>0\) and \(n_{0,L}\) such that
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\ge c_Kn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\qquad(n\ge n_{0,L}).
\tag{1}
\]
The rank-capped upper-bound theorem supplies constants \(C_K<\infty\) and \(n_{0,U}\) and, importantly, the single measurable sample map \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)\) such that its maximal mean absolute error is at most \(C_K\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}\).  Substituting this map into the infimum over estimators in the definition of \(R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\) gives
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\le C_K\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\qquad(n\ge n_{0,U}).
\tag{2}
\]
Taking \(n_0=\max(n_{0,L},n_{0,U})\) proves the first display, with both constants uniform on \(K\).

Now put \(p=(\alpha+\beta)/d\) and \(q=\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\).  The low-region exponent-ordering lemma gives \(0<p<q\) throughout \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  Thus
\[
\frac{n^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}}{n^{-q}}
=n^{q-p}(\log n)^{2p}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
so, for every sufficiently large \(n\), the definition of the upper rate reduces to
\[
\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}=n^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (1)--(3) gives
\[
c_Kn^{-q}\le R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\le C_Kn^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}.
\]
The lower endpoint has exponent \(q\), whereas the upper endpoint has the strictly smaller exponent \(p\).  Hence these inequalities allow, but do not prove, polynomial behavior at any intermediate exponent.  In particular they neither determine a polynomial minimax exponent nor establish that an unknown density confined to a fixed positive band causes a slowdown relative to the density-qualified benchmark.
