\begin{proof}
Fix the class constants and a law \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\).  All constants below may depend on the fixed class constants, \(d\), and \(\gamma\), but not on \(P\) or \(n\).

First, by the definition of the CATE regression,
\[
\mu_{1,P}(x_0)-\mu_{0,P}(x_0)=\tau_P(x_0).
\]
Thus, when the two outcome regressions are supplied as functions, their direct plug-in has identically zero statistical error.  By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:cate-identification}\), the same quantity is the selected continuous version of the causal conditional mean \(\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x_0]\).

We next analyze the separately oracleized local \(R\)-learner.  Write
\[
e(x)=\pi_P(x),\qquad
\eta(x)=e(x)\mu_{1,P}(x)+\{1-e(x)\}\mu_{0,P}(x),
\qquad
\nu(x)=e(x)\{1-e(x)\}.
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), the definitions of \(\eta\), \(\mu_{a,P}\), and \(\tau_P\) give
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y-\eta(X)\mid A=a,X]
=\{a-e(X)\}\tau_P(X).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[(A-e(X))(Y-\eta(X))\mid A,X\right]
=\{A-e(X)\}^2\tau_P(X),
\]
and hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[(A-e(X))(Y-\eta(X))\mid X]
&=\nu(X)\tau_P(X),\\
\mathbb E_P[\{A-e(X)\}^2\mid X]&=\nu(X).
\end{aligned}
\]
Overlap implies \(\nu(X)\geq\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)>0\).  Therefore the conditional weighted normal equation has the unique regression target \(\tau_P(X)\).  This proves the asserted conditional-target claim; Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:cate-identification}\) supplies its causal interpretation.

It remains to prove the uniform mean-absolute rate.  Let \(m=\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\), let \(q\) be the fixed dimension of the polynomials of total degree at most \(m\), and abbreviate \(h=h_n\).  Because \(x_0\in(0,1)^d\), there is \(h_0>0\) such that
\[
x_0+h[-1/2,1/2]^d\subset[0,1]^d
\quad\text{whenever }0<h\leq h_0.
\]
Moreover, the dimensions of the degree-\(\lceil\alpha\rceil-1\) and degree-\(\lceil\beta\rceil-1\) one-cell polynomial spaces are fixed, whereas \(k_n\to\infty\).  Hence there is a fixed \(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\geq n_0\), the localization window is interior and the finite-sample basis fallback in \(\mathrm{def:rank-capped-estimator}\) is not invoked.  For smaller \(n\), both the oracleized output and the target lie in \([-2,2]\) after truncation, so their error is at most four; these finitely many indices do not affect the asserted asymptotic order.

Consider one of the three deterministic balanced evaluation folds, of size \(N\).  For \(n\geq n_0\), balance gives \(N\geq n/4\).  Put \(\mathcal C=[-1/2,1/2]^d\), write \(b=b_\gamma\), and set
\[
T_i=(X_i-x_0)/h,qquad
K_i=h^{-d}\mathbf 1\{T_i\in\mathcal C\}.
\]
The oracle empirical Gram matrix on this fold is
\[
\widehat Q=\frac1N\sum_i K_i b(T_i)b(T_i)^\top\{A_i-e(X_i)\}^2.
\]
Its expectation equals
\[
Q_h=\int_{\mathcal C}b(t)b(t)^\top
\nu(x_0+ht)f_P(x_0+ht)\,dt.
\]
Because the coordinates of \(b\) are Lebesgue-orthonormal on \(\mathcal C\), the density band and overlap imply, for every \(z\in\mathbb R^q\),
\[
\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-\lVert z\rVert_2^2
\leq z^\top Q_hz
\leq \frac{f_+}{4}\lVert z\rVert_2^2.
\]
In particular, the population Gram matrix is uniformly nonsingular.  Its spectrum lies strictly inside the clipping interval \([\varepsilon^2f_-/2,2f_+]\).  Indeed,
\(\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_->\varepsilon^2f_-/2\) because \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), and \(f_+/4<2f_+\).  Choose a fixed \(\delta>0\) smaller than both spectral margins and define
\[
G=\{\lVert\widehat Q-Q_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\delta\}.
\]
The polynomial vector \(b\) is bounded on the compact cube \(\mathcal C\).  Using \(f_P\leq f_+\), \(|A-e(X)|\leq1\), and independence within the fold gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\lVert\widehat Q-Q_h\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2
\leq \frac{C}{Nh^d}.
\]
Since the operator norm is at most the Frobenius norm, Markov's inequality yields
\[
P(G^c)\leq\frac{C}{Nh^d}.
\]
On \(G\), Weyl's eigenvalue inequality places every eigenvalue of \(\widehat Q\) inside the clipping interval.  Thus the spectral safeguard is inactive there and
\[
\mathsf C_{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat Q)=\widehat Q,
\qquad
\lVert\widehat Q^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C.
\]

Let \(p_h(t)\) be the Taylor polynomial of \(x\mapsto\tau_P(x)\) at \(x_0\), evaluated at \(x=x_0+ht\), through total degree \(m\).  Since \(b\) spans that polynomial space, there is a vector \(\theta_h\in\mathbb R^q\) such that
\[
p_h(t)=b(t)^\top\theta_h,
\qquad
b(0)^\top\theta_h=\tau_P(x_0).
\]
The H\"older condition gives the uniform Taylor remainder
\[
\sup_{t\in\mathcal C}
|\tau_P(x_0+ht)-p_h(t)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
\]
For this fold, define
\[
U_i=K_i b(T_i)\left[(A_i-e(X_i))(Y_i-\eta(X_i))
-\{A_i-e(X_i)\}^2p_h(T_i)\right].
\]
The preceding conditional-moment identity and the Taylor bound show
\[
\left\lVert\mathbb E_P U_i\right\rVert_2
=\left\lVert\int_{\mathcal C} b(t)\nu(x_0+ht)f_P(x_0+ht)
\{\tau_P(x_0+ht)-p_h(t)\}\,dt\right\rVert_2
\leq Ch^\gamma.
\]
Bounded potential outcomes and consistency give \(|Y|\leq1\); also \(|\eta|\leq1\).  The H\"older envelope and the Taylor remainder bound make \(p_h\) uniformly bounded on \(\mathcal C\).  It follows, again using \(f_P\leq f_+\), that
\[
\mathbb E_P\lVert U_i\rVert_2^2\leq Ch^{-d}.
\]
Independence and Jensen's inequality therefore give
\[
\mathbb E_P\left\lVert\frac1N\sum_iU_i\right\rVert_2
\leq Ch^\gamma+C(Nh^d)^{-1/2}.
\]
If \(\widehat\theta\) denotes the safeguarded oracle coefficient on this fold, then on \(G\)
\[
\widehat\theta-\theta_h
=\widehat Q^{-1}\frac1N\sum_iU_i.
\]
Since \(b(0)\) is fixed, the fold-intercept error on \(G\) consequently has expectation at most
\[
C\{h^\gamma+(Nh^d)^{-1/2}\}.
\]

Apply this argument to all three evaluation folds and let \(G_{\mathrm{all}}\) be the intersection of their good-Gram events.  The union bound and balanced fold sizes give
\[
P(G_{\mathrm{all}}^c)\leq C(nh^d)^{-1}.
\]
On \(G_{\mathrm{all}}\), the absolute error of the average of the three fold intercepts is bounded by the average of their absolute errors.  Projection onto \([-2,2]\) cannot increase distance from \(\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2,2]\).  On the complementary event, the projected estimator and target differ by at most four.  Combining these facts yields
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{or}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
\leq C\left\{h^\gamma+(nh^d)^{-1/2}+(nh^d)^{-1}\right\}.
\]
Finally, \(h=h_n=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)}\) gives
\[
h^\gamma=(nh^d)^{-1/2}=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},
\qquad
(nh^d)^{-1}=n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Thus the oracleized local \(R\)-estimator has uniform mean-absolute error
\(O(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)})\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
