Let \(K\) be a compact subset of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  Every constant below may depend on \(K,d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\), and \(x_0\), but not on \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\) or \(n\).  Compactness and the strict inequalities defining \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) give, uniformly on \(K\),
\[
0<\beta<\min(\alpha,\gamma).
\tag{1}
\]
Only finitely many polynomial degrees occur on \(K\).  Since \(k_n\to\infty\), all finite-sample fallbacks in the definition of \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)\) are inactive once \(n\ge n_0(K)\).  Each deterministic fold then has size between \(n/4\) and \(n/2\).  We analyze one cyclic fit and write \(N\asymp n\) for any of its three fold sizes; the same bounds hold for all three cycles.

\emph{Step 1: cellwise nuisance regression.}
For \(v\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\), let \(M_v=q_vm_v^d\) be the dimension of \(B_{v,n}\).  Maximality of \(m_v\), together with the uniform bounds on the finitely many values of \(q_v\), gives
\[
c_Kk_n\le M_v\le k_n.
\tag{2}
\]
The basis is cell supported.  Scaling a fixed-dimensional orthonormal polynomial basis from a cell of volume \(m_v^{-d}\) gives
\[
\sup_{x\in[0,1]^d}\lVert B_{v,n}(x)\rVert_2^2\le C_KM_v.
\tag{3}
\]
Lebesgue orthonormality and the density band imply, for
\(G_v^0=\mathbb E_P[B_{v,n}(X)B_{v,n}(X)^{\mathsf T}]\),
\[
f_-I_{M_v}\preceq G_v^0\preceq f_+I_{M_v}.
\tag{4}
\]
Apply the matrix Bernstein lemma to the centered rank-one summands in an empirical nuisance Gram matrix and also to their negatives.  By (3)--(4), a summand has operator norm at most \(C_KM_v\), and the variance proxy of the unnormalized sum is at most \(C_KNM_v\).  Hence
\[
\Pr_P\!\left(\lVert G_{v,J}-G_v^0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}>f_-/2\right)
\le C_KM_v\exp(-c_KN/M_v).
\tag{5}
\]
On the complementary event the nuisance spectral cap is inactive and the empirical inverse has operator norm at most \(2/f_-\).

Let \(g\in\mathcal H_d^v(C_KL)\) be the conditional mean of the bounded response in one nuisance regression.  On a cell \(D\), let \(T_Dg\) be its Taylor polynomial of degree \(\lceil v\rceil-1\).  The H\"older condition gives
\[
\sup_{x\in D}|g(x)-T_Dg(x)|\le C_Km_v^{-v}\le C_Kk_n^{-v/d}=:B_v.
\tag{6}
\]
We spell out why empirical polynomial reproduction does not amplify this remainder.  Write \(B_D\) for the fixed \(q_v\)-dimensional block of basis functions supported on \(D\), and \(G_D\) for its block in \(G_{v,J}\).  The constant basis coordinate and the spectral bounds on \(G_D\) imply
\[
c_KN/M_v\le \#\{i\in J:X_i\in D\}\le C_KN/M_v.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(r_D=g-T_Dg\), Cauchy--Schwarz in the empirical Gram inner product gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|B_D(x)^{\mathsf T}G_D^{-1}
\frac1N\sum_{i\in J:X_i\in D}B_D(X_i)r_D(X_i)\right|\\
&\qquad\le
\{B_D(x)^{\mathsf T}G_D^{-1}B_D(x)\}^{1/2}
\left\{\frac1N\sum_{i\in J:X_i\in D}r_D(X_i)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\le C_KB_v,
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
where (3), (5), (6), and (7) justify the last inequality.  Thus, conditional on the nuisance-fold covariates and the Gram event in (5), the untruncated fit admits
\[
\widetilde g(x)-g(x)=b_g(x)+\zeta_g(x),
\qquad
\sup_x|b_g(x)|\le C_KB_v,
\tag{9}
\]
with \(\mathbb E_P[\zeta_g(x)\mid X_J]=0\) and
\[
\sup_x\mathbb E_P[\zeta_g(x)^2\mid X_J]
\le C_KM_v/N\le C_Kk_n/N=:V^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Because the empirical Gram is block diagonal and its spectral map preserves that block structure, values of \(\zeta_g\) in distinct cells use disjoint collections of centered responses and have zero conditional covariance.

On one cell the stochastic error is a polynomial in a space of dimension \(q_v\), uniformly bounded on \(K\).  Conditional scalar Bernstein bounds for its coefficient coordinates, followed by a fixed finite net of the coefficient sphere, yield, for each fixed \(c_0>0\),
\[
\Pr_P\!\left(\sup_{x\in D}|\zeta_g(x)|>c_0\mid X_J\right)
\le C_K\exp(-c_KN/M_v).
\tag{11}
\]
This is a finite-dimensional coefficient bound, not a union bound over continuum points.  Taking a union over at most \(C_Kk_n\) cells, the Gram failures, and the three nuisance fits produces an event \(\mathcal E_n\) such that
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}P(\mathcal E_n^c)
\le \rho_n:=C_Kk_n\exp(-c_KN/k_n).
\tag{12}
\]
For large \(n\), (6) is smaller than half the fixed clipping buffer.  Hence the two propensity clips and the outcome clip are inactive on \(\mathcal E_n\).  The covariance calculations below are performed for the untruncated errors conditional only on the covariates and Gram events; when the clipped fits occur in the loss decomposition, equality on \(\mathcal E_n\) and domination by the corresponding untruncated absolute moments justify the same bounds.  Thus no response-dependent clipping event is used as a conditioning event.

For the propensity fits take \(g=\pi_P\) and \(v=\alpha\).  For the outcome fit take
\[
\eta_P(x)=\mathbb E_P(Y\mid X=x)
=\mu_{0,P}(x)+\pi_P(x)\tau_P(x).
\tag{13}
\]
By (1), the elementary product rule for bounded H\"older functions shows that \(\pi_P\tau_P\) is \(\beta\)-H\"older with radius at most \(C_KL\); therefore (9)--(12) apply to \(\widehat\pi_a,\widehat\pi_b\), and \(\widehat\eta\).  The fit \(\widehat\pi_a\) is trained independently of the pair \((\widehat\pi_b,\widehat\eta)\).

We next record the density-free same-cell calculation.  Let
\[
w_h(x)=h_n^{-d}w((x-x_0)/h_n)
\mathbf 1\{(x-x_0)/h_n\in\mathcal C\},
\qquad \lVert w\rVert_\infty\le C_K,
\]
where \(w\) is a coordinate or coordinate product of \(b_\gamma\).  Let \(\zeta_a\) be a centered error from the first nuisance fold and \(\zeta_c\) one from the independent second nuisance fold.  Conditional on first-fold covariates, the covariance kernel of \(\zeta_a\) vanishes across distinct \(\alpha\)-cells and is at most \(C_Kk_n/N\) within one cell by (10).  Every cell has \(P_X\)-mass at most \(C_K/k_n\) by the upper density band and (2).  Expanding the square, discarding cross-cell covariances, and using Cauchy--Schwarz on each cell therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_P\left|
\int w_h(x)\zeta_a(x)\zeta_c(x)f_P(x)\,dx
\right|^2\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{C_K}{N}h_n^{-2d}
\int_{x_0+h_n\mathcal C}
\mathbb E_P\{\zeta_c(x)^2\}f_P(x)\,dx
\le C_K\frac{k_n}{N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Taking square roots yields
\[
C_K\sqrt{k_n/N}\,(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}.
\tag{15}
\]
The same calculation with one centered factor replaced by a bias bounded by \(B_v\) gives at most \(C_KB_v(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\).  No continuity or smoothness of \(f_P\) is used in (14).

\emph{Step 2: the local normal equation.}
Suppress the cyclic index and write
\[
\pi=\pi_P,\qquad \eta=\eta_P,\qquad
\nu=\pi(1-\pi),\qquad Z=A-\pi(X),\qquad R=Y-\eta(X).
\]
Consistency with the definitions of the conditional means, followed by iterated expectation, gives
\[
\mathbb E_P(ZR\mid X)=\nu(X)\tau_P(X),
\qquad
\mathbb E_P(Z^2\mid X)=\nu(X).
\tag{16}
\]
For
\[
e_a=\widehat\pi_a-\pi,\qquad
e_b=\widehat\pi_b-\pi,\qquad
e_\eta=\widehat\eta-\eta,
\]
direct algebra gives, for every function \(p\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(A-\widehat\pi_a)(Y-\widehat\eta)
-(A-\widehat\pi_a)(A-\widehat\pi_b)p\\
&\quad=ZR-Z^2p
+e_a(Zp-R)+Z(e_bp-e_\eta)+e_a(e_\eta-e_bp).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The two first-order nuisance terms in (17) are centered conditional on \(X\) and the nuisance folds.  Equations (9)--(10) and a conditional second-moment calculation for their local empirical averages bound their mean absolute contribution by
\[
C_K\{B_\alpha+B_\beta+V\}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
\le C_K(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}.
\tag{18}
\]
For the last product in (17), independence of the two training folds makes its training-sample expectation the product of the conditional biases, bounded by
\[
C_K(B_\alpha B_\beta+B_\alpha^2).
\tag{19}
\]
Its training-centered part is bounded by (15) and the bias--centered variant following (15).  Conditional on both nuisance folds, the centered evaluation-fold average has pointwise second moment at most \(C_K(k_n/N)^2\), and hence contributes at most
\[
C_K(k_n/N)(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2},
\tag{20}
\]
which is no larger than (15).

Let \(b=b_\gamma\), and define the oracle population local Gram matrix
\[
Q_h=\int_{\mathcal C}b(z)b(z)^{\mathsf T}
\nu(x_0+h_nz)f_P(x_0+h_nz)\,dz.
\]
Lebesgue orthonormality, overlap, and the density band imply
\[
\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-I
\preceq Q_h\preceq f_+I/4.
\tag{21}
\]
Its dimension is uniformly bounded on \(K\).  Let \(\delta_K>0\) be the minimum distance, over the finitely many degree strata meeting \(K\), from the spectral interval in (21) to the two boundaries \(\varepsilon^2f_-/2\) and \(2f_+\) of the local cap.  On \(\mathcal E_n\), entrywise scalar Bernstein for the oracle evaluation-fold fluctuation, together with (15), (18), and (19), gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\!left[
\lVert\widehat Q-Q_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}
\right]
\le C_K\left\{(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}+B_\alpha^2
+\sqrt{k_n/N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Consequently, if \(\mathcal G_r\) denotes the event on which the local cap is inactive, the union bound and Markov's inequality applied only on \(\mathcal E_n\) yield
\[
P(\mathcal G_r^c)
\le \rho_n+\delta_K^{-1}
\mathbb E_P\!left[
\lVert\widehat Q-Q_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}
\right].
\tag{23}
\]
This restricted estimate avoids attributing a merely \(O(\rho_n)\) operator-norm contribution to the complement of \(\mathcal E_n\).  On \(\mathcal G_r\), \(\lVert\widehat Q^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le C_K\), while the safeguarded inverse is defined on every sample.

Let \(p_h(z)=b(z)^{\mathsf T}\theta_h\) be the Taylor polynomial of \(\tau_P\) at \(x_0\), evaluated at \(x_0+h_nz\), through degree \(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\).  Then
\[
b(0)^{\mathsf T}\theta_h=\tau_P(x_0),
\qquad
\sup_{z\in\mathcal C}|\tau_P(x_0+h_nz)-p_h(z)|
\le C_Kh_n^\gamma.
\tag{24}
\]
Apply (17) with \(p=p_h\), multiply by the local vector weight, and average over the evaluation fold.  Equations (16), (21), and (24) give the oracle contribution \(C_K\{h_n^\gamma+(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\}\).  Equations (15), (18)--(20) give the nuisance contributions.  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
|\widehat t_r-\tau_P(x_0)|
\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n\cap\mathcal G_r}\right]
\le C_K\big[&h_n^\gamma+(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
+B_\alpha B_\beta+B_\alpha^2\\
&+\sqrt{k_n/N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}+\rho_n\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
The final truncation to \([-2,2]\) cannot increase loss because \(\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2,2]\), and it bounds the loss by four on every failure event.  The probability bound (23) consists exactly of the orders displayed in (25).  Taking the union over the three cycles, averaging their intercepts, and using the triangle inequality therefore preserves (25), up to a constant.

\emph{Step 3: rates.}
Put
\[
p=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{d},
\qquad q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d}.
\]
Since \(N\asymp n\),
\[
h_n^\gamma\asymp(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\asymp n^{-q},
\qquad
B_\alpha B_\beta\le C_Kk_n^{-p}.
\tag{26}
\]
Because \(\alpha>\beta\) on \(K\), \(B_\alpha^2\le C_Kk_n^{-p}\).  Moreover,
\[
\sqrt{k_n/N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
\le \frac{C_K}{\log n}n^{-q}le C_Kn^{-q},
\tag{27}
\]
and
\[
k_n^{-p}\le C_Kn^{-p}(\log n)^{2p},
\qquad
\rho_n\le C_Kk_n\exp\{-c_K(\log n)^2\}.
\tag{28}
\]
The last expression is smaller than every inverse polynomial, uniformly on \(K\).  Substitution of (26)--(28) into (25) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
\le C_K\left\{n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\vee n^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}(\log n)^{2(\alpha+\beta)/d}\right\}
=C_K\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.
\]
The constants and the threshold \(n_0(K)\) are uniform over the chosen compact set, proving the theorem.
