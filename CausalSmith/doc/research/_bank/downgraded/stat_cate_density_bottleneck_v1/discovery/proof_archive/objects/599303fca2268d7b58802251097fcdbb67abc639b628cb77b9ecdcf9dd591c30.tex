Set
\[
\alpha_* = \frac{d}{16(1+d/2)},
\qquad
\beta_* = \frac{\alpha_*}{2},
\qquad
\gamma_* = 1.
\]
Then \(0<\beta_*<\alpha_*<1\), and
\[
s_* =\frac{\alpha_*+\beta_*}{2}
=\frac{3d}{64(1+d/2)}
<\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2\gamma_*)}.
\]
Thus the displayed triple is in \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  All
inequalities are strict at that triple, including \(\alpha_*<1\), so a
sufficiently small open neighborhood is also contained in
\(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).

Now take any member of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  The first chain in
the conclusion is part of \(\mathrm{def:close\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}region}\).
The inequality defining \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) gives
\[
s<\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2\gamma)}
=\frac{d\gamma}{2(2\gamma+d)}
<\frac\gamma2,
\]
because \(d/(2\gamma+d)<1\).  Hence
\(2s=\alpha+\beta<\gamma\), as claimed.
