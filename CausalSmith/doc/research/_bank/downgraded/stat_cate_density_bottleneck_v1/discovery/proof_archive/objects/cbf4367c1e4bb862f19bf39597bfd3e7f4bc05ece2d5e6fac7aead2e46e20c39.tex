We give one data-driven estimator and bound its risk.  Let \(K\) be a compact
subset of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), put
\(\ell=n^{-1/(2\beta+d)}\), and let
\(v_\beta(z)\) be the vector of monomials \(z^r\) with
\(|r|\leq\lceil\beta\rceil-1\).  Write
\(\mathcal C=[-1/2,1/2]^d\) and
\[
M_\beta=\int_{\mathcal C}v_\beta(z)v_\beta(z)^\top\,dz.
\]
This is a fixed positive-definite matrix.  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat Q_a
&=\frac1{n\ell^d}\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}\mathbf1\{(X_i-x_0)/\ell\in\mathcal C\}
v_\beta((X_i-x_0)/\ell)v_\beta((X_i-x_0)/\ell)^\top,\\
\widehat S_a
&=\frac1{n\ell^d}\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}\mathbf1\{(X_i-x_0)/\ell\in\mathcal C\}
v_\beta((X_i-x_0)/\ell)Y_i.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let \(\mathsf C_a(\widehat Q_a)\) denote spectral clipping of
\(\widehat Q_a\) to
\[
\left[\frac{\varepsilon f_-\lambda_{\min}(M_\beta)}2,
2f_+\lambda_{\max}(M_\beta)\right].
\]
Define
\[
\widehat\mu_a(x_0)=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\!\left\{
v_\beta(0)^\top\mathsf C_a(\widehat Q_a)^{-1}\widehat S_a\right\},\qquad
T_n=\operatorname{clip}_{[-2,2]}\{\widehat\mu_1(x_0)-\widehat\mu_0(x_0)\}.
\]
This is an explicit measurable estimator, including on singular empirical
designs.

For all sufficiently large \(n\), the localization cube is contained in the
support.  If \(e_1=\pi_P\) and \(e_0=1-\pi_P\), change of variables gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\widehat Q_a
=\int_{\mathcal C}e_a(x_0+\ell z)f_P(x_0+\ell z)
v_\beta(z)v_\beta(z)^\top\,dz.
\]
The density band and overlap imply
\[
\varepsilon f_-M_\beta\preceq\mathbb E_P\widehat Q_a\preceq f_+M_\beta.
\tag{2}
\]
The dimension of \(v_\beta\) is bounded uniformly on \(K\), and its entries
are bounded on \(\mathcal C\).  Independence and a direct second-moment
calculation consequently give
\[
\mathbb E_P\lVert\widehat Q_a-\mathbb E_P\widehat Q_a\rVert_F^2
\leq\frac{C_K}{n\ell^d}.
\]
Markov's inequality and (2) show that the probability that spectral clipping
is active is at most \(C_K/(n\ell^d)\).

On the complementary event, take the Taylor polynomial of
\(\mu_{a,P}\) at \(x_0\) through degree \(\lceil\beta\rceil-1\).  The defining
H\"older inequality, including the integer-order boundary case, gives
\[
\mu_{a,P}(x_0+\ell z)=v_\beta(z)^\top\theta_a+r_a(z),\qquad
\sup_{z\in\mathcal C}|r_a(z)|\leq C_KL\ell^\beta.
\]
Substitution in the normal equations decomposes the coefficient error into
a remainder part and a centered outcome-noise part.  Bound (2), the good
event, and the constant coordinate of \(v_\beta\) bound the remainder part
by \(C_K\ell^\beta\).  Conditional on \(X_{1:n},A_{1:n}\), bounded outcomes
and the same Gram bounds give variance at most \(C_K/(n\ell^d)\) for the
fitted intercept.  Jensen's inequality therefore gives conditional mean
absolute noise at most \(C_K(n\ell^d)^{-1/2}\).  Truncation cannot increase
error because \(\mu_{a,P}(x_0)\in[-1,1]\); on the bad-Gram event it bounds
the error by \(2\).  Thus
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P|\widehat\mu_a(x_0)-\mu_{a,P}(x_0)|
\leq C_K\left\{\ell^\beta+(n\ell^d)^{-1/2}+(n\ell^d)^{-1}\right\}.
\]
The final term is no larger than the middle term for large \(n\).  Applying
the triangle inequality to the two arms and using
\(\ell=n^{-1/(2\beta+d)}\) yields
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P|T_n-\tau_P(x_0)|\leq C_Kn^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}.
\]
The minimax upper bound follows by using this single \(T_n\) in the
infimum defining \(R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\).
