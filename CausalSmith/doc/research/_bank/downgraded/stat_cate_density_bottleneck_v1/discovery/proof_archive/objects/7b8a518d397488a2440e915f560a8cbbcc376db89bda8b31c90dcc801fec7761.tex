Let \(K\) be a compact subset of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  Constants
below may depend on \(K,d,L,\varepsilon,f_-,f_+\), and \(x_0\), but are
uniform in \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\) and \(n\).  The preceding
region lemma gives, uniformly on \(K\),
\[
0<\beta<\alpha\leq1,qquad 2s=\alpha+\beta<\gamma.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently \(\ell\leq h\) for \(n\geq2\), and compactness makes
\(h/2+\ell/4<\delta_0\) uniformly for all sufficiently large \(n\).  The
fallbacks in the estimator are therefore inactive after a uniform threshold.
Only finitely many polynomial degrees occur on \(K\), so the dimension and
supremum norm of \(b_\gamma\) are uniformly bounded.

\emph{Step 1: the exact pair moment.}
Write
\[
\eta(x)=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=x],\qquad
\pi(x)=\pi_P(x),\qquad
\nu(x)=\pi(x)\{1-\pi(x)\}.
\]
For fixed \(x,x'\), use subscripts \(+\) and \(-\) for evaluation at
\(x\) and \(x'\), respectively.  Independence of the two sampled units and
iterated expectation give
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\mathbb E_P[D_{ij}\mid X_i=x,X_j=x']
&=\pi_+\mu_{1,+}+\pi_-\mu_{1,-}
-\pi_+\eta_- -\pi_-\eta_+,\\
2\mathbb E_P[W_{ij}\mid X_i=x,X_j=x']
&=\pi_++\pi_- -2\pi_+\pi_-.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The identities
\(\mu_1=\eta+(1-\pi)\tau\) and
\(\pi+\pi'-2\pi\pi'=\nu+\nu'+(\pi-\pi')^2\) transform (2) into
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[D_{ij}\mid x,x']
&=\frac12\left\{
\nu(x)\tau_P(x)+\nu(x')\tau_P(x')
+\bigl(\pi(x)-\pi(x')\bigr)
\bigl(\eta(x)-\eta(x')\bigr)
\right\},\\
\mathbb E_P[W_{ij}\mid x,x']
&=\frac12\left\{
\nu(x)+\nu(x')+\bigl(\pi(x)-\pi(x')\bigr)^2
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
These are algebraic conditional identities, not assumed orthogonality
relations.

Put \(m=(x+x')/2\), \(\delta=x-x'\), and let \(p=p(m)\) be any
scalar.  Subtracting \(p\) times the second identity from the first gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[D_{ij}-W_{ij}p\mid x,x']
={}&\frac12\left\{
\nu(x)(\tau_P(x)-p)+\nu(x')(\tau_P(x')-p)
\right\}\\
&+\frac12\bigl(\pi(x)-\pi(x')\bigr)
\left\{
\eta(x)-\eta(x')-\bigl(\pi(x)-\pi(x')\bigr)p
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

\emph{Step 2: symmetric bias cancellation.}
Let \(p_h\) be the Taylor polynomial of \(\tau_P\) at \(x_0\), through
degree \(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\).  On the midpoint box it can be written
\[
p_h(m)=b_\gamma((m-x_0)/h)^{\mathsf T}\theta_h,
\qquad
b_\gamma(0)^{\mathsf T}\theta_h=\tau_P(x_0),
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\lVert\theta_h\rVert\leq C\) and
\[
|\tau_P(m)-p_h(m)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
\tag{6}
\]
The conditional mean \(\eta=\mu_{0,P}+\pi_P\tau_P\) is
\(\beta\)-H\"older with uniformly bounded norm: this follows from the
bounded-product inequality and \(\beta<\min(\alpha,\gamma)\).  Similarly,
\(\nu\) is \(\alpha\)-H\"older because \(\alpha\leq1\).

Write \(x=m+\delta/2\), \(x'=m-\delta/2\),
\(e_\pm=\tau_P(m\pm\delta/2)-\tau_P(m)\), and
\(\bar\nu=(\nu_++\nu_-)/2\).  The first line on the right side of (4)
equals
\[
\bar\nu\{\tau_P(m)-p_h(m)\}
+\frac{\bar\nu}{2}(e_++e_-)
+\frac{\nu_+-\nu_-}{4}(e_+-e_-).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(0<\gamma\leq1\), the two increments in (7) are bounded by
\(C\lVert\delta\rVert^\gamma\).  If \(\gamma>1\), Taylor expansion at
the common midpoint cancels the odd first-order term and gives
\[
|e_++e_-|\leq C\lVert\delta\rVert^{\min(\gamma,2)},
\qquad
|e_+-e_-|\leq C\lVert\delta\rVert.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus (6)--(8) bound (7) by
\[
C\left\{h^\gamma
+\lVert\delta\rVert^{\min(\gamma,2)}
+\lVert\delta\rVert^{\alpha+\min(\gamma,1)}\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
By (1), both exponents of \(\lVert\delta\rVert\) in (9) are strictly
larger than \(\alpha+\beta\): indeed
\(\min(\gamma,2)>\alpha+\beta\), while
\(\min(\gamma,1)>\beta\).  The second line of (4) is bounded by
\[
C\left\{
\lVert\delta\rVert^{\alpha+\beta}
+\lVert\delta\rVert^{2\alpha}|p_h(m)|
\right\}
\leq C\lVert\delta\rVert^{\alpha+\beta},
\tag{10}
\]
because \(\alpha>\beta\), \(p_h\) is bounded, and
\(\lVert\delta\rVert\leq1\).  Combining (4), (9), and (10), on the
support of the two kernels,
\[
\left|\mathbb E_P[D_{ij}-W_{ij}p_h(M_{ij})\mid X_i,X_j]\right|
\leq C\{h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The cancellation in (7) occurs before integration and therefore requires no
regularity of \(f_P\).

\emph{Step 3: population rank.}
Let \(Q_{h,\ell}=\mathbb E_P\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\).  Under the change of
variables
\[
x=m+\delta/2,qquad x'=m-\delta/2,
\tag{12}
\]
the absolute Jacobian is one.  For the active boundary convention the entire
kernel support is inside \([0,1]^{2d}\).  Moreover, (3), overlap, and the
pointwise bound \(0\leq W_{ij}\leq1/2\) imply
\[
\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)
\leq\mathbb E_P[W_{ij}\mid x,x']\leq\frac12.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(f_-^2\leq f_P(x)f_P(x')\leq f_+^2\), the normalization of the
box kernels, (12), and
\(\int_{\mathcal C}b_\gamma(z)b_\gamma(z)^{\mathsf T}\,dz=I\) yield
\[
c_0 I\preceq Q_{h,\ell}\preceq\frac{f_+^2}{2}I,
\qquad c_0=\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-^2.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the population spectrum lies a fixed positive distance from both
thresholds \(c_0/2\) and \(f_+^2\) in the estimator.  This rank argument uses
only the two-sided density band, not density derivatives.

Multiplying (11) by the normalized kernel and by the bounded vector
\(b_\gamma\), then using (12) and the upper density band, gives
\[
\left\|
\mathbb E_P\{\widehat S_{h,\ell}
-\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\theta_h\}
\right\|
\leq C\{h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}\}.
\tag{15}
\]

\emph{Step 4: finite-dimensional Hoeffding bounds.}
Consider any scalar entry of the kernel defining either
\(\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\) or
\(\widehat S_{h,\ell}-\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\theta_h\), and call that
bounded symmetric kernel \(H(O_i,O_j)\).  Apart from a fixed constant,
\[
|H(O_i,O_j)|
\leq K_h(M_{ij}-x_0)K_\ell(\Delta_{ij}),
\tag{16}
\]
because \(|D_{ij}|\leq1\), \(0\leq W_{ij}\leq1/2\), and
\(b_\gamma,\theta_h\) are bounded.  For fixed \(O_i\), integration over
\(X_j\) in (16) has volume at most \(\ell^d\) and hence magnitude at most
\(Ch^{-d}\).  It vanishes unless
\(\lVert X_i-x_0\rVert_\infty\leq h/2+\ell/4\leq3h/4\).  The density
upper bound consequently gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[
\mathbb E_P\{H(O_i,O_j)\mid O_i\}^2
\right]\leq Ch^{-d}.
\tag{17}
\]
Using (12) once more gives
\[
\mathbb E_P H(O_i,O_j)^2\leq Ch^{-d}\ell^{-d}.
\tag{18}
\]

For completeness, put \(\vartheta=\mathbb EH(O_1,O_2)\),
\(H_1(o)=\mathbb E\{H(o,O_2)\}-\vartheta\), and
\(H_2(o,o')=H(o,o')-\vartheta-H_1(o)-H_1(o')\).  Direct expansion gives
the finite-sample Hoeffding identity
\[
\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}H(O_i,O_j)-\vartheta
=\frac2n\sum_{i=1}^n H_1(O_i)
+\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}H_2(O_i,O_j).
\tag{19}
\]
The two terms are orthogonal, and distinct degenerate pairs in the second term
have zero covariance.  Hence (17)--(19) imply
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(
\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}H(O_i,O_j)
\right)
\leq C\left\{(nh^d)^{-1}+(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1}\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]
Because the matrix and vector dimensions are uniformly bounded, (20) holds
for their Euclidean and operator norms as well.  Define
\[
\zeta_n=(nh^d)^{-1/2}+(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}.
\tag{21}
\]
Then (15), (20), and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\mathbb E_P\left\|
\widehat S_{h,\ell}-\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\theta_h
\right\|
\leq C\{h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}+\zeta_n\},
\tag{22}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_P\lVert\widehat Q_{h,\ell}-Q_{h,\ell}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2
\leq C\zeta_n^2.
\tag{23}
\]

Let \(\mathcal G_n\) be the event on which the spectral cap is inactive.
The fixed spectral gaps in (14), (23), and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
P(\mathcal G_n^c)\leq C\zeta_n^2.
\tag{24}
\]
On \(\mathcal G_n\), the inverse norm is at most \(2/c_0\), so (5) and
(22) bound the mean intercept error by
\(C\{h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}+\zeta_n\}\).  On
\(\mathcal G_n^c\), final truncation bounds the loss by four because
\(\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2,2]\).  Since \(\zeta_n\to0\) uniformly on \(K\),
(24) is no larger than the order in (21).  We have proved
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
\leq C\left\{
h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}
+(nh^d)^{-1/2}+(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}
\right\}.
\tag{25}
\]

\emph{Step 5: bandwidth balance.}
By construction,
\[
h^\gamma=\ell^{2s}=n^{-t}.
\tag{26}
\]
The degenerate term satisfies
\[
(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}
=n^{-1+t d/(2\gamma)+t d/(4s)}=n^{-t},
\tag{27}
\]
where the last equality is the defining identity for \(t\).  The established
low-region exponent ordering gives
\[
t<q=\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.
\tag{28}
\]
Equivalently,
\(t(1+d/(2\gamma))<1/2\), and therefore
\[
(nh^d)^{-1/2}
=n^{-1/2+t d/(2\gamma)}=o(n^{-t}).
\tag{29}
\]
Finally, (28) and the definition of the KBRW benchmark imply
\[
r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}=n^{-t}\vee n^{-q}=n^{-t}.
\tag{30}
\]
Substitution of (26)--(30) into (25) proves the asserted compact-uniform
bound.  Every displayed population integral used only
\(f_-\leq f_P\leq f_+\); no density estimator or density modulus entered the
construction or proof.
