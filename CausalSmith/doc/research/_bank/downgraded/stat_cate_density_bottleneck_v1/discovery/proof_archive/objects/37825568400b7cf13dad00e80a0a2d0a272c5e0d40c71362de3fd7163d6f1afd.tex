Let \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) be compact.  Constants below may
depend on \(K,d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\), and \(x_0\), but not on
\(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\) or \(n\).  Compactness and the strict
inequalities defining \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) imply, uniformly on
\(K\),
\[
0<\beta<\min(\alpha,\gamma).
\tag{1}
\]
Only finitely many polynomial degrees occur on \(K\).  Since \(k_n\to
\infty\), the finite-sample fallback in the definition is inactive for all
\(n\ge n_0(K)\).  Each deterministic fold then has size between \(n/4\)
and \(n/2\).  We analyze one cyclic fit, writing \(N\asymp n\) for any of
its three fold sizes; all bounds are uniform over the three cyclic choices.

\emph{Step 1: cellwise nuisance bounds.}
For \(v\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\), let \(M_v=q_vm_v^d\) be the dimension of
\(B_{v,n}\).  Maximality of \(m_v\), together with the uniform bound on
\(q_v\), gives
\[
c_Kk_n\le M_v\le k_n.
\tag{2}
\]
The basis is cell supported.  Scaling a fixed-dimensional orthonormal
polynomial basis from a cell of volume \(m_v^{-d}\) gives
\[
\sup_{x\in[0,1]^d}\|B_{v,n}(x)\|_2^2\le C_KM_v.
\tag{3}
\]
Lebesgue orthonormality and the density band imply, for
\(G_v^0=\mathbb E_P[B_{v,n}(X)B_{v,n}(X)^\top]\),
\[
f_-I_{M_v}\preceq G_v^0\preceq f_+I_{M_v}.
\tag{4}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:matrix\mbox{-}bernstein}\) to the centered rank-one
summands in an empirical nuisance Gram matrix.  By (3)--(4), one summand has
operator norm at most \(C M_v\) and the variance proxy for the unnormalized
sum is at most \(C NM_v\).  Hence
\[
\Pr_P\!\left(\|G_{v,J}-G_v^0\|_{\mathrm{op}}>f_-/2\right)
\le C M_v\exp(-cN/M_v).
\tag{5}
\]
On the complementary event the nuisance spectral cap is inactive and the
empirical inverse has operator norm at most \(2/f_-\).

Let \(g\in\mathcal H_d^v(C_KL)\) be the conditional mean of the bounded
response used in one nuisance regression.  Its cellwise Taylor polynomial of
degree \(\lceil v\rceil-1\) has remainder at most \(C_Kk_n^{-v/d}\).
The empirical normal equations reproduce the polynomial part exactly.
Consequently, conditional on the nuisance-fold covariates and on the event in
(5), the untruncated fit \(\widetilde g\) can be written
\[
\widetilde g(x)-g(x)=b_g(x)+\zeta_g(x),
\qquad
\sup_x|b_g(x)|\le B_v:=C_Kk_n^{-v/d},
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\mathbb E_P(\zeta_g(x)\mid X_J)=0\),
\[
\sup_x\mathbb E_P\{\zeta_g(x)^2\mid X_J\}
\le V^2:=C_Kk_n/N,
\tag{7}
\]
and, conditional on \(X_J\), values of \(\zeta_g\) in distinct partition
cells depend on disjoint collections of centered responses and therefore have
zero covariance.

We next make the continuum-uniform assertion in (6)--(7) explicit.  On one
cell the stochastic error is a polynomial in a space of dimension \(q_v\),
which is bounded uniformly on \(K\).  In the scaled orthonormal coordinates,
the supremum norm of that polynomial is at most \(C_K k_n^{1/2}\) times the
Euclidean norm of its coefficient vector.  Conditional on the covariates,
one-dimensional matrix Bernstein applied to each coordinate, followed by a
fixed finite net of the coefficient sphere, yields
\[
\Pr_P\!\left(sup_{x\text{ in a fixed cell}}|\zeta_g(x)|>c_0
\mid X_J\right)
\le C_K\exp(-c_KN/k_n)
\tag{8}
\]
for every fixed \(c_0>0\).  This is a finite-dimensional coefficient bound,
not a union bound over continuum points.  Taking a union only over the at
most \(C_Kk_n\) cells and the three nuisance fits, and adjoining the Gram
failures in (5), gives an event \(\mathcal E_n\) with
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}P(\mathcal E_n^c)
\le \rho_n:=C_Kk_n\exp(-c_KN/k_n).
\tag{9}
\]
For large \(n\), the deterministic bias in (6) is smaller than half of the
fixed clipping buffer.  Thus (8) shows simultaneously over each cell that
both propensity clips and the outcome clip are inactive on \(\mathcal E_n\).
Equations (6)--(7), including the distinct-cell covariance property, therefore
hold there for the clipped fits themselves.  This also proves continuum-uniform
clipping control without assuming any continuity of \(f_P\).

For the propensity fits take \(g=\pi_P\) and \(v=\alpha\).  For the outcome
fit take
\[
\eta_P(x)=\mathbb E_P(Y\mid X=x)
=\mu_{0,P}(x)+\pi_P(x)\tau_P(x).
\tag{10}
\]
By (1), products of the bounded H\"older functions in (10) are
\(\beta\)-H\"older with radius bounded by \(C_KL\).  Hence (6)--(9) apply
to \(\widehat\pi_a,\widehat\pi_b\), and \(\widehat\eta\).  Notice that
\(\widehat\pi_a\) is trained on one fold and the pair
\((\widehat\pi_b,\widehat\eta)\) on the next fold; the former is independent
of the latter, exactly as required below.

We record the cell-covariance calculation that will control their stochastic
products.  Let
\[
w_h(x)=h_n^{-d}w((x-x_0)/h_n)
\mathbf 1\{(x-x_0)/h_n\in\mathcal C\},
\qquad \|w\|_\infty\le C_K,
\]
where \(w\) is any coordinate or coordinate product of \(b_\gamma\).
Let \(\zeta_a\) be a centered fit error from the first nuisance fold and let
\(\zeta_c\) be a centered fit error from the independent second nuisance
fold.  Conditional on the first-fold covariates, the covariance kernel of
\(\zeta_a\) vanishes across distinct \(\alpha\)-cells and is bounded by
\(C_Kk_n/N\) within a cell by (7).  Since every such cell has volume at most
\(C_K/k_n\), Cauchy--Schwarz cell by cell gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_P\left|
\int w_h(x)\zeta_a(x)\zeta_c(x)f_P(x)\,dx
\right|^2\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{C_K}{N}h_n^{-2d}
\int_{x_0+h_n\mathcal C}
\mathbb E_P\{\zeta_c(x)^2\}f_P(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\le
C_K\frac{k_n}{N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
For clarity, the first inequality is obtained by expanding the square,
discarding pairs in distinct first-fold cells, and using
\[
\left(\int_{D}|w_h\zeta_c|f_P\right)^2
\le \frac{C_K}{k_n}h_n^{-2d}
\int_D\zeta_c^2 f_P
\]
on each cell \(D\).  Taking square roots in (11) yields the required
same-cell stochastic-product bound
\[
C_K\sqrt{k_n/N}\,(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}.
\tag{12}
\]
The identical coefficient calculation with one factor replaced by a bias
bounded by \(B_v\) gives at most
\(C_KB_v(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\).  These assertions remain valid when
\(\zeta_c\) is the centered error of either \(\widehat\pi_b\) or
\(\widehat\eta\); those two fits may be dependent on each other because only
their joint independence from \(\zeta_a\) was used.

\emph{Step 2: the corrected local normal equation.}
Suppress the cyclic index and write
\[
\pi=\pi_P,\qquad
\eta=\eta_P,\qquad
\nu=\pi(1-\pi),\qquad
Z=A-\pi(X),\qquad R=Y-\eta(X).
\]
Iterated expectation gives
\[
\mathbb E_P(ZR\mid X)=\nu(X)\tau_P(X),
\qquad
\mathbb E_P(Z^2\mid X)=\nu(X).
\tag{13}
\]
For the actual double-split nuisance errors
\[
e_a=\widehat\pi_a-\pi,\qquad
e_b=\widehat\pi_b-\pi,\qquad
e_\eta=\widehat\eta-\eta,
\]
direct expansion of one evaluation-fold summand gives, for every function
\(p\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(A-\widehat\pi_a)(Y-\widehat\eta)
-(A-\widehat\pi_a)(A-\widehat\pi_b)p\\
&\quad=ZR-Z^2p
+e_a(Zp-R)+Z(e_bp-e_\eta)+e_a(e_\eta-e_bp).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the two first-order nuisance terms are centered conditional on \(X\) and
the nuisance folds.  By (6)--(7), the root-mean-square norm of their local
empirical averages is bounded by
\[
C_K\{B_\alpha+B_\beta+V\}
(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
\]
and hence their mean absolute contribution is
\[
C_K\{B_\alpha+B_\beta+V\}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
\le C_K(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2},
\tag{15}
\]
where \(V=C_K\sqrt{k_n/N}\).  In particular, the centered stochastic part
of (15) is at most
\(C_K\sqrt{k_n/N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\), the same order as (12).

It remains to bound the last product in (14).  Its expectation over the two
independent nuisance folds is the product of their conditional biases, and is
therefore at most
\[
C_K\{B_\alpha B_\beta+B_\alpha^2\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Its training-sample centered part is bounded by (12), plus the bias--centered
terms described after (12).  Conditional on both nuisance folds, the centered
evaluation-fold empirical average has pointwise second moment at most
\(C_K(k_n/N)^2\), so its mean absolute contribution is at most
\[
C_K(k_n/N)(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2},
\tag{17}
\]
which is no larger than (12).  Equations (12), (15), and (17) explicitly
account for both the same-cell stochastic products and all first-order
centered nuisance empirical terms.

Let \(b=b_\gamma\), and define the oracle population local Gram matrix
\[
Q_h=\int_{\mathcal C}b(z)b(z)^\top
\nu(x_0+h_nz)f_P(x_0+h_nz)\,dz.
\]
Lebesgue orthonormality, overlap, and the density band imply
\[
\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-I
\preceq Q_h\preceq f_+I/4.
\tag{18}
\]
The dimension of this matrix is fixed on each of the finitely many degree
strata meeting \(K\).  Entrywise application of the one-dimensional case of
\(\mathrm{lem:matrix\mbox{-}bernstein}\), together with (12), (15), and
(16), yields
\[
\mathbb E_P\|\widehat Q-Q_h\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le C_K\left\{(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}+B_\alpha^2
+\sqrt{k_n/N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}+\rho_n\right\}.
\tag{19}
\]
Both endpoints in (18) lie a fixed positive distance inside the clipping
interval \([\varepsilon^2f_-/2,2f_+]\).  Markov's inequality applied to
(19) therefore shows that the local spectral cap is inactive except on an
event whose probability is bounded by the right side of (19).  On the good
event, \(\|\widehat Q^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C_K\); on every event the
safeguarded inverse is well defined.  This is the required fixed-dimensional
local Gram control.  Denote this local good event by \(\mathcal G_r\).

Let \(p_h(z)=b(z)^\top\theta_h\) be the Taylor polynomial of \(\tau_P\) at
\(x_0\), evaluated at \(x_0+h_nz\), through degree
\(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\).  Then
\[
b(0)^\top\theta_h=\tau_P(x_0),
\qquad
\sup_{z\in\mathcal C}|\tau_P(x_0+h_nz)-p_h(z)|
\le C_Kh_n^\gamma.
\tag{20}
\]
Apply (14) with \(p=p_h\), multiply by the local vector weight, and average
over the evaluation fold.  The first two terms in (13), (18), and (20) give
the oracle contribution
\(C_K\{h_n^\gamma+(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\}\).  Equations (12), (15)--(17) give
the nuisance contributions.  Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
|\widehat t_r-\tau_P(x_0)|
\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n\cap\mathcal G_r}\right]
\le C_K\big[&h_n^\gamma+(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
+B_\alpha B_\beta+B_\alpha^2\\
&+\sqrt{k_n/N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}+\rho_n\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
The final truncation to \([-2,2]\) cannot increase loss because
\(\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2,2]\), and it bounds loss on the union of all failure
events by four.  The failure probability from (19) has precisely the orders
already displayed in (21).  Averaging the three cyclic intercepts and using
the triangle inequality therefore preserves (21), up to a constant.

\emph{Step 3: substitution of the tuning parameters.}
Put
\[
p=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{d},\qquad
q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d}.
\]
Since \(N\asymp n\),
\[
h_n^\gamma\asymp(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\asymp n^{-q},
\qquad
B_\alpha B_\beta\le C_Kk_n^{-p},
\tag{22}
\]
and (1) gives \(B_\alpha^2\le C_Kk_n^{-p}\).  Moreover,
\[
\sqrt{k_n/N}\,(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
\le \frac{C_K}{\log n}n^{-q}\le C_Kn^{-q},
\tag{23}
\]
so the corrected same-cell term and the first-order centered terms are below
the oracle stochastic term.  Finally,
\[
k_n^{-p}\le C_Kn^{-p}(\log n)^{2p},
\qquad
\rho_n\le C_Kk_n\exp\{-c_K(\log n)^2\},
\tag{24}
\]
and the last quantity is smaller than every inverse polynomial, uniformly on
\(K\).  Substitution of (22)--(24) into (21) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
\le C_K\left\{n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\vee n^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}
(\log n)^{2(\alpha+\beta)/d}\right\}
=C_K\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.
\]
This is uniform over the chosen compact subset \(K\) and proves the theorem.
