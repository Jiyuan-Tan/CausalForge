Let \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) be compact.  All constants below
may depend on \(K,d,L,\varepsilon,f_-,f_+\), and \(x_0\), but not on
\(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\) or \(n\).  Compactness and the strict
inequalities defining \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) imply uniformly on
\(K\) that
\[
0<\beta<\min(\alpha,\gamma),
\]
and that the finitely many polynomial degrees appearing in the construction
are uniformly bounded.  Since \(k_n\to\infty\), the finite-sample fallback in
the corrected definition is inactive for all \(n\geq n_0(K)\).  The three
deterministic fold sizes are between \(n/4\) and \(n/2\) for such \(n\), so
it suffices to analyze one cyclic fit with nuisance-fold and evaluation-fold
sizes denoted by \(N\asymp n\).

We first verify the nuisance bounds required by the localized double-residual
calculation.  For \(v\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\), let
\(M_v=q_vm_v^d\) be the dimension of \(B_{v,n}\).  Maximality of \(m_v\)
and boundedness of \(q_v\) on \(K\) give
\[
c_Kk_n\leq M_v\leq k_n. \tag{1}
\]
The basis is supported cell by cell, and scaling a fixed-dimensional
orthonormal polynomial basis from a cell of volume \(m_v^{-d}\) gives
\[
\sup_x\|B_{v,n}(x)\|_2^2\leq C_KM_v. \tag{2}
\]
For the population Gram matrix \(G_v^0=\mathbb E_P[B_{v,n}(X)
B_{v,n}(X)^\top]\), the density band and Lebesgue orthonormality yield
\[
f_-I_{M_v}\preceq G_v^0\preceq f_+I_{M_v}. \tag{3}
\]
Applying the self-adjoint matrix Bernstein inequality to the independent
rank-one summands, using (2)--(3), gives constants \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
\Pr_P\!\left(\|G_{v,J}-G_v^0\|_{\mathrm{op}}>f_-/2\right)
\leq C M_v\exp(-cN/M_v). \tag{4}
\]
Here the variance proxy is \(C NM_v\), while each centered summand has norm
at most \(CM_v\); substituting the fixed deviation \(Nf_-/2\) gives (4).
By (1) and \(k_n=\lfloor n/(\log n)^2\rfloor\), the right side of (4) is
at most \(C\exp\{-c(\log n)^2\}\), after changing constants.  On the
complement of this event, \(\mathsf C_{\mathrm{nuis}}\) is inactive and the
empirical inverse has norm at most \(2/f_-\).

Let \(g\in\mathcal H_d^v(L)\).  On each grid cell, its Taylor polynomial of
degree \(\lceil v\rceil-1\) belongs to the local span and has uniform
remainder at most \(C_KLm_v^{-v}\).  The empirical least-squares equations
reproduce this polynomial exactly on the good-Gram event.  Thus, conditional
on the nuisance design points, the untruncated series fit \(\widetilde g\)
obeys, uniformly in \(x\),
\[
\left|\mathbb E_P(\widetilde g(x)\mid X_J)-g(x)\right|
\leq C_K k_n^{-v/d},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_P(\widetilde g(x)\mid X_J)
\leq C_K k_n/N. \tag{5}
\]
The variance follows from (2), the blockwise empirical inverse bound, and
bounded conditional response variance.  Fits at points in distinct cells are
conditionally independent; if two points are in the same cell, their distance
is at most \(C_Kk_n^{-1/d}\).  Conditional Bernstein applied to the same
linear weights also gives, for every fixed positive \(c_0\),
\[
\Pr_P\!\left(
|\widetilde g(x)-\mathbb E_P(\widetilde g(x)\mid X_J)|>c_0
\,middle|,X_J\right)
\leq C\exp(-cN/k_n), \tag{6}
\]
uniformly in \(x\) on the good-Gram event.

On \(K\), \(\eta_P=\mu_{0,P}+\pi_P\tau_P\) is
\(\beta\)-H\"older: products of bounded H\"older functions are
H\"older of the minimum order, and \(\beta<\alpha,\gamma\).  Therefore
(5)--(6) apply to the two propensity fits with \(v=\alpha\) and to the
outcome fit with \(v=\beta\).  The true propensity lies in
\([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) and the true \(\eta_P\) lies in
\([-1,1]\).  The corrected clipping ranges have fixed buffers around these
sets.  Hence (6) shows that clipping changes the conditional biases by at most
\(C\exp(-cN/k_n)\); its contraction property and (5) preserve the variance
order.  Consequently, with
\[
B_v=C_Kk_n^{-v/d}+C\exp(-cN/k_n),
\qquad V_v^2=C_Kk_n/N, \tag{7}
\]
the conditional biases are bounded by \(B_v\), the conditional variances by
\(V_v^2\), and dependence between evaluations of a nuisance fit is confined
to a grid cell.  A union bound over the at most \(k_n\) cells makes all these
bounds simultaneous at a cost bounded by
\[
\rho_{n,1}=Ck_n\exp(-cN/k_n). \tag{8}
\]

We next perform the localized double-residual expansion.  Write
\[
\eta=\pi\mu_1+(1-\pi)\mu_0,qquad
\nu=\pi(1-\pi),qquad
\phi=(A-\pi)(Y-\eta).
\]
Iterated expectation gives the two identities
\[
\mathbb E_P(\phi\mid X)=\nu(X)\tau_P(X),
\qquad
\mathbb E_P\{(A-\pi)^2\mid X\}=\nu(X). \tag{9}
\]
Because \(\varepsilon\leq\pi\leq1-\varepsilon\) and
\(f_-\leq f_P\leq f_+\), the population local Gram matrix, after changing
variables to \(z=(x-x_0)/h_n\), satisfies
\[
\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-I
\preceq
\int_{\mathcal C}b_\gamma(z)b_\gamma(z)^\top
\nu(x_0+h_nz)f_P(x_0+h_nz)\,dz
\preceq f_+I/4. \tag{10}
\]
The dimension in (10) is fixed on \(K\).  Scalar Bernstein applied entrywise
to the evaluation-fold local Gram gives a failure probability at most
\[
\rho_{n,2}=C\exp(-cNh_n^d). \tag{11}
\]
The simultaneous constant-deviation bound following (6) makes the nuisance
perturbation of this Gram smaller than half the lower bound in (10), except on
an event absorbed by (8).  Thus the local spectral safeguard is inactive on
the resulting good event; it remains uniformly invertible everywhere by
construction.

For completeness, the algebra producing the two nuisance products is written
out.  Conditional on \(X\) and the two independent nuisance folds,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[(A-\widehat\pi_a)(Y-\widehat\eta)\mid X]
&=\nu\tau_P+(\pi-\widehat\pi_a)(\eta-\widehat\eta),\\
\mathbb E_P[(A-\widehat\pi_a)(A-\widehat\pi_b)\mid X]
&=\nu+(pi-\widehat\pi_a)(\pi-\widehat\pi_b).
\end{aligned} \tag{12}
\]
Independence of the first nuisance fold from the second changes the means of
the products in (12) into products of conditional biases.  Equations
(7)--(8), local support of the weights, and Cauchy--Schwarz therefore bound
the two deterministic nuisance contributions by
\[
C\{B_\alpha B_\beta+B_\alpha^2\}. \tag{13}
\]
The products of the centered nuisance fits have conditional second moment at
most \(C V_\alpha^2V_\beta^2\) and are correlated only when the evaluation
points occupy the same nuisance cell.  Summing these covariances over the
local evaluation sample, using the density upper bound to control the expected
number of same-cell pairs, gives mean absolute contribution
\[
C\frac{k_n}{N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}. \tag{14}
\]
This is the same cell-counting calculation as follows directly by expanding
the square: diagonal pairs contribute
\(C(k_n/N)^2/(Nh_n^d)\), while off-diagonal pairs can occur only within a
cell of volume at most \(C/k_n\), whose probability is at most \(C/k_n\);
taking square roots yields (14), with smaller terms absorbed.

Finally, the local polynomial weights reproduce every polynomial of degree at
most \(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\).  The H\"older remainder of \(\tau_P\) is
at most \(CLh_n^\gamma\), and bounded outcomes with (10) give oracle
standard deviation at most \(C(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\).  Combining these facts with
(12)--(14), the inverse identity
\(Q_1^{-1}-Q_0^{-1}=Q_1^{-1}(Q_0-Q_1)Q_0^{-1}\), and the uniform inverse
bounds yields for one fold
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P|\widehat t_r-\tau_P(x_0)|
\leq C\bigg[&h_n^\gamma+(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
+k_n^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}+k_n^{-2\alpha/d}\\
&+\frac{k_n}{N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
+\rho_{n,1}+\rho_{n,2}\bigg]. \tag{15}
\end{aligned}
\]
The truncation to \([-2,2]\) cannot increase loss because
\(\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2,2]\), and it bounds the loss on all failure events by
four.  Averaging the three folds and applying the triangle inequality leaves
the right side of (15) unchanged up to a constant.

It remains only to substitute the declared tuning.  Put
\[
p=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{d},\qquad
q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d}.
\]
Then
\[
h_n^\gamma=(nh_n^d)^{-1/2}=n^{-q},
\qquad
k_n^{-p}\leq Cn^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}. \tag{16}
\]
Since \(\alpha>\beta\), \(2\alpha>\alpha+\beta\), so the
\(k_n^{-2\alpha/d}\) term is no larger than \(Ck_n^{-p}\).  Also
\(k_n/N\leq C(\log n)^{-2}\), and the two failure terms in (15) decay
faster than every inverse polynomial because
\(N/k_n\asymp(\log n)^2\) and
\(Nh_n^d\asymp n^{2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\).  Equations (15)--(16) therefore
give, uniformly over \(P\) and over \(K\),
\[
\mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)|
\leq C\left\{n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\vee n^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}(\log n)^{2(\alpha+\beta)/d}\right\}
=C\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.
\]
This proves the theorem.
