The lower theorem gives
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\ge c n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
The rank-capped upper theorem supplies one measurable sample map whose maximal
mean absolute error is at most \(C\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}\).
Substituting that map into the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:band\mbox{-}risk}\)
gives
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\le C\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.
\]
Both constants are uniform on compact subsets of
\(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), proving the first display.

Put \(p=(\alpha+\beta)/d\) and
\(q=\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\).  The established exponent-ordering lemma gives
\(0<p<q\) throughout \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  Hence, for all
sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
n^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}\ge n^{-q},
\]
and the definition of the upper rate reduces to
\[
\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}
=n^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}.
\]
This proves the displayed bracket.  Its lower endpoint has exponent \(q\)
and its upper endpoint has the strictly smaller exponent \(p\).  Therefore
the bracket neither determines a polynomial minimax exponent nor proves that
an unknown density confined to a fixed positive band causes a slowdown.
