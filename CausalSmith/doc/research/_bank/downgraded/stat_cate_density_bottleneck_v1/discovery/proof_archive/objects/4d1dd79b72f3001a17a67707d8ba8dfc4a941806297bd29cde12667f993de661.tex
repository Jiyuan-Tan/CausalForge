The proposed version of
\(\mathrm{thm:density\mbox{-}coupled\mbox{-}lower\mbox{-}bound}\) gives
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\geq c n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
The proved rank-capped estimator theorem supplies a single measurable sample
map whose maximal mean absolute error is at most
\(C\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}\).  Substituting that map in the infimum in
\(\mathrm{def:band\mbox{-}risk}\) gives
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\leq C\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.
\]
Both constants are uniform on compact subsets of
\(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), so the first display in the proposed theorem
follows.

Now put \(p=(\alpha+\beta)/d\) and
\(q=\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\).  The already established exponent-ordering lemma
gives \(0<p<q\) throughout \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  Therefore
\[
n^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}\geq n^{-q}
\]
for every sufficiently large \(n\), and the definition of the upper rate
reduces to
\[
\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}
=n^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}.
\]
This proves the displayed bracket.  Because its lower endpoint has exponent
\(q\) and its upper endpoint has the strictly smaller exponent \(p\), the
inequalities permit every possible polynomial exponent between them; they do
not prove that the upper exponent is minimax or that the unknown positive-band
density causes a slowdown.
