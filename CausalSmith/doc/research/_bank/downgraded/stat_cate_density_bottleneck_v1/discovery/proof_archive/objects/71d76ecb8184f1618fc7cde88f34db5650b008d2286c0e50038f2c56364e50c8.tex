The declared object \(\mathfrak H_n^{\mathrm{dens}}\) is only a narrative program: it specifies neither the two priors nor their observed category probabilities, resolutions, amplitudes, target shift, or moment-matching equations.  Its natural paired-Rademacher realization fails quantitatively.  Put \(t=\alpha+\beta\), use inner cells of width \(h\), and use the outer width \(H=h^{t/\gamma}\) forced by a \(\gamma\)-smooth target of height \(\delta=h^t\).  There are \(m\asymp(H/h)^d\) cells.  After first-order sign cancellation, the same-cell two-observation term has scale
\[
n^2m h^{2d}\delta^2\asymp n^2h^{d+2t+td/\gamma}.
\]
At the advertised rank scale \(h=n^{-1/d}\), this is \(n^{1-t/\gamma-2t/d}\), which diverges because the defining low-smoothness inequality is exactly \(1-t/\gamma-2t/d>0\).  Merely adding signed density perturbations does not remove this second moment; a new moment-matched prior must both cancel it and preserve the quadratic target shift.  Assuming those two properties would assume the theorem's crux.
