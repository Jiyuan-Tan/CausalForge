\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}cate\_\allowbreak{}density\_\allowbreak{}bottleneck}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A total close-pair local \(R\)-estimator attains the Kennedy--Balakrishnan--Robins--Wasserman benchmark rate on the nonempty region \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\) using only the fixed density band \(f_{-}\leq f_P\leq f_{+}\), with no density estimator or density smoothness.  The proved positive-band minimax bracket is \(c n^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\leq Cn^{-t}=Cr_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}\) with \(t<q\), so exact minimaxity and a matching positive-band converse remain open.  A broader rank-capped estimator gives the stated logarithmically inflated upper bound on \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The density-qualified pointwise CATE benchmark is known, but under only a fixed positive band on an otherwise arbitrary design density there was no primitive, density-free estimator verification at that benchmark and there is still no matching positive-band converse.  Existing empirical higher-order influence-function results concern global functionals or projection dimensions below the relevant sample rank and do not determine this localized frontier.

\paragraph{Niche.} On \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), midpoint localization and sufficiently close covariate pairs turn treatment and outcome differences into a local \(R\)-normal equation whose nuisance remainder is of order \(\ell^{\alpha+\beta}\).  The two-sided density band alone gives population rank and the pairwise Hoeffding variance calculation, so density smoothness is unnecessary for benchmark-rate achievability.

\paragraph{Fill.} The note constructs a total pair-difference \(U\)-statistic estimator and proves the compact-uniform risk bound \(h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}+(nh^d)^{-1/2}+(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}\), optimized at \(n^{-t}=r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}\).  It combines this estimator with the oracle pointwise-regression lower branch to give the honest bracket \(c n^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\leq Cn^{-t}\), while retaining the broader rank-capped fallback outside the close-pair region.

\section{Related work}

Kennedy, Balakrishnan, Robins, and Wasserman (2024) provide the pointwise CATE benchmark formula and a density-qualified construction; their flat-top lower construction has support holes and therefore is not a converse for the present fixed-positive-band class.  Kennedy (2023) develops local-polynomial doubly robust CATE estimation under high-level nuisance-smoother and Gram conditions.  Liu, Mukherjee, Newey, and Robins (2017), and Liu, Li, Gu, and Liu (2026), motivate empirical or stabilized Gram replacement for global bilinear functionals, but do not settle the localized asymmetric frontier here.  Shen, Gao, Witten, and Han (2020) are the closest random-design precedent for pair-difference \(U\)-statistics without density smoothness; the causal pair identity and midpoint-symmetric remainder used here are specific to CATE estimation.  Robins, Li, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and van der Vaart (2016) concern quadratic-estimator limit theory rather than this pointwise minimax exponent.

\section{Setup}

Target (estimand / structural parameter): The observed-data target is \(\tau_P(x_0)=\mu_{1,P}(x_0)-\mu_{0,P}(x_0)\).  Under consistency, conditional exchangeability, overlap, and the continuous-version convention in \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\), this same point target equals the causal estimand \(\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x_0]\).  The statistical contribution concerns estimation risk for this target over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\); the benchmark rate and minimax frontier are comparison objects, not alternative estimands.
Primary functional / estimator / diagnostic: \[
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}=\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\cap
\{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):0<\beta<\alpha\leq1\},
\qquad
t=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{4s}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d}.
\]
Uniformly over compact subsets of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), the total close-pair estimator satisfies
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
\lesssim n^{-t}=r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}},
\]
using only \(f_-\leq f_P\leq f_+\), without estimating \(f_P\) or assuming density smoothness.  Together with the oracle pointwise-regression lower branch,
\[
n^{-q}\lesssim R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\lesssim n^{-t},
\qquad t<q.
\]
The distinct endpoints leave the exact band-only minimax exponent and a matching positive-band converse open.  On \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), the broader rank-capped fallback remains
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
\lesssim\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.
\]
If \(\pi_P,\mu_{0,P},\mu_{1,P}\) are supplied, the direct plug-in \(\mu_{1,P}(x_0)-\mu_{0,P}(x_0)\) has zero error.  Separately, oracleizing the local \(R\)-normal equations while retaining their evaluation sample gives mean absolute error \(O(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)})\).

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_{1:n}\sim P_O^{\otimes n}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent and identically distributed sampling, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=A Y(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Potential-outcome consistency, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-exchangeability]\label{ass:conditional-exchangeability}
\((Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid X\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (No unmeasured confounding, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:density-band]\label{ass:density-band}
\(f_{-}\le f_P(x)\le f_{+}\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\in[0,1]^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Two-sided bounded random-design density, \cite{LiuMukherjeeNeweyRobins2017EmpiricalHOIF}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:overlap]\label{ass:overlap}
\(\varepsilon\le\pi_P(x)\le1-\varepsilon\) for every \(x\in[0,1]^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform overlap, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-potential-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes}
\(|Y(a)|\le1\) almost surely under \(P\) for every \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly bounded outcomes, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-propensity]\label{ass:holder-propensity}
\(\pi_P\in\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_d(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (H\"older-smooth propensity score, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-outcome-regressions]\label{ass:holder-outcome-regressions}
\(\mu_{a,P}\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(L)\) for every \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (H\"older-smooth outcome regressions, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-cate]\label{ass:holder-cate}
\(\tau_P\in\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}_d(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (H\"older-smooth CATE, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:holder-ball]\label{def:holder-ball}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal\{H\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}\{u\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}d(L)}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathcal{H}^{u}_d(L)=\left\{g:[0,1]^d\to\mathbb{R}:\max_{|v|\le m_u}\lVert D^v g\rVert_\infty\le L,\ \max_{|v|=m_u}\sup_{x\ne x'}\frac{|D^v g(x)-D^v g(x')|}{\lVert x-x'\rVert_2^{u-m_u}}\le L\right\},\qquad m_u=\lceil u\rceil-1.\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:low-smoothness-region]\label{def:low-smoothness-region}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal\{R\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{low\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{low}}=\left\{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in(0,\infty)^3:0<\beta<\min(\alpha,\gamma),\ s=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}<\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2\gamma)}\right\}.\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:band-class]\label{def:band-class}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{band},n}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+},x_0)=\left\{P:\text{for this }n\in\mathbb{N},\text{ every member property enumerated below holds}\right\}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}iid-\allowbreak{}sampling}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}exchangeability}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}density-\allowbreak{}band}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}overlap}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}potential-\allowbreak{}outcomes}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}holder-\allowbreak{}propensity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}holder-\allowbreak{}outcome-\allowbreak{}regressions}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}holder-\allowbreak{}cate}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:band-risk]\label{def:band-risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}n\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{band\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[R_n^{\mathrm{band}}=\inf_{T_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{band},n}}\mathbb{E}_P\left|T_n(O_{1:n})-\tau_P(x_0)\right|,\] where the infimum is over all measurable estimators \(T_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}band-\allowbreak{}class}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:kbrw-rate]\label{def:kbrw-rate}

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:band-rate]\label{def:band-rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{r\_\allowbreak{}n\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{band\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[r_n^{\mathrm{band}}=n^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}\vee n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:band-upper-rate]\label{def:band-upper-rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}overline r\_\allowbreak{}n\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{band\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\vee n^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}(\log n)^{2(\alpha+\beta)/d}.\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:rank-capped-estimator]\label{def:rank-capped-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}tau\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{cap\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}n(x\_\allowbreak{}0)}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For every \(v\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\), put
\[
q_v=\binom{d+\lceil v\rceil-1}{d}.
\]
For \(n<3\), define \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)=0\).  For
\(n\geq3\), if \(q_\alpha>k_n\), if \(q_\beta>k_n\), or if for either
\(v\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\) there is no positive integer \(m\) satisfying
\(q_vm^d\leq k_n\), again define the whole estimator to be zero.  Otherwise,
let
\[
m_v=\max\{m\in\mathbb N:q_vm^d\leq k_n\}.
\]
Partition \([0,1]^d\) into the regular \(m_v^d\) tensor cells, using
half-open cells except at the upper boundary.  On each cell, orthonormalize
the monomials of total degree at most \(\lceil v\rceil-1\) in graded
lexicographic order with positive leading coefficient, and order the cells
lexicographically.  Extending every cell polynomial by zero off its cell
gives a deterministic Lebesgue-orthonormal vector \(B_{v,n}\) of dimension
\(q_vm_v^d\leq k_n\).

Write \(n=3b+r\), where \(b=\lfloor n/3\rfloor\) and
\(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), and set
\[
N_j=b+\mathbf 1\{j\leq r\},\qquad
I_1=\{1,\ldots,N_1\},\quad
I_2=\{N_1+1,\ldots,N_1+N_2\},\quad
I_3=\{N_1+N_2+1,\ldots,n\}.
\]
Subscripts on these folds are read modulo three in \(\{1,2,3\}\).  For a
fold \(J\), define
\[
G_{v,J}=\frac1{|J|}\sum_{i\in J}B_{v,n}(X_i)B_{v,n}(X_i)^\top,
\qquad
\mathsf C_{\mathrm{nuis}}(G)
=U\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
\min\{2f_+,\max\{f_-/2,\lambda_j\}\}
\right)U^\top
\]
when \(G=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_j)U^\top\).  In cyclic fit
\(r\in\{1,2,3\}\), use \(I_r\) to form
\[
\widetilde\pi_{r,a}(x)=B_{\alpha,n}(x)^\top
\mathsf C_{\mathrm{nuis}}(G_{\alpha,I_r})^{-1}
\frac1{|I_r|}\sum_{i\in I_r}B_{\alpha,n}(X_i)A_i,
\]
and use the independent fold \(I_{r+1}\) to form the analogous
\(\widetilde\pi_{r,b}\) and
\[
\widetilde\eta_r(x)=B_{\beta,n}(x)^\top
\mathsf C_{\mathrm{nuis}}(G_{\beta,I_{r+1}})^{-1}
\frac1{|I_{r+1}|}\sum_{i\in I_{r+1}}B_{\beta,n}(X_i)Y_i.
\]
Put
\[
\widehat\pi_{r,a}=\operatorname{clip}_{[\varepsilon/2,1-\varepsilon/2]}
(\widetilde\pi_{r,a}),\qquad
\widehat\pi_{r,b}=\operatorname{clip}_{[\varepsilon/2,1-\varepsilon/2]}
(\widetilde\pi_{r,b}),\qquad
\widehat\eta_r=\operatorname{clip}_{[-2,2]}(\widetilde\eta_r).
\]
The enlarged clipping intervals leave a fixed buffer around the true ranges
\([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) and \([-1,1]\).

Let \(\mathcal C=[-1/2,1/2]^d\), let \(b_\gamma\) be the vector obtained by
orthonormalizing, in graded lexicographic order with positive leading
coefficient, the polynomials on \(\mathcal C\) of total degree at most
\(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\), and set
\[
b_{i,h}=b_\gamma((X_i-x_0)/h_n),\qquad
K_{i,h}=h_n^{-d}\mathbf1\{(X_i-x_0)/h_n\in\mathcal C\}.
\]
On the evaluation fold \(I_{r+2}\), form
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat Q_r&=\frac1{|I_{r+2}|}\sum_{i\in I_{r+2}}
K_{i,h}b_{i,h}b_{i,h}^\top
\{A_i-\widehat\pi_{r,a}(X_i)\}
\{A_i-\widehat\pi_{r,b}(X_i)\},\\
\widehat S_r&=\frac1{|I_{r+2}|}\sum_{i\in I_{r+2}}
K_{i,h}b_{i,h}
\{A_i-\widehat\pi_{r,a}(X_i)\}
\{Y_i-\widehat\eta_r(X_i)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For a symmetric matrix \(Q=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_j)U^\top\), define
\[
\mathsf C_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q)=U\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
\min\{2f_+,\max\{\varepsilon^2f_-/2,\lambda_j\}\}
\right)U^\top.
\]
Let
\[
\widehat\theta_r=\mathsf C_{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat Q_r)^{-1}
\widehat S_r,
\qquad
\widehat t_r=b_\gamma(0)^\top\widehat\theta_r,
\]
and define \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)\) as the truncation to
\([-2,2]\) of \(3^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^3\widehat t_r\).  The two spectral maps
are independent of the choice of eigenvectors on repeated eigenspaces.
Together with the fixed ordering, boundary convention, explicit fallbacks,
and consecutive folds above, this is a total measurable function of
\(O_{1:n}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}iid-\allowbreak{}sampling}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}density-\allowbreak{}band}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}overlap}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}band-\allowbreak{}class}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:close-pair-r-estimator]\label{def:close-pair-r-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}tau\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{pair\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}n(x\_\allowbreak{}0)}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Put \(\mathcal C=[-1/2,1/2]^d\), and let
\(\kappa(z)=\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal C\}=\prod_{r=1}^d
\mathbf 1\{|z_r|\leq1/2\}\).  If
\((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\notin\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), define
\(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)=0\).  Otherwise set
\[
s=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2},\qquad
t=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{4s}\right)^{-1},
\qquad h=n^{-t/\gamma},\qquad \ell=n^{-t/(2s)}.
\]
Let
\[
\delta_0=\min_{1\leq r\leq d}\{x_{0,r},1-x_{0,r}\},
\qquad
K_h(z)=h^{-d}\kappa(z/h),
\qquad K_\ell(z)=\ell^{-d}\kappa(z/\ell).
\]
Orthonormalize, for Lebesgue measure on \(\mathcal C\), the monomials of
total degree at most \(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\), in graded lexicographic order
and with positive leading coefficient; denote the resulting deterministic
column vector by \(b_\gamma\).  If \(n<2\) or
\(h/2+\ell/4\geq\delta_0\), use the fallback
\(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)=0\).

In every remaining case, for each unordered pair \(1\leq i<j\leq n\), put
\[
M_{ij}=\frac{X_i+X_j}{2},\qquad
\Delta_{ij}=X_i-X_j,
\qquad
D_{ij}=\frac{(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)}2,qquad
W_{ij}=\frac{(A_i-A_j)^2}2,
\]
and \(b_{ij}=b_\gamma((M_{ij}-x_0)/h)\).  Define the pair
\(U\)-statistics
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat Q_{h,\ell}
&=\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}
K_h(M_{ij}-x_0)K_\ell(\Delta_{ij})
b_{ij}b_{ij}^{\mathsf T}W_{ij},\\
\widehat S_{h,\ell}
&=\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}
K_h(M_{ij}-x_0)K_\ell(\Delta_{ij})
b_{ij}D_{ij}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Write \(c_0=\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-^2\).  For a symmetric
matrix \(Q=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_j)U^{\mathsf T}\), set
\[
\mathsf C_{\mathrm{pair}}(Q)
=U\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
\min\{f_+^2,\max\{c_0/2,\lambda_j\}\}
\right)U^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Finally define
\[
\widehat\theta_{h,\ell}
=\mathsf C_{\mathrm{pair}}(\widehat Q_{h,\ell})^{-1}
\widehat S_{h,\ell},
\qquad
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)
=\operatorname{clip}_{[-2,2]}
\left\{b_\gamma(0)^{\mathsf T}\widehat\theta_{h,\ell}\right\}.
\]
The half-open convention may be used on one face of each box kernel; the
choice is immaterial on null boundaries.  The spectral map is independent of
the eigenbasis chosen within repeated eigenspaces.  Together with the fixed
polynomial ordering and the stated fallbacks, the construction is a total
measurable function of \(O_{1:n}\).

\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:cate-identification]\label{lem:cate-identification}
For every \(P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{band},n}\), the continuous versions selected by the H\"older restrictions satisfy \[\tau_P(x_0)=\mathbb{E}_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x_0].\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\).  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), let
\(g_a(X)\) be an arbitrary regular version of
\(\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid X]\), and put \(e_1=\pi_P\) and
\(e_0=1-\pi_P\).  If \(\varphi\) is bounded and measurable, consistency gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[\varphi(X)\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y]
&=\mathbb E_P[\varphi(X)\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y(a)] .
\end{aligned}
\]
Conditional exchangeability and iterated expectation then give
\[
\mathbb E_P[\varphi(X)\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y(a)]
=\mathbb E_P[\varphi(X)e_a(X)g_a(X)].
\]
On the other hand, the definition of \(\mu_{a,P}\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[\varphi(X)\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y]
=\mathbb E_P[\varphi(X)e_a(X)\mu_{a,P}(X)].
\]
Because these identities hold for every \(\varphi\),
\(e_a(g_a-\mu_{a,P})=0\) \(P_X\)-almost surely.  Uniform overlap gives
\(e_a\geq\varepsilon>0\), so
\[
g_a=\mu_{a,P}\qquad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Consequently \(\mu_{1,P}-\mu_{0,P}=\tau_P\) is a version of
\(\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X]\).  The H\"older restrictions provide a
continuous version of \(\tau_P\).  Equivalently, one may select the two
potential-outcome conditional-mean versions to be
\(g_a^{\mathrm c}=\mu_{a,P}\), whose difference is that same continuous
version.  Since \(x_0\) is an interior point and the displayed conditional
expectation is, as the statement says, evaluated in this selected continuous
version, evaluation yields
\[
\tau_P(x_0)=\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x_0].
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This selects a law-functional version of the point target before any minimax comparison is made. \textit{Gap.} The downgraded prior CausalSmith proposal left the unrestricted conditional mean at \(x_0\) version-dependent; the H\"older class used by Kennedy et al. selects a continuous version. \textit{Consumer.} The minimax risk \(R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\) and every downstream estimator comparison use this identified point target.

\begin{theorem}[thm:density-coupled-lower-bound]\label{thm:density-coupled-lower-bound}
Fix \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+},x_0\).  Uniformly over compact subsets of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), there are constants \(c>0\) and \(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\geq n_0\),
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\geq c\,n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
This conclusion is the oracle pointwise-regression branch; it does not assert a density-induced \(n^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}\) branch under the fixed positive density band.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compact set \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  By
\(\mathrm{lem:oracle\mbox{-}regression\mbox{-}lower\mbox{-}bound}\), there
are constants \(c_K>0\) and \(n_{0,K}\) such that, uniformly over
\((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in K\),
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\geq c_K n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\qquad(n\geq n_{0,K}).
\]
That lemma proves the inequality by two full-data laws in
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\) having the same uniform covariate density,
the same constant propensity \(1/2\), bounded Rademacher potential outcomes,
and CATEs separated at \(x_0\) by a \(\gamma\)-smooth bump of height
\(c_*n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\).  Thus its constants have exactly the compact
uniformity required here.  Because this pair keeps the design density fixed,
the argument proves only the displayed oracle pointwise-regression branch; it
does not prove a density-induced branch of order
\(n^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem supplies the oracle pointwise-regression lower endpoint used in both positive-band brackets. \textit{Gap.} It keeps the design density fixed and therefore does not prove a density-induced nuisance lower branch or a matching close-pair converse. \textit{Consumer.} It anchors the proved lower endpoint without licensing a slowdown claim.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rank-capped-upper-bound]\label{thm:rank-capped-upper-bound}
Fix \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+},x_0\). Uniformly over compact subsets of \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{low}}\), there are constants \(C<\infty\) and \(n_0\) such that for every \(n\ge n_0\), \[\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{band},n}}\mathbb{E}_P\left|\widehat{\tau}^{\mathrm{cap}}_n(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|\le C\,\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(K\) be a compact subset of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  Every constant below may depend on \(K,d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\), and \(x_0\), but not on \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\) or \(n\).  Compactness and the strict inequalities defining \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) give, uniformly on \(K\),
\[
0<\beta<\min(\alpha,\gamma).
\tag{1}
\]
Only finitely many polynomial degrees occur on \(K\).  Since \(k_n\to\infty\), all finite-sample fallbacks in the definition of \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)\) are inactive once \(n\ge n_0(K)\).  Each deterministic fold then has size between \(n/4\) and \(n/2\).  We analyze one cyclic fit and write \(N\asymp n\) for any of its three fold sizes; the same bounds hold for all three cycles.

\emph{Step 1: cellwise nuisance regression.}
For \(v\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\), let \(M_v=q_vm_v^d\) be the dimension of \(B_{v,n}\).  Maximality of \(m_v\), together with the uniform bounds on the finitely many values of \(q_v\), gives
\[
c_Kk_n\le M_v\le k_n.
\tag{2}
\]
The basis is cell supported.  Scaling a fixed-dimensional orthonormal polynomial basis from a cell of volume \(m_v^{-d}\) gives
\[
\sup_{x\in[0,1]^d}\lVert B_{v,n}(x)\rVert_2^2\le C_KM_v.
\tag{3}
\]
Lebesgue orthonormality and the density band imply, for
\(G_v^0=\mathbb E_P[B_{v,n}(X)B_{v,n}(X)^{\mathsf T}]\),
\[
f_-I_{M_v}\preceq G_v^0\preceq f_+I_{M_v}.
\tag{4}
\]
Apply the matrix Bernstein lemma to the centered rank-one summands in an empirical nuisance Gram matrix and also to their negatives.  By (3)--(4), a summand has operator norm at most \(C_KM_v\), and the variance proxy of the unnormalized sum is at most \(C_KNM_v\).  Hence
\[
\Pr_P\!\left(\lVert G_{v,J}-G_v^0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}>f_-/2\right)
\le C_KM_v\exp(-c_KN/M_v).
\tag{5}
\]
On the complementary event the nuisance spectral cap is inactive and the empirical inverse has operator norm at most \(2/f_-\).

Let \(g\in\mathcal H_d^v(C_KL)\) be the conditional mean of the bounded response in one nuisance regression.  On a cell \(D\), let \(T_Dg\) be its Taylor polynomial of degree \(\lceil v\rceil-1\).  The H\"older condition gives
\[
\sup_{x\in D}|g(x)-T_Dg(x)|\le C_Km_v^{-v}\le C_Kk_n^{-v/d}=:B_v.
\tag{6}
\]
We spell out why empirical polynomial reproduction does not amplify this remainder.  Write \(B_D\) for the fixed \(q_v\)-dimensional block of basis functions supported on \(D\), and \(G_D\) for its block in \(G_{v,J}\).  The constant basis coordinate and the spectral bounds on \(G_D\) imply
\[
c_KN/M_v\le \#\{i\in J:X_i\in D\}\le C_KN/M_v.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(r_D=g-T_Dg\), Cauchy--Schwarz in the empirical Gram inner product gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|B_D(x)^{\mathsf T}G_D^{-1}
\frac1N\sum_{i\in J:X_i\in D}B_D(X_i)r_D(X_i)\right|\\
&\qquad\le
\{B_D(x)^{\mathsf T}G_D^{-1}B_D(x)\}^{1/2}
\left\{\frac1N\sum_{i\in J:X_i\in D}r_D(X_i)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\le C_KB_v,
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
where (3), (5), (6), and (7) justify the last inequality.  Thus, conditional on the nuisance-fold covariates and the Gram event in (5), the untruncated fit admits
\[
\widetilde g(x)-g(x)=b_g(x)+\zeta_g(x),
\qquad
\sup_x|b_g(x)|\le C_KB_v,
\tag{9}
\]
with \(\mathbb E_P[\zeta_g(x)\mid X_J]=0\) and
\[
\sup_x\mathbb E_P[\zeta_g(x)^2\mid X_J]
\le C_KM_v/N\le C_Kk_n/N=:V^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Because the empirical Gram is block diagonal and its spectral map preserves that block structure, values of \(\zeta_g\) in distinct cells use disjoint collections of centered responses and have zero conditional covariance.

On one cell the stochastic error is a polynomial in a space of dimension \(q_v\), uniformly bounded on \(K\).  Conditional scalar Bernstein bounds for its coefficient coordinates, followed by a fixed finite net of the coefficient sphere, yield, for each fixed \(c_0>0\),
\[
\Pr_P\!\left(\sup_{x\in D}|\zeta_g(x)|>c_0\mid X_J\right)
\le C_K\exp(-c_KN/M_v).
\tag{11}
\]
This is a finite-dimensional coefficient bound, not a union bound over continuum points.  Taking a union over at most \(C_Kk_n\) cells, the Gram failures, and the three nuisance fits produces an event \(\mathcal E_n\) such that
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}P(\mathcal E_n^c)
\le \rho_n:=C_Kk_n\exp(-c_KN/k_n).
\tag{12}
\]
For large \(n\), (6) is smaller than half the fixed clipping buffer.  Hence the two propensity clips and the outcome clip are inactive on \(\mathcal E_n\).  The covariance calculations below are performed for the untruncated errors conditional only on the covariates and Gram events; when the clipped fits occur in the loss decomposition, equality on \(\mathcal E_n\) and domination by the corresponding untruncated absolute moments justify the same bounds.  Thus no response-dependent clipping event is used as a conditioning event.

For the propensity fits take \(g=\pi_P\) and \(v=\alpha\).  For the outcome fit take
\[
\eta_P(x)=\mathbb E_P(Y\mid X=x)
=\mu_{0,P}(x)+\pi_P(x)\tau_P(x).
\tag{13}
\]
By (1), the elementary product rule for bounded H\"older functions shows that \(\pi_P\tau_P\) is \(\beta\)-H\"older with radius at most \(C_KL\); therefore (9)--(12) apply to \(\widehat\pi_a,\widehat\pi_b\), and \(\widehat\eta\).  The fit \(\widehat\pi_a\) is trained independently of the pair \((\widehat\pi_b,\widehat\eta)\).

We next record the density-free same-cell calculation.  Let
\[
w_h(x)=h_n^{-d}w((x-x_0)/h_n)
\mathbf 1\{(x-x_0)/h_n\in\mathcal C\},
\qquad \lVert w\rVert_\infty\le C_K,
\]
where \(w\) is a coordinate or coordinate product of \(b_\gamma\).  Let \(\zeta_a\) be a centered error from the first nuisance fold and \(\zeta_c\) one from the independent second nuisance fold.  Conditional on first-fold covariates, the covariance kernel of \(\zeta_a\) vanishes across distinct \(\alpha\)-cells and is at most \(C_Kk_n/N\) within one cell by (10).  Every cell has \(P_X\)-mass at most \(C_K/k_n\) by the upper density band and (2).  Expanding the square, discarding cross-cell covariances, and using Cauchy--Schwarz on each cell therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_P\left|
\int w_h(x)\zeta_a(x)\zeta_c(x)f_P(x)\,dx
\right|^2\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{C_K}{N}h_n^{-2d}
\int_{x_0+h_n\mathcal C}
\mathbb E_P\{\zeta_c(x)^2\}f_P(x)\,dx
\le C_K\frac{k_n}{N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Taking square roots yields
\[
C_K\sqrt{k_n/N}\,(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}.
\tag{15}
\]
The same calculation with one centered factor replaced by a bias bounded by \(B_v\) gives at most \(C_KB_v(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\).  No continuity or smoothness of \(f_P\) is used in (14).

\emph{Step 2: the local normal equation.}
Suppress the cyclic index and write
\[
\pi=\pi_P,\qquad \eta=\eta_P,\qquad
\nu=\pi(1-\pi),\qquad Z=A-\pi(X),\qquad R=Y-\eta(X).
\]
Consistency with the definitions of the conditional means, followed by iterated expectation, gives
\[
\mathbb E_P(ZR\mid X)=\nu(X)\tau_P(X),
\qquad
\mathbb E_P(Z^2\mid X)=\nu(X).
\tag{16}
\]
For
\[
e_a=\widehat\pi_a-\pi,\qquad
e_b=\widehat\pi_b-\pi,\qquad
e_\eta=\widehat\eta-\eta,
\]
direct algebra gives, for every function \(p\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(A-\widehat\pi_a)(Y-\widehat\eta)
-(A-\widehat\pi_a)(A-\widehat\pi_b)p\\
&\quad=ZR-Z^2p
+e_a(Zp-R)+Z(e_bp-e_\eta)+e_a(e_\eta-e_bp).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The two first-order nuisance terms in (17) are centered conditional on \(X\) and the nuisance folds.  Equations (9)--(10) and a conditional second-moment calculation for their local empirical averages bound their mean absolute contribution by
\[
C_K\{B_\alpha+B_\beta+V\}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
\le C_K(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}.
\tag{18}
\]
For the last product in (17), independence of the two training folds makes its training-sample expectation the product of the conditional biases, bounded by
\[
C_K(B_\alpha B_\beta+B_\alpha^2).
\tag{19}
\]
Its training-centered part is bounded by (15) and the bias--centered variant following (15).  Conditional on both nuisance folds, the centered evaluation-fold average has pointwise second moment at most \(C_K(k_n/N)^2\), and hence contributes at most
\[
C_K(k_n/N)(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2},
\tag{20}
\]
which is no larger than (15).

Let \(b=b_\gamma\), and define the oracle population local Gram matrix
\[
Q_h=\int_{\mathcal C}b(z)b(z)^{\mathsf T}
\nu(x_0+h_nz)f_P(x_0+h_nz)\,dz.
\]
Lebesgue orthonormality, overlap, and the density band imply
\[
\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-I
\preceq Q_h\preceq f_+I/4.
\tag{21}
\]
Its dimension is uniformly bounded on \(K\).  Let \(\delta_K>0\) be the minimum distance, over the finitely many degree strata meeting \(K\), from the spectral interval in (21) to the two boundaries \(\varepsilon^2f_-/2\) and \(2f_+\) of the local cap.  On \(\mathcal E_n\), entrywise scalar Bernstein for the oracle evaluation-fold fluctuation, together with (15), (18), and (19), gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\lVert\widehat Q-Q_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}
\right]
\le C_K\left\{(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}+B_\alpha^2
+\sqrt{k_n/N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Consequently, if \(\mathcal G_r\) denotes the event on which the local cap is inactive, the union bound and Markov's inequality applied only on \(\mathcal E_n\) yield
\[
P(\mathcal G_r^c)
\le \rho_n+\delta_K^{-1}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\lVert\widehat Q-Q_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n}
\right].
\tag{23}
\]
This restricted estimate avoids attributing a merely \(O(\rho_n)\) operator-norm contribution to the complement of \(\mathcal E_n\).  On \(\mathcal G_r\), \(\lVert\widehat Q^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le C_K\), while the safeguarded inverse is defined on every sample.

Let \(p_h(z)=b(z)^{\mathsf T}\theta_h\) be the Taylor polynomial of \(\tau_P\) at \(x_0\), evaluated at \(x_0+h_nz\), through degree \(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\).  Then
\[
b(0)^{\mathsf T}\theta_h=\tau_P(x_0),
\qquad
\sup_{z\in\mathcal C}|\tau_P(x_0+h_nz)-p_h(z)|
\le C_Kh_n^\gamma.
\tag{24}
\]
Apply (17) with \(p=p_h\), multiply by the local vector weight, and average over the evaluation fold.  Equations (16), (21), and (24) give the oracle contribution \(C_K\{h_n^\gamma+(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\}\).  Equations (15), (18)--(20) give the nuisance contributions.  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
|\widehat t_r-\tau_P(x_0)|
\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_n\cap\mathcal G_r}\right]
\le C_K\big[&h_n^\gamma+(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
+B_\alpha B_\beta+B_\alpha^2\\
&+\sqrt{k_n/N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}+\rho_n\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
The final truncation to \([-2,2]\) cannot increase loss because \(\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2,2]\), and it bounds the loss by four on every failure event.  The probability bound (23) consists exactly of the orders displayed in (25).  Taking the union over the three cycles, averaging their intercepts, and using the triangle inequality therefore preserves (25), up to a constant.

\emph{Step 3: rates.}
Put
\[
p=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{d},
\qquad q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d}.
\]
Since \(N\asymp n\),
\[
h_n^\gamma\asymp(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\asymp n^{-q},
\qquad
B_\alpha B_\beta\le C_Kk_n^{-p}.
\tag{26}
\]
Because \(\alpha>\beta\) on \(K\), \(B_\alpha^2\le C_Kk_n^{-p}\).  Moreover,
\[
\sqrt{k_n/N}(Nh_n^d)^{-1/2}
\le \frac{C_K}{\log n}n^{-q}\le C_Kn^{-q},
\tag{27}
\]
and
\[
k_n^{-p}\le C_Kn^{-p}(\log n)^{2p},
\qquad
\rho_n\le C_Kk_n\exp\{-c_K(\log n)^2\}.
\tag{28}
\]
The last expression is smaller than every inverse polynomial, uniformly on \(K\).  Substitution of (26)--(28) into (25) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
\le C_K\left\{n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\vee n^{-(\alpha+\beta)/d}(\log n)^{2(\alpha+\beta)/d}\right\}
=C_K\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.
\]
The constants and the threshold \(n_0(K)\) are uniform over the chosen compact set, proving the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem remains the explicit density-free fallback on the full declared low-smoothness region. \textit{Gap.} Its nuisance branch contains a logarithmic inflation and is not the headline close-pair benchmark result. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies coverage outside the close-pair region while the asymmetric frontier remains open.

\begin{theorem}[thm:band-density-polynomial-frontier]\label{thm:band-density-polynomial-frontier}
Under the preceding conditions, uniformly over compact subsets of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\),
\[
n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\lesssim R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\lesssim \overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.
\]
Writing \(p=(\alpha+\beta)/d\) and \(q=\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\), one has \(0<p<q\) on \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), and hence, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
c n^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\leq C n^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}.
\]
This bracket does not determine a polynomial minimax exponent and does not establish a positive-band slowdown relative to the density-qualified benchmark.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compact set \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  The theorem
\(\mathrm{thm:density\mbox{-}coupled\mbox{-}lower\mbox{-}bound}\) gives
constants \(c_K>0\) and \(n_{1,K}\) such that
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\geq c_K n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
\qquad(n\geq n_{1,K})
\tag{1}
\]
uniformly over \(K\).  On the other hand,
\(\mathrm{thm:rank\mbox{-}capped\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}bound}\) supplies the
single total measurable sample map
\(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)\) and constants \(C_K<\infty\) and
\(n_{2,K}\) for which
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{cap}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
\leq C_K\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}.
\tag{2}
\]
Substituting this one data-driven estimator for \(T_n\) in
\(\mathrm{def:band\mbox{-}risk}\) yields
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\leq C_K\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\qquad(n\geq n_{2,K}).
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (1) and (3), with
\(n_0=\max\{n_{1,K},n_{2,K}\}\), prove the first bracket.

Put
\[
p=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{d},
\qquad
q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d}.
\]
The lemma
\(\mathrm{lem:low\mbox{-}region\mbox{-}exponent\mbox{-}ordering}\) gives
\(0<p<q\) throughout \(K\).  Since \(K\) is compact and the inequalities
are strict, \(q-p\) is bounded below by a positive constant on \(K\).
Consequently, uniformly over \(K\),
\[
\frac{n^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}}{n^{-q}}
=n^{q-p}(\log n)^{2p}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{4}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:band\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}rate}\), equation (4) implies
that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\overline r_n^{\mathrm{band}}
=n^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining (1), (3), and (5) proves
\[
c_Kn^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\leq C_Kn^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}.
\]
Because \(p<q\), the endpoints have distinct polynomial exponents.  Hence
this bracket determines neither an exact polynomial minimax exponent nor a
positive-band slowdown relative to the density-qualified benchmark.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem records the strongest general two-sided risk bracket proved over the primitive positive-band class. \textit{Gap.} Its lower and upper polynomial exponents differ, and the broader upper endpoint retains a logarithmic factor. \textit{Consumer.} It tells downstream comparisons exactly which rates are proved and which minimax conclusion remains unavailable.

\begin{proposition}[prop:strict-kbrw-separation]\label{prop:strict-kbrw-separation}
For every \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), define
\[
p=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{d},\qquad
t=\frac{1}{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)},\qquad
q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d}.
\]
Then \(0<p<t<q<1/2\) and \(r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}=n^{-t}\).  Consequently, the additional premise \(R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\geq c n^{-p}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\) would imply
\[
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{band}}}{r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}}
\geq c n^{t-p}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Without that unproved premise, the available positive-band bracket does not determine the limit of \(R_n^{\mathrm{band}}/r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  The lemma
\(\mathrm{lem:low\mbox{-}region\mbox{-}exponent\mbox{-}ordering}\) gives
\[
0<p<t<q<\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(t<q\), \(\mathrm{def:kbrw\mbox{-}rate}\) gives
\[
r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}=n^{-t}\vee n^{-q}=n^{-t}>0.
\tag{2}
\]
If the additional premise in the proposition holds, division by the positive
quantity in (2) yields
\[
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{band}}}{r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}}
\geq c n^{t-p}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
where the limit follows from \(t-p>0\) in (1).

Without that premise,
\(\mathrm{thm:band\mbox{-}density\mbox{-}polynomial\mbox{-}frontier}\)
provides only
\[
c n^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\leq Cn^{-p}(\log n)^{2p}.
\]
Dividing by (2) gives
\[
c n^{t-q}\leq
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{band}}}{r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}}
\leq Cn^{t-p}(\log n)^{2p}.
\tag{3}
\]
The left endpoint in (3) tends to zero, whereas the right endpoint tends to
infinity.  Thus the proved bracket alone determines no limit of the minimax
risk ratio.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition isolates the valid exponent ordering and the additional converse premise that strict KBRW separation would require. \textit{Gap.} The required positive-band nuisance lower branch is not proved. \textit{Consumer.} It prevents an estimator-side rank cap from being misread as an information-theoretic separation.

\begin{proposition}[prop:oracle-regression-reduction]\label{prop:oracle-regression-reduction}
If \(\pi_P\) and both \(\mu_{a,P}\) are supplied as functions, then the direct plug-in \(\mu_{1,P}(x_0)-\mu_{0,P}(x_0)\) equals \(\tau_P(x_0)\) and has zero error.  Separately, oracleize the corrected weighted local \(R\)-normal equations defining \(\widehat{\tau}^{\mathrm{cap}}_n(x_0)\) by replacing their two propensity fits by \(\pi_P\) and their outcome fit by \(\eta_P=\pi_P\mu_{1,P}+(1-\pi_P)\mu_{0,P}\), while retaining the evaluation sample, local-polynomial stage, spectral safeguard, and truncation.  The resulting oracleized estimator has conditional target \(\tau_P\) and, uniformly under the preceding conditions, mean-absolute error \(O(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)})\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix the class constants and a law \(P\) satisfying the stated conditions.  All constants below may depend on those fixed constants, on \(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\), and on \(x_0\), but not on \(P\) or \(n\).

The first assertion is algebraic.  By the definition of the CATE regression,
\[
\mu_{1,P}(x_0)-\mu_{0,P}(x_0)=\tau_P(x_0).
\]
Hence the direct plug-in based on supplied outcome regressions has identically zero error.  By \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\), this observed-data quantity is also the selected continuous version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x_0]\).

We next analyze the separately oracleized sample estimator.  Abbreviate
\[
e(x)=\pi_P(x),\qquad
\eta(x)=e(x)\mu_{1,P}(x)+\{1-e(x)\}\mu_{0,P}(x),
\qquad
\nu(x)=e(x)\{1-e(x)\}.
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), the definitions of \(\eta\), \(\mu_{a,P}\), and \(\tau_P\) give
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y-\eta(X)\mid A=a,X]
=\{a-e(X)\}\tau_P(X).
\]
Multiplying by \(a-e(X)\), then averaging first over \(Y\) and then over \(A\) conditional on \(X\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[(A-e(X))(Y-\eta(X))\mid X]
&=\nu(X)\tau_P(X),\\
\mathbb E_P[\{A-e(X)\}^2\mid X]
&=\nu(X).
\end{aligned}
\]
The overlap assumption gives
\[
\nu(X)\geq\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)>0.
\]
Consequently the weighted conditional normal equation has the unique regression target \(\tau_P(X)\).  This proves the conditional-target assertion, and \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\) supplies its causal interpretation.

It remains to establish the uniform risk bound.  Put
\[
m=\lceil\gamma\rceil-1,
\]
let \(q\) be the fixed dimension of the polynomials of total degree at most \(m\), abbreviate \(h=h_n\), and write \(\mathcal C=[-1/2,1/2]^d\) and \(b=b_\gamma\).  Since \(x_0\in(0,1)^d\), there is \(h_0>0\) such that
\[
x_0+h\mathcal C\subset[0,1]^d
\quad\text{whenever}\quad 0<h\leq h_0.
\]
The dimensions of the degree-\(\lceil\alpha\rceil-1\) and degree-\(\lceil\beta\rceil-1\) one-cell polynomial spaces are fixed, while \(k_n\to\infty\).  Thus there is a fixed \(n_0\) such that for \(n\geq n_0\) the localization window is interior and none of the finite-sample fallbacks in \(\mathrm{def:rank\mbox{-}capped\mbox{-}estimator}\) is invoked.  In each cyclic fit, replacing both propensity fits by \(e\) and the outcome fit by \(\eta\) removes all nuisance-fold randomness; the corresponding evaluation fold, local-polynomial equation, spectral safeguard, and final truncation remain exactly those in that definition.

Fix one evaluation fold and denote its size by \(N\).  The deterministic balanced split gives \(N\geq n/4\) for every sufficiently large \(n\).  For observations in this fold define
\[
T_i=\frac{X_i-x_0}{h},
\qquad
K_i=h^{-d}\mathbf 1\{T_i\in\mathcal C\}.
\]
The oracle empirical local Gram matrix is
\[
\widehat Q
=\frac1N\sum_i K_i b(T_i)b(T_i)^\top\{A_i-e(X_i)\}^2.
\]
By the two conditional identities above and a change of variables, its expectation is
\[
Q_h
=\int_{\mathcal C}b(t)b(t)^\top
\nu(x_0+ht)f_P(x_0+ht)\,dt.
\]
The coordinates of \(b\) are Lebesgue-orthonormal on \(\mathcal C\).  Therefore the density band and overlap imply, for every \(z\in\mathbb R^q\),
\[
\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-\lVert z\rVert_2^2
\leq z^\top Q_hz
\leq \frac{f_+}{4}\lVert z\rVert_2^2.
\]
In particular, \(Q_h\) is uniformly nonsingular.  Moreover, its spectrum lies strictly inside the safeguard interval \([\varepsilon^2f_-/2,2f_+]\), because
\[
\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_->\varepsilon^2f_-/2
\quad\text{and}\quad
f_+/4<2f_+.
\]
Choose a constant \(\delta>0\) smaller than both spectral margins and set
\[
G=\{\lVert\widehat Q-Q_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\delta\}.
\]
The fixed-dimensional polynomial vector \(b\) is bounded on the compact cube \(\mathcal C\).  Using \(f_P\leq f_+\), \(|A-e(X)|\leq1\), independence within the fold, and \(\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm F}\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P\lVert\widehat Q-Q_h\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2
&\leq \frac{1}{N}\mathbb E_P
 \left\lVert K_i b(T_i)b(T_i)^\top\{A_i-e(X_i)\}^2\right\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2\\
&\leq \frac{C}{Nh^d}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence Markov's inequality gives
\[
P(G^c)\leq\frac{C}{Nh^d}.
\]
On \(G\), Weyl's eigenvalue inequality places the spectrum of \(\widehat Q\) inside the safeguard interval.  Thus the safeguard is inactive and
\[
\mathsf C_{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat Q)=\widehat Q,
\qquad
\lVert\widehat Q^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C.
\]

Let \(p_h(t)\) be the Taylor polynomial of \(x\mapsto\tau_P(x)\) at \(x_0\), evaluated at \(x=x_0+ht\), through total degree \(m\).  Since \(b\) spans this polynomial space, there is \(\theta_h\in\mathbb R^q\) such that
\[
p_h(t)=b(t)^\top\theta_h,
\qquad
b(0)^\top\theta_h=\tau_P(x_0).
\]
The H\"older CATE assumption gives the uniform Taylor remainder
\[
\sup_{t\in\mathcal C}
|\tau_P(x_0+ht)-p_h(t)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
\]
Define the vector summand
\[
U_i=K_i b(T_i)\left[
(A_i-e(X_i))(Y_i-\eta(X_i))
-\{A_i-e(X_i)\}^2p_h(T_i)
\right].
\]
The conditional-moment identities and the Taylor remainder imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert\mathbb E_P U_i\rVert_2
&=\left\lVert
\int_{\mathcal C}b(t)\nu(x_0+ht)f_P(x_0+ht)
\{\tau_P(x_0+ht)-p_h(t)\}\,dt
\right\rVert_2\\
&\leq Ch^\gamma.
\end{aligned}
\]
The declared support of \(Y\) gives \(|Y|\leq1\).  Also \(|\eta|\leq1\), and \(|p_h|\leq2+Ch^\gamma\) on \(\mathcal C\).  The boundedness of \(b\) and the density upper bound therefore yield
\[
\mathbb E_P\lVert U_i\rVert_2^2\leq Ch^{-d}.
\]
Independence and Jensen's inequality now give
\[
\mathbb E_P\left\lVert\frac1N\sum_iU_i\right\rVert_2
\leq
\lVert\mathbb E_PU_i\rVert_2
+\left\{
\mathbb E_P\left\lVert
\frac1N\sum_i(U_i-\mathbb E_PU_i)
\right\rVert_2^2
\right\}^{1/2}
\leq Ch^\gamma+C(Nh^d)^{-1/2}.
\]

Let \(\widehat\theta\) be the safeguarded oracle coefficient for this fold.  On \(G\), the safeguard is inactive, so the oracle normal equations give the exact identity
\[
\widehat\theta-\theta_h
=\widehat Q^{-1}\frac1N\sum_iU_i.
\]
Since \(b(0)\) has fixed dimension and norm, it follows that
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\mathbf 1_G
\left|b(0)^\top\widehat\theta-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
\right]
\leq C\{h^\gamma+(Nh^d)^{-1/2}\}.
\]

Apply this bound to all three evaluation folds and let \(G_{\mathrm{all}}\) be the intersection of their good-Gram events.  Balanced fold sizes and a union bound give
\[
P(G_{\mathrm{all}}^c)\leq C(nh^d)^{-1}.
\]
On \(G_{\mathrm{all}}\), the absolute error of the average of the three fold intercepts is at most the average of their absolute errors.  Projection onto \([-2,2]\) cannot increase distance from \(\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2,2]\).  On \(G_{\mathrm{all}}^c\), the projected estimator and target differ by at most four.  Consequently,
\[
\sup_P
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{or}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
\leq
C\left\{h^\gamma+(nh^d)^{-1/2}+(nh^d)^{-1}\right\},
\]
where the supremum is over all laws satisfying the stated conditions.  Finally, \(h=h_n=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)}\) implies
\[
h^\gamma=(nh^d)^{-1/2}=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},
\qquad
(nh^d)^{-1}=n^{-2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
For \(n<n_0\), both the retained truncation and \(\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2,2]\) bound the error by four, so enlargement of the constant covers these finitely many indices.  We conclude uniformly over the stated class that
\[
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{or}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
=O\!\left(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\right),
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This separates the zero-error direct plug-in revealed by supplied outcome regressions from the separately oracleized local R estimator, which retains evaluation-sample error. \textit{Gap.} Known functions remove nuisance uncertainty but do not erase sampling variability from a deliberately retained local-polynomial stage. \textit{Consumer.} It fixes the boundary case used to interpret the upper theorem.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:exact-asymmetric-frontier]\label{oeq:exact-asymmetric-frontier}
Under no regularity on \(f_P\) beyond \(f_{-}\leq f_P\leq f_{+}\), determine the exact minimax exponent and a matching positive-band converse on
\[
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\cap
\{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):0<\beta<\alpha\leq1\},
\]
where the proved close-pair upper endpoint is \(n^{-t}\), the available oracle lower endpoint is \(n^{-q}\), and \(t<q\).  Then determine whether the logarithmic factor in the broader rank-capped upper bound can be removed on the remainder of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), and characterize the corresponding band-only frontier for the opposite ordering \(\alpha<\beta\).  Benchmark-rate achievability on the displayed close-pair region is settled; exact minimaxity and the remaining orderings are not presupposed.
\end{openendedquestion}
On
\[
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}
=\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\cap
\{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):0<\beta<\alpha\leq1\},
\]
the established close-pair upper theorem and oracle lower theorem give,
uniformly over compact subsets,
\[
c n^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\leq C n^{-t}=C r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}},
\qquad
t=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{4s}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d},
\qquad t<q.
\]
Thus benchmark-rate achievability without density estimation or density
smoothness is proved on this nonempty region, and the logarithmic loss of the
broader rank-capped upper bound is absent there.  On the remainder of
\(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), the proved bracket is
\[
c n^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\leq Cn^{-p}(\log n)^{2p},
\qquad
p=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{d}<q.
\]
Finally, the definition of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) itself imposes
\(\beta<\alpha\); hence the opposite ordering \(\alpha<\beta\) lies outside
the presently defined low-smoothness region and has no frontier conclusion in
this core.
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This question records the remaining exact positive-band frontier after benchmark-rate close-pair achievability has been established. \textit{Gap.} A matching close-pair converse, log removal off the close-pair region, and a separately defined opposite-ordering regime are unresolved. \textit{Consumer.} A resolution would determine exact minimaxity and the remaining nuisance-smoothness phase map.

\begin{lemma}[lem:low-region-exponent-ordering]\label{lem:low-region-exponent-ordering}
The set \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) is nonempty.  Moreover, for every
\((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), if
\[
p=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{d}=\frac{2s}{d},\qquad
t=\frac{1}{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)},\qquad
q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d},
\]
then \(0<p<t<q<1/2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For nonemptiness, take
\((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(d/8,d/16,d)\).  Then
\(\beta<\min(\alpha,\gamma)\), \(s=3d/32\), and
\[
\frac{3d}{32}<\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2d)}=\frac d6,
\]
so this triple belongs to \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).

Now fix any member of the region.  Its strict smoothness inequality is
\[
s<\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2\gamma)}.
\]
Multiplication by the positive denominators shows that this is equivalent to
\[
\frac{2s}{d}+\frac{s}{\gamma}<\frac12. \tag{1}
\]
Since \(p=2s/d\), a direct calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
t>p
&\Longleftrightarrow
d>2s\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{4s}\right)\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\frac{2s}{d}+\frac{s}{\gamma}<\frac12,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (1).  The defining inequality also gives
\[
\frac d{4s}>1+\frac d{2\gamma}.
\]
Therefore
\[
1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{4s}>2+\frac d\gamma,
\]
and taking positive reciprocals gives
\(t<(2+d/\gamma)^{-1}=q\).  Finally \(p>0\) and
\(q=\gamma/(2\gamma+d)<1/2\), completing the chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:oracle-regression-lower-bound]\label{lem:oracle-regression-lower-bound}
Fix \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_-,f_+\), and \(x_0\).  Uniformly over compact
subsets of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), there are constants \(c>0\) and
\(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\geq n_0\),
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\geq c\,n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(K\) be a compact subset of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).  Choose a
nonnegative \(C^\infty\) function \(\psi\), supported on
\(\{z:\lVert z\rVert_\infty\leq1/2\}\), with \(\psi(0)=1\).  For a constant
\(c_*>0\) to be chosen, put
\[
H=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)},\qquad
\delta=c_*H^\gamma,\qquad
\tau_1(x)=\delta\,\psi\!\left(\frac{x-x_0}{H}\right),\qquad
\tau_0(x)=0.
\]
Compactness of \(K\) and interiority of \(x_0\) give a common \(n_0\) for
which the support of the bump is contained in \([0,1]^d\).  The scaled-bump
calculation in the definition of the H\"older ball gives, uniformly over
\(K\),
\[
\lVert\tau_1\rVert_{\mathcal H^\gamma_d}\leq C_Kc_*.
\]
Furthermore \(\beta<\gamma\) on \(K\), with the gap bounded away from zero,
so
\[
\left\lVert\frac{\tau_1}{2}\right\rVert_{\mathcal H^\beta_d}
\leq C_Kc_*H^{\gamma-\beta}\leq C_Kc_*.
\]
Choose \(c_*\) small enough that these norms are at most \(L\) and all means
below lie in \([-1/2,1/2]\).

We now construct two full-data laws.  Under both, \(X\) is uniform on
\([0,1]^d\), \(A\mid X\) is Bernoulli with probability \(1/2\), and \(A\) is
conditionally independent of the potential outcomes.  Under \(P_j\), set
\[
\mu_{1,j}(x)=\frac{\tau_j(x)}2,\qquad
\mu_{0,j}(x)=-\frac{\tau_j(x)}2,\qquad j\in\{0,1\},
\]
and, conditionally on \(X=x\), let \(Y(a)\in\{-1,1\}\) have mean
\(\mu_{a,j}(x)\); the two potential outcomes may be taken conditionally
independent.  Finally define \(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\).  Thus consistency and
conditional exchangeability hold by construction.  The propensity is the
constant \(1/2\), so overlap and every propensity H\"older condition hold.
The preceding scaled-bump bounds give the outcome-regression and CATE
H\"older conditions.  The potential outcomes are bounded, and the uniform
density is normalized and satisfies \(f_-<1<f_+\).  Hence \(P_0,P_1\) both
belong to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\).

It remains to bound their statistical distance without invoking an
unproved testing result.  Use the convention
\(H^2(Q_1,Q_0)=\int(\sqrt{q_1}-\sqrt{q_0})^2\).  Conditional on \(X=x\) and
\(A=a\), the outcome is a Rademacher variable whose mean changes from zero
to \(m_a(x)\), where \(|m_a(x)|=|\tau_1(x)|/2\).  Its chi-square divergence
from the mean-zero Rademacher law equals \(m_a(x)^2\), and squared Hellinger
distance is no larger than chi-square divergence.  Averaging over \(X,A\)
therefore yields
\[
H^2(P_{1,O},P_{0,O})\leq C\int\tau_1(x)^2\,dx
\leq C_K\delta^2H^d.
\]
Hellinger affinity tensorizes, so the identity
\[
H^2(P_{1,O}^{\otimes n},P_{0,O}^{\otimes n})
=2-2\left(1-\frac{H^2(P_{1,O},P_{0,O})}{2}\right)^n
\]
and \(1-(1-z)^n\leq nz\) imply
\[
H^2(P_{1,O}^{\otimes n},P_{0,O}^{\otimes n})
\leq C_Kn\delta^2H^d=C_Kc_*^2.
\]
Choose \(c_*\) still smaller so that the last Hellinger distance is at most
\(1/2\).  Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(|p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\) gives total variation no larger
than Hellinger distance, hence at most \(1/2\).

For any estimator \(T_n\), the point targets are separated by
\(\tau_1(x_0)-\tau_0(x_0)=\delta\).  Pointwise, the triangle inequality gives
\(|T_n-\tau_0(x_0)|+|T_n-\tau_1(x_0)|\geq\delta\).  Integrating this inequality
against the minimum of the two \(n\)-sample densities yields
\[
\max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{P_j}|T_n-\tau_j(x_0)|
\geq\frac{\delta}{2}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{1,O}^{\otimes n},P_{0,O}^{\otimes n})\}
\geq\frac{\delta}{4}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(T_n\) proves the result because
\(\delta=c_*n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\).  All constants were chosen uniformly
over \(K\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:matrix-bernstein]\label{lem:matrix-bernstein}
Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_N\) be independent random self-adjoint matrices of a common finite dimension \(D\).  If \(\mathbb E X_i=0\) and \(\lambda_{\max}(X_i)\leq R\) almost surely for every \(i\), then, for every \(t\geq0\),
\[
\Pr\!\left\{\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^N X_i\right)\geq t\right\}
\leq D\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2/2}{\sigma^2+Rt/3}\right\},
\qquad
\sigma^2=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb E(X_i^2)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:close-pair-region-properties]\label{lem:close-pair-region-properties}
The region \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\) contains a nonempty open subset of
\((0,\infty)^3\).  For every
\((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\),
\[
0<\beta<\alpha\leq1,
\qquad
\alpha+\beta=2s<\gamma.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
\alpha_* = \frac{d}{16(1+d/2)},
\qquad
\beta_* = \frac{\alpha_*}{2},
\qquad
\gamma_* = 1.
\]
Then \(0<\beta_*<\alpha_*<1\), and
\[
s_* =\frac{\alpha_*+\beta_*}{2}
=\frac{3d}{64(1+d/2)}
<\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2\gamma_*)}.
\]
Thus the displayed triple is in \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  All
inequalities are strict at that triple, including \(\alpha_*<1\), so a
sufficiently small open neighborhood is also contained in
\(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).

Now take any member of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  The first chain in
the conclusion is part of \(\mathrm{def:close\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}region}\).
The inequality defining \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\) gives
\[
s<\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2\gamma)}
=\frac{d\gamma}{2(2\gamma+d)}
<\frac\gamma2,
\]
because \(d/(2\gamma+d)<1\).  Hence
\(2s=\alpha+\beta<\gamma\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma records the nonvacuity and the strict smoothness inequality used by the close-pair construction. \textit{Gap.} The close-pair estimator needs \(\alpha+\beta<\gamma\), which is derived from the frozen low-smoothness condition rather than assumed separately. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the uniform exponent ordering for the close-pair upper theorem.

\begin{theorem}[thm:close-pair-kbrw-upper-bound]\label{thm:close-pair-kbrw-upper-bound}
Fix \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_-,f_+\), and \(x_0\).  Uniformly over compact
subsets of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), there are constants \(C<\infty\)
and \(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\geq n_0\), if
\[
t=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{4s}\right)^{-1},
\qquad h=n^{-t/\gamma},\qquad \ell=n^{-t/(2s)},
\]
then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
&\leq C\left\{
h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}
+(nh^d)^{-1/2}+(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}
\right\}\\
&\leq C n^{-t}=C r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The estimator uses neither an estimator nor a smoothness condition for
\(f_P\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(K\) be a compact subset of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  All constants below may depend on \(K,d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\), and \(x_0\), but they are uniform in \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}\) and \(n\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:close\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}region\mbox{-}properties}\), uniformly on \(K\),
\[
0<\beta<\alpha\leq1,
\qquad 2s=\alpha+\beta<\gamma.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(\ell\leq h\) for \(n\geq2\).  Let
\[
\delta_0=\min_{1\leq r\leq d}\{x_{0,r},1-x_{0,r}\}>0.
\]
Compactness of \(K\) implies \(h/2+\ell/4<\delta_0\) uniformly for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Thus the fallback clauses in \(\mathrm{def:close\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}r\mbox{-}estimator}\) are inactive after a uniform threshold.  Only finitely many polynomial degrees occur on \(K\), so the dimension and supremum norm of \(b_\gamma\) are uniformly bounded.

\emph{Step 1: exact conditional pair identities.}
Write
\[
\eta(x)=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=x],
\qquad \pi(x)=\pi_P(x),
\qquad \nu(x)=\pi(x)\{1-\pi(x)\}.
\]
For fixed \(x,x'\), use subscripts \(+\) and \(-\) for evaluation at \(x\) and \(x'\), respectively.  Independence from \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\) and iterated expectation give
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\mathbb E_P[D_{ij}\mid X_i=x,X_j=x']
&=\pi_+\mu_{1,+}+\pi_-\mu_{1,-}
-\pi_+\eta_- -\pi_-\eta_+,\\
2\mathbb E_P[W_{ij}\mid X_i=x,X_j=x']
&=\pi_++\pi_- -2\pi_+\pi_-.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The identities
\[
\mu_1=\eta+(1-\pi)\tau_P,
\qquad
\pi+\pi'-2\pi\pi'=\nu+\nu'+(\pi-\pi')^2
\]
transform (2) into
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[D_{ij}\mid x,x']
&=\frac12\left\{
\nu(x)\tau_P(x)+\nu(x')\tau_P(x')
+\bigl(\pi(x)-\pi(x')\bigr)
\bigl(\eta(x)-\eta(x')\bigr)
\right\},\\
\mathbb E_P[W_{ij}\mid x,x']
&=\frac12\left\{
\nu(x)+\nu(x')+\bigl(\pi(x)-\pi(x')\bigr)^2
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Hence, for \(m=(x+x')/2\), \(\delta=x-x'\), and any scalar \(p=p(m)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[D_{ij}-W_{ij}p\mid x,x']
={}&\frac12\left\{
\nu(x)(\tau_P(x)-p)+\nu(x')(\tau_P(x')-p)
\right\}\\
&+\frac12\bigl(\pi(x)-\pi(x')\bigr)
\left\{
\eta(x)-\eta(x')-\bigl(\pi(x)-\pi(x')\bigr)p
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
This is an algebraic conditional identity, not an assumed orthogonality relation.

\emph{Step 2: symmetric remainder bound.}
Let \(p_h\) be the Taylor polynomial of \(\tau_P\) at \(x_0\) through degree \(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:holder\mbox{-}cate}\) and the polynomial basis in \(\mathrm{def:close\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}r\mbox{-}estimator}\), on the midpoint box it has the representation
\[
p_h(m)=b_\gamma((m-x_0)/h)^{\mathsf T}\theta_h,
\qquad
b_\gamma(0)^{\mathsf T}\theta_h=\tau_P(x_0),
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\lVert\theta_h\rVert\leq C\) and
\[
|\tau_P(m)-p_h(m)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
\tag{6}
\]
The function \(\eta=\mu_{0,P}+\pi_P\tau_P\) is \(\beta\)-H\"older with uniformly bounded norm.  Indeed, this follows from \(\mathrm{ass:holder\mbox{-}propensity}\), \(\mathrm{ass:holder\mbox{-}outcome\mbox{-}regressions}\), \(\mathrm{ass:holder\mbox{-}cate}\), the bounded-product inequality on a compact domain, and \(\beta<\min(\alpha,\gamma)\).  Similarly, \(\nu\) is \(\alpha\)-H\"older because \(\alpha\leq1\).

Write \(x=m+\delta/2\), \(x'=m-\delta/2\),
\[
e_\pm=\tau_P(m\pm\delta/2)-\tau_P(m),
\qquad \bar\nu=\frac{\nu_++\nu_-}{2}.
\]
The first line on the right-hand side of (4) equals
\[
\bar\nu\{\tau_P(m)-p_h(m)\}
+\frac{\bar\nu}{2}(e_++e_-)
+\frac{\nu_+-\nu_-}{4}(e_+-e_-).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(0<\gamma\leq1\), H\"older continuity bounds the two increments in (7) by \(C\lVert\delta\rVert_2^\gamma\).  If \(\gamma>1\), Taylor expansion about the common midpoint cancels the odd first-order term and yields
\[
|e_++e_-|\leq C\lVert\delta\rVert_2^{\min(\gamma,2)},
\qquad
|e_+-e_-|\leq C\lVert\delta\rVert_2.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (6)--(8) therefore bound (7) by
\[
C\left\{
h^\gamma+\lVert\delta\rVert_2^{\min(\gamma,2)}
+\lVert\delta\rVert_2^{\alpha+\min(\gamma,1)}
\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Both powers of \(\lVert\delta\rVert_2\) in (9) are strictly larger than \(\alpha+\beta\): by (1), \(\min(\gamma,2)>\alpha+\beta\), and \(\min(\gamma,1)>\beta\).  The second line of (4) is bounded on the kernel support by
\[
C\left\{
\lVert\delta\rVert_2^{\alpha+\beta}
+\lVert\delta\rVert_2^{2\alpha}|p_h(m)|
\right\}
\leq C\lVert\delta\rVert_2^{\alpha+\beta},
\tag{10}
\]
because \(\alpha>\beta\), \(p_h\) is bounded, and eventually \(\lVert\delta\rVert_2\leq1\).  Combining (4), (9), and (10), on the support of the two kernels,
\[
\left|\mathbb E_P[D_{ij}-W_{ij}p_h(M_{ij})\mid X_i,X_j]\right|
\leq C\{h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The cancellation in (7) precedes integration and therefore imposes no regularity on \(f_P\).

\emph{Step 3: population rank under the density band.}
Let \(Q_{h,\ell}=\mathbb E_P\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\).  Under the change of variables
\[
x=m+\delta/2,
\qquad x'=m-\delta/2,
\tag{12}
\]
the absolute Jacobian is one, and the active boundary condition places the entire kernel support inside \([0,1]^{2d}\).  By (3) and \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\),
\[
\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)
\leq\mathbb E_P[W_{ij}\mid x,x']\leq\frac12.
\tag{13}
\]
Using \(\mathrm{ass:density\mbox{-}band}\), the normalization of the two box kernels, (12), and
\[
\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^d}b_\gamma(z)b_\gamma(z)^{\mathsf T}\,dz=I,
\]
we obtain
\[
c_0I\preceq Q_{h,\ell}\preceq\frac{f_+^2}{2}I,
\qquad c_0=\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-^2.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the population spectrum has a fixed positive gap from both clipping thresholds \(c_0/2\) and \(f_+^2\).  Multiplying (11) by the normalized kernels and bounded \(b_\gamma\), and then applying (12) and the density upper bound, gives
\[
\left\|
\mathbb E_P\{\widehat S_{h,\ell}-\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\theta_h\}
\right\|
\leq C\{h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}\}.
\tag{15}
\]

\emph{Step 4: finite-dimensional Hoeffding bounds.}
Consider any scalar entry of the kernel defining either \(\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\) or \(\widehat S_{h,\ell}-\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\theta_h\), and denote that bounded symmetric kernel by \(H(O_i,O_j)\).  Up to a fixed constant,
\[
|H(O_i,O_j)|
\leq K_h(M_{ij}-x_0)K_\ell(\Delta_{ij}),
\tag{16}
\]
because \(|D_{ij}|\leq1\), \(0\leq W_{ij}\leq1/2\), and \(b_\gamma,\theta_h\) are bounded.  For fixed \(O_i\), integration over \(X_j\) in (16) has volume at most \(\ell^d\), so its magnitude is at most \(Ch^{-d}\).  It vanishes unless \(\lVert X_i-x_0\rVert_\infty\leq h/2+\ell/4\leq3h/4\).  The density upper bound therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[
\mathbb E_P\{H(O_i,O_j)\mid O_i\}^2
\right]\leq Ch^{-d}.
\tag{17}
\]
Another application of (12) gives
\[
\mathbb E_PH(O_i,O_j)^2\leq Ch^{-d}\ell^{-d}.
\tag{18}
\]

For completeness, set \(\vartheta=\mathbb EH(O_1,O_2)\),
\[
H_1(o)=\mathbb E\{H(o,O_2)\}-\vartheta,
\qquad
H_2(o,o')=H(o,o')-\vartheta-H_1(o)-H_1(o').
\]
Direct expansion gives the finite-sample Hoeffding identity
\[
\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}H(O_i,O_j)-\vartheta
=\frac2n\sum_{i=1}^nH_1(O_i)
+\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}H_2(O_i,O_j).
\tag{19}
\]
The two terms are orthogonal, and distinct degenerate pairs in the second term have zero covariance.  Hence (17)--(19) imply
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(
\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}H(O_i,O_j)
\right)
\leq C\left\{(nh^d)^{-1}+(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1}\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]
Since the matrix and vector dimensions are uniformly bounded, the same order holds for their Euclidean and operator norms.  Define
\[
\zeta_n=(nh^d)^{-1/2}+(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (15), (20), and Cauchy--Schwarz yield
\[
\mathbb E_P\left\|
\widehat S_{h,\ell}-\widehat Q_{h,\ell}\theta_h
\right\|
\leq C\{h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}+\zeta_n\},
\tag{22}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_P\lVert\widehat Q_{h,\ell}-Q_{h,\ell}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2
\leq C\zeta_n^2.
\tag{23}
\]

Let \(\mathcal G_n\) be the event that the spectral map is inactive.  The fixed gaps in (14), (23), and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
P(\mathcal G_n^c)\leq C\zeta_n^2.
\tag{24}
\]
On \(\mathcal G_n\), the inverse norm is at most \(2/c_0\); hence (5) and (22) bound the mean intercept error by \(C\{h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}+\zeta_n\}\).  On \(\mathcal G_n^c\), final truncation bounds the loss by four because \(\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2,2]\).  Since \(\zeta_n\to0\) uniformly on \(K\), the contribution from (24) is no larger than the order in (21).  Therefore
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
\leq C\left\{
h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}
+(nh^d)^{-1/2}+(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}
\right\}.
\tag{25}
\]

\emph{Step 5: bandwidth balance.}
By construction and \(2s=\alpha+\beta\),
\[
h^\gamma=\ell^{2s}=n^{-t}.
\tag{26}
\]
Moreover,
\[
(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}
=n^{-1+td/(2\gamma)+td/(4s)}=n^{-t},
\tag{27}
\]
where the final equality is the defining identity for \(t\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:low\mbox{-}region\mbox{-}exponent\mbox{-}ordering}\),
\[
t<q=\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.
\tag{28}
\]
Equivalently, \(t(1+d/(2\gamma))<1/2\), and consequently
\[
(nh^d)^{-1/2}
=n^{-1/2+td/(2\gamma)}=o(n^{-t}).
\tag{29}
\]
Finally, (28) and \(\mathrm{def:kbrw\mbox{-}rate}\) imply
\[
r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}=n^{-t}\vee n^{-q}=n^{-t}.
\tag{30}
\]
Substitution of (26)--(30) into (25) proves the asserted compact-uniform bound.  The construction and every population integral above use only \(f_-\leq f_P\leq f_+\); no density estimator or density smoothness modulus enters the argument.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the headline theorem: one explicit total estimator attains \(n^{-t}=r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}\) on \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\) using only the fixed positive density band. \textit{Gap.} It strengthens the estimator-side density requirements relative to the benchmark analysis but supplies no matching positive-band converse. \textit{Consumer.} It is the measurable witness for the log-free minimax upper endpoint and bounded benchmark-risk ratio.

\begin{theorem}[thm:close-pair-band-frontier]\label{thm:close-pair-band-frontier}
Fix \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_-,f_+\), and \(x_0\).  Uniformly over compact
subsets of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), there are constants
\(0<c\leq C<\infty\) and \(n_0\) such that, for every \(n\geq n_0\),
\[
c n^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\leq C n^{-t}=C r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}},
\qquad
t=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{4s}\right)^{-1},
\quad q=\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.
\]
Here \(t<q\).  Thus benchmark-rate achievability is unconditional on this
region, while the displayed bracket does not determine the exact positive-band
minimax exponent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compact set \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  Since
\(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), the theorem
\(\mathrm{thm:density\mbox{-}coupled\mbox{-}lower\mbox{-}bound}\) gives
constants \(c_K>0\) and \(n_{1,K}\) such that
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\geq c_Kn^{-q},
\qquad
q=\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d},
\qquad n\geq n_{1,K},
\tag{1}
\]
uniformly over \(K\).  Only its oracle pointwise-regression branch is used.

The theorem
\(\mathrm{thm:close\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}kbrw\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}bound}\)
supplies the single total measurable estimator
\(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)\) and constants \(C_K<\infty\) and
\(n_{2,K}\) such that
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band},n}}
\mathbb E_P\left|
\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)
\right|
\leq C_Kn^{-t},
\qquad
t=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{4s}\right)^{-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
Substitution of this estimator in the infimum defining
\(R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\) gives
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\leq C_Kn^{-t}
\qquad(n\geq n_{2,K}).
\tag{3}
\]
The lemma
\(\mathrm{lem:low\mbox{-}region\mbox{-}exponent\mbox{-}ordering}\) gives
\(t<q\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{def:kbrw\mbox{-}rate}\) implies
\[
r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}=n^{-t}\vee n^{-q}=n^{-t}.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (1), (3), and (4), and taking
\(n_0=\max\{n_{1,K},n_{2,K}\}\), proves
\[
c_Kn^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}
\leq C_Kn^{-t}=C_Kr_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}.
\]
The strict inequality \(t<q\) shows that the endpoints differ, so the upper
endpoint proves benchmark-rate achievability but the bracket does not identify
the exact fixed-positive-band minimax exponent.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem combines the total close-pair estimator with the oracle lower branch to expose the strongest proved bracket on the close-pair region. \textit{Gap.} The endpoint exponents differ, so exact positive-band minimaxity is not determined. \textit{Consumer.} It separates completed density-free benchmark achievability from the open information-theoretic converse.

\begin{proposition}[prop:kbrw-bounded-ratio-achievability]\label{prop:kbrw-bounded-ratio-achievability}
For every \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), let
\[
t=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{4s}\right)^{-1},
\qquad q=\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.
\]
Then \(t<q\), \(r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}=n^{-t}\), and there are constants
\(C<\infty\) and \(n_0\) such that
\[
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{band}}}{r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}}\leq C
\qquad(n\geq n_0).
\]
This is unconditional bounded-ratio achievability under a density assumed only
to lie in the fixed positive band; it is not a matching minimax converse.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  Apply
\(\mathrm{thm:close\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}band\mbox{-}frontier}\) to the
compact singleton containing this triple.  There are constants
\(C<\infty\) and \(n_0\) such that
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\leq Cn^{-t}
\qquad(n\geq n_0).
\tag{1}
\]
The lemma
\(\mathrm{lem:low\mbox{-}region\mbox{-}exponent\mbox{-}ordering}\) gives
\(t<q\), and hence \(\mathrm{def:kbrw\mbox{-}rate}\) gives
\[
r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}=n^{-t}\vee n^{-q}=n^{-t}>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Division of (1) by (2) yields
\[
\frac{R_n^{\mathrm{band}}}{r_n^{\mathrm{KBRW}}}\leq C
\qquad(n\geq n_0).
\]
This uses only the upper endpoint of the close-pair frontier theorem and
therefore supplies no reverse comparison or matching positive-band converse.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition gives an unconditional estimator-side comparison with the KBRW benchmark under only the positive density band. \textit{Gap.} A bounded upper ratio is not a matching minimax-risk comparison. \textit{Consumer.} It rules out treating the broader rank-capped nuisance term as an unavoidable slowdown on the close-pair region.

\section{Interpretation}

The positive result is estimator-side: close pairs recover the KBRW benchmark over \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\) even when the design density has no smoothness.  The lower result is only the oracle pointwise-regression branch.  Since its exponent \(q\) is larger than the achievable exponent \(t\), the current theory neither proves a density-induced slowdown nor proves that the close-pair rate is minimax.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The unresolved step is information-theoretic, not merely a rank calculation.  A matching converse must construct laws that retain \(f_P\geq f_->0\), satisfy the unequal nuisance H\"older restrictions, separate \(\tau_P(x_0)\) at the \(n^{-t}\) scale, and keep the corresponding \(n\)-sample mixtures close.  The candidate density-coupled program has not verified these properties.  Log removal outside \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\) also remains unresolved, and \(\alpha<\beta\) lies outside the currently defined \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\).

\section{Honest scope}

The proved headline is benchmark-rate achievability, without density estimation or density smoothness, on the nonempty close-pair region.  The strongest minimax conclusion there is the loose bracket \(c n^{-q}\leq R_n^{\mathrm{band}}\leq Cn^{-t}\) with \(t<q\); exact positive-band minimaxity and a matching converse are not claimed.  On the remainder of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{low}}\), only the oracle lower branch and the logarithmically inflated rank-capped upper bound are proved.  The opposite nuisance ordering requires a newly defined regime.  If the outcome regressions are supplied, direct plug-in has zero error; oracleizing only the local normal equations while retaining the evaluation sample gives the classical pointwise-regression rate.
