\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}exposure\_\allowbreak{}ambiguity\_\allowbreak{}design\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), a one-shot exact-budget experiment at \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\) must estimate the all-treated-versus-all-control causal effect of a fixed bounded potential-outcome table obeying one unknown member of a finite exposure-mapping menu. Sign schedules give an exact finite decision reduction, a primal--dual second-order cone saddle computes \(V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)=L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)\), and an optimal solution recovers an attaining design and multilinear rule. The unconditional joint-signature certificate \(R_n\le|\Omega_n|\) yields an exact collision linear program and a universal congestion-\(R_n\) cover; if \(R_n=O(\log n)\), the menu size is fixed, and the incidence encoding is polynomial, the certificate is polynomial-size with polynomial-time coefficient computation. Locality and polynomial orbit tables alone are insufficient: no interference has collision scale \(n^{-1}\), exact frontier scale \(n^{-1/2}\), fiber modulus two, and necessary cover congestion \(\Omega(n^2)\). An explicit dense radius-one split-graph sequence has equal mapping-wise labeled conflict-graph multisets but exact latent-menu risks \(1\) and \(5/4\), a persistent separation of \(1/4\). Bounded-degree or sparse-natural local separation and broad primitive conditions guaranteeing the compression regime remain open. Relative to accepted-bank \texttt{exp\_snipe\_degree\_frontier\_v1/thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier}, which treats one known \(\beta\)-order neighborhood-interference model under unit Bernoulli assignment and clipped SNIPE, the finite-saddle theorem handles a latent finite menu of arbitrary bounded exposure-measurable tables, optimizes over all exact-treated-count assignment laws, and permits arbitrary measurable decision rules.

\section{Related work}

Faridani and Niehaus (2024, revised January 2026) study the same all-treated-versus-none global average treatment effect and jointly derive minimax rates over experimental designs and linear estimators when spillovers decay with a known generalized distance. That comparison is closer than known-operator variance optimization, but it does not cover an unknown member of a finite exposure menu, unrestricted bounded exposure-response tables, arbitrary nonlinear estimators, or the exact finite cross-model observation-cell saddle; the multilinear rule here is recovered losslessly from the unrestricted minimax problem rather than assumed as a linear or Riesz class. The accepted-bank theorem \texttt{exp\_snipe\_degree\_frontier\_v1/thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier} proves a matched field-tier minimax frontier for total-treatment-effect estimation under a known graph, a single known \(\beta\)-order neighborhood-interference response model, and unit-randomized Bernoulli\((p)\) assignment, attained by clipped SNIPE over its stated bounded model classes. The accepted-bank bipartite theorem optimizes a graph-only conservative H\'ajek variance envelope over heterogeneous Bernoulli probabilities under budget and positivity constraints; it does not claim the unrestricted latent-menu exact-count minimax saddle.

Kandiros, Harshaw, and Sävje (2026), Section 2.3, Proposition 2.2, restated and proved in Appendix A.5, represent the single-known-model risk by its labeled conflict graph. The persistent nonlocal witness and the dense radius-one split-graph witness preserve the full multiset of mapping-wise labeled graphs but keep latent-menu risks \(1\) and \(5/4\); the latter establishes the separation inside a graph-local family, though not a bounded-degree or sparse-natural one. Gao, Harshaw, Sävje, and Wang (2026), Theorem 3.1 in Section 3.3, prove minimax testing error one for a null exposure model against a nontrivially separated nested alternative; they do not study squared-error estimation of the global effect and supply neither an attaining estimator nor a two-sided design frontier. Schröder, Oprescu, Feuerriegel, and Kallus (2026) derive sharp sensitivity and partial-identification bounds under exposure-mapping misspecification for experimental and observational data while taking assignment as fixed or externally given. Borusyak, Hull, and Munro (2026) jointly optimize design and recentered instrumental-variables analysis for one correctly specified linear spillover operator. Viviano (2026 revision) optimizes network design under known local interference and identifies alternative exposure mappings as an open design issue. Kandiros, Pipis, Daskalakis, and Harshaw (2024) construct a conflict-graph design for a prespecified effect under a known neighborhood model. Basse and Airoldi (2018) supply the arbitrary-interference negative benchmark, and Aronow and Samii (2017) supply the standard single-exposure-mapping response model.

Duanmu, Roy, and Schrittesser (2022) record the classical fact that, for statistical decision problems with finite parameter space, the upper or minimax value equals the lower or maximin value, and distinguish their extensions beyond that finite setting. This note does not claim finite minimax--maximin equality or the existence of a least-favourable prior as novel; novelty lies in the sign reduction for the causal latent-menu experiment, its explicit primal--dual implementation and recovery map, and the exposure-signature compression and local impossibility boundaries.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-schedule]\label{ass:fixed-schedule}
The table \(y\) is fixed before \(Z\sim\Theta\) is drawn.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Neyman finite-population design-based framework, \cite{basseairoldi2018limitations}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-table]\label{ass:bounded-table}
For every \(i\in V_n\) and \(z\in\mathcal A_n\), \(y_i(z)\in[-1,1]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniformly bounded potential outcomes, \cite{gaoharshawsavjewang2026impossibility}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:menu-measurability]\label{ass:menu-measurability}
There exist \(e\in\mathcal E\) and functions \(h_{i,e,n}:\mathcal X_{i,e,n}\to\mathbb R\) such that \(y_i(z)=h_{i,e,n}(e_{i,n}(z))\) for every \(i\in V_n\) and \(z\in\mathcal A_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A short menu of scientifically interpretable neighborhood, threshold, and weighted-exposure summaries is realistic when domain knowledge narrows spillover propagation without selecting one summary as correct.
\end{assumption}

\end{definition}

\end{definition}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), draw \(Z\sim\Theta\), then reveal \(\mathsf O_n=(Z,(y_i(Z))_{i\in V_n})\) once.

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:minimax-risk]\label{def:minimax-risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{design-\allowbreak{}inclusive ambiguity risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), and finite nonempty \(\mathcal E\),
\[
V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)
=\inf_{\Theta\in\mathcal D_n(\bar p)}
 \inf_{\widehat\theta:\Omega_n\times[-1,1]^n\to\mathbb R}
 \sup_{y\in\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E)}
 \left\{\mathbb E_{Z\sim\Theta}
 \left[(\widehat\theta(\mathsf O_n)-\theta_n(y))^2\right]\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
Holding \((n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\) fixed, this functional is invariant to the choice of the ambient known graph \(G_n\): the graph does not enter the outcome class, exact-budget design class, observation, loss, or unrestricted decision-rule domain.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}ambiguity-\allowbreak{}class}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}design-\allowbreak{}class}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}observed-\allowbreak{}experiment}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:sign-schedules]\label{def:sign-schedules}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{finite sign-\allowbreak{}schedule experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), \(\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)=\{\alpha=(e,b):e\in\mathcal E,\ b=(b_{i,x})_{i\in V_n,x\in\mathcal X_{i,e,n}}\in\{-1,1\}^{d_{e,n}}\}\); for \(\alpha=(e,b)\), set \(t_\alpha=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in V_n}[b_{i,e_{i,n}(\mathbf 1_n)}-b_{i,e_{i,n}(\mathbf 0_n)}]\) and \(s_{\alpha z}=(b_{i,e_{i,n}(z)})_{i\in V_n}\in\mathcal S_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal E}; \texttt{e\_\allowbreak{}\{i,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathbf 0\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathbf 1\_\allowbreak{}n}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:collision-moduli]\label{def:collision-moduli}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{design-\allowbreak{}weighted collision geometry}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), and for every design vector \(\pi\) on \(\Omega_n\), define \(C_n(\pi)=\max_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)}|t_\alpha-t_\beta|\{\sum_{z\in\Omega_n:s_{\alpha z}=s_{\beta z}}\pi_z\}^{1/2}\). For every observation cell set \(d_n(z,s)=\max_{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s}t_\alpha-\min_{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s}t_\alpha\), with diameter zero for a singleton fiber, and define \(U_n(\pi)=\{\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)}\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_z d_n(z,s_{\alpha z})^2\}^{1/2}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}sign-\allowbreak{}schedules}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:finite-primal]\label{def:finite-primal}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{finite primal risk program}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), \(\mathsf P_n\) has complete primitive data \((n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\) through \(\Omega_n\), \(\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\), \(s_{\alpha z}\), and \(t_\alpha\). It minimizes \(r\) over \(\pi_z\ge0\), \(w_{zs}\in\mathbb R\), and \(q_{\alpha z}\ge0\), subject to \(\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_z=1\), \(-2\pi_z\le w_{zs}\le2\pi_z\), \(\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}q_{\alpha z}\le r\) for every \(\alpha\in\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\), and the rotated-cone inequalities \(\pi_zq_{\alpha z}\ge(w_{z,s_{\alpha z}}-\pi_zt_\alpha)^2\) for every \((\alpha,z)\). The closed perspective convention forces \(w_{zs}=0\) when \(\pi_z=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}sign-\allowbreak{}schedules}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:finite-dual]\label{def:finite-dual}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{least-\allowbreak{}favourable-\allowbreak{}prior dual program}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), \(\mathsf Q_n\) has complete primitive data \((n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\) through \(\Omega_n\), \(\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\), \(\mathcal S_n\), \(s_{\alpha z}\), and \(t_\alpha\). It maximizes \(v\) over \(\mu_\alpha\ge0\), \(a_{zs}\ge0\), \(b_{zs}\in\mathbb R\), \(c_{zs}\ge0\), and \(u_{zs}\ge0\), subject to \(\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha=1\), \(a_{zs}=\sum_{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s}\mu_\alpha\), \(b_{zs}=\sum_{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s}\mu_\alpha t_\alpha\), \(c_{zs}=\sum_{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s}\mu_\alpha t_\alpha^2\), \(a_{zs}u_{zs}\ge b_{zs}^2\), and \(v\le\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}(c_{zs}-u_{zs})\) for every \(z\in\Omega_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}sign-\allowbreak{}schedules}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:multilinear-extension]\label{def:multilinear-extension}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{lossless estimator extension}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), for \(z\in\Omega_n\) and \(x\in[-1,1]^n\), define \(\delta^{\mathrm{ml}}(z,x)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s)\prod_{i\in V_n}(1+s_ix_i)/2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}delta\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{bin\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:primal-recovery]\label{def:primal-recovery}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{design and estimator recovery}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), from an optimal primal solution set \(\Theta_n^\star(z)=\pi_z\), set \(\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s)=w_{zs}/\pi_z\) when \(\pi_z>0\) and zero when \(\pi_z=0\), and set \(\widehat\theta_n^\star=\delta^{\mathrm{ml}}\) using \(\mathrm{def{:}multilinear\text{-}extension}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}finite-\allowbreak{}primal}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}multilinear-\allowbreak{}extension}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[lem:sign-reduction]\label{lem:sign-reduction}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). The minimax mean squared error over \(\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E)\) equals the minimax mean squared error over the finite sign schedules \(\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\). More precisely, every rule \(\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}\) on \(\Omega_n\times\mathcal S_n\) has the multilinear extension in \(\mathrm{def{:}multilinear\text{-}extension}\), and for every \(e\in\mathcal E\) and every bounded response array \(h\), its risk is at most the largest risk of \(\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}\) over sign arrays for that same \(e\). Conversely, sign schedules form a subclass of \(\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a design \(\Theta\), and write \(\pi_z=\Theta(Z=z)\).  Projection of any estimate onto \([-2,2]\) cannot increase squared error because every target \(\theta_n(y)\) lies in \([-2,2]\).  It is therefore enough to consider rules with values in \([-2,2]\).

Fix \(e\in\mathcal E\) and a bounded response array \(h=(h_{i,e,n}(x))\).  For every response coordinate \((i,x)\), independently draw a sign \(B_{i,x}\in\{-1,1\}\) with
\[
 \mathbb P(B_{i,x}=1)=\frac{1+h_{i,e,n}(x)}2,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E B_{i,x}=h_{i,e,n}(x).
\]
This is well-defined by \(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}table}\).  The random pair \((e,B)\) is a sign schedule in \(\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\).  For fixed \(z\in\Omega_n\), independence across the unit-indexed response coordinates gives
\[
 \mathbb E_B\!\left[\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s_{(e,B)z})\right]
 =\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s)
   \prod_{i\in V_n}\frac{1+s_i h_{i,e,n}(e_{i,n}(z))}{2}
 =\delta^{\mathrm{ml}}\bigl(z,(h_{i,e,n}(e_{i,n}(z)))_{i\in V_n}\bigr),
\]
where the last equality is \(\mathrm{def{:}multilinear\text{-}extension}\).  Linearity of the endpoint contrast similarly gives
\[
 \mathbb E_B t_{(e,B)}
 =\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}
   \left[h_{i,e,n}(e_{i,n}(\mathbf 1_n))-h_{i,e,n}(e_{i,n}(\mathbf 0_n))\right]
 =\theta_n(y).
\]
The last equality uses \(\mathrm{ass{:}menu\text{-}measurability}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}ambiguity\text{-}class}\).  Jensen's inequality for the square, applied separately at every \(z\), now yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left\{\delta^{\mathrm{ml}}(z,Y^{\mathrm{obs}}(z))-\theta_n(y)\right\}^2\\
 &\quad=\left\{\mathbb E_B[\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s_{(e,B)z})-t_{(e,B)}]\right\}^2\\
 &\quad\le \mathbb E_B\left[\{\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s_{(e,B)z})-t_{(e,B)}\}^2\right].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying by \(\pi_z\), summing over \(z\), and interchanging two finite sums proves
\[
 \mathbb E_{Z\sim\Theta}\!\left[
   \{\delta^{\mathrm{ml}}(\mathsf O_n)-\theta_n(y)\}^2\right]
 \le
 \max_{b\in\{-1,1\}^{d_{e,n}}}
 \sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_z
   \{\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s_{(e,b)z})-t_{(e,b)}\}^2.
\]
This proves the asserted upper reduction for each candidate \(e\), hence for their finite union.  Conversely, every sign array is a bounded fixed response array measurable with respect to its displayed candidate mapping, so it belongs to \(\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E)\) by the three assumptions defining that class.  Restricting an arbitrary real-outcome rule to sign observations therefore gives the reverse minimax inequality.  Taking the infimum over rules, and also over designs when desired, proves equality of the two minimax mean squared errors.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the value-preserving elimination theorem that converts arbitrary real response arrays and arbitrary measurable estimators into a certified finite decision problem. \textit{Gap.} Kandiros, Harshaw, and Sävje (2026), Section 2.3, Proposition 2.2; restated and proved in Appendix A.5, show that for an interference network \(G\), a fixed contrastive effect \(\tau\), and \(q\ge2\), there exists a function \(f_q\) on labeled graphs such that \(R(G,\tau,q)=f_q(H)\) for the single known model's conflict graph \(H\); they do not reduce a latent finite union, cross-model collisions, or the present exact-treated-count slice. \textit{Consumer.} The finite reduction permits exact numerical certification without imposing linearity, unbiasedness, graph locality, or finitely valued observed outcomes.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-saddle]\label{thm:finite-saddle}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). The complete primitive data of \(\mathsf P_n\) and \(\mathsf Q_n\) are \((n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\), and the two programs attain equal optimal objectives. With \(L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)\) equal to the square root of this common objective, one has
\[
 V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)=L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)
 =\sqrt{\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)}
 =\sqrt{\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n)}.
\]
The recovery in \(\mathrm{def{:}primal\text{-}recovery}\) yields \(\Theta_n^\star\in\mathcal D_n(\bar p)\) and an arbitrary-outcome estimator \(\widehat\theta_n^\star\) satisfying
\[
 \sup_{y\in\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E)}
 \operatorname{RMSE}_n(\Theta_n^\star,\widehat\theta_n^\star;y)
 =L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p).
\]
Every \(\Theta\in\mathcal D_n(\bar p)\) and every measurable \(\widehat\theta\) have worst-case root mean squared error at least \(L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)\). Holding \((n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\) fixed, the outcome model, exact-budget design class, finite decision experiment, programs, set of recovered optimal design--rule pairs, and exact minimax value are unchanged when the ambient known graph \(G_n\) changes. In particular, the same optimal primal solution recovers the same pair. Allowing a rule to read a fixed known \(G_n\) does not change the unrestricted decision-rule class, because that fixed constant can be compiled into the rule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(A=\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\). It is finite. Since \(t_\alpha\in[-2,2]\), projection of an estimate onto \([-2,2]\) cannot increase square loss. For \(z\in\Omega_n\) and \(d=(d_s)_{s\in\mathcal S_n}\in[-2,2]^{\mathcal S_n}\), define
\[
 \ell(z,d)_\alpha=(d_{s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2,
 \qquad \alpha\in A,
\]
and let \(K\subset\mathbb R^A\) be the convex hull of these loss vectors. Finiteness of \(A,\Omega_n,\mathcal S_n\) and compactness of the rule cube make \(K\) compact and convex. Randomization over rules conditional on \(z\) can be replaced by the conditional mean rule, because the square is convex coordinatewise. Conversely, a design \(\pi\) and assignment-specific rules give a distribution over \((z,d)\). Thus the sign-experiment minimax mean squared error is
\[
 R_n=\min_{x\in K}\max_{\alpha\in A}x_\alpha. \tag{1}
\]

We prove the required finite minimax equality rather than assume it. Put \(\rho=R_n\). For \(\varepsilon>0\), the compact convex set \(K\) and the closed convex lower orthant
\[
 D_\varepsilon=\{q:q_\alpha\le\rho-\varepsilon\text{ for every }\alpha\}
\]
are disjoint. Let \((x_\varepsilon,q_\varepsilon)\) be a closest pair and put \(h_\varepsilon=x_\varepsilon-q_\varepsilon\). The first-order inequalities for squared distance along line segments are
\[
 h_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}(x-x_\varepsilon)\ge0\quad(x\in K),
 \qquad
 h_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}(q-q_\varepsilon)\le0\quad(q\in D_\varepsilon). \tag{2}
\]
The negative coordinate rays of \(D_\varepsilon\) imply \(h_{\varepsilon,\alpha}\ge0\); whenever this coordinate is positive, the upper face constraint is active, so \(q_{\varepsilon,\alpha}=\rho-\varepsilon\). Normalizing \(h_\varepsilon\) gives a prior \(\mu^\varepsilon\) with
\[
 \min_{x\in K}\sum_\alpha\mu^\varepsilon_\alpha x_\alpha
 \ge \rho-\varepsilon. \tag{3}
\]
A convergent subsequence as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), compactness of the prior simplex, and continuity yield a prior attaining at least \(\rho\). The reverse inequality follows from \(\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha x_\alpha\le\max_\alpha x_\alpha\). Hence
\[
 R_n=\max_{\mu\in\Delta(A)}
 \min_{z\in\Omega_n,\ d\in[-2,2]^{\mathcal S_n}}
 \sum_{\alpha\in A}\mu_\alpha(d_{s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2. \tag{4}
\]

Fix \((\mu,z)\). In cell \((z,s)\), the objective is
\[
 a_{zs}d_s^2-2b_{zs}d_s+c_{zs}. \tag{5}
\]
If \(a_{zs}>0\), its minimizer is \(b_{zs}/a_{zs}\in[-2,2]\), with minimum \(c_{zs}-b_{zs}^2/a_{zs}\). If \(a_{zs}=0\), the defining nonnegative sums give \(b_{zs}=c_{zs}=0\). Therefore, with \(0/0=0\),
\[
 R_n=\max_{\mu\in\Delta(A)}\min_{z\in\Omega_n}
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}
 \left(c_{zs}-\frac{b_{zs}^2}{a_{zs}}\right). \tag{6}
\]
The rotated-cone constraints in \(\mathsf Q_n\) encode (6) exactly: maximization chooses \(u_{zs}=b_{zs}^2/a_{zs}\) for positive \(a_{zs}\) and zero otherwise. Since \(|b_{zs}|\le2a_{zs}\), the convention is continuous at zero. Compactness of the prior simplex and finiteness of the cell sets prove dual attainment and \(\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n)=R_n\).

For the primal, any feasible \((\pi,w,q,r)\) gives \(d_{zs}=w_{zs}/\pi_z\in[-2,2]\) when \(\pi_z>0\), and
\[
 q_{\alpha z}\ge\pi_z(d_{z,s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2. \tag{7}
\]
When \(\pi_z=0\), the closed perspective forces \(w_{zs}=0\) and the cell contributes no design loss. Conversely, any \((\pi,d)\) yields a feasible point by
\[
 w_{zs}=\pi_zd_{zs},\qquad
 q_{\alpha z}=\pi_z(d_{z,s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2,
 \qquad
 r=\max_\alpha\sum_zq_{\alpha z}. \tag{8}
\]
Equations (1), (7), and (8) give
\[
 \operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)=R_n=\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n). \tag{9}
\]
The compact minimizer in (1), conditional-mean derandomization, and (8) prove primal attainment.

The recovered \(\pi\) is a probability vector on the nonempty \(\Omega_n\), hence defines \(\Theta_n^\star\in\mathcal D_n(\bar p)\). The recovered sign rule has worst-case mean squared error \(R_n\). By \(\mathrm{lem{:}sign\text{-}reduction}\), its multilinear extension has risk at most \(R_n\) on all bounded tables, and the sign schedules, being a subclass, give the reverse inequality. Thus
\[
 V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)=\sqrt{R_n}
 =L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p),
\]
the recovered pair attains the value, and the definition of the infimum gives the asserted converse for every individual design and rule.

Finally, inspect the primitive data. Once \((n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\) is fixed, \(\Omega_n\), \(\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E)\), \(A\), \(t_\alpha\), \(s_{\alpha z}\), both programs, and the recovery formulas contain no \(G_n\). For a fixed graph \(g\), a rule allowed to read it has the form \((z,x)\mapsto f(z,x,g)\); defining \(f_g(z,x)=f(z,x,g)\) shows it is an ordinary measurable map on \(\Omega_n\times[-1,1]^n\). Conversely every ordinary rule is allowed. Hence the rule class is identical for every fixed ambient graph. The same optimal program solution may therefore be used after changing \(G_n\), proving all invariance claims. The only boundary condition used is \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), which makes \(\Omega_n\) finite and nonempty; no assignment-level positivity, rank, locality, or further boundary condition is required. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem turns the causal design problem into an exact computable saddle and recovers both an optimal exact-budget assignment law and a lossless estimator for arbitrary bounded outcomes. Relative to accepted-bank \texttt{exp\_snipe\_degree\_frontier\_v1/thm:bounded-outcome-degree-frontier}, which treats one known \(\beta\)-order neighborhood-interference model under unit Bernoulli assignment and clipped SNIPE, the finite-saddle theorem handles a latent finite menu of arbitrary bounded exposure-measurable tables, optimizes over all exact-treated-count assignment laws, and permits arbitrary measurable decision rules. It is also distinct from accepted-bank exp\_design\_pm1\_exactness\_boundary\_v1\_experimentation/thm:sharp-rho-star: that result asks whether a balanced plus-minus-one law implements a two-block covariance relaxation of a fixed HT-GATE worst-case-MSE objective, whereas this theorem solves the unrestricted estimator-and-design minimax problem for a latent exposure menu. \textit{Gap.} Classical finite minimax--maximin equality is substrate. The closest accepted-bank SNIPE theorem solves a known-graph, single-known-model Bernoulli frontier attained by clipped SNIPE; it does not cover latent finite-menu ambiguity, exact-count design choice, arbitrary bounded tables, or arbitrary measurable rules. The paper-specific advance is the lossless causal sign reduction, explicit perspective programs, exact-budget recovery, and universal converse for that broader experiment. The accepted-bank exp\_design\_pm1\_exactness\_boundary\_v1\_experimentation/thm:sharp-rho-star instead certifies the covariance-implementability loss of balanced plus-minus-one designs for a two-block HT-GATE objective; because that covariance objective neither ranges over arbitrary estimators nor encodes latent-menu observation collisions, it does not subsume the present frontier. \textit{Consumer.} A downstream analyst can enumerate the menu-induced finite experiment, solve \(\mathsf P_n\) or \(\mathsf Q_n\), and recover a design--rule pair attaining \(L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)\). \begin{theorem}[thm:minimal-nonrepresentability]\label{thm:minimal-nonrepresentability}
Fix \(\bar p\in[1/2,1)\), so \(m_2=\lfloor2\bar p\rfloor=1\). The construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}mappings}\) is minimal: \(n=2\) and \(m_2=1\) are the smallest admissible population and budget because no integer satisfies \(1\le m\le n-1\) when \(n=1\). Both menus have cardinality two, every unit has distinct endpoint exposures under every mapping, and \(\{H_2(e):e\in\mathcal E_-\}\) and \(\{H_2(e):e\in\mathcal E_+\}\) are identical as multisets of labeled graphs under the explicit identity isomorphisms in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}graphs}\). Nevertheless, the exact ambiguity minimax risks are \(V_2^\star(\mathcal E_-,\bar p)=1\) and \(V_2^\star(\mathcal E_+,\bar p)=5/4\). The two fully tabulated colliding arrays give the first matching lower certificate, the eight-point least-favourable prior gives the second, and the explicit designs and estimators in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}estimators}\) give the matching upper certificates. Hence derived mapping-wise conflict graphs alone do not represent latent-menu risk on the exact-budget slice; this is not a claim of primitive dependence on the ambient graph.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
For \(\bar p\in[1/2,1)\), \(m_2=1\) and \(\Omega_2=\{10,01\}\). No integer budget is nondegenerate at \(n=1\), proving minimality. The endpoint rows \(00\) and \(11\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}mappings}\) give exposure zero and one for every unit. With \(a=(1,0),b=(1,1),c=(2,0),d=(2,1)\), the feasible endpoint-vertex sets are \(\{a,c\},\{a,d\}\) for \(e^{(0)}\), and \(\{a,c\},\{b,c\}\) in opposite orders for \(e^{(-)}\) and \(e^{(+)}\). Their complements give exactly the edge sets in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}graphs}\), so the asserted labeled identity isomorphisms hold.

For \(\mathcal E_-\), schedules \(A\) and \(B\) from \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\) produce the same outcome \((-1,1)\) at both feasible assignments and have targets \(1\) and \(-1\). For any rule values \(D_z\) and any design \(\pi\),
\[
 \sum_z\pi_z\{(D_z-1)^2+(D_z+1)^2\}
 =2\sum_z\pi_zD_z^2+2\ge2,
\]
so the worst mean squared error is at least one. Put all mass on \(10\). For either lower-menu mapping, write the bounded response coordinates as \((a,b,c,d)\). The target is \(t=(b-a+d-c)/2\), whereas \(\delta_-(10,(a,c))=-(a+c)/2\), and the error is \(-(b+d)/2\), of absolute value at most one. Hence
\[
 V_2^\star(\mathcal E_-,\bar p)=1. \tag{1}
\]

For \(\mathcal E_+\), substitute the eight atoms and masses from \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\) into the two exposure tables. Multiplying cell mass, first moment, and second moment by \(224\) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
z&s&(224a_{zs},224b_{zs},224c_{zs})\\ \hline
10&(-1,1)&(112,-84,238)\\
10&(1,-1)&(112,84,238)\\
01&(-1,-1)&(72,54,198)\\
01&(1,1)&(72,-54,198)\\
01&(-1,1)&(40,30,40)\\
01&(1,-1)&(40,-30,40).
\end{array} \tag{2}
\]
Thus the posterior means are \(-3s_2/4\) at \(10\) and \(-3s_1/4\) at \(01\). The posterior-variance contributions are \(25/32,25/32\) at \(10\) and \(45/64,45/64,5/64,5/64\) at \(01\); they sum to \(25/16\) at either assignment. The posterior square-loss identity therefore gives mean squared error at least \(25/16\) for every rule under every design.

Conversely, under the uniform design use \(\delta_+(10,x)=-3x_2/4\) and \(\delta_+(01,x)=-3x_1/4\). For either mapping its risk at a sign vector \((a,b,c,d)\) is
\[
 \frac12\left(-\frac34c-t\right)^2
 +\frac12\left(-\frac34a-t\right)^2,
 \qquad t=\frac{b-a+d-c}{2}. \tag{3}
\]
Direct substitution over the sixteen sign vectors gives only \(1/16,9/16,25/16\). Expression (3) is separately convex in each coordinate, so successive endpoint maximization extends the same bound to \([-1,1]^4\). Hence
\[
 V_2^\star(\mathcal E_+,\bar p)=\frac54. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1)--(4), together with \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), prove the exact risks and certificates. Since the derived mapping-wise graph multisets agree while the risks differ, those graphs do not represent the latent-menu frontier. No ambient graph enters either risk calculation. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:persistent-nonrepresentability]\label{thm:persistent-nonrepresentability}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\). For every \(B\ge1\) such that \(n=2B\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), put \(m_{2B}=\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor\), so \(1\le m_{2B}\le2B-1\). The two menus in \(\mathrm{def{:}persistent\text{-}witness}\) have cardinality two, every unit retains distinct endpoint exposures, and \(\rho_B(\Omega_{2B})=\{10,01\}\). For each \(\kappa\in\{0,-,+\}\), \(H_{2B}(\widetilde e^\kappa)\) is the labeled type blow-up of \(H_2(e^{(\kappa)})\): vertices are \(((j,a),k)\), and two such vertices are adjacent exactly when no \(q\in\{10,01\}\) simultaneously realizes their two base endpoint exposures under \(e^{(\kappa)}\). In particular, the feasible endpoint-exposure vector sets of \(\widetilde e^-\) and \(\widetilde e^+\) coincide, and the identity map \(((j,a),k)\mapsto((j,a),k)\) is an explicit labeled graph isomorphism; the same identity matches the shared \(\widetilde e^0\) graphs. Thus the two mapping-wise labeled graph multisets agree. Their mapping-known oracle benchmarks coincide:
\[
 \max_{\kappa\in\{0,-\}}V_{2B}^\star(\{\widetilde e^\kappa\},\bar p)
 =\max_{\kappa\in\{0,+\}}V_{2B}^\star(\{\widetilde e^\kappa\},\bar p)
 =\frac56.
\]
Nevertheless,
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-},\bar p)=1,
 \qquad
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+},\bar p)=\frac54.
\]
The corresponding additive adaptation prices are \(1/6\) and \(5/12\), and the robust-to-oracle risk ratios are \(6/5\) and \(3/2\). The lower certificates are uniform over every design in \(\mathcal D_{2B}(\bar p)\) and every measurable estimator, while explicit budget-feasible designs and block-averaged estimators attain all singleton and robust values for arbitrary block-specific bounded response coordinates. Consequently, for every fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), every sufficiently large even population size belongs to \(\mathcal N_{\bar p}\); identical derived mapping-wise labeled conflict-graph multisets then conceal both a persistent robust-risk gap \(1/4\) and a persistent additive-adaptation-price gap \(1/4\). The separation concerns those menu-derived graphs, not primitive ambient-graph dependence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(m=\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor\). Admissibility gives \(1\le m\le2B-1\). Treating the distinguished unit and any \(m-1\) others realizes selector cell \(10\); leaving it untreated and treating any \(m\) remaining units realizes \(01\). Every feasible assignment is a nonendpoint, so
\[
 \rho_B(\Omega_{2B})=\{10,01\}. \tag{1}
\]
The selector maps the two endpoints to \(00\) and \(11\), and the base endpoint exposures are distinct, so every replica retains this property.

A replicated endpoint vertex \(((j,a),k)\) is realized by \(z\) precisely when \(e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}(\rho_B(z))\) equals its base endpoint exposure. By (1), two replicated vertices are jointly realizable exactly when some \(q\in\{10,01\}\) realizes both base exposures. Negation gives the asserted blow-up adjacency rule. The base cells for \(e^{(-)}\) and \(e^{(+)}\) realize the same two endpoint-vertex sets in opposite order, so the identity is a labeled isomorphism; the \(\widetilde e^0\) graphs are literally shared.

Replicate schedules \(A\) and \(B\) from the minimal witness in every block. They agree on every feasible assignment and have normalized targets \(1\) and \(-1\). Hence for every rule value \(D_Z\),
\[
 \mathbb E[(D_Z-1)^2+(D_Z+1)^2]=2\mathbb E[D_Z^2]+2\ge2,
\]
so \(V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-},\bar p)\ge1\). Put all design mass on a selector-\(10\) assignment, apply \(\delta_-\) blockwise, and average. In block \(j\), the error is \(-(b_j+d_j)/2\), whose absolute value is at most one; the average error is therefore bounded by one. Thus
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-},\bar p)=1. \tag{2}
\]

For the higher menu, draw one atom from the eight-point least-favourable prior and replicate it in every block. Conditional on any physical assignment, the observed vector is \(B\) copies of the base observation at \(\rho_B(z)\), while the normalized target is the base target. The posterior calculation in \(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\) gives Bayes mean squared error \(25/16\) at either selector cell, hence under every physical design. Conversely, put mass one half on one assignment from each selector cell, apply \(\delta_+\) blockwise, and average. Pointwise,
\[
 \left\{B^{-1}\sum_j(\widehat t_j-t_j)\right\}^2
 \le B^{-1}\sum_j(\widehat t_j-t_j)^2.
\]
Each expected block square is at most \(25/16\), even for block-specific bounded coordinates. Therefore
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+},\bar p)=\frac54. \tag{3}
\]

By \(\mathrm{prop{:}singleton\text{-}witness\text{-}oracle\text{-}values}\), both mapping-known benchmarks equal \(5/6\). Subtracting and dividing, which is valid because \(5/6>0\), yields
\[
 1-\frac56=\frac16,
 \quad \frac54-\frac56=\frac5{12},
 \quad \frac1{5/6}=\frac65,
 \quad \frac{5/4}{5/6}=\frac32.
\]
Both displayed gaps equal \(1/4\). Finally, for all sufficiently large \(B\), \(1\le\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor\le2B-1\), so the construction persists along every fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\). The risk calculations depend on the menus and budget slice, not on the empty ambient graph used to present the witness. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The all-budget blow-up preserves the exact singleton benchmark and all mapping-wise graph data while separating robust risk and adaptation price by one quarter. \textit{Gap.} Candidate-wise graph geometry and oracle risk do not determine the latent-menu adaptation price. \textit{Consumer.} A robust method must encode cross-model collision alignment at growing population sizes.

\begin{proposition}[prop:local-program-solution]\label{prop:local-program-solution}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). For the graph-local two-mapping menu \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}}\), the finite primal and dual solve to \(L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}},\bar p)=1/n\). The estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}local\text{-}solved\text{-}menu}\) is optimal under every exact-budget design. A dual certificate places independent symmetric sign mass on the two response coordinates of unit \(1\), so whichever value is observed, the other contributes posterior target variance \(1/n^2\). The adjective graph-local describes how this menu was selected; the exact value is a functional of the resulting menu and budget.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Under \(e^{\mathrm{loc}}\), write \(a\) and \(b\) for unit \(1\)'s responses at exposures zero and one. Every other endpoint contrast vanishes, and the constant mapping is a subclass, so every table in the union has
\[
 \theta_n(y)=\frac{b-a}{n}. \tag{1}
\]
If \(Z_1=1\), the displayed estimator is \(b/n\) and its error is \(a/n\); if \(Z_1=0\), it is \(-a/n\) and its error is \(-b/n\). Thus its error is pointwise bounded by \(1/n\) under every exact-budget design.

For the lower bound, take \(a,b\) independent symmetric signs and fix all other coordinates. Given any assignment and full observation, exactly one of \(a,b\) remains an independent unseen sign. By (1),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\theta_n(y)\mid Z,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})=n^{-2}.
\]
The posterior square-loss identity gives Bayes mean squared error at least \(n^{-2}\) for every design and rule; worst-case risk dominates this finite prior average. Therefore, by \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\),
\[
 L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}},\bar p)
 =V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}},\bar p)=\frac1n.
\]
No assignment-level positivity is needed. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This menu-generated local family has exact frontier one over n and supplies a nonempty remediable regime. \textit{Gap.} The exact value is a property of the resulting menu, not a direct ambient-graph functional. \textit{Consumer.} The family is a finite-program unit test and a calibration for vanishing risk.

\begin{proposition}[prop:nonlocal-program-solution]\label{prop:nonlocal-program-solution}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). For the genuinely nonlocal two-mapping menu \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}}\), the finite primal and dual solve to \(L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1\) for every \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). The estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}nonlocal\text{-}solved\text{-}menu}\) is optimal under every exact-budget design. Two schedules with identical zero outcomes on \(\Omega_n\) and all-treated response vectors \(\mathbf 1_n\) and \(-\mathbf 1_n\) give the dual lower certificate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Admissibility gives \(1\le m_n\le n-1\), so no feasible assignment is \(\mathbf 1_n\). Under \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), write \(a_i=h_i(0)\) and \(b_i=h_i(1)\). Every feasible observation is \((a_i)_i\), while
\[
 \theta_n(y)=n^{-1}\sum_i(b_i-a_i). \tag{1}
\]
The constant model is contained in this model. The displayed estimator has error \(-n^{-1}\sum_i b_i\), of absolute value at most one, under every design.

For a lower certificate, place equal mass on two fixed schedules having \(a_i=0\) for all \(i\), and respectively \(b_i=1\) and \(b_i=-1\) for all \(i\). Their feasible observations agree and their targets are \(1\) and \(-1\). For any rule value \(D_z\),

 \frac12(D_z-1)^2+\frac12(D_z+1)^2=D_z^2+1\ge1.
\]
Averaging under any design proves a Bayes and hence worst-case mean squared error lower bound of one. Thus
\[
 V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)
 =L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1
\]
by \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), and the displayed rule is optimal under every exact-budget design. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This menu has exact frontier one under every admissible exact-count design and supplies a nonempty design-inert regime. \textit{Gap.} Arbitrary-interference limitations do not give this exact latent-menu saddle with matching certificates. \textit{Consumer.} The family is a unit test for positive-risk dual certificates.

\begin{theorem}[thm:collision-geometry-sandwich]\label{thm:collision-geometry-sandwich}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). The primitive collision moduli satisfy
\[
 \frac12\inf_{\pi}C_n(\pi)
 \le V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)
 \le\frac12\inf_{\pi}U_n(\pi),
\]
where both infima range over the probability simplex on \(\Omega_n\). For the lower bound, on every assignment where schedules \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) collide, \((a-t_\alpha)^2+(a-t_\beta)^2\ge(t_\alpha-t_\beta)^2/2\), and the worse schedule has mean squared error at least one quarter of the squared collision modulus. For the upper bound, assign every sign observation cell the midpoint of its target interval, then apply the sign reduction and multilinear extension. Along \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\) with \(n\to\infty\), \(\inf_\pi U_n(\pi)\to0\) is sufficient for \(V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)\to0\), while \(V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)\to0\) implies \(\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)\to0\). A positive lower limit of \(\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)\) is sufficient for a positive lower limit of \(V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)\), while a positive lower limit of \(V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)\) implies a positive lower limit of \(\inf_\pi U_n(\pi)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a design vector \(\pi\) and an arbitrary rule. For schedules \(\alpha,\beta\), let \(E_{\alpha\beta}=\{z:s_{\alpha z}=s_{\beta z}\}\). On this event the two schedules produce the same observed experiment, so the rule takes a common value \(a_z\). The identity
\[
 (a_z-t_\alpha)^2+(a_z-t_\beta)^2
 =2\left(a_z-\frac{t_\alpha+t_\beta}{2}\right)^2
 +\frac{(t_\alpha-t_\beta)^2}{2}
 \ge\frac{(t_\alpha-t_\beta)^2}{2}
\]
shows, after weighting by \(\pi_z\) and summing, that the larger of the two full mean squared errors is at least
\[
 \frac{(t_\alpha-t_\beta)^2}{4}
 \sum_{z\in E_{\alpha\beta}}\pi_z.
\]
Sign schedules lie in \(\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E)\), so maximizing over pairs and infimizing over rules and designs gives the lower bound.

For the upper bound, for each nonempty fiber \(F_{zs}=\{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s\}\), define
\[
 \delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s)=\frac12
 \left(\max_{\alpha\in F_{zs}}t_\alpha+\min_{\alpha\in F_{zs}}t_\alpha\right).
\]
Finiteness makes the extrema well defined. For every schedule and assignment,
\[
 |\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s_{\alpha z})-t_\alpha|
 \le\frac12d_n(z,s_{\alpha z}),
\]
so its mean squared error is at most one quarter of the corresponding diameter sum. Maximizing over schedules gives root mean squared error at most \(U_n(\pi)/2\). The sign reduction and multilinear extension give an explicit arbitrary-outcome rule with no larger risk. Infimizing over \(\pi\) proves the upper bound.

Writing \(C_n^\circ=\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)\) and \(U_n^\circ=\inf_\pi U_n(\pi)\), the proved inequalities are exactly
\[
 C_n^\circ\le2V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)\le U_n^\circ.
\]
All four asymptotic implications follow immediately by taking limits or lower limits along the admissible sequence. These are one-sided certificates and do not assert a reverse comparison. Nonemptiness of \(\Omega_n\) is the only support condition; no assignment-level positivity, rank, locality, or additional boundary condition is used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The collision sandwich exposes directly evaluable pairwise lower certificates and observation-fiber midpoint upper certificates without solving the full saddle. \textit{Gap.} The two moduli need not agree in order: the no-interference boundary has optimized scales \(n^{-1}\) and two. \textit{Consumer.} Vanishing optimized fiber diameter certifies remediability, while a positive optimized collision modulus certifies design-inertness; the implications are sufficient and necessary on opposite sides, not an exhaustive equivalence.

\begin{proposition}[prop:no-interference-calibration]\label{prop:no-interference-calibration}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). For the singleton menu \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}}\), complete randomization and difference in means satisfy \(\sup_{y\in\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}})}\operatorname{RMSE}_n(\Theta_n^{\mathrm{CR}},\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{DM}};y)\le\{n(n-1)^{-1}[m_n^{-1}+(n-m_n)^{-1}]\}^{1/2}\). Along \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\) with \(n\to\infty\), the finite-program value obeys \(L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}},\bar p)\asymp n^{-1/2}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
For a no-interference table put \(a_i=y_i(\mathbf 0_n)\), \(b_i=y_i(\mathbf 1_n)\), and \(\tau_i=b_i-a_i\). The observed response is \(b_i\) when treated and \(a_i\) otherwise, and the target is \(\bar\tau=n^{-1}\sum_i\tau_i\). Under complete randomization, counting assignments containing one or two specified units gives
\[
 \mathbb EZ_i=\frac{m_n}{n},\quad
 \operatorname{Var}(Z_i)=\frac{m_n(n-m_n)}{n^2},\quad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_i,Z_j)=-\frac{m_n(n-m_n)}{n^2(n-1)}\quad(i\ne j). \tag{1}
\]
Consequently difference in means is unbiased and has Neyman variance
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{DM}})
 =\frac{S_b^2}{m_n}+\frac{S_a^2}{n-m_n}-\frac{S_\tau^2}{n}, \tag{2}
\]
where each \(S^2\) uses denominator \(n-1\). Boundedness in an interval of length two gives \(S_a^2,S_b^2\le n/(n-1)\). Dropping the nonpositive final term in (2) proves
\[
 \sup_y\operatorname{RMSE}_n(\Theta_n^{\mathrm{CR}},\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{DM}};y)
 \le\left\{\frac{n}{n-1}
 \left(\frac1{m_n}+\frac1{n-m_n}\right)\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{3}
\]

For a design-uniform lower bound, place independent symmetric sign mass on the \(2n\) fixed endpoint responses. Conditional on any exact-budget assignment and all observed outcomes, one endpoint response per unit remains unobserved and independent. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\bar\tau\mid Z,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})=\frac1n. \tag{4}
\]
The posterior square-loss identity makes (4) a Bayes mean squared error lower bound for every design and rule. By \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n}\le L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}},\bar p)
 \le\left\{\frac{n}{n-1}
 \left(\frac1{m_n}+\frac1{n-m_n}\right)\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{5}
\]
Since \(m_n/n\to\bar p\in(0,1)\), (5) is the claimed \(n^{-1/2}\) order. Both denominators are positive by admissibility; the prior is only a minimax certificate over fixed schedules, not superpopulation randomness. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The singleton menu recovers the classical complete-randomization order under the corrected frontier index. \textit{Gap.} This is a calibration rather than a novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} The result independently checks the primal, dual, and recovered estimator.

\begin{proposition}[prop:singleton-witness-oracle-values]\label{prop:singleton-witness-oracle-values}
Fix \(\bar p\in[1/2,1)\), so \(2\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). For every \(\kappa\in\{0,-,+\}\),
\[
 V_2^\star(\{e^{(\kappa)}\},\bar p)=\frac56.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \max_{\kappa\in\{0,-\}}V_2^\star(\{e^{(\kappa)}\},\bar p)
 =\max_{\kappa\in\{0,+\}}V_2^\star(\{e^{(\kappa)}\},\bar p)
 =\frac56.
\]
More generally, fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\). For every \(B\ge1\) such that \(n=2B\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), and every \(\kappa\in\{0,-,+\}\),
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\{\widetilde e^\kappa\},\bar p)=\frac56.
\]
Hence
\[
 \max_{\kappa\in\{0,-\}}V_{2B}^\star(\{\widetilde e^\kappa\},\bar p)
 =\max_{\kappa\in\{0,+\}}V_{2B}^\star(\{\widetilde e^\kappa\},\bar p)
 =\frac56.
\]
These identities hold for the full class of block-specific bounded response coordinates and arbitrary measurable estimators.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
For any one of the three two-unit mappings, write its four sign response coordinates, after a mapping-specific relabeling, as \((A,B,C,D)\). The relabeling may also reverse the two observed unit coordinates, which an estimator can undo. In all three cases the finite experiment is
\[
 t=\frac{B+D-A-C}{2},\qquad
 s_{10}=(A,C),\qquad s_{01}=(A,D). \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, for \(e^{(0)}\) this is the original coordinate order. For both \(e^{(-)}\) and \(e^{(+)}\), take \((A,B,C,D)=(c,d,a,b)\) and reverse observed-unit coordinates; for \(e^{(+)}\), also interchange the canonical names of assignments \(10\) and \(01\). The exposure tables in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}mappings}\) verify (1).

Use the uniform design on \(\{10,01\}\) and the sign rule
\[
 \delta_{10}(A,C)=-\frac A2-\frac{2C}{3},
 \qquad
 \delta_{01}(A,D)=-\frac A2+\frac{2D}{3}. \tag{2}
\]
Its two errors are
\[
 \delta_{10}-t=-\frac{B+D}{2}-\frac C6,
 \qquad
 \delta_{01}-t=\frac{C-B}{2}+\frac D6. \tag{3}
\]
For the eight triples \((B,C,D)\in\{-1,1\}^3\), the average of the two squared expressions in (3) is \(25/36\), except at \((-1,-1,1)\) and \((1,1,-1)\), where it is \(1/36\). Thus the worst sign-schedule mean squared error is \(25/36\). The multilinear extension and \(\mathrm{lem{:}sign\text{-}reduction}\) extend this upper bound to every bounded response array.

For the matching lower bound, fix \(A=1\) and put a prior on \((B,C,D)\) with mass \(5/36\) on each of
\[
 (-1,-1,-1),\quad(-1,1,1),\quad(1,-1,-1),\quad(1,1,1),
\]
mass \(2/9\) on each of \((-1,1,-1)\) and \((1,-1,1)\), and zero elsewhere. These six masses sum to one. Direct summation gives
\[
 \mathbb E B=\mathbb E C=\mathbb E D=0,
 \quad
 \mathbb E(BC)=-\frac49,
 \quad
 \mathbb E(BD)=\frac49,
 \quad
 \mathbb E(CD)=\frac19. \tag{4}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E(t\mid A,C)=-\frac A2-\frac{2C}{3},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(t\mid A,D)=-\frac A2+\frac{2D}{3}. \tag{5}
\]
Also, with \(R=(B+D-C)/2\), equations (4) give
\[
 \mathbb E R^2
 =\frac14\{3+2\mathbb E(BD)-2\mathbb E(BC)-2\mathbb E(CD)\}
 =\frac{41}{36}. \tag{6}
\]
The squared conditional means of \(R\) given either \(C\) or \(D\) equal \(4/9\). Hence the posterior target variance, averaged over the observation, is
\[
 \frac{41}{36}-\frac49=\frac{25}{36} \tag{7}
\]
under either assignment. The posterior square-loss identity makes (7) a Bayes mean squared error lower bound under every design and every measurable estimator. Combining it with the upper bound proves
\[
 V_2^\star(\{e^{(\kappa)}\},\bar p)=\frac56
 \qquad(\kappa\in\{0,-,+\}). \tag{8}
\]

Now let \(n=2B\) and use \(\mathrm{def{:}persistent\text{-}witness}\). Admissibility implies \(\rho_B(\Omega_{2B})=\{10,01\}\): an assignment with the distinguished unit treated realizes \(10\), and one with it untreated realizes \(01\). Put half the design mass on one physical assignment of each selector type. Apply (2), after the same mapping-specific relabeling, to every block and average the block estimates. If block \(j\) has target \(t_j\) and error \(E_j\), then
\[
 \left(B^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^B E_j\right)^2
 \le B^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^B E_j^2. \tag{9}
\]
The sign calculation above bounds the design expectation of every term on the right by \(25/36\), even when the bounded response coordinates differ by block; sign reduction then covers arbitrary bounded coordinates. This proves the upper bound \(5/6\).

For the lower bound, draw one of the six prior atoms above and replicate that same atom in every block. Every physical assignment reveals replicated copies of exactly one canonical observation in (1), while the normalized block-average target equals the canonical target. Thus (7) applies under every physical assignment and therefore under every exact-budget design. The Bayes lower bound is again \(25/36\), proving
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\{\widetilde e^\kappa\},\bar p)=\frac56.
\]
Taking the displayed maxima yields the two oracle-benchmark identities. All lower priors are supported on fixed bounded schedules, and the upper rules are measurable functions of the one realized assignment and outcome vector; no superpopulation sampling, assignment-level positivity, or locality condition is used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact five-sixths singleton denominator makes the robust gaps exact adaptation prices. \textit{Gap.} Known-model results do not compute these three witness risks uniformly over every admissible exact treated-count fraction. \textit{Consumer.} The persistent theorem uses these values for its additive and multiplicative comparisons.

\begin{proposition}[prop:nonlocal-collision-sharpness]\label{prop:nonlocal-collision-sharpness}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). For the menu \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}}\), every probability vector \(\pi\) on \(\Omega_n\) satisfies \(C_n(\pi)=U_n(\pi)=2\), and \(V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1\). Consequently equality holds in both sides of \(\mathrm{thm{:}collision\text{-}geometry\text{-}sandwich}\), so the universal factor \(1/2\) is sharp in both the lower and upper bounds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Every \(z\in\Omega_n\) differs from \(\mathbf 1_n\), because \(1\le m_n\le n-1\). Hence a sign schedule under \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\) is observed through its exposure-zero vector \(a=(a_i)_i\) at every feasible assignment, and its target is
\[
 t(a,b)=\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}(b_i-a_i), \tag{1}
\]
where \(b\) is its all-treated response vector. A sign schedule under \(e^{\mathrm c}\) has target zero.

Fix a feasible cell \((z,s)\). Among the \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\) schedules producing \(s\), take \(a=s\) and respectively \(b=\mathbf 1_n\) and \(b=-\mathbf 1_n\). Their target separation is two, so
\[
 d_n(z,s)\ge2. \tag{2}
\]
Conversely, the targets of all \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\) schedules in this fiber lie in
\[
 \left[-1-\frac1n\sum_i s_i, 1-\frac1n\sum_i s_i\right], \tag{3}
\]
an interval of length two containing zero. Adding any colliding constant-mapping schedule therefore does not enlarge the interval. Thus (2) is an equality for every feasible sign cell. It follows directly from the definition that \(U_n(\pi)=2\) for every probability vector \(\pi\).

The same two schedules collide at every feasible assignment and have target separation two, so \(C_n(\pi)\ge2\). Equation (3) shows that any two colliding schedules have target separation at most two, whence \(C_n(\pi)\le2\). Therefore \(C_n(\pi)=2\). Finally, \(\mathrm{prop{:}nonlocal\text{-}program\text{-}solution}\) gives \(V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1\). Substitution into
\[
 \frac12\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
 \le V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)
 \le\frac12\inf_\pi U_n(\pi)
\]
makes both inequalities equalities. Hence neither universal factor \(1/2\) can be increased or decreased, respectively. Nonemptiness of \(\Omega_n\) and exclusion of the all-treated endpoint are guaranteed by \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\); no further support condition is used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The nonlocal menu attains both sides of the collision sandwich and certifies both universal constants. \textit{Gap.} Candidate-wise graph bounds do not establish sharpness of the latent-menu fiber modulus. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition is an exact implementation test for both collision moduli.

\begin{theorem}[thm:orbit-fractional-cover-compression]\label{thm:orbit-fractional-cover-compression}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), and write \(\mathfrak A_n=\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\). Suppose a finite group \(\Gamma_n\) acts on \(\Omega_n\) and on \(\mathfrak A_n\) such that, for every \(g\in\Gamma_n\) and \(z\in\Omega_n\), there is a bijection \(\phi_{g,z}:\mathcal S_n\to\mathcal S_n\) satisfying
\[
 t_{g\alpha}=t_\alpha,
 \qquad
 s_{g\alpha,gz}=\phi_{g,z}(s_{\alpha z})
 \quad\text{for every }\alpha\in\mathfrak A_n.
\]
Let \(O_1,\ldots,O_K\) be the assignment orbits and \(P_1,\ldots,P_R\) the orbits of unordered pairs of sign schedules. Choose \(p_r=\{\alpha_r,\beta_r\}\in P_r\), put \(\Delta_r=|t_{\alpha_r}-t_{\beta_r}|\), and define
\[
 a_{rj}=\frac{1}{|O_j|}\sum_{z\in O_j}
 \mathbf 1\{s_{\alpha_r z}=s_{\beta_r z}\}.
\]
Assume that there is a fractional diameter cover of congestion \(\kappa_n\ge1\): for one representative \(\alpha_h\) of every \(\Gamma_n\)-orbit in \(\mathfrak A_n\), there are unordered pairs \(p_{h\ell}=\{\beta_{h\ell},\gamma_{h\ell}\}\) and weights \(q_{h\ell}\ge0\) such that \(\sum_\ell q_{h\ell}\le\kappa_n\) and, for every \(z\in\Omega_n\),
\[
 d_n(z,s_{\alpha_h z})^2
 \le
 \sum_\ell q_{h\ell}
 |t_{\beta_{h\ell}}-t_{\gamma_{h\ell}}|^2
 \mathbf 1\{s_{\beta_{h\ell}z}=s_{\gamma_{h\ell}z}\}.
\]
For other schedules use the cover obtained by applying the same group element to every pair in the representative cover. Define
\[
 \lambda_n=\min_{x_1,\ldots,x_K,\lambda}
 \left\{\lambda:
 x_j\ge0,\ \sum_{j=1}^Kx_j=1,
 \Delta_r^2\sum_{j=1}^K a_{rj}x_j\le\lambda
 \text{ for }r=1,\ldots,R\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)=\sqrt{\lambda_n},
 \qquad
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
 \le \inf_\pi U_n(\pi)
 \le \sqrt{\kappa_n}\inf_\pi C_n(\pi),
\]
and
\[
 \frac12\sqrt{\lambda_n}
 \le L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)
 \le \frac12\sqrt{\kappa_n\lambda_n}.
\]
An optimizer \(x^\star\) lifts to the explicit invariant design assigning mass \(x_j^\star/|O_j|\) to every \(z\in O_j\); the observation-fiber midpoint rule and its multilinear extension have worst-case root mean squared error at most \(\sqrt{\kappa_n\lambda_n}/2\). Thus, if \(\sup_n\kappa_n<\infty\), the optimized collision moduli match in order. If the orbit tables and fractional cover have polynomial encoding length, the displayed certificate has \(K+1\) variables, \(R\) loss inequalities, \(K\) nonnegativity inequalities, and one simplex equality, and is polynomial-size whenever \(K\) and \(R\) are polynomial in \(n\). The actions here are automorphisms of the menu-induced sign experiment, not necessarily automorphisms of the ambient graph \(G_n\). In graph-local subclasses, \(G_n\) may constrain or organize a menu \(\mathcal E(G_n)\) and certify such actions or incidence bounds, but its effect on the frontier is mediated through the resulting menu. This criterion is sufficient, not exhaustive: mapping-wise conflict-graph multisets fail on the unrestricted class, while the analogous natural graph-local separation remains undecided.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
For an unordered pair \(p=\{\alpha,\beta\}\), define its squared collision loss under a design \(\pi\) by
\[
 F_p(\pi)=|t_\alpha-t_\beta|^2
 \sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_z
 \mathbf 1\{s_{\alpha z}=s_{\beta z}\}. \tag{1}
\]
Then \(C_n(\pi)^2=\max_pF_p(\pi)\). The assumed target invariance and the bijectivity of \(\phi_{g,z}\) imply
\[
 F_{gp}(g\pi)=F_p(\pi), \tag{2}
\]
where \((g\pi)_{gz}=\pi_z\). Average any design over the finite group:
\[
 \bar\pi=|\Gamma_n|^{-1}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_n}g\pi. \tag{3}
\]
Because a maximum of linear functions is convex, (2) and (3) give
\[
 C_n(\bar\pi)^2
 \le |\Gamma_n|^{-1}\sum_g C_n(g\pi)^2
 =C_n(\pi)^2. \tag{4}
\]
Thus an optimizer may be taken invariant. Such a design is uniquely described by its orbit masses \(x_j\), assigning \(x_j/|O_j|\) to each member of \(O_j\). For the representative pair \(p_r\), equation (1) becomes
\[
 F_{p_r}(\pi)=\Delta_r^2\sum_{j=1}^K a_{rj}x_j. \tag{5}
\]
Equation (2) makes this value the same for every pair in \(P_r\). Minimizing the maximum of (5) over the simplex is exactly the displayed linear program. The simplex is compact and nonempty, so an optimizer exists, and
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)^2=\lambda_n. \tag{6}
\]

For every design, every pair \(p=\{\alpha,\beta\}\), and every assignment where the pair collides, the fiber containing \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) has diameter at least \(|t_\alpha-t_\beta|\). Consequently
\[
 \sum_z\pi_zd_n(z,s_{\alpha z})^2\ge F_p(\pi),
\]
and maximizing first over schedules and then over pairs gives
\[
 U_n(\pi)\ge C_n(\pi). \tag{7}
\]

Let \(\pi\) now be invariant. The equivariant cover, applied to any schedule \(\alpha\), and then summed against \(\pi\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_z\pi_zd_n(z,s_{\alpha z})^2
 &\le \sum_\ell q_{\alpha\ell}
 |t_{\beta_{\alpha\ell}}-t_{\gamma_{\alpha\ell}}|^2
 \sum_z\pi_z\mathbf 1\{s_{\beta_{\alpha\ell}z}=s_{\gamma_{\alpha\ell}z}\}\\
 &\le \left(\sum_\ell q_{\alpha\ell}\right)C_n(\pi)^2
 \le\kappa_n C_n(\pi)^2. \tag{8}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first inequality for an orbit translate is valid because the action carries fibers bijectively, preserves targets, and \(\pi\) is invariant. Maximizing (8) over \(\alpha\) yields \(U_n(\pi)\le\sqrt{\kappa_n}C_n(\pi)\). Apply this to the invariant optimizer from (6), and combine it with (7), to obtain
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
 \le\inf_\pi U_n(\pi)
 \le\sqrt{\kappa_n}\inf_\pi C_n(\pi). \tag{9}
\]

By \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), \(L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)=V_n^\star(\mathcal E,\bar p)\). Applying \(\mathrm{thm{:}collision\text{-}geometry\text{-}sandwich}\), (6), and (9) gives
\[
 \frac12\sqrt{\lambda_n}
 \le L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\kappa_n\lambda_n}. \tag{10}
\]
The optimizer \(x^\star\) produces exactly the invariant design stated in the theorem. The observation-fiber midpoint rule used in the sandwich theorem, followed by \(\mathrm{def{:}multilinear\text{-}extension}\), has worst-case root mean squared error at most \(U_n(\pi^\star)/2\), which is at most the right side of (10).

If \(\sup_n\kappa_n<\infty\), (9) is a size-uniform constant-factor comparison. The displayed linear program has the asserted \(K+1\) variables, \(R\) pair-orbit inequalities, \(K\) nonnegativity inequalities, and one simplex equality; hence polynomially bounded \(K,R\) and polynomially encoded coefficients and cover data give a polynomial-size certificate. The action was assumed only on the menu-induced sign experiment. Nothing in (1)--(10) requires it to arise from \(G_n\). If a graph-local construction uses \(G_n\) to specify \(\mathcal E(G_n)\) or certify the action and cover, its mathematical effect enters through those menu-experiment objects. Finally, \(\mathrm{thm{:}persistent\text{-}nonrepresentability}\) and \(\mathrm{prop{:}nonlocal\text{-}collision\text{-}sharpness}\) establish only the stated unrestricted limitations and sharpness; they do not turn this sufficient conditional criterion into an exhaustive characterization. No assignment-level positivity beyond the nonempty exact-budget support, rank condition, or unmentioned locality condition is used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem converts symmetry and an explicit fractional diameter cover into a finite linear certificate, invariant design, and midpoint estimator with a quantified approximation factor. \textit{Gap.} Polynomially many orbits alone do not bound cover congestion, so the cover hypothesis is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. \textit{Consumer.} When an analyst can encode the orbit tables and a low-congestion cover, the theorem gives the exact collision program and an explicit design and risk certificate.

\begin{lemma}[lem:joint-signature-fractional-compression]\label{lem:joint-signature-fractional-compression}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). For \(z\in\Omega_n\), define its joint exposure signature and the number of realized signatures by
\[
 \xi_n(z)=\bigl(e_{i,n}(z)\bigr)_{(e,i)\in\mathcal E\times V_n},
 \qquad
 R_n=\bigl|\{\xi_n(z):z\in\Omega_n\}\bigr|.
\]
Choose one representative \(z_q\) of each signature, where \(q\in[R_n]=\{1,\ldots,R_n\}\). For an unordered pair of sign schedules put
\[
 A(\alpha,\beta)=\{q\in[R_n]:s_{\alpha z_q}=s_{\beta z_q}\},
\]
and, for \(A\subseteq[R_n]\), let
\[
 D_A=\max\{|t_\alpha-t_\beta|:A(\alpha,\beta)=A\},
\]
with \(D_A=0\) if the displayed set is empty. Then the optimized squared collision modulus is the value of the explicit linear program
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)^2
 =\min_{x\in\mathbb R^{R_n},\lambda\in\mathbb R}
 \left\{\lambda:
 x_q\ge0,\ \sum_{q=1}^{R_n}x_q=1,\
 D_A^2\sum_{q\in A}x_q\le\lambda
 \text{ for every }A\subseteq[R_n]\right\}. \tag{1}
\]
Every optimizer in \((1)\) lifts to a design by assigning mass \(x_q\) to an arbitrary assignment with signature \(q\). The trivial action on the menu-induced sign experiment has an explicit fractional diameter cover of congestion at most \(R_n\). Consequently,
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
 \le \inf_\pi U_n(\pi)
 \le \sqrt{R_n}\inf_\pi C_n(\pi), \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 \frac12\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
 \le L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)
 \le\frac12\sqrt{R_n}\inf_\pi C_n(\pi). \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\sup_nR_n<\infty\) is a primitive exposure-incidence condition for a size-uniform reverse comparison. More generally, given the table of the exposures at \(z_1,\ldots,z_{R_n},\mathbf 0_n,\mathbf 1_n\), all coefficients \(D_A\) can be computed in \(O(|\mathcal E|^2n8^{R_n})\) arithmetic operations by a unitwise dynamic program. Hence \(R_n=O(\log n)\), fixed \(|\mathcal E|\), and polynomial encoding of that incidence table give a polynomial-size and polynomial-time certificate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
If \(\xi_n(z)=\xi_n(z')\), then for every sign schedule \(\alpha=(e,b)\),
\[
 s_{\alpha z}
 =\bigl(b_{i,e_{i,n}(z)}\bigr)_{i\in V_n}
 =\bigl(b_{i,e_{i,n}(z')}\bigr)_{i\in V_n}
 =s_{\alpha z'}. \tag{4}
\]
Thus assignments with the same joint signature are identical columns of the entire finite sign experiment. Given a design \(\pi\), aggregate its masses as
\[
 x_q=\sum_{z:\xi_n(z)=\xi_n(z_q)}\pi_z. \tag{5}
\]
For a pair \(\{\alpha,\beta\}\), equations \((4)\)--\((5)\) give
\[
 |t_\alpha-t_\beta|^2
 \sum_{z:s_{\alpha z}=s_{\beta z}}\pi_z
 =|t_\alpha-t_\beta|^2
 \sum_{q\in A(\alpha,\beta)}x_q. \tag{6}
\]
Among pairs having the same collision set \(A\), the right-hand side of \((6)\) is maximized by a pair attaining \(D_A\); such a pair exists whenever the class is nonempty because \(\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\) is finite. Maximizing \((6)\) over pairs and minimizing over the simplex gives \((1)\). Conversely, every simplex vector \(x\) is realized by putting mass \(x_q\) on the chosen representative \(z_q\), which proves both equality and the lifting assertion. Compactness of the simplex gives attainment.

We next construct the cover rather than assume it. Fix a sign schedule \(\alpha\). For each signature \(q\), consider the finite fiber
\[
 \mathcal F_{\alpha q}
 =\{\eta\in\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E):s_{\eta z_q}=s_{\alpha z_q}\}.
\]
If its target diameter is positive, choose \(\beta_{\alpha q}\) and \(\gamma_{\alpha q}\) attaining its maximum and minimum targets and give the pair \(\{\beta_{\alpha q},\gamma_{\alpha q}\}\) weight one; otherwise give it no weight. There are at most \(R_n\) unit weights. For an assignment \(z\) of signature \(q\), equation \((4)\), applied to every schedule, shows that the observation fiber at \(z\) equals the fiber at \(z_q\). Hence the pair indexed by \(q\) collides at \(z\) and
\[
 |t_{\beta_{\alpha q}}-t_{\gamma_{\alpha q}}|^2
 \mathbf 1\{s_{\beta_{\alpha q}z}=s_{\gamma_{\alpha q}z}\}
 =d_n(z,s_{\alpha z})^2. \tag{7}
\]
All other cover terms are nonnegative. Therefore \((7)\) verifies the fractional-diameter-cover inequality with congestion at most \(R_n\) for the trivial group action. Equations \((2)\)--\((3)\) now follow from \(\mathrm{thm{:}orbit\text{-}fractional\text{-}cover\text{-}compression}\).

For completeness, the coefficients in \((1)\) do not require enumeration of all sign schedules. Fix an ordered pair of candidate mappings \((e,f)\). At unit \(i\), only the distinct exposure values in
\[
 \{e_{i,n}(z_q):q\in[R_n]\}
 \cup\{e_{i,n}(\mathbf 0_n),e_{i,n}(\mathbf 1_n)\}
\]
and the analogous set for \(f\) can affect a collision set or a target. Each set has at most \(R_n+2\) values. Enumerate the signs on these values for the two schedules. A local choice determines its mismatch subset \(M_i\subseteq[R_n]\) and its integer contribution
\[
 c_i=
 b^e_{i,e_{i,n}(\mathbf 1_n)}-b^e_{i,e_{i,n}(\mathbf 0_n)}
 -b^f_{i,f_{i,n}(\mathbf 1_n)}+b^f_{i,f_{i,n}(\mathbf 0_n)} \tag{8}
\]
to the numerator of the target difference. A dynamic program over units records, for every union \(M\subseteq[R_n]\), the largest and smallest attainable sum of the contributions in \((8)\). There are \(2^{R_n}\) states and at most \(2^{2(R_n+2)}=16\cdot4^{R_n}\) local choices; updating by \(M\leftarrow M\cup M_i\) costs \(O(n8^{R_n})\) operations for a fixed ordered mapping pair. The final collision set is \([R_n]\setminus M\), and the larger absolute value of the stored extrema, divided by \(n\), gives the relevant \(D_A\). Maximizing over at most \(|\mathcal E|^2\) ordered mapping pairs proves the asserted complexity. The program has \(R_n+1\) variables and at most \(2^{R_n}\) loss inequalities. Under the stated encoding and growth conditions, its table, dynamic program, and linear program are polynomial-size. No assignment-level positivity, rank, superpopulation sampling, or graph-locality condition was used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Joint exposure signatures are an unconditional primitive compression: identical signatures are identical columns of every candidate sign experiment. \textit{Gap.} The universal cover has congestion \(R_n\), not merely \(|\Omega_n|\), but broad primitive conditions forcing \(R_n=O(\log n)\) or bounded congestion remain open. \textit{Consumer.} The result gives an \(R_n+1\)-variable linear program with at most \(2^{R_n}\) loss inequalities and a coefficient algorithm running in \(O(|\mathcal E|^2n8^{R_n})\) arithmetic operations.

\begin{lemma}[lem:split-graph-local-witness]\label{lem:split-graph-local-witness}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and an even admissible size \(n=2B\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\). Label units as \((j,a)\) with \(j\in\{1,\ldots,B\}\) and \(a\in\{1,2\}\), put
\[
 \sigma_n=\max\{m_n,n-m_n\}+1,
\]
choose \(S_n\subseteq V_n\) with \(|S_n|=\sigma_n\), and fix \(c_n\in S_n\). Let \(G_n\) be the complete split graph whose clique is \(S_n\), whose complement \(V_n\setminus S_n\) is independent, and which has every edge between the two parts. Define
\[
 \rho_n^{\mathrm{sp}}(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 00,&z_i=0\text{ for every }i\in S_n,\\
 11,&z_i=1\text{ for every }i\in S_n,\\
 10,&z|_{S_n}\text{ is mixed and }z_{c_n}=1,\\
 01,&z|_{S_n}\text{ is mixed and }z_{c_n}=0,
 \end{cases} \tag{1}
\]
and, for \(\kappa\in\{0,-,+\}\),
\[
 f^\kappa_{(j,a),n}(z)
 =e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}\bigl(\rho_n^{\mathrm{sp}}(z)\bigr). \tag{2}
\]
Set \(\mathcal F_{B,-}=\{f^0,f^-\}\) and \(\mathcal F_{B,+}=\{f^0,f^+\}\). Then every coordinate in both menus is radius-one graph-local relative to \(G_n\), and the joint exposure-signature count on \(\Omega_n\) equals two. Each menu has a polynomially encoded symmetry quotient: the action preserving \(c_n\), membership in \(S_n\), and the type \(a\) has at most \(2(n+1)^4\) assignment orbits and at most \(48(n+1)^{60}\) unordered sign-schedule-pair orbits. The signature program has three variables and at most four loss inequalities, and the menus admit fractional diameter covers of congestion at most two. Hence
\[
 \inf_\pi U_n(\pi)\le\sqrt2\inf_\pi C_n(\pi). \tag{3}
\]
Nevertheless, the mapping-wise labeled conflict-graph multisets of \(\mathcal F_{B,-}\) and \(\mathcal F_{B,+}\) agree under the identity matching of replicated endpoint types, while
\[
 V_n^\star(\mathcal F_{B,-},\bar p)=1,
 \qquad
 V_n^\star(\mathcal F_{B,+},\bar p)=\frac54. \tag{4}
\]
Thus their graph-local ambiguity frontiers are separated by \(1/4\) at every such even size. This construction is dense: it does not assert the same separation for bounded-degree or otherwise sparse-local graph families.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Admissibility gives \(1\le m_n\le n-1\), so \(\sigma_n\le n\) and the set \(S_n\) exists. Every closed neighborhood in the complete split graph contains \(S_n\): a clique vertex is adjacent to every vertex, while a vertex outside the clique is adjacent to every member of \(S_n\). The selector in \((1)\) depends only on \(z|_{S_n}\), and hence every coordinate in \((2)\) is a function of the treatment vector on its closed neighborhood. This is radius-one graph locality.

Because \(|S_n|>m_n\), an exact-budget assignment cannot treat all of \(S_n\). Also \(|S_n|>n-m_n\), so
\[
 |V_n\setminus S_n|=n-|S_n|<m_n,
\]
and an exact-budget assignment cannot leave all of \(S_n\) untreated. Thus \(z|_{S_n}\) is mixed for every \(z\in\Omega_n\). Both values of \(z_{c_n}\) occur on \(\Omega_n\): for value one, treat \(c_n\) and any \(m_n-1\) other units; for value zero, treat any \(m_n\) units other than \(c_n\). Therefore
\[
 \rho_n^{\mathrm{sp}}(\Omega_n)=\{10,01\},
 \qquad
 \rho_n^{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathbf 0_n)=00,
 \qquad
 \rho_n^{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathbf 1_n)=11. \tag{5}
\]
The two feasible selector values give different joint signatures, for example at a replicated unit of type \(a=2\) under \(f^0\). Hence the signature count is exactly two. Applying \(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}signature\text{-}fractional\text{-}compression}\) gives the three-variable, four-row signature program, a cover of congestion at most two, and \((3)\).

We verify the polynomial orbit assertion directly. Partition the units other than \(c_n\) into the at most four classes determined by membership in \(S_n\) and type \(a\). Permutations within these classes preserve the exact budget, the selector, every mapping in either menu, targets, and observations up to coordinate relabeling. An assignment orbit is determined by \(z_{c_n}\) and the treated counts in these four classes, giving at most \(2(n+1)^4\) orbits. Within each nonsingleton class, an unordered schedule pair is described, after choosing its candidate-mapping pair type and a canonical order when necessary, by the counts of the \(16\) possible local four-sign types, giving at most \((n+1)^{15}\) possibilities per class. For unordered schedule pairs there are three candidate-mapping pair types; canonically order a cross-mapping pair by its mapping label. Together with at most 16 possibilities at \(c_n\), this gives at most \(48(n+1)^{60}\) unordered-pair orbits. All orbit sizes and collision counts are products and sums of multinomial coefficients with polynomial bit length, proving polynomial encoding.

It remains to prove the conflict-graph and risk claims. Equation \((5)\) says that the endpoints and feasible columns of the new sign experiment are, coordinate for coordinate, the same base cells \(00,11\) and \(\{10,01\}\) as in \(\mathrm{def{:}persistent\text{-}witness}\). Therefore two replicated endpoint vertices are jointly realizable exactly when one of the base cells \(10,01\) realizes their two base endpoint exposures. Negating joint realizability shows that each \(H_n(f^\kappa)\) is the same labeled type blow-up of \(H_2(e^{(\kappa)})\) as in \(\mathrm{thm{:}persistent\text{-}nonrepresentability}\). The identity matches the shared \(f^0\) graphs and, by the explicit minimal tables, matches the \(f^-\) and \(f^+\) graphs. Hence the two graph multisets agree.

There is also an exact decision-experiment equivalence. A sign schedule for \(f^\kappa\) is the same collection of block-specific response coordinates as a sign schedule for \(\widetilde e^\kappa\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}persistent\text{-}witness}\); targets agree because both selectors send the two global endpoints to \(00\) and \(11\), and observations agree whenever the selectors have the same value in \(\{10,01\}\). Every probability law on these two selector values is feasible in both constructions because both cells have representatives.

Repeated physical assignments within one selector cell cannot improve the square-loss decision problem. Indeed, for a fixed design and rule, condition on the selector value and replace all rule values in that cell by their design-weighted conditional mean for each sign observation. Jensen's inequality weakly decreases the risk of every fixed sign schedule. Conversely, any two-cell rule and design lift by choosing one representative assignment in each cell. Thus the minimax sign experiment for \(\mathcal F_{B,-}\) or \(\mathcal F_{B,+}\) is isomorphic to the corresponding persistent two-cell experiment. The sign reduction transfers the equality to arbitrary bounded response arrays. The exact values already proved in \(\mathrm{thm{:}persistent\text{-}nonrepresentability}\) therefore give \((4)\). Subtraction yields the gap \(1/4\). The maximum degree of the split graph is of order \(n\), so no bounded-degree or sparse-local conclusion has been used or proved. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The dense split-graph construction moves the persistent conflict-graph nonrepresentability witness into an explicit radius-one graph-local family while retaining exact risks. \textit{Gap.} The witness is dense and therefore leaves bounded-degree and otherwise sparse-natural graph-local separation unresolved. \textit{Consumer.} It certifies that two signatures, a polynomial quotient, and congestion two can coexist with equal mapping-wise labeled conflict graphs but latent-menu frontiers separated by \(1/4\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:no-interference-collision-scale]\label{lem:no-interference-collision-scale}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\). For every \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), the singleton no-interference menu \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}}=\{e^{\mathrm{NI}}\}\) is radius-zero graph-local relative to every ambient graph \(G_n\), complete randomization minimizes the pairwise collision modulus, and
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
 =
 \max_{\substack{0\le a\le m_n,\ 0\le b\le n-m_n\\a+b\ge1}}
 \frac{2(a+b)}{n}
 \left\{
 \frac{\binom{n-a-b}{m_n-a}}{\binom n{m_n}}
 \right\}^{1/2}. \tag{1}
\]
Every observation fiber has target diameter two, so
\[
 U_n(\pi)=2\quad\text{for every probability vector \(\pi\) on \(\Omega_n\)}. \tag{2}
\]
Along \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\) with \(n\to\infty\),
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)=\Theta(n^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \inf_\pi U_n(\pi)=2. \tag{3}
\]
Hence no finite constant \(K\), independent of \(n\), satisfies \(\inf_\pi U_n(\pi)\le K\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)\) on all graph-local menu sequences. Moreover, the natural permutation action has one assignment orbit and at most \(\binom{n+15}{15}\) unordered-pair orbits, with polynomially encoded coefficients, but every equivariant fractional diameter cover applicable to that experiment has congestion \(\kappa_n=\Omega(n^2)\). Thus polynomial orbit compression alone does not imply bounded cover congestion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and let \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\) be arbitrary. Write a no-interference sign schedule as \(\alpha=(b_{i0},b_{i1})_{i\in V_n}\). For schedules \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), let
\[
 D_0=\{i:b_{i0}^\alpha\ne b_{i0}^\beta\},
 \qquad
 D_1=\{i:b_{i1}^\alpha\ne b_{i1}^\beta\}. \tag{4}
\]
They collide at assignment \(z\) exactly when every member of \(D_0\) is controlled and every member of \(D_1\) is treated. If \(D_0\cap D_1\ne\varnothing\), collision is impossible. Otherwise put \(a=|D_1|\) and \(b=|D_0|\). Under the uniform law on \(\Omega_n\), the collision probability is
\[
 \frac{\binom{n-a-b}{m_n-a}}{\binom n{m_n}}, \tag{5}
\]
interpreted as zero unless \(a\le m_n\) and \(b\le n-m_n\). Also,
\[
 |t_\alpha-t_\beta|
 \le\frac{2(a+b)}n. \tag{6}
\]
For every disjoint pair of sets of sizes \((a,b)\), equality in \((6)\) is attained by orienting all treatment-response disagreements in one direction and all control-response disagreements in the opposite direction. Thus \((5)\)--\((6)\) give the maximum in \((1)\) under complete randomization.

It remains to verify its optimality over designs. Every permutation of the units acts equivariantly on assignments and schedules, preserves targets, and relabels observed sign vectors bijectively. For any design \(\pi\), average its images under all unit permutations. The resulting law is uniform on \(\Omega_n\). Since \(C_n(\pi)^2\) is a maximum of linear functions of \(\pi\), it is convex, while equivariance preserves its value. Jensen's inequality therefore gives
\[
 C_n(\pi^{\mathrm{unif}})^2
 \le \frac1{n!}\sum_g C_n(g\pi)^2
 =C_n(\pi)^2. \tag{7}
\]
This proves \((1)\).

Fix an assignment and a compatible sign observation. At every unit exactly one of the two endpoint signs is observed and the other remains free. Varying that unobserved sign changes the target range by \(2/n\), independently across the \(n\) units. The target-fiber diameter is therefore exactly two, which proves \((2)\).

Put \(p_n=m_n/n\) and \(q_n=\max\{p_n,1-p_n\}\). Taking either \((a,b)=(1,0)\) or \((0,1)\) in \((1)\) gives
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)\ge\frac{2\sqrt{q_n}}n. \tag{8}
\]
For \(k=a+b\), the probability in \((5)\) is at most \(p_n^a\) and at most \((1-p_n)^b\). Since \(\max\{a,b\}\ge k/2\), it is at most \(q_n^{k/2}\). Choose a constant \(q\) with
\[
 \max\{\bar p,1-\bar p\}<q<1.
\]
Eventually \(q_n\le q\), and \((1)\) then yields
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
 \le\frac2n\sup_{k\ge1}kq^{k/4}
 =\frac{2M(q)}n,
 \qquad M(q)<\infty. \tag{9}
\]
Equations \((8)\)--\((9)\) prove \((3)\) and refute every size-uniform reverse constant.

For the orbit claim, an ordered schedule pair assigns one of \(16\) four-sign types \((b_{i0}^\alpha,b_{i1}^\alpha,b_{i0}^\beta,b_{i1}^\beta)\) to each unit. Its permutation orbit is determined by the counts of these types, so there are at most \(\binom{n+15}{15}\) pair orbits; passage to unordered pairs cannot increase this number. There is one exact-count assignment orbit. Formula \((5)\) shows that the orbit coefficients are ratios of binomial coefficients and have polynomial bit length.

Finally, take any equivariant fractional diameter cover with congestion \(\kappa_n\). The uniform design is invariant under every action on \(\Omega_n\). Summing the cover inequality against that design and bounding each weighted pairwise collision term by \(C_n(\pi^{\mathrm{unif}})^2\) gives
\[
 4=U_n(\pi^{\mathrm{unif}})^2
 \le\kappa_n C_n(\pi^{\mathrm{unif}})^2
 \le\frac{4M(q)^2}{n^2}\kappa_n. \tag{10}
\]
Thus \(\kappa_n\ge n^2/M(q)^2\) eventually. Radius-zero locality follows directly from \(e^{\mathrm{NI}}_{i,n}(z)=z_i\). No positivity beyond the nonempty exact-budget slice is used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The canonical radius-zero local model exactly locates the boundary between orbit compression and diameter-cover compression. \textit{Gap.} Despite one assignment orbit and polynomially many schedule-pair orbits, the fiber modulus stays two and every equivariant cover has congestion \(\Omega(n^2)\). \textit{Consumer.} This is a concrete warning that locality and polynomial symmetry tables do not by themselves yield a size-uniform reverse collision comparison.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exposure-signature-compression-and-local-boundaries]\label{thm:exposure-signature-compression-and-local-boundaries}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and let \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\) diverge. The structural-compression question has the following constructive and negative resolution.

First, write
\[
 R_n=\left|\left\{\bigl(e_{i,n}(z)\bigr)_{(e,i)\in\mathcal E\times V_n}:z\in\Omega_n\right\}\right|
\]
for the number of joint exposure signatures on the exact-budget slice. This is a primitive exposure-incidence certificate. The exact program, algorithm, and cover in \(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}signature\text{-}fractional\text{-}compression}\) imply that \(\sup_nR_n<\infty\) yields a size-uniform reverse comparison with \(K=\sqrt{\sup_nR_n}\); more generally \(R_n=O(\log n)\), fixed menu cardinality, and a polynomially encoded exposure-incidence table yield a polynomial-size exact program for \(\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)^2\) and a polynomial-time coefficient algorithm.

Second, neither graph locality nor polynomial orbit tables alone imply a size-uniform reverse comparison. For the canonical radius-zero graph-local singleton menu \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}}\),
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)=\Theta(n^{-1}),
 \qquad
 L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}},\bar p)=\Theta(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \inf_\pi U_n(\pi)=2, \tag{1}
\]
although the permutation quotient has one assignment orbit and polynomially many pair orbits. Every equivariant fractional diameter cover has congestion \(\Omega(n^2)\). Thus no finite \(K\) works uniformly over graph-local menus.

Third, a positive graph-local conflict-graph separation exists on the explicit dense split-graph sequence in \(\mathrm{lem{:}split\text{-}graph\text{-}local\text{-}witness}\). Each of its two menus has exactly two joint exposure signatures, a polynomially encoded quotient, and a congestion-two cover, yet their mapping-wise labeled conflict-graph multisets agree and their exact ambiguity frontiers are \(1\) and \(5/4\). Hence those frontiers remain separated by \(1/4\) along every admissible even population size. The result is not exhaustive: it does not establish the same separation for bounded-degree or otherwise sparse-local graph sequences.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
The first paragraph is exactly \(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}signature\text{-}fractional\text{-}compression}\), applied along the sequence. In detail, that lemma identifies \(\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)^2\) with an explicit linear program on the \(R_n\) signature masses, lifts every optimizer to an exact-budget design, constructs a fractional diameter cover of congestion at most \(R_n\), and gives the coefficient algorithm with complexity \(O(|\mathcal E|^2n8^{R_n})\). Therefore, if \(\sup_n R_n<\infty\), its comparison
\[
 \inf_\pi U_n(\pi)
 \le \sqrt{R_n}\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
\]
holds uniformly with \(K=\sqrt{\sup_nR_n}\). If instead \(R_n=O(\log n)\), \(|\mathcal E|\) is fixed, and the exposure-incidence table has polynomial encoding length, then both \(2^{R_n}\) and \(8^{R_n}\) are polynomial in \(n\); hence the stated program and coefficient algorithm are polynomial-size and polynomial-time.

For the second paragraph, \(\mathrm{lem{:}no\text{-}interference\text{-}collision\text{-}scale}\) applies to every admissible \(n\) and gives
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)=\Theta(n^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \inf_\pi U_n(\pi)=2.
\]
It also establishes radius-zero graph locality relative to every \(G_n\), one assignment orbit, at most \(\binom{n+15}{15}\) unordered schedule-pair orbits with polynomially encoded coefficients, and congestion \(\kappa_n=\Omega(n^2)\) for every applicable equivariant fractional diameter cover. Meanwhile, \(\mathrm{prop{:}no\text{-}interference\text{-}calibration}\) gives
\[
 L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}},\bar p)=\Theta(n^{-1/2}).
\]
These identities prove \((1)\). If a finite constant \(K\), independent of \(n\), satisfied the reverse comparison uniformly over graph-local menus, then applying it to \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}}\) would yield
\[
 2=\inf_\pi U_n(\pi)
 \le K\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
 =O(n^{-1}),
\]
which is impossible as \(n\to\infty\). Thus neither locality nor polynomial orbit compression alone is sufficient. All risks here are design-based risks for the fixed finite-population causal target; no superpopulation randomness is introduced.

Finally, \(\mathrm{lem{:}split\text{-}graph\text{-}local\text{-}witness}\) supplies, for every admissible even size, two radius-one local menus on the explicit complete split graph. That lemma proves that each menu has exactly two joint exposure signatures, a polynomially encoded quotient, and a fractional diameter cover of congestion at most two. It also proves that the two mapping-wise labeled conflict-graph multisets agree while
\[
 V_n^\star(\mathcal F_{B,-},\bar p)=1,
 \qquad
 V_n^\star(\mathcal F_{B,+},\bar p)=\frac54.
\]
Their exact ambiguity frontiers therefore differ by \(5/4-1=1/4\) at every such size. The witness graph is dense, as that lemma records, so this argument establishes no corresponding separation for bounded-degree or otherwise sparse-local graph sequences. This proves every asserted constructive, negative, and boundary claim. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem combines the unconditional signature compression, the no-interference obstruction, and the dense graph-local separation into a constructive boundary result. \textit{Gap.} Blanket polynomial-compression language is no longer accurate; the residual questions concern broad primitive conditions ensuring the proved compression regime and sparse-natural local nonrepresentability. \textit{Consumer.} Use \(R_n\) for an unconditional exact collision program, verify the stated logarithmic-signature regime for polynomial computation, and do not infer bounded congestion or conflict-graph sufficiency from locality or polynomial orbit tables alone.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal target is the fixed-table contrast \(\theta_n(y)\); the computable frontier \(L_n(\mathcal E,\bar p)\) is the best worst-case design-based root mean squared error for learning that target from one exact-budget realization. Equality \(V_n^\star=L_n\) therefore certifies an optimal design and estimator, not an identification equality between an observed regression functional and the causal effect. Along admissible sizes, \(L_n\to0\) implies design-remediability because the recovered optimal pairs have worst-case error tending to zero, whereas \(\liminf_nL_n>0\) implies design-inertness because every budgeted design and estimator retains positive worst-case error. Both regimes are nonempty: \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}}\) has frontier \(1/n\), the no-interference menu has order \(n^{-1/2}\), and \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}}\) has frontier one. No exhaustiveness is claimed for sequences whose frontiers have other or oscillatory limiting behavior. The ambient graph is ancillary once \((n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\) is fixed; graph language matters only through how a local subclass constructs or constrains the menu and its finite sign experiment.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exact second-order cone programs enumerate assignments and sign schedules and can be exponentially large. Joint-signature aggregation always reduces the collision problem to at most \(R_n\le|\Omega_n|\) assignment columns and supplies a congestion-\(R_n\) cover, but this alone need not be polynomial. Polynomial computation is established when \(R_n=O(\log n)\), the menu cardinality is fixed, and the exposure-incidence table has polynomial encoding length. Polynomial orbit tables without bounded cover congestion are insufficient, as the radius-zero no-interference example forces \(\Omega(n^2)\) congestion. The positive graph-local separation uses a dense complete split graph with degree of order \(n\). Whether equal mapping-wise labeled conflict graphs can conceal a persistent frontier gap on bounded-degree or otherwise sparse-natural graph sequences, and which broad primitive conditions guarantee the signature-compression regime or another comparable compression, remain open.

\section{Honest scope}

For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), every finite program and asymptotic sequence is indexed by \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\); the potential-outcome table is fixed, assignment is the only physical randomness, and exactly \(m_n=\lfloor\bar p n\rfloor\) units are treated. The menu has fixed finite cardinality, response arrays are arbitrary in \([-1,1]\), and the estimator is unrestricted and observes one realization. The exact frontier depends on \((n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\), not primitively on \(G_n\). The unconditional \(R_n\)-signature program and cover apply without graph locality and satisfy \(R_n\le|\Omega_n|\); the stated polynomial guarantee additionally requires \(R_n=O(\log n)\), fixed menu size, and polynomial incidence encoding. Locality plus polynomial orbit tables alone do not ensure bounded congestion. The dense radius-one construction proves a persistent \(1/4\) local conflict-graph separation, but bounded-degree or sparse-natural local separation remains open. The collision sandwich is not an exhaustive characterization by a single simpler graph statistic, and no exhaustive dichotomy is asserted beyond the proved vanishing-frontier and positive-lower-limit regimes.
