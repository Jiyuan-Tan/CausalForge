Put \(A=\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\), which is finite, and first work in the sign experiment supplied by \(\mathrm{lem{:}sign\text{-}reduction}\).  As every \(t_\alpha\in[-2,2]\), projection shows that a finite rule may be restricted to \([-2,2]\).  For \(z\in\Omega_n\) and a vector \(d=(d_s)_{s\in\mathcal S_n}\in[-2,2]^{\mathcal S_n}\), define its loss vector
\[
 \ell(z,d)_\alpha=(d_{s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2,
 \qquad \alpha\in A.
\]
Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^A\) be the convex hull of all these loss vectors.  It is compact because \(\Omega_n\) and \(\mathcal S_n\) are finite and the cube of \(d\)'s is compact.  A distribution over pairs \((z,d)\) can be replaced, conditional on each \(z\), by the conditional mean of \(d\): convexity of the square decreases every coordinate of its loss vector.  Conversely, a design vector \(\pi\) and assignment-specific rules \(d_z\) define such a distribution.  Consequently the finite minimax mean squared error is
\[
 R_n=\min_{x\in K}\max_{\alpha\in A}x_\alpha.
\]

We next derive, rather than assume, the required finite minimax identity.  Set \(\rho=\min_{x\in K}\max_\alpha x_\alpha\).  For \(\varepsilon>0\), the compact convex set \(K\) is disjoint from the closed lower orthant
\[
 D_\varepsilon=\{q\in\mathbb R^A:q_\alpha\le\rho-\varepsilon\text{ for all }\alpha\}.
\]
Choose a closest pair \((x_\varepsilon,q_\varepsilon)\in K\times D_\varepsilon\), which exists because the coordinatewise projection of a bounded set onto \(D_\varepsilon\) is bounded.  Write \(d_\varepsilon=x_\varepsilon-q_\varepsilon\ne0\).  Expanding squared distances along line segments in the two convex sets gives
\[
 d_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}(x-x_\varepsilon)\ge0\quad(x\in K),
 \qquad
 d_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}(q-q_\varepsilon)\le0\quad(q\in D_\varepsilon).
\]
The second inequality and the fact that \(D_\varepsilon\) is unbounded downward imply \(d_{\varepsilon,\alpha}\ge0\) for every \(\alpha\).  Whenever \(d_{\varepsilon,\alpha}>0\), the same inequality forces \(q_{\varepsilon,\alpha}=\rho-\varepsilon\).  Hence, after normalizing
\[
 \mu^\varepsilon_\alpha=
 \frac{d_{\varepsilon,\alpha}}{\sum_{\beta\in A}d_{\varepsilon,\beta}},
\]
we have \(\mu^\varepsilon\) in the probability simplex and
\[
 \min_{x\in K}\sum_\alpha\mu^\varepsilon_\alpha x_\alpha
 \ge \rho-\varepsilon.
\]
Take a convergent subsequence as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).  Compactness of \(K\) makes the displayed minimum continuous in \(\mu\), so its limit \(\mu^\star\) satisfies
\[
 \min_{x\in K}\sum_\alpha\mu^\star_\alpha x_\alpha\ge\rho.
\]
The reverse inequality follows for every probability vector \(\mu\) from
\(\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha x_\alpha\le\max_\alpha x_\alpha\).  Therefore
\[
 R_n
 =\max_{\mu\in\Delta(A)}\min_{z\in\Omega_n,\ d\in[-2,2]^{\mathcal S_n}}
   \sum_{\alpha\in A}\mu_\alpha(d_{s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2.
\]

For fixed \((\mu,z)\), group the last sum by the observed cell \(s\).  With the quantities in \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}dual}\), its contribution at cell \((z,s)\) is
\[
 a_{zs}d_s^2-2b_{zs}d_s+c_{zs}.
\]
If \(a_{zs}>0\), its minimizer is \(d_s=b_{zs}/a_{zs}\in[-2,2]\), and the minimum is \(c_{zs}-b_{zs}^2/a_{zs}\).  If \(a_{zs}=0\), then also \(b_{zs}=c_{zs}=0\), and the contribution is zero.  Thus
\[
 R_n=\max_{\mu\in\Delta(A)}\min_{z\in\Omega_n}
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}
 \left(c_{zs}-\frac{b_{zs}^2}{a_{zs}}\right),
\]
with \(0/0=0\).  The constraints \(a_{zs}u_{zs}\ge b_{zs}^2\), \(u_{zs}\ge0\), and
\(v\le\sum_s(c_{zs}-u_{zs})\) express exactly this maximum: at an optimum one takes \(u_{zs}=b_{zs}^2/a_{zs}\) when \(a_{zs}>0\) and zero otherwise.  Since \(|b_{zs}|\le2a_{zs}\), this convention is continuous as \(a_{zs}\downarrow0\).  Compactness of the prior simplex therefore proves attainment of \(\mathsf Q_n\).

It remains to identify the primal.  Given \((\pi,w,q,r)\) feasible for \(\mathsf P_n\), put \(d_{zs}=w_{zs}/\pi_z\) when \(\pi_z>0\).  The box constraint gives \(d_{zs}\in[-2,2]\), and the rotated-cone constraint gives
\[
 q_{\alpha z}\ge\pi_z(d_{z,s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2.
\]
When \(\pi_z=0\), the closed perspective convention gives \(w_{zs}=0\), and one may take \(q_{\alpha z}=0\).  Hence \(r\) is at least the worst sign-schedule mean squared error.  Conversely, any \((\pi,d)\) yields a feasible point by taking
\[
 w_{zs}=\pi_zd_{zs},\qquad
 q_{\alpha z}=\pi_z(d_{z,s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2,
 \qquad
 r=\max_\alpha\sum_zq_{\alpha z}.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)=R_n=\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n)\).  The compact loss-vector argument above also gives primal attainment.

An optimal \(\pi\) is a probability vector on \(\Omega_n\), so the recovered \(\Theta_n^\star\) lies in \(\mathcal D_n(\bar p)\).  The recovered sign rule has worst-case sign risk \(R_n\), and \(\mathrm{lem{:}sign\text{-}reduction}\) shows that its multilinear extension has worst-case risk at most \(R_n\) on all of \(\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E)\).  The reverse inequality follows because sign schedules are a subclass.  Consequently
\[
 V_n^\star=\sqrt{R_n}
 =\sqrt{\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)}
 =\sqrt{\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n)}
 =L_n(G_n,\mathcal E,\bar p),
\]
and the recovered pair attains this value.  Finally, since \(V_n^\star\) is the infimum over every feasible design and measurable estimator, every individual pair has worst-case root mean squared error at least this common value.
