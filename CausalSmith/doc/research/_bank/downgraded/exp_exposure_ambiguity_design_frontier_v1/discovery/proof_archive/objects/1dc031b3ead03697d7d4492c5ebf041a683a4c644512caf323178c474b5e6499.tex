\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(A=\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\), which is finite, and first work in the sign experiment supplied by \(\mathrm{lem{:}sign\text{-}reduction}\).  Since every \(t_\alpha\in[-2,2]\), projection of an estimate onto \([-2,2]\) cannot increase squared error.  Thus a finite rule may be restricted to that interval.

For \(z\in\Omega_n\) and \(d=(d_s)_{s\in\mathcal S_n}\in[-2,2]^{\mathcal S_n}\), define the loss vector
\[
 \ell(z,d)_\alpha=(d_{s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2,
 \qquad \alpha\in A,
\]
and let \(K\subset\mathbb R^A\) be the convex hull of all such vectors.  The set \(K\) is compact and convex because \(\Omega_n\), \(A\), and \(\mathcal S_n\) are finite and the cube of rules is compact.  A distribution over pairs \((z,d)\) can be replaced, conditional on each \(z\), by the conditional mean of \(d\); coordinatewise convexity of the square weakly decreases every loss.  Conversely, a design vector \(\pi\) and assignment-specific deterministic rules \(d_z\) define a distribution over such pairs.  It follows that the finite minimax mean squared error is
\[
 R_n=\min_{x\in K}\max_{\alpha\in A}x_\alpha. \tag{1}
\]

We derive the finite minimax equality directly.  Write \(\rho\) for the right side of (1).  For \(\varepsilon>0\), the compact convex set \(K\) is disjoint from the closed convex lower orthant
\[
 D_\varepsilon=\{q\in\mathbb R^A:q_\alpha\le\rho-\varepsilon
 \text{ for all }\alpha\}.
\]
A closest pair \((x_\varepsilon,q_\varepsilon)\in K\times D_\varepsilon\) exists: projection of any point of the compact set \(K\) onto \(D_\varepsilon\) is coordinatewise and remains in a bounded set.  Put \(h_\varepsilon=x_\varepsilon-q_\varepsilon\ne0\).  Expanding squared distances along line segments in the two convex sets gives
\[
 h_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}(x-x_\varepsilon)\ge0
 \quad(x\in K),
 \qquad
 h_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}(q-q_\varepsilon)\le0
 \quad(q\in D_\varepsilon). \tag{2}
\]
Because \(D_\varepsilon\) is unbounded in every negative coordinate direction, the second inequality implies \(h_{\varepsilon,\alpha}\ge0\).  If \(h_{\varepsilon,\alpha}>0\), the same inequality, now using a small positive coordinate displacement whenever possible, forces \(q_{\varepsilon,\alpha}=\rho-\varepsilon\).  Hence
\[
 \mu^\varepsilon_\alpha
 =\frac{h_{\varepsilon,\alpha}}
        {\sum_{\beta\in A}h_{\varepsilon,\beta}}
\]
is a probability vector.  From (2) and
\(h_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}x_\varepsilon
=h_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}q_\varepsilon+\lVert h_\varepsilon\rVert_2^2\),
\[
 \min_{x\in K}\sum_\alpha\mu^\varepsilon_\alpha x_\alpha
 \ge \rho-\varepsilon. \tag{3}
\]
Along a convergent subsequence as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), compactness of the probability simplex and continuity of the minimum of a linear functional over compact \(K\) yield a prior \(\mu^\star\) satisfying
\[
 \min_{x\in K}\sum_\alpha\mu^\star_\alpha x_\alpha\ge\rho.
\]
For every probability vector \(\mu\), the reverse weak inequality follows from
\(\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha x_\alpha\le\max_\alpha x_\alpha\).  Since a linear functional has the same minimum on a set and its convex hull, we have proved
\[
 R_n
 =\max_{\mu\in\Delta(A)}
   \min_{z\in\Omega_n,\ d\in[-2,2]^{\mathcal S_n}}
   \sum_{\alpha\in A}\mu_\alpha
   (d_{s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2. \tag{4}
\]

Fix \((\mu,z)\).  Grouping the inner objective in (4) by the observed cell \(s\), and using the quantities in \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}dual}\), gives the cell contribution
\[
 a_{zs}d_s^2-2b_{zs}d_s+c_{zs}. \tag{5}
\]
If \(a_{zs}>0\), its minimizer is \(d_s=b_{zs}/a_{zs}\).  This lies in \([-2,2]\) because it is a convex combination of targets in \([-2,2]\), and the minimum is \(c_{zs}-b_{zs}^2/a_{zs}\).  If \(a_{zs}=0\), the defining nonnegative sums imply \(b_{zs}=c_{zs}=0\), and the contribution is zero.  Therefore, with the convention \(0/0=0\),
\[
 R_n=\max_{\mu\in\Delta(A)}\min_{z\in\Omega_n}
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}
 \left(c_{zs}-\frac{b_{zs}^2}{a_{zs}}\right). \tag{6}
\]
The constraints \(a_{zs}u_{zs}\ge b_{zs}^2\), \(u_{zs}\ge0\), and
\(v\le\sum_s(c_{zs}-u_{zs})\) in \(\mathsf Q_n\) express (6) exactly: at an optimum one takes \(u_{zs}=b_{zs}^2/a_{zs}\) for \(a_{zs}>0\), and zero otherwise.  Moreover \(|b_{zs}|\le2a_{zs}\), so \(0\le b_{zs}^2/a_{zs}\le4a_{zs}\); the convention is continuous as \(a_{zs}\downarrow0\).  Compactness of the prior simplex and finiteness of the assignment and observation sets prove that \(\mathsf Q_n\) attains objective \(R_n\).

It remains to identify the primal.  Given \((\pi,w,q,r)\) feasible for \(\mathsf P_n\), put
\(d_{zs}=w_{zs}/\pi_z\) when \(\pi_z>0\).  The box constraint gives \(d_{zs}\in[-2,2]\), and the rotated-cone constraint gives
\[
 q_{\alpha z}\ge
 \pi_z(d_{z,s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2. \tag{7}
\]
When \(\pi_z=0\), the closed perspective convention forces \(w_{zs}=0\), and that assignment contributes zero design loss.  Thus every feasible primal objective \(r\) is at least the worst sign-schedule mean squared error of its recovered design and rule.  Conversely, any \((\pi,d)\) gives a feasible point by setting
\[
 w_{zs}=\pi_zd_{zs},\qquad
 q_{\alpha z}=\pi_z(d_{z,s_{\alpha z}}-t_\alpha)^2,
 \qquad
 r=\max_\alpha\sum_zq_{\alpha z}. \tag{8}
\]
Equations (1), (7), and (8) show
\[
 \operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)=R_n
 =\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n). \tag{9}
\]
The minimizing point of compact \(K\), followed by conditional-mean derandomization and (8), also proves primal attainment.  Equivalently, one may restrict a minimizing primal sequence to \(0\le r\le4\) and \(0\le q_{\alpha z}\le4\), after which all variables lie in a compact closed feasible set.

An optimal \(\pi\) is a probability vector on the nonempty exact-budget slice \(\Omega_n\), so the recovery gives \(\Theta_n^\star\in\mathcal D_n(\bar p)\).  Its sign rule has worst-case sign risk \(R_n\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}sign\text{-}reduction}\), the multilinear extension \(\widehat\theta_n^\star\) has worst-case mean squared error at most \(R_n\) over all of \(\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E)\); the reverse inequality holds because sign schedules are a subclass.  Consequently
\[
 V_n^\star=\sqrt{R_n}
 =\sqrt{\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)}
 =\sqrt{\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n)}
 =L_n(G_n,\mathcal E,\bar p),
\]
and the recovered pair attains this value.  Finally, the definition of \(V_n^\star\) as an infimum over all feasible designs and measurable estimators implies that every individual pair has worst-case root mean squared error at least this common value.  The only support condition used is \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), which makes \(\Omega_n\) finite and nonempty; no assignment-level positivity, rank, graph-locality, or boundary regularity is required. \(\square\)
