Fix \(\bar p\in[1/2,1)\).  Then
\[
 m_2=\lfloor2\bar p\rfloor=1,
 \qquad
 \Omega_2=\{10,01\}.
\]
Thus the support condition for every admissible design is exactly
\(\Theta(\{10,01\})=1\).  The inequalities \(1\le m\le n-1\) have no
integer solution at \(n=1\), whereas \((n,m)=(2,1)\) is feasible.  This
proves minimality of the population size and budget.  The restriction
\(\bar p<1\) is the boundary condition ensuring that the two-unit budget
does not become degenerate.

Write
\[
 a=(1,0),\qquad b=(1,1),\qquad c=(2,0),\qquad d=(2,1)
\]
for the four endpoint-exposure vertices.  The mapping tables in
\(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}mappings}\) show that every
unit has endpoint exposure zero at \(00\) and endpoint exposure one at
\(11\), under each of \(e^{(0)},e^{(-)},e^{(+)}\).  Hence every unit has
distinct endpoint exposures.  They also show that the sets of endpoint
vertices realized at the two feasible assignments are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &10&01\\ \hline
 e^{(0)}&\{a,c\}&\{a,d\}\\
 e^{(-)}&\{a,c\}&\{b,c\}\\
 e^{(+)}&\{b,c\}&\{a,c\}.
\end{array}
\]
Two distinct endpoint vertices are adjacent in the conflict graph precisely
when neither feasible assignment realizes them jointly.  Consequently
\[
 E(H_2(e^{(0)}))=\{ab,bc,bd,cd\},
 \qquad
 E(H_2(e^{(-)}))=E(H_2(e^{(+)}))=\{ab,ad,bd,cd\}.
\]
The two menus each contain two distinct mappings: \(e^{(0)}\) differs from
\(e^{(-)}\) at \(01\), and from \(e^{(+)}\) at \(10\).  Thus both menus
have cardinality two.  The identity on \(\{a,b,c,d\}\) matches the common
\(e^{(0)}\) graph and matches the \(e^{(-)}\) graph to the
\(e^{(+)}\) graph.  The mapping-wise labeled graph multisets are therefore
identical.

We next compute the two design-inclusive risks.  All response coordinates
used below lie in \([-1,1]\), and the schedules are fixed before assignment,
as required by \(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}table}\) and
\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}schedule}\).

For the lower-risk menu, take schedules \(A\) and \(B\) from
\(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\).  Schedule \(A\)
is measurable under \(e^{(0)}\), has target \(1\), and produces observation
\((-1,1)\) at both \(10\) and \(01\).  Schedule \(B\) is measurable under
\(e^{(-)}\), has target \(-1\), and produces the same observation at both
feasible assignments.  Let \(D_z\) be the value of an arbitrary estimator
on this common observation at assignment \(z\).  For every design supported
on \(\Omega_2\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{Z\sim\Theta}\!left[(D_Z-1)^2+(D_Z+1)^2\right]
 =2\mathbb E_{Z\sim\Theta}[D_Z^2]+2
 \ge 2.
\]
At least one of the two mean squared errors is therefore at least one.  This
argument is uniform over every budget-feasible design and every measurable
estimator, and it gives
\[
 V_2^\star(\mathcal E_-)\ge1.
\]

For the reverse inequality, use the point-mass design at \(10\), which is
admissible because the design class contains every law supported on
\(\Omega_2\).  For either mapping in \(\mathcal E_-\), write its four
bounded response coordinates as
\[
 (a_0,b_0,c_0,d_0)
 =\bigl(h_1(0),h_1(1),h_2(0),h_2(1)\bigr).
\]
The observation at \(10\) is \((a_0,c_0)\), and the target is
\[
 t=\frac{b_0-a_0+d_0-c_0}{2}.
\]
The rule in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}estimators}\)
satisfies
\[
 \delta_-(10,(a_0,c_0))-t
 =-\frac{a_0+c_0}{2}-t
 =-\frac{b_0+d_0}{2}.
\]
Its absolute error is at most one, including at every boundary point of the
response cube.  Hence \(V_2^\star(\mathcal E_-)\le1\), and
\[
 V_2^\star(\mathcal E_-)=1. \tag{1}
\]

For the higher-risk menu, consider the explicit eight-atom prior in
\(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\).  To verify its
certificate rather than assume it, denote a response vector by
\((a_0,b_0,c_0,d_0)\).  The atoms, prior masses, and targets are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{mapping}&(a_0,b_0,c_0,d_0)&\text{mass}&t\\ \hline
e^{(0)}&(-1,-1,1,-1)&15/112&-1\\
e^{(0)}&(1,1,-1,1)&15/112&1\\
e^{(0)}&(-1,1,1,1)&5/32&1\\
e^{(0)}&(1,-1,-1,-1)&5/32&-1\\
e^{(+)}&(-1,-1,1,-1)&5/224&-1\\
e^{(+)}&(1,1,-1,1)&5/224&1\\
e^{(+)}&(-1,1,-1,1)&3/16&2\\
e^{(+)}&(1,-1,1,-1)&3/16&-2.
\end{array}
\]
The masses are positive and sum to one.  For a selector cell \(q\) and
observed sign vector \(s\), let \(A_{qs}\), \(B_{qs}\), and \(C_{qs}\)
be respectively the cell mass and the unnormalized first and second moments
of the target.  Direct substitution into the two mapping tables gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c}
q&s&A_{qs}&B_{qs}&C_{qs}&C_{qs}-B_{qs}^2/A_{qs}\\ \hline
10&(-1,1)&1/2&-3/8&17/16&25/32\\
10&(1,-1)&1/2&3/8&17/16&25/32\\ \hline
01&(-1,-1)&9/28&27/112&99/112&45/64\\
01&(1,1)&9/28&-27/112&99/112&45/64\\
01&(-1,1)&5/28&15/112&5/28&5/64\\
01&(1,-1)&5/28&-15/112&5/28&5/64.
\end{array}
\]
Every displayed cell mass is strictly positive, so every division is valid.
The posterior mean is \(-3s_2/4\) at \(q=10\) and \(-3s_1/4\) at
\(q=01\).  Summing the last column separately at either assignment gives
\[
 \sum_s\left(C_{10,s}-\frac{B_{10,s}^2}{A_{10,s}}\right)
 =\frac{25}{16}
 =\sum_s\left(C_{01,s}-\frac{B_{01,s}^2}{A_{01,s}}\right). \tag{2}
\]
For squared loss the posterior mean minimizes conditional risk, by the
identity
\[
 \mathbb E[(d-T)^2\mid q,s]
 =\operatorname{Var}(T\mid q,s)
  +\bigl(d-\mathbb E[T\mid q,s]\bigr)^2.
\]
The assignment law is selected independently of the fixed adversarial
schedule.  Thus (2) implies Bayes mean squared error at least \(25/16\) for
every design and every estimator.  Worst-case risk dominates Bayes-average
risk over this finite subclass, so
\[
 V_2^\star(\mathcal E_+)\ge\frac54. \tag{3}
\]

It remains to verify the matching upper bound on the full bounded class, not
only on sign schedules.  Use the uniform design on \(\{10,01\}\), whose two
support masses are \(1/2>0\), together with \(\delta_+\).  For either
\(e^{(0)}\) or \(e^{(+)}\), let
\[
 t=\frac{b_0-a_0+d_0-c_0}{2},
 \qquad (a_0,b_0,c_0,d_0)\in[-1,1]^4.
\]
At \(10\) the rule returns \(-3c_0/4\), and at \(01\) it returns
\(-3a_0/4\), under either mapping.  With \(\lambda=b_0+d_0\in[-2,2]\),
its mean squared error is
\[
 R(a_0,c_0,\lambda)
 =\frac12\left[\left(t+\frac{3c_0}{4}\right)^2
                 +\left(t+\frac{3a_0}{4}\right)^2\right]
 =\frac{(2\lambda-2a_0+c_0)^2+(2\lambda+a_0-2c_0)^2}{32}. \tag{4}
\]
The right side of (4) is convex on the box
\([-1,1]^2\times[-2,2]\).  A convex function on a polytope is bounded above
by its largest value at a vertex, because every point is a convex combination
of vertices.  The eight vertex values are summarized by
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
(a_0,c_0)&\lambda=2&\lambda=-2\\ \hline
(1,1)&9/16&25/16\\
(1,-1)&25/16&25/16\\
(-1,1)&25/16&25/16\\
(-1,-1)&25/16&9/16.
\end{array}
\]
Therefore the worst-case mean squared error is at most \(25/16\), including
for arbitrary non-sign bounded response coordinates.  Together with (3),
this proves
\[
 V_2^\star(\mathcal E_+)=\frac54. \tag{5}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), the values in (1) and (5) are
also the exact finite-program frontiers and are attained by budget-feasible
design--estimator pairs.  Since the mapping-wise labeled conflict-graph
multisets are identical but the exact minimax risks differ by \(1/4\), those
graphs alone cannot represent latent-menu risk on the exact-budget slice.
