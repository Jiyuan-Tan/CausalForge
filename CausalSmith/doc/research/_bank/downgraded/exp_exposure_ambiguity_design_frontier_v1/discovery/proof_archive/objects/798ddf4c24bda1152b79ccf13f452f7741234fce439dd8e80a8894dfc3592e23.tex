Write \(\overline a=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in V_n}a_i\).  Because \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), the exact budget satisfies
\[
1\le m_n\le n-1.
\]
Consequently every \(z\in\Omega_n\) differs from \(\mathbf 1_n\).  In particular, under \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), every feasible assignment gives exposure zero to every unit.

\paragraph{Upper bound and primal certificate.}
Consider first an arbitrary table measurable with respect to \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), and write
\[
a_i=h_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(0),
\qquad
b_i=h_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(1).
\]
The support property just proved gives \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=a_i\) for every \(i\), whereas
\[
\theta_n(y)=\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}(b_i-a_i).
\]
The estimator from \(\mathrm{def{:}nonlocal\text{-}solved\text{-}menu}\) therefore obeys
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{nl}}-\theta_n(y)
=-\frac1n\sum_i a_i-\frac1n\sum_i(b_i-a_i)
=-\frac1n\sum_i b_i.
\]
Boundedness gives \(\lvert \widehat\theta^{\mathrm{nl}}-\theta_n(y)\rvert\le1\).  If instead the table is measurable with respect to \(e^{\mathrm c}\), put \(a_i=h_{i,e^{\mathrm c},n}(0)\).  Then \(y_i\) is constant on \(\mathcal A_n\), so \(\theta_n(y)=0\), while
\[
\left\lvert\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{nl}}\right\rvert
=\left\lvert-\frac1n\sum_i a_i\right\rvert\le1.
\]
Thus, under every \(\Theta\in\mathcal D_n(\bar p)\), the worst-case mean squared error of \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{nl}}\) is at most one.

For completeness, this is also a direct feasible certificate for \(\mathsf P_n\).  Let \(\pi\) be any probability vector on \(\Omega_n\), and define on sign observations
\[
\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s)=-\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}s_i.
\]
For any sign schedule based on \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), its error on every feasible assignment is the negative average of its exposure-one signs; for any sign schedule based on \(e^{\mathrm c}\), its error is the negative average of its constant signs.  In either case the squared error is at most one.  Hence the choices
\[
w_{zs}=\pi_z\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s),
\qquad
q_{\alpha z}=\pi_z
 \bigl(\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s_{\alpha z})-t_\alpha\bigr)^2,
\qquad r=1
\]
satisfy all box, perspective-cone, simplex, and risk constraints in \(\mathsf P_n\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)\le1.
\]

\paragraph{A literal finite-dual certificate.}
For \(r,t\in\{-1,1\}\), let \(\alpha_{r,t}\) be the \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-sign schedule having
\[
b_{i,0}=r,
\qquad
b_{i,1}=t
\qquad(i\in V_n),
\]
and assign prior mass \(\mu_{\alpha_{r,t}}=1/4\) to each of the four schedules.  All other prior masses are zero.  For every \(z\in\Omega_n\),
\[
s_{\alpha_{r,t}z}=r(1,\ldots,1),
\qquad
t_{\alpha_{r,t}}=t-r.
\]
For either of the two nonempty observation cells \(s=r(1,\ldots,1)\), the quantities in \(\mathsf Q_n\) are
\[
a_{zs}=\frac12,
\qquad
b_{zs}=\frac14\sum_{t\in\{-1,1\}}(t-r)=-\frac r2,
\qquad
c_{zs}=\frac14\sum_{t\in\{-1,1\}}(t-r)^2=1.
\]
Set \(u_{zs}=1/2\) on those two cells and set \(a_{zs}=b_{zs}=c_{zs}=u_{zs}=0\) on every other cell.  Then
\[
a_{zs}u_{zs}=\frac14=b_{zs}^2
\]
on a nonempty cell, so every rotated-cone constraint holds, and for every feasible assignment
\[
\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}(c_{zs}-u_{zs})
=2\left(1-\frac12\right)=1.
\]
Taking \(v=1\) is feasible for \(\mathsf Q_n\), whence
\[
\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n)\ge1.
\]
The equality of the attained primal and dual objectives from \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), together with the two preceding inequalities, yields
\[
\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)
=\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n)=1,
\qquad
L_n(G_n,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1.
\]

\paragraph{Optimality under each design and the two-schedule witness.}
Fix any \(\Theta\in\mathcal D_n(\bar p)\) and any measurable estimator \(\widehat\theta\).  Define two bounded, fixed, \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-measurable tables \(y^+\) and \(y^-\) by
\[
h^+_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(0)=h^-_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(0)=0,
\qquad
h^+_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(1)=1,
\qquad
h^-_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(1)=-1
\]
for every \(i\in V_n\).  On every assignment in \(\Omega_n\), both tables reveal the identical zero outcome vector, while
\[
\theta_n(y^+)=1,
\qquad
\theta_n(y^-)=-1.
\]
Writing \(D_z=\widehat\theta(z,(0,\ldots,0))\), their two design-based mean squared errors, denoted by \(R_+\) and \(R_-\), satisfy the exact identity
\[
\frac{R_++R_-}{2}
=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\Theta(z)
 \frac{(D_z-1)^2+(D_z+1)^2}{2}
=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\Theta(z)(D_z^2+1)
\ge1.
\]
Thus \(\max\{R_+,R_-\}\ge1\) for every design and every estimator.  Combined with the already established risk bound for \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{nl}}\), this proves that that estimator is minimax optimal under each exact-budget design, including degenerate designs.  The two zero-outcome schedules are the original-experiment two-point lower certificate; the four sign schedules above are their sign lift and give the corresponding literal feasible certificate in \(\mathsf Q_n\).

\paragraph{Boundary and support check.}
The proof uses only \(m_n\le n-1\) to exclude \(\mathbf 1_n\) from the design support, while \(m_n\ge1\) makes the exact budget nondegenerate as required by \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\).  No positivity of individual assignment masses, graph locality, or property of \(G_n\) is used.  Hence the conclusion holds for every fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), every admissible \(n\), and every assignment law supported on \(\Omega_n\).
