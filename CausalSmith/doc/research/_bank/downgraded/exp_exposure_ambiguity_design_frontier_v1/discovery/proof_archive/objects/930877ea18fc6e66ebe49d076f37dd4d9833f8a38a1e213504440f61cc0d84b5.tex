For \(y\in\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}})\), write \(a_i=y_i(1)\), \(b_i=y_i(0)\), their finite-population means as \(\bar a,\bar b\), and
\[
 S_a^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(a_i-\bar a)^2,
 \quad
 S_b^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(b_i-\bar b)^2,
 \quad
 S_\tau^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i\{(a_i-b_i)-(\bar a-\bar b)\}^2.
\]
Under complete randomization, each unit has treatment probability \(m_n/n\), so difference in means is unbiased for \(\bar a-\bar b=\theta_n(y)\).  Expanding the covariances of the indicators under simple random sampling without replacement gives the finite-population identity
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\Theta_n^{\mathrm{CR}}}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{DM}})
 =\frac{S_a^2}{m_n}+\frac{S_b^2}{n-m_n}-\frac{S_\tau^2}{n}.
\]
For completeness, the indicator identities used in that expansion are
\[
 \mathbb E Z_i=\frac{m_n}{n},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(Z_i)=\frac{m_n(n-m_n)}{n^2},\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_i,Z_j)=-\frac{m_n(n-m_n)}{n^2(n-1)}\quad(i\ne j).
\]
Boundedness gives
\[
 S_a^2\le\frac1{n-1}\sum_i a_i^2\le\frac n{n-1},
 \qquad
 S_b^2\le\frac n{n-1}.
\]
Dropping the nonpositive last variance term proves
\[
 \sup_y\operatorname{RMSE}_n(\Theta_n^{\mathrm{CR}},\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{DM}};y)
 \le
 \left\{\frac n{n-1}\left(\frac1{m_n}+\frac1{n-m_n}\right)\right\}^{1/2}.
\]

For a design-uniform lower bound, put independent symmetric signs on all \(2n\) no-interference response coordinates \((a_i,b_i)\).  Conditional on any realized \(Z\) and its observed outcomes, exactly one response coordinate for each unit is unobserved, and these \(n\) missing signs remain independent.  Since each enters \(n^{-1}\sum_i(a_i-b_i)\) with coefficient of magnitude \(1/n\), its conditional posterior variance is
\[
 n\left(\frac1n\right)^2=\frac1n.
\]
Thus every design and estimator has worst-case mean squared error at least \(1/n\).  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), \(L_n=V_n^\star\).  Finally, \(m_n/n\to\bar p\in(0,1)\), so the displayed complete-randomization upper bound is at most a constant depending only on \(\bar p\) times \(n^{-1/2}\), while the prior lower bound is \(n^{-1/2}\).  Hence
\[
 L_n(G_n,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}},\bar p)\asymp n^{-1/2}.
\]
