Because \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), every \(z\in\Omega_n\) has between one and \(n-1\) treated units and therefore differs from \(\mathbf 1_n\).  Under \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), every unit consequently has exposure zero on every feasible assignment.  Write a sign schedule under this mapping as \((b_{i,0},b_{i,1})_{i\in V_n}\).  Its observed sign vector on every \(z\in\Omega_n\) is \((b_{i,0})_{i\in V_n}\), while its target is
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}(b_{i,1}-b_{i,0}).
\]
Under \(e^{\mathrm c}\), the observed vector is likewise its vector of zero-exposure signs, while its target is zero.

Fix \(z\in\Omega_n\) and \(s\in\mathcal S_n\), and write \(\bar s=n^{-1}\sum_i s_i\).  The \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\) schedules in the fiber \((z,s)\) have \(b_{i,0}=s_i\), whereas their \(b_{i,1}\)'s are arbitrary.  Their minimum and maximum targets are therefore
\[
 -1-\bar s
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 1-\bar s.
\]
The constant-mapping schedules add only the target zero, which lies in this interval because \(\bar s\in[-1,1]\).  Hence every observation fiber has target diameter exactly two:
\[
 d_n(z,s)=2.
\]
It follows directly from the definition that, for every \(\pi\),
\[
 U_n(\pi)^2
 =\max_\alpha\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_z\,4
 =4.
\]

We next evaluate the pairwise modulus.  If two \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\) schedules collide at one feasible assignment, their zero-exposure sign vectors agree, and their target difference is the average of coordinatewise differences of their all-treated signs, hence is at most two in absolute value.  Two constant-mapping schedules both have target zero.  If one schedule uses each mapping and they collide, the constant-mapping target is zero and the \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\) target has absolute value at most two.  Thus every colliding pair has target separation at most two, while a noncolliding pair contributes zero to \(C_n(\pi)\).  This proves \(C_n(\pi)\le2\).

For the reverse inequality, take two \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\) schedules whose zero-exposure signs are all \(-1\).  Give the first schedule all \(+1\) all-treated signs and the second all \(-1\) all-treated signs.  Their targets are respectively \(2\) and \(0\), and they produce the same all-\(-1\) observation on every feasible assignment.  Their contribution to \(C_n(\pi)\) is therefore \(2\sqrt{\sum_z\pi_z}=2\).  Hence \(C_n(\pi)=2\), and the preceding calculation gives \(U_n(\pi)=2\).

Finally, \(\mathrm{prop{:}nonlocal\text{-}program\text{-}solution}\) gives \(V_n^\star=1\).  Therefore
\[
 \frac12 C_n(\pi)=V_n^\star=\frac12 U_n(\pi)
\]
for every design \(\pi\).  Any larger universal coefficient in the lower bound or smaller universal coefficient in the upper bound would fail on this menu, proving sharpness.
