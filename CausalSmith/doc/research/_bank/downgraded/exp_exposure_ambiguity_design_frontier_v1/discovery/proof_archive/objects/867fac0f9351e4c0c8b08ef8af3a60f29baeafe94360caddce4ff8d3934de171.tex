\textbf{Proof.}
All sets in the argument are finite. The support condition \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\) gives \(1\le m_n\le n-1\), so \(\Omega_n\) is nonempty. Potential-outcome schedules remain fixed, and the sole randomness is the assignment drawn from the design.

We first record the consequence of the group action for observation fibers. Because the action on \(\mathfrak A_n\) is a bijection, the map \(\alpha\mapsto g\alpha\) sends
\[
 \{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s\}
 \quad\text{bijectively onto}\quad
 \{\alpha':s_{\alpha',gz}=\phi_{g,z}(s)\}.
\]
The target is unchanged because \(t_{g\alpha}=t_\alpha\). Hence the maximum and minimum target on the two fibers agree, and therefore
\[
 d_n(gz,\phi_{g,z}(s))=d_n(z,s). \tag{1}
\]
Equality of two observations is likewise preserved:
\[
 s_{g\alpha,gz}=s_{g\beta,gz}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 s_{\alpha z}=s_{\beta z}. \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1) and (2) show in particular that transforming a representative fractional cover produces a valid cover for every schedule in its orbit.

For a probability vector \(\pi\) on \(\Omega_n\), let
\[
 F_{\alpha\beta}(\pi)
 =|t_\alpha-t_\beta|^2
   \sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_z
   \mathbf 1\{s_{\alpha z}=s_{\beta z}\}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def{:}collision\text{-}moduli}\),
\[
 C_n(\pi)^2=\max_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak A_n}F_{\alpha\beta}(\pi). \tag{3}
\]
For \(g\in\Gamma_n\), define \((g\pi)_z=\pi_{g^{-1}z}\), and let
\[
 \bar\pi=|\Gamma_n|^{-1}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_n}g\pi.
\]
Using (2), target invariance, and the substitution \(u=g^{-1}z\), for every pair \(\{\alpha,\beta\}\) we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_{\alpha\beta}(\bar\pi)
 &=\frac1{|\Gamma_n|}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_n}
   F_{g^{-1}\alpha,g^{-1}\beta}(\pi)\\
 &\le C_n(\pi)^2.
 \end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
Taking the maximum in (4) and using (3) yields
\[
 C_n(\bar\pi)\le C_n(\pi). \tag{5}
\]
Thus an optimizer of \(C_n\) may be chosen invariant. Existence follows from continuity on the finite-dimensional probability simplex.

Every invariant probability vector is uniquely described by its orbit masses
\[
 x_j=\sum_{z\in O_j}\pi_z,
 \qquad
 \pi_z=\frac{x_j}{|O_j|}\quad(z\in O_j). \tag{6}
\]
For the representative pair \(p_r\), (6) gives
\[
 F_{\alpha_r\beta_r}(\pi)
 =\Delta_r^2\sum_{j=1}^K a_{rj}x_j. \tag{7}
\]
If two pairs lie in the same pair orbit, (2), target invariance, and invariance of \(\pi\) show that their values of \(F\) coincide. Hence maximizing (7) over \(r=1,\ldots,R\) is exactly the maximum in (3), not a relaxation. Minimizing that maximum over the simplex of \(x\)'s is precisely the displayed quotient linear program. Consequently
\[
 \lambda_n=\inf_\pi C_n(\pi)^2,
 \qquad
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)=\sqrt{\lambda_n}. \tag{8}
\]
The lifting formula in the theorem is just (6), so it also explicitly constructs the optimizing invariant design.

We next compare the two primitive moduli. Fix any \(\pi\) and any pair \(\alpha,\beta\). Whenever \(s_{\alpha z}=s_{\beta z}\), both targets lie in the same observation fiber, and therefore
\[
 |t_\alpha-t_\beta|
 \le d_n(z,s_{\alpha z}).
\]
After squaring and averaging,
\[
 F_{\alpha\beta}(\pi)
 \le\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_zd_n(z,s_{\alpha z})^2
 \le U_n(\pi)^2.
\]
Maximization over pairs gives the pointwise inequality
\[
 C_n(\pi)\le U_n(\pi), \tag{9}
\]
and hence \(\inf_pi C_n(\pi)\le\inf_pi U_n(\pi)\).

Let \(\pi^\star\) be the invariant lift of a quotient-program optimizer. Fix a schedule \(\alpha\). By (1)--(2), its transformed representative cover is valid. Multiply its cover inequality by \(\pi_z^\star\) and sum over assignments. Each pair appearing in the cover contributes at most \(C_n(\pi^\star)^2\) by (3). The sum of its nonnegative weights is at most \(\kappa_n\), so
\[
 \sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_z^\star d_n(z,s_{\alpha z})^2
 \le\kappa_n C_n(\pi^\star)^2.
\]
Maximizing over \(\alpha\) proves
\[
 U_n(\pi^\star)
 \le\sqrt{\kappa_n}C_n(\pi^\star)
 =\sqrt{\kappa_n\lambda_n}. \tag{10}
\]
It follows from (8)--(10) that
\[
 \inf_\pi C_n(\pi)
 \le\inf_\pi U_n(\pi)
 \le\sqrt{\kappa_n}\inf_\pi C_n(\pi). \tag{11}
\]

By \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), the causal minimax risk equals the exact frontier \(L_n(G_n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\). Apply \(\mathrm{thm{:}collision\text{-}geometry\text{-}sandwich}\), then (8) and (10), to obtain
\[
 \frac12\sqrt{\lambda_n}
 \le L_n(G_n,\mathcal E,\bar p)
 \le\frac12U_n(\pi^\star)
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\kappa_n\lambda_n}. \tag{12}
\]
The upper-bound construction in that sandwich theorem assigns every sign observation fiber the midpoint of its target interval and then applies the multilinear extension in \(\mathrm{def{:}multilinear\text{-}extension}\). Under the explicit lifted design \(\pi^\star\), (10) shows that this rule has worst-case root mean squared error at most the last term of (12). Thus the bracket supplies both a lower certificate valid against every budgeted design and estimator and an explicit attaining-up-to-factor design and estimator.

The quotient program has exactly the dimensions stated in the theorem. Therefore polynomially many explicitly encoded assignment and pair orbits give a polynomial-size linear-program certificate; the fractional cover contributes the audited factor \(\sqrt{\kappa_n}\). If \(\sup_n\kappa_n<\infty\), (11) proves order equivalence and (12) has a uniform constant ratio. The cover condition is nonvacuous even without symmetry: for each \((\alpha,z)\), choose two schedules attaining the finite fiber diameter and include that pair with unit weight in the cover for \(\alpha\); this always gives the coarse bound \(\kappa_n\le|\Omega_n|\). The contribution of symmetry and exposure incidence is precisely to reduce the orbit tables and the cover congestion to polynomial and constant size, respectively.

Finally, \(\mathrm{prop{:}nonlocal\text{-}collision\text{-}sharpness}\) shows that the constants \(1/2\) used in (12) cannot be universally improved. The all-budget common-selector construction in \(\mathrm{thm{:}persistent\text{-}nonrepresentability}\) gives, for every fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), identical mapping-wise conflict-graph multisets with exact risks \(1\) and \(5/4\) at every sufficiently large admissible even size. Hence such graph multisets cannot replace the joint pair-orbit and fiber-cover data on the unrestricted class. Neither that nonlocal witness nor the present sufficient criterion proves or refutes an analogous separation for natural graph-local menus, so no exhaustiveness claim is made. \(\square\)
