\textbf{Proof.}
Because \(1\le m_n\le n-1\), no \(z\in\Omega_n\) equals \(\mathbf 1_n\).  Under \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), write
\[
 a_i=h_i(0),\qquad b_i=h_i(1).
\]
Every feasible observation is \((a_i)_{i\in V_n}\), whereas
\[
 \theta_n(y)=\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}(b_i-a_i). \tag{1}
\]
The constant-mapping class is contained in this class by taking \(b_i=a_i\).  Hence it is enough to analyze \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\).  The estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}nonlocal\text{-}solved\text{-}menu}\) has error
\[
 -\frac1n\sum_i a_i-  heta_n(y)
 =-\frac1n\sum_i b_i,
\]
whose absolute value is at most one.  This proves a worst-case root mean squared error upper bound of one under every exact-budget design.

For the lower bound, place equal prior mass on two fixed bounded schedules.  Both have \(a_i=0\) for every unit, while one has \(b_i=1\) for every unit and the other has \(b_i=-1\) for every unit.  Their feasible observations are identically zero, and their targets are \(1\) and \(-1\).  For an arbitrary estimator value \(D_z\) on the common observation,
\[
 \frac12(D_z-1)^2+\frac12(D_z+1)^2=D_z^2+1\ge1. \tag{2}
\]
After averaging (2) under any design, every estimator has Bayes, hence worst-case, mean squared error at least one.  Thus the estimator is optimal under every exact-budget design and
\[
 V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}})=1.
\]
The equal two-point prior is the stated dual certificate.  Applying \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\) yields
\[
 L_n(G_n,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1.
\]
No endpoint assignment lies in the design support, and no positivity, rank, or locality condition is used. \(\square\)
