\textbf{Proof.}
Admissibility gives \(1\le m_n\le n-1\), so no feasible assignment is \(\mathbf 1_n\). Under \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), write \(a_i=h_i(0)\) and \(b_i=h_i(1)\). Every feasible observation is \((a_i)_i\), while
\[
 \theta_n(y)=n^{-1}\sum_i(b_i-a_i). \tag{1}
\]
The constant model is contained in this model. The displayed estimator has error \(-n^{-1}\sum_i b_i\), of absolute value at most one, under every design.

For a lower certificate, place equal mass on two fixed schedules having \(a_i=0\) for all \(i\), and respectively \(b_i=1\) and \(b_i=-1\) for all \(i\). Their feasible observations agree and their targets are \(1\) and \(-1\). For any rule value \(D_z\),
\[
 \frac12(D_z-1)^2+\frac12(D_z+1)^2=D_z^2+1\ge1.
\]
Averaging under any design proves a Bayes and hence worst-case mean squared error lower bound of one. Thus
\[
 V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)
 =L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1
\]
by \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), and the displayed rule is optimal under every exact-budget design. \(\square\)
