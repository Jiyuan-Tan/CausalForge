\textbf{Proof.}
Every feasible assignment differs from \(\mathbf 1_n\), so an \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\) schedule reveals its exposure-zero vector at every feasible assignment. Two such schedules that collide once have the same zero vector and collide everywhere; their targets differ only through exposure-one responses, hence by at most two. The same bound holds for a collision with a constant schedule, whose target is zero. It is attained by two schedules with common exposure-zero vector \(\mathbf 1_n\), and exposure-one vectors \(\mathbf 1_n\) and \(-\mathbf 1_n\); their targets are zero and minus two. Therefore \(C_n(\pi)=2\) for every \(\pi\).

In any nonempty observation fiber, hold the observed exposure-zero vector fixed and vary the unobserved exposure-one vector over \(\{-1,1\}^n\). The target range is exactly two. The collision argument shows it cannot be larger, so every nonempty fiber has diameter two and \(U_n(\pi)=2\). Finally \(\mathrm{prop{:}nonlocal\text{-}program\text{-}solution}\) gives \(V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1\). Substitution into the collision sandwich gives equality on both sides and proves sharpness. \(\square\)
