Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), and let \(B\ge1\) satisfy \(2B\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\).  Put
\[
 m=m_{2B}=\lfloor 2B\bar p\rfloor.
\]
By the definition of \(\mathcal N_{\bar p}\),
\[
 1\le m\le 2B-1. \tag{1}
\]
All potential-outcome coordinates below are fixed before randomization and lie in \([-1,1]\), by \(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}schedule}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}table}\).  The only randomness used in the risk calculations is the assignment drawn from the displayed exact-budget design.

\emph{Selector surjectivity and endpoint exposures.}
Let \(u_\star=(1,1)\) be the distinguished unit.  The other \(2B-1\) units contain a subset \(S_+\) of size \(m-1\), because \(0\le m-1\le2B-2\) by (1).  The assignment treating \(\{u_\star\}\cup S_+\) belongs to \(\Omega_{2B}\), contains \(u_\star\), and is neither endpoint; hence its selector value is \(10\).  Likewise, the other \(2B-1\) units contain a subset \(S_-\) of size \(m\), because \(1\le m\le2B-1\).  The assignment treating exactly \(S_-\) belongs to \(\Omega_{2B}\), excludes \(u_\star\), and has selector value \(01\).  Every member of \(\Omega_{2B}\) is a non-endpoint assignment by (1), so the definition of \(\rho_B\) permits no other selector value.  Therefore
\[
 \rho_B(\Omega_{2B})=\{10,01\}. \tag{2}
\]
This argument uses no equality between \(m\) and \(B\).

The two menus have cardinality two by their construction.  Moreover, \(\rho_B(\mathbf 0_{2B})=00\) and \(\rho_B(\mathbf 1_{2B})=11\).  The base tables in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}mappings}\) give different exposures at \(00\) and \(11\) for each base unit and each of \(e^{(0)},e^{(-)},e^{(+)}\).  Thus every replicated unit has distinct endpoint exposures under every mapping in either menu.

\emph{Conflict-graph preservation.}
For \(\kappa\in\{0,-,+\}\), base type \(a\in\{1,2\}\), and endpoint label \(k\in\{0,1\}\), write
\[
 x^{\kappa}_{a,k}=e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}(k k),
\]
where \(k k\) denotes \(00\) when \(k=0\) and \(11\) when \(k=1\).  By the definition of the replicated mapping, a physical assignment \(z\in\Omega_{2B}\) realizes vertex \(((j,a),k)\)'s endpoint exposure exactly when
\[
 e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}(\rho_B(z))=x^{\kappa}_{a,k}. \tag{3}
\]
Combining (2) and (3), vertices \(((j,a),k)\) and \(((j',a'),k')\) are jointly realizable if and only if there is a \(q\in\{10,01\}\) such that
\[
 e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}(q)=x^{\kappa}_{a,k}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 e^{(\kappa)}_{a',2}(q)=x^{\kappa}_{a',k'}.
\]
Negating this equivalence gives exactly the asserted type-blow-up adjacency rule for \(H_{2B}(\widetilde e^\kappa)\).

For both \(e^{(-)}\) and \(e^{(+)}\), the two feasible sets of jointly realized base endpoint vertices are \(\{(1,0),(2,0)\}\) and \(\{(1,1),(2,0)\}\); the two selector values merely realize these sets in opposite orders.  Hence their feasible endpoint-exposure vector sets coincide.  The preceding adjacency characterization then shows that the identity on the replicated vertices is a labeled graph isomorphism from \(H_{2B}(\widetilde e^-)\) to \(H_{2B}(\widetilde e^+)\).  The two menus literally share \(\widetilde e^0\), so the identity also matches those graphs.  Thus their mapping-wise graph multisets agree.

\emph{Lower-risk menu: lower bound.}
Replicate in every block the two schedules \(A\) and \(B\) from \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\).  They are members of the two respective components of \(\mathcal Y_{2B}(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-})\), because their response coordinates are fixed, bounded by one, and measurable with respect to \(\widetilde e^0\) and \(\widetilde e^-\), respectively.  At either selector value in (2), both replicated schedules produce the same observed vector, consisting in every block of \((-1,1)\), while their normalized targets are respectively \(1\) and \(-1\).

Let an arbitrary measurable estimator return \(D_z\) on this common observation at physical assignment \(z\), and let \(\Theta\) be any probability law on \(\Omega_{2B}\).  The sum of its two mean squared errors is
\[
 \mathbb E_{Z\sim\Theta}\!\left[(D_Z-1)^2+(D_Z+1)^2\right]
 =2\mathbb E_{Z\sim\Theta}[D_Z^2]+2
 \ge2. \tag{4}
\]
Consequently at least one of the two schedules has mean squared error at least one.  Since both belong to the ambiguity class, (4) proves
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-})\ge1. \tag{5}
\]
This bound holds for every budget-feasible design and every measurable estimator.

\emph{Lower-risk menu: attaining upper bound.}
Choose the point-mass design at the assignment constructed above with selector value \(10\).  This law is allowed because \(\mathcal D_{2B}(\bar p)\) contains every probability distribution supported on \(\Omega_{2B}\); no assignment-probability positivity condition is imposed or needed.  For block \(j\), denote its four bounded response coordinates by \((a_j,b_j,c_j,d_j)\), in the base order used in \(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\).  Under either mapping in the lower-risk menu, selector value \(10\) reveals \((a_j,c_j)\), while the block target is
\[
 t_j=\frac{b_j-a_j+d_j-c_j}{2}.
\]
Apply the base rule \(\delta_-(10,x)=-(x_1+x_2)/2\) in each block and average.  Its block error equals
\[
 -\frac{a_j+c_j}{2}-t_j=-\frac{b_j+d_j}{2},
\]
whose absolute value is at most one.  The global target is \(B^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^B t_j\), so the absolute error of the averaged estimator is also at most one.  Hence
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-})\le1. \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5) and (6) give
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-})=1. \tag{7}
\]

\emph{Higher-risk menu: lower bound.}
Take the eight-point prior from \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\), and for each prior atom replicate its base response coordinates identically in all \(B\) blocks.  Every resulting schedule is fixed, bounded, and measurable with respect to either \(\widetilde e^0\) or \(\widetilde e^+\), and its normalized target equals its base target \(t_\alpha\).  Conditional on a physical assignment \(z\), the observed vector consists of \(B\) repetitions of the base sign observation \(s_{\alpha,\rho_B(z)}\).  Because \(z\) is drawn independently of the fixed prior atom, the full label \(z\) supplies no further information about \(t_\alpha\) beyond this base observation.

The cell calculations in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\), verified in \(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\), give Bayes mean squared error \(25/16\) separately at each selector value \(q\in\{10,01\}\).  Therefore, for every physical \(z\in\Omega_{2B}\) and every estimator value based on \(z\) and its replicated observation,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\alpha}\!\left[
   \bigl(\widehat\theta(z,s_{\alpha,
ho_B(z)}^{\otimes B})-t_\alpha\bigr)^2
 \right]\ge\frac{25}{16}. \tag{8}
\]
Averaging (8) under an arbitrary \(\Theta\in\mathcal D_{2B}(\bar p)\) preserves the bound.  A worst-case risk is at least the Bayes-average risk over this finite subclass, and hence
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+})\ge\frac54. \tag{9}
\]

\emph{Higher-risk menu: attaining upper bound.}
Place probability \(1/2\) on each of the two physical assignments constructed above, one with selector value \(10\) and one with selector value \(01\).  For each block \(j\), apply the base rule
\[
 \delta_+(10,x)=-\frac34x_2,
 \qquad
 \delta_+(01,x)=-\frac34x_1,
\]
and return the average of the \(B\) block estimates.  For an arbitrary bounded response array measurable with respect to either mapping, let \(\widehat t_j\) be the resulting block estimate and \(t_j\) its block target.  Pointwise Jensen gives
\[
 \left\{\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)\right\}^2
 \le \frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)^2. \tag{10}
\]
Under the uniform distribution on the two selector cells, the base bounded-array calculation in \(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\) bounds the expectation of each summand on the right side of (10) by \(25/16\), for both candidate mappings.  Taking assignment expectation in (10) therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{y\in\mathcal Y_{2B}(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+})}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
   \bigl(\widehat\theta-  heta_{2B}(y)\bigr)^2
 \right]
 \le\frac{25}{16}. \tag{11}
\]
Combining (9) and (11) proves
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+})=\frac54. \tag{12}
\]
The exact finite saddle theorem confirms that these matching design-estimator upper certificates and design-uniform lower certificates equal the corresponding finite-program frontiers.

Finally, fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\).  If \(B\ge\lceil(2\bar p)^{-1}\rceil\), then \(\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor\ge1\); and since \(\bar p<1\), one always has \(\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor\le2B-1\).  Hence every sufficiently large even size belongs to \(\mathcal N_{\bar p}\).  Equations (7) and (12) hold at each such size, and their absolute difference is
\[
 \frac54-1=\frac14.
\]
Thus the nonrepresentability gap persists along all sufficiently large even population sizes for every fixed treatment fraction in \((0,1)\), as claimed.
