Every \(z\in\Omega_n\) differs from \(\mathbf 1_n\) because \(m_n\le n-1\).  Under \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), all observed outcomes are therefore the zero-exposure responses \(a_i=h_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(0)\), whereas
\[
 \theta_n(y)=\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}(b_i-a_i),
 \qquad b_i=h_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(1).
\]
The estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}nonlocal\text{-}solved\text{-}menu}\) returns \(-n^{-1}\sum_i a_i\), so its error is \(-n^{-1}\sum_i b_i\), whose absolute value is at most one.  Under \(e^{\mathrm c}\), the target is zero and the same bound follows from boundedness.  This gives an upper bound of one under every exact-budget design.

For a direct indistinguishability lower bound, take two bounded \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-measurable schedules with \(a_i=0\) for all \(i\) and respectively \(b_i=1\) and \(b_i=-1\) for all \(i\).  They have the same zero observation at every \(z\in\Omega_n\) and targets \(1\) and \(-1\), so the identity
\[
 (r-1)^2+(r+1)^2\ge2
\]
forces worst-case mean squared error at least one.  An explicit sign-schedule version of the dual certificate is obtained by drawing independent symmetric \(R,T\), setting every zero-exposure response to \(R\) and every one-exposure response to \(T\).  Conditional on the observed \(R\), the target \(T-R\) has posterior variance one.  Thus the finite dual also has value at least one.  The upper and lower bounds match, and \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\) gives \(L_n(G_n,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1\).
