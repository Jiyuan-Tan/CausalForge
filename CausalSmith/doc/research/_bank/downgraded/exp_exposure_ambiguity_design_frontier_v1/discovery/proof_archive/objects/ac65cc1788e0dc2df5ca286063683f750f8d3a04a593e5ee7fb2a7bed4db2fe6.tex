Since \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), every \(z\in\Omega_n\) has between one and \(n-1\) treated units and hence differs from \(\mathbf 1_n\).  Under \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), every unit has exposure zero on every feasible assignment.  Write an \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-sign schedule as \((b_{i,0},b_{i,1})_{i\in V_n}\).  Its observed sign vector on every \(z\in\Omega_n\) is \((b_{i,0})_{i\in V_n}\), and its target is
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}(b_{i,1}-b_{i,0}).
\]
An \(e^{\mathrm c}\)-sign schedule likewise reveals its vector of zero-exposure signs, but its target is zero.

Fix \(z\in\Omega_n\) and \(s\in\mathcal S_n\), and put \(\bar s=n^{-1}\sum_i s_i\).  The \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-schedules in the fiber \((z,s)\) have \(b_{i,0}=s_i\), while their \(b_{i,1}\)'s are unrestricted signs.  The minimum and maximum targets in this part of the fiber are
\[
 -1-\bar s
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 1-\bar s.
\]
The constant-mapping schedules add only target zero, which lies in this interval because \(\bar s\in[-1,1]\).  Hence every observation fiber has target diameter exactly two:
\[
 d_n(z,s)=2.
\]
It follows for every probability vector \(\pi\) that
\[
 U_n(\pi)^2
 =\max_\alpha\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_z\,4
 =4,
\]
and therefore \(U_n(\pi)=2\).

We next evaluate the pairwise modulus.  If two \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-schedules collide at a feasible assignment, their zero-exposure sign vectors agree, and their target difference is the average of coordinatewise differences of their exposure-one signs; its absolute value is at most two.  Two \(e^{\mathrm c}\)-schedules both have target zero.  If one schedule uses each mapping and they collide, the constant-mapping target is zero and the \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-target lies in \([-2,2]\).  Thus every colliding pair has target separation at most two, whereas a noncolliding pair contributes zero to \(C_n(\pi)\).  This proves \(C_n(\pi)\le2\).

For the reverse inequality, take two \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-schedules whose zero-exposure signs are all \(-1\).  Give the first all \(+1\) exposure-one signs and the second all \(-1\) exposure-one signs.  Their targets are respectively two and zero, and they produce the same all-negative observation under every feasible assignment.  Their contribution is
\[
 2\left(\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\pi_z\right)^{1/2}=2.
\]
Therefore \(C_n(\pi)=2\).

Finally, \(\mathrm{prop{:}nonlocal\text{-}program\text{-}solution}\) gives \(V_n^\star=1\).  Hence for every design vector
\[
 \frac12C_n(\pi)=V_n^\star=\frac12U_n(\pi).
\]
This is equality on both sides of \(\mathrm{thm{:}collision\text{-}geometry\text{-}sandwich}\).  Any larger universal coefficient in the lower bound or smaller universal coefficient in the upper bound would fail on this menu, so the factor \(1/2\) is sharp in both inequalities.  As above, the support condition follows solely from \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\); no positivity of individual masses or graph-locality condition is needed.
