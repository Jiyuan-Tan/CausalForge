\textbf{Proof.}
Under \(e^{\mathrm{loc}}\), write \(a\) and \(b\) for unit \(1\)'s responses at exposures zero and one. Every other endpoint contrast vanishes, and the constant mapping is a subclass, so every table in the union has
\[
 \theta_n(y)=\frac{b-a}{n}. \tag{1}
\]
If \(Z_1=1\), the displayed estimator is \(b/n\) and its error is \(a/n\); if \(Z_1=0\), it is \(-a/n\) and its error is \(-b/n\). Thus its error is pointwise bounded by \(1/n\) under every exact-budget design.

For the lower bound, take \(a,b\) independent symmetric signs and fix all other coordinates. Given any assignment and full observation, exactly one of \(a,b\) remains an independent unseen sign. By (1),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\theta_n(y)\mid Z,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})=n^{-2}.
\]
The posterior square-loss identity gives Bayes mean squared error at least \(n^{-2}\) for every design and rule; worst-case risk dominates this finite prior average. Therefore, by \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\),
\[
 L_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}},\bar p)
 =V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}},\bar p)=\frac1n.
\]
No assignment-level positivity is needed. \(\square\)
