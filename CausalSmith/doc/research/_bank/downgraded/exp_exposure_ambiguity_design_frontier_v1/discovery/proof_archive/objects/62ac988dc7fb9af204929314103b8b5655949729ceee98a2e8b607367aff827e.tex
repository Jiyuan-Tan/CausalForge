Write \(\overline a=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in V_n}a_i\).  Since \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\),
\[
 1\le m_n\le n-1.
\]
Thus every \(z\in\Omega_n\) differs from \(\mathbf 1_n\), and under \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\) every feasible assignment gives every unit exposure zero.

\paragraph{Upper bound.}
Suppose first that \(y\) is measurable with respect to \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\), and write
\[
 a_i=h_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(0),\qquad
 b_i=h_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(1).
\]
The support fact above gives \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=a_i\), while
\[
 \theta_n(y)=\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}(b_i-a_i).
\]
For the rule in \(\mathrm{def{:}nonlocal\text{-}solved\text{-}menu}\),
\[
 \widehat\theta^{\mathrm{nl}}-\theta_n(y)
 =-\frac1n\sum_i a_i-\frac1n\sum_i(b_i-a_i)
 =-\frac1n\sum_i b_i.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}table}\), incorporated in \(\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}})\), implies that the absolute value of this error is at most one.  If instead \(y\) is measurable with respect to \(e^{\mathrm c}\), write \(a_i=h_{i,e^{\mathrm c},n}(0)\).  Then \(y_i\) is constant on \(\mathcal A_n\), so \(\theta_n(y)=0\), and
\[
 \left|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{nl}}\right|
 =\left|-\frac1n\sum_i a_i\right|\le1.
\]
Consequently, under every \(\Theta\in\mathcal D_n(\bar p)\), the worst-case mean squared error of \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{nl}}\) is at most one.

This also gives a literal primal certificate.  Let \(\pi\) be any probability vector on \(\Omega_n\), and define
\[
 \delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s)=-\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}s_i.
\]
For an \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-sign schedule, the error on every feasible assignment is the negative average of its exposure-one signs; for an \(e^{\mathrm c}\)-sign schedule, it is the negative average of its constant signs.  In either case its square is at most one.  Hence
\[
 w_{zs}=\pi_z\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s),\qquad
 q_{\alpha z}=\pi_z\bigl(\delta^{\mathrm{bin}}(z,s_{\alpha z})-t_\alpha\bigr)^2,
 \qquad r=1
\]
satisfy the simplex, box, closed-perspective cone, and risk constraints of \(\mathsf P_n\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)\le1.
\]

\paragraph{Dual lower certificate.}
For \(r,t\in\{-1,1\}\), let \(\alpha_{r,t}\) be the \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-sign schedule with
\[
 b_{i,0}=r,\qquad b_{i,1}=t\qquad(i\in V_n),
\]
and put prior mass \(\mu_{\alpha_{r,t}}=1/4\) on each of the four schedules.  All other masses are zero.  For every \(z\in\Omega_n\),
\[
 s_{\alpha_{r,t}z}=r(1,\ldots,1),\qquad
 t_{\alpha_{r,t}}=t-r.
\]
For either nonempty observation cell \(s=r(1,\ldots,1)\), the coordinates in \(\mathsf Q_n\) equal
\[
 a_{zs}=\frac12,\qquad
 b_{zs}=\frac14\sum_{t\in\{-1,1\}}(t-r)=-\frac r2,
 \qquad
 c_{zs}=\frac14\sum_{t\in\{-1,1\}}(t-r)^2=1.
\]
Set \(u_{zs}=1/2\) on these two cells and set \(a_{zs}=b_{zs}=c_{zs}=u_{zs}=0\) elsewhere.  On each nonempty cell,
\[
 a_{zs}u_{zs}=\frac14=b_{zs}^2,
\]
and, for every feasible assignment,
\[
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}(c_{zs}-u_{zs})
 =2\left(1-\frac12\right)=1.
\]
Thus \(v=1\) is feasible in \(\mathsf Q_n\), so \(\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n)\ge1\).  The exact primal--dual identity in \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\) now yields
\[
 \operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_n)=\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q_n)=1,
 \qquad
 L_n(G_n,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{nl}},\bar p)=1.
\]

\paragraph{Optimality under every design.}
Fix any \(\Theta\in\mathcal D_n(\bar p)\) and any measurable estimator \(\widehat\theta\).  Define two bounded, fixed, \(e^{\mathrm{all}}\)-measurable tables \(y^+\) and \(y^-\) by
\[
 h^+_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(0)=h^-_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(0)=0,
 \qquad
 h^+_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(1)=1,
 \qquad
 h^-_{i,e^{\mathrm{all}},n}(1)=-1.
\]
Both tables reveal the identical zero outcome vector on every \(z\in\Omega_n\), but
\[
 \theta_n(y^+)=1,
 \qquad
 \theta_n(y^-)=-1.
\]
Writing \(D_z=\widehat\theta(z,(0,\ldots,0))\), their mean squared errors \(R_+\) and \(R_-\) satisfy
\[
 \frac{R_++R_-}{2}
 =\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\Theta(z)
   \frac{(D_z-1)^2+(D_z+1)^2}{2}
 =\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}\Theta(z)(D_z^2+1)
 \ge1.
\]
Hence every design and every estimator have worst-case mean squared error at least one.  The equal prior on \(y^+\) and \(y^-\) is the two-point decision-theoretic dual certificate; the four sign schedules above are its literal feasible lift to \(\mathsf Q_n\).  Together with the upper bound, this proves that \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{nl}}\) is optimal under every exact-budget design, including degenerate designs.

The proof used only \(m_n\le n-1\) to exclude \(\mathbf 1_n\) from the design support and \(m_n\ge1\) for nondegeneracy.  It used no positivity of individual assignment masses, graph locality, rank condition, or further overlap condition.
