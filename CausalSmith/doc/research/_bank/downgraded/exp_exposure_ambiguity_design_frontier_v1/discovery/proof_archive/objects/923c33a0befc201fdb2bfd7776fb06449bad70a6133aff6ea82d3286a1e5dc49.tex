\textbf{Proof.}
Under \(e^{\mathrm{loc}}\), write
\[
 a=y_1(z)\quad\text{when }z_1=0,
 \qquad
 b=y_1(z)\quad\text{when }z_1=1.
\]
Every other unit has a constant response, so its endpoint contrast is zero.  The constant mapping \(e^{\mathrm c}\) is a subclass of this model.  Hence every table in the union has
\[
 \theta_n(y)=\frac{b-a}{n}. \tag{1}
\]
If \(Z_1=1\), the estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}local\text{-}solved\text{-}menu}\) equals \(b/n\), and its error is \(a/n\).  If \(Z_1=0\), it equals \(-a/n\), and its error is \(-b/n\).  Boundedness gives, pointwise under every assignment,
\[
 \bigl|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{loc}}-\theta_n(y)\bigr|
 \le\frac1n. \tag{2}
\]
Thus its worst-case root mean squared error is at most \(1/n\) under every exact-budget design.

For the matching lower certificate, take a sign prior within the \(e^{\mathrm{loc}}\) model under which \(a\) and \(b\) are independent symmetric signs and all other response coordinates are fixed.  Conditional on any assignment and the entire observed vector, exactly one of \(a,b\) is observed.  The other remains an independent symmetric sign.  By (1), the posterior variance of the target is therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\theta_n(y)\mid Z,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})=\frac1{n^2}. \tag{3}
\]
The conditional squared-loss decomposition into posterior variance plus squared deviation from the posterior mean shows that every estimator has Bayes mean squared error at least \(1/n^2\), under every design, without any assignment-level positivity.  Worst-case risk dominates Bayes risk.  Combining (2) and (3), and invoking \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), gives
\[
 L_n(G_n,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}},\bar p)
 =V_n^\star(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}})=\frac1n.
\]
Thus the displayed estimator is optimal under every exact-budget design. \(\square\)
