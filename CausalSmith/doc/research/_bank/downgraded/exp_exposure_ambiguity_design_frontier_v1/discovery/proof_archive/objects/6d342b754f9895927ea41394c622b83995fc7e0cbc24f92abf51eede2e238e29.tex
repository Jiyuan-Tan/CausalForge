We prove the stronger scope valid at every fixed treatment fraction.  Fix
\(\bar p\in(0,1)\), and let \(B\ge1\) satisfy
\(n=2B\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\).  Put
\[
 m=m_{2B}=\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor.
\]
By the definition of \(\mathcal N_{\bar p}\),
\[
 1\le m\le2B-1. \tag{6}
\]
All potential-outcome coordinates below are fixed before assignment and lie
in \([-1,1]\).  The only randomness is the stated assignment law supported
on \(\Omega_{2B}\).

Let \(u_\star=(1,1)\) be the distinguished unit in the definition of
\(\rho_B\).  The remaining \(2B-1\) units contain a subset \(S_+\) of
size \(m-1\), because \(0\le m-1\le2B-2\) by (6).  The assignment treating
\(\{u_\star\}\cup S_+\) lies in \(\Omega_{2B}\), is not an endpoint, and
has selector value \(10\).  The remaining units also contain a subset
\(S_-\) of size \(m\), because \(1\le m\le2B-1\).  The assignment treating
exactly \(S_-\) lies in \(\Omega_{2B}\), is not an endpoint, and has
selector value \(01\).  Conversely, every exact-budget assignment is a
non-endpoint assignment by (6), so the selector has no other value there.
Therefore
\[
 \rho_B(\Omega_{2B})=\{10,01\}. \tag{7}
\]
This proves selector surjectivity without using \(m=B\).

The two menus have cardinality two by construction.  Moreover,
\(\rho_B(\mathbf 0_{2B})=00\) and
\(\rho_B(\mathbf 1_{2B})=11\).  Every base unit has distinct exposures at
these two endpoint cells under each base mapping, as established directly
from the tables in the proof of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\).  Hence every
replicated unit retains distinct endpoint exposures under every mapping in
either menu.

For \(\kappa\in\{0,-,+\}\), base type \(a\in\{1,2\}\), and endpoint label
\(k\in\{0,1\}\), set
\[
 x^\kappa_{a,k}=e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}(k k),
\]
where \(k k\) is \(00\) for \(k=0\) and \(11\) for \(k=1\).  A physical
assignment \(z\in\Omega_{2B}\) realizes the endpoint exposure attached to
vertex \(((j,a),k)\) exactly when
\[
 e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}(\rho_B(z))=x^\kappa_{a,k}. \tag{8}
\]
By (7), two replicated endpoint vertices \(((j,a),k)\) and
\(((j',a'),k')\) are jointly realizable if and only if some
\(q\in\{10,01\}\) satisfies
\[
 e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}(q)=x^\kappa_{a,k},
 \qquad
 e^{(\kappa)}_{a',2}(q)=x^\kappa_{a',k'}.
\]
Negating this equivalence gives exactly the asserted type-blow-up adjacency
rule for \(H_{2B}(\widetilde e^\kappa)\).  For \(e^{(-)}\), the two
selector cells realize the base endpoint-vertex sets \(\{a,c\}\) and
\(\{b,c\}\); for \(e^{(+)}\), they realize the same two sets in the
opposite order.  Their feasible endpoint-exposure vector sets therefore
coincide, and the identity on all replicated vertices is a labeled graph
isomorphism from \(H_{2B}(\widetilde e^-)\) to
\(H_{2B}(\widetilde e^+)\).  The other two graphs are identical because
both menus contain the same mapping \(\widetilde e^0\).  Thus the two
mapping-wise labeled graph multisets agree.

For the lower-risk menu, replicate schedule \(A\) from
\(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\) in every block
under \(\widetilde e^0\), and replicate schedule \(B\) in every block under
\(\widetilde e^-\).  The two resulting schedules are fixed, bounded, and
menu-measurable.  At both selector values in (7), they produce the same full
observation, namely \(B\) repetitions of \((-1,1)\), while their normalized
targets remain \(1\) and \(-1\).  For any design and estimator, write
\(D_z\) for the estimator value on this common observation at physical
assignment \(z\).  Then
\[
 \mathbb E_{Z\sim\Theta}\!\left[(D_Z-1)^2+(D_Z+1)^2\right]
 =2\mathbb E_{Z\sim\Theta}[D_Z^2]+2\ge2.
\]
Thus every design--estimator pair has worst-case mean squared error at least
one on this menu, and
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-})\ge1. \tag{9}
\]

For the matching upper bound, put all design mass on the physical assignment
constructed above with selector value \(10\).  This point mass is admissible;
the design class imposes support on \(\Omega_{2B}\) but no positive lower
bound on individual assignment probabilities.  For block \(j\), write its
four response coordinates as \((a_j,b_j,c_j,d_j)\).  Under either mapping in
the lower-risk menu, selector value \(10\) reveals \((a_j,c_j)\), and the
block target is
\[
 t_j=\frac{b_j-a_j+d_j-c_j}{2}.
\]
Apply \(\delta_-(10,x)=-(x_1+x_2)/2\) to each block and average the resulting
block estimates.  The block error is
\[
 -\frac{a_j+c_j}{2}-t_j=-\frac{b_j+d_j}{2},
\]
so its absolute value is at most one.  Since
\[
 \theta_{2B}(y)=\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B t_j, \tag{10}
\]
the averaged estimator also has absolute error at most one.  Combining this
upper bound with (9) gives
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-})=1. \tag{11}
\]

For the higher-risk lower bound, draw a base schedule from the eight-point
prior used in the proof of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\), and replicate that
atom identically in every block.  Each resulting schedule is fixed, bounded,
and measurable with respect to either \(\widetilde e^0\) or
\(\widetilde e^+\).  Its normalized target is the base target.  Conditional
on a physical assignment \(z\), its observation is \(B\) repetitions of
the base sign observation at \(q=\rho_B(z)\).  The design is independent of
the fixed prior draw, so knowing the remaining coordinates of \(z\) cannot
alter the posterior distribution of the target after \(q\) and the repeated
observation are known.  The exact posterior-moment calculation in the proof
of \(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\) gives Bayes mean
squared error \(25/16\) separately for \(q=10\) and \(q=01\).  Hence, for
every physical \(z\) and every estimator,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\alpha}\!\left[
   \left\{\widehat\theta\bigl(z,s_{\alpha,\rho_B(z)}^{\otimes B}\bigr)
          -t_\alpha\right\}^2
 \right]\ge\frac{25}{16}. \tag{12}
\]
Averaging (12) under an arbitrary budget-feasible design preserves the
inequality.  Worst-case risk dominates this Bayes-average risk over the
finite replicated subclass, and therefore
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+})\ge\frac54. \tag{13}
\]

For the upper bound, put probability \(1/2\) on each of the two physical
assignments constructed above, one selecting \(10\) and one selecting
\(01\).  These assignments exist by (6), and both design masses are positive.
For each block apply
\[
 \delta_+(10,x)=-\frac34x_2,
 \qquad
 \delta_+(01,x)=-\frac34x_1,
\]
and average the block estimates.  For an arbitrary, possibly block-specific,
bounded response array under either mapping, denote the block estimate by
\(\widehat t_j\) and its block target by \(t_j\).  Pointwise in the common
random selector cell, convexity of the square gives
\[
 \left\{\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)\right\}^2
 \le\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)^2. \tag{14}
\]
The arbitrary-bounded-array calculation in the proof of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\) applies separately
to every block: under the uniform law on the two selector cells,
\[
 \mathbb E[(\widehat t_j-t_j)^2]\le\frac{25}{16}
\]
for both candidate mappings and for every choice of that block's coordinates
in \([-1,1]^4\).  Taking assignment expectation in (14), using (10), and
averaging these blockwise inequalities yields
\[
 \sup_{y\in\mathcal Y_{2B}(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+})}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
   \bigl(\widehat\theta-\theta_{2B}(y)\bigr)^2
 \right]
 \le\frac{25}{16}. \tag{15}
\]
Equations (13) and (15) establish
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+})=\frac54. \tag{16}
\]
The lower bounds (9) and (13) are uniform over all budget-feasible designs
and measurable estimators, while the upper bounds exhibit admissible designs
and explicit estimators.  Thus
\(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\) identifies (11) and (16) with the
corresponding exact finite-program frontiers.

Finally, for fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), if
\[
 B\ge \left\lceil\frac{1}{2\bar p}\right\rceil,
\]
then \(\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor\ge1\); because \(\bar p<1\), one also has
\(\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor\le2B-1\).  Hence every sufficiently large even
size lies in \(\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), and at every such size (11) and (16)
differ by
\[
 \frac54-1=\frac14.
\]
This proves persistence for every fixed treatment fraction in \((0,1)\),
and in particular proves the stated \(\bar p=1/2\) theorem for every
\(B\ge1\).
