\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), let \(B\ge1\) satisfy \(n=2B\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), and put
\[
 m=m_{2B}=\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor.
\]
Admissibility gives
\[
 1\le m\le2B-1. \tag{1}
\]
All potential-outcome coordinates below are fixed before assignment and lie in \([-1,1]\); the only physical randomness is the assignment drawn from a law supported on \(\Omega_{2B}\).

Let \(u_\star=(1,1)\) be the distinguished unit in \(\rho_B\).  Because \(0\le m-1\le2B-2\), treating \(u_\star\) and any \(m-1\) other units produces a feasible nonendpoint assignment with selector \(10\).  Because \(1\le m\le2B-1\), leaving \(u_\star\) untreated and treating any \(m\) of the remaining units produces a feasible nonendpoint assignment with selector \(01\).  Every exact-budget assignment is a nonendpoint by (1), so the selector takes no other values.  Hence
\[
 \rho_B(\Omega_{2B})=\{10,01\}. \tag{2}
\]

The two menus have cardinality two by construction.  Moreover,
\(\rho_B(\mathbf 0_{2B})=00\) and \(\rho_B(\mathbf 1_{2B})=11\).  The base mapping tables have distinct endpoint exposures for every unit, as verified in \(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\); therefore every replicated unit retains distinct endpoint exposures.

For \(\kappa\in\{0,-,+\}\), type \(a\in\{1,2\}\), and endpoint label \(k\in\{0,1\}\), set
\[
 x^\kappa_{a,k}=e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}(kk),
\]
where \(kk\) is \(00\) for \(k=0\) and \(11\) for \(k=1\).  A physical assignment \(z\in\Omega_{2B}\) realizes the endpoint exposure at vertex \(((j,a),k)\) exactly when
\[
 e^{(\kappa)}_{a,2}(\rho_B(z))=x^\kappa_{a,k}. \tag{3}
\]
By (2), two replicated endpoint vertices are jointly realizable if and only if some \(q\in\{10,01\}\) satisfies both corresponding base equalities in (3).  Negating this statement is exactly the asserted type-blow-up adjacency rule.  The base cells for \(e^{(-)}\) realize endpoint-vertex sets \(\{a,c\}\) and \(\{b,c\}\), while those for \(e^{(+)}\) realize the same two sets in the opposite order.  Thus their feasible endpoint-exposure vector sets coincide, and the identity on every replicated vertex is a labeled graph isomorphism.  The shared \(\widetilde e^0\) graphs coincide identically.  Hence the two mapping-wise labeled graph multisets agree.

For the robust lower-risk menu, replicate in every block schedules \(A\) and \(B\) from \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\).  They are respectively measurable under \(\widetilde e^0\) and \(\widetilde e^-\), produce the same full observation at every feasible assignment, and have normalized targets \(1\) and \(-1\).  If \(D_z\) is an arbitrary estimator's value on this common observation, then under every budget-feasible design
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl[(D_Z-1)^2+(D_Z+1)^2\bigr]
 =2\mathbb E[D_Z^2]+2\ge2. \tag{4}
\]
At least one schedule therefore has mean squared error at least one, so
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-})\ge1. \tag{5}
\]
For the reverse inequality, put all mass on a physical assignment with selector \(10\).  In block \(j\), write the response coordinates as \((a_j,b_j,c_j,d_j)\), apply \(\delta_-(10,(a_j,c_j))=-(a_j+c_j)/2\), and average over blocks.  The block target and error are
\[
 t_j=\frac{b_j-a_j+d_j-c_j}{2},
 \qquad
 -\frac{a_j+c_j}{2}-t_j=-\frac{b_j+d_j}{2}.
\]
The block error has absolute value at most one, and
\[
 \theta_{2B}(y)=\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^Bt_j.
\]
The averaged estimator therefore also has absolute error at most one.  Together with (5),
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-})=1. \tag{6}
\]

For the robust higher-risk lower bound, draw a base schedule from the eight-atom prior in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\) and replicate the atom identically in every block.  Conditional on a physical assignment \(z\), the observation is \(B\) copies of the base sign observation at \(q=\rho_B(z)\), and the normalized target is the base target.  The posterior-moment calculation in \(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\) gives Bayes mean squared error \(25/16\) separately at both selector cells.  Details of \(z\) beyond \(q\) are design-fixed and reveal nothing about the independently drawn atom.  Thus every design and estimator has Bayes mean squared error at least \(25/16\), and worst-case risk dominates this finite-prior average:
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+})\ge\frac54. \tag{7}
\]
For the matching upper bound, put probability \(1/2\) on one physical assignment from each selector cell.  Apply \(\delta_+(10,x)=-3x_2/4\) and \(\delta_+(01,x)=-3x_1/4\) blockwise and average.  Pointwise in the common selector cell,
\[
 \left\{\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)\right\}^2
 \le\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)^2. \tag{8}
\]
The full bounded-array calculation in \(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\), equivalently the sign reduction applied to \(\delta_+\), bounds each block's expected square by \(25/16\) under the uniform selector design.  Taking expectations in (8) proves the reverse inequality in (7), so
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+})=\frac54. \tag{9}
\]
The lower bounds (4) and (7) are uniform over every feasible design and estimator; the upper bounds exhibit admissible designs and block-averaged rules for arbitrary block-specific bounded coordinates.  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), (6) and (9) are also the exact finite-program frontiers.

The singleton proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}singleton\text{-}witness\text{-}oracle\text{-}values}\) gives
\[
 \max_{\kappa\in\{0,-\}}V_{2B}^\star(\{\widetilde e^\kappa\})
 =\max_{\kappa\in\{0,+\}}V_{2B}^\star(\{\widetilde e^\kappa\})
 =\frac56. \tag{10}
\]
The oracle benchmark is strictly positive, so the ratio divisions are valid.  Combining (6), (9), and (10) gives
\[
 1-\frac56=\frac16,
 \qquad
 \frac54-\frac56=\frac5{12},
 \qquad
 \frac{1}{5/6}=\frac65,
 \qquad
 \frac{5/4}{5/6}=\frac32. \tag{11}
\]
Thus the robust-risk gap is \(5/4-1=1/4\), while the additive-adaptation-price gap is \(5/12-1/6=1/4\).

Finally, for fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\), if \(B\ge\lceil(2\bar p)^{-1}\rceil\), then \(\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor\ge1\); because \(\bar p<1\), also \(\lfloor2B\bar p\rfloor\le2B-1\).  Every sufficiently large even size therefore belongs to \(\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), and all identities and both gaps persist along those sizes.  No individual-assignment positivity, rank condition, smoothness condition, graph locality, or endpoint support is used. \(\square\)
