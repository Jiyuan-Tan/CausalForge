\textbf{Proof.}
For a table measurable under \(e^{\mathrm{NI}}\), put
\[
 a_i=y_i(\mathbf 0_n),\qquad b_i=y_i(\mathbf 1_n),
 \qquad \tau_i=b_i-a_i.
\]
No interference means that the observed outcome is \(b_i\) when \(Z_i=1\) and \(a_i\) when \(Z_i=0\), and the causal target is \(\bar\tau=n^{-1}\sum_i\tau_i\).  Under complete randomization,
\[
 \mathbb E Z_i=\frac{m_n}{n},qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(Z_i)=\frac{m_n(n-m_n)}{n^2},qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_i,Z_j)
 =-\frac{m_n(n-m_n)}{n^2(n-1)}\quad(i\ne j). \tag{1}
\]
These identities follow by counting the assignments containing one specified unit or two specified units.  They imply that difference in means is unbiased for \(\bar\tau\).  Define finite-population variances and covariance by
\[
 S_b^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(b_i-\bar b)^2,
 \quad
 S_a^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(a_i-\bar a)^2,
 \quad
 S_{ab}=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(a_i-\bar a)(b_i-\bar b).
\]
Substitution of (1) into the variance of the two sample means gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{DM}})
 =\left(\frac1{m_n}-\frac1n\right)S_b^2
  +\left(\frac1{n-m_n}-\frac1n\right)S_a^2
  +\frac{2}{n}S_{ab}. \tag{2}
\]
Since \(S_\tau^2=S_b^2+S_a^2-2S_{ab}\), (2) is equivalently
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{DM}})
 =\frac{S_b^2}{m_n}+\frac{S_a^2}{n-m_n}-\frac{S_\tau^2}{n}. \tag{3}
\]
Boundedness in \([-1,1]\) implies
\[
 S_a^2\le\frac{n}{n-1},
 \qquad
 S_b^2\le\frac{n}{n-1}, \tag{4}
\]
because the variance with denominator \(n\) of numbers in an interval of length two is at most one.  Dropping the nonpositive last term in (3) and using (4) proves
\[
 \sup_{y\in\mathcal Y_n(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}})}
 \operatorname{RMSE}_n(\Theta_n^{\mathrm{CR}},
 \widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{DM}};y)
 \le
 \left\{\frac{n}{n-1}
 \left(\frac1{m_n}+\frac1{n-m_n}\right)\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{5}
\]

For a design-uniform lower bound, put an independent symmetric-sign prior on the \(2n\) fixed endpoint responses \((a_i,b_i)\).  Conditional on any realized exact-budget assignment and all observed outcomes, precisely one endpoint response per unit remains unobserved, and those \(n\) signs remain independent.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\bar\tau\mid Z,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})
 =\frac{n}{n^2}=\frac1n. \tag{6}
\]
The posterior-variance identity for squared loss implies Bayes mean squared error at least \(1/n\) for every design and estimator.  Thus, by \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n}
 \le L_n(G_n,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}},\bar p)
 \le
 \left\{\frac{n}{n-1}
 \left(\frac1{m_n}+\frac1{n-m_n}\right)\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{7}
\]
Since \(m_n/n\to\bar p\in(0,1)\), the upper endpoint in (7) is \(O(n^{-1/2})\), while the lower endpoint is \(n^{-1/2}\).  Hence
\[
 L_n(G_n,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{NI}},\bar p)\asymp n^{-1/2}.
\]
All conditioning is finite, both denominators in difference in means are positive by \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), and no superpopulation randomness is introduced: the prior is used only as a minimax lower certificate over fixed schedules. \(\square\)
