For \(n=2B\) and \(\bar p=1/2\), one has \(m_{2B}=B\), so \(2B\in\mathcal N_{1/2}\).  An exact-budget assignment containing unit \((1,1)\) exists, as does one not containing it; hence \(\rho_B(\Omega_{2B})=\{10,01\}\).  The two endpoints are sent to \(00\) and \(11\), so distinct base endpoint exposures remain distinct for every replicated unit.

For a vertex \(((j,a),k)\), its desired endpoint exposure is the desired endpoint exposure of base vertex \((a,k)\).  Two replicated vertices are jointly realized by a physical assignment exactly when some \(q\in\rho_B(\Omega_{2B})=\{10,01\}\) realizes both corresponding base exposures.  This proves the stated type-blow-up description of every conflict graph.  At the base level, both \(e^{(-)}\) and \(e^{(+)}\) have feasible realized endpoint-vertex sets \(\{a,c\}\) and \(\{b,c\}\).  Thus their blow-ups agree under the identity on \(((j,a),k)\); the \(\widetilde e^0\) graphs are literally shared.  The graph multisets consequently agree for every \(B\).

For the lower-risk menu, replicate arrays \(A\) and \(B\) identically in every block.  Every physical feasible assignment then produces the repeated common base observation, while the normalized targets remain \(1\) and \(-1\).  The pointwise two-schedule calculation in \(\mathrm{thm{:}minimal\text{-}nonrepresentability}\) gives mean squared error at least one for every design and estimator.  For the higher-risk menu, replicate every atom of the eight-point prior identically across blocks.  Conditional on \(\rho_B(Z)=q\), the repeated observation contains exactly the same information as its base observation, and the normalized target equals the base target.  The two conditional Bayes risks computed there are both \(25/16\), so every physical exact-budget design has Bayes mean squared error at least \(25/16\).

For the matching upper bounds, choose physical assignments realizing precisely the base design: one assignment with \(\rho_B(z)=10\) for the lower-risk menu, and one assignment of each selector value with probability \(1/2\) for the higher-risk menu.  For arbitrary block-specific bounded response coordinates, apply the corresponding base rule to the two observed outcomes in block \(j\), obtaining \(\widehat t_j\), and return \(B^{-1}\sum_j\widehat t_j\).  The global target is \(B^{-1}\sum_jt_j\).  Pointwise Jensen gives
\[
 \left(\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)\right)^2
 \le\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)^2.
\]
For \(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-}\), every summand is at most one at the selected base cell.  For \(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+}\), averaging also over the two selector values makes every block's mean squared error at most \(25/16\).  The two lower bounds therefore match these upper bounds, proving
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,-})=1,
 \qquad
 V_{2B}^\star(\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{B,+})=\frac54
\]
for every \(B\ge1\).
