The budget calculation is immediate: for \(\bar p\in[1/2,1)\), \(m_2=\lfloor2\bar p\rfloor=1\), whereas no nondegenerate integer budget exists for \(n=1\).  The tables in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}mappings}\) show directly that each unit has endpoint exposures zero and one under all three mappings.

Let \(a=(1,0)\), \(b=(1,1)\), \(c=(2,0)\), and \(d=(2,1)\).  On the feasible assignments \(10,01\), the endpoint vertices jointly realized under \(e^{(0)}\) are respectively \(\{a,c\}\) and \(\{a,d\}\).  Its conflict edges are therefore \(\{ab,bc,bd,cd\}\).  Under either \(e^{(-)}\) or \(e^{(+)}\), the two jointly realized sets are \(\{a,c\}\) and \(\{b,c\}\), in an order that is immaterial; the conflict edges are \(\{ab,ad,bd,cd\}\).  This proves the asserted identity isomorphisms and equality of the two graph multisets.

For \(\mathcal E_-\), the two arrays \(A,B\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\) produce the same outcome \((-1,1)\) at both feasible assignments but have targets \(1\) and \(-1\).  If an arbitrary rule returns \(r_z\) on this common observation at assignment \(z\), then
\[
 (r_z-1)^2+(r_z+1)^2=2r_z^2+2\ge2.
\]
Averaging over any design implies that one of the two mean squared errors is at least one.  Conversely, write the four response coordinates under either mapping as \((a,b,c,d)\in[-1,1]^4\).  Both have target \((b-a+d-c)/2\) and observation \((a,c)\) at \(z=10\).  The rule \(\delta_-(10,x)=-(x_1+x_2)/2\) has error
\[
 -\frac{a+c}{2}-\frac{b-a+d-c}{2}=-\frac{b+d}{2},
\]
whose absolute value is at most one.  Thus the exact minimax root mean squared error is one.

For \(\mathcal E_+\), consider the eight atoms and masses in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}arrays}\).  Their masses sum to
\[
 2\left(\frac{15}{112}+\frac5{32}+\frac5{224}+\frac3{16}\right)=1.
\]
At \(z=10\), the two observation cells each have mass \(1/2\), unnormalized first target moment of magnitude \(3/8\), and unnormalized second target moment \(17/16\).  Their total posterior variance is therefore
\[
 2\left(\frac{17}{16}-\frac{(3/8)^2}{1/2}\right)=\frac{25}{16}.
\]
At \(z=01\), two cells have \((a,|b|,c)=(9/28,27/112,99/112)\), and two have \((a,|b|,c)=(5/28,15/112,5/28)\).  Direct substitution gives
\[
 2\left(\frac{99}{112}-\frac{(27/112)^2}{9/28}\right)
 +2\left(\frac5{28}-\frac{(15/112)^2}{5/28}\right)
 =\frac{25}{16}.
\]
Thus the Bayes mean squared error is \(25/16\) under either feasible assignment, so it is at least \(25/16\) under every design and for every estimator.

For the matching upper bound, use the uniform design and \(\delta_+(10,x)=-3x_2/4\), \(\delta_+(01,x)=-3x_1/4\).  For either candidate mapping, the average squared error is a convex function of the four response coordinates.  Repeatedly maximizing a convex function over each interval \([-1,1]\) reduces its maximum to the sixteen sign vertices.  Direct substitution at those vertices gives squared-error averages \(1/16\) at four vertices, \(9/16\) at six vertices, and \(25/16\) at six vertices, for each mapping.  Hence the worst value is \(25/16\), and \(\mathrm{lem{:}sign\text{-}reduction}\) gives the same bound for all bounded arrays.  The exact root mean squared error is therefore \(5/4\).  The two exact values differ although the mapping-wise labeled conflict-graph multisets agree, proving nonrepresentability.
