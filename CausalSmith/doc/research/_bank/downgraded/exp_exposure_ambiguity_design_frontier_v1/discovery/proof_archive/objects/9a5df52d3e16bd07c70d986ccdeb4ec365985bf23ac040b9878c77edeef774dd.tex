For fixed \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and \(n\in\mathcal N_{\bar p}\), \(\mathsf Q_n\) has complete primitive data \((n,\mathcal E,\bar p)\) through \(\Omega_n\), \(\mathcal B_n(\mathcal E)\), \(\mathcal S_n\), \(s_{\alpha z}\), and \(t_\alpha\). It maximizes \(v\) over \(\mu_\alpha\ge0\), \(a_{zs}\ge0\), \(b_{zs}\in\mathbb R\), \(c_{zs}\ge0\), and \(u_{zs}\ge0\), subject to \(\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha=1\), \(a_{zs}=\sum_{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s}\mu_\alpha\), \(b_{zs}=\sum_{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s}\mu_\alpha t_\alpha\), \(c_{zs}=\sum_{\alpha:s_{\alpha z}=s}\mu_\alpha t_\alpha^2\), \(a_{zs}u_{zs}\ge b_{zs}^2\), and \(v\le\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_n}(c_{zs}-u_{zs})\) for every \(z\in\Omega_n\).
