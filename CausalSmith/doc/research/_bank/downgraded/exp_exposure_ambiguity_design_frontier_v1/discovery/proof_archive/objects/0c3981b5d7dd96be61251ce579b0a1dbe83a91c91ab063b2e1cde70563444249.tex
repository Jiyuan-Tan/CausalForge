Under \(e^{\mathrm{loc}}\), write the two response coordinates of unit \(1\) as \(a=h_{1,e^{\mathrm{loc}},n}(0)\) and \(b=h_{1,e^{\mathrm{loc}},n}(1)\).  All other units have constant potential outcomes and hence contribute zero to the endpoint contrast, so
\[
 \theta_n(y)=\frac{b-a}{n}.
\]
If \(Z_1=1\), the estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}local\text{-}solved\text{-}menu}\) returns \(b/n\) and has error \(a/n\).  If \(Z_1=0\), it returns \(-a/n\) and has error \(-b/n\).  In either case the squared error is at most \(1/n^2\).  Under \(e^{\mathrm c}\), the target is zero and the estimator has absolute value at most \(1/n\).  Thus this rule has worst-case root mean squared error at most \(1/n\) under every exact-budget design.

For the matching lower bound, remain within the \(e^{\mathrm{loc}}\) subclass and put independent symmetric signs on \(a\) and \(b\), fixing all other response coordinates.  Conditional on any realized assignment and observation, exactly one of \(a,b\) is observed and the other remains an independent symmetric sign.  The posterior variance of \((b-a)/n\) is therefore exactly \(1/n^2\).  Hence every estimator under every design has worst-case mean squared error at least \(1/n^2\).  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}saddle}\), this common minimax value is the primal and dual value, proving \(L_n(G_n,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{loc}},\bar p)=1/n\) and optimality of the displayed estimator under every exact-budget design.
