\textbf{Proof.}
Write the four response coordinates of a base mapping, in endpoint order, as
\[
 (a,b,c,d)=\bigl(h_1(0),h_1(1),h_2(0),h_2(1)\bigr)\in[-1,1]^4,
 \qquad
 t=\frac{b-a+d-c}{2}.
\]
The mapping tables in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimal\text{-}witness\text{-}mappings}\) give the following observations at the two feasible selector cells:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &10&01\\ \hline
 e^{(0)}&(a,c)&(a,d)\\
 e^{(-)}&(a,c)&(b,c)\\
 e^{(+)}&(b,c)&(a,c).
\end{array} \tag{1}
\]
Use probability \(1/2\) on each cell.  On sign observations define the three rules
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &10&01\\ \hline
 e^{(0)}&-a/2-2c/3&-a/2+2d/3\\
 e^{(-)}&-2a/3-c/2&2b/3-c/2\\
 e^{(+)}&2b/3-c/2&-2a/3-c/2.
\end{array} \tag{2}
\]
Direct substitution at the sixteen vertices \((a,b,c,d)\in\{-1,1\}^4\), in lexicographic order
\[
 ----,---+,--+-,--++,-+--,-+-+,-++-,-+++,
 +---,+--+,+-+-,+-++,++--,++-+,+++-,++++,
\]
gives the following mean squared errors, each row divided by \(36\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrrrrrrrrr}
 e^{(0)}&25&1&25&25&25&25&1&25&25&1&25&25&25&25&1&25\\
 e^{(-)}&25&25&25&25&1&25&1&25&25&1&25&1&25&25&25&25\\
 e^{(+)}&25&25&25&25&1&25&1&25&25&1&25&1&25&25&25&25.
\end{array} \tag{3}
\]
Thus every row has maximum \(25/36\).  The three rules in (2) are the Bernoulli multilinear extensions of their sign restrictions, because each is affine in the observed coordinates.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}sign\text{-}reduction}\), (3) therefore yields, for every bounded response array under the corresponding singleton mapping,
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl[(\widehat t-t)^2\bigr]\le\frac{25}{36}.
 \tag{4}
\]
This proves the three upper bounds \(V_2^\star(\{e^{(\kappa)}\})\le5/6\).

We next verify matching lower bounds.  Use the four-atom priors
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{mapping}&\text{mass}&(a,b,c,d)\\ \hline
e^{(0)}&7/18&(-1,-1,1,-1)\\
       &5/18&(-1,1,-1,-1)\\
       &1/18&(-1,1,-1,1)\\
       &5/18&(-1,1,1,1)\\ \hline
e^{(-)}&5/18&(-1,-1,-1,1)\\
       &1/18&(-1,1,-1,1)\\
       &7/18&(1,-1,-1,-1)\\
       &5/18&(1,1,-1,1)\\ \hline
e^{(+)}&5/18&(-1,-1,-1,-1)\\
       &7/18&(-1,1,-1,1)\\
       &1/18&(1,-1,-1,-1)\\
       &5/18&(1,1,-1,-1).
\end{array} \tag{5}
\]
Every row group in (5) has total mass one.  For a selector cell \(q\) and sign observation \(s\), let \(A_{qs}\), \(B_{qs}\), and \(C_{qs}\) denote the cell mass and the unnormalized first and second moments of \(t\).  Define the four exact moment tuples
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L&=(2/3,-1/9,2/3,35/54),
 &S&=(1/3,7/18,1/2,5/108),\\
 L_+&=(2/3,7/9,14/9,35/54),
 &S_+&=(1/3,-1/18,1/18,5/108),
 \end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
where the four entries are \((A_{qs},B_{qs},C_{qs},C_{qs}-B_{qs}^2/A_{qs})\).  Substitution of (5) into (1) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|cc}
\text{mapping}&q&\text{first observed cell}&\text{second observed cell}\\ \hline
e^{(0)}&10&(-1,1):L&(-1,-1):S\\
e^{(0)}&01&(-1,-1):L&(-1,1):S\\
e^{(-)}&10&(-1,-1):S&(1,-1):L\\
e^{(-)}&01&(-1,-1):L&(1,-1):S\\
e^{(+)}&10&(-1,-1):S_+&(1,-1):L_+\\
e^{(+)}&01&(-1,-1):L_+&(1,-1):S_+.
\end{array} \tag{7}
\]
All displayed masses are positive, so every division in (6) is valid.  The posterior means \(B_{qs}/A_{qs}\) in (7) are exactly the values of the corresponding rule in (2).  Moreover, separately for every mapping and every selector cell, the posterior Bayes mean squared error is
\[
 \sum_s\left(C_{qs}-\frac{B_{qs}^2}{A_{qs}}\right)
 =\frac{35}{54}+\frac{5}{108}
 =\frac{25}{36}. \tag{8}
\]
For squared loss, the conditional identity
\[
 \mathbb E[(u-t)^2\mid q,s]
 =\operatorname{Var}(t\mid q,s)
  +\bigl(u-\mathbb E[t\mid q,s]\bigr)^2
\]
shows that no estimator can have smaller Bayes risk.  Since (8) holds separately at both feasible assignments, it remains \(25/36\) under every design on \(\Omega_2=\{10,01\}\), including boundary designs with one zero mass.  Worst-case risk dominates Bayes-average risk over the finite sign subclass.  Hence
\[
 V_2^\star(\{e^{(\kappa)}\})\ge\frac56
 \qquad(\kappa\in\{0,-,+\}). \tag{9}
\]
Combining (4) and (9) proves all three two-unit identities.  Taking the two displayed maxima gives the asserted base-menu equality.

Now fix \(\bar p\in(0,1)\) and an admissible \(n=2B\).  The exact-budget inequalities \(1\le m_{2B}\le2B-1\) imply that there is a feasible physical assignment with selector \(10\) and one with selector \(01\): for the first, treat the distinguished unit and any \(m_{2B}-1\) others; for the second, leave the distinguished unit untreated and treat any \(m_{2B}\) of the remaining units.  Thus
\[
 \rho_B(\Omega_{2B})=\{10,01\}. \tag{10}
\]
For the lower bound, draw one atom from the appropriate prior in (5) and replicate that same atom in every block.  The normalized \(2B\)-unit target equals its base target, and, conditional on any physical assignment \(z\), the observed vector consists of \(B\) copies of the base observation at \(q=\rho_B(z)\).  The remaining coordinates of \(z\) are fixed by the design and reveal nothing about the independently chosen prior atom.  Equation (8), separately at each value in (10), therefore gives Bayes mean squared error \(25/36\) under every budget-feasible design and every estimator.  Hence
\[
 V_{2B}^\star(\{\widetilde e^\kappa\})\ge\frac56. \tag{11}
\]

For the upper bound, put probability \(1/2\) on one physical assignment from each selector cell in (10).  In block \(j\), apply the rule in (2) for the known mapping to its possibly block-specific bounded coordinates, and average the \(B\) block estimates.  If \(\widehat t_j\) and \(t_j\) are the block estimate and block target, then pointwise in the common selector cell,
\[
 \left\{\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)\right\}^2
 \le\frac1B\sum_{j=1}^B(\widehat t_j-t_j)^2. \tag{12}
\]
The left side is the squared error for \(\theta_{2B}(y)=B^{-1}\sum_jt_j\).  By (4), each term on the right has expectation at most \(25/36\) under the uniform selector design.  Taking expectations in (12) gives the reverse inequality in (11).  Therefore every replicated singleton risk is exactly \(5/6\), and taking the two displayed maxima proves the asserted replicated-menu equality.  No assignment-level positivity beyond the two exhibited design masses, no rank condition, and no graph-locality assumption is used. \(\square\)
