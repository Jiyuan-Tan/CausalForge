We first prove the universal clipping bound. If the selector chooses candidate \(k\), then \(\widehat h\in\mathcal H_k\). Write \(\widehat h=\sum_j\widehat\theta_j\phi_{kj}\). The bounded-basis and coefficient-radius conditions included in \(\mathcal M_n\) give
\[
 \|\widehat h\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le\|\widehat h\|_{L_\infty(P_X)}
 \le\sum_j|\widehat\theta_j|\,\|\phi_{kj}\|_{L_\infty(P_X)}
 \le B.
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:operator-contraction}\), \(\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1\). Hence the positive operator \(T^*T\) has operator norm at most one. Spectral functional calculus, \(\texttt{ass:source-representation}\), and \(\texttt{ass:source-radius}\) yield
\[
 \|h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 =\|(T^*T)^{\beta/2}w\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le\|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le B.
\]
The triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 \le\bigl(\|\widehat h\|_{L_2(P_X)}+\|h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}\bigr)^2
 \le4B^2.
\]

We next construct a counterexample to every sharper universal oracle inequality. It suffices to use the subsequence \(n=3m\) and deterministic splits with \(n_{\mathrm{cr}}=n_{\mathrm{tr}}=n_{\mathrm{va}}=m\). Let \(\mathcal X=\mathcal Z=\{1,2\}\), define
\[
 P(X=Z=1)=P(X=Z=2)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 e_j(x)=\mathbf 1\{x=j\},
\]
and put \(b=1/4\), \(Y=b e_2(X)\), \(B=2\), and \(\eta=1/4\). Then \(|Y|\le B\). Because \(Z=X\), the conditional-expectation operator is the identity on \(L_2(P_X)\). Consequently,
\[
 r=b e_2,
 \qquad
 h^\dagger=b e_2,
 \qquad
 q_{h^\dagger}=0.
\]
For the prescribed \(\beta\in(0,2]\), choose \(w=h^\dagger\). Since \(T^*T=I\),
\[
 h^\dagger=(T^*T)^{\beta/2}w,
 \qquad
 \|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}=\frac{b}{\sqrt2}<B,
\]
so both source assumptions hold.

Let \(\mathcal K=\{1,2\}\), set \(d_{H,1}=d_{H,2}=2\), and use \(\phi_{k1}=e_1\) and \(\phi_{k2}=e_2\) for both candidates. Define
\[
 \mathcal H_1=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_2=\{c e_2:0\le c\le b\},
 \qquad
 J_1=\{2\},
 \qquad
 J_2=\{1\}.
\]
Enumerate \(0\) first in both target classes and then enumerate the rational points densely in \(\mathcal H_2\). Take \(\mathcal A_0=\{0\}\), and set every learned image equal to its ideal image:
\[
 \widehat g_{kj}=g_{kj}=e_j.
\]
The bases are bounded by one; the target classes are compact, convex, contain the origin, and have coefficient \(\ell_1\)-radius at most \(b<B\); the candidate and audit spans are finite-dimensional; the stipulated enumerations are dense; exact images are estimable with radius zero; and the least-squares inverse is attained. Thus the resulting configurations belong to \(\mathcal M_n\). Exact images also imply
\[
 \alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n}=0.
\]

Candidate \(2\) has zero target approximation error because \(h^\dagger\in\mathcal H_2\). For \(h=c e_2\in\mathcal H_2\),
\[
 q_h-q_{h^\dagger}=(b-c)e_2.
\]
The ideal common audit contains every \(u e_2\) with \(|u|\le B\), through candidate \(1\). In particular, it contains \(q_h-q_{h^\dagger}\). For arbitrary \(v,f\in L_2(P_Z)\), completing the square gives
\[
 2E_P[v(Z)f(Z)]-E_P[f(Z)^2]
 =\|v\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2-\|v-f\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2
 \le\|v\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2.
\]
Taking \(v=q_h-q_{h^\dagger}\) and choosing \(f=v\) attains equality. Hence
\[
 \varepsilon_{H,2}=\varepsilon_{A,2}=\alpha_{2,n}=0.
\]
Thus candidate \(k_0=2\) is a zero-defect benchmark according to every term in the proposed oracle scale.

Its own training critic is nevertheless blind to the entire nonzero target direction. Indeed, for \(h=c e_2\in\mathcal H_2\) and \(f=u e_1\in\widehat{\mathcal F}_2\), disjoint support gives pointwise
\[
 (Y-h(X))f(Z)=(b-c)u e_2(X)e_1(X)=0.
\]
Therefore the empirical adversarial part of the training score is
\[
 \sup_{|u|\le B}\{-u^2P_{\mathrm{tr}}e_1\}=0,
\]
and the full training score is \(\lambda c^2P_{\mathrm{tr}}e_2\ge0\). Its value at \(c=0\) is exactly zero. Because \(0\) is enumerated first, the least-index rule selects
\[
 \widehat h_{2,\lambda}=0
 \qquad\text{for every }\lambda\in\Lambda_n.
\]
Candidate \(1\) contains only the zero target, so \(\widehat h_{1,\lambda}=0\) as well. Validation may choose among indices, but every available fitted function is zero. Consequently,
\[
 \widehat h=0\quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 =\|b e_2\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 =\frac{b^2}{2}
 =\frac1{32}.
\]

The population training critic from \(\texttt{def:training-coverage-defect}\) is
\[
 \mathcal F_2^{\mathrm{tr}}=\{u e_1:|u|\le B\}.
\]
For \(h=c e_2\), orthogonality of \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) implies
\[
 \sup_{f\in\mathcal F_2^{\mathrm{tr}}}
 \left\{2E_P[(q_h-q_{h^\dagger})f]-E_P[f^2]\right\}=0,
\]
with the supremum attained at \(f=0\). Since
\[
 \|q_h-q_{h^\dagger}\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2=\frac{(b-c)^2}{2},
\]
the supremum over \(0\le c\le b\) is attained at \(c=0\). Therefore
\[
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr},2}^2=\frac{b^2}{2}=\frac1{32}.
\]
This is precisely the training blindness not charged by \(\varepsilon_{H,2}\), \(\varepsilon_{A,2}\), or \(\alpha_{2,n}\).

It remains to show that the proposed oracle expression vanishes. For candidate \(2\), the only nonzero terms in \(s_{2,n}\) are the finite-dimensional training term and the validation term. Here \(d_{H,2}=d_A=K=2\), \(|\Lambda_n|=1+\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\), and \(n_{\mathrm{tr}}=n_{\mathrm{va}}=n/3\). Hence, for each fixed finite \(C\),
\[
 s_{2,n}
 =O_C\!\left(
 \frac{\log\log n}{n}
 +\sqrt{\frac{\log\log n}{n}}
 \right)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Let \(\rho_n=s_{2,n}^{1/(1+\bar\beta)}\). The positive validation term implies \(n\rho_n\to\infty\). The smallest point of \(\Lambda_n\) is \(2^{-\lceil\log_2n\rceil}\le n^{-1}\). Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the geometric grid contains \(\lambda_n\) satisfying
\[
 \frac{\rho_n}{2}\le\lambda_n\le\rho_n.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \inf_{k\in\mathcal K,\lambda\in\Lambda_n}
 \left\{\lambda^{\bar\beta}+\frac{s_{k,n}}{\lambda}\right\}
 \le \lambda_n^{\bar\beta}+\frac{s_{2,n}}{\lambda_n}
 \le3s_{2,n}^{\bar\beta/(1+\bar\beta)}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
For every fixed finite \(C\), the proposed sharpened right-hand side is eventually strictly smaller than \(1/32\), while the left-hand side equals \(1/32\) for every sample. The claimed event then has probability zero, rather than at least \(1-\eta=3/4\). This proves the impossibility statement. The defect calculation identifies the obstruction: validation observes the \(e_2\) residual through candidate \(1\)'s audit coordinate, but neither candidate's fitted path contains a nonzero estimate learned from a critic that sees that coordinate.
