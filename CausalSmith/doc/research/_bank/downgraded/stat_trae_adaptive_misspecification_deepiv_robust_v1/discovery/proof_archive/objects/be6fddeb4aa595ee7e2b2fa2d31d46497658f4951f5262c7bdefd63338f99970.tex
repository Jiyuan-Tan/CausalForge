\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathscr C=\sigma(W_i:i\in I_{\mathrm{cr}})\). Work conditionally on \(\mathscr C\), so every learned image \(\widehat g_{kj}\) is fixed.

Fix \((k,\lambda,\ell)\). Parameterize \(\widehat{\mathcal F}_k\) by the compact coefficient set
\[
 \mathbb B_{1,k}=\left\{u\in\mathbb R^{|J_k|}:\sum_{j\in J_k}|u_j|\le B\right\}.
\]
For fixed training data, the expression inside the supremum defining \(A_{k,\lambda,\ell}\) is a continuous quadratic polynomial in \(u\). Its supremum over \(\mathbb B_{1,k}\) therefore equals its supremum over a fixed countable dense subset of \(\mathbb B_{1,k}\). At each point of that countable subset the expression is a finite sum of measurable random variables. Hence \(A_{k,\lambda,\ell}\) is measurable as a countable supremum of measurable functions.

Write
\[
 a_{k,\lambda}=\inf_{m\ge1}A_{k,\lambda,m}.
\]
This is a countable infimum of measurable functions. Every score is finite almost surely. It is also nonnegative: the critic body contains \(0\), so its dual supremum is nonnegative, and the regularization term is nonnegative. Therefore
\[
 N_{k,\lambda}=\left\{\ell\ge1:A_{k,\lambda,\ell}\le a_{k,\lambda}+n^{-1}\right\}
\]
is nonempty by the defining property of an infimum. If \(L_{k,\lambda}=\min N_{k,\lambda}\), then, for every \(m\ge1\),
\[
 \{L_{k,\lambda}=m\}
 =\{A_{k,\lambda,m}\le a_{k,\lambda}+n^{-1}\}
 \cap\bigcap_{j<m}\{A_{k,\lambda,j}>a_{k,\lambda}+n^{-1}\}.
\]
Thus \(L_{k,\lambda}\) is measurable. The fixed enumeration then makes \(\widehat h_{k,\lambda}=h_{k,L_{k,\lambda}}\) measurable, and its definition gives
\[
 A_{k,\lambda,L_{k,\lambda}}
 \le \inf_{m\ge1}A_{k,\lambda,m}+n^{-1}.
\]

For validation, conditional on \(\mathscr C\), the span of \(\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\mathrm A}\) is finite-dimensional: under \(\mathcal M_n\), the fixed audit dictionary has finite-dimensional span, and there are finitely many learned images. The closure of its bounded absolute convex coefficient body is compact. For fixed validation data the validation quadratic is continuous in those finite-dimensional coefficients, and taking the closure does not change its supremum. A countable dense subset of the compact coefficient set therefore represents each \(\widehat V_{k,\lambda}\) as a countable supremum of measurable finite-sample quadratics. Each validation score is measurable. Finally, \(\mathcal K\times\Lambda_n\) is finite, so the lexicographically least minimizer of these finitely many measurable scores is measurable. The deterministic fallback is measurable as well and is not reached on the almost-sure event on which the displayed scores are finite.
\end{proof}
