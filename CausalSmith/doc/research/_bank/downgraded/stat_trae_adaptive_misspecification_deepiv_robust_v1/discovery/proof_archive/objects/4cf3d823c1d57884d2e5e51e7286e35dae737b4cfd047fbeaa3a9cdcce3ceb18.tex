\begin{proof}
The revised witness proves sample by sample that
\[
 \widehat h_{k,\lambda}=0
 \quad\text{for every }(k,\lambda)\in\mathcal K\times\Lambda_n.
\]
Thus the set of already-trained path elements is the singleton \(\{0\}\). Any measurable map valued in this set equals zero, irrespective of whether its index is chosen using the sample, the population law, auxiliary randomness, or all three.

More generally, let \(\omega_{k,\lambda}\) be arbitrary finite real-valued coefficients. They may be random and may depend on the data, on \(P\), or on both. Pointwise,
\[
 \sum_{k\in\mathcal K,\lambda\in\Lambda_n}
 \omega_{k,\lambda}\widehat h_{k,\lambda}
 =\sum_{k,\lambda}\omega_{k,\lambda}0
 =0.
\]
This proves the assertion for every signed linear combination. It also proves it for every affine combination, for which the additional restriction \(\sum_{k,\lambda}\omega_{k,\lambda}=1\) does not change the calculation.

By \(\texttt{lem:parameterized-blind-path-witness}\), the resulting squared strong error is
\[
 \|0-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2=\gamma B^2.
\]
For any \(\rho\in(0,B^2]\), choosing \(\gamma=\rho/B^2\in(0,1]\) gives error exactly \(\rho\). The construction always retains its two support atoms with probabilities \(1/2\) and \(1/2\), including when \(\rho=B^2\). The witness's maximality conclusion shows that the source conditions imply \(\|h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2\le B^2\), so a procedure equal to zero cannot have larger squared strong error. Every operation considered here combines only existing zero path elements; the argument makes no assertion about a procedure that refits a candidate or introduces a new function outside that path.
\end{proof}
