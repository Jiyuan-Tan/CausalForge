We first prove the universal clipping bound. If the selector chooses candidate \(k\), then \(\widehat h\in\mathcal H_k\). Write \(\widehat h=\sum_j\widehat\theta_j\phi_{kj}\). The bounded-basis and coefficient-radius conditions in \(\mathcal M_n\) imply
\[
 \|\widehat h\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le\|\widehat h\|_{L_\infty(P_X)}
 \le\sum_j|\widehat\theta_j|\,\|\phi_{kj}\|_{L_\infty(P_X)}
 \le B.
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:operator-contraction}\), \(\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1\), so the positive operator \(T^*T\) has operator norm at most one. Functional calculus and the source representation give
\[
 \|h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 =\|(T^*T)^{\beta/2}w\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le\|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le B.
\]
The triangle inequality therefore yields
\[
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 \le(\|\widehat h\|_{L_2(P_X)}+\|h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)})^2
 \le4B^2.
\]

We now construct a counterexample to every sharper universal oracle inequality. It is enough to use the subsequence \(n=3m\) and splits of size \(m\). Let \(\mathcal X=\mathcal Z=\{1,2\}\), let
\[
 P(X=Z=1)=P(X=Z=2)=\tfrac12,
 \qquad e_j(x)=\mathbf 1\{x=j\},
\]
and fix \(b=1/4\). Set \(Y=b e_2(X)\), \(B=2\), and \(\eta=1/4\). Because \(Z=X\), the conditional-expectation operator is the identity on \(L_2(P_X)\). Moreover,
\[
 r=b e_2,
 \qquad h^\dagger=b e_2,
 \qquad q_{h^\dagger}=0.
\]
For the prescribed \(\beta\), take \(w=h^\dagger\); then \(h^\dagger=(T^*T)^{\beta/2}w\) and \(\|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}=b/\sqrt2<B\).

Let \(\mathcal K=\{1,2\}\), set \(d_{H,1}=d_{H,2}=2\), and use \(\phi_{k1}=e_1\), \(\phi_{k2}=e_2\) for both candidates. Define
\[
 \mathcal H_1=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_2=\{c e_2:0\le c\le b\},
 \qquad
 J_1=\{2\},
 \qquad
 J_2=\{1\}.
\]
Enumerate \(0\) first in each target class and then enumerate the rational points densely in \(\mathcal H_2\). Take \(\mathcal A_0=\{0\}\) and set every learned image equal to its ideal image, \(\widehat g_{kj}=g_{kj}=e_j\). All basis, compactness, convexity, origin, coefficient-radius, finite-dimensionality, measurability, image-estimability, and pseudo-true-attainment conditions in \(\mathcal M_n\) are immediate. The outcome is bounded by \(B\), and exact images give \(\alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n}=0\).

Candidate \(2\) has zero target approximation error because \(h^\dagger\in\mathcal H_2\). Its residual difference at \(h=c e_2\) is
\[
 q_h-q_{h^\dagger}=(b-c)e_2.
\]
The ideal common audit contains every \(u e_2\) with \(|u|\le B\), through candidate \(1\). Hence \(q_h-q_{h^\dagger}\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm A}\). For any \(v\in L_2(P_Z)\), completing the square gives
\[
 2E_P[vf]-E_P[f^2]=\|v\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2-\|v-f\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2
 \le\|v\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2.
\]
Choosing \(f=v=q_h-q_{h^\dagger}\) attains equality. It follows that
\[
 \varepsilon_{H,2}=\varepsilon_{A,2}=\alpha_{2,n}=0.
\]
Thus candidate \(2\) is a zero-defect benchmark according to the proposed oracle scale.

Nevertheless, its training critic is blind to its entire nonzero target direction. Indeed, for \(h=c e_2\in\mathcal H_2\) and \(f=u e_1\in\widehat{\mathcal F}_2\), disjoint support gives pointwise
\[
 (Y-h(X))f(Z)=(b-c)u e_2(X)e_1(X)=0.
\]
Therefore its empirical adversarial term is
\[
 \sup_{|u|\le B}\{-u^2P_{\mathrm{tr}}e_1\}=0,
\]
and its complete training score is \(\lambda c^2P_{\mathrm{tr}}e_2\ge0\). The score at \(c=0\) is exactly zero, so the least-index rule selects \(\widehat h_{2,\lambda}=0\) for every \(\lambda\). Candidate \(1\) has only the zero target, hence \(\widehat h_{1,\lambda}=0\) as well. Validation can choose among indices but all their fitted functions are zero. Consequently
\[
 \widehat h=0\quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 =\|b e_2\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 =\frac{b^2}{2}>0.
\]

For candidate \(2\), the only nonzero terms in \(s_{2,n}\) are the finite-dimensional training term and validation term. Because \(d_{H,2}=d_A=K=2\), \(|\Lambda_n|=1+\lceil\log_2n\rceil\), and both relevant split sizes equal \(n/3\),
\[
 s_{2,n}
 =O_C\!\left(\frac{\log\log n}{n}
 +\sqrt{\frac{\log\log n}{n}}\right)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Let \(\rho_n=s_{2,n}^{1/(1+\bar\beta)}\). The positive validation term also implies \(n\rho_n\to\infty\). Since the smallest grid point is at most \(n^{-1}\), for all sufficiently large \(n\) there is a grid point \(\lambda_n\) with \(\rho_n/2\le\lambda_n\le\rho_n\). Therefore
\[
 \inf_{k,\lambda}
 \left\{\lambda^{\bar\beta}+\frac{s_{k,n}}{\lambda}\right\}
 \le \lambda_n^{\bar\beta}+\frac{s_{2,n}}{\lambda_n}
 \le3s_{2,n}^{\bar\beta/(1+\bar\beta)}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
For every fixed finite \(C\), the proposed sharpened right-hand side is eventually strictly smaller than \(b^2/2\), whereas the left-hand side equals \(b^2/2\) for every sample. Its claimed event then has probability zero, not at least \(3/4\). This proves the impossibility statement and isolates the missing ingredient: validation detects the blind direction, but no fitted path is capable of learning it.
