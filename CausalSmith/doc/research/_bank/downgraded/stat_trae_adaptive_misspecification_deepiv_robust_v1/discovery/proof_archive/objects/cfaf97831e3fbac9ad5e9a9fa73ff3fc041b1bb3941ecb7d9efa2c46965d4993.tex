By \(\texttt{def:two-coordinate-configuration}\), \(X=Z=(U,V)\), where \(U\), \(V\), and \(\xi\) are mutually independent Rademacher variables. Hence, for every \(h\in L_X\),
\[
(Th)(U,V)=E_P[h(X)\mid Z]=E_P[h(U,V)\mid U,V]=h(U,V).
\]
Thus \(T=I\), under the natural identification of \(L_2(P_X)\) and \(L_2(P_Z)\). The same construction directly sets
\[
w=h_0=a_0U+b_0V.
\]
These assignments are compatible with the stated envelope and source-radius conditions: since \(|a_0|,|b_0|\le 1/4\) and \(B=2\),
\[
|Y|=|a_0U+b_0V+\xi|\le \tfrac14+\tfrac14+1<2=B
\]
almost surely, and independence and centering of \(U\) and \(V\) give
\[
\|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2=E_P[(a_0U+b_0V)^2]=a_0^2+b_0^2\le \tfrac18<B^2.
\]
Moreover, \(T^*T=I\), so the displayed source representation holds with this \(w\) for every \(\beta\in(0,2]\).

Suppose now that every learned image in the construction equals its ideal image. By the definition of \(\alpha_{k,n}\),
\[
\alpha_{k,n}=\max_{1\le j\le d_{H,k}}\|\widehat g_{kj}-g_{kj}\|_{L_2(P_Z)}=0,
\qquad k\in\{1,2\}.
\]
In particular, \(\alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n}=0\). Because \(T=I\), the ideal images of the common target basis are
\[
g_{k1}=T U=U,
\qquad
g_{k2}=T V=V.
\]
Candidate \(2\) admits only coordinate \(1\), so under ideal-image equality its critic class is
\[
\widehat{\mathcal F}_2=\{sU:|s|\le2\}.
\]
For every \(f=sU\) in this class,
\[
E_P[Vf(Z)]=sE_P[VU]=sE_P[V]E_P[U]=0,
\]
which proves orthogonality to the \(V\) coordinate. At the blind fit \(a_0U\), the residual is
\[
Y-a_0U=b_0V+\xi.
\]
Mutual independence and the zero means of the Rademacher variables imply \(E_P[UV]=E_P[U\xi]=0\). Therefore, for \(f=sU\),
\[
2E_P[(Y-a_0U)f(Z)]-E_P[f(Z)^2]
=2s\{b_0E_P[VU]+E_P[\xi U]\}-s^2E_P[U^2]
=-s^2.
\]
The value is at most zero for every \(|s|\le2\), and it equals zero at \(s=0\). Consequently,
\[
\sup_{f\in\widehat{\mathcal F}_2}
\left\{2E_P[(Y-a_0U)f(Z)]-E_P[f(Z)^2]\right\}=0.
\]

Candidate \(1\) admits both coordinates. Under the same ideal-image equality,
\[
\widehat{\mathcal F}_1=\{sU+tV:|s|+|t|\le2\}.
\]
Taking \(s=0\) and \(t=1\) shows that \(V\in\widehat{\mathcal F}_1\), and hence \(V\in\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\mathrm A}\) because the absolute convex hull in \(\texttt{def:common-audit}\) contains each class used to generate it. Since \(|b_0|\le1/4\), the function \(f=b_0V\) also belongs to \(\widehat{\mathcal F}_1\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\mathrm A}\). Evaluating the common-audit population criterion at this feasible witness and using \(E_P[\xi V]=0\) and \(E_P[V^2]=1\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&2E_P[(Y-a_0U)b_0V]-E_P[(b_0V)^2]\\
&\qquad=2E_P[(b_0V+\xi)b_0V]-b_0^2E_P[V^2]\\
&\qquad=2b_0^2-b_0^2=b_0^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum over the common audit class therefore gives
\[
\sup_{f\in\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\mathrm A}}
\left\{2E_P[(Y-a_0U)f(Z)]-E_P[f(Z)^2]\right\}\ge b_0^2,
\]
as claimed.
