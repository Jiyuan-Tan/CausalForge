\begin{proof}
Fix \(B>0\), \(\gamma\in(0,1]\), \(\beta\in(0,2]\), and put \(a=B\sqrt{\gamma}\). Let \(S\) be Rademacher and, for every \(n=3m\), draw the observations independently from the law
\[
 X=Z=S,\qquad Y=a.
\]
This law is supported on the two distinct atoms \((a,-1,-1)\) and \((a,1,1)\), each with probability \(1/2\), including when \(\gamma=1\). Moreover, \(|Y|=a\le B\).

Write \(\mathbf 1\) for the constant-one function. Since \(X=Z\), conditional expectation is the identity from \(L_2(P_X)\) to \(L_2(P_Z)\): for every \(h\in L_2(P_X)\),
\[
 (Th)(Z)=E_P[h(X)\mid Z]=h(Z).
\]
Thus \(T=I\), \(r=a\mathbf 1\), and the unique population least-squares solution is
\[
 h^\dagger=a\mathbf 1.
\]
Set \(w=a\mathbf 1\). Then, for the prescribed \(\beta\),
\[
 (T^*T)^{\beta/2}w=w=h^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}=a=B\sqrt{\gamma}\le B.
\]
Hence the source-representation and source-radius assumptions hold, with equality in the radius bound at \(\gamma=1\).

Use \(\mathcal K=\{1,2\}\), \(d_{H,1}=d_{H,2}=1\), and define
\[
 \phi_{11}=\phi_{21}=\mathbf 1,
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_1=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_2=\{c\mathbf 1:0\le c\le a\},
 \qquad
 J_1=\{1\},
 \qquad
 J_2=\varnothing.
\]
Take \(\mathcal A_0=\{0\}\). Enumerate \(\mathcal H_1\) by zero. Enumerate a countable dense subset of \(\mathcal H_2\) with zero first, for example zero followed by the distinct functions \(aq\mathbf 1\) for \(q\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\). Use the deterministic exact-image learners
\[
 \widehat g_{11}=g_{11}=\mathbf 1,
 \qquad
 \widehat g_{21}=g_{21}=\mathbf 1,
\]
and set their deterministic estimation radii to zero. Let the three splits be any deterministic balanced partition, so each split has size \(m\), and take \(\eta=1/4\).

We verify that these objects form a sequence in \(\mathcal M_n\). The observations are independent and identically distributed, the splits are deterministic and nonempty, and the outcome and both basis functions obey their respective envelope bounds. Both target classes are compact, convex, contain zero, and lie in their declared one-dimensional spans. Every coefficient in \(\mathcal H_2\) has magnitude at most \(a\le B\), while \(\mathcal H_1\) contains only zero. The candidate set is finite, and the common ideal audit has one-dimensional linear span. The exact deterministic image learners are measurable from the critic split and satisfy image estimability with radius zero. The population least-squares solution is attained, and the declared enumerations are countable, dense, and contain zero. These are precisely the member conditions in \(\texttt{def:configuration-class}\). In particular,
\[
 \alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n}=0.
\]

Because \(h^\dagger=a\mathbf 1\in\mathcal H_2\), one has \(\varepsilon_{H,2}=0\). For \(h=c\mathbf 1\in\mathcal H_2\),
\[
 q_h-q_{h^\dagger}=(a-c)\mathbf 1.
\]
Candidate \(1\)'s ideal critic body contains every \(u\mathbf 1\) with \(|u|\le B\). Since \(0\le a-c\le a\le B\), the common ideal audit class contains \((a-c)\mathbf 1\). For arbitrary \(v,f\in L_2(P_Z)\), completing the square gives
\[
 2E_P[v(Z)f(Z)]-E_P[f(Z)^2]
 =\|v\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2-\|v-f\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2
 \le \|v\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2.
\]
Applying this identity with \(v=(a-c)\mathbf 1\), and then choosing the admissible critic \(f=v\), yields
\[
 D_{\mathcal F^{\mathrm A}}(q_h-q_{h^\dagger})
 =\|q_h-q_{h^\dagger}\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2.
\]
The equality holds for every \(h\in\mathcal H_2\), so \(\varepsilon_{A,2}=0\).

We next evaluate the entire trained path. Because \(J_2=\varnothing\), the learned training critic for candidate \(2\) is \(\widehat{\mathcal F}_2=\{0\}\). Therefore the training criterion at \(h=c\mathbf 1\) is exactly
\[
 \sup_{f\in\{0\}}
 \left\{2P_{\mathrm{tr}}[(Y-h(X))f(Z)]-P_{\mathrm{tr}}[f(Z)^2]\right\}
 +\lambda P_{\mathrm{tr}}[h(X)^2]
 =\lambda c^2.
\]
Its infimum is zero and is attained by the first enumerated element. Since that element is itself within \(n^{-1}\) of the infimum and has index one, the least-index rule selects
\[
 \widehat h_{2,\lambda}=0
 \qquad\text{for every }\lambda\in\Lambda_n
\]
for every sample. Candidate \(1\)'s target class is the singleton \(\{0\}\), so likewise \(\widehat h_{1,\lambda}=0\) for every \(\lambda\). The validation selector can only select one of these zero functions. Consequently,
\[
 \widehat h=0,
 \qquad
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 =\|a\mathbf 1\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 =a^2
 =\gamma B^2
\]
for every sample.

For candidate \(2\), the empty coordinate set also gives \(\mathcal F_2^{\mathrm{tr}}=\{0\}\). By \(\texttt{lem:training-coverage-distance-identity}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr},2}^2
 &=\sup_{0\le c\le a}
   \operatorname{dist}_{L_2(P_Z)}^2
   \bigl((a-c)\mathbf 1,\{0\}\bigr)\\
 &=\sup_{0\le c\le a}(a-c)^2
 =a^2
 =\gamma B^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This establishes all three asserted equalities and the samplewise collapse of the trained path.

It remains to evaluate the oracle functional. Here \(K=2\), \(d_{H,2}=1\), \(d_A=1\), and \(n_{\mathrm{tr}}=n_{\mathrm{va}}=m=n/3\). Define
\[
 L_n=\log\!\left(\frac{4K|\Lambda_n|}{\eta}\right).
\]
Since \(|\Lambda_n|=1+\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\), one has \(L_n=O(\log\log n)\). The target-approximation, common-audit, and learned-image terms for candidate \(2\) all vanish. Thus, for each fixed finite value of \(C\),
\[
 s_{2,n}
 =CB^2\frac{2+L_n}{m}
  +B^2\sqrt{\frac{L_n}{m}}
 =O_{B,C}\!\left(\frac{\log\log n}{n}
   +\sqrt{\frac{\log\log n}{n}}\right).
\]
Let \(j_n=\lfloor(\log_2 n)/4\rfloor\) and \(\lambda_n=2^{-j_n}\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(j_n\le J_n\), hence \(\lambda_n\in\Lambda_n\), and
\[
 n^{-1/4}\le\lambda_n\le2n^{-1/4}.
\]
Because \(\bar\beta>0\),
\[
 \lambda_n^{\bar\beta}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{s_{2,n}}{\lambda_n}
 =O_{B,C}\!\left(n^{-3/4}\log\log n
   +n^{-1/4}\sqrt{\log\log n}\right)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
It follows that
\[
 0\le
 \inf_{k\in\mathcal K,\lambda\in\Lambda_n}
 \left\{\lambda^{\bar\beta}+\frac{s_{k,n}}{\lambda}\right\}
 \le \lambda_n^{\bar\beta}+\frac{s_{2,n}}{\lambda_n}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

At \(\gamma=1\), the same law retains two distinct support atoms of probabilities \(1/2\) and \(1/2\), while \(a=B\); hence the squared error \(B^2\) is attained without support degeneration. To prove maximality, consider any configuration satisfying the two source conditions and having an entirely zero trained path. By \(\texttt{lem:operator-contraction}\), \(\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1\). Hence \(A=T^*T\) is a positive self-adjoint contraction. Since \(\beta/2\in(0,1]\), \(\texttt{lem:positive-contraction-fractional-power}\) and the source conditions imply
\[
 \|h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 =\|A^{\beta/2}w\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le\|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le B.
\]
A selector from a zero path is zero, so its squared strong error is at most \(B^2\). The preceding construction with \(\gamma=1\) attains that value, proving that \(B^2\) is the largest possible collapsed-zero-path error.
\end{proof}
