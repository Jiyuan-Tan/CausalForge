Set \(b=\bar\beta\) and \(s=s_{k_0,n}\). For \(0<s\le1\), put \(t=s^{1/(1+b)}\), so \(0<t\le1\). By \(\texttt{ass:grid-coverage}\), the smallest point of \(\Lambda_n\) is at most \(t/2\). Since \(1\in\Lambda_n\) and successive grid points differ by a factor of two, there is \(\lambda_t\in\Lambda_n\) such that
\[
 \frac{t}{2}\le\lambda_t\le t.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda_n}
 \left\{\lambda^b+\frac{s}{\lambda}\right\}
 \le \lambda_t^b+\frac{s}{\lambda_t}
 \le t^b+\frac{2s}{t}
 =3s^{b/(1+b)}.
\]
This proves the deterministic grid inequality. The zero-defect equalities identify \(s\) with the stochastic terms in \(\texttt{def:grid-and-scale}\), but they do not turn this numerical optimization into an error bound for the selected estimator. Indeed, \(\texttt{thm:common-audit-oracle-impossibility}\) constructs zero-defect configurations for which the optimized expression converges to zero while
\[
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2=\frac{1}{32}
\]
almost surely. The same theorem separately proves the deterministic clipping inequality
\[
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2\le4B^2.
\]
Finally, because \(b=\min\{\beta,1\}\), the exponent \(b/(1+b)\) equals \(\beta/(1+\beta)\) for \(\beta\le1\) and equals \(1/2\) for \(\beta>1\). Thus the algebraic exponent saturates, but no adaptive TRAE rate follows under the baseline assumptions.
