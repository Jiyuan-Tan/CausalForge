\begin{proof}
Put \(a=\bar\beta\in(0,1]\), \(s=s_{k_0,n}\), and \(t=s^{1/(1+a)}\). Since \(0<s\le1\), one has \(0<t\le1\). The grid-coverage assumption gives \(2^{-J_n}\le t/2\). Because \(\Lambda_n=\{2^{-j}:0\le j\le J_n\}\), there is therefore a grid point \(\lambda_t\in\Lambda_n\) such that
\[
 t/2<\lambda_t\le t.
\]
Indeed, take the largest dyadic grid point not exceeding \(t\); the lower endpoint of the grid lies below \(t/2\), and two consecutive grid points differ by a factor of two. It follows that
\[
 \lambda_t^a+\frac{s}{\lambda_t}
 \le t^a+\frac{2s}{t}
 =3s^{a/(1+a)}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\lambda\in\Lambda_n\) proves the displayed deterministic inequality. The zero-defect conditions are not needed for this algebraic step; they identify the setting in which \(s_{k_0,n}\) contains only the declared stochastic terms.

The replacement theorem \(\texttt{thm:common-audit-oracle-impossibility}\) shows that this numerical optimization is not an estimator bound. In particular, specialize its constant two-atom construction to \(B=2\) and \(\gamma=1/128\). Then \(\gamma B^2=1/32\), candidate \(2\) has \(\varepsilon_{H,2}=\varepsilon_{A,2}=\alpha_{2,n}=0\), the proposed oracle functional converges to zero, and the selected estimator has squared strong error \(1/32\) for every sample. The same theorem supplies the universal deterministic bound \(\|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2\le4B^2\).

Finally, when \(0<\beta\le1\), \(a=\beta\), so \(a/(1+a)=\beta/(1+\beta)\). When \(1<\beta\le2\), \(a=1\), so \(a/(1+a)=1/2\). This proves the asserted saturation statement.
\end{proof}
