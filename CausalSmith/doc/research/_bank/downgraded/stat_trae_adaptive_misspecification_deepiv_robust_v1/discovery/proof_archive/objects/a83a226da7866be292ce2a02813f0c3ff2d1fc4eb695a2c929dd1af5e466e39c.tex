Set \(b=\bar\beta\) and \(s=s_{k_0,n}\). For \(0<s\le1\), let \(t=s^{1/(1+b)}\), so \(0<t\le1\). By \(\texttt{ass:grid-coverage}\), the smallest grid point is at most \(t/2\). Since consecutive grid points differ by a factor of two and \(1\in\Lambda_n\), there is a \(\lambda_t\in\Lambda_n\) such that
\[
 t/2\le\lambda_t\le t.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \inf_{k\in\mathcal K,\lambda\in\Lambda_n}
 \left(\lambda^{b}+\frac{s_{k,n}}{\lambda}\right)
 \le \lambda_t^b+\frac{s}{\lambda_t}
 \le t^b+\frac{2s}{t}
 =3s^{b/(1+b)}.
\]
Absorbing the numerical factor into \(C\) proves the displayed power reduction. The zero-defect equalities identify \(s\) with the stochastic terms for candidate \(k_0\), but the preceding optimization itself only uses its value.

The replacement theorem for \(\texttt{oeq:common-audit-oracle}\) proves directly that every selected estimator obeys \(\|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2\le4B^2\), irrespective of \(s\). Hence the clipped part holds for all \(s\), although the sharper oracle comparison is false without further training-coverage conditions. Finally, because \(b=\min\{\beta,1\}\), the exponent is \(\min\{\beta,1\}/(1+\min\{\beta,1\})\), and it saturates at \(1/2\) when \(\beta>1\), exactly as in the cited Tan--Syrgkanis benchmark.
