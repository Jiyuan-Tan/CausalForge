\begin{proof}
Put \(a=\bar\beta\in(0,1]\), \(s=s_{k_0,n}\), and \(t=s^{1/(1+a)}\). Since \(0<s\le1\), one has \(0<t\le1\). By \(\texttt{ass:grid-coverage}\),
\[
 2^{-J_n}\le \frac{t}{2}.
\]
Because \(\Lambda_n=\{2^{-j}:0\le j\le J_n\}\), there is a grid point \(\lambda_t\in\Lambda_n\) such that
\[
 \frac{t}{2}<\lambda_t\le t.
\]
Indeed, choose the largest dyadic grid point not exceeding \(t\). The lower endpoint of the grid is at most \(t/2\), and adjacent grid points differ by a factor of two. Therefore
\[
 \lambda_t^a+\frac{s}{\lambda_t}
 \le t^a+\frac{2s}{t}
 =s^{a/(1+a)}+2s^{a/(1+a)}
 =3s^{a/(1+a)}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\lambda\in\Lambda_n\) proves
\[
 \inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda_n}
 \left\{\lambda^{\bar\beta}+\frac{s_{k_0,n}}{\lambda}\right\}
 \le3s_{k_0,n}^{\bar\beta/(1+\bar\beta)}.
\]
The zero-defect equalities are not needed for this algebraic optimization; they identify the regime in which the displayed scale contains only the declared stochastic terms.

This numerical inequality is not an estimator guarantee. Apply \(\texttt{thm:common-audit-oracle-impossibility}\) with \(B=2\), \(\gamma=1/128\), and \(\eta=1/4\). Then \(\gamma B^2=1/32\), candidate \(2\) satisfies
\[
 \varepsilon_{H,2}=\varepsilon_{A,2}=\alpha_{2,n}=0,
\]
the optimized oracle functional converges to zero, and the selected estimator satisfies
\[
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2=\frac{1}{32}
\]
for every sample. The same theorem gives the logically separate deterministic clipping bound
\[
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2\le4B^2.
\]
Finally, if \(0<\beta\le1\), then \(a=\beta\) and \(a/(1+a)=\beta/(1+\beta)\). If \(1<\beta\le2\), then \(a=1\) and \(a/(1+a)=1/2\). This proves the asserted saturation statement.
\end{proof}
