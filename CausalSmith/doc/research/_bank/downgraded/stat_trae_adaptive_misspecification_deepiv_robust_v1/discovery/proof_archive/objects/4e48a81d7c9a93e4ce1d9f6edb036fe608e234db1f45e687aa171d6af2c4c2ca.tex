\begin{proof}
Let \(S\) take the values \(-1\) and \(1\), each with probability \(1/2\), and put
\[
 X=Z=S,\qquad \mathbf 1(x)=1,\qquad
 a=B\sqrt{\gamma},\qquad Y=a.
\]
Thus the law of \(W=(Y,X,Z)\) has exactly the two distinct atoms \((a,-1,-1)\) and \((a,1,1)\), each of positive probability, for every \(\gamma\in(0,1]\). Since \(a\le B\), the outcome envelope holds, including at \(\gamma=1\).

Because \(X=Z\), conditional expectation is the identity: \(T=I\) on \(L_2(P_X)=L_2(P_Z)\). Consequently
\[
 r=a\mathbf 1,
 \qquad h^\dagger=a\mathbf 1,
 \qquad q_{h^\dagger}=0.
\]
The least-squares solution is unique, so it is also the minimum-norm solution in \(\texttt{def:pseudotrue-target}\). Set \(w=h^\dagger\). For the prescribed \(\beta\),
\[
 (T^*T)^{\beta/2}w=w=h^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}=a=B\sqrt\gamma\le B.
\]
Hence both source conditions hold, with equality in the source-radius condition when \(\gamma=1\).

Take \(\mathcal K=\{1,2\}\), \(d_{H,1}=d_{H,2}=1\), and
\[
 \phi_{11}=\phi_{21}=\mathbf 1,
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_1=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_2=\{c\mathbf 1:0\le c\le a\},
 \qquad
 J_1=\{1\},
 \qquad
 J_2=\varnothing.
\]
Put \(\mathcal A_0=\{0\}\). Enumerate zero first in each target class; for \(\mathcal H_2\), follow it by \(a q\mathbf 1\) with \(q\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\) in any fixed enumeration. Finally, use the deterministic exact-image learners
\[
 \widehat g_{11}=g_{11}=\mathbf 1,
 \qquad
 \widehat g_{21}=g_{21}=\mathbf 1.
\]
These learners are measurable with respect to the critic split and have deterministic estimation radius zero.

We verify membership in \(\mathcal M_n\). Draw the observations independently from the displayed two-atom law and use deterministic balanced splits of size \(m\). The basis functions have supremum norm one. The two target sets are compact, convex, contain zero, and lie in their declared one-dimensional spans. Their coefficient radii are at most \(a\le B\). There are two candidates, and the common ideal audit span has dimension one. Exact constant-image learners satisfy image estimability. The population least-squares target is attained, and the declared enumerations are countable and dense with zero first. Thus every member condition in \(\texttt{def:configuration-class}\) holds. In particular,
\[
 \alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n}=0.
\]

Candidate \(2\) contains \(h^\dagger\), so \(\varepsilon_{H,2}=0\). For \(h=c\mathbf 1\in\mathcal H_2\),
\[
 q_h-q_{h^\dagger}=(a-c)\mathbf 1.
\]
Candidate \(1\)'s ideal critic body contains every \(u\mathbf 1\) with \(|u|\le B\). Since \(0\le a-c\le a\le B\), the common audit class therefore contains \((a-c)\mathbf 1\). Completing the square gives, for every \(v,f\in L_2(P_Z)\),
\[
 2\langle v,f\rangle-\|f\|^2
 =\|v\|^2-\|v-f\|^2
 \le\|v\|^2.
\]
Taking \(v=(a-c)\mathbf 1\) and \(f=v\), which is admissible, shows
\[
 D_{\mathcal F^{\mathrm A}}(q_h-q_{h^\dagger})
 =\|q_h-q_{h^\dagger}\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2.
\]
This holds for every \(h\in\mathcal H_2\), hence \(\varepsilon_{A,2}=0\).

We next evaluate the entire trained path. Because \(J_2=\varnothing\),
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal F}_2=\{0\}.
\]
For \(h=c\mathbf 1\in\mathcal H_2\), its training criterion is therefore exactly
\[
 \sup_{f\in\{0\}}
 \left\{2P_{\mathrm{tr}}[(Y-h(X))f(Z)]-P_{\mathrm{tr}}[f(Z)^2]\right\}
 +\lambda P_{\mathrm{tr}}[h(X)^2]
 =\lambda c^2.
\]
Its infimum is zero and is attained at the enumeration's first element, \(c=0\). The least-index approximate-minimizer rule consequently selects
\[
 \widehat h_{2,\lambda}=0
 \quad\text{for every }\lambda\in\Lambda_n
\]
for every sample. Candidate \(1\)'s target class is the singleton \(\{0\}\), so \(\widehat h_{1,\lambda}=0\) as well. Validation can only choose among these zero functions. Thus
\[
 \widehat h=0,
 \qquad
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 =\|a\mathbf 1\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 =a^2
 =\gamma B^2
\]
samplewise.

For candidate \(2\), the empty coordinate set also gives \(\mathcal F_2^{\mathrm{tr}}=\{0\}\). By \(\texttt{lem:training-coverage-distance-identity}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr},2}^2
 &=\sup_{0\le c\le a}
   \operatorname{dist}_{L_2(P_Z)}^2
   \bigl((a-c)\mathbf 1,\{0\}\bigr)\\
 &=\sup_{0\le c\le a}(a-c)^2
 =a^2
 =\gamma B^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]

It remains to check the proposed oracle functional. Here \(K=2\), \(d_{H,2}=1\), \(d_A=1\), and \(n_{\mathrm{tr}}=n_{\mathrm{va}}=m=n/3\). Write
\[
 L_n=\log\!\left(\frac{4K|\Lambda_n|}{\eta}\right).
\]
Because \(|\Lambda_n|=1+\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\), one has \(L_n=O(\log\log n)\). All approximation and learned-image terms for candidate \(2\) vanish, so, for each fixed finite \(C\),
\[
 s_{2,n}
 =CB^2\frac{2+L_n}{m}
  +B^2\sqrt{\frac{L_n}{m}}
 =O_{B,C}\!\left(
   \frac{\log\log n}{n}
   +\sqrt{\frac{\log\log n}{n}}
  \right).
\]
Let \(j_n=\lfloor(\log_2 n)/4\rfloor\) and \(\lambda_n=2^{-j_n}\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(j_n\le J_n\), so \(\lambda_n\in\Lambda_n\), and
\[
 n^{-1/4}\le\lambda_n\le2n^{-1/4}.
\]
Since \(\bar\beta>0\), it follows that
\[
 \lambda_n^{\bar\beta}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{s_{2,n}}{\lambda_n}
 =O_{B,C}\!\left(
   n^{-3/4}\log\log n
   +n^{-1/4}\sqrt{\log\log n}
  \right)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \inf_{k\in\mathcal K,\lambda\in\Lambda_n}
 \left\{\lambda^{\bar\beta}+\frac{s_{k,n}}{\lambda}\right\}
 \le
 \lambda_n^{\bar\beta}+\frac{s_{2,n}}{\lambda_n}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

The same fixed two-atom law is valid at \(\gamma=1\): only the constant outcome and target reach the envelope \(B\), while both support atoms retain probability \(1/2\). Hence the error \(B^2\) is attained. Finally, consider any configuration satisfying the two source conditions for which every trained path element is zero. By \(\texttt{lem:operator-contraction}\), \(\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1\), so \(A=T^*T\) is positive and self-adjoint with \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1\). Since \(\beta/2\in(0,1]\), \(\texttt{lem:positive-contraction-fractional-power}\) and the source conditions give
\[
 \|h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 =\|A^{\beta/2}w\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le\|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}
 \le B.
\]
A zero path therefore has squared strong error at most \(B^2\), and the construction at \(\gamma=1\) attains this upper bound.
\end{proof}
