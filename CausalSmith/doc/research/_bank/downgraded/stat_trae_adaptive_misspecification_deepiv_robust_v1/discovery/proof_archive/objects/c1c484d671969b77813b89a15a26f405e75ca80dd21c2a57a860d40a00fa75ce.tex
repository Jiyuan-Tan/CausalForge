Let \(e_j(x)=\mathbf 1\{x=j\}\) on \(\mathcal X=\mathcal Z=\{1,2\}\), and define the observed law by
\[
 P(X=Z=2)=\gamma,
 \qquad
 P(X=Z=1)=1-\gamma,
 \qquad
 Y=B e_2(X).
\]
Both support points have positive probability because \(\gamma\in(0,1)\), and \(|Y|\le B\) almost surely. Since \(Z=X\), \(T=I\) on \(L_2(P_X)\). Therefore
\[
 r=B e_2,
 \qquad
 h^\dagger=B e_2,
 \qquad
 q_{h^\dagger}=0.
\]
For the prescribed \(\beta\), take \(w=h^\dagger\). Then
\[
 (T^*T)^{\beta/2}w=w=h^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \|w\|_{L_2(P_X)}=B\sqrt\gamma<B,
\]
so the source representation and radius conditions hold.

Let \(\mathcal K=\{1,2\}\), let \(d_{H,1}=d_{H,2}=2\), and use \(\phi_{k1}=e_1\), \(\phi_{k2}=e_2\) for both candidates. Set
\[
 \mathcal H_1=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_2=\{c e_2:0\le c\le B\},
 \qquad
 J_1=\{2\},
 \qquad
 J_2=\{1\}.
\]
Enumerate \(0\) first in both classes, and complete the second enumeration with a countable dense subset of \([0,B]e_2\). Take \(\mathcal A_0=\{0\}\) and use the deterministic exact-image learners
\[
 \widehat g_{kj}=g_{kj}=e_j.
\]
The two basis functions are bounded by one; both target sets are compact, convex, and contain zero; every target coefficient vector has \(\ell_1\)-norm at most \(B\); the candidate and audit collections are finite-dimensional; and the least-squares inverse is attained because \(T=I\). The exact learners use no observations outside the critic split and have radius zero. Hence these are configurations in \(\mathcal M_n\), and \(\alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n}=0\).

Candidate \(2\) contains \(h^\dagger\), so \(\varepsilon_{H,2}=0\). For \(h=c e_2\in\mathcal H_2\),
\[
 q_h-q_{h^\dagger}=T(h^\dagger-h)=(B-c)e_2.
\]
Candidate \(1\)'s ideal critic body contains \(u e_2\) for every \(|u|\le B\), and therefore the common audit contains \((B-c)e_2\). Choosing this function in the quadratic dual and completing the square gives
\[
 D_{\mathcal F^{\mathrm A}}(q_h-q_{h^\dagger})
 =\|q_h-q_{h^\dagger}\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(h\in\mathcal H_2\) in the definition of \(\varepsilon_{A,2}\) shows that \(\varepsilon_{A,2}=0\).

We next compute every trained path. For candidate \(2\), \(h=c e_2\), and \(f=u e_1\in\widehat{\mathcal F}_2\), disjoint supports imply pointwise
\[
 (Y-h(X))f(Z)=(B-c)u e_2(X)e_1(X)=0.
\]
Thus its empirical adversarial loss is
\[
 \sup_{|u|\le B}\{-u^2P_{\mathrm{tr}}e_1\}=0,
\]
and its full objective is \(\lambda c^2P_{\mathrm{tr}}e_2\ge0\). The value at \(c=0\) is exactly zero for every sample. Since zero has enumeration index one, it is the least-index \(n^{-1}\)-approximate minimizer even when other coefficients tie. Hence \(\widehat h_{2,\lambda}=0\) for every \(\lambda\). Candidate \(1\) has only the zero target, so \(\widehat h_{1,\lambda}=0\) as well. Validation can select only among zero functions, and therefore
\[
 \widehat h=0,
 \qquad
 \|\widehat h-h^\dagger\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
 =E_P[B^2e_2(X)]=\gamma B^2
\]
for every sample.

Candidate \(2\)'s population training critic body is
\[
 \mathcal F_2^{\mathrm{tr}}=\{u e_1:|u|\le B\}.
\]
The coordinates \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) are orthogonal in \(L_2(P_Z)\). Thus, for \(0\le c\le B\),
\[
 \operatorname{dist}^2((B-c)e_2,\mathcal F_2^{\mathrm{tr}})
 =\|(B-c)e_2\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2
 =\gamma(B-c)^2,
\]
where the closest critic is zero. The maximum occurs at \(c=0\), so \(\texttt{lem:training-coverage-distance-identity}\) gives
\[
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr},2}^2=\gamma B^2.
\]

Finally, use balanced splits of size \(m=n/3\). Here \(K=2\), \(d_{H,2}=2\), \(d_A=2\), and \(|\Lambda_n|=1+\lceil\log_2n\rceil\). All approximation and learned-image terms for candidate \(2\) vanish, whence
\[
 s_{2,n}
 =O_{B,C}\!\left(
 \frac{\log\log n}{n}
 +\sqrt{\frac{\log\log n}{n}}
 \right)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Put \(b=\bar\beta\) and \(t_n=s_{2,n}^{1/(1+b)}\). The positive validation term in \(s_{2,n}\) implies \(nt_n\to\infty\), while the smallest grid point is at most \(n^{-1}\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), the geometric grid therefore contains \(\lambda_n\) with \(t_n/2\le\lambda_n\le t_n\). Consequently,
\[
 \inf_{k,\lambda}
 \left\{\lambda^b+\frac{s_{k,n}}{\lambda}\right\}
 \le \lambda_n^b+\frac{s_{2,n}}{\lambda_n}
 \le3s_{2,n}^{b/(1+b)}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
This completes the construction.
