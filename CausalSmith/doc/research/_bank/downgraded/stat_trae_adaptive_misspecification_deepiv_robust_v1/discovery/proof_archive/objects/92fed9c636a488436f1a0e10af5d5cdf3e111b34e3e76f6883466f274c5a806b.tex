By \(\texttt{ass:iv-validity}\),
\[
E_P[Y-h_0(X)\mid Z]=0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Linearity of conditional expectation therefore gives
\[
r=E_P[Y\mid Z]=E_P[h_0(X)\mid Z]=Th_0
\quad\text{in }L_2(P_Z).
\]
Let
\[
\mathcal S=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{u\in L_X}\|Tu-r\|_{L_2(P_Z)}.
\]
Because \(Th_0=r\), the minimum defining \(\mathcal S\) is zero and
\[
\mathcal S=\{u\in L_X:Tu=r\}.
\]
If \(u\in\mathcal S\), then
\[
T(u-h_0)=Tu-Th_0=r-r=0.
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:completeness}\) implies \(u-h_0=0\), so \(\mathcal S=\{h_0\}\). The minimum-norm selection in \(\texttt{def:pseudotrue-target}\) consequently satisfies
\[
h^\dagger=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{h\in\mathcal S}\|h\|_{L_2(P_X)}=h_0.
\]

The qualifications in the final sentence are substantive. First, if validity is omitted, let \(X=Z\) be Rademacher, let \(Y=X\), and set \(h_0=0\). Then \(T\) is the identity on \(L_2(P_X)\), hence it is injective, but \(r(Z)=Z\) and the unique least-squares inverse is \(h^\dagger(X)=X\ne h_0(X)\). Thus completeness alone does not give causal calibration. Second, if completeness is omitted, let \(X\) be Rademacher, let \(Z\) be constant, let \(Y=X\), and set \(h_0(X)=X\). Validity holds because \(Y-h_0(X)=0\), while \((Th)(Z)=E_P[h(X)]\) and \(r(Z)=E_P[Y]=0\). The least-squares solution set is
\[
\mathcal S=\{h\in L_2(P_X):E_P[h(X)]=0\},
\]
and its unique minimum-norm element is the zero function. Hence \(h^\dagger=0\ne h_0\). These two observable-law witnesses show that, absent the stated calibration conditions, the minimum-norm inverse target is a pseudo-true sensitivity target and is not automatically the causal structural response.
