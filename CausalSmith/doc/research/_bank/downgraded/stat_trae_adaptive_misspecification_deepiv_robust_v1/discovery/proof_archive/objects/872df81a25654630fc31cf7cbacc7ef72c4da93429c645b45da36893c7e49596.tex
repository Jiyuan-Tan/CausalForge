Put \(v=h-h'\). By the definition of \(T\), \((Tv)(Z)=E_P[v(X)\mid Z]\). Conditional Jensen's inequality gives
\[
 |E_P[v(X)\mid Z]|^2\le E_P[|v(X)|^2\mid Z]
\]
almost surely. Taking expectations and using the tower property yields
\[
 \|T(h-h')\|_{L_2(P_Z)}^2
 =E_P\!\left[|E_P[v(X)\mid Z]|^2\right]
 \le E_P[|v(X)|^2]
 =\|h-h'\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2.
\]
Both sides are nonnegative, so taking square roots proves the claim. The sampling assumption \(\texttt{ass:iid}\) ensures that the population conditional expectation defining \(T\) is common to every observation; no sample-level argument is needed.
