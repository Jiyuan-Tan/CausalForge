\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(K(\xi)=\mathcal B_\kappa(\xi)\) for the normalized feasible projective class at instance \(\xi\).  We first record the relevant joint continuities.  On the finite assignment space, total-variation convergence of \(\nu_j\) to \(\nu\) implies entrywise convergence of \(\Pi_j\) and \(\Omega_j\).  At every exposure-positive \(\xi\), all diagonal entries of \(\Pi\) are strictly positive; therefore \(\Pi_j^{-1}\to\Pi^{-1}\) for \(j\) sufficiently large.  If \(F_{a,j}\to F_a\) in the Wasserstein metric on \([0,1]\), their quantiles satisfy
\[
\int_0^1\left|F_{a,j}^{-1}(u)-F_a^{-1}(u)\right|\,du\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1}
\]
Because all quantiles lie in \([0,1]\),
\[
\left|\int_0^1q_{1,j}(u)q_{0,j}(u)\,du-
\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du\right|
\le \lVert q_{1,j}-q_1\rVert_1+\lVert q_{0,j}-q_0\rVert_1,
\tag{2}
\]
and the same inequality holds with the second quantile evaluated at \(1-u\).  The marginal means and second moments also converge.  Hence \(c_L,c_U,Q(c_L),Q(c_U)\) vary continuously with \(\xi\).

For normalized representatives \((b_1,b_0)\) on the product of radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres, the map
\[
(\xi,B)\longmapsto \lambda_{\min}\{n^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega(\xi)B\}
\tag{3}
\]
is continuous.  On its superlevel set at \(\kappa>0\), the inverse obeys
\[
\lVert S_B(\xi)^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
The finite-dimensional formulas in def:gain-loss, together with (1)--(4), therefore show that
\[
(\xi,B)\longmapsto g_{B,\xi}(0),\quad
(\xi,B)\longmapsto r_\xi(B),\quad
(\xi,B)\longmapsto w_\xi(B)
\tag{5}
\]
are jointly continuous on the feasible graph.  The minimum and maximum over two endpoints preserve continuity.

We next prove upper stability of both optimizer correspondences directly.  Let \(\xi_j\to\xi_0\) in \(\mathfrak X_\kappa\), let \(B_j\in\mathcal A_R(\xi_j)\), and suppose along a subsequence that \(B_j\to B\) in the compact normalized projective quotient.  Feasibility and (3) give \(B\in K(\xi_0)\).  Choose any \(B^\circ\in\mathcal A_R(\xi_0)\).  The slack hypothesis gives
\[
\lambda_{\min}\{n^{-1}S_{B^\circ}(\xi_0)\}\ge\kappa+\eta.
\]
By (3), the fixed basis \(B^\circ\) belongs to \(K(\xi_j)\) for every sufficiently large \(j\).  Empirical optimality at \(\xi_j\) and (5) then give
\[
r_{\xi_j}(B_j)\ge r_{\xi_j}(B^\circ)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r_{\xi_0}(B)\ge r_{\xi_0}(B^\circ)=\max_{C\in K(\xi_0)}r_{\xi_0}(C).
\]
Thus \(B\in\mathcal A_R(\xi_0)\).  Replacing \(r\) and \(B^\circ\) by \(g(0)\) and any member of \(\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)\) proves the analogous assertion for \(\mathcal A_0\).  The optimizer-wide slack assumed in the theorem supplies the fixed strictly feasible comparator in both cases.

The optimizer sets are nonempty and compact because every \(K(\xi)\) is nonempty and compact and both objectives are continuous.  For each \(j\), choose
\[
B_{0,j}\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B),
\qquad
B_{R,j}\in\operatorname*{argmax}_{B\in\mathcal A_R(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B).
\]
Every subsequence has a further subsequence on which both bases converge.  The preceding optimizer-stability argument and (5) show that any such limits \(B_0,B_R\) lie in \(\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)\) and \(\mathcal A_R(\xi_0)\), respectively.  Consequently,
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B)
\ge \inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)}w_{\xi_0}(B)
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\sup_{B\in\mathcal A_R(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B)
\le \sup_{B\in\mathcal A_R(\xi_0)}w_{\xi_0}(B).
\tag{7}
\]
Subtracting (7) from (6) yields
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\Delta(\xi_j)\ge\Delta(\xi_0)>0.
\tag{8}
\]
The relative topology on \(\mathfrak X_\kappa\) is metrizable by the stated product metrics.  If no relative-open neighborhood with positive gap existed, one could choose \(\xi_j\to\xi_0\) with \(\Delta(\xi_j)\le0\), contradicting (8).  Hence the required relative-open neighborhood \(\mathcal U\) exists. \(\square\)
