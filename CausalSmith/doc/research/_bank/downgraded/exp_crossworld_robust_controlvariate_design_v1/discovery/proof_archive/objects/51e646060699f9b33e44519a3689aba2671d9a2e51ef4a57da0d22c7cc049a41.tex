\textbf{Proof.}
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  For \(B\in\mathcal B_Z\), def:zhao-basis-class gives \(S_B\succ0\), so \(S_B^{-1}\) exists.  Put
\[
E=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_n\\ I_n&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
By def:moment-matrix, \(Q(c)=Q(0)-cE\).  Cyclicity and linearity of trace therefore give
\[
g_B(c)=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{A_BQ(0)\}
-cn^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_BE),                                      \tag{1}
\]
which is affine in \(c\).

We also compute the uncontrolled term without importing another statement.  Write
\(M=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\) in its two exposure blocks.  Mutual exclusivity gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{i,0})
=\mathbb E_{\nu}(D_{i,1}D_{i,0})-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}
=-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}.
\]
Consequently, the \(i\)-th diagonal entries of both off-diagonal blocks of \(M\) equal \(-1\).  Hence
\[
\operatorname{tr}(ME)=-2n
\]
and
\[
h(c)-h(0)
=-cn^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(ME)
=\frac{2c}{n}.                                                        \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) and \(\ell_B(c)=h(c)-g_B(c)\) show that \(\ell_B\) is affine as well.  Ass:known-exposure and ass:iid-potential-pairs supply the causal exposure and ex ante moment interpretations; the affine conclusion itself follows from the displayed matrix identities.

By ass:frechet-covariance-domain,
\[
\left\{\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}:H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\right\}
=[c_L,c_U].                                                          \tag{3}
\]
An affine function on a compact interval attains its minimum and maximum at an endpoint.  Because (3) is equality of sets, each endpoint is the covariance of at least one coupling in \(\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\).  Applying this observation to \(g_B\) and \(\ell_B\) proves the two endpoint formulas and attainment.  Ass:product-coupling-zero supplies a coupling in \(\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\) with covariance zero; (3) then yields \(c_L\le0\le c_U\), so \(g_B(0)\) is evaluated at a feasible covariance.

Finally write \(g_B(c)=\alpha_B+\beta_Bc\).  If \(\beta_B=0\), the gain is constant, so its value at zero is its infimum.  Conversely, suppose \(c_L<0<c_U\).  If \(\beta_B>0\), then \(g_B(c_L)<g_B(0)\); if \(\beta_B<0\), then \(g_B(c_U)<g_B(0)\).  Thus \(g_B(0)\) is the worst-coupling gain if and only if the affine slope \(\beta_B\) is zero. \(\square\)
