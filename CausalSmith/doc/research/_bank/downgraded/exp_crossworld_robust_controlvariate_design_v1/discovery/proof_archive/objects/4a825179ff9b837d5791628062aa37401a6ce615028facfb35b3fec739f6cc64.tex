\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a rational signed pivot-chart box.  In the \(2n\) coordinates of \((b_1,b_0)\), the entries of
\[
 S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B
\]
are quadratic polynomials with rational or real-algebraic coefficients.  The normalization requirements are the two polynomial equations
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n b_{1i}^2=n,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^n b_{0i}^2=n.
\]
The rational box restrictions and the selected pivot signs are finitely many polynomial weak inequalities.  Finally, write
\[
 S_B-n\kappa I_2=
 \begin{bmatrix}u&v\\v&z\end{bmatrix}.
\]
For a real symmetric two-by-two matrix, the principal-minor criterion gives
\[
 \begin{bmatrix}u&v\\v&z\end{bmatrix}\succeq0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 u\ge0,\quad z\ge0,\quad uz-v^2\ge0. \tag{1}
\]
Thus membership in the chart box and in the normalized feasible set \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is expressed by an existential first-order formula whose atomic predicates are polynomial equalities and weak inequalities over the real-algebraic coefficient field.

Apply lem:real-closed-field-quantifier-elimination to this formula.  Collins's EVAL/ELIM construction returns an equivalent quantifier-free sign formula, and its cylindrical decomposition partitions the relevant Euclidean space into finitely many sign-invariant cells with real-algebraic sample points.  Recursive projection strictly lowers the number of variables, so the construction terminates.  Exact algebraic-number isolation determines every required polynomial sign at every sample.  If some cell sample satisfies the existential formula, it is an exact real-algebraic point of the chart-box intersection.  If none does, the finite sign-invariant decomposition and its sign evaluations form an infeasibility certificate.

All inequalities in (1), as well as the chart and box inequalities, are weak.  Therefore no strict-interior premise was introduced, and the same decision and sample construction applies when feasible points exist only on the equality boundary \(S_B-n\kappa I_2\succeq0\) with zero determinant or a zero diagonal principal minor. \(\square\)
