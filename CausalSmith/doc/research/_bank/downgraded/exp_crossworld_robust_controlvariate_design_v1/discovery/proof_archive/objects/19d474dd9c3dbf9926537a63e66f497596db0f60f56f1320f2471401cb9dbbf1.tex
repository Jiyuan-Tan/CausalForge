\textbf{Proof.}
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:coordinate-bounded-kappa-feasibility}\) makes \(\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\) nonempty.  Put \(N=2n\), index the coordinates of \(D\) by \(\ell\), write \(Z_\ell=D_\ell-\pi_\ell\), and put \(d_n=\max_\ell|N_\ell|\).

If \(\ell\) occurs exactly once in an ordered triple or quadruple and every other distinct index lies outside \(N_\ell\), then \(Z_\ell\) is independent of the product of the remaining centered coordinates by \(\texttt{ass:zhao-dependency-neighborhood-rate}\); hence that mixed moment vanishes.  Charging every singleton in a nonvanishing triple to a directed neighbor gives at most \(8Nd_n^2\) contributing ordered triples.  Partitioning quadruples into multiplicity types \(4\), \(3+1\), \(2+2\), \(2+1+1\), and \(1+1+1+1\), and charging singleton indices in the same way, gives at most \(64N^2d_n^2\) contributing ordered quadruples.  Since \(|Z_\ell|\le1\),
\[
\mathfrak M_{3,n}\le8Nd_n^2,
\qquad
\mathfrak M_{4,n}\le64N^2d_n^2.
\tag{1}
\]
This count uses only coordinate-versus-complement independence.  If \(k\notin N_\ell\), then \(D_\ell\) is independent of \(D_k\), so \(\Omega_{k\ell}=0\).  Therefore
\[
s_n\le d_n\le\overline d n^\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]

Fix a bounded array and \(B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\).  Known exposure and the signed convention in \(Y\) imply
\[
\mathbb E_\nu\widehat Y_\ell^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_\ell,
\qquad
\{\Pi^{-1}(\widehat Y^{\mathrm{obs}}-Y)\}_\ell
=\frac{Y_\ell Z_\ell}{\pi_\ell^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
All divisions are licensed by exposure positivity.  With \(G_B=\widehat\gamma^{\mathrm{HT}}(B)-\gamma_B^*(Y)\), equation (3) gives
\[
G_B=\sum_{\ell=1}^Nu_{B,\ell}Z_\ell,
\qquad
u_{B,\ell}=S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega e_\ell\frac{Y_\ell}{\pi_\ell^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every covariance column has at most \(s_n\) nonzero entries, every covariance entry has absolute value at most one, \(\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}\), and the basis coordinates are bounded by \(\overline a\).  From (2) and \(\pi_{\min,n}\ge\underline\pi n^{-\beta}\), there is a finite constant \(K_u\), independent of \(n,B,Y,H\), such that
\[
\sup_{B,\ell}\lVert u_{B,\ell}\rVert_2
\le K_un^{\alpha+2\beta-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Also
\[
X_B=n^{-1}B^{\top}(D-\mathbb E_\nu D)
=\sum_{\ell=1}^Nx_{B,\ell}Z_\ell,
\qquad
\sup_{B,\ell}\lVert x_{B,\ell}\rVert_2\le\overline a/n.
\tag{6}
\]
Applying the fourth-moment bound (1) coordinatewise to (4) and (6) gives constants \(K_G,K_X<\infty\) for which
\[
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert G_B\rVert_2^4
\le K_Gn^{6\alpha+8\beta-2},
\qquad
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert X_B\rVert_2^4
\le K_Xn^{2\alpha-2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The feasible and oracle estimators obey
\[
\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{CV}}=\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}+\Delta_B,
\qquad \Delta_B=-G_B^{\top}X_B.
\tag{8}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and (7) yield a finite \(K_\Delta\) such that
\[
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\Delta_B^2
\le K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}.
\tag{9}
\]

Put \(T_B=\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}-\tau\).  It is centered and has representation
\[
T_B=\sum_{\ell=1}^Nt_{B,\ell}Z_\ell,
\qquad
t_{B,\ell}=n^{-1}\{Y_\ell/\pi_\ell-B_{\ell\cdot}\gamma_B^*(Y)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The oracle projection inequality is
\[
\gamma_B^*(Y)^{\top}S_B\gamma_B^*(Y)
\le Y^{\top}\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Y.
\tag{11}
\]
At most \(Ns_n\) covariance entries are nonzero.  Bounded outcomes, (2), the exposure-probability lower bound, and \(S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2\) therefore give finite constants \(K_\gamma,K_t\) such that
\[
\sup_B\lVert\gamma_B^*(Y)\rVert_2\le K_\gamma n^{\alpha/2+\beta},
\qquad
\sup_{B,\ell}|t_{B,\ell}|\le K_tn^{\alpha/2+\beta-1}.
\tag{12}
\]
Expanding \(\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)\) and applying the third-moment bound (1), (5), (6), and (12) gives
\[
\sup_B|\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)|
\le K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
\tag{13}
\]
The variance identity for \(T_B+\Delta_B\), together with (9) and (13), proves the first uniform bound with
\[
R_n=K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}
+2K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
\tag{14}
\]
Because \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), the second exponent is no larger than the first; hence
\[
0\le R_n\le K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-2},
\qquad K_R=K_\Delta+2K_{\mathrm{cov}}.
\tag{15}
\]

Completing the square for the fixed oracle coefficient gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
=n^{-2}Y^{\top}\{\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}
-\Pi^{-1}\Omega BS_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\}Y.
\tag{16}
\]
Under \(\texttt{ass:iid-potential-pairs}\), \(\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}(YY^{\top})=Q(c_H)\).  Taking the trace expectation in (16) and applying \(\texttt{def:gain-loss}\) yields
\[
\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
=\ell_B(c_H).
\tag{17}
\]
Integrating the first uniform bound proves the second.  Finally, \(4\alpha+4\beta<1\) gives \(nR_n\to0\).  All constants may depend on fixed \(\kappa,\overline a,\underline\pi,\overline d\), but not on \(n,B,Y\), or \(H\). \(\square\)
