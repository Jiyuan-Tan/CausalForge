\textbf{Proof.}

\textbf{Design, support, and feasibility.}
For the stated exposure maps,
\[
D_{i,1}=W_i,
\qquad
D_{i,0}=1-W_i.
\]
Thus the two exposure events are mutually exclusive for every unit, and their probabilities are \(\pi_{i,1}=\pi_{i,0}=1/2>0\).  Independence across units and the identities
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(W_i)=\frac14,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(W_i,1-W_i)=-\frac14
\]
give
\[
\Pi=\frac12 I_6,
\qquad
\Omega=\frac14
\begin{bmatrix}
I_3&-I_3\\
-I_3&I_3
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The normalized regular class is nonempty.  Indeed, take
\[
b_1=\mathbf 1,
\qquad
b_0=\sqrt{\frac32}(1,-1,0)^{\top}.
\]
Then \(\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=3\), \(b_1^{\top}b_0=0\), and direct substitution into \(S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\) yields
\[
S_B=\frac34 I_2,
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=\frac14>\kappa=\frac18.
\]
This verifies the normalization, rank, positivity, and boundary conditions needed below; in particular, every inverse used for \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\) exists.

\textbf{First marginal pair.}
If \((F_1,F_0)=(\delta_0,\delta_0)\), then \(\mu_1=\mu_0=\sigma_1^2=\sigma_0^2=0\).  Both quantile products vanish, so \(c_L=c_U=0\), and the definition of the endpoint moment matrix gives \(Q(0)=0\).  Therefore the baseline term, the gain, and the residual all vanish for every feasible basis:
\[
n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\right)
=g_B(0)=\ell_B(0)=w(B)=0.
\]
It follows that
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)=0.
\]

\textbf{Second marginal pair.}
If \((F_1,F_0)=(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),\delta_0)\), then
\[
\mu_1=\frac12,
\qquad
\sigma_1^2=\frac14,
\qquad
\mu_0=\sigma_0^2=0.
\]
Because the second marginal is degenerate at zero, its quantile function is identically zero.  Hence again \(c_L=c_U=0\).  In this case
\[
Q(0)=
\begin{bmatrix}
A&0\\
0&0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A=\frac14\left(\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}+I_3\right).
\]
For an arbitrary \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), put \(U=[b_1,-b_0]\in\mathbb R^{3\times2}\).  The displayed form of \(\Omega\) and \(\Pi^{-1}=2I_6\) imply
\[
S_B=\frac14U^{\top}U,
\qquad
\Pi^{-1}\Omega B=\frac12
\begin{bmatrix}
U\\
-U
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The regularity condition makes \(U^{\top}U\) positive definite, so \(U\) has rank two.  Substitution in the gain definition gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_B(0)
&=\frac1{9}\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
\left(\frac14U^{\top}U\right)^{-1}
\left(\frac14U^{\top}AU\right)
\right]\\
&=\frac1{9}\operatorname{tr}(P_UA),
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
P_U=U(U^{\top}U)^{-1}U^{\top}
\]
is the rank-two orthogonal projector onto the column space of \(U\).

The eigenvalues of \(A\) are \(1,1/4,1/4\): the eigenvector \(\mathbf 1\) has eigenvalue \(1\), and every vector orthogonal to \(\mathbf 1\) has eigenvalue \(1/4\).  If \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) is a corresponding orthonormal eigenbasis and \(p_j=e_j^{\top}P_Ue_j\), then \(0\le p_j\le1\) and \(p_1+p_2+p_3=\operatorname{tr}(P_U)=2\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P_UA)
=p_1+\frac14p_2+\frac14p_3
\le1+\frac14=\frac54.
\]
Equality is attained by the feasible basis displayed above: its two columns span \(\mathbf 1\) and a nonzero vector orthogonal to \(\mathbf 1\).  Consequently,
\[
\max_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}g_B(0)=\frac1{9}\cdot\frac54=\frac5{36}.
\]
The basis-free term in the residual is
\[
\begin{aligned}
h(0)
&=\frac1{9}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\right)\\
&=\frac1{9}\operatorname{tr}(A)
=\frac1{9}\left(1+\frac14+\frac14\right)
=\frac16.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since the two covariance endpoints coincide at zero, \(w(B)=\ell_B(0)=h(0)-g_B(0)\).  Thus
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)
=\frac16-\frac5{36}
=\frac1{36}.
\]

\textbf{Information obstruction.}
The assignment law, exposure maps, \(\Pi\), \(\Omega\), covariance endpoints, \(\kappa\), and \(\varepsilon\) are identical in the two instances.  Their endpoint matrices are not identical: this is precisely the information deleted from the restricted input.  Any universally valid interval returned from that common restricted input would have to contain both \(0\) and \(1/36\), and hence would satisfy
\[
U_{\varepsilon}-L_{\varepsilon}\ge\frac1{36}>\frac1{100}=\varepsilon,
\]
contradicting the required certificate width.  The argument is confined to the moment-omitting restriction and therefore makes no claim about a solver that receives \(Q(c_L)\) and \(Q(c_U)\).

Finally, the causal estimand remains the fixed finite-population exposure contrast \(\tau\).  The quantity minimized here is an ex ante design criterion for choosing a control-variate basis; it is not an identifying formula for \(\tau\).  \(\square\)
