\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(c_e=c_L\) for \(e=L\) and \(c_e=c_U\) for \(e=U\).

\emph{Step 1: compact algebraic feasible set and exact boundary feasibility.}
For normalized representatives define
\[
K_\kappa=
\left\{(b_1,b_0):
\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=n,
\quad S_B-n\kappa I_2\succeq0
\right\}.
\]
Assumption ass:kappa-feasibility makes \(K_\kappa\) nonempty.  It is a closed subset of the product of two radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres and hence is compact.  Proposition prop:kappa-exhaustion identifies its projective quotient with the compact set \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) and gives \(\mathcal B_\kappa\subseteq\mathcal B_Z\).  In particular, every feasible \(B\) satisfies
\[
S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2\succ0. \tag{1}
\]
Thus every inverse appearing in the loss is defined, including at the equality boundary \(\lambda_{\min}(n^{-1}S_B)=\kappa\).

On either radius-\(\sqrt n\) sphere, at least one coordinate has absolute value at least one: otherwise the sum of the \(n\) squared coordinates would be less than \(n\).  The finitely many signed pivot regions \(\{b_j\ge1\}\) and \(\{-b_j\ge1\}\), used separately in the two arm factors, therefore cover the normalized projective domain.  Rational boxes in these charts, together with the sphere equations, give a finite initial cover.  By lem:algebraic-box-feasibility-cad, intersection of every such box with \(K_\kappa\) is decided exactly; a nonempty intersection yields a real-algebraic feasible sample even if it is contained wholly in the equality boundary in (1).

\emph{Step 2: an effective uniform modulus for the endpoint loss.}
For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), set
\[
R_e=\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(c_e)\Pi^{-1}\Omega,
\qquad
S(B)=B^{\top}\Omega B,
\qquad
T_e(B)=B^{\top}R_eB.
\]
The input is rational or real-algebraic.  Assumption ass:exposure-positivity therefore permits exact sign verification of every diagonal entry of \(\Pi\), so \(R_e\) is well defined and has real-algebraic entries.  Algebraic-number isolation supplies rational bounds
\[
M_\Omega\ge\lVert\Omega\rVert_2,
\qquad
M_e\ge\lVert R_e\rVert_2.
\]
Every normalized block matrix has \(\lVert B\rVert_F=\sqrt{2n}\).  Hence, for feasible normalized representatives \(B,C\) and \(d=\lVert B-C\rVert_F\), the identity
\[
B^{\top}AB-C^{\top}AC
=(B-C)^{\top}AB+C^{\top}A(B-C)
\]
and submultiplicativity give
\[
\lVert S(B)-S(C)\rVert_2\le K_Sd,
\qquad
\lVert T_e(B)-T_e(C)\rVert_2\le K_{T,e}d, \tag{2}
\]
where one valid choice is
\[
K_S=2\sqrt{2n}\,M_\Omega,
\qquad
K_{T,e}=2\sqrt{2n}\,M_e.
\]
By (1),
\[
\lVert S(B)^{-1}\rVert_2\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
\]
The resolvent identity
\[
S(B)^{-1}-S(C)^{-1}
=S(B)^{-1}\{S(C)-S(B)\}S(C)^{-1}
\]
and (2) imply
\[
\lVert S(B)^{-1}-S(C)^{-1}\rVert_2
\le (n\kappa)^{-2}K_Sd. \tag{3}
\]
Moreover \(\lVert T_e(C)\rVert_2\le2nM_e\).  Using
\(\lvert\operatorname{tr}(XY)\rvert\le2\lVert X\rVert_2\lVert Y\rVert_2\)
for two-by-two matrices in the decomposition
\[
S(B)^{-1}T_e(B)-S(C)^{-1}T_e(C)
=S(B)^{-1}\{T_e(B)-T_e(C)\}
+\{S(B)^{-1}-S(C)^{-1}\}T_e(C),
\]
we obtain
\[
\lvert g_B(c_e)-g_C(c_e)\rvert\le L_ed,
\quad
L_e=n^{-2}\left\{
\frac{2K_{T,e}}{n\kappa}
+\frac{4nM_eK_S}{(n\kappa)^2}
\right\}. \tag{4}
\]
The uncontrolled term \(h(c_e)\) is independent of \(B\), and the map \((x,y)\mapsto\max\{x,y\}\) is one-Lipschitz for the sup norm.  Therefore
\[
\lvert w(B)-w(C)\rvert
\le L\lVert B-C\rVert_F,
\qquad L=\max\{L_L,L_U\}. \tag{5}
\]
All quantities in (4) are effectively bounded from the algebraic input, so algebraic isolation produces and records a rational \(\overline L\ge L\).

\emph{Step 3: finite subdivision and value certificate.}
Because \(\varepsilon>0\) is exactly encoded, effective algebraic isolation produces a rational \(\alpha>0\) with \(4\alpha<\varepsilon\).  Uniform bisection of the finite chart cover reaches, after finitely many subdivisions, a depth at which every box \(C\) has a recorded rational representative-coordinate diameter \(d_C\) satisfying
\[
d_C\le\varepsilon,
\qquad
\overline Ld_C\le\alpha. \tag{6}
\]
There are finitely many boxes at that depth.  The feasibility lemma supplies an exact infeasibility certificate for each empty box and an exact real-algebraic point \(B_C\in C\cap K_\kappa\) for every nonempty box.  Since \(w(B_C)\) is the maximum of two real-algebraic numbers, outward algebraic isolation terminates with rational numbers \(\underline q_C,\overline q_C\) such that
\[
w(B_C)-\alpha\le\underline q_C\le w(B_C)
\le\overline q_C\le w(B_C)+\alpha. \tag{7}
\]
For a feasible box put
\[
\lambda_C=\underline q_C-\overline Ld_C.
\]
Equations (5)--(7) give the valid box lower bound
\[
\lambda_C\le
\inf_{B\in C\cap K_\kappa}w(B). \tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
U_\varepsilon=\min_C\overline q_C,
\qquad
L_\varepsilon=\min_C\lambda_C,
\]
where the minima range over feasible boxes, and take \(B_\varepsilon\) to be the real-algebraic sample attaining the first minimum.  Compactness and continuity from (5) supply a minimizer \(B^\star\).  For every feasible \(C\), (6)--(7) give
\[
\lambda_C\ge w(B_C)-2\alpha\ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}-2\alpha.
\]
For a box containing \(B^\star\), (5)--(7) give
\[
\overline q_C\le w(B_C)+\alpha
\le w(B^\star)+2\alpha
=v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\alpha.
\]
Together with (8) and feasibility of the incumbent, these inequalities yield
\[
v_{\mathrm{mm}}-2\alpha
\le L_\varepsilon\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}
\le w(B_\varepsilon)\le U_\varepsilon
\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\alpha,
\]
and hence
\[
U_\varepsilon-L_\varepsilon\le4\alpha<\varepsilon. \tag{9}
\]
This construction is a valid fair instance of the projective interval branch-and-bound procedure in def:branch-bound-handle: it may perform the uniform finite refinement just described before applying objective pruning.  Consequently the stopping tests are reached after finitely many subdivisions.

\emph{Step 4: outer minimizer set and local checking.}
Discard an exactly feasible box by objective pruning only when \(\lambda_C>U_\varepsilon\).  By (8), no such box contains a global minimizer, because a box containing a minimizer satisfies
\[
\lambda_C\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}\le U_\varepsilon.
\]
Let \(\mathcal C_\varepsilon\) be the union of the quotient images of all unpruned feasible boxes.  Then
\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\subseteq\mathcal C_\varepsilon.
\]
The chart metric is computed from the normalized representative coordinates, so (6) also gives \(h_\varepsilon\le\varepsilon\).

The finite record consists only of the chart cover, CAD sign and feasibility records, algebraic incumbent coordinates, outward rational loss bounds, rational norm and diameter bounds, pruning comparisons, and mesh.  A checker verifies these finite records.  Compactness, (5), (8), and the pruning implication then establish the claims involving the unknown mathematical objects \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\); those claims are not supplied as checker premises.  Boundary-only feasibility is covered by the weak polynomial inequalities and the exact CAD samples from Step 1.  Finally, for any sequence \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), (9) and \(h_\varepsilon\le\varepsilon\) force both certificate gaps and meshes to converge to zero.  The construction is finite for each input but supplies neither a uniform complexity bound nor a semidefinite-program reduction. \(\square\)
