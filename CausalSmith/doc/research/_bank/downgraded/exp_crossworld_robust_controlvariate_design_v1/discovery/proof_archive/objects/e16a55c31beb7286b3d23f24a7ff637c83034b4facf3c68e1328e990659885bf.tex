\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(m=m_1\wedge m_0\).  We first control the empirical marginal functionals.  For probability laws \(R,S\) on \([0,1]\), the one-dimensional area identity gives
\[
\int_0^1|R^{-1}(u)-S^{-1}(u)|\,du
=\int_0^1|R(y)-S(y)|\,dy.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, both sides are the Lebesgue area of the symmetric difference between the two subgraphs.  For each empirical distribution function, Jensen's inequality and the binomial variance formula yield
\[
\mathbb E_P|\widehat F_a(y)-F_a(y)|
\le \sqrt{\frac{F_a(y)\{1-F_a(y)\}}{m_a}}
\le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{m_a}}.
\]
Integrating and using (1) gives
\[
\mathbb E_P W_1(\widehat F_a,F_a)\le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{m_a}}.
\tag{2}
\]
The functions \(y\mapsto y\) and \(y\mapsto y^2\) are Lipschitz on \([0,1]\).  Hence the empirical means, second moments, and variances all have \(L^1(P)\) error of order \(m_a^{-1/2}\).

For \(x,x',y,y'\in[0,1]\),
\[
|xy-x'y'|\le |x-x'|+|y-y'|.
\tag{3}
\]
Apply (3) to the empirical and population quantile functions.  The comonotone product integrals differ by at most
\[
W_1(\widehat F_1,F_1)+W_1(\widehat F_0,F_0).
\]
Replacing the second quantile argument by \(1-u\) proves the same bound for the countermonotone product integral.  Combining these bounds with the empirical mean-product error gives, for \(e\in\{L,U\}\),
\[
|\widehat c_e-c_e|+
\sum_{a\in\{1,0\}}
\bigl(|\widehat\mu_a-\mu_a|+|\widehat\sigma_a^2-\sigma_a^2|\bigr)
=O_{L^1}(m^{-1/2}).
\tag{4}
\]
Every block of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) is a scalar appearing in (4) times either \(I_n\) or \(\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\).  Their operator norms are \(1\) and \(n\), respectively.  Thus
\[
M_n:=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_{L^1}\!\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt m}\right).
\tag{5}
\]

Next define
\[
A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1},
\qquad
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B(B^{\top}\Omega B)^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(Z=\Omega^{1/2}B\), the matrix
\[
P_Z=Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top}
\]
is an orthogonal projector because every \(B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\) has \(B^{\top}\Omega B\succ0\).  Consequently
\[
0\preceq A_B
=\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}P_Z\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}
\preceq A_{\mathrm{HT}}.
\tag{7}
\]
The nuclear norm of a positive semidefinite matrix is its trace, so (7) implies
\[
\lVert A_B\rVert_*\le\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_*.
\tag{8}
\]
The dependency-graph condition makes every row of \(\Omega\) have at most \(\overline d n^\alpha\) possibly nonzero entries: two distinct nonadjacent coordinates are independent and therefore have covariance zero.  Since the coordinates are indicators, every covariance has absolute value at most one.  The exposure-rate condition also gives
\[
\lVert\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\underline\pi^{-1}n^\beta.
\]
The maximum-row-sum bound and the fact that \(A_{\mathrm{HT}}\) is a \(2n\)-dimensional positive semidefinite matrix therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_*
&\le2n\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\\
&\le2\overline d\,\underline\pi^{-2}
n^{1+\alpha+2\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (8)--(9) prove
\[
C_n=O(n^{\alpha+2\beta-1}).
\tag{10}
\]

Trace duality applied to (6) gives, for each endpoint,
\[
|\widehat h_e-h_e|
\le n^{-2}\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_*M_n,
\qquad
|\widehat g_B(c_e)-g_B(c_e)|
\le n^{-2}\lVert A_B\rVert_*M_n.
\tag{11}
\]
The map \((x_L,x_U)\mapsto\max\{x_L,x_U\}\) is one-Lipschitz in the maximum norm.  Since \(\ell_B=h-g_B\), (11) and the definition of \(C_n\) imply
\[
\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}}
|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|\le C_nM_n.
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to control the zero-covariance plug-in matrix rather than silently substituting an endpoint bound.  Let \(\widehat Q(0)\) be formed from the empirical means and variances with covariance set to zero, put
\[
\Delta_0=\widehat Q(0)-Q(0),
\qquad
\Delta_e=\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e),
\qquad
d_e=\widehat c_e-c_e,
\]
and define the exchange matrix
\[
E=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_n\\I_n&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The affine moment-matrix identity, applied once to the empirical marginals and once to the population marginals, gives
\[
\Delta_0=\Delta_e+d_eE.
\tag{13}
\]
The upper-right \(n\)-by-\(n\) block of \(\Delta_e\) is
\[
-\bigl(\widehat\mu_1\widehat\mu_0-\mu_1\mu_0\bigr)
\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}-d_eI_n.
\tag{14}
\]
Because \(n\ge3\), choose a unit vector \(z\perp\mathbf1\).  The matrix in (14) sends \(z\) to \(-d_ez\).  A block compression cannot have larger operator norm than its parent matrix, and hence
\[
|d_e|\le\lVert\Delta_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(\lVert E\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=1\), (13)--(15) show
\[
\lVert\Delta_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le2\lVert\Delta_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le2M_n.
\tag{16}
\]
Trace duality at zero covariance now proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_B|\widehat g_B(0)-g_B(0)|
&\le n^{-2}\sup_B\lVert A_B\rVert_*
\lVert\Delta_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\\
&\le2n^{-2}\sup_B\lVert A_B\rVert_*M_n
\le2C_nM_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the exact sensitivity condition makes \(n\) times both uniform errors converge to zero in \(L^1(P)\).  Finally, (5) and (10) give
\[
nC_nM_n
=O_{L^1}\!\left(
\frac{n^{1+\alpha+2\beta}}{\sqrt m}
\right),
\]
which tends to zero under the displayed pilot-growth condition.  This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
