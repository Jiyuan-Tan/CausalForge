\paragraph{Proof.}
Write \(J_3=\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}\) and
\[
K=\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The assignment law has support all of \(\{0,1\}^3\), assigning probability \(1/8\) to every assignment.  Since \(f_i(W)=W_i\),
\[
D_{i,1}=W_i,\qquad D_{i,0}=1-W_i.
\]
Thus the two exposure events are mutually exclusive and exhaustive for each unit.  Independence of the assignment coordinates, together with \(\mathbb E_\nu W_i=1/2\) and \(\operatorname{Var}_\nu(W_i)=1/4\), gives, entry by entry,
\[
\Pi=\tfrac12 I_6,
\qquad
\Omega=\operatorname{Var}_\nu(D)=\tfrac14K.
\]
In particular every exposure probability equals \(1/2>0\), so \(\Pi^{-1}=2I_6\) exists.

We first check nonemptiness and the rank boundary of the normalized feasible class.  Put
\[
x=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\qquad
y=\sqrt{\tfrac32}\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\qquad
B=\begin{bmatrix}x&0\\0&y\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\lVert x\rVert_2^2=\lVert y\rVert_2^2=3\) and \(x^{\top}y=0\).  Direct multiplication yields
\[
S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B
=\tfrac14\begin{bmatrix}3&-x^{\top}y\\-y^{\top}x&3\end{bmatrix}
=\tfrac34I_2,
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=\tfrac14>\tfrac18=\kappa.
\]
Hence \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is nonempty.  Moreover, every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\) satisfies \(\lambda_{\min}(S_B)\ge 3/8>0\).  Thus all such bases lie in the rank-two stratum, the ordinary inverse is defined even at the \(\kappa\)-boundary, and Zhao's spectral pseudoinverse agrees there with the ordinary inverse.

For the first marginal pair, \((F_1,F_0)=(\delta_0,\delta_0)\), both means, both variances, and both quantile-product integrals vanish.  Therefore \(c_L=c_U=0\) and \(Q(0)=0\).  The definitions of \(h\), \(g_B\), and \(\ell_B\) then imply
\[
h(0)=g_B(0)=\ell_B(0)=0
\]
for every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\).  Since \(w(B)=\max\{\ell_B(c_L),\ell_B(c_U)\}\),
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)=0.
\]

For the second marginal pair, \((F_1,F_0)=(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),\delta_0)\), the comparison-arm quantile is identically zero.  Both quantile-product integrals are consequently zero, and \(\mu_0=0\), so again \(c_L=c_U=0\).  The marginal moments are
\[
\mu_1=\tfrac12,
\qquad \sigma_1^2=\tfrac14,
\qquad \mu_0=\sigma_0^2=0.
\]
Thus the endpoint moment matrix is
\[
Q(0)=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A=\tfrac14(I_3+J_3),
\]
which differs from the zero endpoint matrix of the first instance.  The two instances nevertheless have exactly the same tuple \((\Pi,\Omega,c_L,c_U,\kappa,\varepsilon)\).

It remains to compute the second minimax residual.  Fix \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), write its normalized blocks as \(x=b_1\) and \(y=b_0\), and define
\[
C=\begin{bmatrix}x&-y\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{3\times2}.
\]
Projective normalization gives \(\lVert x\rVert_2^2=\lVert y\rVert_2^2=3\), while block multiplication gives
\[
S_B=\tfrac14C^{\top}C.
\]
The \(\kappa\)-regularity just established makes \(C^{\top}C\) positive definite, so \(C\) has rank two.  Using \(\Pi^{-1}=2I_6\), \(\Omega=K/4\), and the displayed form of \(Q(0)\), direct multiplication also gives
\[
B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\Pi^{-1}\Omega B
=\tfrac14C^{\top}AC.
\]
Let
\[
P_C=C(C^{\top}C)^{-1}C^{\top}.
\]
This is the rank-two orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{col}(C)\).  The gain formula and cyclicity of trace yield
\[
g_B(0)
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(C^{\top}C)^{-1}C^{\top}AC\right]
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}(P_CA).
\]
Similarly,
\[
h(0)
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\right]
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}(A)
=\tfrac16.
\]

Set \(u=3^{-1/2}\mathbf1\).  Since \(J_3=3uu^{\top}\),
\[
A=\tfrac14I_3+\tfrac34uu^{\top}.
\]
For every rank-two orthogonal projector \(P_C\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P_CA)
=\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}(P_C)+\tfrac34u^{\top}P_Cu
=\tfrac12+\tfrac34\lVert P_Cu\rVert_2^2
\le\tfrac54.
\]
Therefore every feasible basis obeys
\[
\ell_B(0)=h(0)-g_B(0)
\ge\tfrac16-\tfrac19\cdot\tfrac54
=\tfrac1{36}.
\]
For the strictly feasible basis constructed above, \(x=\sqrt3u\) and \(y\perp u\).  Hence \(u\in\operatorname{col}(C)\), so \(\lVert P_Cu\rVert_2=1\) and equality is attained.  Since the endpoint interval is the singleton \(\{0\}\), \(w(B)=\ell_B(0)\), proving
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)=\tfrac1{36}.
\]

Finally, an algorithm receiving only \((\Pi,\Omega,c_L,c_U,\kappa,\varepsilon)\) receives identical encoded input on the two instances.  Any interval that is always valid from that input must contain both possible values \(0\) and \(1/36\).  Its width must therefore satisfy
\[
U_\varepsilon-L_\varepsilon\ge\tfrac1{36}>\tfrac1{100}=\varepsilon,
\]
contradicting the requested certificate width.  This proves that the endpoint moment matrices, or equivalent marginal moments, are necessary inputs.  The argument is an input-indistinguishability obstruction only and makes no termination claim for a moment-complete solver.

The causal estimand remains the fixed finite-population contrast \(\tau=n^{-1}\sum_i\{Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\}\).  The quantity \(w(B)\) used here is an ex ante basis-design criterion, not an identifying definition of \(\tau\).  \(\square\)
