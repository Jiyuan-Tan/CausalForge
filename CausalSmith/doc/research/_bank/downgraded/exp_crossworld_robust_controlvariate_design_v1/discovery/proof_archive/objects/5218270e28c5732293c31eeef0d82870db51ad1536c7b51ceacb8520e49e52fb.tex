\textbf{Proof.}
By ass:exposure-positivity, \(\Pi\) is invertible. Assumptions ass:product-coupling-zero and ass:frechet-covariance-domain give \(0\in\mathcal C(F_1,F_0)=[c_L,c_U]\), so every occurrence of \(Q(0)\), \(h(0)\), and \(g_B(0)\) below is well typed. If \(B\in\mathcal B_Z\), the nondegeneracy condition in def:zhao-basis-class gives
\[
S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\succ0,
\]
so \(S_B^{-1}\) exists. Introduce
\[
E=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_n\\ I_n&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1},\qquad
A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
The construction in def:moment-matrix gives
\[
Q(c)=Q(0)-cE. \tag{1}
\]

For each unit \(i\), ass:mutually-exclusive-exposures gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{i,0})
=\mathbb E_{\nu}(D_{i,1}D_{i,0})-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}
=-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}.
\]
Division by the positive exposure probabilities yields
\[
(A_{\mathrm{HT}})_{i,n+i}=(A_{\mathrm{HT}})_{n+i,i}=-1.
\]
The off-diagonal identity blocks of \(E\) select exactly these paired entries, so
\[
\operatorname{tr}(A_{\mathrm{HT}}E)=-2n. \tag{2}
\]
Using (1), (2), cyclicity of trace, and def:gain-loss,
\[
\begin{aligned}
h(c)
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{A_{\mathrm{HT}}Q(c)\}\\
&=h(0)-c n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_{\mathrm{HT}}E)
=h(0)+\frac{2c}{n}, \tag{3}\\
g_B(c)
&=g_B(0)-c n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_BE), \tag{4}\\
\ell_B(c)
&=\ell_B(0)+c\left\{\frac2n+n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_BE)\right\}. \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus all three functions are affine. Assumptions ass:known-exposure and ass:iid-potential-pairs supply the causal-exposure and ex ante moment interpretations; the affine calculation uses only the displayed design identities.

By ass:frechet-covariance-domain and the definition of the covariance image,
\[
\left\{\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}:H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\right\}
=[c_L,c_U]. \tag{6}
\]
Every affine real function on a closed interval reaches each extremum at an endpoint. Equality in (6) also says that both endpoints are attained by couplings in \(\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\). Applying these facts to (4) and (5) proves the endpoint formulas and their attainment.

By ass:product-coupling-zero, the product coupling belongs to \(\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\) and has covariance zero. Together with (6), this gives \(c_L\le0\le c_U\), which types \(g_B(0)\) on the feasible domain. Write \(g_B(c)=\alpha_B+\beta_Bc\). If \(\beta_B=0\), the gain is constant. Conversely, if \(c_L<0<c_U\), then \(\beta_B>0\) implies \(g_B(c_L)<g_B(0)\), while \(\beta_B<0\) implies \(g_B(c_U)<g_B(0)\). Hence the zero-covariance gain is worst-coupling exactly when \(\beta_B=0\).

Finally, substituting (3) into \(\ell_B(c_e)=h(c_e)-g_B(c_e)\) proves the displayed formula for \(w(B)\); the formula for \(r(B)\) is def:robust-objective. When \(c_L<c_U\),
\[
h(c_U)-h(c_L)=\frac{2(c_U-c_L)}n>0.
\]
Therefore there is no basis-independent constant \(K\) for which the endpoint identity reduces to \(w(B)=K-r(B)\) by matching the two endpoint baselines. This proves the stated algebraic distinction. \(\square\)
