Endpoint covariances alone do not determine the minimax worst-copula residual value.  In particular, let \(n=3\), let \(W_1,W_2,W_3\) be independent Bernoulli \(1/2\) variables under \(\nu\), and set \(f_i(w)=w_i\).  With \(\kappa=1/8\) and \(\varepsilon=1/100\), the marginal pairs \((F_1,F_0)=(\delta_0,\delta_0)\) and \((F_1,F_0)=(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),\delta_0)\) produce the same moment-incomplete tuple
\[
\Pi=\tfrac12 I_6,\qquad
\Omega=\tfrac14\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix},
\qquad c_L=c_U=0,
\]
but their endpoint moment matrices differ and their minimax residual values \(v^\star=\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)\) are \(0\) and \(1/36\), respectively.  Consequently, no algorithm whose only input is \((\Pi,\Omega,c_L,c_U,\kappa,\varepsilon)\) can always return rational bounds \(L_{\varepsilon}\le v^\star\le U_{\varepsilon}\) with \(U_{\varepsilon}-L_{\varepsilon}\le\varepsilon\).  Thus endpoint moment matrices such as \(Q(c_L)\) and \(Q(c_U)\), or equivalent marginal moments, are necessary inputs for the proposed certification problem; this obstruction makes no claim that the corrected moment-complete branch-and-bound solver terminates at every positive tolerance.
