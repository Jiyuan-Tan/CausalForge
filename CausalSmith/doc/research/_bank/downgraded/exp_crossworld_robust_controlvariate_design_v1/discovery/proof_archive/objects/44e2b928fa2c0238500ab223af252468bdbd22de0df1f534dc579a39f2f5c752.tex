\textbf{Proof.}
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:coordinate-bounded-kappa-feasibility}\) makes \(\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\) nonempty.  Put \(N=2n\), index the coordinates of \(D\) by \(\ell\), and write \(Z_\ell=D_\ell-\pi_\ell\).  We first derive the only moment bounds used below directly from Zhao's local condition.

If an index \(\ell\) occurs exactly once in an ordered triple or quadruple and every other distinct index lies outside \(N_\ell\), then \(Z_\ell\) is independent of the product of the remaining centered coordinates.  Since \(\mathbb E_\nu Z_\ell=0\), that mixed moment vanishes.  For a nonvanishing ordered triple, each singleton index must therefore have a directed neighbor among the other distinct indices.  With self-membership \(\ell\in N_\ell\), every such triple is a permutation of a directed walk of length two.  There are at most \(N d_n^2\) such walks and at most six permutations, so there are at most \(6Nd_n^2\le8Nd_n^2\) contributing ordered triples.

For four coordinates, partition by the multiplicities of the distinct indices.  The types with no singleton are \(4\) and \(2+2\).  In each remaining type, attach to every singleton a directed edge to one of the other distinct indices in its neighborhood.  There are at most two freely chosen coordinate labels and at most two directed-neighborhood choices; in the all-distinct connected case, the equivalent bound \(Nd_n^3\le N^2d_n^2\) is used.  The five multiplicity types and the possible one-cycle or two-cycle directed patterns give fewer than \(64\) positional patterns.  Hence at most \(64N^2d_n^2\) ordered quadruples can contribute.  Since \(|Z_\ell|\le1\), expansion at the sign-vector extreme points in \(\texttt{def:rank-adaptive-stability-strata}\) yields
\[
\mathfrak M_{3,n}\le8Nd_n^2,
\qquad
\mathfrak M_{4,n}\le64N^2d_n^2.
\tag{1}
\]
This argument uses only singleton-versus-complement independence; it does not use separated-set independence.

The same local condition gives the covariance-column bound.  If \(k\notin N_\ell\), then \(D_\ell\) is independent of \(D_k\), so \(\Omega_{k\ell}=0\).  Therefore
\[
s_n\le d_n\le\overline d n^\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]

Fix a bounded exposure-potential-outcome array and \(B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\).  On \(D_{i,a}=1\), \(\texttt{ass:known-exposure}\) gives \(Y_i(W)=Y_i(a)\).  With the signed coordinate convention in \(Y\),
\[
\mathbb E_\nu\widehat Y_\ell^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_\ell,
\qquad
\{\Pi^{-1}(\widehat Y^{\mathrm{obs}}-Y)\}_\ell
=\frac{Y_\ell Z_\ell}{\pi_\ell^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the possible exposure-mismatch term is identically zero.  On this rank-two class the spectral pseudoinverse is the ordinary inverse.  Consequently, with
\[
G_B:=\widehat\gamma^{\mathrm{HT}}(B)-\gamma_B^*(Y),
\]
equation (3) gives
\[
G_B=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}u_{B,\ell}Z_\ell,
\qquad
u_{B,\ell}:=S_B^{-1}B^\top\Omega e_\ell\frac{Y_\ell}{\pi_\ell^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every covariance entry has absolute value at most one, every covariance column has at most \(s_n\le d_n\) nonzero entries, \(\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}\), and the basis coordinates are bounded by \(\overline a\).  Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:zhao-exposure-probability-rate}\) and (2) therefore give a finite constant \(K_u\), independent of \(n,B,Y,H\), such that
\[
\sup_{B,\ell}\lVert u_{B,\ell}\rVert_2
\le K_u n^{\alpha+2\beta-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Also
\[
X_B=n^{-1}B^\top(D-\mathbb E_\nu D)
=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}x_{B,\ell}Z_\ell,
\qquad
\sup_{B,\ell}\lVert x_{B,\ell}\rVert_2\le\overline a/n.
\tag{6}
\]
Applying the fourth-moment envelope and (1) to the two coordinates of (4) and (6), and using \((z_1^2+z_2^2)^2\le2z_1^4+2z_2^4\), gives constants \(K_G,K_X<\infty\) for which
\[
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert G_B\rVert_2^4
\le K_Gn^{6\alpha+8\beta-2},
\qquad
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert X_B\rVert_2^4
\le K_Xn^{2\alpha-2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The feasible and oracle estimators obey
\[
\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{CV}}
=\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}+\Delta_B,
\qquad
\Delta_B=-G_B^\top X_B.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and (7) yield a finite \(K_\Delta\) such that
\[
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\Delta_B^2
\le K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}.
\tag{8}
\]

Put \(T_B=\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}-\tau\).  Equation (3) makes the Horvitz--Thompson part unbiased, and the basis exposure statistic is centered, so \(\mathbb E_\nu T_B=0\).  Direct expansion gives
\[
T_B=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}t_{B,\ell}Z_\ell,
\qquad
t_{B,\ell}=n^{-1}\{Y_\ell/\pi_\ell-B_{\ell\cdot}\gamma_B^*(Y)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Writing the oracle coefficient as an orthogonal projection in the \(\Omega^{1/2}\)-geometry gives
\[
\gamma_B^*(Y)^\top S_B\gamma_B^*(Y)
\le Y^\top\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Y.
\tag{10}
\]
By (2), at most \(Ns_n\) covariance entries are nonzero.  Bounded outcomes, exposure positivity, and \(S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2\) therefore imply finite constants \(K_\gamma,K_t\) for which
\[
\sup_B\lVert\gamma_B^*(Y)\rVert_2
\le K_\gamma n^{\alpha/2+\beta},
\qquad
\sup_{B,\ell}|t_{B,\ell}|
\le K_tn^{\alpha/2+\beta-1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Expanding \(\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)=\mathbb E_\nu(T_B\Delta_B\mid Y)\) in the two coefficient coordinates, then applying the third-moment envelope, (1), (5), (6), and (11), gives a finite \(K_{\mathrm{cov}}\) such that
\[
\sup_B|\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)|
\le K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
\tag{12}
\]
The exponent is the sum of the factors \(Nd_n^2\), \(\sup|t_{B,\ell}|\), \(\sup\lVert u_{B,\ell}\rVert_2\), and \(\sup\lVert x_{B,\ell}\rVert_2\).

The variance identity
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(T_B+\Delta_B\mid Y)-\operatorname{Var}_\nu(T_B\mid Y)
=\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\Delta_B\mid Y)+2\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)
\]
together with (8)--(12) proves the first uniform bound with
\[
R_n=K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}
+2K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), \((7/2)\alpha+3\beta\le4\alpha+4\beta\).  Hence, with \(K_R=K_\Delta+2K_{\mathrm{cov}}\),
\[
0\le R_n\le K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}.
\tag{14}
\]
All constants may depend on the fixed values \(\kappa,\overline a,\underline\pi,\overline d\), but not on \(n,B,Y\), or \(H\).

It remains to identify the oracle variance.  Completing the square for the fixed oracle coefficient gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
=n^{-2}Y^\top\{\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}
-\Pi^{-1}\Omega BS_B^{-1}B^\top\Omega\Pi^{-1}\}Y.
\tag{15}
\]
Under \(\texttt{ass:iid-potential-pairs}\),
\[
\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}(YY^\top)=Q(c_H).
\tag{16}
\]
Taking the trace expectation in (15), using (16), and applying \(\texttt{def:gain-loss}\) proves
\[
\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
=h(c_H)-g_B(c_H)=\ell_B(c_H).
\tag{17}
\]
Integrating the first uniform bound over \(H^{\otimes n}\) and applying (17) proves the second uniform bound.  Finally,
\[
nR_n\le K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-1}\longrightarrow0
\]
by \(\texttt{ass:zhao-first-order-rate}\).  Every inner variance is conditional design variance under \(\nu\); the expectation under \(H^{\otimes n}\) is only the declared ex ante basis-performance average. \(\square\)
