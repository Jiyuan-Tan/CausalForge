\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1_3\).  We first enumerate the support of the
randomization.  The all-zero and all-one assignments expose no unit to either
of the two displayed labels.  If only unit \(i\) is treated, then
\(D_{i,1}=1\) and \(D_{j,0}=D_{k,0}=1\).  If exactly \(i\) and \(j\) are
treated, then \(D_{k,0}=1\).  These events are mutually exclusive within each
unit, as required by the mutual-exclusivity assumption, and the known-exposure
assumption makes their labels well-defined potential-outcome exposures.  Their
probabilities give
\[
 \pi_{i,1}=\frac4{27},
 \qquad
 \pi_{i,0}=2\frac4{27}+\frac2{27}=\frac{10}{27}.
 \tag{1}
\]
In particular, exposure positivity holds at this instance.

The same enumeration gives the following covariance entries, where the first
column in each row is the diagonal entry and the second is an off-diagonal
entry:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &i=j&i\ne j\\ \hline
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{j,1})&92/729&-16/729\\
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{j,0})&-40/729&68/729\\
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,0},D_{j,0})&170/729&8/729.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
For example, when \(i\ne j\), the event
\(D_{i,1}D_{j,0}=1\) is the singleton assignment treating \(i\), so its
probability is \(4/27=108/729\); subtracting
\((4/27)(10/27)=40/729\) gives \(68/729\).  For another check,
\(D_{i,0}D_{j,0}=1\) occurs at the singleton assignment treating the third
unit, so its covariance is \(108/729-100/729=8/729\).  Equations (1)--(2)
are exactly the displayed matrices \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\).

For either Bernoulli \((1/2)\) marginal,
\(\mu_a=1/2\) and \(\sigma_a^2=1/4\).  The comonotone quantile product has
integral \(1/2\), whereas the countermonotone quantile product is zero almost
everywhere.  The sharp fixed-marginal covariance endpoints therefore are
\[
 c_L=0-\frac14=-\frac14,
 \qquad
 c_U=\frac12-\frac14=\frac14.
 \tag{3}
\]
Substitution of these moments and (3) into the definition of \(Q(c)\) gives
the two endpoint matrices in the statement.

We next solve both optimization problems over their full equality sets.  For a
normalized representative \(B(x,y)\), put
\[
 s=\mathbf1^{\top}x,
 \qquad
 t=\mathbf1^{\top}y,
 \qquad
 q=x^{\top}y.
\]
The normalization and Cauchy--Schwarz inequality imply
\(0\le s^2,t^2\le9\).  Direct block multiplication using (2) gives
\[
 S_B=
 \begin{bmatrix}
  \dfrac49-\dfrac{16s^2}{729}&
  -\dfrac{4q}{27}+\dfrac{68st}{729}\\[4pt]
  -\dfrac{4q}{27}+\dfrac{68st}{729}&
  \dfrac23+\dfrac{8t^2}{729}
 \end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
 d=729+12t^2-36s^2-54q^2+68qst-22s^2t^2.
\]
Then
\[
 \det(S_B)=\frac{8d}{19683}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Every member of \(\mathcal B_{1/8}\) has \(S_B\succ0\), hence (5) gives
\(d>0\).  This verifies the rank and division condition used below.

Substitution of (1)--(4) into the gain definition, followed by inversion of
the \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix in (4), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g_B(c_L)
 &=\frac{76545+9891s^2+30732t^2-5832q^2
          +18232qst-10460s^2t^2}{1200d},\\
 g_B(c_U)
 &=\frac{3\{66825-5181s^2-2652t^2-6048q^2
          +5968qst-1260s^2t^2\}}{400d}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The uncontrolled endpoint variances obtained from the same substitution are
\[
 h(c_L)=\frac{313}{600},
 \qquad
 h(c_U)=\frac{171}{200}.
\tag{7}
\]
All quantities in (6)--(7) are thus explicit rational functions of
\((s,t,q)\), with the denominator positive by (5).

Because \(Q(c)\) is affine in \(c\) and zero is the midpoint of the two
endpoints, the gain satisfies
\(g_B(0)=\{g_B(c_L)+g_B(c_U)\}/2\).  Simplifying (6) gives
\[
 \frac{31}{80}-g_B(0)=\frac{N_0}{1200d},
 \tag{8}
\]
where
\[
 N_0
 =5022\left(q-\frac{1088}{2511}st\right)^2
  +1629s^2+2148t^2-\frac{685118}{2511}s^2t^2.
 \tag{9}
\]
The last three terms of (9) form a bilinear function of
\((s^2,t^2)\in[0,9]^2\).  At the four vertices of this rectangle its values
are
\[
 0,
 \qquad
 14661,
 \qquad
 19332,
 \qquad
 \frac{368665}{31}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Bilinear interpolation of (10) shows that this function is nonnegative and is
zero only at \((s^2,t^2)=(0,0)\).  Together with the square in (9), this proves
\(N_0\ge0\), with equality exactly when \(s=t=q=0\).  Since \(d>0\), (8)
therefore proves
\[
 \max_{B\in\mathcal B_{1/8}}g_B(0)=\frac{31}{80}
\]
and gives precisely the asserted equality conditions for \(\mathcal A_0\).
They are attainable: choose orthogonal vectors of squared norm \(3\) in the
two-dimensional space \(\mathbf1^{\perp}\).  At every such equality point,
\(S_B=\operatorname{diag}(4/9,2/3)\), so
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=\frac4{27}>\frac18.
 \tag{11}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) then give
\[
 w(B_0)=\frac{521}{1200}
 \qquad(B_0\in\mathcal A_0).
 \tag{12}
\]

For the minimax residual problem, set
\[
 \lambda=\frac{1407}{2635}\in(0,1),
 \qquad
 v=\frac{2613}{8432}.
\]
The maximum of two real numbers dominates every convex combination of them.
Using \(\ell_B(c)=h(c)-g_B(c)\) and simplifying (6)--(7), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 w(B)-v
 &\ge \lambda\ell_B(c_L)+(1-\lambda)\ell_B(c_U)-v\\
 &=\frac{3N_{\mathrm{mm}}}{210800d},
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
where
\[
 N_{\mathrm{mm}}
 =274734\left(q-\frac{70411}{137367}st\right)^2
  +s^2\left(34527-\frac{439017980}{137367}t^2\right).
 \tag{14}
\]
The boundary condition \(t^2\le9\) implies
\[
 34527-\frac{439017980}{137367}t^2
 \ge \frac{87967621}{15263}>0.
 \tag{15}
\]
Thus (14) is nonnegative, and equality holds exactly when \(s=q=0\).
It follows from (13) that \(w(B)\ge v\) throughout the feasible class.

It remains to identify when the maximum in (13) equals its strict convex
combination.  Because both weights are positive, this happens exactly when
the two endpoint losses agree.  Under \(s=q=0\), direct subtraction of the
two expressions in (6)--(7) gives
\[
 \ell_B(c_L)-\ell_B(c_U)
 =-\frac{3(55t^2-216)}{10(4t^2+243)}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Consequently equality in (13) holds exactly when
\[
 s=q=0,
 \qquad
 t^2=\frac{216}{55}.
 \tag{17}
\]
These conditions are attainable.  Choose a vector \(y\) of squared norm
\(3\) whose coordinate sum has squared value \(216/55\); this is possible
because \(216/55\in(0,9)\).  Such a \(y\) is not proportional to
\(\mathbf1\), so one may choose a vector \(x\) of squared norm \(3\)
orthogonal to \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,y\}\).  At every point (17),
(4) is diagonal, its first entry is \(4/9\), and its second entry is strictly
larger than \(4/9\).  Hence (11) again holds.  Substitution in (6)--(7) gives
\[
 w(B_{\mathrm{mm}})=v_{\mathrm{mm}}=\frac{2613}{8432},
 \qquad B_{\mathrm{mm}}\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}},
 \tag{18}
\]
and (17) is exactly the full optimizer correspondence stated in the theorem.

Finally, (1) verifies exposure positivity, and either displayed optimizer
family supplies a basis satisfying the \(\kappa=1/8\) feasibility condition.
Thus \(\xi_0\in\mathfrak X_{\kappa}\).  Since the losses are constant on the
two full optimizer correspondences, (12) and (18) imply
\[
 \Delta(\xi_0)
 =\frac{521}{1200}-\frac{2613}{8432}
 =\frac{4912}{39525}>0.
 \tag{19}
\]
Moreover, (11) holds uniformly over their union, and therefore
\[
 \inf_{B\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0)\cup\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)}
 \left\{\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)-\frac18\right\}
 =\frac4{27}-\frac18=\frac5{216}>0.
 \tag{20}
\]
Equations (19)--(20) prove the claimed choices of \(\delta\) and \(\eta\).
In particular, the comparison is against every optimizer of the
zero-covariance surrogate, not merely against a selected candidate, while
the strict improvement is attained by the full, nondegenerate minimax
equality set.  \(\square\)
