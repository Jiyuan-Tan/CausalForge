\textbf{Proof.}
Let
\[
 K_\kappa=\left\{(b_1,b_0):\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=n,
 \ \lambda_{\min}\!\left(n^{-1}B(b_1,b_0)^{\top}\Omega B(b_1,b_0)\right)\ge\kappa\right\}.
\]
The product of the two radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres is compact.  The displayed matrix and its smallest eigenvalue depend continuously on \((b_1,b_0)\), so \(K_\kappa\) is closed and compact.  After normalization, independent sign changes are the residual projective identifications.  The quotient map is continuous; hence its image \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is compact.

If \([B]\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), then
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(B^{\top}\Omega B)\ge n\kappa>0.
\]
Thus \(S_B\) is positive definite, and def:zhao-basis-class gives \(\mathcal B_\kappa\subseteq\mathcal B_Z\).

Conversely, take \([B]\in\mathcal B_Z\).  Positive definiteness forces both basis columns to be nonzero.  Set
\[
 d_a=\frac{\sqrt n}{\lVert b_a\rVert_2},\qquad
 T=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_0),\qquad
 \widetilde B=BT.
\]
Then \(\widetilde B\) is a normalized representative of the same projective class and
\[
 S_{\widetilde B}=T^{\top}S_BT\succ0.
\]
Consequently \(\rho_B=\lambda_{\min}(n^{-1}S_{\widetilde B})>0\).  Taking \(\kappa_B=\rho_B/2\) gives \([B]\in\mathcal B_{\kappa_B}\).  Therefore
\[
 \bigcup_{\kappa>0}\mathcal B_\kappa=\mathcal B_Z.
\]
Every step begins with \(S_B\succ0\); no singular or Moore--Penrose basis is included. \(\square\)
