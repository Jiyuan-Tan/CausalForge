\textbf{Proof.}
Fix normalized representatives of the projective classes in \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  By prop:kappa-exhaustion and ass:kappa-feasibility this is a nonempty compact space.  For every such representative, the two columns of \(B\) have disjoint block supports and squared norm \(n\), while membership in \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) gives
\[
\lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt n,
\qquad
\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le (n\kappa)^{-1}.
\tag{1}
\]
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  All design matrices in what follows are fixed.

We first establish convergence of every pilot ingredient.  Write \(q_a=F_a^{-1}\) and \(\widehat q_a=\widehat F_a^{-1}\).  For a bounded i.i.d. sequence and any bounded scalar transform, the strong law follows here from a fourth-moment argument.  If \(X_j\) are independent, centered, and \(|X_j|\le1\), then
\[
\mathbb E\left(\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\right)^4
=\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb E X_j^4
+6\sum_{j<k}\mathbb E X_j^2\,\mathbb E X_k^2
\le 3m^2.
\]
Markov's inequality gives
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\left|m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\right|>t\right)
\le \frac{3}{m^2t^4}.
\]
The sum of these bounds over \(m\) is finite, and the union bound applied to all events with \(m\ge M\), followed by \(M\to\infty\), shows that only finitely many occur almost surely.  Apply this simultaneously, over the countable rational \(x\), to \(\mathbf 1\{Y^P_{j,a}\le x\}\).  Monotonicity then yields \(\widehat F_a(x)\to F_a(x)\) at every continuity point of \(F_a\).  The generalized-inverse squeeze yields \(\widehat q_a(u)\to q_a(u)\) at every continuity point of \(q_a\).  A monotone function has at most countably many discontinuities, and all quantiles lie in \([0,1]\); splitting the integral outside a set of arbitrarily small measure on which convergence is uniform therefore gives
\[
\int_0^1|\widehat q_a(u)-q_a(u)|\,du\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely},\qquad a\in\{1,0\}.
\tag{2}
\]
The same bounded strong law, applied to \(Y^P_{j,a}\) and \((Y^P_{j,a})^2\), gives \(\widehat\mu_a\to\mu_a\) and \(\widehat\sigma_a^2\to\sigma_a^2\) almost surely.  Since all quantiles are bounded by one,
\[
\left|\int_0^1\widehat q_1(u)\widehat q_0(u)\,du-
\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du\right|
\le\lVert\widehat q_1-q_1\rVert_1+\lVert\widehat q_0-q_0\rVert_1,
\tag{3}
\]
and the identical bound holds with the second quantile evaluated at \(1-u\).  Equations (2)--(3), together with convergence of the means, imply
\[
\widehat c_L\to c_L,
\qquad
\widehat c_U\to c_U
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{4}
\]
This uses only the two marginal samples and never estimates a cross-world copula.

For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), let \(Q_e=Q(c_e)\) and let \(\widehat Q_e\) be its plug-in version, including the plug-in means and variances.  The finite-dimensional formula in def:moment-matrix and (4) imply
\[
\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{5}
\]
Put \(C_\nu=\lVert\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\), which is finite because \(n\) is fixed and \(\Pi\) is invertible.  The inequality \(|\operatorname{tr}(A)|\le2\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) for a \(2\times2\) matrix, def:gain-loss, and (1) yield, uniformly over \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\),
\[
|\widehat g_B(\widehat c_e)-g_B(c_e)|
\le \frac{2}{n^2}\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2 C_\nu^2
\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le \frac{2C_\nu^2}{n^2\kappa}
\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{6}
\]
The minimum map is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm.  Hence (5)--(6) give
\[
\delta_m:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}
|\widehat r(B)-r(B)|\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely as }m_1\wedge m_0\to\infty.
\tag{7}
\]

Both endpoint gains are continuous on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\): matrix inversion is continuous on the uniformly positive-definite set in (1), and the remaining operations are finite-dimensional and continuous.  Thus \(r\) and \(\widehat r\) are continuous and attain their maxima on the compact quotient.  The elementary maximum inequality and (7) show
\[
|\widehat v-v^\star|
=\left|\max_B\widehat r(B)-\max_Br(B)\right|
\le\delta_m\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{8}
\]
Finally, take any sequence with \(m_1\wedge m_0\to\infty\), any \(B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_R\), and a convergent subsequence \(B_{m_j}\to B_\infty\), which exists by compactness.  For any \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_R\), empirical optimality and (7) imply
\[
r(B_{m_j})\ge\widehat r(B_{m_j})-\delta_{m_j}
\ge\widehat r(B^\star)-\delta_{m_j}
\ge r(B^\star)-2\delta_{m_j}=v^\star-2\delta_{m_j}.
\]
Continuity gives \(r(B_\infty)=v^\star\), so \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_R\).  This is exactly \(\limsup\widehat{\mathcal A}_R\subseteq\mathcal A_R\) in the compact projective quotient, and proves all assertions. \(\square\)
