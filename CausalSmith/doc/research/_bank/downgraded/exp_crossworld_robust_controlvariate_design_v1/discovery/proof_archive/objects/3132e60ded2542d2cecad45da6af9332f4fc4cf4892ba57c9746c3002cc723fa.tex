\textbf{Proof.}
Assumption ass:coordinate-bounded-kappa-feasibility makes \(\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\) nonempty.  Index the \(N=2n\) exposure coordinates by \(j\), put \(Z_j=D_j-\pi_j\), and let \(d_n\le\overline d n^\alpha\) be the largest closed dependency neighborhood.  If a tuple of centered coordinates has a distinct index separated from all the others, its joint moment is zero.  Counting the remaining dependency-supported tuples and using \(|Z_j|\le1\) gives, for deterministic arrays \(q,r,s\),
\[
\mathbb E_\nu\left(\sum_jq_jZ_j\right)^4
\le C N^2d_n^2\lVert q\rVert_\infty^4,
\tag{1}
\]
\[
\left|\mathbb E_\nu
\left(\sum_jq_jZ_j\right)
\left(\sum_jr_jZ_j\right)
\left(\sum_js_jZ_j\right)\right|
\le C Nd_n^2\lVert q\rVert_\infty\lVert r\rVert_\infty\lVert s\rVert_\infty.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, a contributing four-tuple either contains two dependency edges, giving at most \(CN^2d_n^2\) choices, or has a connected distinct-index graph, giving at most \(CN d_n^3\le CN^2d_n^2\); a contributing triple has at most \(CN d_n^2\) choices.

Fix a bounded exposure-potential-outcome array and \(B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\).  Known exposure is used at the precise disputed step: on \(D_{i,a}=1\), ass:known-exposure gives \(Y_i(W)=Y_i(a)\).  Therefore, for the signed coordinate \(Y_j\),
\[
\mathbb E_\nu\widehat Y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_j,
\qquad
\{\Pi^{-1}(\widehat Y^{\mathrm{obs}}-Y)\}_j
=\frac{Y_jZ_j}{\pi_j^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the term \(D_{i,a}\{Y_i(W)-Y_i(a)\}/\pi_{i,a}^2\) is identically zero, rather than silently dropped.  On this rank-two class Zhao's pseudoinverse is ordinary, and
\[
G_B:=\widehat\gamma^{\mathrm{HT}}(B)-\gamma_B^*(Y)
=\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{B,j}Z_j,
\qquad
u_{B,j}=S_B^{-1}B^\top\Omega e_j\frac{Y_j}{\pi_j^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every column of \(\Omega\) has at most \(d_n\) nonzero entries, each bounded by one.  The coordinate envelope, probability-rate bound, and \(S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2\) imply
\[
\sup_{B,j}\lVert u_{B,j}\rVert_2
\le K_u n^{\alpha+2\beta-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Also
\[
X_B=n^{-1}B^\top(D-\mathbb E_\nu D)
=\sum_jx_{B,j}Z_j,
\qquad
\sup_{B,j}\lVert x_{B,j}\rVert_2\le\overline a/n.
\tag{6}
\]
Applying (1) coordinatewise gives
\[
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert G_B\rVert_2^4
\le K_Gn^{6\alpha+8\beta-2},
\qquad
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert X_B\rVert_2^4
\le K_Xn^{2\alpha-2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The feasible and oracle estimators satisfy
\[
\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{CV}}=\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}+\Delta_B,
\qquad \Delta_B=-G_B^\top X_B,
\]
so Cauchy--Schwarz and (7) yield
\[
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\Delta_B^2
\le K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}.
\tag{8}
\]

Put \(T_B=\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}-\tau\).  Equation (3) makes the Horvitz--Thompson part unbiased, and the control statistic is centered, so \(\mathbb E_\nu T_B=0\).  Write
\[
T_B=\sum_jt_{B,j}Z_j,
\qquad
t_{B,j}=n^{-1}\{Y_j/\pi_j-B_{j\cdot}\gamma_B^*(Y)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The projector inequality
\[
\gamma_B^*(Y)^\top S_B\gamma_B^*(Y)
\le Y^\top\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Y
\tag{10}
\]
and the row-sparsity, boundedness, probability, and \(\kappa\)-bounds imply
\[
\sup_B\lVert\gamma_B^*(Y)\rVert_2\le K_\gamma n^{\alpha/2+\beta},
\qquad
\sup_{B,j}|t_{B,j}|\le K_tn^{\alpha/2+\beta-1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Expanding the signed covariance and applying (2), (5), (6), and (11) gives
\[
\sup_B|\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)|
\le K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Combining (8) and (12) in the variance expansion proves the first uniform bound with
\[
R_n=K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}
+2K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \((7/2)\alpha+3\beta\le4\alpha+4\beta\), (13) is bounded by \(K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}\).

Finally, completing the square for the fixed oracle coefficient gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
=n^{-2}Y^\top\{\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}
-\Pi^{-1}\Omega BS_B^{-1}B^\top\Omega\Pi^{-1}\}Y.
\tag{14}
\]
Under ass:iid-potential-pairs, \(\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}(YY^\top)=Q(c_H)\).  Taking the trace expectation in (14) yields exactly
\[
\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}\operatorname{Var}_\nu
(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)=h(c_H)-g_B(c_H)=\ell_B(c_H).
\tag{15}
\]
Integrating the first bound and using (15) proves the second.  Ass:zhao-first-order-rate gives \(nR_n\le K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-1}\to0\).  All inner variances are conditional design variances; the outer expectation is only the declared ex ante evaluation. \(\square\)
