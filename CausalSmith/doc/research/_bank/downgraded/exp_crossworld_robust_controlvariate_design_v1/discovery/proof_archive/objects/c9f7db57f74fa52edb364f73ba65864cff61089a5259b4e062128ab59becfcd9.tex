\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(\kappa>0\), and let
\[
K_\kappa=\left\{(b_1,b_0)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n:
\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=n,\ 
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(n^{-1}B(b_1,b_0)^{\top}\Omega B(b_1,b_0)\right)\ge\kappa
\right\}.
\]
The product of the two radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres is compact.  The map
\[
(b_1,b_0)\longmapsto B(b_1,b_0)^{\top}\Omega B(b_1,b_0)
\]
is continuous, and the smallest-eigenvalue map is continuous on symmetric matrices.  Hence \(K_\kappa\) is a closed subset of that compact product and is compact, including when it is empty.  Let \(q\) be the quotient map that identifies independent nonzero rescalings of the two columns.  By def:kappa-basis-class, \(\mathcal B_\kappa=q(K_\kappa)\).  Since \(q\) is continuous for the quotient topology, \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is compact.

For every \((b_1,b_0)\in K_\kappa\),
\[
\lambda_{\min}\{B(b_1,b_0)^{\top}\Omega B(b_1,b_0)\}
\ge n\kappa>0.
\]
Thus its block-separable basis satisfies the nondegeneracy predicate in def:zhao-basis-class, proving \(\mathcal B_\kappa\subseteq\mathcal B_Z\).

Conversely, take a projective class \([B]\in\mathcal B_Z\), represented as
\[
B=\begin{bmatrix}b_1&0\\0&b_0\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The condition \(S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\succ0\) implies \(b_1\ne0\) and \(b_0\ne0\).  Define
\[
d_a=\frac{\sqrt n}{\lVert b_a\rVert_2}\quad(a\in\{1,0\}),
\qquad T=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_0),
\qquad \widetilde B=BT.
\]
Then \(\widetilde B\) represents the same projective class as \(B\), and its two columns satisfy ass:projective-normalization.  Furthermore,
\[
S_{\widetilde B}=\widetilde B^{\top}\Omega\widetilde B
=T S_B T\succ0,
\]
because congruence by the invertible matrix \(T\) preserves positive definiteness.  Consequently
\[
\rho_B=\lambda_{\min}(n^{-1}S_{\widetilde B})>0.
\]
Taking \(\kappa_B=\rho_B/2>0\), the normalized representative \(\widetilde B\) satisfies
\[
\lambda_{\min}(n^{-1}S_{\widetilde B})\ge\kappa_B,
\]
so \([B]\in\mathcal B_{\kappa_B}\).  Thus every element of \(\mathcal B_Z\) belongs to \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) for some basis-dependent \(\kappa>0\).  Together with the first inclusion, this proves
\[
\bigcup_{\kappa>0}\mathcal B_\kappa=\mathcal B_Z.
\]
\(\square\)
