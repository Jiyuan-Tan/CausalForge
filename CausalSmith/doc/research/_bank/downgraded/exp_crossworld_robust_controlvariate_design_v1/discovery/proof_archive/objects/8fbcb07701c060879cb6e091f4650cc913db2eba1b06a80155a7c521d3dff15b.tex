\textbf{Proof.}
Work on the probability-one event from thm:plugin-correspondence.  If \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}=\{B^\star\}\) and a measurable selection does not converge to \(B^\star\), a subsequence remains outside some neighborhood of \(B^\star\).  Compactness supplies a convergent further subsequence, and the outer-limit theorem forces its limit to equal \(B^\star\), a contradiction.

For the converse witness, take \(n=3\), \(f_i(w)=w_i\), independent Bernoulli \((1/2)\) assignment, \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\), and \(\kappa=1/8\).  Then
\[
\Pi=\tfrac12I_6,
\qquad
\Omega=\tfrac14\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix},
\qquad Q(0)=\widehat Q(0)=0.
\]
Thus \(w=\widehat w=0\) on all of \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  The distinct projective bases
\[
B^{(2)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_2),
\qquad B^{(3)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_3)
\]
both have \(S_B=3I_2/4\) and are feasible.  Alternating between them according to the parity of the common pilot size is a measurable empirical minimizer selection with no point limit, while the outer-set conclusion holds exactly. \(\square\)
