\textbf{Proof.}
Let
\[
E=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_n\\ I_n&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
The product-coupling assumption and the sharp covariance-domain assumption ensure that \(0\in\mathcal C(F_1,F_0)\), so \(Q(0)\) and \(h(0)\) are well typed.  From the definition of the moment matrix,
\[
Q(c)=Q(0)-cE
\qquad\text{for every }c\in\mathcal C(F_1,F_0).
\]

For each unit \(i\), mutual exclusivity gives \(D_{i,1}D_{i,0}=0\) almost surely.  Hence
\[
\Omega_{i,n+i}
=\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(D_{i,1},D_{i,0})
=\mathbb E_\nu[D_{i,1}D_{i,0}]-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}
=-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}.
\]
Exposure positivity permits division by both probabilities.  Since \(\Omega\), and therefore \(A_{\mathrm{HT}}\), is symmetric,
\[
\operatorname{tr}(A_{\mathrm{HT}}E)
=2\sum_{i=1}^n(A_{\mathrm{HT}})_{i,n+i}
=2\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\Omega_{i,n+i}}{\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}}
=-2n.
\]
Substitution into the trace definition of \(h\) yields, for every feasible \(c\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
h(c)
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{A_{\mathrm{HT}}Q(c)\right\}\\
&=h(0)-cn^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_{\mathrm{HT}}E)\\
&=h(0)+\frac{2c}{n}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using \(\ell_B(c)=h(c)-g_B(c)\) at \(c_L\) and \(c_U\), followed by the definition of \(w\), gives
\[
w(B)=h(0)+\max\left\{\frac{2c_L}{n}-g_B(c_L),\frac{2c_U}{n}-g_B(c_U)\right\}.
\]

It remains to verify the claimed algebraic non-equivalence.  Assume \(c_L<c_U\), put \(a_e=2c_e/n\), and for a formal nonnegative endpoint-gain pair \(x=(x_L,x_U)\) define
\[
\Phi(x)=\max\{a_L-x_L,a_U-x_U\},
\qquad
\Psi(x)=-\min\{x_L,x_U\}.
\]
If the two criteria differed by a constant independent of \(x\), there would be a \(K\) such that \(\Phi(x)=\Psi(x)+K\) for every \(x\).  At \(x=(t,0)\) with \(t\ge0\), one has \(\Psi(x)=0\) and, because \(a_L<a_U\), \(\Phi(x)=a_U\); hence \(K=a_U\).  At \(x=(0,t)\) with \(t\ge a_U-a_L\), one has \(\Psi(x)=0\) and \(\Phi(x)=a_L\); hence \(K=a_L\).  This contradicts \(a_L<a_U\).  Therefore minimization of the endpoint-drift-adjusted residual criterion is not algebraically equivalent to maximization of the minimum endpoint gain. \(\square\)
