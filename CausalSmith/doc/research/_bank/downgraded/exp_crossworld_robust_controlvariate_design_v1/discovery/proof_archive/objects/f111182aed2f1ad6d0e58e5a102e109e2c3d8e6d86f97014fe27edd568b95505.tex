\paragraph{Proof.}
The only randomness in the main experiment is \(W\sim\nu\); the network, exposure maps, and potential-outcome schedule are fixed.  A schedule satisfying the known-exposure condition is obtained by taking \(Y_i(w)=\widetilde Y_i(f_i(w))\) for fixed maps \(\widetilde Y_i:\{0,1,\star\}\to[0,1]\).  The Bernoulli marginal laws are used only for the ex ante basis criterion and do not replace this finite-population schedule.

\emph{Exact design calculation.}
The exposure truth table, including every assignment in the support of \(\nu\), is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
w&(D_{1,1},D_{2,1},D_{3,1})&(D_{1,0},D_{2,0},D_{3,0})&\nu(w)\\ \hline
000&(0,0,0)&(0,0,0)&8/27\\

010&(0,1,0)&(1,0,1)&4/27\\
001&(0,0,1)&(1,1,0)&4/27\\
110&(0,0,0)&(0,0,1)&2/27\\
101&(0,0,0)&(0,1,0)&2/27\\
011&(0,0,0)&(1,0,0)&2/27\\
111&(0,0,0)&(0,0,0)&1/27
\end{array}
\]
Thus the two tracked events are mutually exclusive for each unit.  Summing the table gives, for distinct \(i,j\),
\[
\mathbb E_\nu D_{i,1}=\frac4{27},
\quad
\mathbb E_\nu D_{i,0}=\frac{10}{27},
\quad
\mathbb E_\nu(D_{i,1}D_{j,1})=0,

\[
\mathbb E_\nu(D_{i,1}D_{i,0})=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\nu(D_{i,1}D_{j,0})=\frac4{27}.
\]
Every exposure probability is positive.  Subtracting the products of the displayed means gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(D_{i,1})=\frac{92}{729},
\quad
\operatorname{Cov}(D_{i,1},D_{j,1})=-\frac{16}{729},
\]
\[
\operatorname{Var}(D_{i,0})=\frac{170}{729},
\quad
\operatorname{Cov}(D_{i,0},D_{j,0})=\frac8{729},
\]
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(D_{i,1},D_{i,0})=-\frac{40}{729},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(D_{i,1},D_{j,0})=\frac{68}{729}.
\]
These identities are exactly the asserted \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\).

\emph{Exact cross-world endpoints.}
For a Bernoulli \(1/2\) marginal, the quantile equals zero on the lower half of \((0,1)\) and one on the upper half.  Therefore
\[
\int_0^1F_1^{-1}(u)F_0^{-1}(1-u)\,du=0,
\qquad
\int_0^1F_1^{-1}(u)F_0^{-1}(u)\,du=\frac12.
\]
Since \(\mu_1=\mu_0=1/2\) and \(\sigma_1^2=\sigma_0^2=1/4\), the endpoint definitions give \(c_L=-1/4\) and \(c_U=1/4\).  Substitution into the moment-matrix definition gives the two displayed matrices \(Q(c_L)\) and \(Q(c_U)\).  Hence every endpoint input is an exact rational matrix.

\emph{A three-scalar quotient reduction.}
Let \(x=b_1\), \(y=b_0\), and \(u=3^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\).  For normalized representatives define
\[
p=\frac{u^{\top}x}{\sqrt3}=\frac{\mathbf 1^{\top}x}{3},
\qquad
q=\frac{u^{\top}y}{\sqrt3}=\frac{\mathbf 1^{\top}y}{3},
\qquad
r=\frac{(x-p\mathbf 1)^{\top}(y-q\mathbf 1)}{3}.
\]
Normalization and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
|p|\le1,
\qquad |q|\le1,
\qquad
r^2\le(1-p^2)(1-q^2).
\]
Using the displayed block form of \(\Omega\), direct multiplication yields
\[
S_B=\frac1{729}
\begin{bmatrix}

Put
\[

Expanding the determinant of the preceding matrix gives
\[
\det(S_B)=-\frac8{729}d.
\]
For \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), the defining eigenvalue condition makes \(S_B\) positive definite, so \(d<0\).  Thus every division below is licensed by the rank condition.

\emph{The full zero-covariance optimizer correspondence.}
Algebra applied to the first reduced identity gives
\[
\frac{31}{80}-g_B(0)=\frac{N_0}{1200d},
\]
where
\[
N_0=668p^2q^2-543p^2+1004pqr-716q^2-1674r^2.
\]
Write \(\alpha=p^2\) and \(\beta=q^2\).  Completing the square in \(|r|\) gives
\[
1004pqr-1674r^2
\le1004\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\,|r|-1674r^2
\le\frac{126002}{837}\alpha\beta.
\]
Consequently
\[
N_0\le
\frac{685118}{837}\alpha\beta-543\alpha-716\beta.
\]
The negative of the right-hand side is affine in each coordinate on \([0,1]^2\).  Its values at the four corners are
\[
0,qquad 543,qquad716,qquad
1259-\frac{685118}{837}=\frac{368665}{837}>0.
\]
It is therefore nonnegative throughout the square and vanishes only at \((\alpha,\beta)=(0,0)\).  At that corner the original expression is \(N_0=-1674r^2\).  Hence \(N_0\le0\), with equality exactly when \(p=q=r=0\).  Since \(d<0\),
\[
g_B(0)\le\frac{31}{80},

with equality exactly when
\[
\mathbf 1^{\top}x=\mathbf 1^{\top}y=x^{\top}y=0.
\]
This proves the asserted equality-set description of the full correspondence \(\mathcal A_0\).  Substitution of \(p=q=r=0\) into the endpoint-loss identities gives, for every \(B_0\in\mathcal A_0\),

\qquad

\emph{The full minimax-residual optimizer correspondence.}
Set
\[
\lambda=\frac{1407}{2635},
\qquad
1-\lambda=\frac{1228}{2635},
\qquad

The maximum of two real numbers is at least every convex combination, so
\[
w(B)\ge
m(B):=\lambda\ell_B(c_L)+(1-\lambda)\ell_B(c_U).

Combining the two exact loss formulas and collecting terms gives the endpoint-mixture certificate
\[

where
\[

As a quadratic form in \((p,r)\), its matrix is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
11509+16892q^2&-49244q\\
-49244q&91578
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Its first leading principal minor is positive, and its determinant is
\[
1053971202-878035960q^2
\ge175935242>0

because \(|q|\le1\).  The two-by-two positive-definiteness criterion therefore gives \(M\ge0\), with equality exactly when \(p=r=0\).  Since \(d<0\), the mixture identity implies
\[
w(B)\ge m(B)\ge V.

If \(w(B)=V\), then both inequalities are equalities.  The equality in the second forces \(p=r=0\); because both mixture weights are strictly positive, equality in the first then forces \(\ell_B(c_L)=\ell_B(c_U)=V\).  With \(Q_0=q^2\), the two endpoint formulas on \(p=r=0\) reduce to

At this value each loss is \(2613/8432\).  Conversely, \(p=r=0\) and \(q^2=24/55\) make every preceding inequality an equality.  Rewriting these three scalar conditions in terms of \(x,y\) gives

\qquad x^{\top}y=0,
\qquad(\mathbf 1^{\top}y)^2=\frac{216}{55}.

This proves both the minimax value and the stated full equality-set description of \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).

\emph{Nonemptiness, regularity, and the two rational margins.}
Let
\[
u=\frac1{\sqrt3}(1,1,1)^{\top},
\qquad
v=\frac1{\sqrt6}(1,1,-2)^{\top},
\qquad
x=\sqrt{\frac32}(1,-1,0)^{\top}.
\]
Then \(x\), \(u\), and \(v\) are pairwise orthogonal after normalization.  Taking \(y=\sqrt3v\) supplies a member of \(\mathcal A_0\), while
\[
y=\sqrt3\left(\sqrt{\frac{24}{55}}u+\sqrt{\frac{31}{55}}v\right)
\]
supplies a member of \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Thus both full correspondences are nonempty.

For every member of \(\mathcal A_0\), the exact scale matrix is
\[
S_B=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac49,\frac23\right).
\]
For every member of \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), it is
\[
S_B=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac49,\frac{1054}{1485}\right).

Therefore in both cases
\[
\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=\frac4{27}>\frac18=\kappa.

The first displayed basis is also a direct \(\kappa\)-feasibility witness, while the already-computed exposure probabilities certify positivity.  Hence \(\xi_0\in\mathfrak X_\kappa\).  Finally,
\[

and

The equality-set characterizations show that these are bounds over every optimizer, not merely over the two displayed representatives.  This supplies the requested rational \(\delta\) and \(\eta\) certificates.

The causal target remains the fixed finite-population exposure contrast \(\tau\). The exact analytic reduction certifies this instance without assuming finite termination of the general moment-complete branch-and-bound handle. \(\square\)
