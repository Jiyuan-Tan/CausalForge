\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(e\in\{L,U\}\).  Def:moment-matrix gives \(Q(c_e)\succeq0\), and exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  Congruence therefore gives
\[
 A_e=\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}Q(c_e)\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}\succeq0.
 \tag{1}
\]
Since \(S_B=Z_B^{\top}Z_B\), cyclicity of trace and def:published-pseudoinverse-loss give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 n^2g_B^+(c_e)
 &=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(Z_B^{\top}Z_B)^+Z_B^{\top}A_eZ_B\right]\\
 &=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[Z_B(Z_B^{\top}Z_B)^+Z_B^{\top}A_e\right]
 =\operatorname{tr}(P_BA_e).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]
The singular-value decomposition of \(Z_B\) shows directly that \(P_B\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{range}(Z_B)\), with rank equal to \(\operatorname{rank}(Z_B)=\operatorname{rank}(Z_B^{\top}Z_B)=\operatorname{rank}(S_B)\).  If this rank is two, the Moore--Penrose inverse is the ordinary inverse, so (2) equals def:gain-loss.

Now suppose \(B\) is singular and choose \(C\in\mathcal B_Z\).  Put \(B_t=B+tC\); it remains block separable.  The Gram determinant
\[
 p(t)=\det\{(Z_B+tZ_C)^{\top}(Z_B+tZ_C)\}
\]
is a polynomial whose leading coefficient is \(\det(Z_C^{\top}Z_C)>0\).  It is therefore not identically zero and has only finitely many roots.  Choose \(t_j\downarrow0\) avoiding those roots and normalize the two nonzero columns projectively.  The resulting \(B_j\) belongs to \(\mathcal B_Z\) and has the same projector as \(B_{t_j}\).

Rank-two orthogonal projectors form a compact set, so along a subsequence \(P_{B_j}\to P_\infty\).  If \(x=Z_Bu\in\operatorname{range}(Z_B)\), then \((Z_B+t_jZ_C)u\to x\) and belongs to \(\operatorname{range}(Z_B+t_jZ_C)\).  Hence
\[
 \lVert P_{B_j}x-x\rVert
 \le2\lVert x-(Z_B+t_jZ_C)u\rVert\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \(P_\infty x=x\), so \(\operatorname{range}(P_B)\subseteq\operatorname{range}(P_\infty)\) and \(P_\infty-P_B\succeq0\).  Together with (1),
\[
 \operatorname{tr}\{(P_\infty-P_B)A_e\}\ge0.
\]
By (2), for both endpoints simultaneously,
\[
 \lim_{j\to\infty}g_{B_j}(c_e)
 =n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(P_\infty A_e)
 \ge g_B^+(c_e).
\]
The uncontrolled term \(h(c_e)\) is basis independent, so \(\limsup_j\ell_{B_j}(c_e)\le\ell_B^+(c_e)\).  Taking the maximum over the two endpoints proves \(\limsup_jw(B_j)\le w^+(B)\). \(\square\)
