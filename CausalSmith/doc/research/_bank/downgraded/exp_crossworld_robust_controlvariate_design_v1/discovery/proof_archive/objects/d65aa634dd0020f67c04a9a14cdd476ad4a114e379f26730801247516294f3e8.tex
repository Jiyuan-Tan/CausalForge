\textbf{Proof.}

\emph{Step 1: compact feasible charts.}
Work first with normalized representatives.  Put
\[
K_\kappa=
\left\{(b_1,b_0):
\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=n,
\quad
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(n^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega B\right)\ge\kappa
\right\}.
\]
By \(\kappa\)-feasibility this set is nonempty.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}kappa\mbox{-}exhaustion}\) proves that its projective quotient is the nonempty compact set \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  If \(b\) lies on the radius-\(\sqrt n\) sphere, then some coordinate satisfies \(|b_j|\ge1\), hence also \(|b_j|\ge n^{-1/2}\).  After the projective sign identification, that coordinate can be taken positive and recovered from the remaining coordinates by
\[
b_j=\left(n-\sum_{k\ne j}b_k^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
On the stated pivot region the radicand is at least \(n^{-1}\).  Thus the finitely many pivot maps and their inverses have effective uniform continuity moduli.  Their products give the finite quotient-chart cover used by the procedure.  Sphere feasibility and the closed inequality defining \(K_\kappa\), including equality, are decided by the exact feasibility interface.

\emph{Step 2: an effective modulus for the loss.}
For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), abbreviate
\[
Q_e=Q(c_e),
\qquad
R_e=\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q_e\Pi^{-1}\Omega,
\qquad
S(B)=B^{\top}\Omega B,
\qquad
T_e(B)=B^{\top}R_eB.
\]
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well-defined.  Because there are finitely many diagonal entries, refinement of their certified enclosures terminates with a positive rational lower bound for \(\min_{i,a}\pi_{i,a}\).  Likewise, exact positivity of \(\kappa\) yields a positive rational lower bound for \(\kappa\).  Hence the input oracles provide finite rational upper bounds \(M_\Omega\ge\lVert\Omega\rVert_2\) and \(M_e\ge\lVert R_e\rVert_2\).

Every normalized block matrix satisfies \(\lVert B\rVert_F=\sqrt{2n}\).  Therefore, with \(d=\lVert B-C\rVert_F\),
\[
\lVert S(B)-S(C)\rVert_2
\le K_Sd,
\qquad
\lVert T_e(B)-T_e(C)\rVert_2
\le K_{T,e}d,
\]
where the effective constants
\[
K_S=2\sqrt{2n}\,M_\Omega,
\qquad
K_{T,e}=2\sqrt{2n}\,M_e
\]
are finite.  On \(K_\kappa\),
\[
S(B)\succeq n\kappa I_2,
\qquad
\lVert S(B)^{-1}\rVert_2\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
\]
The resolvent identity gives
\[
S(B)^{-1}-S(C)^{-1}
=S(B)^{-1}\{S(C)-S(B)\}S(C)^{-1}.
\]
Using \(|\operatorname{tr}(XY)|\le2\lVert X\rVert_2\lVert Y\rVert_2\) for two-by-two matrices and \(\lVert T_e(C)\rVert_2\le2nM_e\), the gain formula implies
\[
|g_B(c_e)-g_C(c_e)|
\le L_e d,
\]
with the effective finite bound
\[
L_e=n^{-2}\left\{
\frac{2K_{T,e}}{n\kappa}
+\frac{4nM_eK_S}{(n\kappa)^2}
\right\}.
\]
The uncontrolled terms \(h(c_e)\) do not depend on \(B\), and the maximum map is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm.  Consequently
\[
|w(B)-w(C)|\le L\lVert B-C\rVert_F,
\qquad
L=\max\{L_L,L_U\}.
\]
Composition with the finitely many pivot maps gives an effective chartwise modulus \(\omega(\rho)\downarrow0\).

This modulus also controls boxes that merely meet the feasible set.  Indeed, if a box of ambient diameter \(\rho\) contains \(B\in K_\kappa\), then every \(C\) in that box satisfies
\[
\lambda_{\min}\{S(C)\}
\ge n\kappa-K_S\rho.
\]
Choose \(\rho\) small enough that \(K_S\rho\le n\kappa/2\).  Then \(S(C)\succeq(n\kappa/2)I_2\) throughout every such box.  Matrix inversion is therefore uniformly stable there.  Outward-rounded interval evaluation of addition, multiplication, trace, inversion, the two endpoint losses, and their maximum consequently has a computable width tending to zero with the box diameter and arithmetic refinement.  The endpoint maximum causes no difficulty: if \([\underline\ell_e,\overline\ell_e]\) encloses \(\ell_B(c_e)\), then
\[
\left[
\max_e\underline\ell_e,
\max_e\overline\ell_e
\right]
\]
encloses \(w(B)\), and its width converges to zero.  Interval-Hessian underestimators may tighten these bounds but are not needed for convergence.

\emph{Step 3: exact feasible witness extraction at the boundary.}
Let \(C\) be a chart box for which the exact feasibility oracle answers that \(C\cap K_\kappa\ne\varnothing\).  Bisect \(C\), query its finitely many children, retain the first feasible child, and repeat.  This produces nested closed boxes \(C=C_0\supseteq C_1\supseteq\cdots\) with
\[
C_m\cap K_\kappa\ne\varnothing,
\qquad
\operatorname{diam}(C_m)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Their intersection is a singleton \(\{B_C\}\).  Since \(K_\kappa\) is closed and every \(C_m\) meets it, \(B_C\in K_\kappa\).  The finite program ``perform this bisection to the requested precision,'' together with the effective diameter modulus and the exact feasibility oracle, is a certified inclusion-oracle name for \(B_C\).  Thus it is an exact representation of a feasible point, not a floating-point point asserted to be feasible.  Each requested finite precision uses finitely many exact decisions.  This is precisely the oracle representation permitted by the moment-complete interface.  In particular, the construction remains valid when every feasible point in \(C\) lies on
\[
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(n^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega B\right)=\kappa.
\]

\emph{Step 4: simultaneous value and mesh stopping.}
Fix the exactly encoded \(\varepsilon>0\), and choose an exactly encoded rational \(\alpha\) with \(0<\alpha<\varepsilon/4\).  By the effective moduli in Step 2, a finite chart depth \(N\) and finite arithmetic refinement precision can be found such that every depth-\(N\) box has chart diameter at most \(\varepsilon\), every box meeting \(K_\kappa\) lies in the stable-inverse region, and both of the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item its certified lower bound \(\underline w(C)\) satisfies
\[
\inf_{B\in C\cap K_\kappa}w(B)-\alpha
\le \underline w(C)
\le \inf_{B\in C\cap K_\kappa}w(B);
\]
\item the exact feasible name \(B_C\) extracted in Step 3 has a rational outward-rounded upper bound \(\overline w(B_C)\) satisfying
\[
w(B_C)\le\overline w(B_C)\le w(B_C)+\alpha.
\]
\end{enumerate}
The first inequality remains valid although the interval calculation ranges over the entire chart box: every point of that box is within its diameter of a feasible point in the same box, and the stable-region modulus absorbs this additional oscillation.

There are finitely many depth-\(N\) boxes.  Exact infeasibility deletes those with empty feasible intersection.  Among the remaining boxes, choose the smallest rational upper bound \(\overline w(B_C)\), and call its exact feasible name \(B_{\varepsilon}\) and its upper bound \(U_{\varepsilon}\).  Let \(B^\star\) minimize \(w\) on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\); existence follows from nonempty compactness and the continuity proved in Step 2.  A retained depth-\(N\) box \(C^\star\) contains \(B^\star\).  Refining \(N\), if necessary, so that the loss oscillation within such a box is at most \(\alpha\), gives
\[
U_{\varepsilon}
\le\overline w(B_{C^\star})
\le w(B_{C^\star})+\alpha
\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\alpha.
\]

Set \(L_{\varepsilon}\) equal to the minimum certified lower bound over all unpruned feasible boxes.  The box containing \(B^\star\) has lower bound at most \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Conversely, every feasible box has true infimum at least \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\), so its lower bound is at least \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}-\alpha\).  Therefore
\[
v_{\mathrm{mm}}-\alpha
\le L_{\varepsilon}
\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}
\le w(B_{\varepsilon})
\le U_{\varepsilon}
\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\alpha.
\]
It follows that
\[
U_{\varepsilon}-L_{\varepsilon}
\le3\alpha<\varepsilon.
\]
All interval endpoints are rational because they are produced by outward rounding.

Pruning is safe.  If a box has \(\underline w(C)>U_{\varepsilon}\), then every feasible point in it has loss greater than the loss of the feasible incumbent, so the box contains no global minimizer.  A box containing any member of \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) is neither exactly infeasible nor objective-prunable because
\[
\underline w(C)\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}\le w(B_{\varepsilon})\le U_{\varepsilon}.
\]
Hence the union \(\mathcal C_{\varepsilon}\) of the feasible portions of the retained quotient-chart boxes contains \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), and its maximum box diameter satisfies \(h_{\varepsilon}\le\varepsilon\).

\emph{Step 5: finite termination and checking.}
The initial chart family is finite, binary subdivision to depth \(N\) produces only finitely many boxes, every exact feasibility query terminates by the input specification, and every requested interval refinement terminates by its effective modulus.  Thus the procedure reaches the two stopping inequalities no later than the finite exhaustive refinement depth \(N\); earlier valid pruning can only reduce the work.  If a box meets the exact \(\kappa\)-boundary, its feasible intersection is nonempty, so the exact oracle cannot label it infeasible.  It is retained unless the displayed valid objective-pruning inequality holds, which cannot remove a minimizer.

The finite chart list, the exact-name program for \(B_{\varepsilon}\), the rational bounds, all infeasibility or objective-pruning records, and the retained boxes form \(\mathscr K_{\varepsilon}\).  The preceding inequalities are exactly the checks required of that certificate.  Since the argument applies to every positive \(\varepsilon\), applying it along \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives \(U_{\varepsilon}-L_{\varepsilon}\to0\) and \(h_{\varepsilon}\to0\).  The proof supplies only an instance-dependent finite depth \(N\), so it gives no uniform complexity or semidefinite-reduction claim. \(\square\)
