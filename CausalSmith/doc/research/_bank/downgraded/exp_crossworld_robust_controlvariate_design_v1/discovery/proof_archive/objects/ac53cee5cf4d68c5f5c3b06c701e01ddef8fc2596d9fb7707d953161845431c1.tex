\textbf{Proof.}

\emph{Step 1: compact feasible charts.}
Work with normalized representatives and put
\[
K_\kappa=
\left\{(b_1,b_0):
\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=n,
\quad
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(n^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega B\right)\ge\kappa
\right\}.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}kappa\mbox{-}feasibility}\) makes \(K_\kappa\) nonempty, and Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}kappa\mbox{-}exhaustion}\) shows that its projective quotient is the nonempty compact set \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  If \(b\) lies on the radius-\(\sqrt n\) sphere, then some coordinate satisfies \(\lvert b_j\rvert\ge1\), and hence \(\lvert b_j\rvert\ge n^{-1/2}\).  After the projective sign identification, that coordinate can be taken positive and recovered from the other coordinates by
\[
b_j=\left(n-\sum_{k\ne j}b_k^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
On the stated pivot region the radicand is at least \(n^{-1}\).  The finitely many pivot maps and their inverses therefore have effective uniform-continuity moduli.  Their products give the finite quotient-chart cover used by the procedure.  The exact feasibility interface decides sphere feasibility and the closed inequality defining \(K_\kappa\), including equality at the \(\kappa\)-boundary.

\emph{Step 2: an effective modulus for the loss.}
For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), abbreviate
\[
Q_e=Q(c_e),
\qquad
R_e=\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q_e\Pi^{-1}\Omega,
\qquad
S(B)=B^{\top}\Omega B,
\qquad
T_e(B)=B^{\top}R_eB.
\]
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well-defined.  There are finitely many exposure probabilities, so refinement of their certified enclosures terminates with a positive rational lower bound for \(\min_{i,a}\pi_{i,a}\).  Exact positivity of \(\kappa\) similarly gives a positive rational lower bound for \(\kappa\).  The moment-complete input consequently supplies computable finite rational bounds
\[
M_\Omega\ge\lVert\Omega\rVert_2,
\qquad
M_e\ge\lVert R_e\rVert_2.
\]

Every normalized block matrix satisfies \(\lVert B\rVert_F=\sqrt{2n}\).  Hence, for \(d=\lVert B-C\rVert_F\), submultiplicativity and the identity
\(B^{\top}MB-C^{\top}MC=(B-C)^{\top}MB+C^{\top}M(B-C)\) give
\[
\lVert S(B)-S(C)\rVert_2\le K_Sd,
\qquad
\lVert T_e(B)-T_e(C)\rVert_2\le K_{T,e}d,
\]
where
\[
K_S=2\sqrt{2n}\,M_\Omega,
\qquad
K_{T,e}=2\sqrt{2n}\,M_e.
\]
For \(B\in K_\kappa\),
\[
S(B)\succeq n\kappa I_2,
\qquad
\lVert S(B)^{-1}\rVert_2\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
\]
If \(B,C\in K_\kappa\), the resolvent identity
\[
S(B)^{-1}-S(C)^{-1}
=S(B)^{-1}\{S(C)-S(B)\}S(C)^{-1}
\]
and \(\lvert\operatorname{tr}(XY)\rvert\le2\lVert X\rVert_2\lVert Y\rVert_2\) for two-by-two matrices imply
\[
\lvert g_B(c_e)-g_C(c_e)\rvert\le L_e d,
\]
with the effective finite constant
\[
L_e=n^{-2}\left\{
\frac{2K_{T,e}}{n\kappa}
+\frac{4nM_eK_S}{(n\kappa)^2}
\right\},
\]
because \(\lVert T_e(C)\rVert_2\le2nM_e\).  The uncontrolled term \(h(c_e)\) is independent of \(B\), and the maximum map is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm.  Thus
\[
\lvert w(B)-w(C)\rvert
\le L\lVert B-C\rVert_F,
\qquad
L=\max\{L_L,L_U\}.
\]
Composition with the finitely many pivot maps yields an effective chartwise modulus \(\omega(\rho)\downarrow0\).

The same construction controls interval boxes that merely meet the feasible set.  If a box of ambient diameter \(\rho\) contains \(B\in K_\kappa\), then every \(C\) in that box obeys
\[
\lambda_{\min}\{S(C)\}
\ge n\kappa-K_S\rho.
\]
For \(K_S\rho\le n\kappa/2\), one has \(S(C)\succeq(n\kappa/2)I_2\) throughout that box.  Matrix inversion is uniformly stable there.  Outward-rounded interval evaluation of addition, multiplication, trace, inversion, the two endpoint losses, and their maximum therefore has a computable width tending to zero as the box diameter and arithmetic-enclosure widths tend to zero.  Explicitly, if \([\underline\ell_e,\overline\ell_e]\) encloses \(\ell_B(c_e)\), then
\[
\left[
\max_e\underline\ell_e,
\max_e\overline\ell_e
\right]
\]
encloses \(w(B)\), and its width tends to zero.  Interval-Hessian underestimators may improve these bounds but are not needed for convergence.

\emph{Step 3: exact feasible witness extraction, including at the boundary.}
Let \(C\) be a closed chart box for which the exact feasibility oracle answers \(C\cap K_\kappa\ne\varnothing\).  Bisect \(C\), query its finitely many children, retain the first child having nonempty intersection with \(K_\kappa\), and repeat.  This constructs nested closed boxes \(C=C_0\supseteq C_1\supseteq\cdots\) such that
\[
C_m\cap K_\kappa\ne\varnothing,
\qquad
\operatorname{diam}(C_m)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Their intersection is a singleton \(\{B_C\}\).  Since \(K_\kappa\) is closed and each \(C_m\) meets \(K_\kappa\), choose \(x_m\in C_m\cap K_\kappa\); then \(x_m\to B_C\), so \(B_C\in K_\kappa\).  The finite program that performs this bisection to any requested precision, together with the effective diameter modulus and exact feasibility oracle, is a certified inclusion-oracle name for \(B_C\).  Every requested precision terminates after finitely many exact decisions.  It is therefore an exact representation of a feasible point, as expressly allowed for \(B_\varepsilon\) by the certified-output contract, and not a floating-point point whose feasibility is merely asserted.  Nothing in this construction requires strict inequality; it remains valid when all feasible points in \(C\) satisfy
\[
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(n^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega B\right)=\kappa.
\]

\emph{Step 4: simultaneous value and mesh guarantees.}
Fix the exactly encoded \(\varepsilon>0\).  Its exact positive encoding permits selection of a rational \(\alpha\) with \(0<\alpha<\varepsilon/4\).  The effective moduli in Step 2 allow the procedure to find a finite common chart depth \(N\) and a finite arithmetic-refinement precision such that every depth-\(N\) box has chart diameter at most \(\varepsilon\), every such box meeting \(K_\kappa\) lies in the stable-inverse region, and, for each feasible box \(C\),
\[
\inf_{B\in C\cap K_\kappa}w(B)-\alpha
\le \underline w(C)
\le \inf_{B\in C\cap K_\kappa}w(B),
\]
while the exact feasible name from Step 3 has a rational outward-rounded upper bound satisfying
\[
w(B_C)\le\overline w(B_C)\le w(B_C)+\alpha.
\]
To justify the first display even though interval evaluation ranges over the whole chart box, fix any feasible point of \(C\).  Every point of \(C\) is within its diameter of that feasible point; the stable-region modulus and sufficiently fine arithmetic refinement bound the resulting downward interval overestimation by \(\alpha\).

There are finitely many depth-\(N\) boxes.  Delete those certified exactly infeasible.  Among the remaining boxes, choose the least rational upper bound \(\overline w(B_C)\); call its exact feasible inclusion-oracle name \(B_\varepsilon\) and call that bound \(U_\varepsilon\).  Continuity from Step 2 and nonempty compactness from Step 1 give a minimizer \(B^\star\in\mathcal B_\kappa\).  Let \(C^\star\) be a depth-\(N\) box containing it.  Refining \(N\), if necessary, so that the loss oscillation on every feasible box is at most \(\alpha\), gives
\[
U_\varepsilon
\le\overline w(B_{C^\star})
\le w(B_{C^\star})+\alpha
\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\alpha.
\]

Let \(L_\varepsilon\) be the minimum certified lower bound over all unpruned feasible boxes.  The box containing \(B^\star\) has lower bound at most \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Every feasible box has true infimum at least \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\), so its lower bound is at least \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}-\alpha\).  Therefore
\[
v_{\mathrm{mm}}-\alpha
\le L_\varepsilon
\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}
\le w(B_\varepsilon)
\le U_\varepsilon
\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\alpha,
\]
and hence
\[
U_\varepsilon-L_\varepsilon\le3\alpha<\varepsilon.
\]
All reported interval endpoints are rational because they are obtained by outward rounding.

Pruning preserves every global minimizer.  If \(\underline w(C)>U_\varepsilon\), then each feasible point in \(C\) has loss strictly greater than the loss of the feasible incumbent, so \(C\) contains no global minimizer.  Conversely, a box containing a member of \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) is neither exactly infeasible nor objective-prunable, because
\[
\underline w(C)\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}
\le w(B_\varepsilon)
\le U_\varepsilon.
\]
Consequently the union \(\mathcal C_\varepsilon\) of the retained quotient-chart boxes contains \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), and its maximum box diameter obeys \(h_\varepsilon\le\varepsilon\).

\emph{Step 5: finite termination and certificate verification.}
The initial chart family is finite, subdivision to depth \(N\) creates only finitely many boxes, every exact feasibility query terminates under the input contract, and every requested inclusion-oracle and interval refinement terminates under the effective moduli.  Thus the procedure reaches the displayed value-gap and mesh conditions no later than the finite exhaustive refinement depth \(N\); valid earlier pruning can only reduce the work.  A box meeting the exact feasible \(\kappa\)-boundary has nonempty feasible intersection, so the exact oracle cannot label it infeasible.  Such a box is retained unless its certified objective lower bound exceeds the incumbent upper bound, and the preceding pruning inequality shows that this rule cannot discard a minimizer.

The finite chart cover, the certified inclusion-oracle name of \(B_\varepsilon\), the rational bounds, every exact-infeasibility or objective-pruning record, and the retained boxes form \(\mathscr K_\varepsilon\).  Steps 3 and 4 establish exactly the checks required by the certified-output definition:
\[
L_\varepsilon\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}\le w(B_\varepsilon)\le U_\varepsilon,
\qquad
U_\varepsilon-L_\varepsilon\le\varepsilon,
\qquad
h_\varepsilon\le\varepsilon,
\qquad
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\subseteq\mathcal C_\varepsilon.
\]
Because the construction works for every positive \(\varepsilon\), along any sequence \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) it yields \(U_\varepsilon-L_\varepsilon\to0\) and \(h_\varepsilon\to0\).  The depth \(N\), enclosure precision, and number of boxes may depend on the encoded instance and on \(\varepsilon\); no uniform complexity or semidefinite-program representation has been used or proved. \(\square\)
