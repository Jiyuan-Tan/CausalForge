\textbf{Proof.}
For arm \(a\), write \(F_{a,m_a}\) for the distribution function of \(\widehat F_a\).  For fixed rational \(t\in[0,1]\), the variables
\[
X_{j,a}(t)=\mathbf 1\{Y^P_{j,a}\le t\}
\]
are independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(F_a(t)\).  If \(X\) is Bernoulli with mean \(p\), then
\[
\mathbb E\exp\{\lambda(X-p)\}
=e^{-\lambda p}(1-p+pe^\lambda)\le e^{\lambda^2/8}.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the second derivative of \(\log(1-p+pe^\lambda)\) is the variance of a tilted Bernoulli variable and is at most \(1/4\), while that logarithm minus \(p\lambda\) and its first derivative vanish at zero.  Exponential Markov inequality, independence, and optimization over \(\lambda\) give, for \(s>0\),
\[
P\!\left(\left|F_{a,m_a}(t)-F_a(t)\right|>s\right)
\le2e^{-2m_as^2}.
\tag{2}
\]
For every rational \(s>0\), the right side is summable in \(m_a\).  The union bound and the tail-sum form of the Borel--Cantelli argument imply
\[
P\!\left(\limsup_{m_a\to\infty}
\{|F_{a,m_a}(t)-F_a(t)|>s\}\right)=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Intersecting the probability-one events in (3) over the countable rational pairs \((t,s)\) gives simultaneous convergence at every rational \(t\).

Fix an integer \(K\ge1\) and the rational grid \(x_j=j/K\).  Monotonicity of the two distribution functions implies, for \(y\in[x_j,x_{j+1}]\),
\[
|F_{a,m_a}(y)-F_a(y)|
\le
\max_{r\in\{j,j+1\}}|F_{a,m_a}(x_r)-F_a(x_r)|
+F_a(x_{j+1})-F_a(x_j).
\tag{4}
\]
Integrating (4), summing over \(j\), using convergence on the finite grid, and telescoping the last increments gives
\[
\limsup_{m_a\to\infty}
\int_0^1|F_{a,m_a}(y)-F_a(y)|\,dy\le K^{-1}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{5}
\]
Letting \(K\to\infty\) proves that the integral in (5) converges to zero.

For distribution functions \(R,S\) on \([0,1]\), the layer-cake representation and Tonelli's theorem give the one-dimensional identity
\[
\int_0^1|R^{-1}(u)-S^{-1}(u)|\,du
=\int_0^1|R(y)-S(y)|\,dy.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) show
\[
\int_0^1|\widehat F_a^{-1}(u)-F_a^{-1}(u)|\,du
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely},
\qquad a\in\{1,0\}.
\tag{7}
\]
The bounded support in (4)--(7) is exactly the bounded-outcomes assumption.

The functions \(y\) and \(y^2\) are respectively one- and two-Lipschitz on \([0,1]\), so (7) gives convergence of the empirical means, second moments, and variances.  Moreover,
\[
|xy-x'y'|\le|x-x'|+|y-y'|
\qquad(x,x',y,y'\in[0,1]).
\tag{8}
\]
Applying (8) to both the comonotone and antimonotone quantile pairs proves almost-sure convergence of the two empirical cross moments and hence of both empirical covariance endpoints.

By the moment-matrix formula and the endpoint-matrix construction in \(\texttt{def:pilot-plugin}\), every block of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) is a convergent scalar multiplying either \(I_n\) or \(\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\).  Because \(n\) is fixed,
\[
\lVert I_n\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=1,
\qquad
\lVert\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=n.
\tag{9}
\]
Therefore
\[
M_m=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely under }P.
\tag{10}
\]
The maximal-rank enumeration and first-qualifying selector in the pilot definition do not enter this endpoint calculation. \(\square\)
