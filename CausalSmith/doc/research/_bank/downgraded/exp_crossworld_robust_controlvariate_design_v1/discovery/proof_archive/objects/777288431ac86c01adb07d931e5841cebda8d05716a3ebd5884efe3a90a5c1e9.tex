\textbf{Proof.}
Fix normalized representatives of the projective classes in \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  By prop:kappa-exhaustion and ass:kappa-feasibility, this is a nonempty compact space.  The two columns of every representative have disjoint block supports and squared norm \(n\), while feasibility gives
\[
\lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt n,
\qquad
\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
\tag{1}
\]
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined, and all design matrices are fixed.

Write \(q_a=F_a^{-1}\) and \(\widehat q_a=\widehat F_a^{-1}\).  We first derive convergence of every pilot ingredient.  If \(X_j\) are independent, centered, and \(|X_j|\le1\), expansion of the fourth moment gives
\[
\mathbb E\left(\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\right)^4
\le 3m^2.
\]
Markov's inequality and summability of \(3/(m^2t^4)\) imply the bounded strong law by the Borel--Cantelli argument.  Apply it to the outcomes, their squares, and, simultaneously over rational \(x\), the indicators \(\mathbf1\{Y^P_{j,a}\le x\}\).  Monotonicity then gives empirical-distribution convergence at all continuity points.  The generalized-inverse squeeze gives \(\widehat q_a(u)\to q_a(u)\) at every continuity point of \(q_a\).  Since monotone functions have at most countably many discontinuities and all quantiles lie in \([0,1]\), dominated convergence yields
\[
\lVert\widehat q_a-q_a\rVert_{L^1(0,1)}\longrightarrow0,
\quad
\widehat\mu_a\longrightarrow\mu_a,
\quad
\widehat\sigma_a^2\longrightarrow\sigma_a^2
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{2}
\]
Because all quantiles are bounded by one,
\[
\left|\int_0^1\widehat q_1(u)\widehat q_0(u)\,du-
\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du\right|
\le\lVert\widehat q_1-q_1\rVert_1+
\lVert\widehat q_0-q_0\rVert_1,
\tag{3}
\]
and the same inequality holds after replacing the second quantile by its reflection at \(1-u\).  Thus
\[
\widehat c_L\to c_L,
\qquad \widehat c_U\to c_U
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), let \(Q_e=Q(c_e)\) and let \(\widehat Q_e\) be its plug-in counterpart.  Equations (2)--(4) and the finite-dimensional formula in def:moment-matrix imply
\[
\rho_m:=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{5}
\]

Put \(C_\nu=\lVert\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\).  The inequality \(|\operatorname{tr}(M)|\le d\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) for a \(d\times d\) matrix, def:gain-loss, and (1) give, uniformly in \(B\),
\[
\max_e|\widehat g_{B,e}-g_{B,e}|
\le \frac{2C_\nu^2}{n^2\kappa}\rho_m,
\qquad
\max_e|\widehat h_e-h_e|
\le \frac{2}{n}\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\rho_m.
\tag{6}
\]
Therefore \(\max_e|\widehat\ell_{B,e}-\ell_{B,e}|\) is bounded by the sum of the two right sides in (6), uniformly in \(B\).  Since the maximum of two coordinates is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm,
\[
\delta_m:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}
|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{7}
\]

Each endpoint loss is continuous on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\), because inversion is continuous on the uniformly positive-definite set in (1); hence \(w\) and \(\widehat w\) attain their minima.  The elementary minimum inequality gives
\[
|\widehat v_{\mathrm{mm}}-v_{\mathrm{mm}}|
\le\delta_m\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{8}
\]
Finally, take \(B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and a convergent subsequence \(B_{m_j}\to B_\infty\), which exists by compactness.  For any \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), empirical optimality and (7) give
\[
w(B_{m_j})
\le\widehat w(B_{m_j})+\delta_{m_j}
\le\widehat w(B^\star)+\delta_{m_j}
\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\delta_{m_j}.
\]
Continuity and the defining lower bound \(w\ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}\) imply \(w(B_\infty)=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\), so \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  This is precisely the asserted one-sided outer-limit inclusion. \(\square\)
