\textbf{Proof.}
For arm \(a\), write \(F_{a,m_a}\) for the distribution function of \(\widehat F_a\).  For every rational \(t\in[0,1]\), the variables
\[
X_{j,a}(t)=\mathbf 1\{Y^P_{j,a}\le t\}
\]
are independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(F_a(t)\) by \(\texttt{ass:unpaired-pilot}\).  Hoeffding's elementary exponential argument gives, for \(s>0\),
\[
P\!\left(\left|F_{a,m_a}(t)-F_a(t)\right|>s\right)
\le 2e^{-2m_as^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, for a Bernoulli variable \(X\) of mean \(p\), convexity gives
\(\mathbb E e^{\lambda(X-p)}\le e^{\lambda^2/8}\); exponential Markov inequality, independence, and optimization in \(\lambda\) yield (1).  The right side is summable in \(m_a\).  Intersecting the resulting probability-one events over rational \(t\) and rational \(s>0\) shows that \(F_{a,m_a}(t)\to F_a(t)\) simultaneously at every rational \(t\).

Fix \(K\ge1\) and put \(x_j=j/K\).  Monotonicity of the two distribution functions implies, for \(y\in[x_j,x_{j+1}]\),
\[
|F_{a,m_a}(y)-F_a(y)|
\le \max_{r\in\{j,j+1\}}|F_{a,m_a}(x_r)-F_a(x_r)|
+F_a(x_{j+1})-F_a(x_j).
\tag{2}
\]
Integrating, summing, passing to the limit on the finite grid, and telescoping the last increments gives
\[
\limsup_{m_a\to\infty}\int_0^1|F_{a,m_a}(y)-F_a(y)|\,dy\le K^{-1}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{3}
\]
Letting \(K\to\infty\) proves convergence of this integral to zero.  For probability laws \(R,S\) on \([0,1]\), the layer-cake representation and Tonelli's theorem give
\[
\int_0^1|R^{-1}(u)-S^{-1}(u)|\,du
=\int_0^1|R(y)-S(y)|\,dy.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus
\[
\int_0^1|\widehat F_a^{-1}(u)-F_a^{-1}(u)|\,du\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely},\qquad a\in\{1,0\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The bounded support used in (2)--(5) is exactly \(\texttt{ass:bounded-outcomes}\).

The functions \(y\) and \(y^2\) are respectively one- and two-Lipschitz on \([0,1]\), so (5) yields convergence of the empirical means, second moments, and variances.  Moreover,
\[
|xy-x'y'|\le |x-x'|+|y-y'|
\qquad(x,x',y,y'\in[0,1]).
\tag{6}
\]
Applying (6) to the comonotone and antimonotone quantile pairs proves convergence of both empirical endpoint cross moments and hence of both empirical covariance endpoints.  Every block of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) is therefore a convergent scalar multiplying either \(I_n\) or \(\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\).  Since \(n\) is fixed and
\[
\lVert I_n\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=1,
\qquad \lVert\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=n,
\tag{7}
\]
we obtain
\[
M_m=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely under }P.
\]
The additional stable and unrestricted selectors now present in \(\texttt{def:pilot-plugin}\) do not enter this endpoint calculation. \(\square\)
