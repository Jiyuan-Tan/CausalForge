\textbf{Proof.}
Work on the probability-one event supplied by thm:plugin-correspondence. Let \(U\) be any neighborhood of \(B^\star\). The asserted isolation gives
\[
\gamma_U:=\inf_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\setminus U}w(B)-v_{\mathrm{mm}}>0. \tag{1}
\]
Let \(\delta_m=\sup_B|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|\), so \(\delta_m\to0\). If \(2\delta_m<\gamma_U\), then every \(B\notin U\) satisfies
\[
\widehat w(B)\ge w(B)-\delta_m
\ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}+\gamma_U-\delta_m
>v_{\mathrm{mm}}+\delta_m
\ge\widehat w(B^\star).
\]
Thus \(\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\subset U\) eventually. Since \(U\) was arbitrary, every measurable selection converges almost surely to \(B^\star\). Uniqueness of the active endpoint identifies the limiting saddle state; once the basis is a gap-isolated singleton, it is not additionally needed for convergence of the basis coordinate.

For the failure under ties, let \(n=3\), take the empty network, set \(f_i(w)=w_i\), and let the coordinates of \(W\) be independent Bernoulli \(1/2\). Then
\[
\Pi=\frac12I_6,
\qquad
\Omega=\frac14\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix}. \tag{2}
\]
Take \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\), \(H=\delta_{(0,0)}\), and independent unpaired pilot samples. All outcomes and all pilot observations equal zero, so
\[
Q(0)=0,qquad c_L=c_U=0,qquad w(B)=\widehat w(B)=0 \tag{3}
\]
for every feasible \(B\) and every pilot size. Choose \(\kappa=1/8\). With \(e_j\) denoting the standard coordinate vectors, define
\[
B^{(2)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_2),
\qquad
B^{(3)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_3).
\]
Equation (2) gives \(S_{B^{(j)}}=3I_2/4\), hence \(\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_{B^{(j)}})=1/4>\kappa\). The two bases are distinct points of the projective quotient. By (3),
\[
\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}=\mathcal B_\kappa
\]
at every pilot size. Selecting \(B^{(2)}\) at even common pilot sizes and \(B^{(3)}\) at odd common pilot sizes is deterministic, hence measurable, and has two distinct subsequential limits. The outer-set inclusion nevertheless holds identically. \(\square\)
