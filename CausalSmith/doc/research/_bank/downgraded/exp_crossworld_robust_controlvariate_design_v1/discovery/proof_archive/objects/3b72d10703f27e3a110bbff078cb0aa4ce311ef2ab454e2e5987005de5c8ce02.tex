\textbf{Proof.}
Work on the probability-one event on which all conclusions of thm:plugin-correspondence hold.  Let \(U\) be any neighborhood of \(B^\star\) in the compact projective quotient.  The asserted isolation supplies
\[
\gamma_U:=v^\star-\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\setminus U}r(B)>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(\delta_m=\sup_B|\widehat r(B)-r(B)|\), so \(\delta_m\to0\) by thm:plugin-correspondence.  If \(2\delta_m<\gamma_U\), then every \(B\notin U\) satisfies
\[
\widehat r(B)\le r(B)+\delta_m
\le v^\star-\gamma_U+\delta_m
< v^\star-\delta_m
\le\widehat r(B^\star).
\]
Consequently \(\widehat{\mathcal A}_R\subset U\) for all sufficiently large pilot sizes.  Since \(U\) was arbitrary, every measurable selection \(\widehat B\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_R\) converges almost surely to \(B^\star\).  The hypothesis that exactly one endpoint is active labels the limiting saddle unambiguously; it is not needed for convergence of the basis once the singleton and gap hypotheses hold.

It remains to show that generic point convergence cannot replace the outer-set conclusion.  Consider the following bounded instance.  Let \(n=3\), take the empty network, put \(f_i(w)=w_i\), and draw the coordinates of \(W\) independently as Bernoulli \(1/2\).  Then \(D_{i,1}=W_i\), \(D_{i,0}=1-W_i\), exposure positivity holds, and
\[
\Pi=\frac12I_6,
\qquad
\Omega=\frac14
\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\), let the potential-outcome pairs have law \(H=\delta_{(0,0)}\), and use the corresponding independent unpaired pilot samples.  These outcomes are bounded, all pilots equal zero, and
\[
\mu_1=\mu_0=\sigma_1^2=\sigma_0^2=c_L=c_U=0,
\qquad Q(0)=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Choose \(\kappa=1/8\).  With \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) the coordinate vectors of \(\mathbb R^3\), define
\[
B^{(2)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_2),
\qquad
B^{(3)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_3).
\]
For either basis, (2) gives
\[
S_{B^{(j)}}=\frac34I_2,
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}\{3^{-1}S_{B^{(j)}}\}=\frac14>\kappa.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus ass:kappa-feasibility holds, and the two displayed bases are distinct points of the projective quotient.  By (3), however, \(g_B(0)=r(B)=0\) for every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\).  The empirical moments and endpoints are identically zero for every pilot size, so also \(\widehat r(B)=0\) for every feasible \(B\).  Therefore
\[
\widehat{\mathcal A}_R=\mathcal A_R=\mathcal B_\kappa
\quad\text{for every pilot size}.
\tag{5}
\]
Along \(m_1=m_0=m\), choose the deterministic, hence measurable, selection \(\widehat B_m=B^{(2)}\) for even \(m\) and \(\widehat B_m=B^{(3)}\) for odd \(m\).  It has two distinct subsequential limits and hence no point limit, whereas (5) makes the outer-set conclusion hold exactly.  This supplies the required bounded two-marginal counterexample. \(\square\)
