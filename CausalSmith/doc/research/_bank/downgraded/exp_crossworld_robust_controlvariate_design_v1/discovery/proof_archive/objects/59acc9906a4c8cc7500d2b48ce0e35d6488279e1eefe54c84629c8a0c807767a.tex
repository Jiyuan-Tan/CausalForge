\textbf{Proof.}
By ass:exposure-positivity, \(\Pi\) is invertible.  If \(B\in\mathcal B_Z\), then the defining nondegeneracy condition for \(\mathcal B_Z\) gives \(S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\succ0\), so \(S_B^{-1}\) exists.  Put
\[
E=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_n\\ I_n&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1},\qquad
A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
The moment-matrix construction gives the exact identity
\[
Q(c)=Q(0)-cE.
\]
Cyclicity of trace and def:gain-loss therefore yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_B(c)
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{A_BQ(c)\}\\
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{A_BQ(0)\}
-c\,n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_BE),
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell_B(c)
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{(A_{\mathrm{HT}}-A_B)Q(c)\}\\
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{(A_{\mathrm{HT}}-A_B)Q(0)\}
-c\,n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{(A_{\mathrm{HT}}-A_B)E\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus both maps are affine in \(c\).  The assumptions ass:known-exposure, ass:mutually-exclusive-exposures, and ass:iid-potential-pairs supply the causal and ex ante interpretation of the moment and loss constructions; the displayed affine reduction is algebraic, with every inverse justified above.

By ass:frechet-covariance-domain and the definition of the covariance image,
\[
\bigl\{\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}:H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\bigr\}
=[c_L,c_U].
\]
An affine real-valued function on this closed interval attains its minimum and maximum at endpoints.  Equality of the covariance image with the closed interval also implies that each endpoint is the covariance of at least one coupling in \(\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\).  Applying these facts to the two displayed affine functions proves
\[
\inf_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}g_B\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}\right)
=\min\{g_B(c_L),g_B(c_U)\}
\]
and
\[
\sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}\ell_B\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}\right)
=\max\{\ell_B(c_L),\ell_B(c_U)\},
\]
with both extrema attained by endpoint couplings.

By ass:product-coupling-zero, the product coupling belongs to \(\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\) and has covariance zero.  Combining this with ass:frechet-covariance-domain gives \(0\in[c_L,c_U]\), equivalently \(c_L\le0\le c_U\), and hence \(g_B(0)\) is evaluated at a feasible covariance.

Finally, write \(g_B(c)=\alpha_B+\beta_Bc\), where
\[
\beta_B=-n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_BE).
\]
If \(\beta_B=0\), then \(g_B\) is constant, so its value at zero equals its infimum.  Conversely, suppose \(c_L<0<c_U\).  If \(\beta_B>0\), then
\[
g_B(c_L)=g_B(0)+\beta_Bc_L<g_B(0),
\]
whereas if \(\beta_B<0\), then
\[
g_B(c_U)=g_B(0)+\beta_Bc_U<g_B(0).
\]
Thus equality of \(g_B(0)\) with the worst-coupling gain is impossible unless \(\beta_B=0\).  This proves the stated equivalence. \(\square\)
