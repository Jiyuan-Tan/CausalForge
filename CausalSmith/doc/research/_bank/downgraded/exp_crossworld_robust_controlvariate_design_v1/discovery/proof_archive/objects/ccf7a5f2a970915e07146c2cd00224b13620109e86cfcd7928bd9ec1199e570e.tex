\textbf{Proof.}
By the \(\kappa\)-feasibility assumption, \(\mathcal B_\kappa\neq\varnothing\).  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}kappa\mbox{-}exhaustion}\) gives compactness of \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) and the inclusion \(\mathcal B_\kappa\subseteq\mathcal B_Z\).  Hence all ordinary inverses appearing below are well defined.

We first verify attainment from the primitive design conditions.  Exposure positivity gives an invertible diagonal matrix \(\Pi\).  Every normalized representative of an element of \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) satisfies
\[
S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\succeq n\kappa I_2,
\]
so \(B\mapsto S_B^{-1}\) is continuous and uniformly bounded on the normalized representative set.  For each fixed \(e\in\{L,U\}\), the matrix
\[
M_e=\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(c_e)\Pi^{-1}\Omega
\]
is fixed and finite, and therefore
\[
g_B(c_e)=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(S_B^{-1}B^{\top}M_eB\right)
\]
is continuous in a normalized representative.  The formula is invariant under the residual independent sign changes of the two normalized columns, so it descends continuously to the projective quotient \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  The same argument applies to \(g_B(0)\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}ht\mbox{-}affine\mbox{-}drift}\),
\[
w(B)=h(0)+\max\left\{\frac{2c_L}{n}-g_B(c_L),\frac{2c_U}{n}-g_B(c_U)\right\},
\]
and thus \(w\) is continuous as the maximum of two continuous functions.  The Weierstrass theorem on the nonempty compact set \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) now shows that both
\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}=\operatorname*{argmin}_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathcal A_0=\operatorname*{argmax}_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}g_B(0)
\]
are nonempty.

For arbitrary \(B_{\mathrm{mm}}\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and \(B_0\in\mathcal A_0\), the definition of the minimum gives
\[
w(B_{\mathrm{mm}})=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\le w(B_0).
\]
Every member of \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) has loss exactly \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Taking the supremum over \(B_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and then the infimum over \(B_0\) therefore yields
\[
\sup_{B_{\mathrm{mm}}\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}}w(B_{\mathrm{mm}})
=v_{\mathrm{mm}}
\le
\inf_{B_0\in\mathcal A_0}w(B_0).
\]

Finally, strictness cannot be asserted uniformly over lawful instances.  Fix any design satisfying exposure positivity and \(\kappa\)-feasibility, and take the lawful bounded marginals \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\).  Then \(\mu_1=\mu_0=\sigma_1^2=\sigma_0^2=0\), the two sharp covariance endpoints are \(c_L=c_U=0\), and
\[
Q(0)=0.
\]
Consequently \(h(0)=g_B(0)=\ell_B(0)=w(B)=0\) for every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\).  Both optimizer correspondences equal the whole feasible class and the displayed weak inequality is an equality.  Thus a separate lawful witness is necessary for strict dominance. \(\square\)
