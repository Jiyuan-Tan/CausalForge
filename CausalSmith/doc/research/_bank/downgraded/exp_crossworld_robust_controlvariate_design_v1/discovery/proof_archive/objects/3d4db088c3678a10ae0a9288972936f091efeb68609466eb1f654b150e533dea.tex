\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(K_0\) be the ordered field generated over \(\mathbb Q\) by the finitely many entries of \(\Pi\), \(\Omega\), \(Q(c_L)\), and \(Q(c_U)\), together with all exactly encoded input constants.  By def:certified-input-interface, every generator is real algebraic, so \(K_0\) is a subfield of the real-algebraic numbers.  Ass:exposure-positivity gives \(\pi_{i,a}>0\) for every diagonal entry of \(\Pi\).  Therefore \(\Pi^{-1}\) exists, and all of its entries belong to \(K_0\).

We first remove the projective redundancy.  By def:novel-pseudoinverse-completion, every class in \(\mathcal B_Z^+\) has a representative for which each arm column is either zero or has squared Euclidean norm \(n\).  It is consequently enough to optimize over
\[
\mathcal K=
\left\{(b_1,b_0):
\bigl(b_1=0\ \vee\ b_1^{\top}b_1=n\bigr),\quad
\bigl(b_0=0\ \vee\ b_0^{\top}b_0=n\bigr)
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is the union of four compact basic semialgebraic sets over \(K_0\), and its quotient image is all of \(\mathcal B_Z^+\).

The loss is constant on the quotient fibers.  To see this, let \(C=\Omega^{1/2}B\), and, for \(e\in\{L,U\}\), let
\[
R_e=\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}Q(c_e)\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}.
\]
Both \(R_e\) and \(Q(c_e)\) are positive semidefinite.  Since \(S_B=C^{\top}C\), cyclicity of trace and def:completion-pseudoinverse-loss give
\[
g_B^+(c_e)
=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
C(C^{\top}C)^+C^{\top}R_e
\right].
\tag{2}
\]
Diagonalizing \(C^{\top}C\) shows directly that
\(C(C^{\top}C)^+C^{\top}\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{col}(C)\).  Independent nonzero rescaling of the nonzero arm columns leaves this column space unchanged.  Thus (2), and hence \(w^+(B)\), is invariant under the equivalence relation defining \(\mathcal B_Z^+\).  Moreover,
\[
0\preceq C(C^{\top}C)^+C^{\top}\preceq I,
\]
so positivity of \(R_e\) and cyclicity of trace yield
\[
0\le g_B^+(c_e)
\le n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(R_e)=h(c_e),
\qquad
0\le \ell_B^+(c_e)\le h(c_e).
\tag{3}
\]
Hence the attainable losses are nonempty, finite, and bounded below.  Nonemptiness also follows explicitly from the zero representative in \(\mathcal K\).

We next obtain exact rational formulas on every rank stratum.  Write \(S=S_B\).  This is a \(2\times2\) positive-semidefinite matrix, so precisely one row of the following table applies:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\operatorname{rank}(S)&\text{semialgebraic condition}&S^+\\ \hline
0&\operatorname{tr}(S)=0&0,\\[2mm]
1&\operatorname{tr}(S)>0,\ \det(S)=0&
\displaystyle\frac{S}{\{\operatorname{tr}(S)\}^2},\\[3mm]
2&\det(S)>0&
\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{adj}(S)}{\det(S)}.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]
For rank one, the nonzero eigenvalue is \(\lambda=\operatorname{tr}(S)\), and \(S=\lambda uu^{\top}\) for a unit vector \(u\); hence the middle expression is \(\lambda^{-1}uu^{\top}\).  The rank-zero and rank-two formulas follow from the definitions of the Moore--Penrose inverse and the ordinary inverse.  Every denominator in (4) is strictly positive on its stated stratum.

For each row of (4), substitute the displayed rational expression into the two endpoint gains in def:completion-pseudoinverse-loss.  Introduce variables \(z_L,z_U,y\) for the two endpoint losses and their maximum, and impose
\[
y\ge z_L,\qquad y\ge z_U,\qquad
(y=z_L\ \vee\ y=z_U).
\tag{5}
\]
Multiplication by the positive denominator belonging to the relevant row of (4) converts the gain equalities, the loss equalities, and (5) into polynomial sign conditions over \(K_0\).  Together, (1), (4), and (5) therefore define a finite-union semialgebraic graph
\[
\mathcal G=
\{(b_1,b_0,y):(b_1,b_0)\in\mathcal K,\ y=w^+(B)\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The construction covers ranks zero, one, and two, including every equality boundary, and never evaluates a rational formula where its denominator vanishes.  It also supplies the finite normalized-representative chart realization with respect to which quotient-invariant sublevel sets in \(\mathcal B_Z^+\) are semialgebraic.

Define the upper attainable-value set
\[
\mathcal T=
\left\{t\in\mathbb R:
\exists(b_1,b_0,y)\in\mathcal G\ \text{with}\ y\le t
\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
By (3), \(\mathcal T\) is nonempty and bounded below, and
\[
\inf\mathcal T
=\inf_{[B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+}w^+(B)
=v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+.
\tag{8}
\]
The defining formula (7) is first order over a real closed field.  Applying lem:real-closed-field-quantifier-elimination eliminates the basis, loss, and auxiliary variables and gives a quantifier-free description of \(\mathcal T\) by finitely many polynomial sign conditions over \(K_0\).  A boundary point of such a one-dimensional set must be a zero of one of those finitely many polynomials: if all of them were nonzero there, continuity would make every sign locally constant, contradicting the boundary property.  The finite infimum in (8) is a boundary point of the upper set \(\mathcal T\).  It follows that \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\) is algebraic over \(K_0\), and hence is a real-algebraic number.

The same elimination construction gives a terminating cylindrical-algebraic computation.  There are finitely many variables and input polynomials.  Each projection stage forms finitely many coefficients, discriminants, and resultants; exact real-algebraic root isolation makes finitely many sign cells; and lifting repeats this finite operation through finitely many coordinates.  The equivalence supplied by lem:real-closed-field-quantifier-elimination certifies all resulting sign decisions.  Exact root isolation at \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\) can therefore be refined until it returns rational, or more generally real-algebraic, endpoints \(L_\varepsilon^+\) and \(U_\varepsilon^+\) such that
\[
L_\varepsilon^+\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\le U_\varepsilon^+,
\qquad
U_\varepsilon^+-L_\varepsilon^+\le\varepsilon.
\tag{9}
\]
Termination of this refinement follows from the exact strict inequality \(\varepsilon>0\): successive rational bisection widths tend to zero, and comparison of a rational width with the real-algebraic number \(\varepsilon\) is decidable by exact sign determination.

It remains to produce an incumbent even when the infimum is not attained.  By the defining property of the finite infimum and \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\exists [B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+
\quad w^+(B)<v_{\mathrm{mm}}^++\varepsilon.
\tag{10}
\]
All coefficients in the formula obtained by adjoining (10) to (1), (4), and (5) are real algebraic.  Quantifier elimination shows that this formula, true in \(\mathbb R\), is also true in the real closed field of real-algebraic numbers: after elimination its truth is determined solely by signs of the same real-algebraic constants.  Repeating elimination one coordinate at a time and selecting a sign cell on which the residual existential formula remains true produces a representative with real-algebraic coordinates.  Denote its quotient class by \([B_\varepsilon]_+\).  Then
\[
[B_\varepsilon]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+,
\qquad
w^+(B_\varepsilon)<v_{\mathrm{mm}}^++\varepsilon,
\tag{11}
\]
which implies the weak inequality required in the statement.

Finally, replace the strict inequality in (10) by
\(w^+(B)\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}^++\varepsilon\).  The rank-stratified formulas above show that the resulting normalized-representative preimage, and therefore the corresponding subset of the finite projective chart realization of \(\mathcal B_Z^+\), is semialgebraic.  It is nonempty by (11).  Retain the four normalized-support pieces, the rank predicate and rational pseudoinverse formula from (4) on every cell, the projection polynomials and sign table, the algebraic sample from (11), and the isolating data proving (9).  Exact algebraic arithmetic checks every retained item.  This is the asserted finite CAD sign, sample, and value-isolation certificate covering all three ranks.

The construction uses only an \(\varepsilon\)-incumbent.  It neither requires an attained minimizer nor proves outer convergence, a uniform complexity bound, or a semidefinite-program reduction. \(\square\)
