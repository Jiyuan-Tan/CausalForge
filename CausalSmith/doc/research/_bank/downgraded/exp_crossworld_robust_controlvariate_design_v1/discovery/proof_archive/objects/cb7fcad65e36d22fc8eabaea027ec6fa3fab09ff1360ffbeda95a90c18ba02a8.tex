\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1_3\).  The exposure events can be enumerated over the eight assignments.  The all-zero and all-one assignments expose no unit to either focal label.  If only unit \(i\) is treated, then \(D_{i,1}=1\) and \(D_{j,0}=1\) for the other two units.  If exactly \(i\) and \(j\) are treated, then \(D_{k,0}=1\) for the remaining unit.  These events are mutually exclusive within a unit, as required by ass:mutually-exclusive-exposures.  Their probabilities under the displayed Bernoulli design give
\[
 \pi_{i,1}=\frac{4}{27},
 \qquad
 \pi_{i,0}=2\frac{4}{27}+\frac{2}{27}=\frac{10}{27}.
\]
In particular, exposure positivity holds.  For distinct \(i,j\), direct intersection-probability calculations give
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &i=j&i\ne j\\ \hline
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{j,1})&92/729&-16/729\\
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{j,0})&-40/729&68/729\\
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,0},D_{j,0})&170/729&8/729.
 \end{array}
\]
For example, when \(i\ne j\), the event \(D_{i,1}D_{j,0}=1\) is the singleton assignment treating \(i\), and hence has probability \(4/27=108/729\); subtracting \((4/27)(10/27)=40/729\) gives \(68/729\).  Similarly, \(D_{i,0}D_{j,0}=1\) is the singleton assignment treating the third unit and has probability \(4/27\).  These entries prove the asserted formulas for \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\).  Ass:known-exposure makes the two displayed labels legitimate potential-outcome exposures; outcomes under the label \(\star\) do not enter any calculation below.

For a Bernoulli \((1/2)\) variable, \(\mu_a=1/2\), \(\sigma_a^2=1/4\), and its quantile takes only the values zero and one.  The countermonotone and comonotone quantile integrals in the definitions of \(c_L\) and \(c_U\) therefore give
\[
 c_L=0-\frac14=-\frac14,
 \qquad
 c_U=\frac12-\frac14=\frac14.
\]
Substitution in def:moment-matrix gives the two displayed endpoint matrices, because each diagonal block is
\[
 \frac14J_3+\frac14I_3=A,
\]
whereas the cross block \(-J_3/4-cI_3\) is \((I_3-J_3)/4\) at \(c_L\) and \(-A\) at \(c_U\).

It remains to certify the global optimizer sets rather than merely exhibit candidate bases.  Fix a normalized representative \(B(x,y)\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), and put
\[
 s=\mathbf 1^{\top}x,
 \qquad t=\mathbf 1^{\top}y,
 \qquad q=x^{\top}y.
\]
The normalization in def:kappa-basis-class and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 0\le s^2\le9,
 \qquad 0\le t^2\le9.
 \tag{1}
\]
Block multiplication using the already verified \(\Omega\) gives
\[
 S_B=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 \dfrac49-\dfrac{16s^2}{729}&-\dfrac{4q}{27}+\dfrac{68st}{729}\\[4pt]
 -\dfrac{4q}{27}+\dfrac{68st}{729}&\dfrac23+\dfrac{8t^2}{729}
 \end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Define
\[
 d=729+12t^2-36s^2-54q^2+68qst-22s^2t^2.
 \tag{3}
\]
Equation (2) yields
\[
 \det(S_B)=\frac{8d}{19683}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The rank boundary is explicit here: membership in \(\mathcal B_\kappa\), with \(\kappa=1/8>0\), implies \(S_B\succeq(3/8)I_2\).  Thus \(d>0\), every inverse used below exists, and no division is made at a singular basis.

For a transparent check of the objective algebra, let \(C_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B\).  Its two block rows are
\[
 C_B=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 x-\dfrac{4s}{27}\mathbf1&-y+\dfrac{17t}{27}\mathbf1\\[3pt]
 -\dfrac25x+\dfrac{34s}{135}\mathbf1&\dfrac35y+\dfrac{4t}{135}\mathbf1
 \end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Writing \(Q_L=Q(c_L)\) and \(Q_U=Q(c_U)\), equations (1)--(5) give
\[
 C_B^{\top}Q_LC_B=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 (1195s^2+6561)/24300&-(729q-1820st)/12150\\
 -(729q-1820st)/12150&(1240t^2+729)/6075
 \end{bmatrix},
 \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 C_B^{\top}Q_UC_B=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 3(49-5s^2)/100&(5st-14q)/25\\
 (5st-14q)/25&(48-5t^2)/25
 \end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The trace identities in def:gain-loss now yield
\[
 \begin{split}
 g_B(c_L)
 &=\frac{76545+9891s^2+30732t^2-5832q^2+18232qst-10460s^2t^2}{1200d},\\
 g_B(c_U)
 &=\frac{3\{66825-5181s^2-2652t^2-6048q^2+5968qst-1260s^2t^2\}}{400d}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{8}
\]
The uncontrolled endpoint traces are
\[
 h(c_L)=\frac{313}{600},
 \qquad
 h(c_U)=\frac{171}{200}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Because def:moment-matrix is affine in \(c\) and zero is the midpoint of \([c_L,c_U]\), def:gain-loss gives
\[
 g_B(0)=\frac{g_B(c_L)+g_B(c_U)}2.
\]
Combining this identity with (8),
\[
 \frac{31}{80}-g_B(0)=\frac{N_0(s,t,q)}{1200d},
 \tag{10}
\]
where
\[
 N_0=5022q^2-4352qst+670s^2t^2+1629s^2+2148t^2.
\]
Completing the square gives
\[
 N_0
 =5022\left(q-\frac{1088}{2511}st\right)^2
 +1629s^2+2148t^2-\frac{685118}{2511}s^2t^2.
 \tag{11}
\]
The last three terms are a bilinear function of \((u,v)=(s^2,t^2)\in[0,9]^2\).  Its values at the four vertices are, respectively,
\[
 0,
 \qquad14661,
 \qquad19332,
 \qquad\frac{368665}{31}.
\]
A bilinear function on this rectangle is the convex combination of its four vertex values with the usual bilinear interpolation weights.  Hence (11) is nonnegative.  Moreover, equality in its bilinear part forces \(s=t=0\), and equality in the square then forces \(q=0\).  Since \(d>0\), (10) proves
\[
 \max_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}g_B(0)=\frac{31}{80}
\]
with equality exactly when \(s=t=q=0\), provided such bases are feasible.  They are: for these invariants, (2) becomes \(\operatorname{diag}(4/9,2/3)\), so
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=\frac4{27}>\frac18.
 \tag{12}
\]
They are also nonempty; for example one may take
\[
 x=\sqrt{\frac32}(1,-1,0)^{\top},
 \qquad
 y=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,1,-2)^{\top}.
\]
This proves the asserted full description of \(\mathcal A_0\).  Substitution in (8)--(9) gives
\[
 g_B(c_L)=\frac7{80},
 \qquad
 g_B(c_U)=\frac{11}{16},
 \qquad
 w(B)=\max\left\{\frac{313}{600}-\frac7{80},
 \frac{171}{200}-\frac{11}{16}\right\}
 =\frac{521}{1200}
 \tag{13}
\]
for every \(B\in\mathcal A_0\).

We next give a matching lower-bound certificate for the primary minimax problem.  Let
\[
 \lambda=\frac{1407}{2635}\in(0,1),
 \qquad
 v=\frac{2613}{8432},
\]
and abbreviate \(\ell_L=\ell_B(c_L)\), \(\ell_U=\ell_B(c_U)\).  Since a maximum dominates every strict convex combination, (8)--(9) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 w(B)-v
 &\ge \lambda\ell_L+(1-\lambda)\ell_U-v\\
 &=\frac{3N_{\mathrm{mm}}(s,t,q)}{210800d},
 \end{split}
 \tag{14}
\]
where
\[
 N_{\mathrm{mm}}
 =274734q^2-281644qst+68986s^2t^2+34527s^2.
\]
Another exact square completion yields
\[
 N_{\mathrm{mm}}
 =274734\left(q-\frac{70411}{137367}st\right)^2
 +s^2\left(34527-\frac{439017980}{137367}t^2\right).
 \tag{15}
\]
By (1), the coefficient in the second term is bounded below by
\[
 34527-9\frac{439017980}{137367}
 =\frac{87967621}{15263}>0.
 \tag{16}
\]
Thus (14) proves \(w(B)\ge v\) over the entire feasible class, and equality in the second line of (14) occurs exactly when \(s=q=0\).  Because both weights in the first line of (14) are positive, equality \(w(B)=v\) additionally requires \(\ell_L=\ell_U\).  When \(s=q=0\), direct simplification of (8)--(9) gives
\[
 \ell_L-\ell_U
 =-\frac{3(55t^2-216)}{10(4t^2+243)}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Consequently equality in the minimax lower bound holds exactly when
\[
 s=0,
 \qquad q=0,
 \qquad t^2=\frac{216}{55}.
 \tag{18}
\]
Conversely, (17) and (14) show that every basis satisfying (18) has both endpoint losses equal to \(v\).  Such bases exist: choose any normalized \(y\) with \((\mathbf1^{\top}y)^2=216/55<9\), and choose normalized \(x\) in the nonzero orthogonal complement of \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,y\}\).  For every such pair, (2) is diagonal with entries
\[
 \frac49,
 \qquad \frac23+\frac{8t^2}{729},
\]
so it is feasible and again satisfies (12).  This proves both the claimed full description of \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and
\[
 v_{\mathrm{mm}}=\frac{2613}{8432}.
\]

Finally, the positive exposure probabilities and the feasible bases just constructed verify the two membership predicates in def:lawful-instance-class, so \(\xi_0\in\mathfrak X_\kappa\).  Equations (12), (13), and (18) apply to every member of the two optimizer correspondences, not merely to selected representatives.  Therefore
\[
 \Delta(\xi_0)
 =\frac{521}{1200}-\frac{2613}{8432}
 =\frac{4912}{39525}=\delta>0,
\]
and the optimizer-wide regularity slack is
\[
 \frac4{27}-\frac18=\frac5{216}=\eta>0.
\]
These are precisely the two asserted full-correspondence certificates. \(\square\)
