\textbf{Proof.}
On the compact rate-admissible class define
\[
d_n=\sup_B|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|,
\qquad
e_n=\sup_B|\widehat g_B(0)-g_B(0)|.
\tag{1}
\]
The exact sensitivity condition and lem:uniform-pilot-endpoint-matrix-rate, including its factor-two control at zero covariance, imply
\[
n\mathbb E_Pd_n\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
n\mathbb E_Pe_n\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(B_n^\star\) minimize \(w\) on \(\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\).  Empirical optimality and (1) give, pointwise in the pilot sample,
\[
\begin{aligned}
w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})
&\le\widehat w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})+d_n\\
&\le\widehat w(B_n^\star)+d_n\\
&\le w(B_n^\star)+2d_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The reverse inequality \(w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})\ge w(B_n^\star)\) follows from the definition of \(B_n^\star\).  Taking pilot expectations in (3) and using (2) proves
\[
\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})
=v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}+o(n^{-1}).
\tag{4}
\]

We next bridge the endpoint loss to the feasible estimator.  Conditional on the pilot, \(\widehat B_s\) is fixed and is independent of the main assignment and main potential-outcome array by ass:pilot-main-outcome-independence.  The uniform conclusion of lem:uniform-zhao-ht-oracle-variance-equivalence therefore gives, uniformly in the realized selected basis and in \(H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\),
\[
\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}
\operatorname{Var}_\nu
(\widehat\tau_{\widehat B_s}^{\mathrm{CV}}
\mid\widehat B_s,Y)
=\ell_{\widehat B_s}(c_H)+o(n^{-1}).
\tag{5}
\]
The remainder in (5) is uniform, so taking a supremum over \(H\) preserves it.  Assumption ass:frechet-covariance-domain and thm:fixed-basis-endpoints imply, for every fixed \(B\),
\[
\sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}\ell_B(c_H)=w(B).
\tag{6}
\]
Apply (6) conditionally at \(B=\widehat B_s\), then integrate over the pilot.  Equations (5)--(6) prove, for \(s\in\{\mathrm{mm},0\}\),
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[
\sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}
\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}
\operatorname{Var}_\nu
(\widehat\tau_{\widehat B_s}^{\mathrm{CV}}
\mid\widehat B_s,Y)
\right]
=\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_s)+o(n^{-1}).
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to derive the zero-selector expectation bound from the stated quantitative modulus.  Let \(B_n^0\in\mathcal A_{0,\overline a}\).  Empirical optimality and (1) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_n^\star-g_{\widehat B_0}(0)
&=g_{B_n^0}(0)-g_{\widehat B_0}(0)\\
&\le
|g_{B_n^0}(0)-\widehat g_{B_n^0}(0)|
+|\widehat g_{\widehat B_0}(0)-g_{\widehat B_0}(0)|\\
&\le2e_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
By the definition of \(\omega_n\), equation (8) yields the pointwise inequality
\[
[q_n-w(\widehat B_0)]_+\le\omega_n(2e_n),
\]
and hence
\[
w(\widehat B_0)\ge q_n-\omega_n(2e_n).
\tag{9}
\]
Taking \(P\)-expectations in (9) and applying the assumed integrability rate gives
\[
\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_0)
\ge q_n-o(n^{-1})
=\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_{0,\overline a}}w(B)-o(n^{-1}).
\tag{10}
\]
Unlike a convergence-in-probability argument, (9)--(10) directly control the expectation on the \(n^{-1}\) scale.  This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
