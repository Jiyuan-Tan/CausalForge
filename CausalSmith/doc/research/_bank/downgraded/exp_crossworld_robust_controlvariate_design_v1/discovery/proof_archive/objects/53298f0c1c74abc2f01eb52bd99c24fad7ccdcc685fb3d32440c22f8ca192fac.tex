\textbf{Proof.}
Assumption ass:coordinate-bounded-kappa-feasibility makes \(\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\) nonempty.  Index the \(2n\) exposure coordinates by \(j\), write \(\pi_j=\mathbb E_\nu D_j\), and put \(Z_j=D_j-\pi_j\).  Thus \(|Z_j|\leq1\) and \(\mathbb E_\nu Z_j=0\).  Let \(N=2n\) and let \(d_n^{\mathrm{dep}}\leq\overline d n^\alpha\) be the maximum closed-neighborhood size in \(\mathcal G_n\).

We first record the dependency-graph counts used below.  In the expansion of a joint moment of centered coordinates, a tuple contributes zero whenever one of its distinct indices has no dependency edge to any other index in the tuple.  A contributing ordered four-tuple either contains two disjoint dependency edges, of which there are at most \(C N^2(d_n^{\mathrm{dep}})^2\), or its distinct-index dependency subgraph is connected, of which there are at most \(C N(d_n^{\mathrm{dep}})^3\le C N^2(d_n^{\mathrm{dep}})^2\).  Every contributing ordered three-tuple is connected after repeated indices are represented by closed-neighborhood edges, so there are at most \(C N(d_n^{\mathrm{dep}})^2\) of them.  Consequently, for deterministic scalar coefficient arrays \(q,r,s\),
\[
 \mathbb E_\nu\!\left(\sum_jq_jZ_j\right)^4
 \leq C N^2(d_n^{\mathrm{dep}})^2\lVert q\rVert_\infty^4,
 \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_\nu
 \left(\sum_jq_jZ_j\right)
 \left(\sum_jr_jZ_j\right)
 \left(\sum_js_jZ_j\right)\right|
 \leq C N(d_n^{\mathrm{dep}})^2
 \lVert q\rVert_\infty\lVert r\rVert_\infty\lVert s\rVert_\infty.
 \tag{2}
\]
These inequalities follow directly by expansion, the preceding vanishing argument, \(|Z_j|\leq1\), and the tuple counts; hence no normal approximation is being assumed.

Fix a bounded potential-outcome array and \(B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\).  Write \(S_B=B^\top\Omega B\), so \(S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2\) and Zhao's pseudoinverse coefficient equals the ordinary-inverse coefficient used below.  If coordinate \(j\) represents \((i,a)\), ass:known-exposure gives
\[
D_{i,a}\{Y_i(W)-Y_i(a)\}=0.
\]
Thus the signed observed stack has coordinate \(D_jY_j/\pi_j\), with no exposure-mismatch remainder.  Consequently
\[
 \{\Pi^{-1}(\widehat Y^{\mathrm{obs}}-Y)\}_j
 =\frac{D_jY_j}{\pi_j^2}-\frac{Y_j}{\pi_j}
 =\frac{Y_jZ_j}{\pi_j^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 G_B:=\widehat\gamma^{\mathrm{HT}}(B)-\gamma_B^*(Y)
 =\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{B,j}Z_j,
 \qquad
 u_{B,j}=S_B^{-1}B^\top\Omega e_j\frac{Y_j}{\pi_j^2}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Every column of \(\Omega\) has at most \(d_n^{\mathrm{dep}}\) nonzero entries, each of absolute value at most one.  The coordinate envelope, exposure-rate bound, and \(\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq(n\kappa)^{-1}\) thus imply
\[
 \sup_{B,j}\lVert u_{B,j}\rVert_2
 \leq K_u n^{\alpha+2\beta-1},
 \qquad
 K_u=\frac{\sqrt2\,\overline a\,\overline d}{\kappa\underline\pi^2}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Also set
\[
 X_B=n^{-1}B^\top(D-\mathbb E_\nu D)
 =\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{B,j}Z_j,
 \qquad
 \sup_{B,j}\lVert x_{B,j}\rVert_2\leq\frac{\overline a}{n}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Applying (1) coordinatewise to (3)--(5), and absorbing the fixed dimension two into constants, gives
\[
 \sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert G_B\rVert_2^4
 \leq K_G n^{6\alpha+8\beta-2},
 \qquad
 \sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert X_B\rVert_2^4
 \leq K_X n^{2\alpha-2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The feasible and oracle estimators obey
\[
 \widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{CV}}
 =\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}+\Delta_B,
 \qquad
 \Delta_B=-G_B^\top X_B.
 \tag{7}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and (6) yield the finite bound
\[
 \sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\Delta_B^2
 \leq K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}.
 \tag{8}
\]

It remains to control the covariance term without replacing it by an unjustified absolute-third-moment count.  Put \(T_B=\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}-\tau\).  The Horvitz--Thompson term is unbiased and the oracle control statistic is centered, so \(\mathbb E_\nu T_B=0\).  Moreover
\[
 T_B=\sum_{j=1}^{N}t_{B,j}Z_j,
 \qquad
 t_{B,j}=n^{-1}\left\{\frac{Y_j}{\pi_j}-B_{j\cdot}\gamma_B^*(Y)\right\}.
 \tag{9}
\]
For \(Z_B=\Omega^{1/2}B\), the matrix \(Z_B(Z_B^\top Z_B)^{-1}Z_B^\top\) is an orthogonal projector.  Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \gamma_B^*(Y)^\top S_B\gamma_B^*(Y)
 &=Y^\top\Pi^{-1}\Omega B S_B^{-1}B^\top\Omega\Pi^{-1}Y\\
 &\leq Y^\top\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Y.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{10}
\]
The row-sparsity bound gives \(\lVert\Omega\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\overline d n^\alpha\), while bounded outcomes and exposure positivity give \(\lVert\Pi^{-1}Y\rVert_2^2\leq2\underline\pi^{-2}n^{1+2\beta}\).  Combining these facts with \(S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2\) in (10) proves
\[
 \sup_B\lVert\gamma_B^*(Y)\rVert_2
 \leq K_\gamma n^{\alpha/2+\beta}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Equations (9), (11), the coordinate envelope, and \(n\geq3\) then imply
\[
 \sup_{B,j}|t_{B,j}|
 \leq K_t n^{\alpha/2+\beta-1}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Using (3), (5), and (9), and the fact that \(T_B\) is centered, we may expand the covariance exactly as
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)
 =-\sum_{l=1}^{2}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}
 t_{B,i}u_{B,j,l}x_{B,k,l}\,
 \mathbb E_\nu(Z_iZ_jZ_k).
 \tag{13}
\]
Applying the connected-triple bound (2) to (13), followed by (4), (5), and (12), gives
\[
 \sup_B\left|\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)\right|
 \leq K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
 \tag{14}
\]
This direct signed-moment calculation is the required covariance control.

Define the nonnegative, deterministic finite-\(n\) envelope
\[
 R_n
 :=K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}
 +2K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Because \(\alpha,\beta\geq0\),
\[
 \frac{7}{2}\alpha+3\beta
 \leq4\alpha+4\beta.
 \tag{16}
\]
Thus, for \(K_R=K_\Delta+2K_{\mathrm{cov}}\) and every \(n\geq3\),
\[
 R_n\leq K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Expanding the variance in (7), using \(\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\Delta_B\mid Y)\leq\mathbb E_\nu\Delta_B^2\), and applying (8) and (14), we obtain the uniform finite-sample certificate
\[
 \sup_{\substack{B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\\0\leq Y_i(a)\leq1}}
 \left|
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{CV}}\mid Y)
 -\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
 \right|
 \leq R_n.
 \tag{18}
\]
Assumption ass:zhao-first-order-rate states \(4\alpha+4\beta<1\).  Therefore (17) gives \(nR_n\leq K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-1}\to0\), proving the first asymptotic assertion.

For the exact oracle identity, completing the square gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
 =n^{-2}Y^\top
 \left\{
 \Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}
 -\Pi^{-1}\Omega BS_B^{-1}B^\top\Omega\Pi^{-1}
 \right\}Y.
 \tag{19}
\]
Under ass:iid-potential-pairs, \(\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}(YY^\top)=Q(c_H)\).  Taking expectation in (19), using \(\mathbb E(Y^\top MY)=\operatorname{tr}\{M\mathbb E(YY^\top)\}\), and invoking def:gain-loss yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
 =h(c_H)-g_B(c_H)=\ell_B(c_H).
 \tag{20}
\]
Finally, integrating (18) under \(H^{\otimes n}\) and applying (20) produces the finite ex ante envelope
\[
 \sup_{\substack{B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\\H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}}
 \left|
 \mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{CV}}\mid Y)
 -\ell_B(c_H)
 \right|
 \leq R_n=o(n^{-1}).
 \tag{21}
\]
The inner variance throughout is design based, conditional on the fixed array; the expectation in (20)--(21) is only the declared ex ante evaluation.  This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
