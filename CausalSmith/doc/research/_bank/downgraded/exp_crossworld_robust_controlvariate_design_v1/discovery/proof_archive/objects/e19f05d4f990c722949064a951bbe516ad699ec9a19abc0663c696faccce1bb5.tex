\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(\mathcal K_n=\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\).  Assumption ass:coordinate-bounded-kappa-feasibility makes this class nonempty.  Its normalized representatives form a closed subset of a product of two spheres, and the residual sign quotient is compact.  The empirical criteria are continuous in the basis and Borel measurable in the finite pilot sample.  Successively minimizing the coordinates in a fixed ordered list of signed pivot charts therefore gives the stated measurable lexicographic selectors.  Known exposure is inherited through def:feasible-ht-pilot-selected-estimator and def:observable-coefficient-map, so each deployed outcome stack has the exposure-potential-outcome interpretation used by the variance bridge.

Let \(M_n,d_n,e_n\) be the displayed quantities.  Lem:uniform-pilot-endpoint-matrix-rate gives pathwise
\[
d_n\le C_nM_n,
\qquad e_n\le2C_nM_n.
\tag{1}
\]
Choose \(B_n^\star\in\mathcal K_n\) with \(w(B_n^\star)=v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}\).  Empirical optimality yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})
&\le\widehat w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})+d_n\\
&\le\widehat w(B_n^\star)+d_n\\
&\le v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}+2d_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The population minimum gives the reverse inequality.  Taking pilot expectations and applying (1) proves
\[
0\le\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})-v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}
\le2C_n\mathbb E_PM_n.
\tag{3}
\]

Conditional on the pilot, \(\widehat B_s\) is fixed and lies in \(\mathcal K_n\).  Ass:pilot-main-outcome-independence permits application of lem:uniform-zhao-ht-oracle-variance-equivalence at this random but pilot-measurable basis.  Ass:frechet-covariance-domain and thm:fixed-basis-endpoints give
\[
\sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}\ell_B(c_H)=w(B).
\tag{4}
\]
Using \(|\sup f-sup g|\le\sup|f-g|\), then integrating over the pilot, proves
\[
|V_s-\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_s)|\le R_n,
\qquad s\in\{\mathrm{mm},0\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The exact sensitivity condition implies \(nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n\to0\), while the oracle lemma gives \(nR_n\to0\).  Equations (3)--(5) prove all unconditional robust-selector first-order assertions.

For the conditional zero-surrogate assertion, choose \(B_n^0\in\mathcal A_{0,\overline a}\).  Empirical optimality and (1) give
\[
g_n^\star-g_{\widehat B_0}(0)\le2e_n\le4C_nM_n.
\tag{6}
\]
If the displayed deterministic linear error bound holds, then
\[
[q_n-w(\widehat B_0)]_+
\le L_n\{g_n^\star-g_{\widehat B_0}(0)\}
\le4L_nC_nM_n.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(x\ge q-[q-x]_+\), taking expectations in (7) proves
\[
q_n-\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_0)
\le4L_nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(\sup_nL_n<\infty\), the right side is \(o(n^{-1})\).  No primitive theorem establishing such a uniformly bounded \(L_n\) is proved here: (7)--(8) are explicitly conditional on that separately checkable geometry.  Without it, (6) gives nominal-gain regret only and cannot supply a first-order worst-loss ordering. \(\square\)
