\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(m=m_1\wedge m_0\), and let \(W_1\) denote the one-dimensional Wasserstein distance.  For probability laws \(R,S\) on \([0,1]\), the layer-cake identity and Fubini's theorem give
\[
W_1(R,S)
=\int_0^1|R^{-1}(u)-S^{-1}(u)|\,du
=\int_0^1|R(y)-S(y)|\,dy.
\tag{1}
\]
For each arm \(a\), Jensen's inequality and the binomial variance identity give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_PW_1(\widehat F_a,F_a)
&=\int_0^1\mathbb E_P|\widehat F_a(y)-F_a(y)|\,dy\\
&\le\int_0^1
\sqrt{\frac{F_a(y)\{1-F_a(y)\}}{m_a}}\,dy
\le\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m_a}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Because \(y\mapsto y\) and \(y\mapsto y^2\) are respectively one- and two-Lipschitz on \([0,1]\), (1) implies
\[
|\widehat\mu_a-\mu_a|
\le W_1(\widehat F_a,F_a),
\qquad
|\widehat\sigma_a^2-\sigma_a^2|
\le4W_1(\widehat F_a,F_a).
\tag{3}
\]
For either the comonotone or countermonotone quantile product, the elementary inequality
\[
|xy-x'y'|\le|x-x'|+|y-y'|
\qquad(x,y,x',y'\in[0,1])
\tag{4}
\]
together with (1) gives
\[
|\widehat c_e-c_e|
\le2\{W_1(\widehat F_1,F_1)+W_1(\widehat F_0,F_0)\},
\qquad e\in\{L,U\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Every block of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) is a linear combination of \(I_n\) and \(\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\), with coefficients bounded by the errors in (3)--(5) and the corresponding product-of-means error.  Since
\[
\|I_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1,
\qquad
\|\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=n,
\tag{6}
\]
equations (2)--(6) imply, for a numerical constant \(K_M<\infty\),
\[
\mathbb E_PM_n
\le K_M\frac{n}{\sqrt m}.
\tag{7}
\]
This is exactly \(M_n=O_{L^1}(n/\sqrt m)\).

We next bound the deterministic sensitivity without a dependency-neighborhood calculation.  For \(B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\), set \(Z_B=\Omega^{1/2}B\).  Regularity makes \(Z_B^{\top}Z_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\) invertible, and
\[
P_B=Z_B(Z_B^{\top}Z_B)^{-1}Z_B^{\top}
\tag{8}
\]
is the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{range}(Z_B)\).  Thus \(0\preceq P_B\preceq I_{2n}\).  Congruence by \(\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}\), which is well defined by the exposure-probability condition, yields
\[
0\preceq
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}P_B\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}
\preceq
\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}=A_{\mathrm{HT}}.
\tag{9}
\]
Both matrices in (9) are positive semidefinite, so their nuclear norms equal their traces.  Each coordinate of \(D\) is Bernoulli with mean \(\pi_{i,a}\), and therefore \(\Omega_{(i,a),(i,a)}=\pi_{i,a}(1-\pi_{i,a})\).  It follows from ass:zhao-exposure-probability-rate that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|A_B\|_*
&\le\operatorname{tr}(A_{\mathrm{HT}})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a\in\{1,0\}}
\frac{\pi_{i,a}(1-\pi_{i,a})}{\pi_{i,a}^2}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a\in\{1,0\}}
\frac{1-\pi_{i,a}}{\pi_{i,a}}
\le2\underline\pi^{-1}n^{1+\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
The same last bound applies to \(\|A_{\mathrm{HT}}\|_*\).  By the definition of \(C_n\),
\[
C_n\le4\underline\pi^{-1}n^{\beta-1}.
\tag{11}
\]

For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), the endpoint residual is
\[
n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{(A_{\mathrm{HT}}-A_B)Q(c_e)\},
\tag{12}
\]
and its empirical analogue replaces \(Q(c_e)\) by \(\widehat Q_e\).  Trace duality, (9), and
\[
|\max_e x_e-\max_e y_e|\le\max_e|x_e-y_e|
\tag{13}
\]
therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}}
|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|
&\le n^{-2}\sup_B
\|A_{\mathrm{HT}}-A_B\|_*M_n\\
&\le C_nM_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

It remains to compare the empirical zero-covariance matrix with its population counterpart.  Let
\[
\Delta_e=\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e),
\qquad
d_e=\widehat c_e-c_e.
\tag{15}
\]
The affine identities \(Q(c)=Q(0)-cE\) and \(\widehat Q_e=\widehat Q(0)-\widehat c_eE\) imply
\[
\widehat Q(0)-Q(0)=\Delta_e+d_eE.
\tag{16}
\]
Choose a unit vector \(z\perp\mathbf1\), which exists because \(n\ge3\).  The off-diagonal block of \(\Delta_e\), evaluated between \(z\) and itself, equals \(-d_e\).  With \(u=(z^{\top},0)^{\top}\) and \(v=(0,z^{\top})^{\top}\), both unit vectors, this gives
\[
|d_e|=|u^{\top}\Delta_ev|
\le\|\Delta_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{17}
\]
Because \(\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\), equations (16)--(17) prove
\[
\|\widehat Q(0)-Q(0)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le2M_n.
\tag{18}
\]
Trace duality now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}}
|\widehat g_B(0)-g_B(0)|
&\le2n^{-2}\sup_B\|A_B\|_*M_n\\
&\le2C_nM_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
If \(nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n\to0\), taking pilot expectations in (14) and (19) makes both uniform errors \(o(n^{-1})\).  Finally, (7) and (11) give
\[
nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n
\le4\underline\pi^{-1}K_M
\frac{n^{1+\beta}}{\sqrt{m_1\wedge m_0}},
\tag{20}
\]
so \(\sqrt{m_1\wedge m_0}/n^{1+\beta}\to\infty\) is sufficient.  The derivation used only the pilot law, bounded support, nonemptiness and regularity of the basis class, and the exposure-probability rate; neither known exposure nor a dependency graph was used. \(\square\)
