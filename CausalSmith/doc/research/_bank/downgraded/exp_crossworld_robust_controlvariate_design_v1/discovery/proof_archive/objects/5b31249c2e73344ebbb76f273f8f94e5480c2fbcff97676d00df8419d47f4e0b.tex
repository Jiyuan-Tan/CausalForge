\textbf{Proof.}
Direct algebra gives
\[
\lVert x\rVert_2^2=\lVert y_0\rVert_2^2
=\lVert y_R\rVert_2^2=3,
\qquad
\mathbf1^{\top}x=0,
\qquad
x^{\top}y_R=0,
\qquad
(\mathbf1^{\top}y_R)^2=\frac{216}{55}.
\tag{1}
\]
All displayed coordinates have absolute value below \(2\).  The equality-set formulas in \(\texttt{thm:triangle-completion-separation-certificate}\) therefore place \(B_0\) in \(\mathcal A_0\) and \(B_R\) in \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), and the same theorem gives
\[
\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_{B_0})
=\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_{B_R})=\frac4{27}>\frac18.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus both bases lie in \(\mathcal S_{2,3}(1/8,2)\).  In particular, the corrected selected-rank condition in \(\texttt{thm:rank-adaptive-oracle-observable-variance-bridge}\) is certified here by the exact eigenvalue and coordinate signs, rather than inferred from pilot independence.

The two-point pilot empirical law is exactly \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) in each arm.  Consequently its means, variances, countermonotone endpoint, comonotone endpoint, and both endpoint matrices equal their population counterparts.  The two uniform pilot objective errors are therefore exactly zero.  Every input coordinate is rational or real algebraic, so \(\texttt{thm:encoded-cad-pilot-selector-certificate}\) applies; no arbitrary-real computation claim is used.

We spell out the finite observable calculation.  For \(e\in\{L,U\}\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}^3\), set
\[
Y_i^{(e,z)}(1)=z_i,
\qquad
Y_i^{(U,z)}(0)=z_i,
\qquad
Y_i^{(L,z)}(0)=1-z_i.
\tag{3}
\]
The first two schedules give the upper endpoint coupling and the first and third give the lower endpoint coupling.  For a displayed basis \(B\), let \(T_B(w,z,e)\) be the observable estimator obtained by substituting assignment \(w\), schedule \((e,z)\), the exact triangle \(\Pi,\Omega\), and \(B\) into \(\texttt{def:observable-coefficient-map}\).  Since the design has eight assignments and either endpoint coupling has eight equally weighted potential-outcome arrays,
\[
V_{B,e}
=\frac18\sum_{z\in\{0,1\}^3}
\left[
\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^3}\nu(w)T_B(w,z,e)^2
-\left\{\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^3}\nu(w)T_B(w,z,e)\right\}^2
\right].
\tag{4}
\]
Equation (4) is an explicit sum of sixty-four rational or real-algebraic terms for each basis and endpoint.  Exact substitution and common-denominator reduction give
\[
V_{0,L}=\frac{3112847}{480000},
\qquad
V_{0,U}=\frac{1019349}{160000},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
V_{R,L}
=\frac{948747698543}{133309920000}
-\frac{9641367\sqrt{93}}{44795000},
\qquad
V_{R,U}
=\frac{304083944181}{44436640000}
-\frac{8821467\sqrt{93}}{44795000}.
\tag{6}
\]
The endpoint differences are
\[
V_{0,L}-V_{0,U}=\frac{137}{1200}>0,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
V_{R,L}-V_{R,U}
=\frac{2943215-196776\sqrt{93}}{10750800}>0.
\tag{8}
\]
For (8), use \(\sqrt{93}<193/20\), whose square comparison is \(93<37249/400\).  Thus the lower covariance endpoint is worst for both bases.

Subtracting the two lower-endpoint values in (5)--(6) gives the displayed \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  The rank-two population equality-set theorem gives \(w(B_0)=521/1200\) and \(w(B_R)=2613/8432\), hence the displayed \(\delta\).  Subtracting the two exact observable-minus-oracle discrepancies gives the displayed \(E_{\mathrm{bridge}}\).  Its sign is positive because \(\sqrt{93}>241/25\), whose square comparison is \(93>58081/625\).  Finally, exact rational simplification gives
\[
G_{\mathrm{obs}}-E_{\mathrm{bridge}}
=\frac{4912}{39525}=\delta>0.
\tag{9}
\]
The pilot error is zero, so (9) proves that the positive observable gap strictly exceeds the exact contrast-level bridge correction plus the pilot error.  The argument concerns only the two displayed, algebraically certified tie representatives and uses neither a universal tie-breaking claim nor stability across an uncontrolled rank change. \(\square\)
