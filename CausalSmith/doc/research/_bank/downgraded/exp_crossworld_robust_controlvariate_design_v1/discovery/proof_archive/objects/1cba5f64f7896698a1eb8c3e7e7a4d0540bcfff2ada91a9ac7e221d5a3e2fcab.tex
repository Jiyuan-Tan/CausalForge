\textbf{Proof.}
Compactness of \(\mathcal K\) and continuity of the two endpoint criteria give the displayed extrema and nonempty optimizer sets.  The strict nominal gap in (G1) says that no point of \(\mathcal Z\setminus\mathcal K\) can attain \(a^\star\), while the strict robust gap says that no such point can attain \(v\).  Therefore
\[
\mathcal A_{0,Z}=A_0,
\qquad
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm},Z}=A_{\mathrm{mm}}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is containment derived from the exact inside/outside CAD signs.

We next derive, rather than assume, the two local estimates.  Fix a certified stratum \(V_j\).  The tubular chart writes every sufficiently close feasible point uniquely as
\[
B=\Psi_j(s,t,z),
\tag{2}
\]
where \(s\) indexes the foot point \(C(s)=\Psi_j(s,0,0)\in V_j\), \(t\) is feasible tangent and orthogonal to \(T_{C(s)}V_j\), and \(z\) is an inward active-normal coordinate.  The certified lower singular-value and derivative bounds imply, after shrinking a common algebraic chart radius over the finite cover, constants \(c_-,c_+>0\) such that
\[
c_-\lVert(t,z)\rVert_2
\le d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,A_0)
\le c_+\lVert(t,z)\rVert_2.
\tag{3}
\]
This follows from the mean-value formula applied to \(\Psi_j\): the lower singular-value bound controls its linear transverse part, while the certified quadratic remainder is absorbed by taking the radius small enough.  Thus (3) is a consequence of the tubular-coordinate primitives, not an input inequality.

Because \(a\) is constant on \(V_j\), its first and second derivatives along changes of the foot-point coordinate \(s\) vanish after restriction to the stratum.  At the realized foot point \(C(s)\), nominal KKT stationarity cancels the remaining free first-order tangent term.  Strict complementarity gives a positive loss \(c_z\lVert z\rVert_1\) in every nonzero feasible active-normal displacement.  The certified reduced-Hessian eigenvalue gives a loss \(c_t\lVert t\rVert_2^2\) only in directions transverse to \(V_j\).  Interval bounds on cross terms and the second-order remainder give, after one more finite shrinkage,
\[
a^\star-a(B)
\ge \frac{c_t}{2}\lVert t\rVert_2^2
+\frac{c_z}{2}\lVert z\rVert_1.
\tag{4}
\]
On the radius-\(\rho\) tube, \(\lVert z\rVert_1\ge\rho^{-1}\lVert z\rVert_2^2\) after decreasing the positive constant if necessary.  Equations (3)--(4), and finiteness of the chart cover, therefore yield a single \(\mu>0\) such that
\[
a^\star-a(B)\ge\mu d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,A_0)^2.
\tag{5}
\]
No curvature along an optimizer stratum has been used.

Now suppose the foot point \(C=C(s)\) belongs to \(N_q\).  Define
\[
\phi_C(B)=\theta_L(C)\ell_B(c_L)+\theta_U(C)\ell_B(c_U).
\]
The support condition on the weights gives \(\phi_C(C)=w(C)\ge q\), and convexity of the maximum gives \(w(B)\ge\phi_C(B)\).  The certified weighted KKT sign makes the first feasible directional derivative of \(\phi_C\) nonnegative.  The endpoint Hessian and remainder bounds therefore yield a finite \(M_C\) with
\[
w(B)\ge\phi_C(B)
\ge w(C)-M_C\lVert(t,z)\rVert_2^2
\ge q-M_C\lVert(t,z)\rVert_2^2.
\tag{6}
\]
If instead \(C\in A_0\setminus N_q\), the certified positive value gap \(w(C)-q>0\), uniform on that compact remainder, and continuity with its interval modulus imply \(w(B)>q\) on a sufficiently small neighborhood, so the positive part \([q-w(B)]_+\) is zero there.  Combining this observation, (3), (6), and the finite cover produces one \(M<\infty\) such that
\[
[q-w(B)]_+\le M d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,A_0)^2
\tag{7}
\]
throughout the same radius-\(\rho\) tube.  Equations (5) and (7) prove (G4) entirely from the transverse KKT, Hessian, tubular, and remainder records.

On the local tube, division by the positive constant \(\mu\) gives
\[
[q-w(B)]_+\le(M/\mu)\{a^\star-a(B)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
On its compact complement, the exact CAD bounds (G2), with \(\gamma>0\), give
\[
[q-w(B)]_+\le(R/\gamma)\{a^\star-a(B)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Taking \(L=\max\{M/\mu,R/\gamma\}\) proves (G6).  The algebraic witness in (G3) gives
\[
v\le w(B_R)\le q-\zeta_\Delta,
\tag{10}
\]
so \(q-v\ge\zeta_\Delta>0\).  Together with (1), this proves (G5) and the claimed positive gap without assuming an optimizer ordering.

For an empirical nominal maximizer, two applications of the uniform gain error give
\[
a^\star-a(\widehat B_0)\le2e.
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (11) with (G6) yields \(w(\widehat B_0)\ge q-2Le\).  Similarly, comparison with a population minimizer gives
\[
w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})\le v+2d.
\tag{12}
\]
By the definitions and displayed bridge hypotheses,
\[
V_0\ge w(\widehat B_0)-R_0,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{mm}}\le w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})+R_{\mathrm{mm}}.
\tag{13}
\]
Subtracting (12) from the loss consequence of (11) and applying (13) proves (G7).  The rank-adaptive theorem supplies such errors only when its almost-sure selected-stratum certificate is present, exactly as stated. \(\square\)
