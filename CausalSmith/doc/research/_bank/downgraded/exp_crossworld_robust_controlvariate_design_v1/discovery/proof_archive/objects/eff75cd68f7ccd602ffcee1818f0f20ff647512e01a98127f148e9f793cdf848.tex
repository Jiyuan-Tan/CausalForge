\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(m=m_1\wedge m_0\), and let \(W_1\) denote the one-dimensional Wasserstein distance.  For probability laws \(R,S\) on \([0,1]\), the layer-cake identity followed by Fubini's theorem gives
\[
W_1(R,S)
=\int_0^1\lvert R^{-1}(u)-S^{-1}(u)\rvert\,du
=\int_0^1\lvert R(y)-S(y)\rvert\,dy.
\tag{1}
\]
For each arm \(a\), Jensen's inequality and the binomial variance formula give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_PW_1(\widehat F_a,F_a)
&=\int_0^1\mathbb E_P\lvert\widehat F_a(y)-F_a(y)\rvert\,dy\\
&\le\int_0^1\sqrt{\frac{F_a(y)\{1-F_a(y)\}}{m_a}}\,dy
\le\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m_a}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Because \(y\mapsto y\) and \(y\mapsto y^2\) are respectively one- and two-Lipschitz on \([0,1]\), equation (1) implies
\[
\lvert\widehat\mu_a-\mu_a\rvert\le W_1(\widehat F_a,F_a),
\qquad
\lvert\widehat\sigma_a^2-\sigma_a^2\rvert
\le4W_1(\widehat F_a,F_a).
\tag{3}
\]
For either the comonotone or countermonotone quantile product, the elementary inequality \(\lvert xy-x'y'\rvert\le\lvert x-x'\rvert+\lvert y-y'\rvert\) on \([0,1]^4\), together with (1), yields
\[
\lvert\widehat c_e-c_e\rvert
\le2\{W_1(\widehat F_1,F_1)+W_1(\widehat F_0,F_0)\},
\qquad e\in\{L,U\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every block of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) is a linear combination of \(I_n\) and \(\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\), with coefficients bounded by the errors in (3)--(4) and the analogous product-of-means error.  Since \(\lVert I_n\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=1\) and \(\lVert\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=n\), equations (2)--(4) imply, for a numerical constant \(K_M<\infty\),
\[
\mathbb E_PM_n
\le K_M\frac{n}{\sqrt m}.
\tag{5}
\]
This proves \(M_n=O_{L^1}(n/\sqrt m)\).

We next bound the deterministic sensitivity without using assignment dependency neighborhoods.  For \(B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\), put \(Z_B=\Omega^{1/2}B\).  The regularity of this class makes \(Z_B^\top Z_B=B^\top\Omega B\) invertible, and
\[
P_B=Z_B(Z_B^\top Z_B)^{-1}Z_B^\top
\]
is the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{range}(Z_B)\).  Therefore \(0\preceq P_B\preceq I_{2n}\), and congruence by \(\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}\) gives
\[
0\preceq
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}P_B\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}
\preceq
\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}=A_{\mathrm{HT}}.
\tag{6}
\]
Both matrices in (6) are positive semidefinite, so their nuclear norms equal their traces.  Each coordinate of \(D\) is Bernoulli with mean \(\pi_{i,a}\), whence \(\Omega_{(i,a),(i,a)}=\pi_{i,a}(1-\pi_{i,a})\).  It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert A_B\rVert_*
&\le\operatorname{tr}(A_{\mathrm{HT}})
=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a\in\{1,0\}}
\frac{\pi_{i,a}(1-\pi_{i,a})}{\pi_{i,a}^2}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a\in\{1,0\}}
\frac{1-\pi_{i,a}}{\pi_{i,a}}
\le2\underline\pi^{-1}n^{1+\beta},
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
where the last inequality is ass:zhao-exposure-probability-rate.  The same bound applies to \(\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_*\).  Hence
\[
C_n\le4\underline\pi^{-1}n^{\beta-1}.
\tag{8}
\]

For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), the endpoint residual loss is \(n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{(A_{\mathrm{HT}}-A_B)Q(c_e)\}\), with the empirical analogue obtained by replacing \(Q(c_e)\) by \(\widehat Q_e\).  Trace duality and the inequality \(\lvert\max_e x_e-\max_e y_e\rvert\le\max_e\lvert x_e-y_e\rvert\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}}
\lvert\widehat w(B)-w(B)\rvert
&\le n^{-2}\sup_B
\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}-A_B\rVert_*M_n\\
&\le C_nM_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

It remains to compare the empirical zero-covariance matrix with an endpoint matrix.  Let \(\Delta_e=\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) and \(d_e=\widehat c_e-c_e\).  The affine identities \(Q(c)=Q(0)-cE\) and \(\widehat Q_e=\widehat Q(0)-\widehat c_eE\) give
\[
\widehat Q(0)-Q(0)=\Delta_e+d_eE.
\tag{10}
\]
Choose a unit vector \(z\perp\mathbf1\), which exists because \(n\ge3\).  The cross block of \(\Delta_e\), evaluated between \(z\) and itself, equals \(-d_e\); consequently
\[
\lvert d_e\rvert\le\lVert\Delta_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(\lVert E\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=1\), equations (10)--(11) prove
\[
\lVert\widehat Q(0)-Q(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2M_n.
\tag{12}
\]
Trace duality now gives
\[
\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}}
\lvert\widehat g_B(0)-g_B(0)\rvert
\le2n^{-2}\sup_B\lVert A_B\rVert_*M_n
\le2C_nM_n.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n\to0\), taking pilot expectations in (9) and (13) proves that both uniform errors are \(o(n^{-1})\).  Finally, (5) and (8) give
\[
nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n
\le4\underline\pi^{-1}K_M
\frac{n^{1+\beta}}{\sqrt{m_1\wedge m_0}},
\tag{14}
\]
so \(\sqrt{m_1\wedge m_0}/n^{1+\beta}\to\infty\) is sufficient.  The derivation used only pilot sampling, bounded support, nonemptiness and regularity of the basis class, and the exposure-probability rate; neither known exposure nor a dependency graph entered. \(\square\)
