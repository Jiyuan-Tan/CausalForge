\textbf{Proof.}
Work with the normalized representatives of the projective classes.  By prop:kappa-exhaustion and ass:kappa-feasibility, \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is a nonempty compact metrizable quotient.  Its two block columns have disjoint supports and squared norm \(n\).  Hence, for every normalized representative,
\[
 \lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 \lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le (n\kappa)^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The second inequality follows from \(\lambda_{\min}(S_B)\ge n\kappa>0\).  In particular, every inverse used below exists.  Assumption ass:exposure-positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined, while \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\) are fixed because the design is fixed.

We first establish convergence of every pilot quantity entering the objective.  Write \(q_a=F_a^{-1}\) and \(\widehat q_a=\widehat F_a^{-1}\).  We use the following elementary bounded strong-law argument.  If \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) are independent, identically distributed, centered, and satisfy \(\lvert X_j\rvert\le1\), then
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\right)^4
 =\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb EX_j^4
  +6\sum_{1\le i<j\le m}\mathbb EX_i^2\mathbb EX_j^2
 \le 3m^2.
 \tag{2}
\]
Markov's inequality at \(m=k^2\) makes the probabilities of
\(\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{k^2}X_j\rvert>\epsilon k^2\) summable in \(k\).  The union bound for tail unions therefore shows that only finitely many of these events occur.  Between \(k^2\) and \((k+1)^2\), boundedness changes a partial sum by at most \(2k+1\), which is \(o(k^2)\).  Thus \(m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\to0\) almost surely.

Apply this argument in each pilot arm to the outcome, its square, and the indicators \(\mathbf1\{Y^P_{j,a}\le x\}\) at every rational \(x\in[0,1]\).  A countable intersection gives a common probability-one event.  Monotonicity and rational squeezing then yield \(\widehat F_a(x)\to F_a(x)\) at every continuity point of \(F_a\).  A distribution function has at most countably many discontinuities, because for each positive integer \(k\) it has only finitely many jumps exceeding \(1/k\).  Dominated convergence consequently gives
\[
 \int_0^1\lvert\widehat F_a(y)-F_a(y)\rvert\,dy\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{3}
\]
The area identity between two distribution functions and their generalized inverses,
\[
 \int_0^1\lvert F(y)-G(y)\rvert\,dy
 =\int_0^1\lvert F^{-1}(u)-G^{-1}(u)\rvert\,du,
 \tag{4}
\]
follows by integrating the indicator of the region lying between the two graphs, first in \(y\) and then in \(u\).  Equations (3)--(4), together with the bounded strong law for the first two moments, give, almost surely,
\[
 \lVert\widehat q_a-q_a\rVert_{L^1(0,1)}\to0,
 \qquad
 \widehat\mu_a\to\mu_a,
 \qquad
 \widehat\sigma_a^2\to\sigma_a^2,
 \quad a\in\{1,0\}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Here ass:bounded-outcomes supplies the common bound, and ass:unpaired-pilot supplies the independent, identically distributed samples in each arm.  Pairing across arms is neither used nor available.

Since all quantiles take values in \([0,1]\),
\[
 \left\lvert
  \int_0^1\widehat q_1(u)\widehat q_0(u)\,du
  -\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du
 \right\rvert
 \le
 \lVert\widehat q_1-q_1\rVert_1+
 \lVert\widehat q_0-q_0\rVert_1.
 \tag{6}
\]
The same inequality holds with the second quantile evaluated at \(1-u\).  Combining (5)--(6) with the products of the empirical means yields
\[
 \widehat c_L\to c_L,
 \qquad
 \widehat c_U\to c_U
 \tag{7}
\]
almost surely.  Assumption ass:frechet-covariance-domain identifies \(c_L,c_U\) as the population covariance endpoints used by \(w\).

For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), set \(Q_e=Q(c_e)\), and let \(\widehat Q_e\) be the empirical endpoint matrix prescribed by def:pilot-plugin.  Since \(n\) is fixed, def:moment-matrix and (5)--(7) imply
\[
 \rho_m:=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 \lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely as }m_1\wedge m_0\to\infty.
 \tag{8}
\]

It remains to make this convergence uniform over the basis class.  Put
\[
 C_\nu=\lVert\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
 \qquad
 A_\nu=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
Both are finite fixed-design constants.  The trace inequality
\(\lvert\operatorname{tr}(M)\rvert\le d\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) for a \(d\)-dimensional square matrix, def:gain-loss, and (1) give, uniformly in \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_e\left\lvert
  \widehat g_B(\widehat c_e)-g_B(c_e)
 \right\rvert
 &\le
 \frac{2}{n^2}\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \lVert B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \rho_m
 \lVert\Pi^{-1}\Omega B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}} \\
 &\le \frac{2C_\nu^2}{n^2\kappa}\rho_m,
 \\
 \max_e\left\lvert
  \widehat h(\widehat c_e)-h(c_e)
 \right\rvert
 &\le \frac{2}{n}\lVert A_\nu\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\rho_m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, both rectangular factors in the first line have operator norm at most \(\sqrt n C_\nu\), the first trace is two-dimensional, and the second trace is \(2n\)-dimensional.  Because subtraction and the maximum of two coordinates are Lipschitz with constant one in the sup norm, (9) implies
\[
 \delta_m:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}
 \lvert\widehat w(B)-w(B)\rvert
 \le
 \left\{\frac{2}{n}\lVert A_\nu\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
       +\frac{2C_\nu^2}{n^2\kappa}\right\}\rho_m
 \longrightarrow0
 \tag{10}
\]
almost surely.  This proves the asserted uniform convergence.

All matrix operations in the two criteria are continuous on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\), because (1) keeps inversion uniformly away from singularity; invariance under the residual sign representatives makes these continuous functions on the projective quotient.  Compactness therefore gives nonempty population and empirical argmin sets.  The elementary minimum inequality applied to (10) yields
\[
 \left\lvert\widehat v_{\mathrm{mm}}-v_{\mathrm{mm}}\right\rvert
 \le\delta_m\longrightarrow0
 \tag{11}
\]
almost surely.

Finally, let \(B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\), where the pilot-size pair along the sequence satisfies \(m_1\wedge m_0\to\infty\), and suppose a subsequence satisfies \(B_{m_j}\to B_\infty\) in the quotient.  Choose any \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Empirical optimality and (10) imply
\[
 w(B_{m_j})
 \le \widehat w(B_{m_j})+\delta_{m_j}
 \le \widehat w(B^\star)+\delta_{m_j}
 \le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\delta_{m_j}.
 \tag{12}
\]
By continuity, (12), and the defining lower bound \(w\ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}\), one has \(w(B_\infty)=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Hence \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  This is exactly
\(\limsup\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\subseteq\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) in the projective quotient.  \(\square\)
