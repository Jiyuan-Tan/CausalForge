\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(m=m_1\wedge m_0\).  For probability laws \(R,S\) on \([0,1]\),
\[
W_1(R,S)=\int_0^1|R^{-1}(u)-S^{-1}(u)|\,du
=\int_0^1|R(y)-S(y)|\,dy.
\tag{1}
\]
Jensen's inequality and the binomial variance formula give
\[
\mathbb E_PW_1(\widehat F_a,F_a)
\le\int_0^1\sqrt{\frac{F_a(y)\{1-F_a(y)\}}{m_a}}\,dy
\le\frac1{2\sqrt{m_a}}.
\tag{2}
\]
First and second moments have the same order.  Since \(|xy-x'y'|\le|x-x'|+|y-y'|\) on \([0,1]^4\), both quantile-coupling products and both covariance endpoints have \(L^1\) error \(O(m^{-1/2})\).  Each block of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) is such a scalar times \(I_n\) or \(\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\), so
\[
M_n:=\max_e\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_{L^1}(n/\sqrt m).
\tag{3}
\]

Define
\[
A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1},
\qquad
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B(B^{\top}\Omega B)^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
For \(Z=\Omega^{1/2}B\), the middle matrix \(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top}\) is an orthogonal projector, so \(0\preceq A_B\preceq A_{\mathrm{HT}}\).  The dependency graph makes each row of \(\Omega\) have at most \(\overline d n^\alpha\) nonzero entries, and \(\lVert\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\underline\pi^{-1}n^\beta\).  Hence
\[
\lVert A_B\rVert_*\le\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_*
\le2\overline d\,\underline\pi^{-2}n^{1+\alpha+2\beta}.
\tag{4}
\]
This proves \(C_n=O(n^{\alpha+2\beta-1})\).  Trace duality and the one-Lipschitz property of a maximum prove
\[
\sup_B|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|\le C_nM_n.
\tag{5}
\]

For the zero-covariance comparison, write \(\Delta_e=\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\), \(d_e=\widehat c_e-c_e\), and \(E=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_n\\I_n&0\end{bmatrix}\).  Affinity gives
\[
\widehat Q(0)-Q(0)=\Delta_e+d_eE.
\tag{6}
\]
Compressing the off-diagonal block of \(\Delta_e\) to a unit vector orthogonal to \(\mathbf1\) gives eigenvalue \(-d_e\); since \(n\ge3\), \(|d_e|\le\lVert\Delta_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\).  Therefore \(\lVert\widehat Q(0)-Q(0)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2M_n\), and trace duality proves the displayed gain bounds.  Thus \(nC_nM_n\to0\) in \(L^1\) gives first-order uniform accuracy.  Combining (3) and (4) gives the stated sufficient pilot-growth condition. \(\square\)
