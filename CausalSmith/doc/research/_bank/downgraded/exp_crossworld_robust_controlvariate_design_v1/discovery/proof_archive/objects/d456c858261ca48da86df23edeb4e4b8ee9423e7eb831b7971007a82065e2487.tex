\textbf{Proof.}
Work on the probability-one event on which thm:plugin-correspondence holds, and let \(U\) be any neighborhood of \(B^\star\).  The assumed isolation by a positive loss gap says
\[
 \gamma_U:=inf_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\setminus U}w(B)-v_{\mathrm{mm}}>0.
 \tag{1}
\]
Let \(\delta_m=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|\), so thm:plugin-correspondence gives \(\delta_m\to0\).  If \(2\delta_m<\gamma_U\), every \(B\notin U\) satisfies
\[
 \widehat w(B)
 \ge w(B)-\delta_m
 \ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}+\gamma_U-\delta_m
 >v_{\mathrm{mm}}+\delta_m
 \ge\widehat w(B^\star).
 \tag{2}
\]
Consequently \(\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\subset U\) eventually.  Since \(U\) was arbitrary, every measurable selection \(\widehat B\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) converges almost surely to \(B^\star\).  The hypothesis that exactly one endpoint is active labels the limiting saddle unambiguously; once singleton isolation is assumed, it is not additionally needed for convergence of the basis coordinate.

We next construct the claimed failure of point convergence when the singleton condition is absent.  Let \(n=3\), take \(f_i(w)=w_i\), and let the coordinates of \(W\) be independent Bernoulli random variables with success probability \(1/2\).  The two exposure events are mutually exclusive, every exposure probability equals \(1/2\), and direct calculation gives
\[
 \Pi=\frac12 I_6,
 \qquad
 \Omega=\frac14
 \begin{bmatrix}
 I_3&-I_3\\
 -I_3&I_3
 \end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Let \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\), take \(H=\delta_{(0,0)}\), and use the corresponding two unpaired pilot sequences.  The outcomes are bounded and the pilot samples are i.i.d., unpaired, and independent of the later assignment.  Moreover,
\[
 \mu_1=\mu_0=\sigma_1^2=\sigma_0^2=c_L=c_U=0,
 \qquad
 Q(0)=0.
 \tag{4}
\]
Choose \(\kappa=1/8\).  With \(e_j\) denoting the coordinate vectors of \(\mathbb R^3\), define
\[
 B^{(2)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_2),
 \qquad
 B^{(3)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_3).
\]
For \(j\in\{2,3\}\), equation (3) and the orthogonality of the selected coordinates give
\[
 S_{B^{(j)}}=\frac34 I_2,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}\!\left(3^{-1}S_{B^{(j)}}\right)=\frac14>\kappa.
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus ass:kappa-feasibility holds, and the two displayed matrices determine distinct feasible projective points.  By (4), however, def:gain-loss gives \(h(0)=g_B(0)=\ell_B(0)=w(B)=0\) for every feasible \(B\).  Every pilot observation is identically zero, so the same equalities hold for the plug-in quantities at every pilot size.  Therefore
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}
 =\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}
 =\mathcal B_\kappa
 \quad\text{at every pilot size}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Along the diagonal sequence \(m_1=m_0=m\), select \(B^{(2)}\) for even \(m\) and \(B^{(3)}\) for odd \(m\).  This deterministic rule is a measurable plug-in selection and has two distinct subsequential limits.  At the same time, (6) makes the outer-set inclusion of thm:plugin-correspondence exact.  Hence generic outer consistency does not imply point convergence without the singleton condition. \(\square\)
