\textbf{Proof.}
Let
\[
K_\kappa=
\left\{(b_1,b_0):
\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=n,
\ \lambda_{\min}\!\left(n^{-1}B(b_1,b_0)^{\top}\Omega B(b_1,b_0)\right)\ge\kappa
\right\}.
\]
The product of the two radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres is compact.  The map
\[
(b_1,b_0)\longmapsto n^{-1}B(b_1,b_0)^{\top}\Omega B(b_1,b_0)
\]
is continuous, as is the smallest-eigenvalue map on symmetric matrices.  Hence \(K_\kappa\) is closed in a compact set and is compact, including when it is empty.  Independent changes of sign of \(b_1\) and \(b_0\) preserve every defining condition and represent the residual projective identifications after normalization.  The quotient map is continuous, so its image \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is compact.

If \([B]\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), then
\[
\lambda_{\min}(B^{\top}\Omega B)\ge n\kappa>0.
\]
Thus \(S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\) is positive definite, and def:zhao-basis-class gives \(\mathcal B_\kappa\subseteq\mathcal B_Z\).

Conversely, take \([B]\in\mathcal B_Z\), represented by the block-separable matrix \(B=B(b_1,b_0)\).  Positive definiteness of \(S_B\) forces \(b_1\ne0\) and \(b_0\ne0\).  Define
\[
d_a=\frac{\sqrt n}{\lVert b_a\rVert_2},
\qquad
T=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_0),
\qquad
\widetilde B=BT.
\]
Then \(\widetilde B\) is a normalized representative of the same projective class, and congruence gives
\[
S_{\widetilde B}=T^{\top}S_BT=TS_BT\succ0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\rho_B=\lambda_{\min}(n^{-1}S_{\widetilde B})>0.
\]
For example, with \(\kappa_B=\rho_B/2\), the representative \(\widetilde B\) belongs to \(\mathcal B_{\kappa_B}\).  Hence every element of \(\mathcal B_Z\) belongs to at least one \(\mathcal B_\kappa\), while the reverse inclusion was already proved, so
\[
\bigcup_{\kappa>0}\mathcal B_\kappa=\mathcal B_Z.
\]
The argument begins with \(S_B\succ0\) and uses a basis-dependent threshold.  It therefore exhausts precisely the nondegenerate ordinary-inverse projective repair in def:zhao-basis-class; it neither equates any fixed \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) with Zhao's unquotiented block-diagonal domain nor reaches singular or pseudoinverse bases. \(\square\)
