\textbf{Proof.}
Work on the probability-one event furnished by thm:plugin-correspondence.  If \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}=\{B^\star\}\) and a measurable empirical minimizer selection fails to converge to \(B^\star\), some subsequence remains outside a neighborhood of \(B^\star\).  Compactness supplies a convergent further subsequence, while the outer-limit theorem forces its limit to be \(B^\star\), a contradiction.

For the converse, take \(n=3\), \(f_i(w)=w_i\), independent Bernoulli \((1/2)\) assignment, \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\), and \(\kappa=1/8\).  Then
\[
 \Pi=\tfrac12I_6,qquad
 \Omega=\tfrac14\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad Q(0)=\widehat Q(0)=0.
\]
Thus \(w=\widehat w=0\) throughout \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  The two distinct projective classes
\[
B^{(2)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_2),qquad
B^{(3)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_3)
\]
are feasible because \(S_B=3I_2/4\).  Alternating between them according to the parity of the common pilot size is a measurable empirical minimizer selection with no point limit, although the outer-set conclusion holds identically. \(\square\)
