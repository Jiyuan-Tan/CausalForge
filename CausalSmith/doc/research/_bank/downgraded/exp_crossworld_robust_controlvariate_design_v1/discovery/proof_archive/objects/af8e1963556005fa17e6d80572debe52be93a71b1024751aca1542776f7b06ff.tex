\textbf{Proof.}
Fix normalized representatives of the projective classes in \(\mathcal B_\kappa\). By prop:kappa-exhaustion and ass:kappa-feasibility this is a nonempty compact space. The two columns of every representative have disjoint block supports and norm \(\sqrt n\), while \(\kappa\)-regularity gives
\[
\lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt n,
\qquad
\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}. \tag{1}
\]
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined, and all design matrices are fixed.

Let \(q_a=F_a^{-1}\) and \(\widehat q_a=\widehat F_a^{-1}\). We first verify the needed pilot convergence without using paired observations. If \(X_j\) are independent, centered, and bounded by one, then
\[
\mathbb E\left(\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\right)^4
=\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb EX_j^4
+6\sum_{j<k}\mathbb EX_j^2\mathbb EX_k^2
\le3m^2.
\]
Markov's inequality and summability of \(3/(m^2t^4)\) prove the strong law by the first Borel--Cantelli argument. Apply this to pilot indicators at a countable dense set of continuity points, and use monotonicity to obtain convergence of each empirical distribution function at every continuity point. On the bounded interval, a finite partition with continuity-point boundaries then shows
\[
\int_0^1|\widehat F_a(x)-F_a(x)|\,dx\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
The elementary one-dimensional area identity
\[
\int_0^1|\widehat q_a(u)-q_a(u)|\,du
=\int_0^1|\widehat F_a(x)-F_a(x)|\,dx \tag{2}
\]
follows by applying Fubini's theorem to the region lying between the two distribution graphs. Hence the left side of (2) converges to zero almost surely. The same bounded strong-law calculation applied to \(Y^P_{j,a}\) and \((Y^P_{j,a})^2\) gives convergence of the empirical means and variances.

Because all quantiles take values in \([0,1]\),
\[
\left|\int_0^1\widehat q_1(u)\widehat q_0(u)\,du-
\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du\right|
\le\lVert\widehat q_1-q_1\rVert_1+\lVert\widehat q_0-q_0\rVert_1,
\]
and the same bound holds with the second quantile evaluated at \(1-u\). Therefore
\[
\widehat c_L\to c_L,qquad \widehat c_U\to c_U \tag{3}
\]
almost surely. For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), let \(Q_e=Q(c_e)\) and let \(\widehat Q_e\) be its plug-in version. The finite-dimensional construction in def:moment-matrix and (3) imply
\[
d_m:=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{4}
\]

Put \(C_1=\lVert\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) and \(C_0=\lVert\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\). The trace bound \(|\operatorname{tr}(M)|\le d\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) for a \(d\)-dimensional square matrix, def:gain-loss, and (1) give constants independent of \(B\) such that
\[
\max_e|\widehat h_e-h_e|\le \frac{2C_0}{n}d_m,
\qquad
\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}\max_e|\widehat g_{B,e}-g_{B,e}|
\le\frac{2C_1^2}{n^2\kappa}d_m. \tag{5}
\]
Consequently the residuals converge uniformly at both endpoints. Since the maximum of two coordinates is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm,
\[
\delta_m:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{6}
\]

Matrix inversion is continuous on the uniformly positive-definite set in (1), and every remaining operation is finite-dimensional and continuous. Thus \(w\) and \(\widehat w\) are continuous and attain their minima on the compact quotient. The elementary minimum inequality and (6) give
\[
|\widehat v_{\mathrm{mm}}-v_{\mathrm{mm}}|
=\left|\min_B\widehat w(B)-\min_Bw(B)\right|
\le\delta_m\longrightarrow0. \tag{7}
\]

Take any \(B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and any convergent subsequence \(B_{m_j}\to B_\infty\), which exists by compactness. For a fixed \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), empirical optimality and (6) imply
\[
w(B_{m_j})
\le\widehat w(B_{m_j})+\delta_{m_j}
\le\widehat w(B^\star)+\delta_{m_j}
\le w(B^\star)+2\delta_{m_j}
=v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\delta_{m_j}.
\]
Continuity and feasibility yield \(w(B_\infty)=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\), so \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\). This is precisely the asserted outer-limit inclusion. \(\square\)
