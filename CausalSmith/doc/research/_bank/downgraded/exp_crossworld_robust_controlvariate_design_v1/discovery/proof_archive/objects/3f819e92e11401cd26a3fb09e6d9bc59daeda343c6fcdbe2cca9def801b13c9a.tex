The causal estimand remains the fixed finite-population exposure contrast \(\tau\).  Cross-world laws are used only for ex ante basis evaluation: for a supplied basis, \(w(B)\) is the largest endpoint ex ante residual conditional-design variance.  The independent pilot selects a basis before the main assignment, and the main estimator then uses only realized exposure indicators, observed outcomes, known exposure probabilities, and the ordinary inverse.  Thus the pilot and coupling expectations evaluate design performance; they do not replace assignment as the source of randomness in the causal experiment.
