\textbf{Proof.}
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  For
\(B\in\mathcal B_Z\), the ordinary inverse \(S_B^{-1}\) occurring in
def:gain-loss is well defined.  The product coupling and the imported
Fr\'echet covariance domain give
\[
0\in\mathcal C(F_1,F_0)=[c_L,c_U],
\tag{1}
\]
so \(Q(0)\) is a typed endpoint-moment functional.  Define
\[
E=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_n\\ I_n&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
By def:moment-matrix,
\[
Q(c)=Q(0)-cE.
\tag{3}
\]
Cyclicity and linearity of trace in def:gain-loss therefore yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_B(c)
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{A_BQ(c)\}\\
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{A_BQ(0)\}
-c n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_BE).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(g_B\) is affine on \([c_L,c_U]\).

It remains to verify directly that the uncontrolled term is affine.  Put
\(M=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\), and partition it into the two exposure
blocks.  For every unit \(i\), mutual exclusivity gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{i,0})
=\mathbb E_{\nu}(D_{i,1}D_{i,0})-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}
=-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}.
\tag{5}
\]
Because each exposure probability is positive, the \(i\)-th diagonal
entry of either off-diagonal block of \(M\) is therefore \(-1\).  Hence
\[
\operatorname{tr}(ME)=-2n.
\tag{6}
\]
Using (3), (6), and def:gain-loss,
\[
\begin{aligned}
h(c)-h(0)
&=-c n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(ME)\\
&=\frac{2c}{n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently \(h\) is affine, and
\(\ell_B(c)=h(c)-g_B(c)\) is affine by (4) and (7).

Assumption ass:frechet-covariance-domain states the equality of sets
\[
\left\{\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}:
H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\right\}=[c_L,c_U].
\tag{8}
\]
Every affine real-valued function on the compact interval in (8) attains
both its minimum and its maximum at an endpoint.  Since (8) is equality of
sets, each endpoint is attained by at least one coupling in
\(\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\).  Applying this fact to \(g_B\) and \(\ell_B\)
proves
\[
\inf_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}
g_B\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}\right)
=\min\{g_B(c_L),g_B(c_U)\}
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
\sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}
\ell_B\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}\right)
=\max\{\ell_B(c_L),\ell_B(c_U)\},
\tag{10}
\]
with both extrema attained.  Equation (1) also proves
\(c_L\le 0\le c_U\) and shows that \(g_B(0)\) is evaluated at an
ordinarily typed feasible covariance.

Finally, write the affine function in (4) as
\(g_B(c)=\alpha_B+\beta_Bc\).  If \(\beta_B=0\), then \(g_B\) is
constant, so its value at zero equals its infimum over (8).  Conversely,
suppose \(c_L<0<c_U\).  If \(\beta_B>0\), then
\(g_B(c_L)<g_B(0)\); if \(\beta_B<0\), then
\(g_B(c_U)<g_B(0)\).  Therefore \(g_B(0)\) equals the worst-coupling
gain if and only if the affine slope \(\beta_B\) is zero.  This argument
completes the proof. \(\square\)
