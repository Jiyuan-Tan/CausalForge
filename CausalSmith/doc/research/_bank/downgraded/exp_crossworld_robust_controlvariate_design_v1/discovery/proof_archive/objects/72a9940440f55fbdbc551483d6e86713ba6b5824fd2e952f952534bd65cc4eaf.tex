\textbf{Proof.}
By ass:product-coupling-zero, zero belongs to \(\mathcal C(F_1,F_0)\), so \(Q(0)\) is well defined.  Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  Fix \(B\in\mathcal B_Z\).  By def:zhao-basis-class, \(S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\succ0\), and hence \(S_B^{-1}\) exists.  Define
\[
E=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_n\\ I_n&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
The construction in def:moment-matrix gives
\[
Q(c)=Q(0)-cE.                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Using def:gain-loss, cyclicity of trace, and linearity of trace, equation (1) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_B(c)
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(c)\Pi^{-1}\Omega B
\right]\\
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{A_BQ(0)\}
-c\,n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_BE).
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(g_B\) is affine on \(\mathcal C(F_1,F_0)\).

It remains to verify directly that the uncontrolled term is affine.  Put
\[
M=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
For every \(i\), ass:mutually-exclusive-exposures gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{i,0})
=\mathbb E_{\nu}(D_{i,1}D_{i,0})-pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}
=-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}.                                            \tag{3}
\]
After left and right multiplication by \(\Pi^{-1}\), equation (3) says that the \(i\)-th diagonal entry of each off-diagonal \(n\)-by-\(n\) block of \(M\) is \(-1\).  Because \(E\) exchanges the two arm blocks,
\[
\operatorname{tr}(ME)=-2n.                                       \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (4), and def:gain-loss imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
h(c)-h(0)
&=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{M(Q(c)-Q(0))\}\\
&=-c\,n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(ME)
=\frac{2c}{n}.                                                     
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{5}
\]
Consequently, \(h\) is affine, and \(\ell_B=h-g_B\) is affine as well.  More explicitly, if
\[
g_B(c)=\alpha_B+\beta_Bc,
\]
then
\[
\ell_B(c)=h(0)-\alpha_B+\left(\frac{2}{n}-\beta_B\right)c.       \tag{6}
\]

Ass:frechet-covariance-domain states the set equality
\[
\left\{\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}:
H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\right\}=[c_L,c_U].                           \tag{7}
\]
Every affine function on the compact interval in (7) attains its minimum and maximum at an endpoint.  Because (7) is equality of sets rather than merely an inclusion, couplings attaining both \(c_L\) and \(c_U\) exist.  Applying this fact first to \(g_B\) and then to \(\ell_B\) proves
\[
\inf_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}
g_B\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}\right)
=\min\{g_B(c_L),g_B(c_U)\},                                      \tag{8}
\]
and
\[
\sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}
\ell_B\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}\right)
=\max\{\ell_B(c_L),\ell_B(c_U)\},                               \tag{9}
\]
with attainment in both displays.

The product coupling in ass:product-coupling-zero has covariance zero and belongs to \(\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\).  Combining that fact with (7) gives \(c_L\le0\le c_U\); hence \(g_B(0)\) is evaluated at an ordinarily feasible covariance.

Finally suppose \(c_L<0<c_U\).  If \(\beta_B=0\), equation (2) shows that \(g_B\) is constant, so \(g_B(0)\) equals the infimum in (8).  Conversely, if \(\beta_B>0\), then \(g_B(c_L)<g_B(0)\), while if \(\beta_B<0\), then \(g_B(c_U)<g_B(0)\).  Thus \(g_B(0)\) equals the worst-coupling gain if and only if the affine slope \(\beta_B\) is zero.  This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
