\textbf{Proof.}
By ass:exposure-positivity, every diagonal entry of \(\Pi\) is strictly positive, so \(\Pi^{-1}\) exists.  If \(B\in\mathcal B_Z\), def:zhao-basis-class gives \(S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\succ0\), so \(S_B^{-1}\) exists.  Define
\[
E=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_n\\ I_n&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
The construction in def:moment-matrix gives the exact identity
\[
Q(c)=Q(0)-cE.
\]
Therefore, by cyclicity and linearity of trace,
\[
g_B(c)
=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{A_BQ(c)\}
=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{A_BQ(0)\}
-c n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(A_BE),
\]
which is affine in \(c\).

It remains to check the uncontrolled term rather than importing its slope.  Put \(M=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\).  For each \(i\), ass:mutually-exclusive-exposures and the definition of \(\pi_{i,a}\) imply
\[
\Omega_{i,n+i}
=\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{i,0})
=\mathbb E_{\nu}(D_{i,1}D_{i,0})-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}
=-\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}.
\]
The divisions by the two exposure probabilities are licensed by ass:exposure-positivity, and symmetry of \(\Omega\) yields
\[
\operatorname{tr}(ME)
=2\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\Omega_{i,n+i}}{\pi_{i,1}\pi_{i,0}}
=-2n.
\]
Using def:gain-loss and \(Q(c)=Q(0)-cE\),
\[
h(c)=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\{MQ(c)\}
=h(0)-c n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(ME)
=h(0)+\frac{2c}{n}.
\]
Thus \(h\) is affine, and the identity \(\ell_B(c)=h(c)-g_B(c)\) in def:gain-loss proves that \(\ell_B\) is affine.  Ass:known-exposure and ass:iid-potential-pairs supply, respectively, the causal exposure interpretation and the ex ante second-moment interpretation of these matrix functionals; the displayed algebra establishes the asserted shape.

By ass:frechet-covariance-domain and def:coupling-class,
\[
\left\{\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}:H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\right\}
=[c_L,c_U].
\]
An affine real function on a compact interval attains its minimum and maximum at an endpoint.  Equality of the covariance image with the entire closed interval also says that each endpoint is attained by some coupling in \(\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\).  Consequently,
\[
\inf_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}
g_B\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}\right)
=\min\{g_B(c_L),g_B(c_U)\}
\]
and
\[
\sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}
\ell_B\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\}\right)
=\max\{\ell_B(c_L),\ell_B(c_U)\},
\]
with both extrema attained.

By ass:product-coupling-zero, \(F_1\otimes F_0\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)\) has covariance zero.  Ass:frechet-covariance-domain therefore gives \(0\in[c_L,c_U]\), equivalently \(c_L\le0\le c_U\), and \(g_B(0)\) is an ordinarily typed feasible-covariance gain.

Finally, write \(g_B(c)=\alpha_B+\beta_Bc\).  If \(\beta_B=0\), then \(g_B\) is constant and its value at zero equals its infimum over the coupling class.  Conversely, suppose \(c_L<0<c_U\).  If \(\beta_B>0\), then
\[
g_B(c_L)=g_B(0)+\beta_Bc_L<g_B(0),
\]
whereas if \(\beta_B<0\), then
\[
g_B(c_U)=g_B(0)+\beta_Bc_U<g_B(0).
\]
Thus \(g_B(0)\) equals the worst-coupling gain if and only if \(\beta_B=0\). \(\square\)
