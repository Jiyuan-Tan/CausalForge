\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a rational signed pivot-chart box and write the \(2n\) coordinates of \((b_1,b_0)\) as \(x\).  By \(\operatorname{def{:}kappa\mbox{-}basis\mbox{-}class}\), the normalization conditions are the polynomial equations
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n b_{1i}^2=n,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n b_{0i}^2=n.
\tag{1}
\]
Every box restriction and signed pivot restriction is a polynomial weak inequality with rational coefficients.  Since \(S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\), each entry of \(S_B\) is a quadratic polynomial in \(x\), with coefficients in the real-algebraic coefficient field supplied by \(\mathfrak I_{\mathrm{cert}}\).

Write
\[
S_B-n\kappa I_2=
\begin{bmatrix}u&v\\v&z\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{2}
\]
If the matrix in (2) is positive semidefinite, its principal minors give
\[
u\ge0,
\qquad z\ge0,
\qquad uz-v^2\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]
Conversely, suppose (3) holds.  If \(u>0\), then for every \((s,t)\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
us^2+2vst+zt^2
=u\left(s+\frac vu t\right)^2
+\left(z-\frac{v^2}{u}\right)t^2\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(u=0\), the determinant inequality in (3) forces \(v=0\), and the quadratic form is \(zt^2\ge0\).  Thus (3) is necessary and sufficient for the matrix inequality in (2).  In particular, the use of weak inequalities retains the equality boundary.

Intersection of the chart box with the normalized feasible set is therefore represented by one existential first-order formula
\[
\exists x\;\Phi(x),
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\Phi\) is a finite conjunction of the polynomial equalities (1), the chart and box inequalities, and the three weak polynomial inequalities (3).  By \(\operatorname{lem{:}real\mbox{-}closed\mbox{-}field\mbox{-}quantifier\mbox{-}elimination}\), cylindrical algebraic decomposition returns a quantifier-free formula equivalent to (5), and exact real-algebraic sign isolation decides that formula.

It remains to verify the asserted form of the output.  During cylindrical lifting, each projection polynomial specializes to a univariate polynomial with real-algebraic coefficients.  Exact root isolation partitions the line into finitely many open sectors and root sections.  Choosing a rational point in a sector, or the isolated real-algebraic root in a section, and proceeding recursively through the finitely many coordinates produces a real-algebraic sample in every sign-invariant cell.  If one such cell satisfies \(\Phi\), its sample is an exactly feasible point.  If none does, the finite sign-invariant cell list together with the elimination trace certifies infeasibility.  Root sections are retained because every constraint is tested with its stated weak inequality, so the conclusion also holds when feasibility occurs only on a sphere boundary or on \(\lambda_{\min}(n^{-1}S_B)=\kappa\). \(\square\)
