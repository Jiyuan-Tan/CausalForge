\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\mathcal K_n=\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\).  Assumption ass:coordinate-bounded-kappa-feasibility gives \(\mathcal K_n\ne\varnothing\).  Its normalized representatives form a closed subset of the product of two radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres: the coordinate envelope and eigenvalue constraint are closed.  Quotienting by the finite sign action therefore leaves a nonempty compact projective space.  On this uniformly positive-definite class, the empirical criteria \(\widehat w\) and \(\widehat g_B(0)\) are continuous in \(B\) and Borel measurable in the finite pilot observations.  Hence their optimizer sets are nonempty and compact for every pilot realization.

For completeness, fix the finite signed pivot-chart list and order it deterministically.  Select the first chart meeting the optimizer set and then minimize its Euclidean coordinates successively over the remaining compact optimizer section.  At each step the coordinate minimum is Borel measurable: for a compact metric section with Borel graph, the event that its minimum is below a rational number is the projection witnessed by a point in a fixed countable dense subset after an arbitrarily small continuous-objective tolerance.  Successive coordinate minimization leaves a singleton, so this construction is precisely the fixed lexicographic chart rule and is measurable.  It proves that the two selectors named by def:feasible-ht-pilot-selected-estimator exist and use no data outside the pilot; no fallback outside \(\mathcal K_n\) is introduced.

Define
\[
 M_n=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 \lVert\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
 \quad
 d_n=\sup_{B\in\mathcal K_n}|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|,
 \quad
 e_n=\sup_{B\in\mathcal K_n}|\widehat g_B(0)-g_B(0)|.
 \tag{1}
\]
Lemma lem:uniform-pilot-endpoint-matrix-rate gives the pathwise finite-sample inequalities
\[
 d_n\leq C_nM_n,
 \qquad
 e_n\leq2C_nM_n.
 \tag{2}
\]

Since \(w\) and \(g_B(0)\) are continuous on \(\mathcal K_n\), their minimum and maximum are attained.  Choose \(B_n^\star\in\mathcal K_n\) with \(w(B_n^\star)=v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}\).  Empirical optimality of \(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}}\) gives, for every pilot realization,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})
 &\leq\widehat w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})+d_n\\
 &\leq\widehat w(B_n^\star)+d_n\\
 &\leq v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}+2d_n.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3}
\]
The reverse inequality \(w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})\geq v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}\) follows from the definition of the population minimum.  Taking pilot expectations in (3) and using (2) proves
\[
 0\leq
 \mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})-v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}
 \leq2C_n\mathbb E_PM_n.
 \tag{4}
\]

Let \(R_n\) be the deterministic envelope constructed in lem:uniform-zhao-ht-oracle-variance-equivalence, so
\[
 0\leq R_n\leq K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}=o(n^{-1}).
 \tag{5}
\]
For \(s\in\{\mathrm{mm},0\}\), define
\[
 V_s=
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}
 \mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu
 (\widehat\tau_{\widehat B_s}^{\mathrm{CV}}\mid\widehat B_s,Y)
 \right].
 \tag{6}
\]
Conditional on the pilot, \(\widehat B_s\) is fixed.  Assumption ass:pilot-main-outcome-independence makes this selected basis independent of both the main assignment and the main potential-outcome array.  The uniform finite ex ante envelope in lem:uniform-zhao-ht-oracle-variance-equivalence therefore applies at \(B=\widehat B_s\).  Assumption ass:frechet-covariance-domain and thm:fixed-basis-endpoints give
\[
 \sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}\ell_B(c_H)=w(B).
 \tag{7}
\]
The elementary inequality \(|\sup_Hf_H-\sup_Hg_H|\leq\sup_H|f_H-g_H|\), followed by integration over the pilot, now yields
\[
 |V_s-\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_s)|\leq R_n,
 \qquad s\in\{\mathrm{mm},0\}.
 \tag{8}
\]

The exact sensitivity condition says \(nC_nM_n\to0\) in \(L^1(P)\), hence
\[
 nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{9}
\]
Combining (4) and (9) proves
\[
 \mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})
 =v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}+o(n^{-1}).
 \tag{10}
\]
Equations (5), (8), and (10) give the displayed feasible Horvitz--Thompson coefficient bridge in the theorem for both selectors.

It remains to quantify the zero-covariance selector.  Choose \(B_n^0\in\mathcal A_{0,\overline a}\), so \(g_{B_n^0}(0)=g_n^\star\).  Empirical optimality of \(\widehat B_0\) and (1) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 g_n^\star-g_{\widehat B_0}(0)
 &\leq
 |g_{B_n^0}(0)-\widehat g_{B_n^0}(0)|
 +|\widehat g_{\widehat B_0}(0)-g_{\widehat B_0}(0)|\\
 &\leq2e_n.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
Under the displayed deterministic linear error bound in the theorem,
\[
 [q_n-w(\widehat B_0)]_+
 \leq L_n\{g_n^\star-g_{\widehat B_0}(0)\}
 \leq2L_ne_n.
 \tag{12}
\]
Because \(x\geq q-[q-x]_+\), equations (2) and (12) give
\[
 q_n-\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_0)
 \leq4L_nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n.
 \tag{13}
\]
If \(\sup_nL_n<\infty\), equation (9) makes the right side of (13) \(o(n^{-1})\), which is precisely the theorem's zero-surrogate conclusion.  Without the deterministic linear error bound, (11) controls nominal-gain regret but supplies no \(n^{-1}\)-scale control of the worst-loss deficit, so outer argmax consistency alone cannot replace (12).  No primitive hypothesis here establishes the displayed global linear error bound or a uniformly bounded sequence \(L_n\); the zero-surrogate first-order conclusion is conditional on that separate geometric certificate.  This completes the proof. \(\square\)
