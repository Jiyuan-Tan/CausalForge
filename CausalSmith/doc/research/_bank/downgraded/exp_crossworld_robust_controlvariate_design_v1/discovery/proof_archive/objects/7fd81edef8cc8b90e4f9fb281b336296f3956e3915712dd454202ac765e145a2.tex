\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\mathcal K_n=\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\).  Coordinate-bounded feasibility makes \(\mathcal K_n\) nonempty.  Normalization and the coordinate envelope make it compact, while \(S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2\) makes inversion and all population and empirical criteria continuous.  Hence both optimizer sets are nonempty.  The fixed finite-chart lexicographic rule is pilot measurable: the empirical endpoints are Borel functions of finitely many order statistics, the objective is jointly Borel in the pilot and continuous in \(B\), and successive lexicographic coordinate minima are limits of infima over compact near-optimizer sets.  Thus both selectors exist and are measurable.

Let
\[
d_n^{\mathrm{pl}}=\sup_{B\in\mathcal K_n}|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|,
\qquad
e_n=\sup_{B\in\mathcal K_n}|\widehat g_B(0)-g_B(0)|.
\]
The uniform pilot lemma gives pathwise
\[
d_n^{\mathrm{pl}}\le C_nM_n,
\qquad e_n\le2C_nM_n.
\tag{1}
\]
Choose \(B_n^\star\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{\mathcal K_n}w\).  Empirical optimality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})
&\le\widehat w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})+d_n^{\mathrm{pl}}\\
&\le\widehat w(B_n^\star)+d_n^{\mathrm{pl}}\\
&\le v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}+2d_n^{\mathrm{pl}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The population minimum gives the reverse inequality.  Taking pilot expectations proves
\[
0\le\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})-v_{\mathrm{mm},\overline a}
\le2C_n\mathbb E_PM_n.
\tag{3}
\]

Conditional on the independent pilot, \(\widehat B_s\) is fixed in \(\mathcal K_n\).  The uniform observable-to-oracle lemma applies, and the sharp covariance-domain equality together with \(\texttt{thm:fixed-basis-endpoints}\) gives
\[
\sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}\ell_B(c_H)=w(B).
\tag{4}
\]
Using \(|\sup_Hu_H-\sup_Hv_H|\le\sup_H|u_H-v_H|\) and integrating over the pilot yields
\[
|V_s-\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_s)|\le R_n,
\qquad s\in\{\mathrm{mm},0\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The exact premise \(nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n\to0\) makes the right side of (3) \(o(n^{-1})\), while the first-order design-rate assumption gives \(nR_n\to0\).  Equations (3) and (5) prove the two first-order conclusions.

For the nominal selector, choose \(B_n^0\in\mathcal A_{0,\overline a}\).  Inserting and subtracting empirical gains and using empirical optimality gives
\[
g_n^\star-g_{\widehat B_0}(0)\le2e_n\le4C_nM_n.
\tag{6}
\]
The displayed deterministic geometric inequality then implies
\[
[q_n-w(\widehat B_0)]_+\le4L_nC_nM_n.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(x\ge q-[q-x]_+\), taking expectations gives
\[
q_n-\mathbb E_Pw(\widehat B_0)\le4L_nC_n\mathbb E_PM_n.
\tag{8}
\]
Boundedness of \(L_n\) and the exact sensitivity premise make (8) \(o(n^{-1})\).  The proof uses the global linear error bound only for this nominal first-order statement and does not infer it from outer argmax consistency. \(\square\)
