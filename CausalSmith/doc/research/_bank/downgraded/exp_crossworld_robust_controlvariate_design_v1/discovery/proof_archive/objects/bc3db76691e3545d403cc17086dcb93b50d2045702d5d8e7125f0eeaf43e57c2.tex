\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a realized pilot sample.  The empirical endpoint matrix \(\widehat Q_e\), for \(e\in\{L,U\}\), is the second-moment matrix of the signed vector formed from independent unit copies of an endpoint coupling of the two empirical marginals.  Hence
\[
\widehat Q_e\succeq0.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(Z_B=\Omega^{1/2}B\), let
\[
P_B=Z_B(Z_B^{\top}Z_B)^+Z_B^{\top}.
\]
The projector calculation in \(\texttt{lem:rank-stratified-projector-reduction}\), with \(Q(c_e)\) replaced by \(\widehat Q_e\), gives
\[
\widehat g_B^+(e)
=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(
P_B\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}\widehat Q_e
\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}
\right).
\tag{2}
\]
Only positive semidefiniteness of the endpoint matrix is used in the approximation part of that lemma.  Therefore, for every \([B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+\), there is a sequence \(([B_j]_+)\) of rank \(r_\Omega\) and a projector \(P_\infty\) such that
\[
P_{B_j}\longrightarrow P_\infty,
\qquad P_\infty-P_B\succeq0.
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (1)--(3) imply, simultaneously for \(e=L,U\),
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\widehat g_{B_j}^+(e)
\ge \widehat g_B^+(e),
\qquad
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\widehat w_m^+(B_j)
\le \widehat w_m^+(B).
\tag{4}
\]

We next pass from maximal-rank classes to rational-support maximal-rank classes.  Normalize every nonzero arm column to squared norm \(n\).  Rational vectors are dense in \(\mathbb R^n\), and normalization is continuous away from zero.  If \(r_\Omega=2\), positivity of \(\det(B^{\top}\Omega B)\) is an open condition.  If \(r_\Omega=1\), no block class can have rank two by the definition of \(r_\Omega\), while \(\operatorname{tr}(B^{\top}\Omega B)>0\) is open at every rank-one class.  If \(r_\Omega=0\), the zero class itself is rational-support.  Thus every maximal-rank class is the limit of normalized rational-support classes of the same rank.  On a fixed positive-rank stratum the map
\[
B\longmapsto P_B
\]
is continuous: at rank two this follows from the ordinary inverse formula, and at maximal rank one it follows from the spectral formula with the unique positive eigenvalue bounded away from zero locally.  At rank zero the projector is identically zero.  By (2), \(\widehat w_m^+\) is consequently continuous along these rational approximations.

For each \(j\), choose from the fixed enumeration a class \(C_{k(j)}\) satisfying
\[
\widehat w_m^+(C_{k(j)})
\le \widehat w_m^+(B_j)+j^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining (4) and (5), and then taking the infimum over \([B]_+\), yields
\[
\inf_{k\ge1}\widehat w_m^+(C_k)
\le \inf_{[B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+}\widehat w_m^+(B).
\tag{6}
\]
The reverse inequality is immediate because every \(C_k\) belongs to \(\mathcal B_Z^+\).  This proves equality of the two infima.  It also proves that the enumeration is nonempty: for positive \(r_\Omega\) the preceding rational approximation produces a maximal-rank rational-support class, and for \(r_\Omega=0\) the zero class is present.

It remains to verify the selection claim.  For fixed \(k\), the empirical marginal moments are Borel functions of the pilot sample.  The empirical quantile integrals are finite sums of products of order statistics over the finitely many common subintervals of the two empirical quantile grids, so they too are Borel functions.  Hence
\[
X_k:=\widehat w_m^+(C_k)
\]
is pilot-measurable.  The countable infimum \(\rho_m=\inf_{j\ge1}X_j\) is pilot-measurable.  Because \(\varepsilon_m>0\) is finite, the definition of an infimum ensures that at least one \(k\) satisfies \(X_k\le\rho_m+\varepsilon_m\).  Moreover,
\[
\{\widehat k_m=k\}
=\{X_k\le\rho_m+\varepsilon_m\}
\cap\bigcap_{j<k}\{X_j>\rho_m+\varepsilon_m\},
\tag{7}
\]
so \(\widehat k_m\) is finite and pilot-measurable.  Equality of the infima gives
\[
\widehat w_m^+(C_{\widehat k_m})
\le
\inf_{[B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+}\widehat w_m^+(B)
+\varepsilon_m.
\tag{8}
\]
Every enumerated class has rank \(r_\Omega\), so the selected class has that rank and is rank two exactly when rank two is achievable.  Construction (7) is purely countable and set-theoretic; none of the asserted measurability or value inequalities supplies a finite stopping rule, a zero-tolerance selector, or agreement with the separately defined exact-input cylindrical-algebraic selector. \(\square\)
