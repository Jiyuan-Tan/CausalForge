\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\xi_j=(\nu_j,F_{1j},F_{0j})\to\xi_0\).  Total-variation convergence on the finite assignment space gives
\[
\Pi_j\to\Pi_0,
\qquad
\Omega_j\to\Omega_0.
\tag{1}
\]
Exposure positivity at \(\xi_0\) makes \(\Pi_j^{-1}\to\Pi_0^{-1}\) after discarding finitely many indices.  Wasserstein convergence on \([0,1]\) gives convergence of the first and second marginal moments.  Applying lem:restricted-marginal-extrema-continuity to the continuous product map \((y_1,y_0)\mapsto y_1y_0\) gives
\[
c_{Lj}\to c_{L0},
\qquad
c_{Uj}\to c_{U0}.
\tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) and def:moment-matrix imply convergence of both endpoint matrices.

Work with the normalized representatives on the product of the two radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres, modulo the finite residual sign action.  This ambient quotient is compact.  The function
\[
\rho(\xi,B)=\lambda_{\min}\{n^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega(\xi)B\}
\tag{3}
\]
is jointly continuous, and \(\mathcal B_\kappa(\xi)=\{B:\rho(\xi,B)\ge\kappa\}\).  On this closed region, \(S_B^{-1}\) has norm at most \((n\kappa)^{-1}\).  Hence (1)--(2), def:gain-loss, and the maximum over the two endpoints show that
\[
(\xi,B)\longmapsto g_{B,\xi}(0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(\xi,B)\longmapsto w_\xi(B)
\tag{4}
\]
are jointly continuous wherever \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa(\xi)\).

Put \(v_j=\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B)\).  Choose \(B_*\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0)\).  The assumed slack gives \(\rho(\xi_0,B_*)\ge\kappa+\eta\); by (3), \(B_*\) is feasible at \(\xi_j\) for all large \(j\).  Therefore
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}v_j
\le\lim_{j\to\infty}w_{\xi_j}(B_*)
=v_0.
\tag{5}
\]

Next define
\[
q_j=\min_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B).
\]
Compactness makes the minimum well defined.  For a subsequence realizing \(\liminf_jq_j\), choose \(B_j\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)\) with \(w_{\xi_j}(B_j)=q_j\).  Compactness gives a further subsequence, not relabeled, with \(B_j\to\bar B\).  Closedness of the feasibility inequalities gives \(\bar B\in\mathcal B_\kappa(\xi_0)\).  Choose any \(C_*\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)\).  Its assumed slack makes it feasible at \(\xi_j\) eventually.  Since \(B_j\) maximizes the nominal gain,
\[
g_{B_j,\xi_j}(0)\ge g_{C_*,\xi_j}(0).
\]
Passing to the limit by (4) gives
\[
g_{\bar B,\xi_0}(0)\ge g_{C_*,\xi_0}(0)
=\max_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa(\xi_0)}g_{B,\xi_0}(0),
\]
so \(\bar B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)\).  A final use of (4) yields
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}q_j
=w_{\xi_0}(\bar B)
\ge q_0.
\tag{6}
\]
Since def:dominance-gap gives \(\Delta(\xi_j)=q_j-v_j\), (5)--(6) imply
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\Delta(\xi_j)
\ge\liminf_jq_j-\limsup_jv_j
\ge q_0-v_0
=\Delta(\xi_0).
\]
This is the claimed lower semicontinuity.  \(\square\)
