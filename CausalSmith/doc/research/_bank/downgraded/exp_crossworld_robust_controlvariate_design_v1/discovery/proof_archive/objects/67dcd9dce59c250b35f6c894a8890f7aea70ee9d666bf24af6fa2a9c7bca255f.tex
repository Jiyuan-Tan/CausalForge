\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\mathbf1=\mathbf1_3\).  Enumeration of the eight assignments gives the following exposure events.  The all-zero and all-one assignments expose no unit to either displayed label.  If only unit \(i\) is treated, then \(D_{i,1}=1\) and \(D_{j,0}=1\) for the other two units.  If exactly \(i,j\) are treated, then \(D_{k,0}=1\).  Hence
\[
 \pi_{i,1}=\frac4{27},\qquad \pi_{i,0}=2\frac4{27}+\frac2{27}=\frac{10}{27}.
\]
Direct intersection probabilities give, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&i=j&i\ne j\\ \hline
\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(D_{i,1},D_{j,1})&92/729&-16/729\\
\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(D_{i,1},D_{j,0})&-40/729&68/729\\
\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(D_{i,0},D_{j,0})&170/729&8/729.
\end{array}
\]
For example, \(D_{i,1}D_{j,0}=1\) at the singleton assignment treating \(i\), whose probability is \(108/729\); subtracting \((4/27)(10/27)=40/729\) gives \(68/729\).  These entries prove the stated \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\), as well as positivity and mutual exclusivity.

For Bernoulli \((1/2)\) marginals, \(\mu_a=1/2\), \(\sigma_a^2=1/4\), and the countermonotone and comonotone quantile products give
\[
 c_L=-\frac14,\qquad c_U=\frac14.
\]
Substitution in def:moment-matrix gives the displayed endpoint matrices.

For a normalized \(B(x,y)\), put
\[
 s=\mathbf1^{\top}x,\qquad t=\mathbf1^{\top}y,\qquad q=x^{\top}y.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(0\le s^2,t^2\le9\).  Block multiplication gives
\[
 S_B=\begin{bmatrix}
 \frac49-\frac{16s^2}{729}&-\frac{4q}{27}+\frac{68st}{729}\\
 -\frac{4q}{27}+\frac{68st}{729}&\frac23+\frac{8t^2}{729}
 \end{bmatrix},
\]
and, with
\[
 d=729+12t^2-36s^2-54q^2+68qst-22s^2t^2,
\]
one has \(\det(S_B)=8d/19683\).  Membership in \(\mathcal B_{1/8}\) implies \(d>0\).  Direct multiplication in def:gain-loss yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_B(c_L)&=\frac{76545+9891s^2+30732t^2-5832q^2+18232qst-10460s^2t^2}{1200d},\\
g_B(c_U)&=\frac{3(66825-5181s^2-2652t^2-6048q^2+5968qst-1260s^2t^2)}{400d},
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 h(c_L)=\frac{313}{600},\qquad h(c_U)=\frac{171}{200}. \tag{2}
\]

Since zero is the endpoint midpoint, \(g_B(0)=\{g_B(c_L)+g_B(c_U)\}/2\), and
\[
 \frac{31}{80}-g_B(0)=\frac{N_0}{1200d},
\]
where
\[
 N_0=5022\left(q-\frac{1088}{2511}st\right)^2
 +1629s^2+2148t^2-\frac{685118}{2511}s^2t^2.
\]
The last three terms are bilinear in \((s^2,t^2)\in[0,9]^2\); their four vertex values are
\[
 0,\quad14661,\quad19332,\quad\frac{368665}{31}.
\]
Bilinear interpolation and the square prove nonnegativity, with equality exactly at \(s=t=q=0\).  Such pairs exist, and then \(S_B=\operatorname{diag}(4/9,2/3)\), so \(\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=4/27>1/8\).  This proves the full set \(\mathcal A_0\), and (1)--(2) give \(w(B_0)=521/1200\).

For minimax loss set \(\lambda=1407/2635\) and \(v=2613/8432\).  Since a maximum dominates a convex combination,
\[
 w(B)-v\ge\lambda\ell_B(c_L)+(1-\lambda)\ell_B(c_U)-v
 =\frac{3N_{\mathrm{mm}}}{210800d},
\]
where
\[
 N_{\mathrm{mm}}
 =274734\left(q-\frac{70411}{137367}st\right)^2
 +s^2\left(34527-\frac{439017980}{137367}t^2\right).
\]
Because \(t^2\le9\), the final coefficient is at least \(87967621/15263>0\).  Hence \(w(B)\ge v\).  Equality forces \(s=q=0\) and equality of the two endpoint losses.  At \(s=q=0\),
\[
 \ell_B(c_L)-\ell_B(c_U)
 =-\frac{3(55t^2-216)}{10(4t^2+243)},
\]
so equality holds exactly when \(t^2=216/55\).  These bases exist by choosing normalized \(y\) with that squared sum and normalized \(x\perp\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,y\}\).  Their \(S_B\) is diagonal with first entry \(4/9\), so their normalized smallest eigenvalue again equals \(4/27\).  This proves the asserted \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and value.

Finally, positivity and the exhibited feasible bases put \(\xi_0\) in \(\mathfrak X_\kappa\).  The equality sets show
\[
 \Delta(\xi_0)=\frac{521}{1200}-\frac{2613}{8432}
 =\frac{4912}{39525},
 \qquad
 \frac4{27}-\frac18=\frac5{216},
\]
uniformly over both optimizer correspondences. \(\square\)
