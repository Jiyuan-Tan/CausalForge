\textbf{Proof.}
Work on the probability-one event supplied by thm:plugin-correspondence.  By prop:kappa-exhaustion, \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is compact, and the proof of thm:plugin-correspondence establishes continuity of \(w\) on this quotient.  Let \(U\) be any neighborhood of the unique minimizer \(B^\star\).  The compact set \(\mathcal B_\kappa\setminus U\) does not contain a minimizer.  Therefore the extreme-value theorem and uniqueness give the derived, rather than assumed, gap
\[
 \gamma_U:=
 \min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\setminus U}w(B)-v_{\mathrm{mm}}>0.
 \tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
 \delta_m=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}
 \lvert\widehat w(B)-w(B)\rvert.
\]
By thm:plugin-correspondence, \(\delta_m\longrightarrow0\) almost surely.  Whenever \(2\delta_m<\gamma_U\), every \(B\notin U\) obeys
\[
 \widehat w(B)
 \ge w(B)-\delta_m
 \ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}+\gamma_U-\delta_m
 >v_{\mathrm{mm}}+\delta_m
 \ge\widehat w(B^\star).
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\subset U\) eventually.  Since \(U\) was arbitrary, every measurable selection \(\widehat B\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) converges almost surely to \(B^\star\).  Neither a separately assumed isolation condition nor uniqueness of the active covariance endpoint is used.

It remains to show that outer consistency does not imply point convergence under ties.  Take \(n=3\), let \(f_i(w)=w_i\), and let the coordinates of \(W\) be independent Bernoulli \(1/2\) variables.  Then
\[
 \Pi=\frac12I_6,
 \qquad
 \Omega=\frac14
 \begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Let \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\), let \(H=\delta_{(0,0)}\), and use the corresponding two unpaired pilot sequences.  All outcomes are bounded, and ass:unpaired-pilot holds.  Moreover
\[
 \mu_1=\mu_0=\sigma_1^2=\sigma_0^2=c_L=c_U=0,
 \qquad Q(0)=0.
 \tag{4}
\]
Choose \(\kappa=1/8\).  With \(e_j\) the coordinate vectors of \(\mathbb R^3\), define
\[
 B^{(2)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_2),
 \qquad
 B^{(3)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_3).
\]
Equation (3) and orthogonality give, for \(j\in\{2,3\}\),
\[
 S_{B^{(j)}}=\frac34I_2,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}\!\left(3^{-1}S_{B^{(j)}}\right)=\frac14>\kappa.
 \tag{5}
\]
Hence the two matrices are distinct feasible projective points.  By (4), however, def:gain-loss gives \(h(0)=g_B(0)=\ell_B(0)=w(B)=0\) for every feasible \(B\).  Every pilot observation is also zero, so
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}
 =\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}
 =\mathcal B_\kappa
 \quad\text{at every pilot size}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Along \(m_1=m_0=m\), select \(B^{(2)}\) for even \(m\) and \(B^{(3)}\) for odd \(m\).  This deterministic rule is measurable and has two distinct subsequential limits, while (6) makes the outer-set inclusion exact.  The singleton condition is therefore necessary for a generic point-convergence conclusion. \(\square\)
