\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(K(\xi)=\mathcal B_\kappa(\xi)\). On the finite assignment space, total-variation convergence \(\nu_j\to\nu\) implies entrywise convergence of \(\Pi_j\) and \(\Omega_j\). At an exposure-positive instance the diagonal entries of \(\Pi\) are strictly positive, so \(\Pi_j^{-1}\to\Pi^{-1}\). Wasserstein convergence of bounded marginals is convergence of their quantiles in \(L^1(0,1)\). Since all quantiles lie in \([0,1]\),
\[
\left|\int_0^1 q_{1,j}(u)q_{0,j}(u)\,du-
\int_0^1 q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du\right|
\le\lVert q_{1,j}-q_1\rVert_1+\lVert q_{0,j}-q_0\rVert_1, \tag{1}
\]
and the same inequality holds after reflection of the second quantile at \(1-u\). Means and second moments also converge on bounded support. Hence \(c_L,c_U,Q(c_L),Q(c_U)\) vary continuously with \(\xi\).

For normalized representatives on the product of the two radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres,
\[
(\xi,B)\longmapsto\lambda_{\min}\{n^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega(\xi)B\} \tag{2}
\]
is continuous. On its superlevel set at \(\kappa>0\), \(\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}\). The finite-dimensional formulas in def:gain-loss and thm:fixed-basis-endpoints therefore imply joint continuity, on the feasible graph, of
\[
(\xi,B)\longmapsto g_{B,\xi}(0),
\qquad
(\xi,B)\longmapsto w_\xi(B). \tag{3}
\]

We prove upper stability of both optimizer correspondences. Suppose \(\xi_j\to\xi_0\), \(B_j\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_j)\), and a subsequence satisfies \(B_j\to B\). Closed feasibility and (2) imply \(B\in K(\xi_0)\). Choose \(B^\circ\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0)\). The assumed slack gives
\[
\lambda_{\min}\{n^{-1}S_{B^\circ}(\xi_0)\}\ge\kappa+\eta,
\]
so the fixed basis \(B^\circ\) belongs to \(K(\xi_j)\) eventually. Optimality and (3) yield
\[
w_{\xi_j}(B_j)\le w_{\xi_j}(B^\circ)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
w_{\xi_0}(B)\le w_{\xi_0}(B^\circ).
\]
Thus \(B\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0)\). The same argument, with the inequality reversed and \(g(0)\) in place of \(w\), proves upper stability of \(\mathcal A_0\).

The optimizer sets are nonempty and compact because \(K(\xi)\) is nonempty and compact and both objectives are continuous. For each \(j\), choose
\[
B_{0,j}\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B),
\qquad
B_{\mathrm{mm},j}\in\operatorname*{argmax}_{B\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B).
\]
Every subsequence has a further subsequence along which both bases converge. The upper-stability conclusions and (3) imply
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B)
\ge\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)}w_{\xi_0}(B) \tag{4}
\]
and
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\sup_{B\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B)
\le\sup_{B\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0)}w_{\xi_0}(B). \tag{5}
\]
Subtracting (5) from (4) gives
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\Delta(\xi_j)\ge\Delta(\xi_0)>0. \tag{6}
\]
The relative topology on \(\mathfrak X_\kappa\) is metrizable by the stated product metrics. If no relative-open neighborhood with positive gap existed, a sequence \(\xi_j\to\xi_0\) with \(\Delta(\xi_j)\le0\) would contradict (6). \(\square\)
