Under ass:known-exposure, ass:mutually-exclusive-exposures, ass:exposure-positivity, and ass:iid-potential-pairs, every \(B\in\mathcal B_Z\) has affine functions \(c\mapsto h(c)\), \(c\mapsto g_B(c)\), and \(c\mapsto\ell_B(c)\) on the imported feasible domain \(\mathcal C(F_1,F_0)=[c_L,c_U]\), with the exact baseline identity \(h(c)=h(0)+2c/n\). Consequently, \(\inf_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}g_B(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\})=\min\{g_B(c_L),g_B(c_U)\}\) and \(\sup_{H\in\Gamma(F_1,F_0)}\ell_B(\operatorname{Cov}_H\{Y_i(1),Y_i(0)\})=\max\{\ell_B(c_L),\ell_B(c_U)\}\), with both extrema attained. The product coupling gives \(c_L\le0\le c_U\), so \(g_B(0)\) is an ordinarily typed feasible-covariance gain. If \(c_L<0<c_U\), then \(g_B(0)\) equals the worst-coupling gain if and only if the affine slope of \(g_B\) is zero. Moreover,
\[
w(B)=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}\{h(0)+2c_e/n-g_B(c_e)\},
\qquad
r(B)=\min_{e\in\{L,U\}}g_B(c_e).
\]
Thus, when \(c_L<c_U\), minimization of worst-copula residual variance is not the constant-minus-gain reduction of maximin gain: the two endpoint baselines differ by \(2(c_U-c_L)/n>0\).
