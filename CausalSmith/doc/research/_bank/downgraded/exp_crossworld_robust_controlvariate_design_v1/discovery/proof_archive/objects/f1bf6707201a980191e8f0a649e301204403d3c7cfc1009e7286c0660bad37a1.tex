\paragraph{Proof.}
Write \(J_3=\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}\) and
\[
K=\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix}.
\]
We first verify the design, support, and rank conditions. Since \(f_i(W)=W_i\),
\[
D_{i,1}=W_i,
\qquad
D_{i,0}=1-W_i.
\]
Thus the two exposure events are complementary, hence mutually exclusive, for every unit and every assignment. The three independent Bernoulli coordinates give probability \(2^{-3}=1/8\) to each assignment in \(\{0,1\}^3\). Entrywise covariance calculation, using \(\mathbb E_\nu W_i=1/2\), \(\operatorname{Var}_\nu(W_i)=1/4\), and independence across distinct units, yields
\[
\Pi=\tfrac12 I_6,
\qquad
\Omega=\operatorname{Var}_\nu(D)=\tfrac14K.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, all six exposure probabilities equal \(1/2>0\), so \(\Pi^{-1}=2I_6\) exists.

The normalized regular class is nonempty, including away from its regularity boundary. Let
\[
x=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\qquad
y=\sqrt{\tfrac32}\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\qquad
B_\circ=\begin{bmatrix}x&0\\0&y\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\lVert x\rVert_2^2=\lVert y\rVert_2^2=3\) and \(x^{\top}y=0\). By (1),
\[
S_{B_\circ}=B_\circ^{\top}\Omega B_\circ
=\tfrac14\begin{bmatrix}3&-x^{\top}y\\-y^{\top}x&3\end{bmatrix}
=\tfrac34 I_2,
\]
and hence
\[
\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_{B_\circ})=\tfrac14>\tfrac18=\kappa.
\tag{2}
\]
For every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), including every point on the boundary where the defining inequality is an equality,
\[
\lambda_{\min}(S_B)\ge 3\kappa=\tfrac38>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus every inverse used by the fixed-\(\kappa\) criterion is defined; no boundary point is discarded.

For the first marginal pair, \((F_1,F_0)=(\delta_0,\delta_0)\), both laws have support in \([0,1]\), all four marginal means and variances are zero, and both quantile-product integrals are zero. Therefore \(c_L=c_U=0\) and \(Q(0)=0\). The definitions of the uncontrolled variance, gain, and loss give
\[
h(0)=g_B(0)=\ell_B(0)=0
\qquad\text{for every }B\in\mathcal B_\kappa.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)=0.
\tag{4}
\]

For the second marginal pair, \((F_1,F_0)=(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),\delta_0)\), both laws again have support in \([0,1]\). Since \(F_0^{-1}(u)=0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(u\in(0,1)\), both endpoint quantile products vanish; since \(\mu_0=0\),
\[
c_L=c_U=0.
\tag{5}
\]
The marginal moments are
\[
\mu_1=\tfrac12,
\qquad
\sigma_1^2=\tfrac14,
\qquad
\mu_0=\sigma_0^2=0.
\]
The endpoint moment matrix is therefore
\[
Q(0)=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A=\tfrac14(I_3+J_3).
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (1) and (5) show that the two instances have the same moment-incomplete input \((\Pi,\Omega,c_L,c_U,\kappa,\varepsilon)\), while (6) differs from the zero endpoint matrix in the first instance.

It remains to optimize the second loss exactly. Fix \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), write its normalized arm blocks as \(x_B=b_1\) and \(y_B=b_0\), and define
\[
C_B=\begin{bmatrix}x_B&-y_B\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{3\times2}.
\]
Direct block multiplication using (1) gives
\[
S_B=\tfrac14C_B^{\top}C_B.
\tag{7}
\]
By (3), \(C_B\) has rank two. A second direct multiplication, now using (1) and (6), gives
\[
B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\Pi^{-1}\Omega B
=\tfrac14C_B^{\top}AC_B.
\tag{8}
\]
Let
\[
P_B=C_B(C_B^{\top}C_B)^{-1}C_B^{\top}.
\]
This is the rank-two orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{col}(C_B)\). Substituting (7)--(8) into the gain formula and using cyclicity of trace yields
\[
g_B(0)
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(C_B^{\top}C_B)^{-1}C_B^{\top}AC_B\right]
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}(P_BA).
\tag{9}
\]
The uncontrolled term is, again by (1) and (6),
\[
h(0)
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\right]
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}(A)
=\tfrac16.
\tag{10}
\]

Put \(u=3^{-1/2}\mathbf1\). Since \(J_3=3uu^{\top}\),
\[
A=\tfrac14I_3+\tfrac34uu^{\top}.
\]
For every rank-two orthogonal projector \(P_B\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P_BA)
=\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}(P_B)+\tfrac34u^{\top}P_Bu
=\tfrac12+\tfrac34\lVert P_Bu\rVert_2^2
\le\tfrac54.
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (9)--(11) proves the global lower bound
\[
\ell_B(0)=h(0)-g_B(0)
\ge\tfrac16-\tfrac19\cdot\tfrac54
=\tfrac1{36}.
\tag{12}
\]
The feasible basis \(B_\circ\) from (2) attains equality: its first arm column is \(x=\sqrt3u\), so \(u\in\operatorname{col}(C_{B_\circ})\) and \(P_{B_\circ}u=u\). Since (5) makes the covariance interval a singleton, \(w(B)=\ell_B(0)\). Hence (12) is sharp and
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)=\tfrac1{36}.
\tag{13}
\]

Finally, an algorithm restricted to \((\Pi,\Omega,c_L,c_U,\kappa,\varepsilon)\) receives identical input on the two instances. Any interval that is always valid for that input must therefore contain both values (4) and (13). Its width must satisfy
\[
U_\varepsilon-L_\varepsilon\ge\tfrac1{36}>\tfrac1{100}=\varepsilon,
\]
contradicting the demanded tolerance. Thus the endpoint moment matrices, or equivalent marginal moments, are necessary inputs. This is an input-indistinguishability obstruction only and proves no termination claim for a moment-complete solver.

The causal estimand remains the fixed finite-population contrast \(\tau\). The optimized quantity \(w(B)\) is an ex ante basis-design criterion and is not an identifying definition of \(\tau\). \(\square\)
