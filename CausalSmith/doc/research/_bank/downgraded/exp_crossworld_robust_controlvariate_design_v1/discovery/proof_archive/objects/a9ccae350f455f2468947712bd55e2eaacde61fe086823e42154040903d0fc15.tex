\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\xi_j\to\xi_0\).  Total-variation convergence on the finite assignment space gives \(\Pi_j\to\Pi_0\) and \(\Omega_j\to\Omega_0\); exposure positivity makes the inverses converge locally.  Wasserstein convergence on \([0,1]\) gives convergence of first and second moments, while lem:restricted-marginal-extrema-continuity gives \(c_{Lj}\to c_{L0}\) and \(c_{Uj}\to c_{U0}\).  Hence both endpoint matrices converge.

On normalized representatives, \(\rho(\xi,B)=\lambda_{\min}(n^{-1}B^\top\Omega(\xi)B)\) is jointly continuous.  On \(\rho\ge\kappa\), inversion is uniformly bounded and both \(w_\xi(B)\) and \(g_{B,\xi}(0)\) are jointly continuous.  A minimizer at \(\xi_0\) remains feasible nearby by the assumed slack, so
\[
\limsup_jv_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_j)\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0).
\tag{1}
\]
Choose at each \(j\) a zero-covariance optimizer minimizing \(w\) on that optimizer set.  Compactness gives a convergent subsequence.  Comparing its nominal gain with a fixed slack-interior optimizer at \(\xi_0\) shows that its limit lies in \(\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)\).  Joint continuity therefore yields
\[
\liminf_j\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B)
\ge\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)}w_{\xi_0}(B).
\tag{2}
\]
Subtracting (1) from (2) proves the asserted lower semicontinuity of \(\Delta\). \(\square\)
