\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\rho(\xi,B)=\lambda_{\min}\{n^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega(\xi)B\}\).  On normalized representatives, total-variation convergence of the finite assignment law gives continuous variation of \(\Pi\), \(\Omega\), and \(\rho\).  Wasserstein convergence of the bounded marginals gives convergence of their first two moments and, by continuity of fixed-marginal extrema for the continuous product function, convergence of \(c_L,c_U\).  Exposure positivity persists locally, so matrix inversion and both endpoint criteria are jointly continuous wherever \(\rho\ge\kappa\).

Let \(\xi_j\to\xi_0\).  The assumed slack keeps every population minimax and zero-covariance optimizer at \(\xi_0\) feasible for all sufficiently large \(j\).  A fixed minimax optimizer therefore yields
\[
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}v_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_j)
 \le v_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0).
\tag{1}
\]
For each \(j\), select \(B_{0j}\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)\) minimizing \(w_{\xi_j}\) on that compact optimizer set.  Compactness of the product of normalized spheres gives a convergent subsequence.  Comparing its zero-covariance gain against any slack-interior optimizer at \(\xi_0\), then passing to the limit by joint continuity, shows that every such limit belongs to \(\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)\).  Hence
\[
 \liminf_{j\to\infty}\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B)
 \ge \inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)}w_{\xi_0}(B).
\tag{2}
\]
Subtracting (1) from (2) gives \(\liminf_j\Delta(\xi_j)\ge\Delta(\xi_0)>0\).  If no relative-open neighborhood had positive gap, metrizability of \(\mathfrak X_\kappa\) would supply \(\xi_j\to\xi_0\) with \(\Delta(\xi_j)\le0\), a contradiction. \(\square\)
