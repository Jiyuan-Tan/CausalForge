\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(\mathbf1=\mathbf1_3\).  We first verify the observable design inputs.  The eight assignments split as follows.  The all-zero and all-one assignments expose no unit to either displayed label.  If only unit \(i\) is treated, then \(D_{i,1}=1\) and \(D_{j,0}=1\) for the other two units.  If exactly \(i\) and \(j\) are treated, then \(D_{k,0}=1\) for the remaining unit.  The two exposure events are therefore mutually exclusive within each unit.  Their probabilities give
\[
\pi_{i,1}=\frac4{27},
\qquad
\pi_{i,0}=2\frac4{27}+\frac2{27}=\frac{10}{27}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, all displayed exposure probabilities are positive.  Direct intersection-probability calculations give, for distinct \(i,j\),
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&i=j&i\ne j\\ \hline
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{j,1})&92/729&-16/729\\
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,1},D_{j,0})&-40/729&68/729\\
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(D_{i,0},D_{j,0})&170/729&8/729.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
For instance, when \(i\ne j\), the event \(D_{i,1}D_{j,0}=1\) is the singleton assignment treating \(i\), whose probability is \(4/27=108/729\); subtracting \((4/27)(10/27)=40/729\) gives \(68/729\).  Likewise, \(D_{i,0}D_{j,0}=1\) is the singleton assignment treating the third unit and has probability \(4/27\).  Equations (1)--(2) prove the displayed \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\).  The known-exposure condition makes the labels legitimate potential-outcome exposures; outcomes under \(\star\) do not enter the calculation.

For a Bernoulli \((1/2)\) variable, \(\mu_a=1/2\), \(\sigma_a^2=1/4\), and its generalized inverse takes only the values zero and one.  The countermonotone and comonotone integrals therefore give
\[
c_L=0-\frac14=-\frac14,
\qquad
c_U=\frac12-\frac14=\frac14.
\tag{3}
\]
Substitution in the moment-matrix definition gives the displayed endpoint matrices: each diagonal block is \(J_3/4+I_3/4=A\), while the cross block \(-J_3/4-cI_3\) is \((I_3-J_3)/4\) at \(c_L\) and \(-A\) at \(c_U\).

We next solve the rank-two problem globally.  Normalize both nonzero arm vectors and put
\[
s=\mathbf1^{\top}x,
\qquad t=\mathbf1^{\top}y,
\qquad q=x^{\top}y.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
0\le s^2\le9,
\qquad 0\le t^2\le9.
\tag{4}
\]
Block multiplication gives
\[
S_B=
\begin{bmatrix}
\dfrac49-\dfrac{16s^2}{729}&-\dfrac{4q}{27}+\dfrac{68st}{729}\\[4pt]
-\dfrac{4q}{27}+\dfrac{68st}{729}&\dfrac23+\dfrac{8t^2}{729}
\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]
Define
\[
d=729+12t^2-36s^2-54q^2+68qst-22s^2t^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
\det(S_B)=\frac{8d}{19683}.
\tag{7}
\]
The positive-definiteness calculation in the singular-strata lemma shows that the displayed \(\Omega\) is positive definite.  Since the two block-supported columns of \(B(x,y)\) are linearly independent when \(x,y\ne0\), (7) has \(d>0\) throughout the entire rank-two projective stratum.  Thus the calculation below covers all of \(\mathcal B_Z\), not only the fixed \(\kappa\)-regular subset.

Let \(C_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B\).  Its block rows are
\[
C_B=
\begin{bmatrix}
x-\dfrac{4s}{27}\mathbf1&-y+\dfrac{17t}{27}\mathbf1\\[3pt]
-\dfrac25x+\dfrac{34s}{135}\mathbf1&\dfrac35y+\dfrac{4t}{135}\mathbf1
\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{8}
\]
Writing \(Q_L=Q(c_L)\) and \(Q_U=Q(c_U)\), multiplication yields
\[
C_B^{\top}Q_LC_B=
\begin{bmatrix}
(1195s^2+6561)/24300&-(729q-1820st)/12150\\
-(729q-1820st)/12150&(1240t^2+729)/6075
\end{bmatrix},
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
C_B^{\top}Q_UC_B=
\begin{bmatrix}
3(49-5s^2)/100&(5st-14q)/25\\
(5st-14q)/25&(48-5t^2)/25
\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]
The ordinary-inverse trace formula now gives
\[
\begin{split}
g_B(c_L)
&=\frac{76545+9891s^2+30732t^2-5832q^2+18232qst-10460s^2t^2}{1200d},\\
g_B(c_U)
&=\frac{3\{66825-5181s^2-2652t^2-6048q^2+5968qst-1260s^2t^2\}}{400d}.
\end{split}
\tag{11}
\]
The uncontrolled endpoint traces are
\[
h(c_L)=\frac{313}{600},
\qquad h(c_U)=\frac{171}{200}.
\tag{12}
\]
Because the moment matrix is affine in \(c\) and zero is the midpoint of \([c_L,c_U]\),
\[
g_B(0)=\frac{g_B(c_L)+g_B(c_U)}2.
\]
Combining this identity with (11),
\[
\frac{31}{80}-g_B(0)=\frac{N_0(s,t,q)}{1200d},
\tag{13}
\]
where
\[
N_0=5022q^2-4352qst+670s^2t^2+1629s^2+2148t^2.
\]
The exact square completion
\[
N_0
=5022\left(q-\frac{1088}{2511}st\right)^2
+1629s^2+2148t^2-\frac{685118}{2511}s^2t^2
\tag{14}
\]
is nonnegative.  Indeed, its last three terms are bilinear in \((s^2,t^2)\in[0,9]^2\), and their four vertex values are
\[
0,
\qquad14661,
\qquad19332,
\qquad\frac{368665}{31},
\]
all nonnegative.  Bilinear interpolation expresses the value at every point of the rectangle as the convex combination of these four values.  Equality forces \(s=t=0\), and then the square forces \(q=0\).  Since \(d>0\), (13)--(14) prove that the rank-two maximum is \(31/80\), with precisely the displayed equality set.  It is nonempty; for example,
\[
x=\sqrt{\frac32}(1,-1,0)^{\top},
\qquad
y=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,1,-2)^{\top}
\]
satisfies the equality conditions.  At every equality point (5) becomes \(\operatorname{diag}(4/9,2/3)\), so
\[
\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=\frac4{27}>\frac18.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus the equality set is exactly \(\mathcal A_0\) on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\), and substitution in (11)--(12) gives
\[
g_B(c_L)=\frac7{80},
\qquad
g_B(c_U)=\frac{11}{16},
\qquad
w(B)=\frac{521}{1200}.
\tag{16}
\]

For the minimax problem, set
\[
\lambda=\frac{1407}{2635}\in(0,1),
\qquad
v=\frac{2613}{8432},
\]
and abbreviate \(\ell_L=\ell_B(c_L)\), \(\ell_U=\ell_B(c_U)\).  A maximum dominates every strict convex combination, so (11)--(12) give
\[
\begin{split}
w(B)-v
&\ge\lambda\ell_L+(1-\lambda)\ell_U-v\\
&=\frac{3N_{\mathrm{mm}}(s,t,q)}{210800d},
\end{split}
\tag{17}
\]
where
\[
N_{\mathrm{mm}}
=274734q^2-281644qst+68986s^2t^2+34527s^2.
\]
The square completion is
\[
N_{\mathrm{mm}}
=274734\left(q-\frac{70411}{137367}st\right)^2
+s^2\left(34527-\frac{439017980}{137367}t^2\right).
\tag{18}
\]
By (4), the second coefficient is bounded below by
\[
34527-9\frac{439017980}{137367}
=\frac{87967621}{15263}>0.
\tag{19}
\]
Therefore (17) proves \(w(B)\ge v\) on the full rank-two stratum.  Equality in its second line forces \(s=q=0\).  Since both convex-combination weights are positive, equality in the first line additionally forces \(\ell_L=\ell_U\).  When \(s=q=0\), direct simplification gives
\[
\ell_L-\ell_U
=-\frac{3(55t^2-216)}{10(4t^2+243)}.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus equality holds exactly when
\[
s=0,
\qquad q=0,
\qquad t^2=\frac{216}{55}.
\tag{21}
\]
These points exist: choose a normalized \(y\) with \((\mathbf1^{\top}y)^2=216/55<9\), and then choose normalized \(x\) in the nonzero orthogonal complement of \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,y\}\).  At every such point (5) is diagonal with entries \(4/9\) and \(2/3+8t^2/729\), so (15) again holds.  This proves the stated rank-two minimizer set and its value \(2613/8432\).

The carrier-identification lemma gives a bijection \(\iota\) from these ordinary rank-two classes to the rank-two stratum of the published domain and preserves both \(g(c)\) and \(w\).  The singular-strata certificate gives
\[
\sup_{\operatorname{rank}(S_B)\le1}g_B^+(0)
=\frac{87}{400}<\frac{31}{80}
\tag{22}
\]
and
\[
\inf_{\operatorname{rank}(S_B)\le1}w^+(B)
=\frac{39}{80}>\frac{2613}{8432}.
\tag{23}
\]
Hence no rank-zero or rank-one class is an optimizer, and (22)--(23) upgrade the rank-two equality sets to the asserted full published-domain correspondences.

Finally, (1) verifies exposure positivity, and (15) exhibits feasible \(\kappa\)-regular bases.  Hence \(\xi_0\in\mathfrak X_\kappa\).  Equations (15), (16), and (21) apply to every member of the optimizer correspondences, so
\[
\delta
=\frac{521}{1200}-\frac{2613}{8432}
=\frac{4912}{39525}>0
\]
in both domains, while the optimizer-wide regularity slack is
\[
\eta=\frac4{27}-\frac18=\frac5{216}>0.
\]
This proves all claimed equality sets, values, support conditions, rank conditions, and boundary exclusions. \(\square\)
