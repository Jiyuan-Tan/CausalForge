\paragraph{Proof.}
Write \(J_3=\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}\) and
\[
K=\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Because \(f_i(W)=W_i\), the two exposure events for unit \(i\) are complementary:
\[
D_{i,1}=W_i,
\qquad
D_{i,0}=1-W_i.
\]
Thus they are mutually exclusive on every assignment.  Independence of the three assignments, together with \(\mathbb E_\nu W_i=1/2\) and \(\operatorname{Var}_\nu(W_i)=1/4\), gives, entry by entry,
\[
\Pi=\tfrac12 I_6,
\qquad
\Omega=\operatorname{Var}_\nu(D)=\tfrac14K.
\]
In particular, every exposure probability is \(1/2>0\), so the required exposure support condition holds and \(\Pi^{-1}=2I_6\) exists.

We first verify that the normalized regular basis class is nonempty, including at the stated value of \(\kappa\).  Let
\[
x=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\qquad
y=\sqrt{\tfrac32}\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\qquad
B=\begin{bmatrix}x&0\\0&y\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\lVert x\rVert_2^2=\lVert y\rVert_2^2=3\) and \(x^{\top}y=0\).  Direct multiplication yields
\[
S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B
=\tfrac14
\begin{bmatrix}
3&-x^{\top}y\\
-y^{\top}x&3
\end{bmatrix}
=\tfrac34 I_2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=\tfrac14>\tfrac18=\kappa.
\]
This supplies a strictly feasible point and proves, rather than assumes, nonemptiness.  More generally, for every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), the inequality \(\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)\ge 1/8\) implies \(S_B\succ0\), so every ordinary inverse appearing below is defined.  Equality at the \(\kappa\)-boundary would still give \(S_B\succ0\), so no boundary point is discarded by this rank argument.

Consider first \((F_1,F_0)=(\delta_0,\delta_0)\).  Both laws have support in \([0,1]\), and all marginal means and variances vanish.  Both quantile-product integrals vanish, hence \(c_L=c_U=0\).  The moment-matrix formula gives \(Q(0)=0\).  Therefore \(h(0)=g_B(0)=\ell_B(0)=0\) for every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), and
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)=0.
\]

Now consider \((F_1,F_0)=(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),\delta_0)\).  Again both marginals have support in \([0,1]\).  Since \(F_0^{-1}(u)=0\) for every \(u\in(0,1)\), both quantile-product integrals are zero; because \(\mu_0=0\), this gives \(c_L=c_U=0\).  The marginal moments are
\[
\mu_1=\tfrac12,
\qquad
\sigma_1^2=\tfrac14,
\qquad
\mu_0=\sigma_0^2=0.
\]
Hence
\[
Q(0)=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A=\tfrac14(I_3+J_3).
\]
The two instances therefore have exactly the same displayed tuple \((\Pi,\Omega,c_L,c_U,\kappa,\varepsilon)\), while their endpoint moment matrices differ.

It remains to compute the second minimax residual.  Fix any \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), write its two normalized blocks as \(x=b_1\) and \(y=b_0\), and set
\[
C=\begin{bmatrix}x&-y\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{3\times2}.
\]
Projective normalization gives \(\lVert x\rVert_2^2=\lVert y\rVert_2^2=3\).  Moreover,
\[
S_B=\tfrac14 C^{\top}C.
\]
The preceding positive-definiteness argument thus shows that \(C\) has rank two.  Using \(\Pi^{-1}=2I_6\), \(\Omega=K/4\), and the displayed block form of \(Q(0)\), direct block multiplication also gives
\[
B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\Pi^{-1}\Omega B
=\tfrac14 C^{\top}AC.
\]
Therefore, with
\[
P_C=C(C^{\top}C)^{-1}C^{\top},
\]
the rank-two orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{col}(C)\), cyclicity of trace yields
\[
g_B(0)
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(C^{\top}C)^{-1}C^{\top}AC\right]
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}(P_CA).
\]
The uncontrolled term is
\[
h(0)
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\right]
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}(A)
=\tfrac16.
\]

Let \(u=3^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\).  Since \(J_3=3uu^{\top}\),
\[
A=\tfrac14I_3+\tfrac34uu^{\top}.
\]
For any rank-two orthogonal projector \(P_C\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P_CA)
=\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}(P_C)+\tfrac34u^{\top}P_Cu
=\tfrac12+\tfrac34\lVert P_Cu\rVert_2^2
\le\tfrac54.
\]
It follows that every feasible basis obeys
\[
\ell_B(0)=h(0)-g_B(0)
\ge \tfrac16-\tfrac19\cdot\tfrac54
=\tfrac1{36}.
\]
For the strictly feasible basis exhibited above, \(x=\sqrt3u\) and \(y\perp u\).  Hence \(u\in\operatorname{col}(C)\), so \(\lVert P_Cu\rVert_2=1\) and equality holds in the preceding bound.  Since the covariance interval is the singleton \(\{0\}\), the endpoint maximum satisfies \(w(B)=\ell_B(0)\).  Consequently,
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)=\tfrac1{36}.
\]

Finally, an algorithm whose only input is \((\Pi,\Omega,c_L,c_U,\kappa,\varepsilon)\) receives identical input on these two lawful instances and therefore must return the same rational interval \([L_\varepsilon,U_\varepsilon]\).  If that interval were valid on both instances, it would contain both \(0\) and \(1/36\).  Thus
\[
U_\varepsilon-L_\varepsilon\ge\tfrac1{36}>\tfrac1{100}=\varepsilon,
\]
contradicting the required tolerance.  Endpoint moment matrices, or equivalent marginal moments, are therefore necessary for this certification task.  The argument is only an input-indistinguishability obstruction; it neither assumes nor proves termination of the corrected moment-complete solver.

The causal target remains the fixed finite-population exposure contrast \(\tau\).  The quantity \(w(B)\) is an ex ante design criterion for choosing a control-variate basis and is not an identifying definition of \(\tau\).  \(\square\)
