\textbf{Proof.}
Let
\[
A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1},
\qquad
A_B^+=\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}P_B\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}.
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:rank-stratified-projector-reduction}\), \(P_B\) is an orthogonal projector, so \(0\preceq P_B\preceq I\).  Congruence first by \(\Omega^{1/2}\) and then by \(\Pi^{-1}\), which exists by exposure positivity, gives
\[
0\preceq A_B^+\preceq A_{\mathrm{HT}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Put \(K_B=A_{\mathrm{HT}}-A_B^+\succeq0\).  The definitions of the empirical and population losses give
\[
\widehat w_m^+(B)=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}n^{-2}
\operatorname{tr}(K_B\widehat Q_e),
\qquad
w^+(B)=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}n^{-2}
\operatorname{tr}\{K_BQ(c_e)\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For two finite collections of real numbers, the absolute difference of their maxima is at most the maximum of their absolute pairwise differences.  Also, since \(K_B\succeq0\),
\[
|\operatorname{tr}(K_BM)|
\le\lVert K_B\rVert_*\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
=\operatorname{tr}(K_B)\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{3}
\]
Now \(\Omega_{jj}=\pi_j(1-\pi_j)\le\pi_j\) for each of the \(2n\) exposure indicators.  Hence
\[
\operatorname{tr}(K_B)
\le\operatorname{tr}(A_{\mathrm{HT}})
=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\frac{\Omega_{jj}}{\pi_j^2}
\le\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\frac1{\pi_j}
\le\frac{2n}{\pi_{\min,n}}.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (2)--(4) gives the slightly stronger bound
\[
d_m^+\le2\pi_{\min,n}^{-1}n^{-1}M_m
\le4\pi_{\min,n}^{-1}n^{-1}M_m.
\tag{5}
\]
Under the exposure-probability rate condition, \(\pi_{\min,n}^{-1}\le\underline\pi^{-1}n^\beta\), so (5) proves (A).

The selector lemma and the definition of \(d_m^+\) yield, realization by realization,
\[
\begin{aligned}
w^+(\widehat B_{m,\varepsilon_m}^+)
&\le \widehat w_m^+(\widehat B_{m,\varepsilon_m}^+)+d_m^+\\
&\le \inf_{[B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+}\widehat w_m^+(B)
+\varepsilon_m+d_m^+\\
&\le \inf_{[B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+}w^+(B)
+\varepsilon_m+2d_m^+\\
&=v_{\mathrm{mm}}^++\varepsilon_m+2d_m^+.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\) is an infimum, the left side of (6) minus \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\) is nonnegative.  Taking pilot expectations and using (A) proves (B).

We finally verify the rate consequence from the primitive bounded-pilot model.  Let
\[
e_a=\mathbb E_P\int_0^1
|\widehat F_a^{-1}(u)-F_a^{-1}(u)|\,du.
\]
For every \(t\), Cauchy--Schwarz and the variance of an empirical Bernoulli mean give
\[
\mathbb E_P|\widehat F_a(t)-F_a(t)|
\le\sqrt{F_a(t)\{1-F_a(t)\}/m_a}
\le(2\sqrt{m_a})^{-1}.
\]
The quantile identity proved in \(\texttt{lem:bounded-unpaired-endpoint-strong-convergence}\) and Tonelli's theorem therefore imply
\[
e_a\le(2\sqrt{m_a})^{-1}.
\tag{7}
\]
Let hats denote the empirical scalar moments.  From boundedness and (7),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P|\widehat\mu_a^2-\mu_a^2|&\le2e_a,
&\mathbb E_P|\widehat\sigma_a^2-\sigma_a^2|&\le4e_a,\\
\mathbb E_P|\widehat\mu_1\widehat\mu_0-\mu_1\mu_0|
&\le e_1+e_0,
&\mathbb E_P|\widehat c_e-c_e|&\le2(e_1+e_0),
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
uniformly for \(e=L,U\).  Applying the operator-norm triangle inequality to the four blocks of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\), using \(\lVert\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=n\), gives
\[
\mathbb E_P M_m
\le(4n+8)(e_1+e_0)
\le\frac{7n}{\sqrt{m_1\wedge m_0}},
\qquad n\ge3.
\tag{9}
\]
Multiplying (B) by \(n\) and applying (9),
\[
n\mathbb E_P\!\left[
w^+(\widehat B_{m,\varepsilon_m}^+)-v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+
\right]
\le
56\underline\pi^{-1}
\frac{n^{1+\beta}}{\sqrt{m_1\wedge m_0}}
+n\mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_m],
\tag{10}
\]
which tends to zero under the two displayed rate conditions.  The selected-rank assertion follows directly from \(\texttt{lem:full-carrier-countable-measurable-selector}\).  Nothing in (1)--(10) controls the observable coefficient across a singular rank boundary, so the result is only a basis-objective regret statement there. \(\square\)
