\textbf{Proof.}
Work on the probability-one event supplied by thm:plugin-correspondence.  By prop:kappa-exhaustion, \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is compact.  Let \(U\) be any open neighborhood of the unique minimizer \(B^\star\).  If empirical minimizers occurred outside \(U\) along an infinite subsequence, compactness would give a further convergent subsequence with limit in the closed set \(\mathcal B_\kappa\setminus U\).  The outer-limit conclusion of thm:plugin-correspondence would place that limit in \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}=\{B^\star\}\), a contradiction.  Thus \(\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\subset U\) eventually.  Since this holds for every \(U\), every measurable selection \(\widehat B\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) converges almost surely to \(B^\star\).

We now show that the singleton condition cannot in general be removed.  Take \(n=3\), let \(f_i(w)=w_i\), and assign the three units independently with probability \(1/2\).  The two exposure events are then mutually exclusive, exposure positivity holds, and direct enumeration gives
\[
 \Pi=\frac12I_6,
 \qquad
 \Omega=\frac14
 \begin{bmatrix}
  I_3&-I_3\\
  -I_3&I_3
 \end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Let \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\), let \(H=\delta_{(0,0)}\), and take \(\kappa=1/8\).  These marginals and their coupling are bounded, and independent unpaired pilot samples exist and are identically zero.  Their means, variances, and both covariance endpoints vanish, so def:moment-matrix gives
\[
 Q(0)=0.
 \tag{2}
\]
Consequently def:gain-loss gives \(w(B)=\widehat w(B)=0\) for every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), at every pilot size.

The feasible class contains at least the two normalized bases
\[
 B^{(2)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_2),
 \qquad
 B^{(3)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_3).
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the arm vectors in each basis are orthogonal, so (1) gives
\[
 S_{B^{(2)}}=S_{B^{(3)}}=\frac34I_2,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}\!\left(3^{-1}S_{B^{(j)}}\right)=\frac14>\kappa.
 \tag{4}
\]
The two bases are distinct in the projective quotient because their comparison-arm columns span different lines.  Thus ass:kappa-feasibility holds and
\(B^{(2)},B^{(3)}\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}=\mathcal B_\kappa\).

Along the diagonal pilot-size sequence \(m_1=m_0=m\), select \(B^{(2)}\) when \(m\) is even and \(B^{(3)}\) when \(m\) is odd.  This selection is deterministic, hence measurable, and it does not converge.  Nevertheless its every limit point belongs to \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), and indeed the outer-set inclusion holds identically because the empirical and population argmin sets coincide.  This proves the claimed failure of point convergence in the presence of a tie.  \(\square\)
