\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\xi_j=(\nu_j,F_{1j},F_{0j})\to\xi_0\).  Use normalized representatives in the fixed compact product of the two radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres modulo independent sign changes; this is the compact representation supplied by prop:kappa-exhaustion.  Because the assignment space is finite, total-variation convergence of \(\nu_j\) implies entrywise convergence
\[
\Pi_j\longrightarrow\Pi_0,
\qquad
\Omega_j\longrightarrow\Omega_0.                                    \tag{1}
\]
Exposure positivity at \(\xi_0\) implies that every relevant diagonal entry of \(\Pi_j\) is uniformly bounded away from zero for all sufficiently large \(j\), and hence
\[
\Pi_j^{-1}\longrightarrow\Pi_0^{-1}.                                \tag{2}
\]

Wasserstein convergence on \([0,1]\) implies weak convergence and convergence of first and second moments.  Apply lem:restricted-marginal-extrema-continuity with \(\varphi(y_1,y_0)=y_1y_0\).  The minimum and maximum cross moments over the coupling sets converge.  Subtracting the convergent products of marginal means yields
\[
c_{Lj}\longrightarrow c_{L0},
\qquad
c_{Uj}\longrightarrow c_{U0}.                                       \tag{3}
\]
Def:moment-matrix and convergence of the marginal first and second moments then give
\[
Q_j(c_{ej})\longrightarrow Q_0(c_{e0})
\qquad(e\in\{L,U\}).                                                \tag{4}
\]

For a normalized basis \(B\), put
\[
\rho(\xi,B)=\lambda_{\min}\{n^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega(\xi)B\}.
\]
This function is jointly continuous by (1).  On the closed region \(\rho\ge\kappa\), the inverse exists and satisfies
\[
\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.               \tag{5}
\]
Equations (1)--(5), def:gain-loss, and the endpoint reduction of thm:fixed-basis-endpoints show that \(g_{B,\xi}(0)\), both endpoint losses, and \(w_\xi(B)\) are jointly continuous on this region.

Choose \(B^\star_{\mathrm{mm}}\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0)\), which exists because ass:kappa-feasibility and prop:kappa-exhaustion make the feasible set nonempty and compact.  The assumed slack gives
\(\rho(\xi_0,B^\star_{\mathrm{mm}})\ge\kappa+\eta\), so this fixed representative is feasible for all sufficiently large \(j\).  Therefore
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}v_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_j)
\le\lim_{j\to\infty}w_{\xi_j}(B^\star_{\mathrm{mm}})
=v_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0).                                           \tag{6}
\]

For each \(j\), compactness and continuity allow a choice
\[
B_{0j}\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B).
\]
Choose a subsequence that realizes the lower limit of these minimum values and, by compactness, a further subsequence such that \(B_{0j}\to\bar B_0\).  Closedness of the inequality \(\rho\ge\kappa\) makes \(\bar B_0\) feasible at \(\xi_0\).  Choose any \(C_0\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)\).  Its assumed slack makes it feasible for all sufficiently large \(j\), and the defining optimality of \(B_{0j}\) for the zero-covariance criterion gives
\[
g_{B_{0j},\xi_j}(0)\ge g_{C_0,\xi_j}(0).
\]
Passing to the limit shows \(g_{\bar B_0,\xi_0}(0)\ge g_{C_0,\xi_0}(0)\).  Since \(C_0\) already attains the feasible maximum and \(\bar B_0\) is feasible, equality holds and \(\bar B_0\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)\).  Joint continuity now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_j)}w_{\xi_j}(B)
&=w_{\xi_0}(\bar B_0)\\
&\ge\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)}w_{\xi_0}(B).
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{7}
\]
The subsequence was selected to realize the lower limit, so (7) holds for the original sequence.

Finally, def:dominance-gap, (6), (7), and the elementary inequality \(\liminf(a_j-b_j)\ge\liminf a_j-\limsup b_j\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\Delta(\xi_j)
&\ge
\inf_{B\in\mathcal A_0(\xi_0)}w_{\xi_0}(B)-v_{\mathrm{mm}}(\xi_0)\\
&=\Delta(\xi_0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every division, inverse, moving-feasible-set comparison, and boundary passage is justified respectively by exposure positivity, the \(\kappa\)-bound, compactness, and the stated optimizer-wide slack. \(\square\)
