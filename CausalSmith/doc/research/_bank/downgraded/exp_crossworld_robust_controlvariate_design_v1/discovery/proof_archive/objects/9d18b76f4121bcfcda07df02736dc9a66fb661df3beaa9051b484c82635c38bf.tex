\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\texttt{lem:bounded-unpaired-endpoint-strong-convergence}\), for fixed \(n\),
\[
M_m=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\longrightarrow0
\quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{1}
\]
The uniform inequality in \(\texttt{lem:full-carrier-plugin-objective-regret-rate}\) then gives
\[
0\le d_m^+
\le4\pi_{\min,n}^{-1}n^{-1}M_m
\longrightarrow0
\quad P\text{-almost surely},
\tag{2}
\]
where \(\pi_{\min,n}>0\) follows from exposure positivity.

For arbitrary real-valued functions \(f,\widetilde f\) on the same nonempty domain,
\[
\left|\inf\widetilde f-\inf f\right|
\le\sup|\widetilde f-f|.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, \(\widetilde f\ge f-\sup|\widetilde f-f|\) and the reverse inequality follows after interchanging the two functions.  Applying (3) to \(\widehat w_m^+\) and \(w^+\), and recalling that \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+=\inf w^+\), proves
\[
\left|\inf_{[B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+}\widehat w_m^+(B)
-v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\right|
\le d_m^+\longrightarrow0
\quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{4}
\]

For every strictly positive finite tolerance, the classical selector is pilot-measurable and satisfies the empirical \(\varepsilon_m\)-optimality inequality by \(\texttt{lem:full-carrier-countable-measurable-selector}\).  Adding and subtracting the empirical criterion twice gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
w^+(\widehat B_{m,\varepsilon_m}^+)
&\le\widehat w_m^+(\widehat B_{m,\varepsilon_m}^+)+d_m^+\\
&\le\inf_{[B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+}\widehat w_m^+(B)
+\varepsilon_m+d_m^+\\
&\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}^++2d_m^++\varepsilon_m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(\varepsilon_m\to0\) almost surely and (2) imply
\[
0\le w^+(\widehat B_{m,\varepsilon_m}^+)-v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+
\le2d_m^++\varepsilon_m\longrightarrow0
\quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{6}
\]
The selector lemma also proves that every selected class has maximal achievable rank \(r_\Omega\), and hence has rank two exactly when rank two is achievable.  The argument uses only the countable first-qualifying rule.  It neither constructs a zero-tolerance minimizer nor gives a finite stopping test on arbitrary real pilot input; it does not compare the selected class with the separate exact-input cylindrical-algebraic output.  Since (5) is a value inequality rather than convergence of classes, it also yields no cross-rank outer-argmin or point convergence, and no singular-rank observable-estimator variance bridge. \(\square\)
