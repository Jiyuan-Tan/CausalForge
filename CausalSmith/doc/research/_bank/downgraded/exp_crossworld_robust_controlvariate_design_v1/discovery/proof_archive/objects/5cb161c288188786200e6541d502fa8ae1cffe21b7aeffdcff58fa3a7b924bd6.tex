\textbf{Proof.}
For a fixed vertex \(i\), the event \(D_{i,1}=1\) is the assignment in which \(i\) alone is treated, whereas \(D_{i,0}=1\) means that \(i\) is untreated and at least one of the other two vertices is treated.  Enumeration of the eight assignments therefore gives
\[
\pi_{i,1}=\frac4{27},
\qquad
\pi_{i,0}=\frac{10}{27},
\]
and
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&i=j&i\ne j\\ \hline
\operatorname{Cov}(D_{i,1},D_{j,1})&92/729&-16/729\\
\operatorname{Cov}(D_{i,1},D_{j,0})&-40/729&68/729\\
\operatorname{Cov}(D_{i,0},D_{j,0})&170/729&8/729.
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
This proves the displayed \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\), as well as exposure positivity and mutual exclusivity.  The Bernoulli \((1/2)\) quantile is zero on \((0,1/2]\) and one on \((1/2,1]\), so the countermonotone and comonotone products give \(c_L=-1/4\) and \(c_U=1/4\).  Substitution of \(\mu_a=1/2\) and \(\sigma_a^2=1/4\) in def:moment-matrix gives the two displayed endpoint matrices.

For a normalized representative \(B(x,y)\), put
\[
s=\mathbf1^{\top}x,
\qquad t=\mathbf1^{\top}y,
\qquad q=x^{\top}y.
\]
Direct block multiplication using (1) yields
\[
S_B=
\begin{bmatrix}
4/9-16s^2/729&-4q/27+68st/729\\
-4q/27+68st/729&2/3+8t^2/729
\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{2}
\]
With
\[
d=729+12t^2-36s^2-54q^2+68qst-22s^2t^2,
\tag{3}
\]
one has \(\det S_B=8d/19683\), so \(d>0\) on \(\mathcal B_{1/8}\).  Exact substitution in def:gain-loss gives
\[
\frac{31}{80}-g_B(0)=\frac{N_0}{1200d},
\tag{4}
\]
where
\[
N_0=5022q^2-4352qst+670s^2t^2+1629s^2+2148t^2.
\tag{5}
\]
The following completion makes its sign and equality set explicit:
\[
N_0
=5022\left(q-\frac{1088}{2511}st\right)^2
+1629s^2+2148t^2-\frac{685118}{2511}s^2t^2.
\tag{6}
\]
The last three terms are bilinear in \((s^2,t^2)\in[0,9]^2\).  Their values at the four vertices are
\[
0,\qquad14661,\qquad19332,\qquad\frac{368665}{31},
\]
respectively.  Bilinear interpolation therefore proves nonnegativity, with equality only at \(s=t=0\).  Equation (6) then requires \(q=0\).  Thus (4) is an equality exactly when \(s=t=q=0\).  Substitution at this equality set gives
\[
g_B(0)=\frac{31}{80},
\qquad
w(B)=\frac{521}{1200}.
\tag{7}
\]

For the minimax loss, let \(\lambda=1407/2635\in(0,1)\) and \(v=2613/8432\).  Since a maximum dominates every convex combination,
\[
\begin{aligned}
w(B)-v
&\ge \lambda\ell_B(c_L)+(1-\lambda)\ell_B(c_U)-v\\
&=\frac{3N_{\mathrm{mm}}}{210800d},
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
where exact reduction gives
\[
N_{\mathrm{mm}}
=274734\left(q-\frac{70411}{137367}st\right)^2
+s^2\left(34527-\frac{439017980}{137367}t^2\right).
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(t^2\le9\), the second coefficient is bounded below by
\[
34527-9\frac{439017980}{137367}
=\frac{87967621}{15263}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
Hence equality in (9) requires \(s=q=0\).  Since \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), equality in the first line of (8) additionally requires \(\ell_B(c_L)=\ell_B(c_U)\).  Under \(s=q=0\), exact substitution gives
\[
\ell_B(c_L)-\ell_B(c_U)
=-\frac{3(55t^2-216)}{10(4t^2+243)},
\tag{11}
\]
so equality holds exactly when \(t^2=216/55\).  This proves the displayed minimizer equality set and \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}=2613/8432\).

Both equality sets are nonempty.  For the nominal set choose an orthogonal pair of norm \(\sqrt3\) in \(\mathbf1^\perp\).  For the minimax set choose a norm-\(\sqrt3\) vector \(y\) with \((\mathbf1^{\top}y)^2=216/55<9\), and then choose a norm-\(\sqrt3\) vector \(x\) perpendicular to both \(\mathbf1\) and \(y\).  On either equality set, (2) is diagonal and
\[
\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=\frac4{27}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the equality sets lie in \(\mathcal B_{1/8}\), proving \(\kappa\)-feasibility and membership of \(\xi_0\) in \(\mathfrak X_\kappa\).  Finally,
\[
\frac{521}{1200}-\frac{2613}{8432}
=\frac{4912}{39525},
\qquad
\frac4{27}-\frac18=\frac5{216},
\tag{13}
\]
which are the claimed full-correspondence gap and regularity slack.  Assumption ass:known-exposure supplies the causal interpretation of the two exposure labels.  \(\square\)
