\textbf{Proof.}
Assumption ass:coordinate-bounded-kappa-feasibility makes \(\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\) nonempty.  Index the \(N=2n\) exposure coordinates by \(j\), put \(Z_j=D_j-\pi_j\), and let \(d_n\le\overline d n^\alpha\) be the largest closed dependency neighborhood.  If a tuple of centered coordinates has a distinct index separated from all the others, its joint moment is zero.  Counting the remaining dependency-supported tuples and using \(|Z_j|\le1\) gives, for deterministic arrays \(q,r,s\),
\[
\mathbb E_\nu\left(\sum_jq_jZ_j\right)^4
\le C N^2d_n^2\lVert q\rVert_\infty^4,
\tag{1}
\]
\[
\left|\mathbb E_\nu
\left(\sum_jq_jZ_j\right)
\left(\sum_jr_jZ_j\right)
\left(\sum_js_jZ_j\right)\right|
\le C Nd_n^2\lVert q\rVert_\infty\lVert r\rVert_\infty\lVert s\rVert_\infty.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, a contributing four-tuple either contains two dependency edges, giving at most \(CN^2d_n^2\) choices, or has a connected distinct-index graph, giving at most \(CNd_n^3\le CN^2d_n^2\); a contributing triple has at most \(CNd_n^2\) choices.  The constant \(C\) is numerical and does not depend on the array, the basis, or the coupling.

Fix a bounded exposure-potential-outcome array and \(B\in\mathcal B_{\kappa,\overline a}\).  Known exposure is used at the exact consistency step: on \(D_{i,a}=1\), ass:known-exposure gives \(Y_i(W)=Y_i(a)\).  Therefore, for the signed coordinate \(Y_j\),
\[
\mathbb E_\nu\widehat Y_j^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_j,
\qquad
\{\Pi^{-1}(\widehat Y^{\mathrm{obs}}-Y)\}_j
=\frac{Y_jZ_j}{\pi_j^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the possible exposure-mismatch term \(D_{i,a}\{Y_i(W)-Y_i(a)\}/\pi_{i,a}^2\) is identically zero; it has not been omitted from the algebra.  On this rank-two class Zhao's spectral pseudoinverse is the ordinary inverse.  Hence, with one consistently named Latin coefficient,
\[
G_B:=\widehat\gamma^{\mathrm{HT}}(B)-\gamma_B^*(Y)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{B,j}Z_j,\qquad u_{B,j}:=S_B^{-1}B^\top\Omega e_j\frac{Y_j}{\pi_j^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every column of \(\Omega\) has at most \(d_n\) nonzero entries, and every covariance entry has absolute value at most one.  The coordinate envelope, the exposure-probability rate, and \(S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2\) therefore imply
\[
\sup_{B,j}\lVert u_{B,j}\rVert_2
\le K_u n^{\alpha+2\beta-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Also
\[
X_B=n^{-1}B^\top(D-\mathbb E_\nu D)
=\sum_jx_{B,j}Z_j,
\qquad
\sup_{B,j}\lVert x_{B,j}\rVert_2\le\overline a/n.
\tag{6}
\]
Applying (1) coordinatewise to the two coordinates of \(G_B\) and \(X_B\) gives
\[
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert G_B\rVert_2^4
\le K_Gn^{6\alpha+8\beta-2},
\qquad
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\lVert X_B\rVert_2^4
\le K_Xn^{2\alpha-2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The feasible and oracle estimators satisfy
\[
\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{CV}}=\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}+\Delta_B,
\qquad
\Delta_B=-G_B^\top X_B.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and (7) yield
\[
\sup_B\mathbb E_\nu\Delta_B^2
\le K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}.
\tag{8}
\]

Put \(T_B=\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}-\tau\).  Equation (3) makes the Horvitz--Thompson part unbiased, and the basis exposure statistic is centered by construction, so \(\mathbb E_\nu T_B=0\).  Direct expansion gives
\[
T_B=\sum_jt_{B,j}Z_j,
\qquad
t_{B,j}=n^{-1}\{Y_j/\pi_j-B_{j\cdot}\gamma_B^*(Y)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The projector inequality
\[
\gamma_B^*(Y)^\top S_B\gamma_B^*(Y)
\le Y^\top\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Y
\tag{10}
\]
follows by writing the right-hand quadratic form in the \(\Omega^{1/2}\)-geometry and projecting onto \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega^{1/2}B)\).  Row sparsity, outcome boundedness, the exposure-probability rate, and \(S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2\) then give
\[
\sup_B\lVert\gamma_B^*(Y)\rVert_2\le K_\gamma n^{\alpha/2+\beta},
\qquad
\sup_{B,j}|t_{B,j}|\le K_tn^{\alpha/2+\beta-1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Expanding \(\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)\) as a sum of centered triple moments and applying (2), (5), (6), and (11) gives
\[
\sup_B|\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y)|
\le K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, its exponent is the sum of the factors \(N d_n^2\), \(\sup|t_{B,j}|\), \(\sup\lVert u_{B,j}\rVert_2\), and \(\sup\lVert x_{B,j}\rVert_2\).  Since
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(T_B+\Delta_B\mid Y)-\operatorname{Var}_\nu(T_B\mid Y)
=\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\Delta_B\mid Y)+2\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(T_B,\Delta_B\mid Y),
\]
(8) and (12) prove the first uniform bound with
\[
R_n=K_\Delta n^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}
+2K_{\mathrm{cov}}n^{(7/2)\alpha+3\beta-2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), one has \((7/2)\alpha+3\beta\le4\alpha+4\beta\).  Thus, with \(K_R=K_\Delta+2K_{\mathrm{cov}}\),
\[
0\le R_n\le K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-2}.
\tag{14}
\]
All constants may depend on the fixed bounds \(\kappa,\overline a,\underline\pi,\overline d\), but not on \(n,B,Y\), or \(H\).

It remains to identify the oracle variance.  Completing the square for the fixed oracle coefficient gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
=n^{-2}Y^\top\{\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}
-\Pi^{-1}\Omega BS_B^{-1}B^\top\Omega\Pi^{-1}\}Y.
\tag{15}
\]
Under ass:iid-potential-pairs, the signed vector satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}(YY^\top)=Q(c_H).
\tag{16}
\]
Taking the trace expectation in (15), using (16), and invoking def:gain-loss yields exactly
\[
\mathbb E_{H^{\otimes n}}
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\widehat\tau_B^{\mathrm{or}}\mid Y)
=h(c_H)-g_B(c_H)=\ell_B(c_H).
\tag{17}
\]
Integrating the first uniform bound over \(H^{\otimes n}\) and applying (17) proves the second uniform bound.  Finally, ass:zhao-first-order-rate gives
\[
nR_n\le K_Rn^{4\alpha+4\beta-1}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Every inner variance above is a conditional design variance under \(\nu\); the expectation under \(H^{\otimes n}\) is only the declared ex ante performance average. \(\square\)
