\textbf{Proof.}
Work with normalized representatives.  By prop:kappa-exhaustion and ass:kappa-feasibility, \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is a nonempty compact metrizable quotient.  Its block columns have squared norm \(n\), and \(\kappa\)-regularity gives
\[
 \lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt n,
 \qquad \lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined, while \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\) are fixed.

Write \(q_a=F_a^{-1}\) and \(\widehat q_a=\widehat F_a^{-1}\).  The bounded strong law, applied in both pilot arms to outcomes, squared outcomes, and empirical distribution indicators, yields almost surely
\[
 \lVert\widehat q_a-q_a\rVert_{L^1(0,1)}\to0,
 \qquad \widehat\mu_a\to\mu_a,
 \qquad \widehat\sigma_a^2\to\sigma_a^2.
 \tag{2}
\]
For completeness, the bounded strong law follows without an undeclared moment hypothesis: for independent centered \(X_j\) with \(\lvert X_j\rvert\le1\),
\[
 \mathbb E\left(\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\right)^4
 =\sum_j\mathbb EX_j^4+6\sum_{i<j}\mathbb EX_i^2\mathbb EX_j^2\le3m^2;
\]
Markov's inequality on a geometric subsequence, Borel--Cantelli, and bounded interpolation give the strong law.  Monotonicity of empirical distribution functions, the generalized-inverse squeeze, and dominated convergence give the first assertion in (2).

Since every quantile lies in \([0,1]\),
\[
 \left|\int_0^1\widehat q_1(u)\widehat q_0(u)\,du
 -\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du\right|
 \le\lVert\widehat q_1-q_1\rVert_1+\lVert\widehat q_0-q_0\rVert_1,
\]
and the same bound holds with the second quantile evaluated at \(1-u\).  Hence
\[
 \widehat c_L\to c_L,
 \qquad \widehat c_U\to c_U
 \tag{3}
\]
almost surely.  Ass:frechet-covariance-domain identifies these as the population endpoints.  If \(Q_e=Q(c_e)\) and \(\widehat Q_e\) denotes its empirical analogue, def:moment-matrix and (2)--(3) imply
\[
 \rho_m:=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\to0
 \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{4}
\]

Put \(C_\nu=\lVert\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\).  Using \(\lvert\operatorname{tr}M\rvert\le d\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\), def:gain-loss, and (1), uniformly in \(B\),
\[
 \max_e\lvert\widehat g_B(\widehat c_e)-g_B(c_e)\rvert
 \le\frac{2C_\nu^2}{n^2\kappa}\rho_m,
\qquad
 \max_e\lvert\widehat h(\widehat c_e)-h(c_e)\rvert
 \le\frac2n\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\rho_m.
 \tag{5}
\]
Subtracting and using that the maximum is one-Lipschitz gives
\[
 \delta_m:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}\lvert\widehat w(B)-w(B)\rvert\to0
 \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{6}
\]

All matrix operations are continuous on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\), because (1) keeps inversion away from singularity.  Thus both criteria attain their minima and
\[
 \lvert\widehat v_{\mathrm{mm}}-v_{\mathrm{mm}}\rvert\le\delta_m\to0. \tag{7}
\]
Finally, let \(B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and take a convergent subsequence \(B_{m_j}\to B_\infty\).  For any \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), empirical optimality gives
\[
 w(B_{m_j})\le\widehat w(B_{m_j})+\delta_{m_j}
 \le\widehat w(B^\star)+\delta_{m_j}
 \le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\delta_{m_j}.
\]
Continuity and \(w\ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}\) imply \(w(B_\infty)=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\), hence \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  This is the asserted outer-limit inclusion. \(\square\)
