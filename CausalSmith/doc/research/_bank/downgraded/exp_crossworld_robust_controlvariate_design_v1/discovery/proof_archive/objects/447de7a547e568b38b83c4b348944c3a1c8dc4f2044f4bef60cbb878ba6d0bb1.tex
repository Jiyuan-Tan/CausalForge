\textbf{Proof.}
Assumption ass:kappa-feasibility makes \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) nonempty, and prop:kappa-exhaustion makes it compact.  Assumption ass:exposure-positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  For every feasible \(B\),
\[
 S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2,
 \qquad
 \lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus inversion, multiplication, and trace are continuous uniformly on the feasible class.  Def:gain-loss therefore makes \(B\mapsto g_B(0)\), \(B\mapsto\ell_B(c_L)\), and \(B\mapsto\ell_B(c_U)\) continuous on normalized representatives.  These functions are invariant under the residual projective sign changes and descend continuously to \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  Hence \(w\), the maximum of the two endpoint losses, is continuous.

The extreme-value theorem makes both \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and \(\mathcal A_0\) nonempty and compact.  For arbitrary \(B_{\mathrm{mm}}\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and \(B_0\in\mathcal A_0\), the defining minimization gives
\[
 w(B_{\mathrm{mm}})=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\le w(B_0).
\]
The left side is constant over \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Taking the indicated supremum and infimum yields
\[
 \sup_{B_{\mathrm{mm}}\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}}w(B_{\mathrm{mm}})
 =v_{\mathrm{mm}}
 \le\inf_{B_0\in\mathcal A_0}w(B_0).
 \tag{2}
\]

Lemma lem:ht-affine-drift shows why (2) is a residual-minimax certificate and not a disguised maximin-gain statement:
\[
 w(B)=h(0)+\max\left\{\frac{2c_L}{n}-g_B(c_L),
 \frac{2c_U}{n}-g_B(c_U)\right\},
\]
whereas the secondary diagnostic maximizes \(\min\{g_B(c_L),g_B(c_U)\}\).  When \(c_L<c_U\), the endpoint drifts differ, so the two formal criteria are not separated by a basis-independent constant.

Strictness is not implied by the assumptions.  For any lawful design, take \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\).  Then \(c_L=c_U=0\), all marginal moments vanish, and def:moment-matrix gives \(Q(0)=0\).  Hence def:gain-loss gives \(g_B(0)=\ell_B(0)=w(B)=0\) for every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), so equality holds in (2).

For positioning only, this proof concerns a fixed assignment law and chooses a copula-robust control-variate span.  The separate results \(\texttt{exp\_bipartite\_minimax\_design\_v1/thm:hetero-envelope}\) and \(\texttt{exp\_bipartite\_minimax\_design\_v1/thm:convex-design}\) instead choose heterogeneous assignment propensities for a conservative H\'ajek envelope.  No mathematical step is borrowed from those results. \(\square\)
