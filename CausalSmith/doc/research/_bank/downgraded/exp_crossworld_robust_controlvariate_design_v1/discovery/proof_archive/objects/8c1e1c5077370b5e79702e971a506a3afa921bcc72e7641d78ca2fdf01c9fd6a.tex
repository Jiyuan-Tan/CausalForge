\textbf{Proof.}
Work with normalized representatives of the projective classes in \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  By prop:kappa-exhaustion and ass:kappa-feasibility, this quotient is a nonempty compact metrizable space.  Its two block-supported columns have squared Euclidean norm \(n\), and the defining regularity inequality gives
\[
 \lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 \lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Assumption ass:exposure-positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  The matrices \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\) are fixed because \(G\), \(\nu\), and the exposure maps are fixed.

Write \(q_a=F_a^{-1}\) and \(\widehat q_a=\widehat F_a^{-1}\).  We first prove convergence of every pilot ingredient without assuming a limit law.  If \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\) are independent, centered, and bounded in absolute value by one, independence and fourth-moment expansion yield
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\right)^4
 =\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb EX_j^4
 +6\sum_{i<j}\mathbb EX_i^2\,\mathbb EX_j^2
 \le3m^2.
 \tag{2}
\]
Markov's inequality therefore bounds the probability that the empirical average exceeds a fixed \(t>0\) in absolute value by \(3/(m^2t^4)\).  The sum of these probabilities is finite.  For completeness, the first Borel--Cantelli implication here follows directly from the union bound:
\[
 \Pr\!\left(\bigcup_{m\ge M}A_m\right)
 \le\sum_{m\ge M}\Pr(A_m)\longrightarrow0,
\]
so \(A_m\) occurs only finitely often almost surely.  Applying this argument in each arm to outcomes, squared outcomes, and simultaneously to indicators at rational thresholds proves the bounded strong law and weak convergence of \(\widehat F_a\) to \(F_a\).  The generalized-inverse squeeze gives \(\widehat q_a(u)\to q_a(u)\) at every continuity point of \(q_a\).  Since monotone functions have only countably many discontinuities and the quantiles lie in \([0,1]\), dominated convergence gives
\[
 \lVert\widehat q_a-q_a\rVert_{L^1(0,1)}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \widehat\mu_a\longrightarrow\mu_a,
 \qquad
 \widehat\sigma_a^2\longrightarrow\sigma_a^2
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Assumption ass:unpaired-pilot supplies the two i.i.d. sequences; intersecting their probability-one events is valid because there are only two arms.

Boundedness of the quantiles gives
\[
 \left|\int_0^1\widehat q_1(u)\widehat q_0(u)\,du
 -\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du\right|
 \le\lVert\widehat q_1-q_1\rVert_1
 +\lVert\widehat q_0-q_0\rVert_1,
 \tag{4}
\]
and the same inequality holds with the second quantile evaluated at \(1-u\).  Hence the quantile formulas for the Fr\'echet endpoints imply
\[
 \widehat c_L\longrightarrow c_L,
 \qquad
 \widehat c_U\longrightarrow c_U
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Assumption ass:frechet-covariance-domain identifies these as the two sharp endpoint covariances used by the population criterion.  For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), put \(Q_e=Q(c_e)\), and let \(\widehat Q_e\) be its empirical-moment counterpart.  The finite-dimensional formula in def:moment-matrix and (3)--(5) give
\[
 \rho_m:=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 \lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely as }m_1\wedge m_0\to\infty.
 \tag{6}
\]

Put \(C_\nu=\lVert\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\).  The inequality \(\lvert\operatorname{tr}(M)\rvert\le d\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) for a \(d\)-dimensional square matrix, def:gain-loss, and (1) imply, uniformly over \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\),
\[
 \max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 \lvert\widehat g_B(\widehat c_e)-g_B(c_e)\rvert
 \le\frac{2C_\nu^2}{n^2\kappa}\rho_m,
 \tag{7}
\]
because the gain trace is two-dimensional.  The analogous \(2n\)-dimensional trace bound gives
\[
 \max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 \lvert\widehat h(\widehat c_e)-h(c_e)\rvert
 \le\frac{2}{n}\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\rho_m.
 \tag{8}
\]
Subtracting gain from uncontrolled variance bounds both endpoint residual errors by the sum of the right sides of (7) and (8), uniformly in \(B\).  Since the maximum of two coordinates is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm,
\[
 \delta_m:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}
 \lvert\widehat w(B)-w(B)\rvert
 \longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Every endpoint loss is continuous on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\): matrix multiplication and trace are continuous, and (1) keeps inversion uniformly away from singularity.  Thus \(w\) and \(\widehat w\) attain their minima on the nonempty compact quotient.  The elementary minimum comparison inequality yields
\[
 \lvert\widehat v_{\mathrm{mm}}-v_{\mathrm{mm}}\rvert
 \le\delta_m\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{10}
\]

Finally, take \(B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and a convergent subsequence \(B_{m_j}\to B_\infty\), whose existence follows from compactness.  For any \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), empirical optimality and (9) give
\[
 w(B_{m_j})
 \le\widehat w(B_{m_j})+\delta_{m_j}
 \le\widehat w(B^\star)+\delta_{m_j}
 \le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\delta_{m_j}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Continuity of \(w\) and the inequality \(w\ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}\) imply \(w(B_\infty)=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Therefore \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), proving
\[
 \limsup_{m_1,m_0\to\infty}\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}
 \subseteq\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}
\]
almost surely in the projective quotient.  This theorem concerns the pilot-selected basis and its robust value; deployment through estimated coefficients is the separate observable construction in def:observable-coefficient-map. \(\square\)
