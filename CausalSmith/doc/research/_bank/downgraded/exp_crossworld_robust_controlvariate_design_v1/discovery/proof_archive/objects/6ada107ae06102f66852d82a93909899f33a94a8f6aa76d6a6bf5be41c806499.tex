\textbf{Proof.}
Let
\[
K_\kappa=\left\{(b_1,b_0):
\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=n,\quad
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(n^{-1}B(b_1,b_0)^{\top}\Omega
B(b_1,b_0)\right)\geq\kappa\right\}.
\]
The assumption of \(\kappa\)-feasibility makes \(K_\kappa\) nonempty.  By the compactness argument in Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}kappa\mbox{-}exhaustion}\), it is a closed subset of the product of two radius-\(\sqrt n\) spheres, and its image after the independent projective sign identifications is the compact set \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  Moreover, for every \(B\in K_\kappa\),
\[
S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\succeq n\kappa I_2,
\qquad
\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq (n\kappa)^{-1}. \tag{1}
\]

For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), put \(Q_e=Q(c_e)\) and write the endpoint loss as
\[
\ell_e(B)
=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q_e\right)
-n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q_e\Pi^{-1}\Omega B
\right]. \tag{2}
\]
Equation (1) and the continuity of matrix multiplication, inversion on the positive-definite cone, and trace show that both functions \(\ell_L\) and \(\ell_U\) are continuous on \(K_\kappa\).  Consequently
\[
w(B)=\max\{\ell_L(B),\ell_U(B)\} \tag{3}
\]
is continuous.  The expressions in (2) are unchanged by independently changing the signs of the two normalized columns, so (3) descends to a continuous function on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  Compactness therefore gives an attained minimum \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and a nonempty compact minimizer set \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).

We next establish the finite and effective part of the claim.  The finitely many signed pivot charts in the stated procedure cover both normalized projective factors: on a vector of squared norm \(n\), at least one coordinate has absolute value at least \(1\), and hence at least \(n^{-1/2}\).  On each closed chart box, the moment-complete input gives outward-rounded enclosures of every coefficient in (2), effective refinement moduli, and an exact decision whether the box meets the sphere constraints and the inequality defining \(K_\kappa\), including equality in that inequality.  Because \(\kappa>0\), continuity of \(S_B\), compactness, and (1) imply that sufficiently small boxes meeting \(K_\kappa\) admit a certified neighborhood bound
\[
S_B\succeq \frac{n\kappa}{2}I_2 \tag{4}
\]
throughout the box.  Thus ordinary interval evaluation of the two-by-two inverse is valid on every such box.  The effective refinement moduli, together with (4), imply the following effective uniform statement: for every positive rational \(\alpha\), after finitely many bisections and finitely much coefficient-oracle refinement, each chart box \(C\) that meets \(K_\kappa\) has outward-rounded rational intervals
\[
I_e(C)=[\underline\ell_{e,C},\overline\ell_{e,C}],
\qquad
\{\ell_e(B):B\in C\cap K_\kappa\}\subseteq I_e(C),
\qquad
\operatorname{wid}\{I_e(C)\}\leq\alpha,
\tag{5}
\]
for \(e\in\{L,U\}\).  This is not an appeal to an uneffective compactness principle: for exact rational or real-algebraic input the requisite arithmetic bounds are computed exactly, while for inclusion-oracle input the required stopping depths are supplied by the effective refinement moduli in \(\mathfrak I_{\mathrm{cert}}\).  The positive lower bounds needed for division are eventually certified because exposure positivity and \(\kappa>0\) separate the finitely many relevant denominators from zero.

Taking componentwise maxima in (5) gives
\[
I_w(C)=[\underline w_C,\overline w_C]
=\left[
\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}\underline\ell_{e,C},
\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}\overline\ell_{e,C}
\right],
\quad
\{w(B):B\in C\cap K_\kappa\}\subseteq I_w(C),
\quad
\overline w_C-\underline w_C\leq\alpha. \tag{6}
\]
The last inequality follows from
\(\max_e\overline\ell_{e,C}\leq
\max_e(\underline\ell_{e,C}+\alpha)
=\max_e\underline\ell_{e,C}+\alpha\).

There is also an exact feasible point name for every box \(C\) declared feasible.  Starting from \(C\), bisect and use the exact feasibility decision to select, in a fixed order, a child that still meets \(K_\kappa\).  This produces nested nonempty compact sets \(C_j\cap K_\kappa\) whose diameters tend to zero.  Their intersection is a singleton \(\{B_C\}\) with \(B_C\in K_\kappa\).  The finite rule that returns the successive boxes on request is a certified inclusion-oracle name for \(B_C\), exactly of the kind authorized in \(\mathscr K_\varepsilon\).  This argument does not require relative interior and applies unchanged when every feasible point satisfies
\[
\lambda_{\min}(n^{-1}S_B)=\kappa. \tag{7}
\]

Fix the exactly encoded \(\varepsilon>0\), and effectively choose a positive rational \(\alpha\) such that \(4\alpha<\varepsilon\).  Refine the finite chart cover until (5) holds and every surviving box has chart diameter at most \(\varepsilon\).  Exact feasibility deletes precisely the boxes disjoint from \(K_\kappa\).  Let \(\mathcal P\) be the resulting finite family of nonempty boxes and construct \(B_C\) for every \(C\in\mathcal P\).  Choose
\[
C_0\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{C\in\mathcal P}\overline w_C,
\qquad
B_\varepsilon=B_{C_0},
\qquad
U_\varepsilon=\overline w_{C_0},
\qquad
L_\varepsilon=\min_{C\in\mathcal P}\underline w_C. \tag{8}
\]
All comparisons and both bounds in (8) are rational and checkable.  If \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and \(C^\star\in\mathcal P\) contains a representative of \(B^\star\), validity of (6) gives
\[
L_\varepsilon\leq w(B^\star)=v_{\mathrm{mm}}
\leq w(B_\varepsilon)\leq U_\varepsilon. \tag{9}
\]
If \(C_-\) attains the minimum defining \(L_\varepsilon\), then (6) and \(B_{C_-}\in C_-\cap K_\kappa\) imply
\[
v_{\mathrm{mm}}leq w(B_{C_-})
\leq\overline w_{C_-}
\leq L_\varepsilon+\alpha.
\tag{10}
\]
Likewise, since \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\in I_w(C^\star)\),
\[
U_\varepsilon\leq\overline w_{C^\star}
\leq v_{\mathrm{mm}}+\alpha. \tag{11}
\]
Combining (10) and (11) yields
\[
U_\varepsilon-L_\varepsilon\leq2\alpha<\varepsilon. \tag{12}
\]

It remains to verify the outer-set certificate and the boundary rule.  At any stage, a valid box lower bound cannot exceed \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\) on a box containing a minimizer, whereas every incumbent upper bound is at least \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Therefore a box containing a minimizer is never removed by the strict pruning test
\(\underline w_C>U_\varepsilon\).  It is not removed by exact infeasibility either.  Hence the union \(\mathcal C_\varepsilon\) of unpruned quotient-chart boxes satisfies
\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\subseteq\mathcal C_\varepsilon. \tag{13}
\]
Every retained box has diameter at most \(\varepsilon\), so
\[
h_\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon. \tag{14}
\]
In particular, a box meeting \(K_\kappa\) only on the equality boundary (7) is declared feasible by the exact decision oracle and cannot be discarded as infeasible; it can be discarded only through the valid objective-lower-bound test.  Equations (8)--(14), together with the recorded chart cover, interval calculations, point names, and pruning decisions, form the checkable certificate \(\mathscr K_\varepsilon\).

All required chart depths and arithmetic precisions are finite for the fixed input and are eventually reached by the stated refinement rule.  Thus its stopping tests are satisfied after finitely many subdivisions.  Finally, for any sequence \(\varepsilon_j\downarrow0\), the certificate inequalities give
\[
0\leq U_{\varepsilon_j}-L_{\varepsilon_j}\leq\varepsilon_j\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
0\leq h_{\varepsilon_j}\leq\varepsilon_j\longrightarrow0.
\]
The proof depends on the instance-specific refinement moduli and supplies neither a uniform subdivision bound nor a semidefinite-program reduction. \(\square\)
