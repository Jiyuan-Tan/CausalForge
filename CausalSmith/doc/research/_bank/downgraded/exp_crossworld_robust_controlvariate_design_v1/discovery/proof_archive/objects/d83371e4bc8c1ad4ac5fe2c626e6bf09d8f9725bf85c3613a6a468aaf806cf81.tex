\paragraph{Proof.}
Write \(J_3=\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}\) and
\[
K=\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Because \(f_i(W)=W_i\), the two exposure indicators are \(D_{i,1}=W_i\) and \(D_{i,0}=1-W_i\).  Independence and \(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(W_i)=1/4\) therefore give, entry by entry,
\[
\Pi=\tfrac12 I_6,
\qquad
\Omega=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(D)=\tfrac14 K.
\]
Exposure positivity holds because every diagonal entry of \(\Pi\) is \(1/2\).

We first verify that the regularized basis class is nonempty.  Set
\[
b_1=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\qquad
b_0=\sqrt{\tfrac32}\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\end{bmatrix}^{\top}.
\]
Then \(\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=3\) and \(b_1^{\top}b_0=0\).  For the associated block-separable basis,
\[
S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B
=\tfrac14
\begin{bmatrix}
3&-b_1^{\top}b_0\\
-b_0^{\top}b_1&3
\end{bmatrix}
=\tfrac34 I_2.
\]
Hence \(\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_B)=1/4\ge1/8=\kappa\).  This proves the required feasibility rather than assuming it.

For \((F_1,F_0)=(\delta_0,\delta_0)\), all four marginal means and variances vanish.  The quantile formulas give \(c_L=c_U=0\), and the moment-matrix definition gives \(Q(0)=0\).  Therefore \(\ell_B(0)=0\) for every \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), so
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)=0.
\]

Now take \((F_1,F_0)=(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),\delta_0)\).  Since the comparison potential outcome is constant, every coupling has covariance zero; equivalently, direct evaluation of both quantile integrals gives \(c_L=c_U=0\).  Here
\[
\mu_1=\tfrac12,
\quad \sigma_1^2=\tfrac14,
\quad \mu_0=\sigma_0^2=0,
\quad
Q(0)=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A=\tfrac14(I_3+J_3).
\]
Thus the two marginal pairs have the same displayed moment-incomplete tuple, although their endpoint moment matrices are different.

It remains to calculate the second minimax residual exactly.  Let \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\) be arbitrary, write \(x=b_1\), \(y=b_0\), and put \(C=[x,-y]\).  Projective normalization gives \(\lVert x\rVert_2^2=\lVert y\rVert_2^2=3\).  Moreover, \(\kappa\)-regularity makes \(S_B\) positive definite, so \(C\) has rank two.  Direct block multiplication, using \(\Pi^{-1}=2I_6\) and \(\Omega=K/4\), yields
\[
S_B=\tfrac14 C^{\top}C,
\qquad
B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\Pi^{-1}\Omega B
=\tfrac14 C^{\top}AC.
\]
Consequently, if \(P_C=C(C^{\top}C)^{-1}C^{\top}\) denotes the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{col}(C)\), cyclicity of trace gives
\[
g_B(0)
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(C^{\top}C)^{-1}C^{\top}AC\right]
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}(P_CA).
\]
The basis-free term in the residual is
\[
h(0)
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(0)\right]
=\tfrac19\operatorname{tr}(A)
=\tfrac16.
\]
Let \(u=3^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\).  Since \(J_3=3uu^{\top}\),
\[
A=\tfrac14 I_3+\tfrac34 uu^{\top}.
\]
The projector \(P_C\) has rank two, and hence
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P_CA)
=\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}(P_C)+\tfrac34u^{\top}P_Cu
=\tfrac12+\tfrac34\lVert P_Cu\rVert_2^2
\le\tfrac54.
\]
It follows for every feasible basis that
\[
\ell_B(0)=h(0)-g_B(0)
\ge \tfrac16-\tfrac19\cdot\tfrac54
=\tfrac1{36}.
\]
The previously displayed feasible choice has \(b_1=\sqrt3u\) and \(b_0\perp u\), so \(u\in\operatorname{col}(C)\), \(\lVert P_Cu\rVert_2=1\), and equality holds throughout.  Because the covariance interval is the singleton \(\{0\}\), \(w(B)=\ell_B(0)\), proving
\[
\min_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}w(B)=\tfrac1{36}.
\]

Finally, an algorithm receiving only the displayed moment-incomplete tuple must return the same \((L_{\varepsilon},U_{\varepsilon})\) on these two instances.  Validity on both would force \([L_{\varepsilon},U_{\varepsilon}]\) to contain both \(0\) and \(1/36\), so
\[
U_{\varepsilon}-L_{\varepsilon}\ge\tfrac1{36}>\tfrac1{100}=\varepsilon,
\]
contradicting the required tolerance.  The contradiction concerns only omission of the endpoint moment matrices and therefore does not establish termination of the repaired, moment-complete solver. \(\square\)
