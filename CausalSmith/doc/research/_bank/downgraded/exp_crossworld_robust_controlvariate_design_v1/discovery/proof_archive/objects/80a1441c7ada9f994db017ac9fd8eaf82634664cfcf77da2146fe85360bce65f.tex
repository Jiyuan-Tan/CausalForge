\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(K_\kappa\) be the set of pairs \((b_1,b_0)\) satisfying
\[
 \lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=n,
 \qquad B^{\top}\Omega B\succeq n\kappa I_2.
\]
It is closed in \(\sqrt n\mathbb S^{n-1}\times\sqrt n\mathbb S^{n-1}\), hence compact.  Passing to the residual independent sign quotient preserves compactness and gives \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  Every member has \(B^{\top}\Omega B\succ0\), so \(\mathcal B_\kappa\subseteq\mathcal B_Z\).

Conversely, take \([B]\in\mathcal B_Z\).  Positive definiteness forces both arm columns to be nonzero.  With
\[
 T=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt n}{\lVert b_1\rVert_2},
 \frac{\sqrt n}{\lVert b_0\rVert_2}\right),
\]
the representative \(BT\) is normalized and \(T^{\top}B^{\top}\Omega BT\succ0\).  Therefore
\[
 \rho_B=\lambda_{\min}\{n^{-1}T^{\top}B^{\top}\Omega BT\}>0,
\]
and \([B]\in\mathcal B_{\rho_B}\).  Hence \(\bigcup_{\kappa>0}\mathcal B_\kappa=\mathcal B_Z\).  Surjectivity and the exact fibers of \(q_Z\), supplied by thm:zhao-ordinary-domain-quotient-transfer, imply
\[
q_Z^{-1}\!\left(\bigcup_{\kappa>0}\mathcal B_\kappa\right)
=q_Z^{-1}(\mathcal B_Z)=\widetilde{\mathcal B}_Z.
\]
This argument uses positive definiteness throughout and therefore includes no singular or Moore--Penrose class. \(\square\)
