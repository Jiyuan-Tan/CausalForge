\textbf{Proof.}
Assumption ass:kappa-feasibility makes \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) nonempty, and prop:kappa-exhaustion makes it compact.  Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  On this class,
\[
 S_B\succeq n\kappa I_2,
 \qquad \lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
\]
Therefore \(B\mapsto g_B(0)\), the two endpoint losses, and their maximum \(w(B)\) are continuous on normalized representatives and invariant under the residual sign quotient.  The extreme-value theorem makes both \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and \(\mathcal A_0\) nonempty.  For arbitrary \(B_{\mathrm{mm}}\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and \(B_0\in\mathcal A_0\), the defining minimization gives
\[
 w(B_{\mathrm{mm}})=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\le w(B_0).
\]
Taking the displayed supremum and infimum proves the certificate.

Strictness is not implied.  For any lawful design, take \(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\).  Then \(Q(0)=0\), so \(g_B(0)=\ell_B(0)=w(B)=0\) for every feasible \(B\), and equality holds.

The decision variable here differs from the in-repository results \(\texttt{exp\_bipartite\_minimax\_design\_v1/thm:hetero-envelope}\) and \(\texttt{exp\_bipartite\_minimax\_design\_v1/thm:convex-design}\): those choose heterogeneous assignment probabilities for a graph-only conservative H\'ajek envelope, whereas this theorem fixes \(\nu\) and chooses a control-variate span against cross-world copula ambiguity.  This comparison supplies provenance and is not used in the inequalities above. \(\square\)
