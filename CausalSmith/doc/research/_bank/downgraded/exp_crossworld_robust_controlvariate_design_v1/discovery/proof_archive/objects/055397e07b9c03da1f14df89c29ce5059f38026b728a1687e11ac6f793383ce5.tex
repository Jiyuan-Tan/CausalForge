\textbf{Proof.}
Work with normalized representatives of the projective classes in \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  By prop:kappa-exhaustion and ass:kappa-feasibility, the quotient is a nonempty compact metrizable space.  The two columns of every normalized representative have disjoint block supports and squared Euclidean norm \(n\), while the defining \(\kappa\)-regularity inequality gives
\[
 \lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 \lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le (n\kappa)^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  The matrices \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\) are fixed because the network, exposure maps, and assignment law are fixed.

Write \(q_a=F_a^{-1}\) and \(\widehat q_a=\widehat F_a^{-1}\).  We first establish convergence of every pilot ingredient.  If \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\) are independent, centered, and bounded in absolute value by one, expansion of the fourth moment and independence give
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^m X_j\right)^4
 =\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb E X_j^4
   +6\sum_{1\le i<j\le m}\mathbb E X_i^2\,\mathbb E X_j^2
 \le 3m^2.
 \tag{2}
\]
For every \(t>0\), Markov's inequality therefore bounds the probability that the empirical average has absolute value greater than \(t\) by \(3/(m^2t^4)\).  Summability and the Borel--Cantelli lemma prove the bounded strong law.  Apply this argument in each pilot arm to the outcomes, their squares, and, simultaneously over rational \(x\), the indicators \(\mathbf 1\{Y^P_{j,a}\le x\}\).  Monotonicity of distribution functions then gives \(\widehat F_a(x)\to F_a(x)\) at every continuity point of \(F_a\).  The generalized-inverse squeeze consequently gives \(\widehat q_a(u)\to q_a(u)\) at every continuity point of \(q_a\).  Since a monotone function has at most countably many discontinuities and all these quantiles lie in \([0,1]\), dominated convergence yields
\[
 \lVert\widehat q_a-q_a\rVert_{L^1(0,1)}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \widehat\mu_a\longrightarrow\mu_a,
 \qquad
 \widehat\sigma_a^2\longrightarrow\sigma_a^2
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The two armwise probability-one events may be intersected because there are only two arms; ass:unpaired-pilot supplies the required i.i.d. sequences.  Independence from \(W\) is compatible with, and keeps separate, the pilot probability-one event and the later design randomization.

Because all quantiles are bounded by one,
\[
 \left|
  \int_0^1\widehat q_1(u)\widehat q_0(u)\,du
  -\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du
 \right|
 \le
 \lVert\widehat q_1-q_1\rVert_1
 +\lVert\widehat q_0-q_0\rVert_1.
 \tag{4}
\]
The same bound holds with the second quantile evaluated at \(1-u\).  Thus the quantile definitions of the Fr\'echet endpoints imply
\[
 \widehat c_L\longrightarrow c_L,
 \qquad
 \widehat c_U\longrightarrow c_U
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Assumption ass:frechet-covariance-domain ensures that these are exactly the two sharp feasible-covariance endpoints used by the population worst-copula criterion.  For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), set \(Q_e=Q(c_e)\), and let \(\widehat Q_e\) denote the matrix obtained from the corresponding empirical moments and empirical endpoint.  Equations (3)--(5) and the finite-dimensional formula in def:moment-matrix imply
\[
 \rho_m:=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 \lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely as }m_1\wedge m_0\to\infty.
 \tag{6}
\]

Put \(C_\nu=\lVert\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\).  For a \(d\)-dimensional square matrix \(M\), the elementary singular-value inequality \(|\operatorname{tr}(M)|\le d\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\), together with def:gain-loss and (1), gives uniformly over \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\)
\[
 \max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 |\widehat g_B(\widehat c_e)-g_B(c_e)|
 \le \frac{2C_\nu^2}{n^2\kappa}\rho_m,
 \tag{7}
\]
because the trace in the gain is two-dimensional.  The analogous \(2n\)-dimensional trace bound gives
\[
 \max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 |\widehat h(\widehat c_e)-h(c_e)|
 \le \frac{2}{n}\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\rho_m.
 \tag{8}
\]
Subtracting the gain from the uncontrolled variance, as prescribed by def:gain-loss, bounds the two endpoint residual errors by the sum of the right sides of (7) and (8), uniformly in \(B\).  The maximum of two real coordinates is one-Lipschitz for the sup norm.  Hence
\[
 \delta_m:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}
 |\widehat w(B)-w(B)|longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{9}
\]

For each endpoint, the loss is continuous on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\): all matrix operations are continuous, and (1) keeps inversion uniformly away from singularity.  Therefore \(w\) and \(\widehat w\) are continuous and attain minima on the nonempty compact quotient.  The elementary inequality comparing minima of two uniformly close functions gives
\[
 |\widehat v_{\mathrm{mm}}-v_{\mathrm{mm}}|
 \le\delta_m\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{10}
\]

It remains to prove the outer correspondence statement.  Take any sequence \(B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and any convergent subsequence \(B_{m_j}\to B_\infty\); compactness ensures that subsequences of this kind exist.  For an arbitrary \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), empirical optimality and (9) imply
\[
 w(B_{m_j})
 \le \widehat w(B_{m_j})+\delta_{m_j}
 \le \widehat w(B^\star)+\delta_{m_j}
 \le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\delta_{m_j}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Continuity of \(w\), (9), and the defining inequality \(w\ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}\) now yield \(w(B_\infty)=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Thus \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), which is exactly
\[
 \limsup_{m_1,m_0\to\infty}\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}
 \subseteq\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}
\]
in the projective quotient. \(\square\)
