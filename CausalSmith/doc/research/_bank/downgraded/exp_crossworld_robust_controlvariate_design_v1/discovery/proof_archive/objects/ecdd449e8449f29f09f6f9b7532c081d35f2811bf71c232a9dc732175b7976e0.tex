\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(m=m_1\wedge m_0\).  For probability laws \(P,Q\) on \([0,1]\), the one-dimensional identity \(W_1(P,Q)=\int_0^1|P^{-1}(u)-Q^{-1}(u)|\,du\) and the bound \(W_1(P,Q)\le\sup_y|P(y)-Q(y)|\) apply.  The Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz inequality, integrated over its tail, gives
\[
\mathbb E W_1(\widehat F_a,F_a)
\le\mathbb E\sup_y|\widehat F_a(y)-F_a(y)|
\le C m_a^{-1/2} \tag{1}
\]
for a universal finite \(C\).  Because \(0\le y\le1\), the empirical mean and second-moment errors have the same \(O_{L^1}(m_a^{-1/2})\) rate.

For the upper endpoint, \(|xy-x'y'|\le|x-x'|+|y-y'|\) on \([0,1]^2\), so
\[
\left|\int_0^1\widehat F_1^{-1}(u)\widehat F_0^{-1}(u)\,du
-\int_0^1F_1^{-1}(u)F_0^{-1}(u)\,du\right|
\le W_1(\widehat F_1,F_1)+W_1(\widehat F_0,F_0). \tag{2}
\]
Replacing the second quantile by its reflection \(u\mapsto1-u\) proves the identical lower-endpoint bound.  Equations (1)--(2), together with the empirical mean product, show
\[
|\widehat c_e-c_e|+\sum_{a\in\{1,0\}}
(|\widehat\mu_a-\mu_a|+|\widehat\sigma_a^2-\sigma_a^2|)
=O_{L^1}(m^{-1/2}). \tag{3}
\]
Each block of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) is a scalar from (3) times either \(I_n\) or \(\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\).  Since their operator norms are one and \(n\), respectively,
\[
\max_e\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_{L^1}(nm^{-1/2}). \tag{4}
\]

Let
\[
A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1},
\qquad
A_B=\Pi^{-1}\Omega B(B^{\top}\Omega B)^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
Writing \(Z=\Omega^{1/2}B\), the middle factor
\(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top}\) is an orthogonal projector.  Therefore
\[
0\preceq A_B
=\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}
Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top}
\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}
\preceq A_{\mathrm{HT}}. \tag{5}
\]
Both matrices are positive semidefinite, so their nuclear norms are their traces and (5) gives \(\lVert A_B\rVert_*\le\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_*\).  The dependency-neighborhood condition makes each row of \(\Omega\) have at most \(\overline d n^\alpha\) possibly nonzero entries, each of absolute value at most one.  The positivity rate gives \(\lVert\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\underline\pi^{-1}n^\beta\).  Hence
\[
\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_*
\le2n\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le2\overline d\,\underline\pi^{-2}n^{1+\alpha+2\beta},
\]
which proves \(C_n=O(n^{\alpha+2\beta-1})\).

For \(\Delta Q_e=\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\), trace duality gives
\[
|\widehat h_e-h_e|\le n^{-2}\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_*\lVert\Delta Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
\quad
|\widehat g_B(c_e)-g_B(c_e)|
\le n^{-2}\lVert A_B\rVert_*\lVert\Delta Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Their sum and the one-Lipschitz property of the maximum prove the uniform loss inequality.  The gain inequality is its second term.  Multiplication by \(n\) proves the exact sensitivity implication.  Finally, (4) and the bound on \(C_n\) give
\[
nC_n\max_e\lVert\Delta Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_{L^1}\!\left(\frac{n^{1+\alpha+2\beta}}{\sqrt m}\right),
\]
which tends to zero under the displayed sufficient pilot growth. \(\square\)
