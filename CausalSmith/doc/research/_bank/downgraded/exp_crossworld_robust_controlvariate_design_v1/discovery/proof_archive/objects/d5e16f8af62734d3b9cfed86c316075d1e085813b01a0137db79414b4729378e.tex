Rare network exposures make the Horvitz--Thompson exposure-effect estimator noisy, while an ex ante control-variate basis depends on a cross-world covariance that an unpaired pilot does not identify.  The paper imports the sharp Fr\'echet covariance interval, proves that every fixed-basis gain and residual are affine in that covariance, solves the worst-copula problem at the two endpoints, and develops both fixed-\(\kappa\) certified optimization and an all-rank pseudoinverse analysis on the note's quotient of Zhao's published carrier.  A triangle witness gives the exact strict worst-loss reduction \(4912/39525\) and a relative-open persistence result.  For the full carrier, the repaired pilot rule is an explicit first-qualifying element of a fixed countable positive-definite rank-two chart enumeration; when \(\mathcal B_Z\ne\varnothing\), its empirical infimum equals the all-rank empirical infimum, the selector is measurable for every finite \(\varepsilon_m>0\), and its objective regret obeys the stated almost-sure and first-order rates.  No singular-rank feasible-estimator variance bridge is claimed.
