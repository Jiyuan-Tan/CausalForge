\textbf{Proof.}
Work on the probability-one event supplied by \(\texttt{thm:plugin-correspondence}\).  Suppose first that \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}=\{B^\star\}\).  Let \(\widehat B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) be any measurable selection along sample sizes with \(m_1\wedge m_0\to\infty\).  If \(\widehat B_m\) did not converge to \(B^\star\), some subsequence would remain outside an open neighborhood of \(B^\star\).  Compactness of \(\mathcal B_\kappa\), from \(\texttt{prop:kappa-exhaustion}\) as used in the theorem, would give a convergent further subsequence.  The outer-limit conclusion of \(\texttt{thm:plugin-correspondence}\) forces its limit to lie in \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), hence to equal \(B^\star\), contradicting that it remains outside the neighborhood.  Therefore every such selection converges almost surely to \(B^\star\).

For the failure without uniqueness, consider \(n=3\), take an empty fixed network with exposure map \(f_i(w)=w_i\), and assign the three units independently with probability \(1/2\).  Let every assignment-indexed potential outcome be zero, take
\(F_1=F_0=\delta_0\), and let the independent unpaired pilot observations also be identically zero.  Known exposure holds, both exposure probabilities equal \(1/2\), the outcomes are bounded, the pilot law is independent of the main assignment, and the unique fixed-marginal coupling is concentrated at \((0,0)\).  Direct calculation gives
\[
\Pi={1\over2}I_6,
\qquad
\Omega={1\over4}
\begin{bmatrix}I_3&-I_3\\-I_3&I_3\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
Q(0)=\widehat Q(0)=0.
\tag{16}
\]
Hence \(c_L=c_U=0\) and
\[
w(B)=\widehat w(B)=0
\qquad\text{for every }B\in\mathcal B_\kappa.
\tag{17}
\]
Set \(\kappa=1/8\) and define the two normalized block-separable representatives
\[
B^{(2)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_2),
\qquad
B^{(3)}=B(\sqrt3e_1,\sqrt3e_3).
\tag{18}
\]
For each one, the two selected coordinate directions are distinct, so the off-diagonal entry of \(B^{\top}\Omega B\) is zero, while both diagonal entries equal \(3/4\).  Therefore
\[
S_{B^{(2)}}=S_{B^{(3)}}={3\over4}I_2,
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(3^{-1}S_{B^{(j)}})={1\over4}>{1\over8}.
\tag{19}
\]
Both bases are feasible, and their comparison-arm projective directions are different, so they are distinct points of the quotient.  By (17), both belong to every empirical argmin and to the population argmin.  Along the diagonal pilot-size sequence \(m_1=m_0=m\), choose \(B^{(2)}\) for even \(m\) and \(B^{(3)}\) for odd \(m\).  This selection is deterministic, hence pilot-measurable, and is always an exact plug-in minimizer, but it has the two distinct subsequential limits in (18) and therefore no point limit.  Meanwhile the outer-set conclusion holds exactly because both the empirical and population argmin sets equal \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  This proves that the singleton condition cannot in general be discarded. \(\square\)
