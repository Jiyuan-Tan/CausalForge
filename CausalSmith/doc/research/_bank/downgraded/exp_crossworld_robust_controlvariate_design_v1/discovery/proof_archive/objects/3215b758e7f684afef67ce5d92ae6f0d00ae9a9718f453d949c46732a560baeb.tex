\textbf{Proof.}
Write \(F_{a,m}\) for the empirical distribution function in arm \(a\), and \(q_{a,m}=\widehat F_a^{-1}\), \(q_a=F_a^{-1}\).  We first prove the almost-sure convergence needed below directly from boundedness and independence.

Fix an arm \(a\) and a rational \(t\in[0,1]\).  Put
\[
X_j=\mathbf1\{Y^P_{j,a}\le t\}-F_a(t).
\]
The variables \(X_j\) are independent, centered, and bounded by one.  Expanding the fourth power and using independence and centering gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\right)^4
=\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb E_PX_j^4
+6\sum_{1\le i<j\le m}
\mathbb E_PX_i^2\mathbb E_PX_j^2
\le4m^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
P\!\left(\left|F_{a,m}(t)-F_a(t)\right|>\epsilon\right)
\le4\epsilon^{-4}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-2}<\infty.
\tag{2}
\]
The probability of the union of the events in (2) over \(m\ge M\) is bounded by the corresponding tail sum, which tends to zero.  Thus \(F_{a,m}(t)\to F_a(t)\) almost surely.  Intersecting these probability-one events over the two arms and countably many rational \(t\), and then using monotonicity to squeeze between rational points, gives
\[
F_{a,m}(t)\longrightarrow F_a(t)
\quad\text{at every continuity point of }F_a,quad a\in\{1,0\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Every distribution function has at most countably many discontinuities.  Since the integrands are bounded by one, (3) and dominated convergence imply
\[
\int_0^1|F_{a,m}(t)-F_a(t)|\,dt\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{4}
\]

For completeness, the one-dimensional quantile identity needed here follows by putting the two quantiles on the same auxiliary uniform variable \(U\).  The threshold events for \(q_{a,m}(U)\) and \(q_a(U)\) are nested intervals of lengths \(F_{a,m}(t)\) and \(F_a(t)\); integrating the measure of their symmetric difference over \(t\) gives
\[
\int_0^1|q_{a,m}(u)-q_a(u)|\,du
=\int_0^1|F_{a,m}(t)-F_a(t)|\,dt.
\tag{5}
\]
Hence the left side of (5) tends to zero almost surely.  Because all quantiles take values in \([0,1]\),
\[
\left|\int_0^1q_{a,m}(u)^k\,du
-\int_0^1q_a(u)^k\,du\right|
\le k\int_0^1|q_{a,m}(u)-q_a(u)|\,du
\longrightarrow0
\tag{6}
\]
for \(k=1,2\).  Thus the empirical means and variances converge almost surely to \(\mu_a\) and \(\sigma_a^2\).

For either the comonotone sign or the countermonotone sign, boundedness yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|\int_0^1q_{1,m}(u)q_{0,m}(u)\,du
-\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du\right|\\
&\qquad\le
\int_0^1|q_{1,m}(u)-q_1(u)|\,du
+\int_0^1|q_{0,m}(u)-q_0(u)|\,du,
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
and the same bound holds after replacing every second-arm argument \(u\) by \(1-u\).  Equations (5)--(7) therefore show almost-sure convergence of both empirical Fr\'echet covariance endpoints.

Every entry of \(\widehat Q_e\) is a polynomial in these empirical means, variances, and endpoint covariances, while the corresponding entry of \(Q(c_e)\) is the same polynomial in their limits.  Since \(n\) is fixed, entrywise convergence implies convergence in Frobenius norm and hence in operator norm.  Taking the maximum over the two endpoints proves \(M_m\to0\) almost surely under \(P\). \(\square\)
