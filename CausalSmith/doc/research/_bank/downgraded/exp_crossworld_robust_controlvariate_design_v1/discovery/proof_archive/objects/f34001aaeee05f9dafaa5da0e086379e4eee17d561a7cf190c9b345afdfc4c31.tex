\textbf{Proof.}
Decompose \(\mathbb R^3=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\oplus\mathbf1^\perp\).  Multiplication by \(729\) shows that \(\Omega\) restricts on the mean component to
\[
 M_{\parallel}=\begin{bmatrix}60&96\\96&186\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det(M_{\parallel})=1944>0,
\]
and on each copy of the orthogonal component to
\[
 M_{\perp}=\begin{bmatrix}108&-108\\-108&162\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det(M_{\perp})=5832>0.
\]
Both matrices have positive leading diagonal entry, hence both are positive definite.  Therefore \(\Omega\succ0\).  It follows that
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(S_B)
 =\operatorname{rank}(B^{\top}\Omega B)
 =\operatorname{rank}(B).
 \tag{19}
\]
For a block-separable basis, the two columns have disjoint supports.  Thus rank zero means both arm columns vanish, rank one means exactly one arm column is nonzero, and rank two means both are nonzero.  This is also the triangle specialization of lem:rank-stratified-projector-reduction.

On the focal-only stratum, normalize \(\lVert x\rVert_2^2=3\) and set \(u=(\mathbf1^{\top}x)^2\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(0\le u\le9\).  The upper-left entries of the exact matrices in equations (2), (6), and (7) of the proof of thm:triangle-minimax-separation-certificate give
\[
 S_x=\frac49-\frac{16u}{729},
\]
\[
 g_{(x,0)}^{+}(c_L)
 =\frac{1195u+6561}{1200(81-4u)},
 \qquad
 g_{(x,0)}^{+}(c_U)
 =\frac{243(49-5u)}{400(81-4u)}.
 \tag{20}
\]
The denominator \(81-4u\) is at least \(45>0\), so every division is valid.  Since zero is the midpoint of the two covariance endpoints, averaging (20) yields
\[
 g_{(x,0)}^{+}(0)
 =\frac{21141-1225u}{1200(81-4u)}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Its derivative is
\[
 -\frac{4887}{400(4u-81)^2}<0,
\]
so its maximum on \([0,9]\) is \(87/400\), attained at \(u=0\).  Using \(h(c_L)=313/600\) and \(h(c_U)=171/200\), direct subtraction gives
\[
 \{h(c_U)-g_{(x,0)}^{+}(c_U)\}
 -\{h(c_L)-g_{(x,0)}^{+}(c_L)\}
 =\frac{27(u+1)}{10(81-4u)}>0.
\]
Hence the upper-endpoint loss is always active and
\[
 w^{+}(x,0)=\frac{9(1755-17u)}{400(81-4u)}.
 \tag{22}
\]
Its derivative is positive, so its minimum is \(39/80\), again at \(u=0\).

On the comparison-only stratum, normalize \(\lVert y\rVert_2^2=3\) and set \(v=(\mathbf1^{\top}y)^2\in[0,9]\).  The corresponding lower-right entries give
\[
 g_{(0,y)}^{+}(c_L)
 =\frac{1240v+729}{150(243+4v)},
 \qquad
 g_{(0,y)}^{+}(c_U)
 =\frac{81(48-5v)}{50(243+4v)}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Thus
\[
 g_{(0,y)}^{+}(0)
 =\frac{25v+12393}{300(4v+243)}.
 \tag{24}
\]
Its derivative is \(-14499/\{100(4v+243)^2\}<0\), so its maximum is \(17/100\) at \(v=0\).  Moreover,
\[
 \{h(c_U)-g_{(0,y)}^{+}(c_U)\}
 -\{h(c_L)-g_{(0,y)}^{+}(c_L)\}
 =\frac{3(59v+27)}{10(4v+243)}>0,
\]
and hence
\[
 w^{+}(0,y)=\frac{9(256v+2889)}{200(4v+243)}.
 \tag{25}
\]
This function is increasing, so its minimum is \(107/200\) at \(v=0\).

On the rank-zero stratum, the Moore--Penrose gain is zero.  Equation (9) of the triangle proof therefore gives
\[
 g_0^{+}(0)=0,
 \qquad
 w^{+}(0)=\max\left\{\frac{313}{600},\frac{171}{200}\right\}=\frac{171}{200}.
\]
The exact comparisons with the rank-two minimax value are
\[
 \frac{39}{80}-\frac{2613}{8432}=\frac{468}{2635}>0,
 \qquad
 \frac{107}{200}-\frac{2613}{8432}=\frac{47453}{210800}>0,
\]
\[
 \frac{171}{200}-\frac{2613}{8432}=\frac{114909}{210800}>0.
\]
Also \(87/400<31/80\), \(17/100<31/80\), and \(0<31/80\).  These strict rational inequalities prove that neither singular stratum contains a zero-covariance maximizer or a minimax-loss minimizer. \(\square\)
