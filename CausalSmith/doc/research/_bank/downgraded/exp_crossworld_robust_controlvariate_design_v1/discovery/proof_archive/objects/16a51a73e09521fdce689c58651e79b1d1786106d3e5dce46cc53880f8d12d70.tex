\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(K_0\) be the ordered field generated over \(\mathbb Q\) by the finitely many entries of \(\Pi\), \(\Omega\), \(Q(c_L)\), and \(Q(c_U)\), together with the exactly encoded input constants.  The input hypothesis makes \(K_0\) a field of real-algebraic numbers.  By ass:exposure-positivity, every diagonal entry of \(\Pi\) is strictly positive.  Hence \(\Pi^{-1}\) exists and all of its entries also belong to \(K_0\).

First remove the harmless projective redundancy.  By def:zhao-published-pseudoinverse-domain, every class has a representative for which each arm column is either zero or has squared Euclidean norm \(n\).  Thus it suffices to work on
\[
 \mathcal K
 =\left\{(b_1,b_0):
   \bigl(b_1=0\ \vee\ b_1^{\top}b_1=n\bigr),\quad
   \bigl(b_0=0\ \vee\ b_0^{\top}b_0=n\bigr)
  \right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is a union of four compact basic semialgebraic sets over \(K_0\), and its image under the quotient map is all of \(\mathcal B_Z^+\).  The loss is constant on its quotient fibers.  Indeed, put \(C=\Omega^{1/2}B\), and, for \(e\in\{L,U\}\), put
\[
 R_e=\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}Q(c_e)\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}.
\]
Since \(S_B=C^{\top}C\), cyclicity of trace gives
\[
 g_B^+(c_e)
 =n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
 C(C^{\top}C)^+C^{\top}R_e
 \right].
\tag{2}
\]
The matrix \(C(C^{\top}C)^+C^{\top}\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{col}(C)\): this follows directly from a singular-value decomposition of \(C\).  Independent nonzero rescaling of the nonzero arm columns leaves this column space unchanged, so (2), and hence \(w^+(B)\), is invariant.  Moreover \(0\preceq C(C^{\top}C)^+C^{\top}\preceq I\) and \(R_e\succeq0\), whence
\[
 0\le g_B^+(c_e)\le h(c_e),
 \qquad
 0\le \ell_B^+(c_e)\le h(c_e).
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, the attainable losses are bounded below, and they are nonempty and finite because the zero representative belongs to \(\mathcal K\).

We now give exact formulas on every rank stratum.  Write \(S=S_B\).  It is a \(2\times2\) positive-semidefinite matrix.  Consequently its three possible ranks are described, without a limiting convention, by
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\operatorname{rank}(S)&\text{semialgebraic condition}&S^+\\ \hline
0&\operatorname{tr}(S)=0&0,\\[2mm]
1&\operatorname{tr}(S)>0,\ \det(S)=0&
\displaystyle\frac{S}{\{\operatorname{tr}(S)\}^2},\\[3mm]
2&\det(S)>0&
\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{adj}(S)}{\det(S)}.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]
For rank one, if the nonzero eigenvalue is \(\lambda=\operatorname{tr}(S)\), then \(S=\lambda uu^{\top}\) and the middle formula equals \(\lambda^{-1}uu^{\top}\).  The other two formulas are immediate.  The denominators in (4) are strictly positive on their stated strata.

For each row of (4), substitute its displayed rational expression into the two endpoint gains in def:completion-pseudoinverse-loss.  Introduce variables \(z_L,z_U,y\) for the two endpoint losses and their maximum, and impose
\[
 y\ge z_L,\qquad y\ge z_U,\qquad
 (y=z_L\ \vee\ y=z_U).
\tag{5}
\]
After multiplication by the positive denominator belonging to that row of (4), the gain equalities, the loss equalities, and (5) are polynomial sign conditions over \(K_0\).  Equations (1), (4), and (5) therefore give a finite-union semialgebraic graph
\[
 \mathcal G
 =\{(b_1,b_0,y):(b_1,b_0)\in\mathcal K,\ y=w^+(B)\}.
\tag{6}
\]
This representation covers ranks zero, one, and two, including every equality boundary; no inverse is evaluated where its denominator vanishes.

Consider the upper attainable-value set
\[
 \mathcal T
 =\left\{t\in\mathbb R:
   \exists (b_1,b_0,y)\in\mathcal G\ \text{such that}\ y\le t
  \right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
It is nonempty, bounded below by (3), and
\[
 \inf\mathcal T
 =\inf_{[B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+}w^+(B)
 =v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+.
\tag{8}
\]
The formula in (7) is first order over a real closed field.  Apply lem:real-closed-field-quantifier-elimination to eliminate the basis, loss, and auxiliary variables.  Its conclusion yields a quantifier-free description of \(\mathcal T\) by finitely many polynomial sign conditions over \(K_0\).  Every finite boundary point of a one-dimensional semialgebraic set so described is a zero of one of those finitely many polynomials.  By (3) and (8), \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\) is a finite boundary point of the nonempty upper set \(\mathcal T\).  It is therefore algebraic over \(K_0\), and hence is a real-algebraic number.

The corresponding CAD computation terminates.  Concretely, there are finitely many variables and finitely many input polynomials; every projection step forms only finitely many coefficients, discriminants, and resultants; exact real-algebraic root isolation splits the line into finitely many sign cells; and lifting repeats this finite operation through finitely many coordinates.  The equivalence guaranteed by lem:real-closed-field-quantifier-elimination certifies the resulting sign decomposition.  Exact root isolation at the boundary \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\) therefore returns rational or real-algebraic numbers \(L_\varepsilon^+\) and \(U_\varepsilon^+\) satisfying
\[
 L_\varepsilon^+\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\le U_\varepsilon^+,
 \qquad
 U_\varepsilon^+-L_\varepsilon^+\le\varepsilon.
\tag{9}
\]
The strict positivity and exact encoding of \(\varepsilon\) allow the final isolating interval to be refined until the second inequality in (9) is decided exactly.

It remains to produce an incumbent even when the infimum is not attained.  From the definition of the infimum and \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \exists [B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+
 \quad w^+(B)<v_{\mathrm{mm}}^++\varepsilon.
\tag{10}
\]
Adjoin the algebraic constant \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}^+\) to the coefficient field and run CAD lifting on the semialgebraic formula in (10), using (1), (4), and (5).  A nonempty semialgebraic set over real-algebraic coefficients has a CAD sample point with real-algebraic coordinates.  The returned normalized representative defines a published-class quotient basis \([B_\varepsilon]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+\), and (10) implies
\[
 w^+(B_\varepsilon)\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}^++\varepsilon.
\tag{11}
\]
The weak sublevel set in the theorem is described by the same formulas with \(y\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}^++\varepsilon\); it is semialgebraic and is nonempty by (10).

Finally, retain the finite normalized-support list, the rank condition and pseudoinverse formula from (4) on each cell, the CAD projection polynomials and sign table, the real-algebraic sample from (10), and the isolating data establishing (9).  Exact algebraic arithmetic checks every entry, so this is the asserted finite sign, sample, and value-isolation certificate covering all three ranks.  The construction uses only an \(\varepsilon\)-incumbent.  It neither requires an attained minimizer nor supplies outer convergence, a uniform complexity bound, or a semidefinite-program reduction. \(\square\)
