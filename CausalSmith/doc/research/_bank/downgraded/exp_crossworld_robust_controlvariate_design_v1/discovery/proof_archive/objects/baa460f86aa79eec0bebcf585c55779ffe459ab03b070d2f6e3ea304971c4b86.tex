\textbf{Proof.}
Work with the normalized representatives in def:kappa-basis-class.  By prop:kappa-exhaustion and ass:kappa-feasibility, \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is a nonempty compact metrizable quotient.  Every representative has two orthogonal block columns of norm \(\sqrt n\), while \(\kappa\)-regularity gives
\[
 \lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 \lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le \frac{1}{n\kappa}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  The matrices \(\Pi\) and \(\Omega\) remain fixed throughout the pilot limit.

We first establish convergence of every pilot input.  Write \(q_a=F_a^{-1}\) and \(\widehat q_a=\widehat F_a^{-1}\).  A bounded strong law is available directly from ass:unpaired-pilot.  Indeed, if \(X_j\) are independent, centered, and \(\lvert X_j\rvert\le1\), then
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\right)^4
 =\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb E X_j^4
 +6\sum_{1\le i<j\le m}\mathbb E X_i^2\mathbb E X_j^2
 \le 3m^2.
\]
Markov's inequality and Borel--Cantelli on \(m=k^2\) imply \(k^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^{k^2}X_j\to0\) almost surely.  The number of indices between \(k^2\) and \((k+1)^2\) is \(2k+1=o(k^2)\), so bounded interpolation proves the strong law along all positive integers.

Apply this argument in each pilot arm to \(y\), \(y^2\), and \(\mathbf1\{y\le t\}\) for every rational \(t\in[0,1]\), and intersect the resulting countably many probability-one events.  Monotonicity and rational squeezing then give \(\widehat F_a(y)\to F_a(y)\) at every continuity point of \(F_a\).  Dominated convergence yields
\[
 \int_0^1\lvert\widehat F_a(y)-F_a(y)\rvert\,dy\longrightarrow0.
\]
For distributions on \([0,1]\), the layer-cake identity gives
\[
 \int_0^1\lvert\widehat q_a(u)-q_a(u)\rvert\,du
 =\int_0^1\lvert\widehat F_a(y)-F_a(y)\rvert\,dy.
\]
This identity follows by writing the right side as the area between the two distribution-function hypographs and integrating that same area first in the quantile coordinate.  Consequently, almost surely,
\[
 \lVert\widehat q_a-q_a\rVert_{L^1(0,1)}\to0,
 \qquad
 \widehat\mu_a\to\mu_a,
 \qquad
 \widehat\sigma_a^2\to\sigma_a^2,
 \quad a\in\{1,0\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The last two assertions use the strong law for \(y\) and \(y^2\), together with \(\sigma_a^2=\int y^2\,dF_a(y)-\mu_a^2\).

Because all population and empirical quantiles take values in \([0,1]\),
\[
 \left\lvert
 \int_0^1\widehat q_1(u)\widehat q_0(u)\,du
 -\int_0^1q_1(u)q_0(u)\,du
 \right\rvert
 \le
 \lVert\widehat q_1-q_1\rVert_1
 +\lVert\widehat q_0-q_0\rVert_1.
\]
The same inequality holds after replacing the second quantile's argument by \(1-u\).  Combining this fact with convergence of the empirical means proves
\[
 \widehat c_L\to c_L,
 \qquad
 \widehat c_U\to c_U
 \tag{3}
\]
almost surely.  Ass:frechet-covariance-domain ensures that \(c_L,c_U\) are precisely the population covariance endpoints used by the robust criterion.

For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), put \(Q_e=Q(c_e)\), and let \(\widehat Q_e\) be the empirical analogue prescribed by def:pilot-plugin.  Each entry in def:moment-matrix is a continuous polynomial in the two means, the two variances, and the selected endpoint covariance.  Since \(n\) is fixed, (2)--(3) therefore imply
\[
 \rho_m:=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 \lVert\widehat Q_e-Q_e\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely as }m_1\wedge m_0\to\infty.
 \tag{4}
\]

It remains to propagate (4) uniformly over the basis class.  Set
\[
 C_\nu=\lVert\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
 \qquad
 A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}.
\]
For a \(d\)-dimensional square matrix \(M\), \(\lvert\operatorname{tr}M\rvert\le d\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\).  Thus def:gain-loss, (1), submultiplicativity, and symmetry of \(\Omega\) give, uniformly in \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\),
\[
 \max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 \left\lvert\widehat g_B(\widehat c_e)-g_B(c_e)\right\rvert
 \le \frac{2C_\nu^2}{n^2\kappa}\rho_m,
 \tag{5}
\]
because the gain trace is two-dimensional and each factor containing \(B\) has operator norm at most \(\sqrt n C_\nu\).  Similarly, the uncontrolled trace is \(2n\)-dimensional, so
\[
 \max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
 \left\lvert\widehat h(\widehat c_e)-h(c_e)\right\rvert
 \le \frac{2}{n}\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\rho_m.
 \tag{6}
\]
Subtracting the gain from the uncontrolled variance and using
\(\lvert\max(x_1,x_2)-\max(y_1,y_2)\rvert\le\max_e\lvert x_e-y_e\rvert\) yields
\[
 \delta_m:=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}
 \lvert\widehat w(B)-w(B)\rvert
 \le
 \left\{
 \frac{2}{n}\lVert A_{\mathrm{HT}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\frac{2C_\nu^2}{n^2\kappa}
 \right\}\rho_m
 \longrightarrow0
 \tag{7}
\]
almost surely.  This proves the uniform objective conclusion.

All matrix operations in the two endpoint criteria are continuous on \(\mathcal B_\kappa\), since (1) keeps inversion uniformly away from singularity.  They are invariant under the residual sign choices of normalized projective representatives, and hence descend continuously to the quotient.  Compactness therefore guarantees that both population and empirical minima are attained.  The elementary infimum inequality gives
\[
 \left\lvert\widehat v_{\mathrm{mm}}-v_{\mathrm{mm}}\right\rvert
 \le\delta_m\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{8}
\]

Finally, consider an arbitrary cofinal sequence of pilot sizes and points \(B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  If a subsequence satisfies \(B_{m_j}\to B_\infty\), then for any \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), empirical optimality and (7) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 w(B_{m_j})
 &\le \widehat w(B_{m_j})+\delta_{m_j}\\
 &\le \widehat w(B^\star)+\delta_{m_j}\\
 &\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\delta_{m_j}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Continuity gives \(w(B_\infty)\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}\), while the definition of \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\) gives the reverse inequality.  Hence \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  By the sequential definition of the outer limit in this compact metrizable quotient,
\[
 \limsup_{m_1,m_0\to\infty}\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}
 \subseteq\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}
\]
almost surely. \(\square\)
