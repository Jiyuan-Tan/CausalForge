\textbf{Proof.}
\emph{Step 1: compact algebraic feasible set and finite charts.}
Use normalized representatives and write
\[
 K_\kappa=\left\{(b_1,b_0):\lVert b_1\rVert_2^2=\lVert b_0\rVert_2^2=n,
 \quad S_B-n\kappa I_2\succeq0\right\}.
\]
Assumption ass:kappa-feasibility makes \(K_\kappa\) nonempty, and prop:kappa-exhaustion shows that its projective quotient is the compact set \(\mathcal B_\kappa\).  On either radius-\(\sqrt n\) sphere, at least one coordinate has absolute value at least \(1\), and hence at least \(n^{-1/2}\).  The finitely many signed pivot regions therefore cover both projective factors.  Keeping the sphere equalities and a pivot sign in each region gives the finite semialgebraic quotient-chart cover specified by def:branch-bound-handle.

By lem:algebraic-box-feasibility-cad, intersection of any rational chart box with \(K_\kappa\) is decidable by a terminating cylindrical algebraic decomposition.  If the intersection is nonempty, that decomposition returns a point with real-algebraic coordinates.  Because the feasibility formula uses weak polynomial inequalities, the assertion remains true when the entire feasible set lies on
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(n^{-1}S_B)=\kappa.
\]

\emph{Step 2: an effective uniform modulus for the endpoint losses.}
For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), set
\[
 R_e=\Omega\Pi^{-1}Q(c_e)\Pi^{-1}\Omega,
 \qquad S(B)=B^{\top}\Omega B,
 \qquad T_e(B)=B^{\top}R_eB.
\]
The input is rational or real algebraic.  Assumption ass:exposure-positivity and the exact sign-decision interface therefore give a certified positive lower bound for every diagonal entry of \(\Pi\), while the exactly encoded condition \(\kappa>0\) gives a certified positive lower bound for \(\kappa\).  Algebraic-number isolation also gives rational upper bounds
\[
 M_\Omega\ge\lVert\Omega\rVert_2,
 \qquad M_e\ge\lVert R_e\rVert_2.
\]
Every normalized block matrix satisfies \(\lVert B\rVert_F=\sqrt{2n}\).  Hence, for normalized representatives \(B,C\) and \(d=\lVert B-C\rVert_F\), submultiplicativity and the identities
\[
 S(B)-S(C)=(B-C)^{\top}\Omega B+C^{\top}\Omega(B-C),
\]
\[
 T_e(B)-T_e(C)=(B-C)^{\top}R_eB+C^{\top}R_e(B-C)
\]
give
\[
 \lVert S(B)-S(C)\rVert_2\le K_Sd,
 \qquad
 \lVert T_e(B)-T_e(C)\rVert_2\le K_{T,e}d,
\]
where
\[
 K_S=2\sqrt{2n}\,M_\Omega,
 \qquad K_{T,e}=2\sqrt{2n}\,M_e.
\]
For \(B,C\in K_\kappa\), the regularity constraint yields
\[
 S(B),S(C)\succeq n\kappa I_2,
 \qquad
 \lVert S(B)^{-1}\rVert_2,\lVert S(C)^{-1}\rVert_2\le(n\kappa)^{-1}.
\]
The resolvent identity
\[
 S(B)^{-1}-S(C)^{-1}
 =S(B)^{-1}\{S(C)-S(B)\}S(C)^{-1}
\]
and \(\lvert\operatorname{tr}(XY)\rvert\le2\lVert X\rVert_2\lVert Y\rVert_2\) for two-by-two matrices imply
\[
 \lvert g_B(c_e)-g_C(c_e)\rvert
 \le L_ed,
\]
with
\[
 L_e=n^{-2}\left\{
 \frac{2K_{T,e}}{n\kappa}
 +\frac{4nM_eK_S}{(n\kappa)^2}
 \right\}.
\]
Indeed, the first summand bounds
\(n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}[S(B)^{-1}\{T_e(B)-T_e(C)\}]\), and the second uses \(\lVert T_e(C)\rVert_2\le2nM_e\) together with the resolvent identity.  Since \(h(c_e)\) does not depend on \(B\), the same constant bounds the difference of the endpoint residual losses.  Since the maximum map is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm,
\[
 \lvert w(B)-w(C)\rvert
 \le L\lVert B-C\rVert_F,
 \qquad L=\max\{L_L,L_U\}. \tag{1}
\]
All quantities in this display are algebraic or bounded by algebraic input data, so algebraic isolation produces and records a rational number \(\overline L\ge L\).

\emph{Step 3: a finite exhaustive subdivision depth and certified value bounds.}
Choose a positive rational \(\alpha\) such that \(4\alpha<\varepsilon\); exact isolation of the positive algebraic number \(\varepsilon\) makes this choice effective.  Uniform rational bisection of the finite chart cover reaches a finite depth at which every chart box \(C\) has a recorded rational representative-space diameter \(d_C\) satisfying
\[
 d_C\le\varepsilon,
 \qquad \overline Ld_C\le\alpha. \tag{2}
\]
If \(\overline L=0\), the second condition is automatic.  There are only finitely many boxes at this depth.  Apply lem:algebraic-box-feasibility-cad to every box.  An empty box receives its finite CAD infeasibility record; a feasible box receives an exact real-algebraic sample \(B_C\).

For each feasible \(C\), exact algebraic evaluation followed by outward rational rounding gives, for both \(e=L,U\), bounds of width at most \(2\alpha\) containing \(\ell_{B_C}(c_e)\).  Enlarging each endpoint interval by \(L_ed_C\) gives a valid outward enclosure of \(\ell_B(c_e)\) for every \(B\in C\cap K_\kappa\).  Taking the maximum of the two lower endpoint bounds gives a rational number \(\lambda_C\) satisfying
\[
 \lambda_C\le\inf_{B\in C\cap K_\kappa}w(B). \tag{3}
\]
Equivalently, one may take an outward lower approximation \(\underline q_C\) at the sample with
\[
 w(B_C)-\alpha\le\underline q_C\le w(B_C)
\]
and set \(\lambda_C=\underline q_C-\overline Ld_C\).  This latter expression also shows, using (2), that
\[
 \lambda_C\ge \inf_{B\in C\cap K_\kappa}w(B)-2\alpha. \tag{4}
\]
Similarly obtain an outward upper approximation \(\overline q_C\) with
\[
 w(B_C)\le\overline q_C\le w(B_C)+\alpha.
\]
Define
\[
 U_\varepsilon=\min_{C\text{ feasible}}\overline q_C,
 \qquad
 L_\varepsilon=\min_{C\text{ feasible}}\lambda_C,
\]
and choose as \(B_\varepsilon\) the exact algebraic sample attaining the first minimum.  These are minima of finite nonempty sets.  By (3),
\[
 L_\varepsilon\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}
 \le w(B_\varepsilon)\le U_\varepsilon. \tag{5}
\]
Compactness supplies a minimizer \(B^\star\).  For a feasible box \(C^\star\) containing it, (1)--(2) give \(w(B_{C^\star})\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+\alpha\); hence
\[
 U_\varepsilon\le v_{\mathrm{mm}}+2\alpha.
\]
On the other hand, (4) holds for every feasible box and its box infimum is at least \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\), so
\[
 L_\varepsilon\ge v_{\mathrm{mm}}-2\alpha.
\]
Consequently
\[
 U_\varepsilon-L_\varepsilon\le4\alpha<\varepsilon. \tag{6}
\]

\emph{Step 4: outer minimizer boxes and local checkability.}
Discard a feasible box only when its certified lower bound satisfies \(\lambda_C>U_\varepsilon\).  By (3) such a box contains no global minimizer.  Conversely, if a box contains a minimizer, then its valid lower bound is at most \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\), which is at most \(U_\varepsilon\) by (5), so that box is never discarded.  Let \(\mathcal C_\varepsilon\) be the quotient image of the union of all retained feasible boxes.  Then
\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\subseteq\mathcal C_\varepsilon,
 \qquad h_\varepsilon\le\varepsilon
\]
by (2).

The record \(\mathscr K_\varepsilon\) is finite: it contains the finite signed-chart cover; the CAD sign record and exact infeasibility or algebraic sample for every terminal box; the outward endpoint-loss, norm, Lipschitz, and diameter bounds; the incumbent and inequalities (5)--(6); the pruning comparisons; and the retained-box mesh.  A checker verifies only these local algebraic and rational records.  The assertions about \(v_{\mathrm{mm}}\) and \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\) follow from (3)--(6), rather than being supplied to the checker.

Every chart is subdivided only to the finite depth selected in (2), and every CAD and algebraic-isolation call terminates, so the whole procedure terminates after finitely many subdivisions.  If \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), (6) and \(h_\varepsilon\le\varepsilon\) force the certified value gaps and meshes to zero.  The argument supplies neither a uniform bound on the number of CAD cells or subdivisions nor a semidefinite-program reduction.  This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
