\textbf{Proof.}
By \(\texttt{ass:kappa-feasibility}\) and \(\texttt{prop:kappa-exhaustion}\), \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) is a nonempty compact metrizable projective quotient.  For every normalized representative \(B\in\mathcal B_\kappa\), block separability and projective normalization give
\[
B^{\top}B=nI_2,
\qquad
\lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt n,
\qquad
\lVert S_B^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le (n\kappa)^{-1}.
\tag{1}
\]
The last inequality follows from \(S_B=B^{\top}\Omega B\succeq n\kappa I_2\), so every inverse used below exists uniformly over the feasible class.

We first prove convergence of the two endpoint moment matrices from the pilot primitives.  For arm \(a\), write \(F_{a,m_a}\) for the distribution function of \(\widehat F_a\).  At every fixed rational \(t\in[0,1]\), the variables
\[
X_{j,a}(t)=\mathbf 1\{Y^P_{j,a}\le t\}
\]
are independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(F_a(t)\) by \(\texttt{ass:unpaired-pilot}\).  If \(X\) is Bernoulli with mean \(p\), then
\[
\mathbb E\exp\{\lambda(X-p)\}
=e^{-\lambda p}(1-p+pe^\lambda)\le e^{\lambda^2/8}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the second derivative of \(\log(1-p+pe^\lambda)\) is the variance of a tilted Bernoulli variable and is at most \(1/4\), while the centered logarithm and its first derivative vanish at zero.  Exponential Markov inequality, independence, and optimization over \(\lambda\) therefore yield
\[
P\!\left(\left|F_{a,m_a}(t)-F_a(t)\right|>s\right)
\le 2e^{-2m_as^2},
\qquad s>0.
\tag{3}
\]
For rational \(s>0\), the right-hand side is summable in \(m_a\).  The union bound on tail unions, followed by intersection over the countable rational pairs \((t,s)\), proves simultaneous almost-sure convergence at every rational \(t\).

Fix \(K\ge1\) and put \(x_j=j/K\).  Monotonicity of the empirical and population distribution functions implies, for \(y\in[x_j,x_{j+1}]\),
\[
|F_{a,m_a}(y)-F_a(y)|
\le
\max_{r\in\{j,j+1\}}|F_{a,m_a}(x_r)-F_a(x_r)|
+F_a(x_{j+1})-F_a(x_j).
\tag{4}
\]
Integrating, summing over \(j\), taking the limit superior, and telescoping the population increments give
\[
\limsup_{m_a\to\infty}
\int_0^1|F_{a,m_a}(y)-F_a(y)|\,dy\le K^{-1}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{5}
\]
Letting \(K\to\infty\) makes the integral converge to zero.  For distribution functions \(R,S\) supported on \([0,1]\), Tonelli's theorem applied to threshold indicators gives the one-dimensional identity
\[
\int_0^1|R^{-1}(u)-S^{-1}(u)|\,du
=\int_0^1|R(y)-S(y)|\,dy.
\tag{6}
\]
To verify (6), express the absolute difference of two numbers in \([0,1]\) as the integral of the absolute difference of their threshold indicators and use
\(\mathbf 1\{R^{-1}(u)\le y\}=\mathbf 1\{u\le R(y)\}\) outside a null set.  Consequently,
\[
\int_0^1|\widehat F_a^{-1}(u)-F_a^{-1}(u)|\,du\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely},
\qquad a\in\{1,0\}.
\tag{7}
\]
The support condition used in this step is exactly \(\texttt{ass:bounded-outcomes}\).

Since \(y\mapsto y\) and \(y\mapsto y^2\) are respectively one- and two-Lipschitz on \([0,1]\), (7) gives convergence of the empirical means, second moments, and variances.  Moreover,
\[
|xy-x'y'|\le |x-x'|+|y-y'|,
\qquad x,x',y,y'\in[0,1].
\tag{8}
\]
Applying (8) first to the comonotone quantiles and then to the antimonotone quantiles proves convergence of both empirical Fr\'echet cross moments and hence
\[
\widehat c_L\to c_L,
\qquad
\widehat c_U\to c_U
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{9}
\]
By the block formula in \(\texttt{def:moment-matrix}\) and the endpoint construction in \(\texttt{def:pilot-plugin}\), every block of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) is a convergent scalar multiplying either \(I_n\) or \(\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}\).  Since \(n\) is fixed,
\[
R_m:=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}
\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{10}
\]

It remains to transfer (10) uniformly through the design criterion.  Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) a fixed finite matrix.  For the uncontrolled term, the trace inequality \(|\operatorname{tr}(MZ)|\le\lVert M\rVert_*\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) gives
\[
n^{-2}\left|
\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}
\{\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\}\right]\right|
\le {2\over n}\lVert\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}R_m.
\tag{11}
\]
For the gain term, cyclicity of trace, (1), and
\(\lVert\Pi^{-1}\Omega B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\lVert\Pi^{-1}\Omega\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\sqrt n\) give, uniformly in \(B\),
\[
\begin{split}
&n^{-2}\left|\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
S_B^{-1}B^{\top}\Omega\Pi^{-1}
\{\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\}
\Pi^{-1}\Omega B\right]\right|\\
&\hspace{25mm}\le
{2\lVert\Pi^{-1}\Omega\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2
\over n^2\kappa}R_m.
\end{split}
\tag{12}
\]
The elementary inequality
\(\left|\max_e x_e-\max_e y_e\right|\le\max_e|x_e-y_e|\), together with (11)--(12), supplies a finite constant \(K_0\), depending only on the fixed design and \(\kappa\), such that
\[
\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|
\le K_0R_m\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{13}
\]

On \(\mathcal B_\kappa\), the map \(B\mapsto S_B^{-1}\) is continuous because of the uniform spectral lower bound in (1).  All remaining matrix operations and the maximum over the two endpoints are continuous and invariant under the projective sign representatives.  Thus \(w\) and every \(\widehat w\) are continuous on the compact quotient, and their minima are attained.  The elementary comparison of two minima gives
\[
|\widehat v_{\mathrm{mm}}-v_{\mathrm{mm}}|
\le\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{14}
\]

Finally, work on the probability-one event on which (13) holds.  Let the sample sizes tend to infinity along an arbitrary sequence, let \(B_m\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\), and suppose a subsequence converges to \(B_\infty\).  Choose any \(B^\star\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), which exists by compactness.  Empirical optimality and (13) imply
\[
w(B_m)
\le w(B^\star)+2\sup_{B\in\mathcal B_\kappa}
|\widehat w(B)-w(B)|.
\tag{15}
\]
Continuity and passage to the convergent subsequence give \(w(B_\infty)\le w(B^\star)=v_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  The reverse inequality follows from the definition of the minimum, so \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}\).  Since the sequence and convergent subsequence were arbitrary, this is exactly
\[
\limsup_{m_1,m_0\to\infty}\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}
\subseteq\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}
\]
in the projective quotient.  The argument concerns only the fixed-\(\kappa\), positive-definite rank-two class and makes no assertion across singular pseudoinverse strata. \(\square\)

%%% FIELD proof-unique-saddle-point-convergence
\textbf{Proof.}
Equip the compact metrizable quotient \(\mathcal B_\kappa\) with any compatible metric \(d\), and work on the probability-one event furnished by \(\texttt{thm:plugin-correspondence}\).  Suppose first that \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}=\{B^\star\}\).  If a selection \(\widehat B\in\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) did not converge to \(B^\star\), there would be a number \(\epsilon_0>0\) and a sequence of pilot-size pairs with \(m_1\wedge m_0\to\infty\) such that
\[
d(\widehat B,B^\star)\ge\epsilon_0.
\tag{16}
\]
Compactness supplies a convergent subsequence, with limit \(B_\infty\).  The outer-limit conclusion of \(\texttt{thm:plugin-correspondence}\) gives \(B_\infty\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}=\{B^\star\}\), whereas continuity of \(d\) and (16) give \(d(B_\infty,B^\star)\ge\epsilon_0\), a contradiction.  Thus every such selection converges almost surely.  Measurability ensures that the selected basis is a legitimate pilot statistic; the pathwise compactness argument itself needs no stronger selection condition.

We next give an explicit bounded counterexample showing why singletonness cannot be omitted.  Let \(n=3\), take the empty fixed network, put \(f_i(w)=w_i\), and let \(W_1,W_2,W_3\) be independent Bernoulli variables with success probability \(1/2\).  Then
\[
D=(W_1,W_2,W_3,1-W_1,1-W_2,1-W_3)^{\top},
\qquad
\Pi={1\over2}I_6,
\]
and direct covariance calculation gives
\[
\Omega={1\over4}
\begin{bmatrix}
I_3&-I_3\\
-I_3&I_3
\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{17}
\]
Both exposure labels therefore have probability \(1/2\) for every unit.  Set every potential outcome equal to zero under every assignment, take
\[
H=\delta_{(0,0)},
\qquad
F_1=F_0=\delta_0,
\tag{18}
\]
and let both pilot samples consist of their corresponding independent degenerate draws.  This instance has bounded outcomes, known exposures, mutually exclusive exposure events, identically distributed potential-outcome pairs, fixed marginals, exposure positivity, and an unpaired pilot independent of the main assignment.  Degenerate random variables are independent, so the independence clauses are satisfied literally rather than only vacuously.

Let \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) denote the coordinate vectors in \(\mathbb R^3\), and consider
\[
B^{(2)}=
\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt3e_1&0\\0&\sqrt3e_2\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
B^{(3)}=
\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt3e_1&0\\0&\sqrt3e_3\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{19}
\]
Their columns satisfy the required squared-norm-three normalization.  By (17) and orthogonality of the displayed arm vectors,
\[
3^{-1}S_{B^{(2)}}=3^{-1}S_{B^{(3)}}={1\over4}I_2.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus, for example, both classes belong to \(\mathcal B_{1/8}\).  They are distinct in the projective quotient because their comparison-arm lines are respectively \(\operatorname{span}(e_2)\) and \(\operatorname{span}(e_3)\).

Under (18), \(\mu_1=\mu_0=\sigma_1^2=\sigma_0^2=c_L=c_U=0\), and hence \(Q(0)=0\).  The pilot empirical laws equal the same point masses for every positive pair of sample sizes, so also \(\widehat Q_L=\widehat Q_U=0\).  The definitions of the population and plug-in losses therefore give, for every \(B\in\mathcal B_{1/8}\),
\[
w(B)=0,
\qquad
\widehat w(B)=0,
\qquad
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{mm}}
=\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}
=\mathcal B_{1/8}.
\tag{21}
\]
In particular, the outer-set conclusion holds identically.  Define a deterministic, hence measurable, plug-in selection by choosing \(B^{(2)}\) when \(m_1\) is even and \(B^{(3)}\) when \(m_1\) is odd.  It belongs to \(\widehat{\mathcal A}_{\mathrm{mm}}\) for every pilot sample by (21), but along \(m_1=m_0\to\infty\) it alternates between the two distinct quotient points in (19) and therefore has no point limit.  This proves the asserted existential failure without singletonness. \(\square\)
