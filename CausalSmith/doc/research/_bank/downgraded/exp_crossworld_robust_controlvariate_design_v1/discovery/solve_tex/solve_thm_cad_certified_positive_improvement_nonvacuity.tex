\textbf{Proof.}
For arm \(a\), write \(F_{a,m_a}\) for the distribution function of \(\widehat F_a\).  For every rational \(t\in[0,1]\), the variables
\[
X_{j,a}(t)=\mathbf 1\{Y^P_{j,a}\le t\}
\]
are independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(F_a(t)\) by \(\texttt{ass:unpaired-pilot}\).  Hoeffding's elementary exponential argument gives, for \(s>0\),
\[
P\!\left(\left|F_{a,m_a}(t)-F_a(t)\right|>s\right)
\le 2e^{-2m_as^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, for a Bernoulli variable \(X\) of mean \(p\), convexity gives
\(\mathbb E e^{\lambda(X-p)}\le e^{\lambda^2/8}\); exponential Markov inequality, independence, and optimization in \(\lambda\) yield (1).  The right side is summable in \(m_a\).  Intersecting the resulting probability-one events over rational \(t\) and rational \(s>0\) shows that \(F_{a,m_a}(t)\to F_a(t)\) simultaneously at every rational \(t\).

Fix \(K\ge1\) and put \(x_j=j/K\).  Monotonicity of the two distribution functions implies, for \(y\in[x_j,x_{j+1}]\),
\[
|F_{a,m_a}(y)-F_a(y)|
\le \max_{r\in\{j,j+1\}}|F_{a,m_a}(x_r)-F_a(x_r)|
+F_a(x_{j+1})-F_a(x_j).
\tag{2}
\]
Integrating, summing, passing to the limit on the finite grid, and telescoping the last increments gives
\[
\limsup_{m_a\to\infty}\int_0^1|F_{a,m_a}(y)-F_a(y)|\,dy\le K^{-1}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{3}
\]
Letting \(K\to\infty\) proves convergence of this integral to zero.  For probability laws \(R,S\) on \([0,1]\), the layer-cake representation and Tonelli's theorem give
\[
\int_0^1|R^{-1}(u)-S^{-1}(u)|\,du
=\int_0^1|R(y)-S(y)|\,dy.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus
\[
\int_0^1|\widehat F_a^{-1}(u)-F_a^{-1}(u)|\,du\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely},\qquad a\in\{1,0\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The bounded support used in (2)--(5) is exactly \(\texttt{ass:bounded-outcomes}\).

The functions \(y\) and \(y^2\) are respectively one- and two-Lipschitz on \([0,1]\), so (5) yields convergence of the empirical means, second moments, and variances.  Moreover,
\[
|xy-x'y'|\le |x-x'|+|y-y'|
\qquad(x,x',y,y'\in[0,1]).
\tag{6}
\]
Applying (6) to the comonotone and antimonotone quantile pairs proves convergence of both empirical endpoint cross moments and hence of both empirical covariance endpoints.  Every block of \(\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\) is therefore a convergent scalar multiplying either \(I_n\) or \(\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\).  Since \(n\) is fixed and
\[
\lVert I_n\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=1,
\qquad \lVert\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=n,
\tag{7}
\]
we obtain
\[
M_m=\max_{e\in\{L,U\}}\lVert\widehat Q_e-Q(c_e)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely under }P.
\]
The additional stable and unrestricted selectors now present in \(\texttt{def:pilot-plugin}\) do not enter this endpoint calculation. \(\square\)

\textbf{Proof.}
Fix the design.  Exposure positivity makes \(\Pi^{-1}\) well defined.  For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), let \(\widehat H_e\) be the comonotone or antimonotone quantile coupling of the two empirical marginals.  If
\[
\mathcal Y=(Y_1(1),\ldots,Y_n(1),-Y_1(0),\ldots,-Y_n(0))^{\top}
\]
under \(\widehat H_e^{\otimes n}\), the endpoint construction in \(\texttt{def:pilot-plugin}\) gives
\[
\widehat Q_e=\mathbb E_{\widehat H_e^{\otimes n}}(\mathcal Y\mathcal Y^{\top})\succeq0.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently
\[
\widehat A_e=\Omega^{1/2}\Pi^{-1}\widehat Q_e\Pi^{-1}\Omega^{1/2}\succeq0.
\tag{2}
\]
For every carrier class, cyclicity of trace and \(\texttt{lem:rank-stratified-projector-reduction}\) write the empirical endpoint residual as
\[
\widehat\ell_{B,e}^+
=\widehat h_e-n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(P_B\widehat A_e),
\qquad e\in\{L,U\},
\tag{3}
\]
where \(\widehat h_e=n^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(\Pi^{-1}\Omega\Pi^{-1}\widehat Q_e)\) is independent of \(B\).

We first show density in objective value on the maximal-rank stratum.  If \(r_\Omega=2\), positive definiteness of \(B^{\top}\Omega B\) is open, rational directions are dense in every signed-pivot chart, and normalization is continuous away from zero.  Hence normalized rational-support rank-two classes exist and are dense in each rank-two chart.  On a fixed rank stratum the projector, and thus (3), is continuous.

If \(r_\Omega=1\), let \([B]_+\) have rank one.  At least one arm column has nonzero image \(z_a=\Omega^{1/2}B_ae_a\).  Choose nonzero rational vectors converging to that arm direction.  Their images remain nonzero eventually.  Put the normalized rational vector in arm \(a\) and zero in the other arm.  The resulting class has rank one because maximal rank is one, and its one-dimensional projector converges to the projector onto \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega^{1/2}B)\).  These classes occur in \((C_k)\).  If \(r_\Omega=0\), every projector is zero and the repeated zero class prescribed by \(\texttt{def:pilot-plugin}\) is nonempty and has every carrier value.

Now fix any \([B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+\).  The approximation clause of \(\texttt{lem:rank-stratified-projector-reduction}\) supplies maximal-rank classes \([B_j]_+\) and a projector \(P_\infty\) such that
\[
P_{B_j}\longrightarrow P_\infty,
\qquad P_\infty-P_B\succeq0.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) and trace monotonicity imply, endpoint by endpoint and then after taking the maximum,
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\widehat w_m^+(B_j)\le\widehat w_m^+(B).
\tag{5}
\]
Approximate each fixed \(B_j\) by the rational-support classes just constructed.  It follows that, for every \(\delta>0\), some \(C_k\) satisfies
\[
\widehat w_m^+(C_k)\le\widehat w_m^+(B)+\delta.
\tag{6}
\]
Taking infima and letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves
\[
\inf_{k\ge1}\widehat w_m^+(C_k)
=\inf_{[B]_+\in\mathcal B_Z^+}\widehat w_m^+(B),
\tag{7}
\]
the reverse inequality being immediate because every \(C_k\) belongs to the carrier.  No cross-rank continuity or attainment is used.

For measurability, empirical means, variances, and endpoint cross moments are measurable functions of finitely many pilot order statistics.  Hence \(X_k:=\widehat w_m^+(C_k)\) is pilot measurable for each fixed \(k\), and \(\rho_m=\inf_{j\ge1}X_j\) is measurable as a countable infimum.  Strict positivity and finiteness of \(\varepsilon_m\) make the qualifying set nonempty.  Moreover,
\[
\{\widehat k_m=k\}
=\{X_k\le\rho_m+\varepsilon_m\}
\cap\bigcap_{j<k}\{X_j>\rho_m+\varepsilon_m\},
\tag{8}
\]
so \(\widehat k_m\) is finite and measurable.  The selected class has rank \(r_\Omega\), obeys the asserted positive-tolerance inequality by (7), and has rank two exactly when rank two is achievable.  The argument supplies only a classical countable measurable selector; it supplies no finite arbitrary-real stopping rule, zero-tolerance selector, or agreement with the distinct exact-input CAD selector. \(\square\)

\widehat\ell_{B,e}^+-\ell_B^+(c_e)

\tag{4}

Trace duality, (3), and the one-Lipschitz property of the maximum in the supremum norm imply

d_m^+\le4\pi_{\min,n}^{-1}n^{-1}M_m.
\tag{5}

Under \(\texttt{ass:zhao-exposure-probability-rate}\), \(\pi_{\min,n}^{-1}\le\underline\pi^{-1}n^\beta\), which proves (A).

\[
|\inf f-\inf g|\le\sup|f-g|.
\tag{10}
\]

\le2d_m^++\varepsilon_m.
\tag{11}

\le8K_M\underline\pi^{-1}\frac{n^{1+\beta}}{\sqrt{m_1\wedge m_0}}

\tag{12}

The rank claim follows from the selector construction.  The proof controls the all-rank basis objective and does not assert an observable-estimator bridge at an uncontrolled singular rank. \(\square\)

Fix a stratum \(V_j\) and one of its certified tubular charts.  Write a feasible point near \(C\in V_j\) in the certified coordinates as
\[
B=\Psi_C(s,z,r),
\tag{4}
\]
where \(s\) is tangent to \(V_j\), \(z\) is feasible tangent and transverse to \(V_j\), and \(r\) is the active-normal coordinate.  The record's lower singular-value bounds provide constants \(c_1,c_2>0\), uniform over its finite chart list, for which
\[
c_1(\lVert z\rVert+\lVert r\rVert)
\le d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,A_0)
\le c_2(\lVert z\rVert+\lVert r\rVert)
\tag{5}
\]
after shrinking the tubes.  Motion in \(s\) changes the nearest point within \(V_j\) and therefore does not enter the distance.

The nominal KKT equations make the first derivative vanish in \(z\).  The certified transverse Lagrangian Hessian bound gives a number \(\lambda_j>0\) such that the second-order contribution to \(a^\star-a(B)\) is at least \(\lambda_j\lVert z\rVert^2\).  Linear independence and strict complementarity give a number \(\chi_j>0\) such that the feasible active-normal contribution is at least \(\chi_j\lVert r\rVert\).  The certified derivative and remainder bounds give \(C_j<\infty\) and a tube radius \(\rho_j>0\) for which Taylor's formula yields
\[
a^\star-a(B)
\ge\lambda_j\lVert z\rVert^2+\chi_j\lVert r\rVert
-C_j(\lVert z\rVert+\lVert r\rVert)^3.
\tag{6}
\]
Choose \(\rho_j\) smaller, if necessary, so the remainder is at most one half of the first two terms.  Because \(\lVert r\rVert\le\rho_j\), the linear normal term also satisfies
\(\chi_j\lVert r\rVert\ge(\chi_j/\rho_j)\lVert r\rVert^2\).  Hence there is \(\mu_j>0\) such that
\[
a^\star-a(B)\ge\mu_j d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,A_0)^2
\tag{7}
\]
throughout that tube, by (5).  Taking the minimum of the positive constants and radii over the finite refined chart and stratum list gives \(\rho>0\) and \(\mu>0\) for the first inequality in (G4).  Thus quadratic growth has been derived from KKT, transverse Hessian, strict-complementarity, and remainder data; it was not placed in the record.

\emph{Quadratic control of the robust deficit.}
For \(C\in N_q\), let
\[
\phi_C(B)=\theta_L(C)\ell_B(c_L)+\theta_U(C)\ell_B(c_U).
\tag{8}
\]
The weights are nonnegative and sum to one, so
\[
\phi_C(B)\le w(B)
\quad\text{for every }B.
\tag{9}
\]
Because weights are supported only on endpoint losses active at \(C\),
\[
\phi_C(C)=w(C)\ge q.
\tag{10}
\]
The certified KKT normal data state that the first feasible directional derivative of \(\phi_C\) at \(C\) is nonnegative.  Its uniform endpoint-Hessian and Taylor-remainder bounds therefore give \(M_0<\infty\), uniformly over the finitely many strata in \(N_q\), such that
\[
\phi_C(B)\ge\phi_C(C)-M_0d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,C)^2
\tag{11}
\]
in a sufficiently small certified tube.  Equations (9)--(11) imply
\[
[q-w(B)]_+\le M_0d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,C)^2.
\tag{12}
\]
Choose \(C\) as a chartwise nearest point in \(A_0\); the finite-chart definition of \(d_{\mathrm{pr}}\) makes the right side in (12) \(M_0d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,A_0)^2\).

On the compact remainder \(A_0\setminus N_q\), the certificate gives a positive gap \(w(C)-q\ge\chi_q>0\).  Uniform continuity and the interval Taylor bounds permit a common smaller tube in which \(w(B)\ge q\); there the positive part in (12) is zero.  Combining the two cases and again taking the maximum over finitely many bounds gives \(M<\infty\) such that
\[
[q-w(B)]_+\le Md_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,A_0)^2
\quad\text{whenever }d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,A_0)\le\rho.
\tag{13}
\]
Together, (7) and (13) prove (G4).

\emph{Global comparison.}
If \(d_{\mathrm{pr}}(B,A_0)\le\rho\), dividing (13) by (7) gives
\[
[q-w(B)]_+\le\frac M\mu\{a^\star-a(B)\}.
\tag{14}
\]
On the compact complement of this tube, (G2) gives
\[
[q-w(B)]_+\le R
\le\frac R\gamma\{a^\star-a(B)\}.
\tag{15}
\]
With \(L=\max\{M/\mu,R/\gamma\}\), equations (14)--(15) prove (G6) on all of \(\mathcal K\).

\emph{Plug-in and observable consequences.}
Let \(\widehat B_0\) maximize the empirical nominal gain.  If \(B_0\in A_0\), empirical optimality and the definition of \(e\) give
\[
a^\star-a(\widehat B_0)
\le |a(B_0)-\widehat a(B_0)|
+|\widehat a(\widehat B_0)-a(\widehat B_0)|
\le2e.
\tag{16}
\]
Applying (G6) to (16) yields
\[
w(\widehat B_0)\ge q-2Le.
\tag{17}
\]
Similarly, if \(B^\star\in A_{\mathrm{mm}}\), empirical optimality and the definition of \(d\) give
\[
w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})
\le\widehat w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})+d
\le\widehat w(B^\star)+d
\le v+2d.
\tag{18}
\]
Finally, the two supplied observable bridge inequalities imply
\[
V_0\ge w(\widehat B_0)-R_0,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{mm}}\le w(\widehat B_{\mathrm{mm}})+R_{\mathrm{mm}}.
\tag{19}
\]
Subtracting (19) and using (17)--(18) proves
\[
V_0-V_{\mathrm{mm}}
\ge(q-v)-2Le-2d-R_0-R_{\mathrm{mm}},
\]
which is (G7).  Every constant used above is obtained by a minimum or maximum of finitely many exact algebraic signs, singular-value bounds, interval derivative bounds, and compact-complement CAD bounds in \(\mathscr G_{\mathrm{CAD}}\).  Thus all stated consequences are derived from the primitive record. \(\square\)
