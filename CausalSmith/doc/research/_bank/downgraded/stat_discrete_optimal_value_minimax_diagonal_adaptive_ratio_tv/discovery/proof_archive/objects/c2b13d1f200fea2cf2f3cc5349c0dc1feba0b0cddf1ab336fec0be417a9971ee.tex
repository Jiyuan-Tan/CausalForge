\textbf{Proof.}
The proof is an algebraic consequence of the two stated risk lemmas.  By the definition of minimax risk, evaluating the infimum at the particular estimator \(\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P[(\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}-V^\star(P))^2].
\]
The lemma \(\mathrm{lem:diagonal-polynomial-upper}\) supplies the last inequality in the theorem.

The lemma \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\) supplies constants \(b_{1,\epsilon},b_{2,\epsilon}>0\) such that
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\ge \frac{b_{1,\epsilon}}n,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\ge
 b_{2,\epsilon}\frac{d}{n\log(en)}.
\]
Since \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\), these displays imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \ge \frac12\min\{b_{1,\epsilon},b_{2,\epsilon}\}
 \left\{\frac1n+\frac{d}{n\log(en)}\right\}.
\]
Shrinking the lower constant, enlarging the upper constant, and taking the smaller of the two range constants proves the displayed risk sandwich.

Now consider any sequence in the stated range.  If \(d=O(\log n)\), then \(d/\{n\log(en)\}=O(n^{-1})\), so the sandwich gives \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\asymp_\epsilon n^{-1}\).  Conversely, if \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=O(n^{-1})\), the lower bound forces \(d/\log(en)=O(1)\), equivalently \(d=O(\log n)\).  Likewise, the upper bound tends to zero when \(d=o(n\log n)\), while the lower bound forces this condition whenever the risk tends to zero.  Finally, \(\mathrm{def:l1-embedding}\) is an equal-propensity subexperiment, whereas the upper lemma takes its supremum over the full class \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\); this proves the asserted distinction in model-class scope.
