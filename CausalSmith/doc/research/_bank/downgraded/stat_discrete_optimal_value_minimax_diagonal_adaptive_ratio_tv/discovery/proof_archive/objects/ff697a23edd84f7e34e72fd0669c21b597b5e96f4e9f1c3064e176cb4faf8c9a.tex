\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Apply \(\mathrm{thm:global-elementary-minimax-bracket}\), and set \(\rho_\epsilon=1/16\).  For \(2\leq d\leq\rho_\epsilon n\log(en)\), the elementary bound \(\log(en)\leq n\) gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac d{n\log(en)}
 \leq\sqrt{\rho_\epsilon}+\rho_\epsilon
 =\frac5{16}<1.
\]
Thus the cap in the global lower bound is inactive throughout the displayed range, and the global theorem gives precisely
\[
 c_\epsilon\left\{\frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac d{n\log(en)}\right\}
 \leq\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P\!\left[
  \left\{\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}-V^\star(P)\right\}^2
 \right]
 \leq\min\!\left\{1,C_\epsilon\frac dn\right\}.
\]
The same theorem computes the ratio of the two nonsaturated lower terms as \(\log(en)/\sqrt d\), proving the \(\log^2(en)\) elbow and the two dominance claims.  Its sequence conclusions are global, so they imply in particular the necessary condition \(d=o(n\log n)\), the sufficient condition \(d=o(n)\), and the equivalence between parametric mean-squared error and \(d=O(1)\) on the range stated here.  The global theorem also records that the unmatched interval and the two matching-achievability questions remain unresolved.  This proves every assertion of the present range-limited formulation.
