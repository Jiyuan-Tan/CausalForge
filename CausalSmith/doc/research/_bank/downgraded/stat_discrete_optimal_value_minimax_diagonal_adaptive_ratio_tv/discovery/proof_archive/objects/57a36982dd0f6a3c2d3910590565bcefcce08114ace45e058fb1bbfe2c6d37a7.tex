\textbf{Proof.}
The four displayed joint masses in \(\mathrm{def:l1-embedding}\) sum within cell \(x\) to
\[
 p_x=\frac{P_x+Q_x}{2}.
\]
Their treatment-one sum and treatment-zero sum both equal \((P_x+Q_x)/4\).  Hence \(\pi_x=1/2\) whenever \(p_x>0\), and fixed overlap follows from \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Summing the cell masses gives
\[
 \sum_{x=1}^dp_x=\frac12\sum_{x=1}^d(P_x+Q_x)=1.
\]
For a nonnull cell,
\[
 \mu_{1x}=\frac{q_{11,x}}{q_{10,x}+q_{11,x}}
 =\frac{P_x}{P_x+Q_x},
 \qquad
 \mu_{0x}=\frac{q_{01,x}}{q_{00,x}+q_{01,x}}
 =\frac{Q_x}{P_x+Q_x}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V^\star(P)
 &=\sum_{x=1}^d\frac{P_x+Q_x}{2}
       \max\!\left\{\frac{P_x}{P_x+Q_x},\frac{Q_x}{P_x+Q_x}\right\}\\
 &=\frac12\sum_{x=1}^d\max\{P_x,Q_x\}\\
 &=\frac14\sum_{x=1}^d\{P_x+Q_x+\lvert P_x-Q_x\rvert\}\\
 &=\frac12+\frac14\lVert\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\rVert_1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Cells with \(P_x+Q_x=0\) contribute zero, so the ratio convention causes no boundary problem.  The slice is proper: for example, the full class permits any supported cell with propensity \(\pi_x=\epsilon\), whereas every law on the embedded slice has \(\pi_x=1/2\).  The estimator in \(\mathrm{def:diagonal-adaptive-estimator}\) is defined on arbitrary four-cell counts, not only on the equal-propensity identities, which establishes the final scope statement.
