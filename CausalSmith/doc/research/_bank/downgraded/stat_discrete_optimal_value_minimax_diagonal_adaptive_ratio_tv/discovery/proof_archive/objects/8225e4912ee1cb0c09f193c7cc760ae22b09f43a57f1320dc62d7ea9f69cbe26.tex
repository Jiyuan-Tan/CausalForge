\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(m_2=\lvert I_2\rvert\) and \(k=\lvert r\rvert\).  For \(k\le m_2\), expand
\(\prod_{a,y}(N^{(2)}_{ay,x})_{r_{ay}}\) as a sum over ordered selections of \(k\) distinct indices from \(I_2\), with exactly \(r_{ay}\) selected indices assigned to category \((X,A,Y)=(x,a,y)\).  There are \((m_2)_k\) such ordered selections.  By independent and identically distributed sampling, every corresponding product of indicators has expectation
\[
 \prod_{a,y}q_{ay,x}^{r_{ay}}.
\]
Linearity of expectation hence yields
\[
 E_P\!\left[\prod_{a,y}(N^{(2)}_{ay,x})_{r_{ay}}\right]
 =(m_2)_k\prod_{a,y}q_{ay,x}^{r_{ay}}.
\]
Division by \((m_2)_k>0\) proves the display.  For \(k=0\), both sides equal one.  Since the split is deterministic and the observations are independent, the same calculation holds conditionally on the pilot block \(I_0\).
