\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(L_n=\log(en)\) and \(d_n=\max\{2,\lfloor L_n\rfloor\}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(2\leq d_n\leq n^2\), and \(d_n\geq L_n/2\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \geq\frac{\sqrt{d_n}}{4096\sqrt2\,n}
 \geq\frac{\sqrt{L_n}}{8192n}.
\]
This is the stated quantitative obstruction.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that finite \(C_\epsilon\), positive \(\rho_\epsilon\), and estimators \(\widehat V_{n,d}\) satisfied the proposed certificate.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(d_n\leq\rho_\epsilon nL_n\).  By \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\) and \(d_n\leq L_n\), the certificate would imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d_n}{nL_n}\right\}
 \leq\frac{2C_\epsilon}{n},
\]
contradicting the preceding lower bound because \(\sqrt{L_n}\to\infty\).  Since every finite computable four-cell construction produces a measurable estimator, it cannot evade this minimax contradiction.

Finally, consider any sequence with \(2\leq d\leq n^2\).  If its risk is \(O(n^{-1})\), the paired-sign lower bound implies
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d}{4096\sqrt2\,n}=O(n^{-1}),
\]
and hence \(d=O(1)\).  Conversely, if \(d=O(1)\), \(\mathrm{thm:global-elementary-minimax-bracket}\) supplies an \(O(n^{-1})\) upper bound, while its parametric floor supplies an \(\Omega(n^{-1})\) lower bound.  Thus bounded alphabets have risk of exact order \(n^{-1}\), and only they do.
