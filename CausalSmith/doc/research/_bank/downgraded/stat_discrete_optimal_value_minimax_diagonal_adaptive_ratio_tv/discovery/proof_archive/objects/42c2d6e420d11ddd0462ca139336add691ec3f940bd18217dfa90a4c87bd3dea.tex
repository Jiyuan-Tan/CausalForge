\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(L_n=\log(en)\) and \(d_n=\lfloor L_n^{3/2}\rfloor\).  Then
\[
 \frac{d_n}{\log n}\sim L_n^{1/2}\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \frac{d_n}{n\log n}\sim\frac{L_n^{1/2}}n\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{1}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(2\leq d_n\leq n^2\).  Applying
\(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) and using
\(\sqrt{d_n}\leq L_n^{3/4}\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{n\log n}{d_n}\mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 &\geq\frac{\log n}{4096\sqrt2\,\sqrt{d_n}}\\
 &\geq\frac{L_n-1}{4096\sqrt2\,L_n^{3/4}}
 \longrightarrow+\infty.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]
Here the first denominator uses \(d_n/\sqrt{d_n}=\sqrt{d_n}\).  Equations (1) and (2) exhibit a sequence inside the proposed regime on which the normalized risk diverges.  Therefore no finite constant can hold uniformly over that regime, and in particular no program, including \(\mathscr H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{dual}}\), can characterize or attain such a nonexistent universal limit.  The paired-sign obstruction uses the normalization \(n/\sqrt d\); the normalized-distance obstruction uses \(n\log n/d\) only above the \(\log^2 n\) elbow.  The theorem makes no assertion that either narrower normalization has a limit, so the remaining exact-constant questions stated there are not affected by this contradiction.
