\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(L_n=\log(en)\) and
\(d_n=\max\{2,\lfloor L_n\rfloor\}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\(d_n=\lfloor L_n\rfloor\), \(d_n\leq n^2\), and
\(d_n\geq L_n/2\).  Hence
\(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \geq\frac{\sqrt{d_n}}{4096\sqrt2,n}
 \geq\frac{\sqrt{L_n}}{8192n}.
 \tag{1}
\]

Suppose, contrary to the first assertion, that finite \(C_\epsilon\), positive
\(\rho_\epsilon\), and such estimators existed.  For all sufficiently large
\(n\), one has \(2\leq d_n\leq\rho_\epsilon nL_n\).  By the definition of minimax risk, their asserted certificate and \(d_n\leq L_n\) would give
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{
       \frac1n+\frac{d_n}{nL_n}
      \right\}
 \leq\frac{2C_\epsilon}{n},
\]
contradicting (1) as \(L_n\to\infty\).  Since every finite computable four-cell construction in the statement is a measurable estimator, the same contradiction rules out its proposed uniform certificate.

Now consider any sequence with \(2\leq d\leq n^2\).  If its risk is
\(O(n^{-1})\), the paired-sign bound implies
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d}{4096\sqrt2,n}=O(n^{-1}),
\]
and therefore \(d=O(1)\).  Conversely, if \(d=O(1)\), the upper bound in
\(\mathrm{thm:global-elementary-minimax-bracket}\) is \(O(n^{-1})\), while the paired-sign lower bound is bounded below by a positive constant times \(n^{-1}\) because \(d\geq2\).  Thus bounded alphabets have risk of order \(n^{-1}\), and the equivalence follows.
