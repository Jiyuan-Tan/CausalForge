\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Let \(\rho_\epsilon^{(1)}>0\) be the range constant supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\), and define
\[
 \rho_\epsilon:=\min\{\rho_\epsilon^{(1)},1\}.
\]
For every integer \(n\ge1\), the elementary inequality \(\log(en)=1+\log n\le n\) holds.  Thus, whenever
\(2\le d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log(en)\), one also has \(d\le n^2\).  The support and range conditions of \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) are therefore satisfied, and that lemma gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \ge c_0\frac{\sqrt d}{n},
 \qquad
 c_0:=\frac1{4096\sqrt2}.
\]
On the same range, \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\) supplies a constant \(c_{1,\epsilon}>0\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \ge c_{1,\epsilon}
 \left\{\frac1n+\frac d{n\log(en)}\right\}
 \ge c_{1,\epsilon}\frac d{n\log(en)}.
\]
Both applications are valid for every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\): their embedded causal laws have propensity \(1/2\), and the overlap condition is imposed only on supported cells.

For nonnegative \(u\) and \(v\), \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\).  Applying this identity to the preceding two lower bounds yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \ge \frac12\min\{c_0,c_{1,\epsilon}\}
 \left\{\frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac d{n\log(en)}\right\}.
\]
Set \(c_\epsilon:=\frac12\min\{c_0,c_{1,\epsilon}\}\).

For the upper bound, the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\) is no larger than the worst-case risk of any particular measurable estimator.  The estimator \(\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}\) in \(\mathrm{def:diagonal-adaptive-estimator}\) is measurable and data driven, and \(\mathrm{lem:empirical-cellwise-ratio-upper}\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 &\le \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P\!\left[
  \left\{\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}-V^\star(P)\right\}^2
 \right]\\
 &\le \min\!\left\{1,
   \left(\frac12+\frac6\epsilon\right)\frac dn
  \right\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Take \(C_\epsilon:=1/2+6/\epsilon\), decreasing \(c_\epsilon\) if necessary so that \(c_\epsilon\le C_\epsilon\).  This proves the finite-sample bracket.  The estimator's divisions use the declared zero-denominator convention, and fixed overlap is required only for cells with positive mass, exactly as verified in the upper-bound lemma.

The ratio of the paired-sign term to the normalized-distance term is
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d/n}{d/\{n\log(en)\}}
 =\frac{\log(en)}{\sqrt d}.
\]
Consequently the two terms balance at \(d\asymp\{\log(en)\}^2\); the first dominates below this scale and the second above it.

Consider next any sequence in the displayed range.  If \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\to0\), the normalized-distance lower term forces
\[
 \frac d{n\log(en)}\longrightarrow0,
\]
which is equivalent to \(d=o(n\log n)\).  Conversely, if \(d=o(n)\), the upper bound \(C_\epsilon d/n\) tends to zero.  These are respectively the asserted necessary and sufficient conditions; the interval between them is not resolved by the bracket.

Finally, if \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=O(n^{-1})\), then the paired-sign lower bound implies \(\sqrt d=O(1)\), hence \(d=O(1)\).  Conversely, when \(d=O(1)\), the upper bound is \(O(n^{-1})\), while \(d\ge2\) makes the paired-sign lower bound at least \(c_\epsilon\sqrt2/n\).  Therefore \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=\Theta(n^{-1})\) if and only if \(d=O(1)\).  Outside that bounded-alphabet boundary the certified lower and upper rates differ, so the proof makes no matching-achievability claim.
