\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(L=\log(en)\).  We prove two overlapping bands.

\emph{The paired-sign band.}
Let \(k=2\lfloor d/2\rfloor\), let \(Q_x=1/k\) for \(x\le k\), and pad the remaining coordinates by zero.  For \(\sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{k/2})\in\{-1,1\}^{k/2}\) and \(0<\alpha\le1/2\), set
\[
 p_{\sigma,2j-1}=\frac{1+\alpha\sigma_j}{k},
 \qquad
 p_{\sigma,2j}=\frac{1-\alpha\sigma_j}{k}.
\]
These coordinates are nonnegative and sum to one.  Moreover,
\[
 \lVert\mathbf P_\sigma-\mathbf Q\rVert_1
 =\sum_{j=1}^{k/2}\frac{2\alpha}{k}=\alpha.
\]
Let \(M\) be the uniform mixture of the \(n\)-sample laws \(\mathbf P_\sigma^n\), and use \(\mathbf Q^n\) as the null law.  The likelihood-ratio second-moment identity gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 1+\chi^2(M,\mathbf Q^n)
 &=E_{\sigma,\tau}\left[
  1+\frac{2\alpha^2}{k}
       \sum_{j=1}^{k/2}\sigma_j\tau_j
  \right]^n\\
 &\le E_{\sigma,\tau}\exp\!\left{
  \frac{2n\alpha^2}{k}
       \sum_{j=1}^{k/2}\sigma_j\tau_j
 \right}\\
 &=\left\{\cosh\left(\frac{2n\alpha^2}{k}\right)\right\}^{k/2}
 \le\exp\left(\frac{n^2\alpha^4}{k}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(1+t\le e^t\), independence of the Rademacher products, and \(\cosh t\le e^{t^2/2}\) justify the three successive steps.  Choose
\[
 \alpha^2=c_0\frac{\sqrt{k}}n
\]
with a sufficiently small universal \(c_0\); when the displayed value exceeds \(1/4\), use \(\alpha=1/2\).  The chi-square distance is then bounded by a constant smaller than one after reducing \(c_0\).  The inequality \(\lVert M-\mathbf Q^n\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\sqrt{\chi^2(M,\mathbf Q^n)/2}\) and the usual nearest-target test imply that every estimator has maximum squared risk at least \(c\alpha^2\).  The second sample, drawn from the same fixed \(\mathbf Q\) under null and alternatives, contains no information for this test.  Thus
\[
 \mathfrak R^{L_1}_{n,d}
 \ge c\min\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt{k}}n\right\}.
\]
If \(d\le C_1L^2\), then \(k\ge d/2\) and
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{k}}n\ge c\frac{d}{nL}.
\]
The capped case is no smaller because \(d/(nL)\le\rho\) in the stated range.  This proves the desired bound throughout the lower band, including the finite small-\(n\) cases after decreasing \(c\).

\emph{The uniform known-\(\mathbf Q\) band.}
Take \(Q_x=1/d\).  For \(d\le\rho nL\), the functional in \(\mathrm{lem:jiao-han-weissman-known-q-lower}\) equals
\[
 T_{n,d}(\mathbf Q)
 =d\sqrt{\frac{1/d}{n\log n}}
 =\sqrt{\frac d{n\log n}},
\]
with \(L\) replacing \(\log n\) for the finitely many small values of \(n\).  If \(d\ge C_2L^2\), then
\[
 T_{n,d}(\mathbf Q)
 \ge C'\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
\]
Also
\[
 \frac{T_{n,d}(\mathbf Q)}{\sqrt d\log n/n}
 =\frac{\sqrt n}{(\log n)^{3/2}},
\]
which exceeds the required constant for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Since \(d\le\rho nL\), choosing the theorem's logarithmic constant below one gives \(\log n\ge C\log d\); finite exceptions are again absorbed by decreasing the universal lower constant.  The cited theorem therefore gives known-\(\mathbf Q\) risk at least
\[
 cT_{n,d}(\mathbf Q)^2
 =c\frac d{n\log n}.
\]
An experiment in which \(\mathbf Q\) is unknown cannot have smaller minimax risk than the subexperiment in which it is fixed and revealed.  The fixed-multinomial comparison in the cited result transfers the displayed Poissonized bound, with only a universal constant change.  The two bands overlap after taking \(C_1\ge C_2\), and \(\log n\asymp\log(en)\) away from the absorbed finite cases.  This proves the lemma.
