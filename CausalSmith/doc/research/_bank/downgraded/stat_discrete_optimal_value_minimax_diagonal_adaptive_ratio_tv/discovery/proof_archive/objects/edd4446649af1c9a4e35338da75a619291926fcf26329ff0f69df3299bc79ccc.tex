\textbf{Proof.}
Fix (P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}).  On a null cell (p_x=0), set (mu_{0x}=mu_{1x}=0) for the proof; that cell has (overline N_x=0) almost surely and contributes zero to both the estimator and the target.  On a supported cell define
\[
 v_x=\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\mu_{ax},\qquad
 \widehat\mu_{ax}=\frac{\overline S_{ax}}{\overline N_{ax}},\qquad
 \widehat v_x=\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\widehat\mu_{ax},\qquad
 \widehat p_x=\frac{\overline N_x}{n},
\]
using the stated zero-denominator convention.  By (mathrm{def:oracle-value}),
\[
 \widehat V_n^{\mathrm{plug}}-V^\star(P)
 =T_1+T_2,
 \qquad
 T_1=\sum_x(\widehat p_x-p_x)v_x,
 \qquad
 T_2=\sum_x\widehat p_x(\widehat v_x-v_x).
\]
The first term is the centered sample average
\[
 T_1=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \left\{v_{X_i}-E_P(v_X)\right\}.
\]
Since (0\leq v_X\leq1), independent sampling gives
\[
 E_P(T_1^2)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(v_X)}n\leq\frac1{4n}.
\]

It remains to control the random ratios.  Conditional on (overline N_x=k\geq1), let
\[
 \pi_{1x}=\pi_x,qquad \pi_{0x}=1-\pi_x.
\]
Then (overline N_{ax}) has the binomial distribution (operatorname{Binomial}(k,\pi_{ax})).  Conditional on (overline N_{ax}=j>0), the corresponding (j) outcomes are independent Bernoulli variables of mean (mu_{ax}).  Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P\!\left[(\widehat\mu_{ax}-\mu_{ax})^2
       \mid \overline N_x=k\right]
 &\leq \frac14E\!\left[
       \frac{\mathbf 1\{\overline N_{ax}>0\}}{\overline N_{ax}}
       \mathrel{\Big|}\overline N_x=k\right]
       +P(\overline N_{ax}=0\mid\overline N_x=k)\\
 &\leq \frac1{2k\pi_{ax}}+e^{-k\pi_{ax}}
 \leq \frac{3}{2k\pi_{ax}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For the second inequality, (j^{-1}\leq2(j+1)^{-1}) for (j\geq1) and the binomial identity
\[
 E\!\left[\frac1{\overline N_{ax}+1}\mathrel{\Big|}\overline N_x=k\right]
 =\frac{1-(1-\pi_{ax})^{k+1}}{(k+1)\pi_{ax}}
 \leq\frac1{k\pi_{ax}}
\]
were used.  The final inequality follows from (e^{-t}\leq t^{-1}) for (t>0).  Fixed overlap supplies (pi_{ax}\geq\epsilon) for both arms.

The map ((z_0,z_1)\mapsto\max\{z_0,z_1\}) is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm.  Since (sum_x\widehat p_x=1), weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and the preceding conditional bound give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P(T_2^2)
 &\leq E_P\!\left[
   \sum_x\widehat p_x(\widehat v_x-v_x)^2
 \right]\\
 &\leq \sum_xE_P\!\left[
   \frac{\overline N_x}{n}
   \sum_{a=0}^1(\widehat\mu_{ax}-\mu_{ax})^2
 \right]\\
 &\leq \frac{3d}{n\epsilon}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, on the event (overline N_x=kgeq1), multiplication by (k/n) cancels the (k^{-1}) in the conditional ratio bound, and on (overline N_x=0) the summand is zero.  Therefore
\[
 E_P\!\left[
   \{\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{plug}}-V^\star(P)\}^2
 \right]
 \leq 2E_P(T_1^2)+2E_P(T_2^2)
 \leq \frac1{2n}+\frac{6d}{n\epsilon}
 \leq C_\epsilon\frac d n.
\]
Finally, (widehat V_n^{\mathrm{plug}}\in[0,1]) because it is a convex combination of numbers in ([0,1]), while (V^\star(P)\in[0,1]).  Thus the squared loss is also at most one.  Taking the supremum over (P) proves the asserted minimum of the two bounds.  All divisions have been covered by the zero-denominator convention, and fixed overlap was used only on supported cells.
