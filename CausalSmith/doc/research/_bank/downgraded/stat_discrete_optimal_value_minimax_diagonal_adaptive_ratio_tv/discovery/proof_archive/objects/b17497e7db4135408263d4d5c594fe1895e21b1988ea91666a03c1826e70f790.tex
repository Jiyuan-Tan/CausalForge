\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(L_n=\log(en)\) and \(d_n=\max\{2,\lfloor L_n\rfloor\}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(2\le d_n\le n^2\) and \(\lfloor L_n\rfloor\ge L_n/2\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt{d_n}}{4096\sqrt2\,n}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt{L_n}}{8192n}.
\]

Suppose, to the contrary, that finite \(C_\epsilon\), positive \(\rho_\epsilon\), and estimators with the asserted uniform risk certificate existed.  Since
\[
 \frac{d_n}{nL_n}\longrightarrow0,
\]
the point \(d=d_n\) lies in \(2\le d\le\rho_\epsilon nL_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  By \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\), the assumed estimator bound would imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d_n}{nL_n}\right\}
 \le\frac{2C_\epsilon}{n}.
\]
This contradicts the preceding lower bound because \(\sqrt{L_n}\to\infty\).  A finite computable four-cell construction carrying the proposed certificate would in particular define such a measurable estimator collection, so it is excluded by the same contradiction.

For the final assertion, consider any sequence with \(2\le d\le n^2\).  If \(d=O(1)\), \(\mathrm{thm:global-elementary-minimax-bracket}\) gives the upper bound \(O(n^{-1})\), and the parametric floor contained in that theorem gives the matching \(\Omega(n^{-1})\) bound.  Conversely, the paired-sign lemma gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt d}{4096\sqrt2\,n}.
\]
Thus an \(O(n^{-1})\) risk bound forces \(\sqrt d=O(1)\), equivalently \(d=O(1)\).  This proves both directions.
