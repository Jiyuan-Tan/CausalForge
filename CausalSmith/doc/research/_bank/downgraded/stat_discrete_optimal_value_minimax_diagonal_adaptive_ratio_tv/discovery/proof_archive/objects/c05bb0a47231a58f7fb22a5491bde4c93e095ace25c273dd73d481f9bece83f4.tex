\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(L_n=\log(en)\), \(A_{n,d}=\sqrt d/n\), and
\(B_{n,d}=d/(nL_n)\).  We first globalize the two lower obstructions.

For \(n\geq2\) and \(d\leq n^2\),
\(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c_1A_{n,d}.
 \tag{1}
\]
If \(d>n^2\), set \(m=2\lfloor n^2/2\rfloor\).  Then
\(2\leq m\leq n^2\), \(m\geq n^2/2\), and \(d\geq m\).  Applying (1) at alphabet size \(m\), followed by
\(\mathrm{lem:alphabet-monotonicity}\), gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq\mathfrak R_{n,m,\epsilon}
 \geq c_1\frac{\sqrt m}{n}
 \geq\frac{c_1}{\sqrt2}.
\]
For \(n=1\), the parametric floor in
\(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\) supplies a positive numerical constant.  Renaming constants, these cases show
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq c_2\min\{1,A_{n,d}\}
 \quad\text{for every }n\geq1, d\geq2.
 \tag{2}
\]

Let \(\eta>0\) be the range constant furnished by
\(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\), reduced so that \(\eta\leq1\).  If
\(d\leq\eta nL_n\), that lemma gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c_3B_{n,d}.
 \tag{3}
\]
If \(d>\eta nL_n\) and \(\eta nL_n\geq4\), put
\(m=\lfloor\eta nL_n\rfloor\).  Then
\(2\leq m\leq d\) and \(m\geq\eta nL_n/2\).  Equation (3) at \(m\) and alphabet monotonicity yield
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq\mathfrak R_{n,m,\epsilon}
 \geq c_3\frac{m}{nL_n}
 \geq\frac{c_3\eta}{2}.
\]
If instead \(\eta nL_n<4\), then \(n<4/\eta\), and the parametric floor gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq\frac1{1024n}
 >\frac{\eta}{4096}.
\]
Thus, after another renaming,
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq c_4\min\{1,B_{n,d}\}
 \quad\text{for every }n\geq1, d\geq2.
 \tag{4}
\]

For nonnegative \(A,B\),
\[
 \min\{1,A\}+\min\{1,B\}\geq\min\{1,A+B\}.
\]
Since a number bounded below by both \(c_2\min\{1,A\}\) and
\(c_4\min\{1,B\}\) is bounded below by one half of their sum with the smaller constant, (2)--(4) imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq c_\epsilon\min\!\left\{1,
       \frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac d{nL_n}
      \right\}.
 \tag{5}
\]

For the upper bound, the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\) is no larger than the worst-case risk of the particular measurable estimator
\(\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}\).  The estimator is defined on every sample outcome, including zero cell and arm counts, by the conventions in
\(\mathrm{def:diagonal-adaptive-estimator}\).  Therefore
\(\mathrm{lem:empirical-cellwise-ratio-upper}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P[(\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}-V^\star(P))^2]
 \leq\min\!\left\{1,
  \left(\frac12+\frac6\epsilon\right)\frac dn
 \right\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5) and (6) prove the displayed bracket.

The ratio of the two nonsaturated lower terms is
\[
 \frac{A_{n,d}}{B_{n,d}}=\frac{L_n}{\sqrt d},
\]
so they balance exactly at order \(d=L_n^2\).  If the minimax risk tends to zero, (5) forces
\(d/(nL_n)\to0\), which is equivalent to the stated necessary condition.  Conversely, \(d=o(n)\) makes (6) tend to zero.

Finally, if \(d=O(1)\), (5) and (6), together with \(d\geq2\), give
\(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=\Theta(n^{-1})\).  Conversely, if
\(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=O(n^{-1})\), multiplying (5) by \(n\) gives a uniform bound on
\[
 \min\!\left\{n,\sqrt d+\frac d{L_n}\right\}.
\]
As \(n\to\infty\), this forces \(\sqrt d=O(1)\), hence \(d=O(1)\).  The lower and upper exponents remain different away from bounded alphabets, so none of these algebraic consequences closes the intermediate region.  This proves every assertion.
