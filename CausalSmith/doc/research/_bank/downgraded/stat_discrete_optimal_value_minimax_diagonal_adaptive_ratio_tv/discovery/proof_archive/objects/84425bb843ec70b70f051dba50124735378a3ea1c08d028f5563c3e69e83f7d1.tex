\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), let \(L_n=\log(en)\), and take \(d_n=\lfloor L_n^{3/2}\rfloor\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(2\le d_n\le n^2\), so \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) applies and yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \ge \frac{\sqrt{d_n}}{4096\sqrt2\,n}.
\]
Because \(L_n/\log n\to1\) and \(d_n/L_n^{3/2}\to1\),
\[
 \frac{d_n}{\log n}\sim\sqrt{\log n}\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \frac{d_n}{n\log n}\sim\frac{\sqrt{\log n}}n
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus this is an admissible sequence for the question.  Multiplying the paired-sign inequality by \(n\log n/d_n\) gives
\[
 \frac{n\log n}{d_n}\mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \ge \frac1{4096\sqrt2}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{d_n}}
 \sim \frac1{4096\sqrt2}(\log n)^{1/4}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
This contradicts the existence of a finite all-sequence leading constant under the normalization in the statement.  In particular, no program, including \(\mathscr H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{dual}}\), can characterize and attain such a nonexistent finite universal constant.

Finally, the two certified lower terms are \(\sqrt d/n\) and \(d/\{n\log(en)\}\).  Their ratio is \(\log(en)/\sqrt d\), so they balance at \(d\asymp\log^2 n\); the first dominates below and the second above.  This algebra justifies the three stated residual normalizations, while the absence of matching upper bounds means that no exact constant in those narrower regimes is proved.
