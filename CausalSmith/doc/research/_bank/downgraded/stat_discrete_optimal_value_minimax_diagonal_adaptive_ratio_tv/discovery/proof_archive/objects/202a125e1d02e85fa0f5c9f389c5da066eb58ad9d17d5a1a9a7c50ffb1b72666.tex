\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(L_n=\log(en)\) and \(d_n=\lfloor L_n^{3/2}\rfloor\).  Then, as \(n\to\infty\),
\[
 \frac{d_n}{\log n}\sim\sqrt{\log n}\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \frac{d_n}{n\log n}\sim\frac{\sqrt{\log n}}n\longrightarrow0.
\]
Moreover, \(2\leq d_n\leq n^2\) eventually.  The paired-sign lower bound therefore yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{n\log n}{d_n}\,
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 &\geq
 \frac{n\log n}{d_n}
 \frac{\sqrt{d_n}}{4096\sqrt2\,n}\\
 &=\frac{\log n}{4096\sqrt2\sqrt{d_n}}
 \geq\frac{\log n}{4096\sqrt2\,L_n^{3/4}}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves the displayed liminf and rules out any finite leading constant uniformly over the stated sequence class.  In particular, an output of \(\mathscr H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{dual}}\) that purported to give such a universal finite limit would contradict this explicit sequence.  The calculation uses only the below-elbow \(n/\sqrt d\) obstruction; it neither proves nor refutes convergence under the separate above-elbow, below-elbow, or elbow normalizations listed in the statement, so those narrower exact-constant questions remain unresolved as claimed.
