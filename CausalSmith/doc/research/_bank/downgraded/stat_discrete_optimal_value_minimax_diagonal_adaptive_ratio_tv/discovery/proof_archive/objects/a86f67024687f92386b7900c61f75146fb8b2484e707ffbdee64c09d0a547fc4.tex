\textbf{Proof.}
The frontier theorem gives
\[
 c_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac d{n\log(en)}\right\}
 \le\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac d{n\log(en)}\right\}.
\]
Multiplication by \(n\log(en)/d\) gives
\[
 c_\epsilon\left\{1+\frac{\log(en)}d\right\}
 \le\frac{n\log(en)}d\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{1+\frac{\log(en)}d\right\}.
\]
Because \(d/\log n\to\infty\), the second term in braces tends to zero.  Taking the liminf and limsup proves the displayed bracket.  Neither inequality compares the upper and lower constants, and \(\mathrm{def:exact-constant-handle}\) only names an approximation-duality program without asserting convergence or a saddle-point identity.  Therefore no equality of the liminf and limsup, and no exact-constant attainment claim, follows from the frozen core.  The theorem states precisely the strongest conclusion that is proved.
