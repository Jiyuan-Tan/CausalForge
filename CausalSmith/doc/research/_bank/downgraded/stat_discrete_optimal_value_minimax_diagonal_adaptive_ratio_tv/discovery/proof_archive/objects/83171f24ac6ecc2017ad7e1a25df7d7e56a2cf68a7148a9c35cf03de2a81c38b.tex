\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and write \(L=\log(en)\).  Let \(\rho_1>0\) be the range constant supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\).  Choose
\[
 0<\rho_0\leq\min\{\rho_1,1/16\}.
\]
If \(2\leq d\leq\rho_0nL\), then \(L\leq n\) implies \(d\leq n^2\).  Hence \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\) give constants \(a_\epsilon,b_\epsilon>0\) such that
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq a_\epsilon\frac{\sqrt d}{n},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq b_\epsilon\frac{d}{nL}.
\]
Since \(\max\{u,v\}\geq(u+v)/2\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq c_{0,\epsilon}\left\{
    \frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac{d}{nL}
 \right\}
 \qquad(2\leq d\leq\rho_0nL)
\]
for \(c_{0,\epsilon}=\min\{a_\epsilon,b_\epsilon\}/2\).

We extend this lower bound to the saturated range.  Choose \(n_0\) so that \(\rho_0nL\geq4\) for \(n\geq n_0\).  When \(n\geq n_0\) and \(d>\rho_0nL\), put \(m=\lfloor\rho_0nL\rfloor\).  Then \(2\leq m\leq d\) and \(m\geq\rho_0nL/2\).  Null-cell padding in \(\mathrm{lem:alphabet-monotonicity}\) and the preceding nonsaturated bound imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq\mathfrak R_{n,m,\epsilon}
 \geq c_{0,\epsilon}\frac{m}{nL}
 \geq\frac{c_{0,\epsilon}\rho_0}{2}.
\]
For the finitely many \(n<n_0\), the parametric floor in \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\) gives \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq1/(1024n_0)\).  Decreasing the constant once therefore yields, for every \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq c_\epsilon\min\!\left\{1,
   \frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac{d}{nL}
 \right\}.
\]

For the upper bound, the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\) is no larger than the risk of the explicit statistic in \(\mathrm{def:diagonal-adaptive-estimator}\).  The already proved \(\mathrm{lem:empirical-cellwise-ratio-upper}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P\!\left[
  \{\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}-V^\star(P)\}^2
 \right]
 \leq\min\!\left\{1,
   \left(\frac12+\frac6\epsilon\right)\frac dn
 \right\}.
\]
Thus one may take \(C_\epsilon=1/2+6/\epsilon\).

It remains to verify the rate interpretations.  In the nonsaturated lower expression,
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d}{n}=\frac{d}{nL}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d=L^2,
\]
so the claimed elbow and dominance directions follow.  Along any sequence with \(n\to\infty\), consistency forces \(d/(nL)\to0\) by the lower bound, equivalently \(d=o(n\log n)\); the upper bound gives consistency whenever \(d=o(n)\).  If \(d=O(1)\), the upper bound and the parametric floor give \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=\Theta(n^{-1})\).  Conversely, if \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=O(n^{-1})\), then for all large \(n\) the minimum in the global lower bound cannot equal one, and
\[
 c_\epsilon\frac{\sqrt d}{n}\leq\frac Cn
\]
forces \(d\leq(C/c_\epsilon)^2\).  Thus the parametric order occurs if and only if \(d=O(1)\).  The unequal necessary and sufficient consistency conditions display, rather than conceal, the unresolved intermediate region.
