\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and suppose for contradiction that finite \(C_\epsilon\), positive \(\rho_\epsilon\), and estimators satisfying the displayed uniform bound exist.  Put
\[
 L_n=\log(en),\qquad d_n=\max\{2,\lfloor L_n\rfloor\}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 2\le d_n\le n^2,qquad
 d_n\le\rho_\epsilon nL_n,qquad
 \frac{L_n}{2}\le d_n\le L_n.
\]
The first inequality places \((n,d_n)\) in the range of \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\), and the second places it in the range of the supposed upper bound.  By \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\), the minimax risk is no larger than the worst-case risk of any particular measurable estimator.  The supposed bound would thus imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d_n}{nL_n}\right\}
 \le\frac{2C_\epsilon}{n}.
\]
On the other hand, \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt{d_n}}{4096\sqrt2\,n}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt{L_n}}{8192n}.
\]
Since \(L_n\to\infty\), this lower bound eventually exceeds \(2C_\epsilon/n\), a contradiction.  No measurable estimator can therefore satisfy the proposed bound uniformly.  A finite computable estimator with the requested approximation and covariance certificate would be a measurable estimator, so it is ruled out a fortiori.

It remains to verify the corrected parametric boundary.  Along any sequence with \(2\le d\le n^2\), the paired-sign lemma shows that \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=O(n^{-1})\) forces \(\sqrt d=O(1)\), equivalently \(d=O(1)\).  Conversely, when \(d=O(1)\), \(\mathrm{lem:empirical-cellwise-ratio-upper}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \le \left(\frac12+\frac6\epsilon\right)\frac dn
 =O(n^{-1}).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), bounded \(d\ge2\) also lies in the range of \(\mathrm{thm:elementary-minimax-bracket}\), whose parametric component gives \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\ge c_\epsilon/n\).  Thus bounded \(d\) yields risk of exact order \(n^{-1}\), completing the proof.
