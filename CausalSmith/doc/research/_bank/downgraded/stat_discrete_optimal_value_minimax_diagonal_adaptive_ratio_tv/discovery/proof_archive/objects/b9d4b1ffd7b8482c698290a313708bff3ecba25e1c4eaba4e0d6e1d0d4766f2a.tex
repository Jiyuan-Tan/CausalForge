\textbf{Proof.}
The lower inequality is \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\), after denoting its positive range constant by \(\rho_\epsilon\).  By \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\), the infimum over all estimators is no larger than the worst-case risk of the single measurable, data-driven estimator in \(\mathrm{lem:empirical-cellwise-ratio-upper}\).  That lemma gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P\!\left[
  \left\{\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}-V^\star(P)\right\}^2
 \right]
 \le\min\!\left\{1,
   \left(\frac12+\frac6\epsilon\right)\frac dn
 \right\}.
\]
Taking \(C_\epsilon=1/2+6/\epsilon\), and decreasing \(c_\epsilon\) if necessary, proves the finite-sample bracket.

Now let \(n\to\infty\) along a sequence satisfying the displayed range condition.  If \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\to0\), the lower bound forces
\[
 \frac d{n\log(en)}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so \(d=o(n\log n)\) is necessary.  If \(d=o(n)\), the explicit upper bound tends to zero, proving sufficiency.  If \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=O(n^{-1})\), multiplication of the lower bound by \(n\) forces
\[
 1+\frac d{\log(en)}=O(1),
\]
and hence \(d=O(\log n)\).  Finally, when \(d\ge2\) is fixed, the upper bound is \(O(n^{-1})\), while the parametric component of the lower bound is \(\Omega(n^{-1})\).  Thus fixed \(d\) gives minimax risk of order \(n^{-1}\).  The two pairs of necessary and sufficient conditions differ, so no assertion about the intervening regimes follows.
