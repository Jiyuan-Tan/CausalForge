\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and suppose for contradiction that finite \(C_\epsilon\), positive \(\rho_\epsilon\), and estimators with the displayed uniform risk bound exist.  Put
\[
 L_n=\log(en),\qquad d_n=\max\{2,\lfloor L_n\rfloor\}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 2\le d_n\le n^2,qquad
 d_n\le\rho_\epsilon nL_n,qquad
 d_n\ge\frac{L_n}{2}.
\]
The first two inequalities verify respectively the range of \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) and the range demanded by the proposed upper bound.  By \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\), the minimax risk is no larger than the worst-case risk of any particular estimator.  The supposed bound would therefore imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac1n+\frac{d_n}{nL_n}\right\}
 \le\frac{2C_\epsilon}{n}.
\]
On the other hand, \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt{d_n}}{4096\sqrt2\,n}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt{L_n}}{8192n}.
\]
Because \(L_n\to\infty\), the last lower bound eventually exceeds \(2C_\epsilon/n\), a contradiction.  Thus no measurable estimator satisfies the proposed rate uniformly.  Arithmetic computability and the requested approximation and covariance certificate are stricter requirements, so they are impossible as well.

It remains to establish the corrected parametric boundary.  Consider any sequence in the stated range.  For sufficiently large \(n\), that range is contained in \(d\le n^2\).  If \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=O(n^{-1})\), the paired-sign lower bound forces \(\sqrt d=O(1)\), hence \(d=O(1)\).  Conversely, if \(d=O(1)\), the upper inequality in \(\mathrm{thm:elementary-minimax-bracket}\) gives \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=O(d/n)=O(n^{-1})\), and its parametric lower component gives \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,epsilon}=\Omega(n^{-1})\).  Therefore bounded \(d\) is both necessary and sufficient for parametric-order mean-squared risk.
