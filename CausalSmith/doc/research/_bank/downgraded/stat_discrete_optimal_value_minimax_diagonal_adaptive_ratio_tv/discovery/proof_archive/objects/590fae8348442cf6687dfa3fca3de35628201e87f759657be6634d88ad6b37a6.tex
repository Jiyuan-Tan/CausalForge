\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Let \(\rho_\epsilon^{(1)}>0\) be the range constant in \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\), and set
\[
 \rho_\epsilon=\min\{\rho_\epsilon^{(1)},1\}.
\]
The elementary inequality \(\log(en)\le n\) holds for every integer \(n\ge1\).  Hence the displayed range and \(\rho_\epsilon\le1\) imply \(d\le n^2\).  The support condition \(d\ge2\) therefore permits application of \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\), which gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \ge c_0\frac{\sqrt d}{n},
 \qquad c_0=\frac1{4096\sqrt2}.
\]
On the same range, \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\) gives a constant \(c_{1,\epsilon}>0\) such that
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \ge c_{1,\epsilon}\left\{\frac1n+
       \frac d{n\log(en)}\right\}
 \ge c_{1,\epsilon}\frac d{n\log(en)}.
\]
For nonnegative \(u,v\), \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\).  Combining the preceding two inequalities thus yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \ge \frac12\min\{c_0,c_{1,\epsilon}\}
 \left\{\frac{\sqrt d}{n}+
       \frac d{n\log(en)}\right\}.
\]
This proves the asserted lower bound after naming its constant \(c_\epsilon\).

For the upper bound, \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\) implies that the infimum over all measurable estimators is no larger than the worst-case risk of the particular measurable, data-driven statistic \(\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}\).  The bound in \(\mathrm{lem:empirical-cellwise-ratio-upper}\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \le \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P\!\left[
  \left\{\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}-V^\star(P)\right\}^2
 \right]
 \le \min\!\left\{1,
   \left(\frac12+\frac6\epsilon\right)\frac dn
 \right\}.
\]
Take \(C_\epsilon=1/2+6/\epsilon\), decreasing \(c_\epsilon\) if necessary so that \(c_\epsilon\le C_\epsilon\).  This establishes the finite-sample bracket.  All divisions used by the witness estimator obey the zero-denominator convention in \(\mathrm{def:diagonal-adaptive-estimator}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:fixed-overlap}\) is required only on supported cells, as verified by the upper-bound lemma.

The equality
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d}{n}=\frac d{n\log(en)}
\]
holds exactly when \(d=\{\log(en)\}^2\), which proves the elbow description.  Now take any sequence in the displayed range.  If \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\to0\), the normalized-distance lower term forces
\[
 \frac d{n\log(en)}\longrightarrow0,
\]
equivalently \(d=o(n\log n)\).  Conversely, if \(d=o(n)\), the upper bound \(C_\epsilon d/n\) tends to zero.

If \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=O(n^{-1})\), the paired-sign lower term implies \(\sqrt d=O(1)\), hence \(d=O(1)\).  Conversely, if \(d=O(1)\), the upper bound is \(O(n^{-1})\), whereas the lower bound is at least \(c_\epsilon\sqrt2/n\) because \(d\ge2\).  Thus the risk is \(\Theta(n^{-1})\) exactly for bounded alphabet size.  The available lower and upper bounds differ outside that boundary, so no unproved matching achievability or additional consistency implication is asserted.
