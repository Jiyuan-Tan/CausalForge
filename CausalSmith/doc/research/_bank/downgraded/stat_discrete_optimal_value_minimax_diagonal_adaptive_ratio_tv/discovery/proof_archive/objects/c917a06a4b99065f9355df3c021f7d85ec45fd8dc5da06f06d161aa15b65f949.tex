\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and write \(L_n=\log(en)\).  The established range-limited lower lemmas first give constants \(b_\epsilon>0\) and \(\bar\rho_\epsilon\in(0,1]\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,j,\epsilon}
 \geq b_\epsilon\left\{
   \frac{\sqrt j}{n}+\frac{j}{nL_n}
 \right\}
 \qquad
 \text{whenever }2\leq j\leq\bar\rho_\epsilon nL_n.
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, because \(L_n\leq n\) for \(n\geq1\) and \(\bar\rho_\epsilon\leq1\), the displayed range has \(j\leq n^2\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{lem:paired-sign-small-alphabet-floor}\) gives the first term in (1), while \(\mathrm{lem:l1-lower-transfer}\) gives the second term there.  The inequality \(\max\{u,v\}\geq(u+v)/2\) combines them after decreasing \(b_\epsilon\).  The same transfer lemma also gives the all-\(n\), all-\(j\geq2\) parametric floor
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,j,\epsilon}\geq\frac1{1024n}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Every lower-bound submodel used here has propensity \(1/2\), so it satisfies fixed overlap for the declared boundary \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).

Choose an integer \(N_\epsilon\geq1\) so large that
\[
 \bar\rho_\epsilon nL_n\geq4
 \qquad(n\geq N_\epsilon).
\]
For such \(n\), put
\[
 M_n=\lfloor\bar\rho_\epsilon nL_n\rfloor,
 \qquad
 m=\min\{d,M_n\}.
\]
Then \(2\leq m\leq\bar\rho_\epsilon nL_n\) and
\[
 M_n\geq\frac{\bar\rho_\epsilon}{2}nL_n.
 \tag{3}
\]
If \(d\leq M_n\), (1) directly gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq b_\epsilon\left\{
   \frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac d{nL_n}
 \right\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
If \(d>M_n\), \(\mathrm{lem:alphabet-monotonicity}\), (1), and (3) instead give
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq\mathfrak R_{n,M_n,\epsilon}
 \geq b_\epsilon\frac{M_n}{nL_n}
 \geq\frac{b_\epsilon\bar\rho_\epsilon}{2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Since the capped expression in the theorem is at most one, (4)--(5) imply its lower bound for every \(n\geq N_\epsilon\), after replacing the lower constant by
\[
 c_{1,\epsilon}
 :=b_\epsilon\min\left\{1,\frac{\bar\rho_\epsilon}{2}\right\}.
\]
For the finitely many \(n<N_\epsilon\), alphabet monotonicity and (2), used with \(j=2\), yield
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq\mathfrak R_{n,2,\epsilon}
 \geq\frac1{1024n}
 \geq\frac1{1024N_\epsilon}.
\]
This dominates \((1024N_\epsilon)^{-1}\) times the capped expression.  Thus the asserted global lower bound holds with
\[
 c_\epsilon
 =\min\left\{c_{1,\epsilon},\frac1{1024N_\epsilon}\right\}>0.
\]

For the upper bound, the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\) is no larger than the risk of the measurable, full-sample statistic in \(\mathrm{def:diagonal-adaptive-estimator}\).  The uniform calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:empirical-cellwise-ratio-upper}\) applies for every \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq1\), including null cells and zero arm counts, and gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 &\leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P\!\left[
  \left\{\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}-V^\star(P)\right\}^2
 \right]\\
 &\leq\min\!\left\{1,
  \left(\frac12+\frac6\epsilon\right)\frac dn
 \right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Take \(C_\epsilon=1/2+6/\epsilon\), and decrease \(c_\epsilon\) if necessary so that \(c_\epsilon\leq C_\epsilon\).  This proves the global finite-sample bracket with an explicit data-driven upper-bound witness.

Before saturation, the ratio of the two lower-rate terms is
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d/n}{d/(nL_n)}=\frac{L_n}{\sqrt d},
\]
so equality occurs at \(d\asymp L_n^2\), with the stated dominance on either side.

Now let \(d=d_n\geq2\) be an arbitrary sequence.  If \(\mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}\to0\), the global lower bound forces
\[
 \min\left\{1,\frac{d_n}{nL_n}\right\}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and hence \(d_n=o(n\log n)\).  If \(d_n=o(n)\), the upper bound \(C_\epsilon d_n/n\) tends to zero, proving sufficiency.

Finally, suppose \(\mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}=O(n^{-1})\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), the right side of that assertion is smaller than \(c_\epsilon\), so the cap in the global lower bound cannot be active.  Multiplying the resulting inequality
\[
 c_\epsilon\frac{\sqrt{d_n}}n
 \leq\mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
 \leq\frac Cn
\]
by \(n\) shows \(d_n=O(1)\).  Conversely, if \(d_n=O(1)\), the empirical-ratio upper bound is \(O(n^{-1})\), while (2) is \(\Omega(n^{-1})\).  Thus the minimax risk has exact order \(n^{-1}\) exactly for bounded alphabets.  The differing certified lower and upper exponents elsewhere prove no sharper achievability claim.
