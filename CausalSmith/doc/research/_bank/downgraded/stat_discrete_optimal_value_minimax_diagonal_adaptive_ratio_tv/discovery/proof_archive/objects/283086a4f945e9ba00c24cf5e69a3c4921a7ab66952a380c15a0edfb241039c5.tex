\textbf{Proof.}
The membership, normalization, target identity, exact Markov simulation, decision-theoretic factor \(1/16\), and parametric floor are proved in \(\mathrm{lem:exact-l1-transfer-parametric-floor}\).  In particular, for every \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2\), the constructed four-cell masses sum to one, the completed law belongs to \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\), and
\[
 V^\star(P)=\frac12+\frac14\lVert\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\rVert_1,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq\frac1{16}\mathfrak R^{L_1}_{n,d},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq\frac1{1024n}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The same helper verifies that the independent input streams and independent kernel coins produce exactly \(n\) independent observations with the embedded causal law.  For completeness, total variation contracts under this kernel: for input laws \(R,S\) and any output event \(E\),
\[
 \left|\int\mathsf K_{n,d}(z,E)\,d(R-S)(z)\right|
 \leq\lVert R-S\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}},
\]
because \(0\leq\mathsf K_{n,d}(z,E)\leq1\) and total variation has the corresponding dual characterization.  The composed rule
\(\widehat L=4\widehat V\circ\mathsf K_{n,d}-2\) has squared error exactly sixteen times the causal squared error.  If the kernel makes it randomized conditional on the two streams, conditional Jensen's inequality replaces it by its conditional mean without increasing risk.  This establishes every decision-theoretic assertion in the statement.

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:two-band-l1-lower}\) to the middle inequality in (1).  Its paired-sign part gives the asserted bound below \(D\{\log(en)\}^2\), and its uniform-\(\mathbf Q\) specialization of \(\mathrm{lem:jiao-han-weissman-known-q-lower}\) gives the same bound above that threshold.  Hence, for suitable positive constants \(c\) and \(\rho\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq c\frac d{n\log(en)},
 \qquad 2\leq d\leq\rho n\log(en).
 \tag{2}
\]
Combining (2) with the last inequality in (1) and using
\(\max\{u,v\}\geq(u+v)/2\) yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq c_\epsilon\left\{
       \frac1n+\frac d{n\log(en)}
      \right\}
\]
on that range.  The constants may be renamed as functions of \(\epsilon\).  In fact the embedded propensity equals \(1/2\), so the construction uses only the boundary condition \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  This proves the lemma.
