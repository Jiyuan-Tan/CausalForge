\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), put \(L=\log(en)\), and invoke \(\mathrm{thm:global-elementary-minimax-bracket}\).  Choose \(\rho_\epsilon>0\) small enough that
\[
 \rho_\epsilon\leq\frac1{16},
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\rho_\epsilon}+\rho_\epsilon\leq1.
\]
For \(2\leq d\leq\rho_\epsilon nL\), the elementary inequality \(L\leq n\) gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d}{n}
 \leq\sqrt{\rho_\epsilon\frac Ln}
 \leq\sqrt{\rho_\epsilon},
 \qquad
 \frac d{nL}\leq\rho_\epsilon.
\]
Thus the minimum in the global lower bound equals its second argument throughout the displayed range.  The global theorem therefore gives exactly
\[
 c_\epsilon\left\{\frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac d{nL}\right\}
 \leq\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P\!\left[
  \{\widehat V_n^{\mathrm{DAT}}-V^\star(P)\}^2
 \right]
 \leq\min\!\left\{1,C_\epsilon\frac dn\right\}.
\]

The equality \(\sqrt d/n=d/(nL)\) is equivalent to \(d=L^2\), proving the elbow and the two dominance statements.  Along sequences in this range, the global lower bound makes \(d=o(n\log n)\) necessary for consistency, while the explicit upper bound makes \(d=o(n)\) sufficient.  The global theorem also proves that order \(n^{-1}\) occurs exactly for bounded \(d\).  Since the lower and upper rates differ outside bounded dimension, neither the intermediate consistency region nor matching achievability is asserted.
