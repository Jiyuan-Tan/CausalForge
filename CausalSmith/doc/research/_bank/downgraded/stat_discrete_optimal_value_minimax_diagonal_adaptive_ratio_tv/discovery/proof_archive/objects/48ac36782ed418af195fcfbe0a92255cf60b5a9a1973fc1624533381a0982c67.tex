\textbf{Proof.}
Apply \(\mathrm{thm:global-elementary-minimax-bracket}\).  Let
\(L_n=\log(en)\), and choose the range constant in the present theorem so that
\(0<\rho_\epsilon\leq1/16\).  Since \(L_n/n\leq1\) for every \(n\geq1\), the restriction
\(d\leq\rho_\epsilon nL_n\) implies
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac d{nL_n}
 \leq\sqrt{\rho_\epsilon\frac{L_n}{n}}+\rho_\epsilon
 \leq\sqrt{\rho_\epsilon}+\rho_\epsilon<1.
\]
Thus the minimum with one in the global lower bound equals the sum of its two rate terms on the displayed range.  The upper chain is unchanged, and the global upper constant can be renamed \(C_\epsilon\).  This proves the displayed inequalities.

The equality
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d}{n}=\frac d{nL_n}
\]
is equivalent to \(d=L_n^2\), proving the elbow assertion and the stated dominance on either side.  The necessary condition for consistency, the sufficient condition, and the characterization of parametric risk are direct restrictions of the corresponding conclusions of the global theorem.  Because the lower and upper bounds differ whenever \(d\) diverges in the nonsaturated region, the displayed inequalities alone do not settle the intermediate consistency region or provide matching achievability.  This completes the proof.
