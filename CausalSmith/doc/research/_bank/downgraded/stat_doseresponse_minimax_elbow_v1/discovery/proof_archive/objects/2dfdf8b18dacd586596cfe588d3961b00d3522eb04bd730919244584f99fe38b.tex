Let \((\mathsf Y,\mathcal Y)\), \(e\), and \(\mathbf Y\) be furnished by Assumption~\texttt{ass:potential-outcome-path-typing}, and let \(K_x\) denote the conditional-law kernel \(\mathcal L(\mathbf Y\mid X=x)\) appearing in process-level conditional exchangeability. Define
\[
\overline m_P(a,x)=\int_{\mathsf Y}e(y,a)K_x(dy).
\]
Bounded kernel integration shows that \(\overline m_P\) is jointly Borel, and for each fixed \(a\) it is a version of \(\mathbb E[Y(a)\mid X=x]\). Process-level conditional exchangeability gives
\[
\mathbb E[e(\mathbf Y,A)\mid A,X]
=
\int e(y,A)K_X(dy)
=
\overline m_P(A,X)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
The path-typing identity is pointwise in both sample point and dose, so \(Y(A)=e(\mathbf Y,A)\). Consistency then yields
\[
\mathbb E[Y\mid A,X]=\overline m_P(A,X)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Because \(\mu_P(A,X)\) is another version of this conditional mean,
\[
0=\int\!\int
\lvert\mu_P(a,x)-\overline m_P(a,x)\rvert
\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x).
\]
Uniform positivity therefore implies
\[
\mu_P(a,x)=\overline m_P(a,x)
\quad\text{for }da\,dP_X(x)\text{-almost every }(a,x). \tag{1}
\]
For every fixed \(a\), the assumed function \(m_P(a,\cdot)\) and \(\overline m_P(a,\cdot)\) are versions of the same conditional expectation. Joint measurability and Tonelli's theorem imply
\[
m_P(a,x)=\overline m_P(a,x)
\quad\text{for }da\,dP_X(x)\text{-almost every }(a,x). \tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2) proves the asserted almost-everywhere bridge \(\mu_P=m_P\).

By Fubini's theorem, for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\), the bridge holds for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\), the section \(a\mapsto m_P(a,x)\) is continuous at \(t_0\), and \(a\mapsto\mu_P(a,x)\) is continuous by its Hölder condition. At such an \(x\), choose bridge points \(a_k\to t_0\), which is possible because \(t_0\) is interior. Passing to the limit gives \(\mu_P(t_0,x)=m_P(t_0,x)\). Hence, using the tower property,
\[
\theta_P(t_0)
=
\int\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx
=
\int m_P(t_0,x)\,dP_X(x)
=
\mathbb E[Y(t_0)].
\]
Proposition~\texttt{prop:estimand-version-invariant} makes the observed-data expression independent of the admissible designated versions.

For the final negative assertion, take the observed law \(X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]^d)\), \(A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([0,1])\), and \(Y=0\), with \(p_X=1\), \(\pi_P=1\), and \(\mu_P=0\). It lies in every admissible observed-data class by Proposition~\texttt{prop:class-nonempty}. Fix \(0<\eta\le M_Y\), take the standard Borel space \(\mathsf Y=\{y_0,y_1\}\), and define
\[
e(y_0,a)=0,
\qquad
e(y_1,a)=\eta\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}.
\]
In completion zero let \(\mathbf Y=y_0\) surely; in completion one let \(\mathbf Y=y_1\) surely. In both cases the random element is conditionally independent of \(A\) given \(X\). Since \(P(A=t_0)=0\), both completions obey \(Y(A)=0=Y\) almost surely and hence consistency, and both induce the same observed law. Nevertheless,
\[
\mathbb E[Y^{(0)}(t_0)]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[Y^{(1)}(t_0)]=\eta.
\]
The second completion has \(m_P(a,x)=\eta\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}\), which is discontinuous at \(t_0\). Thus the other three causal conditions alone do not identify the singleton-dose mean.
