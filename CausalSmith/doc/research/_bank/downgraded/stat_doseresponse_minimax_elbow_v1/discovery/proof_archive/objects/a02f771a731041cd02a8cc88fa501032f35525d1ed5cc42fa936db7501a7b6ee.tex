Write \(a_0=2+A_0^{-1}\), \(b_0=2+B_0^{-1}\), and \(C_0=2\alpha+1\). On every point with positive denominators,
\[
F_\mu'(z)=\frac{dC_0}{(C_0-a_0z)^2}>0,
\qquad
F_\pi'(z)=-\frac{dC_0}{\{C_0-b_0(\alpha-z)\}^2}<0.
\]
Endpoint evaluation, using \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\), shows that \(F_\mu\le F_\pi\) at the left endpoint of \(I\) and \(F_\mu\ge F_\pi\) at the right endpoint. Hence the maximum is minimized at the unique crossing.

Let \(\tau\) be the common value divided by \(d\). Solving each fractional equation for its numerator gives
\[
z=\frac{C_0\tau}{1+a_0\tau},
\qquad
\alpha-z=\frac{C_0\tau}{1+b_0\tau}.
\]
Their sum equals \(\alpha\) precisely when
\[
D_0\tau^2+E_0\tau-\alpha=0,
\]
where
\[
D_0=C_0(a_0+b_0)-\alpha a_0b_0
=4(\alpha+1)+\frac{A_0+B_0-\alpha}{A_0B_0}>0,
\]
\[
E_0=2C_0-\alpha(a_0+b_0)
=2-\frac{\alpha(A_0+B_0)}{A_0B_0}.
\]
The unique positive root is
\[
\tau=\frac{\sqrt{E_0^2+4\alpha D_0}-E_0}{2D_0},
\]
which is exactly \(g(\alpha,\beta,d)/d\). The displayed representations imply
\[
C_0-a_0z=\frac{C_0}{1+a_0\tau}>0,
\qquad
C_0-b_0(\alpha-z)=\frac{C_0}{1+b_0\tau}>0,
\]
so the crossing is in the admissible denominator region. The endpoint comparison places it in \(I\).

For an explicit branch description, let \(q=A_0^{-1}-B_0^{-1}\). The crossing equation is
\[
C_0(2z-\alpha)+qz(\alpha-z)=0.
\]
Thus
\[
z_*=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\alpha q+2C_0-\sqrt{\alpha^2q^2+4C_0^2}}{2q},&q>0\ \text{that is, }A_0<B_0,\\[6pt]
\alpha/2,&q=0\ \text{that is, }A_0=B_0,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{\alpha q+2C_0-\sqrt{\alpha^2q^2+4C_0^2}}{2q},&q<0\ \text{that is, }A_0>B_0.
\end{cases}
\]
The sign of \(q\) and the monotonicity already proved give respectively \(z_*<\alpha/2\), equality, and \(z_*>\alpha/2\). Expanding the square root at \(q=0\), or applying l'Hopital's rule, gives \(z_*\to\alpha/2\) from both sides. At the boundary \(A_0=B_0\), substitution gives the common threshold
\[
g(\alpha,\beta,d)=\frac{d\alpha}{2\alpha+2-\alpha/A_0},
\]
so the branches agree.

To compare with shared tuning, put \(m(z)=\max\{z,\alpha-z\}\) and \(r(z)=\max\{z/A_0,(\alpha-z)/B_0\}\). Whenever the shared denominator is positive,
\[
F_\mu(z)\le\frac{dm(z)}{C_0-r(z)-2m(z)},
\qquad
F_\pi(z)\le\frac{dm(z)}{C_0-r(z)-2m(z)},
\]
because the separated numerator is no larger and its denominator is no smaller. Taking maxima and then minima proves that the separated threshold is at most the shared threshold.

For \(\alpha=0.4\), \(A_0=0.4\), \(B_0=0.2\), and \(d=1\),
\[
D_0=4(1.4)+\frac{0.4+0.2-0.4}{0.4\cdot0.2}=8.1,
\qquad E_0=2-\frac{0.4(0.6)}{0.08}=-1.
\]
Hence
\[
g=\frac{1+\sqrt{13.96}}{16.2}\approx0.29236.
\]
The shared-pilot formula gives \(0.4\cdot0.4/(1.8\cdot0.2)=4/9\approx0.44444\), proving strict improvement at this tuple.
