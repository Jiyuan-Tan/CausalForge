The two-point construction in \(\mathrm{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor}\) gives
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\ge C_-n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=C_-r_n(\alpha)
\]
uniformly over \(\beta\) and \(s\).  For the upper bound, apply \(\mathrm{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk}\) with the tuning supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:oracle-region-tuning}\).  The tuning is admissible for all sufficiently large \(n\), and gives \(\mathcal M_n\le C_+r_n(\alpha)\).  For the finitely many smaller sample sizes, the constant estimator zero has risk at most \(M_Y^2\); enlarging \(C_+\) makes the upper inequality valid for every \(n\).  If \(\beta<\alpha\), then \(\alpha\wedge\beta=\beta\).  The function \(x\mapsto2x/(2x+1)\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), so
\[
\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+1}<\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
The cited \(\mathrm{lem:bonvini-kennedy-treatment-smoothing-bound}\) is a conditional upper bound for a particular higher-order estimator, whereas \(\mathcal M_n\) infimizes over all measurable estimators.  The strict exponent comparison therefore does not contradict that published estimator-specific result.
