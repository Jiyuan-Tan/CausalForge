We construct two fuzzy hypotheses inside the class with the H\"older-seminorm convention of Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class}. Choose a nonzero function \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty((0,1)^d)\) with \(\lVert\varphi\rVert_\infty\le1\) that is antisymmetric under reflection of its first coordinate about \(1/2\). Then
\[
\int\varphi(u)\,du=0,
\qquad
\int\varphi(u)^{2r+1}\,du=0\quad(r\ge0),
\qquad
c_\varphi:=\int\varphi(u)^2\,du>0.
\]
Choose \(\eta,\zeta\in C_c^\infty((-1,1))\) such that \(\eta(0)=\zeta(0)=1\), \(\operatorname{supp}(\eta)\subset(-1/4,1/4)\), \(\zeta=1\) on \([-1/2,1/2]\), and \(\int\zeta(u)\,du=0\).

Put \(B_*=\alpha\vee\beta\) and
\[
D_*=4B_*+2+\frac{B_*d}{s},
\qquad
w=\kappa n^{-2/D_*},
\qquad
\epsilon=w^{B_*},
\]
where the fixed constant \(\kappa>0\) will be chosen below. Let \(m\asymp\epsilon^{-1/s}\) be an integer, put \(k=m^d\), and partition \([0,1]^d\) into \(k\) regular cubes \(C_1,\ldots,C_k\) of side \(m^{-1}\). If \(r_j\) is the lower grid corner of \(C_j\), define
\[
\varphi_j(x)=
\begin{cases}
\varphi(m(x-r_j)),&x\in C_j,\\
0,&x\notin C_j.
\end{cases}
\]
After a fixed contraction of the support of \(\varphi\), these functions have disjoint supports separated from the cell boundaries. They satisfy
\[
\int\varphi_j(x)\,dx=0,
\qquad
\int\varphi_j(x)^2\,dx=\frac{c_\varphi}{k},
\]
and scaled-bump calculus gives, uniformly over signs \(\sigma_j\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
\left[\sum_{j=1}^k\sigma_j\varphi_j\right]_{C^s}
\le C m^s,
\]
where \([\cdot]_{C^s}\) denotes the H\"older seminorm fixed in Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class}. Set \(\eta_w(a)=\eta((a-t_0)/w)\) and \(\zeta_w(a)=\zeta((a-t_0)/w)\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), their supports lie inside \((0,1)\), \(\int\zeta_w(a)\,da=0\), and \(\zeta_w=1\) on the support of \(\eta_w\).

For sign vectors \(\lambda,\nu\in\{-1,1\}^k\), write
\[
S_\lambda(x)=\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_j\varphi_j(x),
\qquad
S_\nu(x)=\sum_{j=1}^k\nu_j\varphi_j(x).
\]
Choose fixed positive constants \(a_\mu,a_\pi\), sufficiently small as specified below, and define
\[
p_X(x)=1,
\qquad
\pi_\nu(a\mid x)=1+a_\pi\epsilon S_\nu(x)\zeta_w(a),
\]
\[
\mu_{\lambda,\nu}(a,x)
=
\frac{M_Ya_\mu\epsilon S_\lambda(x)\eta_w(a)}
{\pi_\nu(a\mid x)}.
\]
Conditionally on \((A=a,X=x)\), let \(Y\in\{-M_Y,M_Y\}\) have mean \(\mu_{\lambda,\nu}(a,x)\). Since \(\int\zeta_w=0\), the function \(\pi_\nu(\cdot\mid x)\) integrates to one. Its treatment-direction H\"older seminorm satisfies
\[
[\pi_\nu(\cdot\mid x)-1]_{C^\beta}
\le C a_\pi\epsilon w^{-\beta}
=C a_\pi w^{B_*-\beta}
\le C a_\pi.
\]
On the support of \(\eta_w\), one has \(\zeta_w=1\); hence
\[
\mu_{\lambda,\nu}(a,x)
=
\frac{M_Ya_\mu\epsilon S_\lambda(x)}
{1+a_\pi\epsilon S_\nu(x)}\eta_w(a),
\]
and the function is zero off that support. The denominator is bounded away from zero under the positivity choice below, so the product and quotient rules for scaled smooth bumps give
\[
[\mu_{\lambda,\nu}(\cdot,x)]_{C^\alpha}
\le C a_\mu\epsilon w^{-\alpha}
=C a_\mu w^{B_*-\alpha}
\le C a_\mu.
\]
At \(a=t_0\), the same calculus and \(\epsilon m^s\asymp1\) give the seminorm bounds
\[
[\pi_\nu(t_0\mid\cdot)-1]_{C^s}\le C a_\pi,
\qquad
[\mu_{\lambda,\nu}(t_0,\cdot)]_{C^s}\le C M_Ya_\mu.
\]
Choose \(a_\pi>0\) so that all H\"older seminorm bounds involving \(\pi_\nu\) are at most \(L\) and, separately, so that
\[
a_\pi\epsilon\lVert S_\nu\zeta_w\rVert_\infty
\le\min\{1-c,1/c-1\},
\]
which ensures \(c\le\pi_\nu\le1/c\). Choose \(a_\mu>0\) so that all H\"older seminorm bounds involving \(\mu_{\lambda,\nu}\) are at most \(L\) and, separately, so that \(\lvert\mu_{\lambda,\nu}\rvert\le M_Y\). These simultaneous smallness conditions are compatible because \(0<c<1\), \(L>0\), and \(M_Y>0\); no lower bound such as \(L\ge1\) is needed. All designated functions are Borel. Thus every fixed-sign law belongs to \(\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)\).

At \(t_0\), reflection symmetry gives, cell by cell,
\[
\int_{C_j}
\frac{\lambda_j\varphi_j(x)}{1+a_\pi\epsilon\nu_j\varphi_j(x)}\,dx
=
-\frac{a_\pi\epsilon\lambda_j\nu_j}{k}C_\varphi(\epsilon),
\]
where
\[
C_\varphi(\epsilon)
=
\int
\frac{\varphi(u)^2}{1-a_\pi^2\epsilon^2\varphi(u)^2}\,du
\]
is bounded above and away from zero. Indeed, multiplying numerator and denominator by \(1-a_\pi\epsilon\nu_j\varphi_j\) splits the integral into an odd term, which vanishes by antisymmetry, and the displayed even term. Therefore
\[
\theta_{\lambda,\nu}(t_0)
=
-D_n\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_j\nu_j,
\qquad
D_n=M_Ya_\mu a_\pi\epsilon^2C_\varphi(\epsilon)
\asymp\epsilon^2.
\]
Let \(\Pi_1\) choose \(\lambda\) uniformly and set \(\nu=\lambda\). Under \(\Pi_1\), the target equals \(-D_n\) deterministically. Let \(\Pi_0\) choose \(\lambda\) and \(\nu\) independently and uniformly. Under \(\Pi_0\), the products \(\lambda_j\nu_j\) are independent Rademacher variables. Applying Lemma~\texttt{lem:bounded-sum-concentration} with \(R=1\), \(V=k\), and \(t=k/4\) gives
\[
\Pi_0\left\{\lvert\theta_{\lambda,\nu}(t_0)\rvert>D_n/4\right\}
\le
2\exp\left\{-\frac{(k/4)^2}{2(k+k/12)}\right\}
=2e^{-3k/104}.
\]

It remains to compare the observable mixtures. Let \(R\) be the law under which \(X\) and \(A\) are independent uniforms and \(Y\) is an independent symmetric random variable on \(\{-M_Y,M_Y\}\). The one-observation density of the fixed-sign law relative to \(R\) is exactly
\[
f_{\lambda,\nu}(y,a,x)
=
1+a_\pi\epsilon S_\nu(x)\zeta_w(a)
+a_\mu\epsilon\frac{y}{M_Y}S_\lambda(x)\eta_w(a).
\]
Define
\[
u_j(z)=a_\pi\epsilon\varphi_j(x)\zeta_w(a),
\qquad
v_j(z)=a_\mu\epsilon\frac{y}{M_Y}\varphi_j(x)\eta_w(a).
\]
Under \(R\), functions from different cells are orthogonal, and every \(u_j\) is orthogonal to every \(v_\ell\) because \(\mathbb E_RY=0\). Moreover,
\[
U:=\lVert u_j\rVert_{L_2(R)}^2=C_u\frac{\epsilon^2w}{k},
\qquad
V:=\lVert v_j\rVert_{L_2(R)}^2=C_v\frac{\epsilon^2w}{k}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\int f_{\lambda,\nu}f_{\lambda',\nu'}\,dR
=
1+U\sum_{j=1}^k\nu_j\nu_j'
+V\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_j\lambda_j'.
\]
Let \(L_1\) and \(L_0\) be the \(n\)-sample mixture likelihoods under \(\Pi_1\) and \(\Pi_0\), respectively, relative to \(R^n\). Averaging the last display over the sign priors yields
\[
\mathbb E_RL_1^2
=
\mathbb E\left[1+(U+V)\sum_{j=1}^k\xi_j\right]^n,
\]
\[
\mathbb E_RL_0^2
=
\mathbb E\left[1+U\sum_{j=1}^k\xi_j+V\sum_{j=1}^k\zeta_j\right]^n,
\]
where \((\xi_j)\) and \((\zeta_j)\) are independent Rademacher arrays. In the cross moment \(\mathbb E_RL_1L_0\), the two arrays of sign products are likewise independent Rademacher arrays, so
\[
\mathbb E_RL_1L_0=\mathbb E_RL_0^2.
\]
It follows that
\[
\mathbb E_R(L_1-L_0)^2
=
\mathbb E_RL_1^2-\mathbb E_RL_0^2
\le
\mathbb E_RL_1^2-1.
\]
Using \(1+x\le e^x\) and \(\cosh(t)\le e^{t^2/2}\), we obtain
\[
\mathbb E_RL_1^2
\le
\cosh\{n(U+V)\}^k
\le
\exp\left\{\frac{k n^2(U+V)^2}{2}\right\}
\le
\exp\left\{C\frac{n^2\epsilon^4w^2}{k}\right\}.
\]
Since \(k\asymp\epsilon^{-d/s}=w^{-B_*d/s}\),
\[
\frac{n^2\epsilon^4w^2}{k}
\asymp
n^2w^{4B_*+2+B_*d/s}
=
\kappa^{D_*}.
\]
Choose \(\kappa\) so small that the preceding exponential is at most \(5/4\). Then
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\overline P_1^n,\overline P_0^n)
\le
\frac12\{\mathbb E_R(L_1-L_0)^2\}^{1/2}
\le\frac14,
\]
where \(\overline P_j^n\) denotes the observable mixture under \(\Pi_j\).

For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), use the test \(\psi=\mathbf 1\{T<-D_n/2\}\). Under \(\Pi_1\), the event \(\psi=0\) implies squared error at least \(D_n^2/4\). Under \(\Pi_0\), on the event \(\lvert\theta_{\lambda,\nu}(t_0)\rvert\le D_n/4\), the event \(\psi=1\) implies squared error at least \(D_n^2/16\). Hence the larger of the two prior-averaged risks is at least
\[
\frac{D_n^2}{32}
\left[1-\operatorname{TV}(\overline P_1^n,\overline P_0^n)-2e^{-3k/104}\right].
\]
Because \(k\to\infty\), the bracket is bounded below by a positive constant for all sufficiently large \(n\). Thus there are constants \(c_0,c_1>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\sup_{\lambda,\nu}
\mathbb E_{\lambda,\nu}[(T-\theta_{\lambda,\nu}(t_0))^2]
\ge c_0D_n^2
\ge c_1\epsilon^4.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(T\in\mathfrak T_n\) proves the fuzzy lower bound. Finally,
\[
\epsilon^4=w^{4B_*}
\asymp
n^{-8B_*/(4B_*+2+B_*d/s)}
=
n^{-8/(4+2/B_*+d/s)}.
\]
Combining this inequality with Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} gives the displayed minimum of the two exponents. Direct cross-multiplication shows that \(r_{\mathrm f}<2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\) exactly when
\[
\frac ds>4+\frac4\alpha-\frac{2}{B_*}
=4+\frac4\alpha-\frac{2}{\alpha\vee\beta}.
\]
This proves the strict-improvement characterization.
