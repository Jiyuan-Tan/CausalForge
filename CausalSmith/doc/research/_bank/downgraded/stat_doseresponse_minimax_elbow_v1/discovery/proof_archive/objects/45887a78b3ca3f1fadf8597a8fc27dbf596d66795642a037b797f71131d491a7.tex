Put \(A=A_0\), \(B=B_0\), \(S=S_0\), \(C=2\alpha+1\), \(z=z_*\), and \(q=\alpha-z\). Feasibility gives \(0<z\le A\) and \(0<q\le B\). Under the displayed powers,
\[
\delta_\mu^S\asymp b_\mu^A\asymp n^{-kz},
\qquad
\delta_\pi^S\asymp b_\pi^B\asymp n^{-kq}.
\]
Since \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\in\Omega\),
\[
S\ge g(\alpha,\beta,d)\ge F_\mu(z),
\qquad
S\ge g(\alpha,\beta,d)\ge F_\pi(z).
\]
The first inequality is equivalent to \(dz/S\le C-z/A-2z\), and consequently
\[
(nb_\mu\delta_\mu^d)^{-1/2}
\asymp n^{-\{1-kz/A-kdz/S\}/2}
\le n^{-kz}.
\]
The same calculation with \(q\) gives \((nb_\pi\delta_\pi^d)^{-1/2}\le Cn^{-kq}\). Hence \(u=O(n^{-kz})\), \(v=O(n^{-kq})\). Since \(z\le A\) and \(q\le B\),
\[
\{(u+h^A)(v+h^B)\}^2
=O(n^{-2k(z+q)})=O(n^{-2k\alpha}).
\]
Also \(h^{2\alpha}\asymp n^{-2k\alpha}\), \((nh)^{-1}\asymp n^{-(1-k)}=n^{-2k\alpha}\), \(n^{-1}=O(n^{-2k\alpha})\), and \(e^{-c_1nh}=o(n^{-2k\alpha})\). The positive cell-count exponents make every tuning restriction valid for sufficiently large \(n\). Substitution proves the claim.
