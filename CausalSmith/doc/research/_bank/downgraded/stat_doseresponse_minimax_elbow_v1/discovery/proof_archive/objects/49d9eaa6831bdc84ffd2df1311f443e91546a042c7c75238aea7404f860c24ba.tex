When \(c=1\), Assumptions~\texttt{ass:positivity-lower} and~\texttt{ass:positivity-upper} give
\[
1\le \pi_P(a\mid x)\le 1
\]
for every \((a,x)\), and hence \(\pi_P(a\mid x)=1\) pointwise. It follows that \(A\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and independent of \(X\). Consequently, a version of the marginal regression of \(Y\) on \(A\) is
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a]
=
\int \mu_P(a,x)p_X(x)\,dx
=
\theta_P(a).
\]
Lemma~\texttt{lem:partial-mean-holder-transfer} shows that \(\theta_P\) is uniformly H\"older of order \(\alpha\).

We construct a single data-driven estimator. Let \(p=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\), let \(S(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^p)^\top\), and choose a bounded nonnegative kernel \(K\), supported on \([-1,1]\), that is bounded below on a neighbourhood of zero. Put
\[
K_h(a)=h^{-1}K((a-t_0)/h),
\qquad
S_h(a)=S((a-t_0)/h),
\]
and, for \(h\le \min(t_0,1-t_0)\), define
\[
\widehat D_h=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nK_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)^\top,
\qquad
\widehat R_h=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nK_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)Y_i.
\]
The fixed matrix
\[
D=\int_{-1}^1K(u)S(u)S(u)^\top\,du
\]
is positive definite. Write \(\lambda_0=\lambda_{\min}(D)>0\), and set
\[
\widehat\theta_h
=
\operatorname{clip}_{[-M_Y,M_Y]}
\left[
 e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h
 \mathbf 1\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0/2\}
\right],
\]
with value zero inside the brackets on the complementary event. This is a Borel element of \(\mathfrak T_n\).

Because the density of \(A\) is one, \(\mathbb E_P\widehat D_h=D\). Define
\[
b_h=D^{-1}\mathbb E_P[K_h(A)S_h(A)Y],
\qquad
\vartheta_h=e_0^\top b_h.
\]
The identity
\[
e_0^\top D^{-1}
\int_{-1}^1K(u)S(u)q(t_0+hu)\,du=q(t_0)
\]
holds for every polynomial \(q\) of degree at most \(p\). A Taylor polynomial for the H\"older function \(\theta_P\), with the bounded-derivative convention at integer orders from Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class}, therefore gives
\[
\lvert\vartheta_h-\theta_P(t_0)\rvert\le C h^\alpha.
\]
The constant is uniform over the model class. Moreover, boundedness of \(Y\), boundedness of \(S\) on \([-1,1]\), and invertibility of \(D\) give \(\lVert b_h\rVert\le C\).

Put
\[
W_i=K_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)\{Y_i-S_h(A_i)^\top b_h\}.
\]
Then \(\mathbb E_PW_i=0\), \(\lVert W_i\rVert\le C/h\), and \(\mathbb E_P\lVert W_i\rVert^2\le C/h\). On the event \(G_h=\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0/2\}\),
\[
e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h-\vartheta_h
=
e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nW_i.
\]
Hence independence and \(\lVert\widehat D_h^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/\lambda_0\) on \(G_h\) imply
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[
\left(e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h-\vartheta_h\right)^2
\mathbf 1_{G_h}
\right]
\le \frac{C}{nh}.
\]
Apply Lemma~\texttt{lem:bounded-sum-concentration} entrywise to the fixed-dimensional matrix \(\widehat D_h-D\), and then use the union bound and the inequality \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0-\lVert\widehat D_h-D\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\). This yields
\[
P(G_h^c)\le C\exp(-c_1nh).
\]
Clipping cannot increase squared loss because \(\theta_P(t_0)\in[-M_Y,M_Y]\). Combining the preceding displays gives, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\theta_h-\theta_P(t_0))^2]
\le
C\left(h^{2\alpha}+\frac1{nh}+e^{-c_1nh}\right).
\]
Choose \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\alpha+1)}\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), the bandwidth lies inside the two-sided neighbourhood of \(t_0\), and the last display is at most \(C_+n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=C_+r_n(\alpha)\). Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-mse} transfers this risk bound to \(\mathcal M_n\). The matching lower bound is Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor}, which applies also at \(c=1\).
