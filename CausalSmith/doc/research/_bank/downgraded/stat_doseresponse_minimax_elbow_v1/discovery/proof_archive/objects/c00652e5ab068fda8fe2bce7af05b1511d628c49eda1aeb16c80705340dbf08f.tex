On an interval of diameter one, the Hölder balls under the convention in \(\mathrm{def:holder\text{-}class}\) are nested in their order when the radius is fixed. Indeed, if the two orders have the same integer part, then \(\lvert u-v\rvert^{\beta^{\prime}-\lfloor\beta^{\prime}\rfloor}\le \lvert u-v\rvert^{\beta-\lfloor\beta\rfloor}\) for \(\lvert u-v\rvert\le1\). If an integer is crossed, the mean-value theorem and the bound on the next derivative give the required lower-order Hölder inequality; all derivatives required at the lower order are among those bounded at the higher order. Therefore every univariate Hölder-\(\beta^{\prime}\) section of radius \(L\) is also a Hölder-\(\beta\) section of radius \(L\). All other member properties of the two displayed law classes are identical, which proves the class inclusion. For any fixed estimator, its supremum risk over the larger class is at least its supremum risk over the subclass. Taking the infimum over the common estimator class preserves this inequality and gives the minimax conclusion.
