We prove the stated result over arbitrary Borel covariate laws. Fix \(P\in\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)\), and define
\[
\vartheta_P(a)=\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(a,x)\,P_X(dx).
\]
This definition uses the jointly Borel H\"older version \(\mu_P\) appearing in the definition of \(\mathcal Q_1\). It is version invariant. Indeed, if \(\mu_P\) and \(\mu'_P\) are two admissible versions, then, because the joint law of \((A,X)\) is \(da\,P_X(dx)\), the two versions agree for \(da\,P_X(dx)\)-almost every \((a,x)\). Fubini's theorem implies that, for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\), the two treatment sections agree for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\). Both sections are continuous in \(a\), so they agree for every \(a\), in particular at \(t_0\). Thus their integrals at \(t_0\) coincide.

Because the sections \(a\mapsto\mu_P(a,x)\) lie uniformly in the same univariate H\"older ball, integration against the probability measure \(P_X\) transfers every required derivative bound and the terminal H\"older remainder. More explicitly, the uniform derivative bounds dominate the relevant difference quotients, so dominated convergence permits differentiation under the integral. If \(j=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\) and \(\alpha\notin\mathbb N\), then
\[
\left\lvert\int\{\partial_a^j\mu_P(a,x)-\partial_a^j\mu_P(a',x)\}\,P_X(dx)\right\rvert
\le C L\lvert a-a'\rvert^{\alpha-j}.
\]
Under the integer-order convention, the same argument transfers the bounded highest derivative. Hence \(\vartheta_P\) belongs uniformly to a univariate H\"older ball of order \(\alpha\). No density or other regularity of \(P_X\) is used.

Since \(A\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and independent of \(X\), its joint law with \(X\) is \(da\,P_X(dx)\). The defining conditional-regression identity for \(\mu_P\) therefore implies
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a]=\vartheta_P(a)
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\). The H\"older function \(\vartheta_P\) supplies the continuous version whose value at \(t_0\) is the target; no value of an arbitrary conditional expectation on the null event \(\{A=t_0\}\) is selected.

We now give one estimator for the whole class. Put \(p=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\), \(S(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^p)^\top\), and choose a bounded nonnegative kernel \(K\), supported on \([-1,1]\) and bounded below on a neighbourhood of zero. For \(h\le\min\{t_0,1-t_0\}\), set
\[
K_h(a)=h^{-1}K\!\left(\frac{a-t_0}{h}\right),
\qquad
S_h(a)=S\!\left(\frac{a-t_0}{h}\right),
\]
\[
\widehat D_h=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nK_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)^\top,
\qquad
\widehat R_h=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nK_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)Y_i.
\]
The fixed matrix
\[
D=\int_{-1}^1K(u)S(u)S(u)^\top\,du
\]
is positive definite: if \(v^\top Dv=0\), then the polynomial \(v^\top S\) vanishes on an interval on which \(K\) is bounded below, and hence vanishes identically. Let \(\lambda_0=\lambda_{\min}(D)>0\), and define the Borel estimator
\[
\widehat\vartheta_h=
\operatorname{clip}_{[-M_Y,M_Y]}
\left[
 e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h
 \mathbf 1\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0/2\}
\right],
\]
where the bracketed expression is defined to be zero on the complementary event. This estimator depends only on \((Y_i,A_i)_{i=1}^n\), so it is equally well defined for singular and nonsingular \(P_X\).

Uniformity of \(A\) gives \(\mathbb E_P\widehat D_h=D\). Define
\[
b_h=D^{-1}\mathbb E_P[K_h(A)S_h(A)Y],
\qquad
\vartheta_h=e_0^\top b_h.
\]
The linear functional
\[
q\longmapsto e_0^\top D^{-1}
\int_{-1}^1K(u)S(u)q(t_0+hu)\,du
\]
reproduces every polynomial of degree at most \(p\) at \(t_0\). Applying it to the H\"older Taylor expansion of \(\vartheta_P\) gives
\[
\lvert\vartheta_h-\vartheta_P(t_0)\rvert\le Ch^\alpha.
\]
When \(\alpha\) is an integer, the bounded highest derivative gives the same order by the integral Taylor remainder. Boundedness of \(Y\), \(K\), and \(S\), together with invertibility of \(D\), also yields \(\lVert b_h\rVert\le C\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal Q_1\).

Put
\[
W_i=K_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)\{Y_i-S_h(A_i)^\top b_h\}.
\]
The population normal equation gives \(\mathbb E_PW_i=0\). Uniformly over the enlarged class,
\[
\lVert W_i\rVert\le Ch^{-1},
\qquad
\mathbb E_P\lVert W_i\rVert^2\le Ch^{-1}.
\]
On \(G_h=\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0/2\}\), exact algebra gives
\[
e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h-\vartheta_h
=e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nW_i.
\]
Since \(\lVert\widehat D_h^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/\lambda_0\) on \(G_h\), independence and centering imply
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[
\left(e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h-\vartheta_h\right)^2
\mathbf 1_{G_h}
\right]
\le
C\mathbb E_P\left\lVert\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nW_i\right\rVert^2
\le\frac{C}{nh}.
\]

For the Gram event, each centered entry of
\(K_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)^\top-D\) is bounded by \(C/h\), and the sum of its \(n\) variances is at most \(Cn/h\). Applying Lemma~\texttt{lem:bounded-sum-concentration} to every entry at a fixed threshold, followed by a finite-dimensional union bound and Weyl's inequality, gives constants \(C_0,c_0>0\), uniform over \(P\in\mathcal Q_1\), such that
\[
P(G_h^c)\le C_0e^{-c_0nh}.
\]
Clipping cannot increase squared loss because \(\vartheta_P(t_0)\in[-M_Y,M_Y]\), and the squared loss on \(G_h^c\) is at most \(M_Y^2\). Combining the bias, good-event variance, and bad-event bounds gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\vartheta_h-\vartheta_P(t_0))^2]
\le C\left(h^{2\alpha}+\frac1{nh}+e^{-c_0nh}\right).
\]
With \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\alpha+1)}\), the bandwidth restrictions hold for all sufficiently large \(n\), and Definition~\texttt{def:oracle-rate} therefore gives
\[
\mathcal N_n(\alpha,d,M_Y,L,t_0)\le C_+r_n(\alpha).
\]

For the converse, fix arbitrary \(\beta_0,s_0>0\). If \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta_0,s_0,d,M_Y,L,1,t_0)\), the two positivity inequalities force \(\pi_P(a\mid x)=1\) for every \((a,x)\). Hence \(A\) is uniform and independent of \(X\), and all the remaining defining conditions put \(P\) in the enlarged class \(\mathcal Q_1\). Moreover, the same designated jointly Borel regression version is used in both targets, and the designated density of the frozen-class marginal satisfies
\[
\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)\,P_X(dx)
=
\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx
=
\theta_P(t_0).
\]
Thus minimax monotonicity and Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} yield
\[
\mathcal N_n(\alpha,d,M_Y,L,t_0)
\ge
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta_0,s_0,d,M_Y,L,1,t_0)
\ge C_-r_n(\alpha).
\]
This proves the two-sided rate with exponent \(2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\).

Finally, the inclusion of every frozen \(c=1\) class is proper. Let \(X\) be uniform on \([0,1]^d\), let \(A\) be an independent uniform variable, choose \(0<\delta<\min\{M_Y,L\}\), put \(q(x)=\delta\mathbf 1\{x_1\le1/2\}\), and, conditionally on \((A,X)\), let \(Y\in\{-M_Y,M_Y\}\) have mean \(q(X)\). Then \(\mu_P(a,x)=q(x)\) is jointly Borel and constant, hence H\"older of every positive order, in \(a\), so the law belongs to \(\mathcal Q_1\). If it belonged to a frozen class, an admissible version continuous in \(a\) would agree with \(q(x)\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\) and \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\), and continuity in \(a\) would force its \(t_0\)-slice to equal \(q\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). No continuous, and hence no positive-order H\"older, function on \([0,1]^d\) equals the two distinct constants defining \(q\) almost everywhere on the adjacent open half-cubes. Thus the law is outside every frozen class with \(s>0\). In addition, taking \(P_X\) to be a point mass and \(Y=0\) gives a member of the enlarged \(\mathcal Q_1\) with no Lebesgue density at all. The inclusion is therefore proper for every \(\beta,s>0\).
