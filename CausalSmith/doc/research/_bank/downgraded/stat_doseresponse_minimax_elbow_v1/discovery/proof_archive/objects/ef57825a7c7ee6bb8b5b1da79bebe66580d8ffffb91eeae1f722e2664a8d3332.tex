Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)\), and let \(m_P\) satisfy the compatible-version conditions in the proposed statement. Both \(m_P\) and \(\mu_P\) are bounded by \(M_Y\), so their difference is integrable. The compatibility equality and uniform positivity give
\[
0=\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
\lvert\mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)\rvert\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x)
\ge c\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
\lvert\mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)\rvert\,da\,dP_X(x).
\]
The last integrand is nonnegative, and hence its integral is zero. By Fubini's theorem, there is a Borel set \(E_1\subseteq[0,1]^d\) with \(P_X(E_1)=1\) such that, for every \(x\in E_1\),
\[
\mu_P(a,x)=m_P(a,x)
\quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }a\in[0,1].
\]
By the continuity condition, there is a Borel set \(E_2\subseteq[0,1]^d\) with \(P_X(E_2)=1\) on which both maps \(a\mapsto\mu_P(a,x)\) and \(a\mapsto m_P(a,x)\) are continuous. For \(x\in E_1\cap E_2\), the continuous function \(a\mapsto\mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)\) vanishes Lebesgue-almost everywhere. If it were nonzero at some point, continuity would make its absolute value bounded away from zero on a relative open interval of positive Lebesgue measure, a contradiction. Therefore
\[
\mu_P(a,x)=m_P(a,x)
\quad\text{for every }a\in[0,1]
\quad\text{and for }P_X\text{-almost every }x.
\]
In particular, \(\mu_P(t_0,x)=m_P(t_0,x)\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). Since \(p_X\) is a density of \(P_X\), since \(m_P(t_0,\cdot)\) is a version of \(\mathbb E[Y(t_0)\mid X=\cdot]\), and since the relevant variables are bounded, the tower property yields
\[
\theta_P(t_0)
=\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx
=\int_{[0,1]^d}m_P(t_0,x)\,dP_X(x)
=\mathbb E[Y(t_0)].
\]
The observed-data quantity on the left is independent of the admissible designated versions by \(\mathrm{prop:estimand\text{-}version\text{-}invariant}\). Notice that neither consistency nor conditional exchangeability was used: once the displayed compatibility equality with the potential-outcome conditional mean is assumed, those premises are redundant.

It remains to prove the singleton-dose obstruction asserted in the proposed statement. Fix any admissible parameter tuple. Let \(X\) be uniform on \([0,1]^d\), let \(A\) be uniform on \([0,1]\) independently of \(X\), and let \(Y=0\). This observed law belongs to the model class with designated versions
\[
p_X(x)=1,\qquad \pi_P(a\mid x)=1,\qquad \mu_P(a,x)=0.
\]
Indeed, the versions are Borel, the two positivity bounds hold because \(0<c\le1\), the density bound holds because \(pbar\ge1\), and the two zero or constant functions lie in every displayed Hölder ball. Put \(\eta=M_Y/2>0\). Two causal completions of this same observed law are
\[
Y^{(0)}(a)=0\quad\text{for every }a\in[0,1],
\qquad
Y^{(1)}(a)=\eta\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}
\quad\text{for every }a\in[0,1].
\]
For each fixed \(a\), both potential outcomes are deterministic, so \(Y^{(j)}(a)\perp A\mid X\). Also \(P(A=t_0)=0\), whence \(Y=Y^{(j)}(A)=0\) almost surely. Thus both completions satisfy consistency and conditional exchangeability, yet
\[
\mathbb E[Y^{(0)}(t_0)]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[Y^{(1)}(t_0)]=\eta.
\]
They therefore have the same observed-data law and different fixed-dose causal means.

Finally, the second completion cannot satisfy the compatible continuous-version condition. If a function \(m_P\) represented \(\mathbb E[Y^{(1)}(a)\mid X=\cdot]\) for every fixed \(a\) and were continuous in \(a\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\), choose a sequence \((q_k)_{k\ge1}\) in \([0,1]\setminus\{t_0\}\) with \(q_k\to t_0\). For every \(k\), \(m_P(q_k,x)=0\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\), whereas \(m_P(t_0,x)=\eta\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). Intersecting these countably many full-measure sets with the full-measure continuity set gives, for every \(x\) in the intersection,
\[
\eta=m_P(t_0,x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}m_P(q_k,x)=0,
\]
a contradiction. Hence consistency and conditional exchangeability alone do not identify the causal mean at the null event \(A=t_0\), while the compatible continuous-version bridge closes exactly that ambiguity.
