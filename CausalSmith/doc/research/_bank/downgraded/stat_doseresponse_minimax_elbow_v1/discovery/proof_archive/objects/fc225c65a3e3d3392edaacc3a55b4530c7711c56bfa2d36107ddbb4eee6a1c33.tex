First replace the empirical \(I_X\)-fold marginalizations by their population counterparts. Conditional on the pilot fold, the exact remainder identity says that the resulting pseudo-outcome has conditional mean \(\theta_P(a)+B(a)\). The population local-polynomial intercept of \(\theta_P\) differs from \(\theta_P(t_0)\) by at most \(Ch^\alpha\). The equivalent weight in the bias proof has uniformly bounded \(L_1(f_A)\) norm, so its action on \(B\) is bounded by \(C\sup_{\lvert a-t_0\rvert\le h}\lvert B(a)\rvert\). The off-slice lemma consequently gives conditional bias at most
\[
C\left[h^\alpha+UV+Uh^{\bar\beta}+Vh^{\bar\alpha}+h^{\bar\alpha+\bar\beta}\right]
=C\left[h^\alpha+(U+h^{\bar\alpha})(V+h^{\bar\beta})\right].
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz and the pilot MSE lemma,
\[
\mathbb E U^2\le Cu^2,
\qquad
\mathbb E V^2\le Cv^2,
\qquad
\mathbb E[U^2V^2]\le C u^2v^2.
\]
For the last inequality one may condition cellwise on the counts: bounded responses give fourth moments of the same order as the squares of the displayed second-moment envelopes; equivalently, applying the reciprocal-binomial calculation in the pilot proof with powers two and four yields the bound directly. Expanding the squared conditional bias and using these inequalities gives
\[
\mathbb E[\mathrm{bias}^2]
\le C\left[h^{2\alpha}+\{(u+h^{\bar\alpha})(v+h^{\bar\beta})\}^2\right].
\]
The localized variance lemma contributes \(C/(nh)+C/n\) on the good-Gram event. Both the estimator and the target lie in \([-M_Y,M_Y]\) after clipping, so the bad-Gram risk is at most \(4M_Y^2P(\mathrm{bad})\le Ce^{-c_1nh}\). Clipping cannot enlarge squared error relative to a target in the clipping interval. Adding bias, variance, empirical-marginalization, and bad-event terms proves the inequality uniformly over the class. The construction uses only the frozen class properties, so it is a single measurable minimax witness rather than a law-dependent oracle.
