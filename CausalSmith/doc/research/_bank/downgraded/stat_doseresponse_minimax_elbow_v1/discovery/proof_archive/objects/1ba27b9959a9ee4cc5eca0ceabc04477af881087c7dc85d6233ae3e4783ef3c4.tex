First optimize the separated-pilot envelope. Take power tunings
\[
h\asymp n^{-\gamma},
\quad b_\mu\asymp n^{-r_\mu},
\quad\delta_\mu\asymp n^{-q_\mu},
\quad b_\pi\asymp n^{-r_\pi},
\quad\delta_\pi\asymp n^{-q_\pi}.
\]
For the regression pilot, the exponent of \(u+h^{A_0}\) in Lemma~\texttt{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk} cannot exceed
\[
\min\left\{S_0q_\mu,A_0r_\mu,
\frac{1-r_\mu-dq_\mu}{2},A_0\gamma\right\}.
\]
If the first three entries are all at least \(e\), then
\[
r_\mu\ge e/A_0,
\qquad q_\mu\ge e/S_0,
\qquad 1-r_\mu-dq_\mu\ge2e,
\]
which implies
\[\ne\left(2+A_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1}\right)\le1.
\]
Thus the largest possible regression-pilot exponent at a fixed \(\gamma\) is at most \(\min\{A_0\gamma,E_\mu\}\). This value is attained by taking
\[
e_\mu=\min\{A_0\gamma,E_\mu\},
\qquad r_\mu=e_\mu/A_0,
\qquad q_\mu=e_\mu/S_0,
\]
because then
\[
\frac{1-r_\mu-dq_\mu}{2}\ge e_\mu.
\]
The identical calculation for the density pilot gives the attainable exponent
\[
e_\pi=\min\{B_0\gamma,E_\pi\}.
\]
For every \(0<\gamma<1\), these choices have positive count exponents and satisfy all tuning restrictions for sufficiently large \(n\). Substitution in Lemma~\texttt{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk} gives the exponent
\[
\min\{2\alpha\gamma,2(e_\mu+e_\pi),1-\gamma,1\}.
\]
The term \(1\) is redundant because \(1-\gamma\le1\). The displayed objective is continuous on \([0,1]\), so it attains its maximum. It is positive at some interior \(\gamma\), while it is zero at both endpoints after taking the minimum; consequently an interior optimizer supplies admissible tunings. The preceding upper-envelope argument also shows that no other power choices in this master-risk bound give a larger exponent. This proves the formula for \(R_{\mathrm{sep}}\).

For the regressogram, Lemma~\texttt{lem:explicit-histogram-window-upper} gives, at \(h\asymp n^{-u}\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-v}\),
\[
R(u,v)=\min\{2A_0u,4S_0v,2S_0v+2B_0u,1-u-dv,1\}.
\]
Fix a candidate \(r>0\). After writing \(x=u/r\) and \(y=v/r\), the first three constraints \(R(u,v)\ge r\) require
\[
x\ge\frac1{2A_0},
\qquad y\ge\frac1{4S_0},
\qquad B_0x+S_0y\ge\frac12.
\]
The minimum possible normalized bandwidth cost \(x+dy\) is
\[
C_*=\frac1{2A_0}+\frac d{4S_0}
+\left(\frac12-\frac{B_0}{A_0}\right)_+
\min\left\{\frac1{2B_0},\frac d{2S_0}\right\}.
\]
Indeed, the lower corner has deficit
\[
\frac12\left(\frac12-\frac{B_0}{A_0}\right)_+
\]
in the last constraint, and filling one unit of deficit through \(x\) or \(y\) costs respectively \(1/B_0\) or \(d/S_0\). The remaining variance constraint is \(rC_*\le1-r\). Therefore the largest feasible exponent is \((1+C_*)^{-1}=R_{\mathrm{hist}}\). At an optimizer, \(u+dv=1-R_{\mathrm{hist}}<1\), so the count restriction holds for all sufficiently large \(n\).

The minimax definition permits using whichever of the two explicit estimators has the larger exponent for the fixed model-class indices. Combining their upper bounds yields
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\le C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}}.
\]
Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} and Definition~\texttt{def:oracle-rate} give the lower inequality.

It remains to locate equality with the oracle exponent. For every \(\gamma\in[0,1]\),
\[
\min\{2\alpha\gamma,1-\gamma\}
\le\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1},
\]
and equality is possible only at \(\gamma_0=(2\alpha+1)^{-1}\). Hence \(R_{\mathrm{sep}}\) equals the oracle exponent if and only if
\[
\min\{A_0\gamma_0,E_\mu\}
+
\min\{B_0\gamma_0,E_\pi\}
\ge\alpha\gamma_0.
\]
This holds if and only if there is \(z\in[(\alpha-B_0)_+,A_0]\) such that
\[
E_\mu\ge z\gamma_0,
\qquad E_\pi\ge(\alpha-z)\gamma_0.
\]
Writing \(C_0=2\alpha+1\), the first inequality is equivalent to
\[
D_\mu(z)=C_0-z/A_0-2z>0,
\qquad
S_0\ge\frac{dz}{D_\mu(z)},
\]
and the second is equivalent to
\[
D_\pi(z)=C_0-(\alpha-z)/B_0-2(\alpha-z)>0,
\qquad
S_0\ge\frac{d(\alpha-z)}{D_\pi(z)}.
\]
By Definition~\texttt{def:oracle-threshold} and Lemma~\texttt{lem:separated-threshold-optimization}, such a \(z\) exists exactly when \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\) and \(S_0\ge g(\alpha,\beta,d)\), which is precisely membership in \(\Omega\).

Finally, the strictly positive term \(d/(4S_0)\) in \(C_*\) gives
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}<\left(1+\frac1{2\alpha}\right)^{-1}
=
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}
\]
when \(\alpha\le1\). When \(\alpha>1\), it gives \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}<2/3<2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\). Thus both components of \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\) are strictly below the oracle exponent on the complement of \(\Omega\), which is exactly the union of the two residual regions. If \(B_0\ge A_0/2\), the positive-part term in \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\) vanishes. If \(\beta\ge1\), then \(B_0=1\), so both \(E_\pi\) and every occurrence of \(B_0\) are constant in \(\beta\). These observations prove the final assertions without claiming that either achievable invariance property is a minimax converse.
