Put \(a_0=2+A_0^{-1}\) and \(b_0=2+B_0^{-1}\). On the positive-denominator region,
\[
F_\mu'(z)=\frac{dC_0}{(C_0-a_0z)^2}>0,
\qquad
F_\pi'(z)=-\frac{dC_0}{\{C_0-b_0(\alpha-z)\}^2}<0.
\]
Their unconstrained crossing satisfies
\[
C_0(2z-\alpha)+\Delta z(\alpha-z)=0,
\qquad \Delta=A_0^{-1}-B_0^{-1}.
\]
For \(\Delta=0\) this gives \(z_c=\alpha/2\). For \(\Delta\ne0\), solving the quadratic and selecting the root in \((0,\alpha)\) gives
\[
z_c=\frac{2\alpha C_0}{2C_0+\alpha\Delta+\sqrt{4C_0^2+\alpha^2\Delta^2}}.
\]
Because one function increases and the other decreases, the maximum is minimized at the crossing when it lies in \(I\), at \(\ell\) when the crossing lies to the left, and at \(A_0\) when it lies to the right. This is exactly the clamp \(z_*\). Direct substitution, using \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\), shows that both denominators at the selected point are positive. Substitution of the three possible values of \(z_*\) gives the three formulas defining \(g\); equality of adjacent formulas at \(z_c=\ell\) or \(z_c=A_0\) follows because the same point is being evaluated.
