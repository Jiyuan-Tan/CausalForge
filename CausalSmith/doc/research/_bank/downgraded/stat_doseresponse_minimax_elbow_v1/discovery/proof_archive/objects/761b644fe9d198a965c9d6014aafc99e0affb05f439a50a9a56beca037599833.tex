Put \(\bar\alpha=\alpha\wedge1\), \(\bar\beta=\beta\wedge1\), and \(\bar s=s\wedge1\). Partition \([0,1]^d\) into half-open regular-grid cells \(B_1,\ldots,B_K\) of diameter at most \(C_d\delta\), where \(K\le(2/\delta)^d\). Split the sample deterministically into independent folds \(I_1\) and \(I_2\), of sizes \(N_1,N_2\asymp n\). On the first fold define
\[
\widehat p_j=\frac1{N_1}\sum_{i\in I_1}\mathbf 1\{X_i\in B_j\}.
\]
On the second fold, let \(\widehat m_j\) be the average of those \(Y_i\) for which \(X_i\in B_j\) and \(\lvert A_i-t_0\rvert\le h\), assigning the value zero when no such observation exists. Set
\[
\widehat\theta_{h,\delta}=\sum_{j=1}^K\widehat p_j\widehat m_j.
\]
This is a Borel element of \(\mathfrak T_n\): it is obtained from finitely many Borel indicators, counts, sums, and ratios with an explicitly assigned value at a zero denominator. Moreover, \(\lvert\widehat m_j\rvert\le M_Y\) by Assumption~\texttt{ass:bounded-outcome}.

Let \(p_j=P_X(B_j)\), and define
\[
q_h(x)=P(\lvert A-t_0\rvert\le h\mid X=x)
      =\int_{t_0-h}^{t_0+h}\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da.
\]
Choose \(h_0\le\min\{t_0,1-t_0\}\). Assumptions~\texttt{ass:positivity-lower} and~\texttt{ass:positivity-upper} then give, for \(0<h\le h_0\),
\[
2ch\le q_h(x)\le \frac{2h}{c}
\qquad\text{for every }x.
\]
For a cell of positive \(P_X\)-mass, put
\[
m_j=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X\in B_j,\ \lvert A-t_0\rvert\le h],
\qquad
\bar\mu_j=\mathbb E_P[\mu_P(t_0,X)\mid X\in B_j],
\]
and assign arbitrary values when \(p_j=0\), since such values are always multiplied by \(p_j\). By the regression-version assumptions and iterated expectation, the conditional mean at a fixed \(x\) among observations in the dose window is
\[
\eta_h(x)=q_h(x)^{-1}\int_{t_0-h}^{t_0+h}\mu_P(a,x)\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da.
\]
The seminorm convention in Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class}, together with the separate value bounds \(\lvert\mu_P\rvert\le M_Y\), implies
\[
\sup_x\lvert\eta_h(x)-\mu_P(t_0,x)\rvert
\le
q_h(x)^{-1}\int_{t_0-h}^{t_0+h}L\lvert a-t_0\rvert^{\bar\alpha}\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da
\le C h^{\bar\alpha}.
\]
Here and below \(C\) may depend on the fixed class parameters and on \(d\), but not on \(n\), \(h\), \(\delta\), or \(P\). Likewise, treatment-direction H\"older regularity of \(\pi_P\), with its values controlled separately by \(c\le\pi_P\le1/c\), gives
\[
\frac{q_h(x)}{2h}=\pi_P(t_0\mid x)+r_h(x),
\qquad
\lVert r_h\rVert_\infty\le C h^{\bar\beta}.
\]

On each cell, the coordinatewise H\"older seminorm conditions at \(t_0\) imply
\[
\operatorname{osc}_{B_j}\mu_P(t_0,\cdot)\le C\delta^{\bar s},
\qquad
\operatorname{osc}_{B_j}\pi_P(t_0\mid\cdot)\le C\delta^{\bar s}.
\]
Indeed, when the smoothness order exceeds one, the bounded positive-order derivatives supplied by the seminorm convention yield the displayed Lipschitz bounds; no zeroth-order seminorm bound is used. Let \(E_j\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}_j\) denote expectation and covariance under \(P_X(\cdot\mid B_j)\), and put \(w_h=q_h/(2h)=\pi_P(t_0\mid\cdot)+r_h\). Since \(E_jw_h\ge c\),
\[
\frac{E_j[\mu_P(t_0,X)q_h(X)]}{E_j[q_h(X)]}-E_j[\mu_P(t_0,X)]
=
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_j(\mu_P(t_0,X),w_h(X))}{E_jw_h}.
\]
The covariance with \(\pi_P(t_0\mid X)\) is bounded by the product of the two cell oscillations, and the covariance with \(r_h(X)\) is bounded by twice the oscillation of \(\mu_P(t_0,\cdot)\) times \(\lVert r_h\rVert_\infty\). Combining these bounds with the preceding control of \(\eta_h-\mu_P(t_0,\cdot)\) yields, uniformly in \(j\),
\[
\lvert m_j-\bar\mu_j\rvert
\le C\{h^{\bar\alpha}+\delta^{2\bar s}+\delta^{\bar s}h^{\bar\beta}\}.
\]
Because \(\theta_P(t_0)=\sum_jp_j\bar\mu_j\), it follows that
\[
\left\lvert\sum_jp_jm_j-\theta_P(t_0)\right\rvert^2
\le C\{h^{2\bar\alpha}+\delta^{4\bar s}+\delta^{2\bar s}h^{2\bar\beta}\}.
\]

It remains to control sampling error. The exact decomposition
\[
\widehat\theta_{h,\delta}-\sum_jp_jm_j
=
\sum_j(\widehat p_j-p_j)\widehat m_j
+
\sum_jp_j(\widehat m_j-m_j)
\]
separates the two independent folds. Conditional on \(I_2\), the first sum is an empirical-mean error of a function bounded by \(M_Y\); hence
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\left\{\sum_j(\widehat p_j-p_j)\widehat m_j\right\}^2
\middle| I_2\right]
\le \frac{M_Y^2}{N_1}
\le \frac Cn.
\]
For the second sum define
\[
Q_j=P(X\in B_j,\ \lvert A-t_0\rvert\le h).
\]
Positivity implies \(Q_j\ge2chp_j\). If \(N_j\) is the selected second-fold count in cell \(j\), then \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N_2,Q_j)\), and
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N_j>0\}}{N_j}\right]
\le\frac{2}{N_2Q_j}.
\]
For completeness, this follows from \(1/k\le2/(k+1)\) for \(k\ge1\) and the binomial identity
\[
\mathbb E[(N_j+1)^{-1}]
=
\frac{1-(1-Q_j)^{N_2+1}}{(N_2+1)Q_j}.
\]
Conditional on all cell counts, the centered nonempty-cell averages have zero cross-products. Therefore their weighted sum has second moment at most
\[
C\sum_{j:Q_j>0}p_j^2
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N_j>0\}}{N_j}\right]
\le
\frac Cn\sum_{j:Q_j>0}\frac{p_j^2}{Q_j}
\le\frac{C}{nh}.
\]
The contribution of empty selected cells is bounded in absolute value by \(M_YW\), where
\[
W=\sum_jp_j\mathbf1\{N_j=0\}.
\]
Since \(0\le W\le1\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[W^2]
\le\mathbb E_P[W]
=
\sum_jp_j(1-Q_j)^{N_2}
\le
\sum_jp_j e^{-N_2Q_j}
\le
\frac{CK}{nh}
\le
\frac{C}{nh\delta^d}.
\]
The penultimate inequality uses \(Q_j\ge2chp_j\) and \(x e^{-ax}\le1/(ae)\), while the last uses \(K\le(2/\delta)^d\). The centered term has conditional mean zero given the counts, so its cross-product with the count-measurable empty-cell term is zero. Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\left\{\widehat\theta_{h,\delta}-\sum_jp_jm_j\right\}^2\right]
\le C\left\{\frac1n+\frac1{nh}+\frac1{nh\delta^d}\right\}
\le C\left\{\frac1n+\frac1{nh\delta^d}\right\},
\]
where the final inequality uses \(\delta\le1\). Combining this sampling bound with the squared approximation bias gives, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\theta_{h,\delta}-\theta_P(t_0))^2]
\le C\left\{h^{2\bar\alpha}+\delta^{4\bar s}+\delta^{2\bar s}h^{2\bar\beta}+\frac1{nh\delta^d}+\frac1n\right\}.
\]
The condition \(nh\delta^d\ge2^{d+3}\) ensures that the deterministic half-sample sizes are uniformly proportional to \(n\) throughout the stated range. Taking the supremum over the class and then using this single Borel estimator as a witness in Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-mse} proves the first assertion.

Finally, for admissible bandwidth sequences \(h\asymp n^{-u}\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-v}\), the five terms in the risk bound have polynomial exponents
\[
2\bar\alpha u,\qquad 4\bar s v,\qquad 2\bar s v+2\bar\beta u,\qquad 1-u-dv,\qquad 1.
\]
If \(u+dv<1\), then \(nh\delta^d\to\infty\), so the cell-count condition holds for all sufficiently large \(n\). The slowest of these five terms therefore gives
\[
\mathcal M_n=O(n^{-R(u,v)}),
\qquad
R(u,v)=\min\{2\bar\alpha u,4\bar s v,2\bar s v+2\bar\beta u,1-u-dv,1\},
\]
as claimed.
