Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} gives, for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\ge C_-n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
=C_-r_n(\alpha),
\]
where \(C_->0\) is independent of \(n\). This lower bound is furnished by a two-point submodel with \(p_X(x)=1\) and \(\pi_P(a\mid x)=1\), and therefore applies at the declared \(\beta\) and \(s\).

For the upper bound, apply Lemma~\texttt{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk} with the tuning supplied by Lemma~\texttt{lem:oracle-region-tuning}. The membership condition \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\in\Omega\), together with Definitions~\texttt{def:oracle-threshold} and~\texttt{def:oracle-region}, makes those bandwidths and grid widths admissible for every sufficiently large \(n\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:oracle-region-tuning} then bounds every term in the master-risk envelope by a constant multiple of \(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\). Consequently, for some \(n_0\) and some \(C_+^0<\infty\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le C_+^0n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
=C_+^0r_n(\alpha)
\qquad(n\ge n_0).
\]
For \(1\le n<n_0\), the constant estimator zero belongs to \(\mathfrak T_n\). Since \(\mu_P(t_0,x)\in[-M_Y,M_Y]\),
\[
\lvert\theta_P(t_0)\rvert
=\left\lvert\int\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx\right\rvert
\le M_Y,
\]
and hence its worst-case squared error is at most \(M_Y^2\). Because there are only finitely many such \(n\), enlarging \(C_+^0\) to
\[
C_+=\max\left\{C_+^0,\ M_Y^2\max_{1\le n<n_0}r_n(\alpha)^{-1}\right\}
\]
proves
\[
C_-r_n(\alpha)
\le
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le
C_+r_n(\alpha)
\]
for every \(n\ge1\). If the finite maximum is over the empty set, it is omitted.

It remains to verify the strict comparison in the statement. If \(\beta<\alpha\), then \(\alpha\wedge\beta=\beta\). The function \(x\mapsto2x/(2x+1)\) has derivative \(2/(2x+1)^2>0\) on \((0,\infty)\), and therefore
\[
\frac{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)}{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)+1}
=
\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+1}
<
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
Lemma~\texttt{lem:bonvini-kennedy-treatment-smoothing-bound} records that the smaller exponent arises from the treatment-smoothing term in the conditional risk bound for Bonvini and Kennedy's specified higher-order estimator. The present quantity \(\mathcal M_n\), by Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-mse}, takes the infimum over all Borel measurable estimators. Thus the strict exponent improvement is a minimax achievability statement on \(\Omega\) and does not contradict their estimator-specific upper bound.

For the direct fixed-dose comparison, Lemma~\texttt{lem:takatsu-westling-pointwise-comparator} records that Takatsu and Westling obtain a \(\sqrt{nh}\)-Gaussian pointwise limit for the same partial-mean functional under twice continuous differentiability, a positive Lipschitz marginal treatment density, and high-level nuisance-rate conditions. With \(h\asymp n^{-1/5}\), their normalization is \(n^{2/5}\). Their theorem supplies neither a minimax lower bound over the present Hölder class nor a comparison across nested values of the conditional-treatment-density smoothness \(\beta\). It is therefore a direct inference comparator, but it does not imply the two-sided minimax conclusion proved above.
