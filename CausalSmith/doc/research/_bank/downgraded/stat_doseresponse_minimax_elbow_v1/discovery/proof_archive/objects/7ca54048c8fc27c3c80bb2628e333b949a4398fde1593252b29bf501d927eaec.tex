Put \(z=z_*\) and \(w=\alpha-z\). Membership in \(\Omega\) gives \(z\in[(\alpha-B_0)_+,A_0]\) and
\[
S_0\ge g(\alpha,\beta,d)=\max\{F_\mu(z),F_\pi(z)\}.
\]
With the displayed tuning, the deterministic pilot terms equal \(n^{-kz}\) and \(n^{-kw}\). The inequalities \(S_0\ge F_\mu(z)\) and \(S_0\ge F_\pi(z)\) are algebraically equivalent to the corresponding variance terms being at most these same orders. Hence \(u=O(h^z)\) and \(v=O(h^w)\). The occupancy products diverge: when \(z>0\), the first grows at least as \(n^{2kz}\), while when \(z=0\) its tuning exponents vanish and it is of order \(n\); the same argument applies to \(w\).

Because \(z+w=\alpha\), \(z\le A_0\), \(w\le B_0\), and \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\),
\[
(u+h^{A_0})(v+h^{B_0})=O(h^\alpha).
\]
Finally \(h^{2\alpha}\asymp(nh)^{-1}\asymp n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\), whereas \(n^{-1}\) and \(e^{-cnh}\) are smaller. Substitution in the master-risk bound proves the result.
