For \(h\asymp n^{-u}\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-v}\), Lemma~\texttt{lem:explicit-histogram-window-upper} gives
\[
R(u,v)=\min\{2a_0u,4q_0v,2q_0v+2b_0u,1-u-dv,1\}.
\]
For a candidate \(r>0\), the inequalities \(R(u,v)\ge r\) are equivalent to
\[
u\ge\frac r{2a_0},
\quad v\ge\frac r{4q_0},
\quad b_0u+q_0v\ge\frac r2,
\quad u+dv\le1-r.
\]
With \(x=u/r\) and \(y=v/r\), the smallest normalized bandwidth cost is
\[
C_*=
\min\left\{x+dy:x\ge\frac1{2a_0},\ y\ge\frac1{4q_0},\ b_0x+q_0y\ge\frac12\right\}.
\]
At the lower corner, the deficit in the last constraint is \(\tfrac12(\tfrac12-b_0/a_0)_+\). Increasing \(x\) supplies it at cost \(1/b_0\) per unit, whereas increasing \(y\) costs \(d/q_0\). Hence
\[
C_*=
\frac1{2a_0}+\frac d{4q_0}
+\left(\frac12-\frac{b_0}{a_0}\right)_+
\min\left\{\frac1{2b_0},\frac d{2q_0}\right\}.
\]
The variance constraint is feasible exactly when \(rC_*\le1-r\), so the largest feasible exponent is \(r_*=(1+C_*)^{-1}=R_{\mathrm{hist}}\). An optimizer \((x_*,y_*)\) gives \(u=r_*x_*\), \(v=r_*y_*\), with \(nh\delta^d\asymp n^{r_*}\to\infty\), proving the upper bound. Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} gives the lower bound.

If \(b_0\ge a_0/2\), the positive-part term vanishes, so the exponent is independent of \(\beta\). If \(\alpha\le1\), the strictly positive term \(d/(4q_0)\) gives \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}<(1+1/(2\alpha))^{-1}=2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\). If \(\alpha>1\), it gives \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}<2/3<2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\).
