Put \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), \(S_0=s\wedge1\),
\[
E_\mu=\left(2+A_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
E_\pi=\left(2+B_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1}\right)^{-1},
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{sep}}=
\max_{0\le\gamma\le1}
\min\left\{2\alpha\gamma,1-\gamma,
2\left[\min\{A_0\gamma,E_\mu\}+\min\{B_0\gamma,E_\pi\}\right]\right\},
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}=
\left[1+\frac1{2A_0}+\frac d{4S_0}
+\left(\frac12-\frac{B_0}{A_0}\right)_+
\min\left\{\frac1{2B_0},\frac d{2S_0}\right\}
\right]^{-1},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{ach}}=\max\{R_{\mathrm{sep}},R_{\mathrm{hist}}\},
\]
and
\[
r_{\mathrm{low}}=
\min\left\{
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1},
\frac{8}{4+2/(\alpha\vee\beta)+d/s}
\right\}.
\]
For every residual tuple and every fixed \(0<c<1\), there are constants \(0<C_{\mathrm{low}}\le C_{\mathrm{up}}<\infty\) and \(n_0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-r_{\mathrm{low}}}
\le
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\le
C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}}
\qquad(n\ge n_0).
\]
Necessarily \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le r_{\mathrm{low}}\). If an exact exponent \(\rho\) exists, then \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le\rho\le r_{\mathrm{low}}\); wherever the endpoints agree, the exact exponent is their common value. The fuzzy converse improves the oracle floor exactly when \(d/s>4+4/\alpha-2/(\alpha\vee\beta)\). The converse exponent is independent of the numerical value of \(\beta\) for \(\beta\le\alpha\); \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\) is independent of \(\beta\) when \(\beta\wedge1\ge(\alpha\wedge1)/2\); and all three achievable exponents are independent of the numerical value of \(\beta\) on \(\beta\ge1\). These facts establish minimax \(\beta\)-invariance only at tuples where the bracket closes.
