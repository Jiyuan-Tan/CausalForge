For every residual tuple and fixed \(0<c<1\), there are positive constants independent of \(n\) such that
\[
C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-r_{\mathrm{low}}}
\le
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\le
C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}},
\]
where
\[
r_{\mathrm{low}}
=
\min\left\{
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1},
\frac{8}{4+2/(\alpha\vee\beta)+d/s}
\right\},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{ach}}=\max\{R_{\mathrm{sep}},R_{\mathrm{hist}}\},
\]
with \(R_{\mathrm{sep}}\) and \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\) given by Lemma~\texttt{lem:optimized-deficient-rate-bracket}. Hence any exact exponent, if it exists, must satisfy \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le\rho(\alpha,\beta,s,d)\le r_{\mathrm{low}}\). On every explicitly checkable endpoint-equality subregion where \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}=r_{\mathrm{low}}\), the exact exponent equals that common value. At the separate degenerate boundary \(c=1\), Lemma~\texttt{lem:unit-positivity-oracle-rate} gives the exact \(\beta\)-independent exponent \(2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\) for all \(\beta,s,d\), but this does not answer the fixed-\(0<c<1\) question.
