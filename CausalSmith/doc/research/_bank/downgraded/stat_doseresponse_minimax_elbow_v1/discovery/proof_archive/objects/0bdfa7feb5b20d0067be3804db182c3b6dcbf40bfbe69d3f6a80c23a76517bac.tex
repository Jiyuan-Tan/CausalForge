The two-point construction in \(\mathrm{lem:all\text{-}beta\text{-}oracle\text{-}lower\text{-}floor}\) yields
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\ge C_-n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=C_-r_n(\alpha)
\]
for every \(n\), uniformly over the declared treatment-density and slice-smoothness orders. For the upper bound, apply \(\mathrm{lem:localized\text{-}polynomial\text{-}master\text{-}risk}\) with the tuning supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:oracle\text{-}region\text{-}tuning}\). Membership of \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\) in \(\Omega\) makes that tuning admissible for all sufficiently large \(n\), and the resulting envelope is
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le C_+n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=C_+r_n(\alpha).
\]
For the finitely many smaller sample sizes, the constant estimator zero has squared risk at most \(M_Y^2\), because \(\theta_P(t_0)\in[-M_Y,M_Y]\). Enlarging \(C_+\) therefore makes the upper inequality valid for every \(n\).

If \(\beta<\alpha\), then \(\alpha\wedge\beta=\beta\). Since the map \(x\mapsto2x/(2x+1)\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\),
\[
\frac{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)}{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)+1}
=
\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+1}
<
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
The cited \(\mathrm{lem:bonvini\text{-}kennedy\text{-}treatment\text{-}smoothing\text{-}bound}\) concerns the conditional upper bound for a particular higher-order estimator, whereas \(\mathcal M_n\) takes the infimum over all Borel measurable estimators. The strict exponent comparison is therefore a same-class improvement in minimax achievability on \(\Omega\), not a contradiction of that estimator-specific bound.

The direct fixed-dose comparison is also precise. By the cited \(\mathrm{lem:takatsu\text{-}westling\text{-}pointwise\text{-}comparator}\), Takatsu and Westling prove a pointwise \(\sqrt{nh}\)-limit law for the same partial-mean functional under twice continuous differentiability of the target curve, a positive Lipschitz marginal treatment density, and high-level nuisance-rate conditions. At \(h\asymp n^{-1/5}\), their normalization is \(n^{2/5}\), corresponding to the familiar pointwise distributional rate at the twice-smooth case \(\alpha=2\). That theorem neither supplies a minimax lower bound over a common Hölder class nor indexes nested classes by the treatment-direction smoothness of the conditional density. Thus it is a direct fixed-dose inference comparator, but it does not establish the uniform two-sided minimax statement or the treatment-density-smoothness comparison proved here.
