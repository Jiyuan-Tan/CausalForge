Take \(h_0<\min(t_0,1-t_0)\), and partition \([0,1]^d\) into \(K\le(2/\delta)^d\) half-open cubes \(B_1,\ldots,B_K\) of diameter at most \(C_d\delta\). Split the observations into two folds of sizes \(N_1,N_2\asymp n\). On the first fold let \(\widehat p_j=N_1^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_1}\mathbf 1\{X_i\in B_j\}\). On the second fold let \(\widehat m_j\) be the average of the outcomes satisfying \(X_i\in B_j\) and \(\lvert A_i-t_0\rvert\le h\), with \(\widehat m_j=0\) if there is no such observation. Define the Borel estimator \[\widehat\theta_{h,\delta}=\sum_{j=1}^K\widehat p_j\widehat m_j.\]

Write \(p_j=P_X(B_j)\), \(q_h(x)=P(\lvert A-t_0\rvert\le h\mid X=x)\), and \[m_j=\mathbb E[Y\mid X\in B_j,\lvert A-t_0\rvert\le h],\qquad \bar\mu_j=\mathbb E[\mu_P(t_0,X)\mid X\in B_j].\] Positivity gives \(2ch\le q_h(x)\le2h/c\). The treatment-direction Hölder conditions and bounded first derivatives when the order exceeds one give, uniformly in \(x\), \[\left\lvert\frac{1}{q_h(x)}\int_{t_0-h}^{t_0+h}\mu_P(a,x)\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da-\mu_P(t_0,x)\right\rvert\le C h^{\bar\alpha}\] and \[\frac{q_h(x)}{2h}=\pi_P(t_0\mid x)+r_h(x),\qquad \lVert r_h\rVert_\infty\le C h^{\bar\beta}.\] Within a cell, both \(\mu_P(t_0,\cdot)\) and \(\pi_P(t_0\mid\cdot)\) oscillate by at most \(C\delta^{\bar s}\). The difference between a \(q_h\)-weighted and an unweighted cell mean is a covariance divided by the mean of \(q_h\). Decomposing \(q_h/(2h)=\pi_P(t_0\mid\cdot)+r_h\) therefore yields \[\lvert m_j-\bar\mu_j\rvert\le C\{h^{\bar\alpha}+\delta^{2\bar s}+\delta^{\bar s}h^{\bar\beta}\}.\] Since \(\theta_P(t_0)=\sum_jp_j\bar\mu_j\), the squared deterministic approximation bias is at most \[C\{h^{2\bar\alpha}+\delta^{4\bar s}+\delta^{2\bar s}h^{2\bar\beta}\}.\]

It remains to bound sampling error. Conditional on the second fold, \(\widehat\theta_{h,\delta}\) is the average over the first fold of the bounded function \(x\mapsto\widehat m_{j(x)}\). Thus its conditional variance is at most \(M_Y^2/N_1\), and its conditional mean is \(\sum_jp_j\widehat m_j\). Let \(Q_j=P(X\in B_j,\lvert A-t_0\rvert\le h)\); positivity gives \(Q_j\ge2chp_j\). If \(N_j\) is the selected count in cell \(j\), then \(N_j\) is binomial with parameters \((N_2,Q_j)\), and the elementary identity for \(\mathbb E[(N_j+1)^{-1}]\) gives \[\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1\{N_j>0\}}{N_j}\right]\le\frac{2}{N_2Q_j}.\] Conditional independence of outcomes within the selected cells consequently bounds the nonempty-cell variance by \(C\sum_jp_j^2/(N_2Q_j)\le C/(nh)\). The total mass of empty cells is \(W=\sum_jp_j\mathbf 1\{N_j=0\}\in[0,1]\). Hence \[\mathbb E[W^2]\le\mathbb E[W]\le\sum_jp_j e^{-N_2Q_j}\le \frac{CK}{nh}\le\frac{C}{nh\delta^d}.\] Combining the conditional variance, the nonempty-cell term, the empty-cell term, and the deterministic bias proves the displayed risk bound. Finally substitute \(h\asymp n^{-u}\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-v}\); the condition \(u+dv<1\) ensures \(nh\delta^d\to\infty\), and taking the smallest of the five displayed powers gives \(R(u,v)\).
