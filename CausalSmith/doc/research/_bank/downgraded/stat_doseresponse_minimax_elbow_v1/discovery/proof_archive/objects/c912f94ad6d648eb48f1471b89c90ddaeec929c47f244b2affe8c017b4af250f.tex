Write \(A_0=\bar\alpha\), \(B_0=\bar\beta\), and let
\[
I=[(\alpha-B_0)_+,A_0],\quad
m(z)=\max\{z,\alpha-z\},\quad
\varrho(z)=\max\{z/A_0,(\alpha-z)/B_0\}.
\]
The interval \(I\) is nonempty exactly when \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\), the structural condition in \(\Omega\). For any \(z\in I\), the proposed choices give
\[
b^{A_0}\le n^{-kz},\qquad b^{B_0}\le n^{-k(\alpha-z)},\qquad
\delta^{\bar s}=n^{-km(z)}\le n^{-kz}\wedge n^{-k(\alpha-z)}.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
(nb\delta^d)^{-1/2}\le n^{-km(z)}
\]
is equivalent, after multiplying exponents by \(2(2\alpha+1)\), to
\[
\bar s\ge G(z):=\frac{d\,m(z)}{2\alpha+1-\varrho(z)-2m(z)}.
\]
The denominator is positive at the minimizers below.

It remains to minimize \(G\) over \(I\). On each interval cut out by \(z=\alpha/2\), \(z/A_0=(\alpha-z)/B_0\), and the lower endpoint \(z=(\alpha-B_0)_+\), both \(m\) and \(\varrho\) are affine. Differentiating the resulting linear-fractional expressions shows that a minimum can occur only at one of those cut points. Direct substitution gives the minimizer
\[
z_*=\begin{cases}
\alpha/2,&B_0\ge A_0,\\
\max\{\alpha/2,\alpha-B_0\},&B_0<A_0\text{ and }B_0\ge\alpha/(2\alpha+1),\\
\alpha A_0/(A_0+B_0),&B_0<A_0\text{ and }B_0<\alpha/(2\alpha+1).
\end{cases}
\]
The corresponding values are, respectively,
\[
\frac{d\alpha}{2\alpha+2-\alpha/A_0},
\quad
\frac{d\alpha}{2\alpha+2-\alpha/B_0}
\ \text{when }B_0\ge\alpha/2,
\quad
\frac{d(\alpha-B_0)}{2B_0}
\ \text{when }B_0<\alpha/2,
\]
and
\[
\frac{d\alpha A_0}{A_0-\alpha+(2\alpha+1)B_0}.
\]
These are exactly the four branches of \(g(\alpha,\beta,d)\). Membership in \(\Omega\) therefore implies \(\bar s\ge G(z_*)\), so the stochastic pilot term is at most \(n^{-km_*}\). Consequently the master-risk quantities obey
\[
u=O(n^{-kz_*}),\qquad v=O(n^{-k(\alpha-z_*)}).
\]
Since \(z_*\in I\),
\[
uv=O(h^\alpha),\qquad uh^{B_0}=O(h^\alpha),\qquad vh^{A_0}=O(h^\alpha).
\]
The structural condition also gives \(h^{A_0+B_0}=O(h^\alpha)\). Hence \((u+h^{A_0})(v+h^{B_0})=O(h^\alpha)\). Finally,
\[
h^{2\alpha}\asymp (nh)^{-1}\asymp n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)},
\]
while \(n^{-1}\) and \(e^{-c_1nh}\) are of smaller order. Substitution into the master inequality proves the claim.
