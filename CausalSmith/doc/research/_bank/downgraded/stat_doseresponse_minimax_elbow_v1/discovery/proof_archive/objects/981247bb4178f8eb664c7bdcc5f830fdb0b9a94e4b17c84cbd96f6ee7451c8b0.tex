Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} and Definition~\texttt{def:oracle-rate} give, for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\ge C_-n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=C_-r_n(\alpha).
\]
For the upper bound, apply Lemma~\texttt{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk} with the tuning supplied by Lemma~\texttt{lem:oracle-region-tuning}. Membership of \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\) in \(\Omega\), together with Definitions~\texttt{def:oracle-threshold} and~\texttt{def:oracle-region}, makes those bandwidths and grid widths admissible for all sufficiently large \(n\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:oracle-region-tuning} bounds every term in the master-risk envelope by a constant multiple of \(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\). Hence there are \(n_0\in\mathbb N\) and \(C_+^0<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\le C_+^0r_n(\alpha)
\qquad(n\ge n_0).
\]
For \(1\le n<n_0\), the constant-zero estimator belongs to \(\mathfrak T_n\) by Definition~\texttt{def:estimator-class}. Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class} gives \(\lvert\mu_P(t_0,x)\rvert\le M_Y\), and therefore \(\lvert\theta_P(t_0)\rvert\le M_Y\). The constant-zero estimator's worst-case squared error is consequently at most \(M_Y^2\). With the finite maximum omitted when its index set is empty, put
\[
C_+=\max\left\{C_-,C_+^0,
M_Y^2\max_{1\le n<n_0}r_n(\alpha)^{-1}\right\}.
\]
Then \(0<C_-\le C_+<\infty\), and the asserted two-sided inequality holds for every \(n\ge1\).

It remains to verify the comparison. If \(\beta<\alpha\), then \(\alpha\wedge\beta=\beta\). The map \(x\mapsto2x/(2x+1)\) has derivative \(2/(2x+1)^2>0\), so
\[
\frac{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)}{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)+1}
=
\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+1}
<
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
Lemma~\texttt{lem:bonvini-kennedy-treatment-smoothing-bound} records that Bonvini and Kennedy's conditional bias and variance bounds contain, respectively, \(h^{\alpha\wedge\beta}\) and \((nh)^{-1}\). Squaring the bias and balancing \(h^{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)}\) with \((nh)^{-1}\) gives the displayed comparator exponent \(2(\alpha\wedge\beta)/\{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)+1\}\). Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-mse}, by contrast, takes the infimum over all Borel measurable estimators. Thus the strict same-class minimax improvement on \(\Omega\) does not contradict that estimator-specific conditional upper bound.
