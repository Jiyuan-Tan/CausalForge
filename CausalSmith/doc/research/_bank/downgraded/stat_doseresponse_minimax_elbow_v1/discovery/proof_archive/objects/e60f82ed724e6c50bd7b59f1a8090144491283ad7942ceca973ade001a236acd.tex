Let
+\[
+D=\alpha(4s+d)+4s>0.
+\]
+The prescribed bandwidth obeys \(h\asymp n^{-4s/D}\). Therefore
+\[
+nh\asymp n^{1-4s/D}
+=n^{\{D-4s\}/D}
+=n^{\alpha(4s+d)/D}.
+\]
+Substitution into the prescribed formula for \(k\) gives
+\[
+\begin{aligned}
+k
+&\asymp(nh)^{2d/(4s+d)}\\
+&\asymp
+\left(n^{\alpha(4s+d)/D}\right)^{2d/(4s+d)}
+=n^{2\alpha d/D}
+=n^{2\alpha d/\{\alpha(4s+d)+4s\}}.
+\end{aligned}
+\]
+It follows that
+\[
+\frac{k}{n}\asymp
+n^{\,2\alpha d/D-1}.
+\]
+Because \(D>0\), the exponent is positive exactly when
+\[
+2\alpha d>D
+\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
+2\alpha d>\alpha(4s+d)+4s
+\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
+\alpha d>4s(\alpha+1)
+\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
+s<\frac{\alpha d}{4(\alpha+1)}.
+\]
+Thus \(k/n\to\infty\) exactly in the displayed subregion. At the boundary the ratio is of constant order, and above the boundary it tends to zero.
+
+For \((\alpha,d,s)=(1,1,1/10)\),
+\[
+D=1\left(\frac{4}{10}+1\right)+\frac{4}{10}
+=\frac95.
+\]
+Hence
+\[
+\frac{4s}{D}=\frac{2}{9},
+\qquad
+1-\frac{4s}{D}=\frac79,
+\qquad
+\frac{2\alpha d}{D}=\frac{10}{9},
+\]
+and therefore
+\[
+h\asymp n^{-2/9},
+\qquad
+nh\asymp n^{7/9},
+\qquad
+k\asymp n^{10/9}.
+\]
+In particular, \(k/n\asymp n^{1/9}\to\infty\), so \(k>n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
+
+Finally, an empirical Gram matrix for a \(k\)-dimensional feature vector \(\Phi(X_i)\) formed from \(N\le n\) observations has the form
+\[
+\widehat G=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\Phi(X_i)\Phi(X_i)^\top.
+\]
+Each summand has rank at most one. Rank subadditivity consequently gives
+\[
+\operatorname{rank}(\widehat G)
+\le\sum_{i=1}^N
+\operatorname{rank}\!\left(\Phi(X_i)\Phi(X_i)^\top\right)
+\le N\le n.
+\]
+Whenever \(k>n\), the \(k\times k\) matrix \(\widehat G\) has nullity at least \(k-n>0\), so it is singular. It therefore cannot furnish an invertible empirical projection of rank \(k\), which proves the final assertion.
