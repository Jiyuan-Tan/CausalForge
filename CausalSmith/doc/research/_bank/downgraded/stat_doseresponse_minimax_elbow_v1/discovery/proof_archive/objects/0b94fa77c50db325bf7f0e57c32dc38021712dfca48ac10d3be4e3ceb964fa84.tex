Partition \([0,1]^d\) into \(K\le(2/\delta)^d\) half-open cubes \(B_1,\ldots,B_K\) of diameter at most \(C_d\delta\), and split the sample into independent folds \(I_1,I_2\) of sizes \(N_1,N_2\asymp n\). On the first fold put
\[
\widehat p_j=N_1^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_1}\mathbf 1\{X_i\in B_j\}.
\]
On the second fold let \(\widehat m_j\) be the average of the \(Y_i\) satisfying \(X_i\in B_j\) and \(\lvert A_i-t_0\rvert\le h\), with value zero if the selected cell is empty. Define
\[
\widehat\theta_{h,\delta}=\sum_{j=1}^K\widehat p_j\widehat m_j.
\]
Finite sums, indicator functions, counts, and the assigned empty-cell value make this a Borel element of \(\mathfrak T_n\).

Write \(p_j=P_X(B_j)\) and
\[
q_h(x)=P(\lvert A-t_0\rvert\le h\mid X=x)
=\int_{t_0-h}^{t_0+h}\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da.
\]
For \(h\le h_0:=\min\{t_0,1-t_0\}\), uniform positivity gives
\[
2ch\le q_h(x)\le 2h/c.
\]
Let
\[
m_j=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X\in B_j,\lvert A-t_0\rvert\le h],
\qquad
\bar\mu_j=\mathbb E_P[\mu_P(t_0,X)\mid X\in B_j],
\]
with arbitrary values when \(p_j=0\). By treatment-direction H\"older continuity,
\[
\left|q_h(x)^{-1}\int_{t_0-h}^{t_0+h}
\mu_P(a,x)\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da-\mu_P(t_0,x)
\right|
\le C h^{\bar\alpha}.
\]
The density H\"older condition similarly yields
\[
\frac{q_h(x)}{2h}=\pi_P(t_0\mid x)+r_h(x),
\qquad \lVert r_h\rVert_\infty\le C h^{\bar\beta}.
\]
On every cell, each of \(x\mapsto\mu_P(t_0,x)\) and \(x\mapsto\pi_P(t_0\mid x)\) has oscillation at most \(C\delta^{\bar s}\). The difference between the \(q_h\)-weighted and unweighted cell means of \(\mu_P(t_0,\cdot)\) is a covariance divided by the cell mean of \(q_h\). After dividing numerator and denominator by \(2h\), the covariance with \(\pi_P(t_0\mid\cdot)\) is bounded by the product of the two cell oscillations, while the covariance with \(r_h\) is bounded by the oscillation of \(\mu_P(t_0,\cdot)\) times \(\lVert r_h\rVert_\infty\). The denominator is at least \(c\). Consequently,
\[
\lvert m_j-\bar\mu_j\rvert
\le C\{h^{\bar\alpha}+\delta^{2\bar s}+\delta^{\bar s}h^{\bar\beta}\}.
\]
Since \(\theta_P(t_0)=\sum_jp_j\bar\mu_j\), the squared approximation bias is at most
\[
C\{h^{2\bar\alpha}+\delta^{4\bar s}+\delta^{2\bar s}h^{2\bar\beta}\}.
\]

It remains to bound sampling error. Conditional on the second fold, \(\lvert\widehat m_j\rvert\le M_Y\), so independence of the folds gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[
\left\{\sum_j(\widehat p_j-p_j)\widehat m_j\right\}^2
\,\middle|\,I_2\right]
\le \frac{M_Y^2}{N_1}.
\]
Put
\[
Q_j=P(X\in B_j,\lvert A-t_0\rvert\le h)\ge2chp_j.
\]
If \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N_2,Q_j)\) is the selected second-fold count, then
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N_j>0\}}{N_j}\right]
\le\frac{2}{N_2Q_j}.
\]
Conditional on the cell counts, the centered nonempty-cell averages have zero cross-products, and hence their weighted sum has variance at most
\[
C\sum_{j:Q_j>0}\frac{p_j^2}{N_2Q_j}
\le\frac{C}{nh}.
\]
For the total population mass of empty selected cells,
\[
W=\sum_jp_j\mathbf1\{N_j=0\},
\]
one has \(0\le W\le1\), and therefore
\[
\mathbb E_P[W^2]
\le\mathbb E_P[W]
\le\sum_jp_j e^{-N_2Q_j}
\le\frac{CK}{nh}
\le\frac{C}{nh\delta^d}.
\]
Here the penultimate inequality uses \(x e^{-a x}\le1/(ae)\), and constants absorb the fixed fold proportions. Combining approximation bias, the two sampling bounds, and Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-mse} proves the displayed risk inequality uniformly over the class. Finally, substituting \(h\asymp n^{-u}\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-v}\) gives the five displayed polynomial exponents. The condition \(u+dv<1\) makes the cell-count restriction valid for all sufficiently large \(n\), and taking the smallest exponent proves the last assertion.
