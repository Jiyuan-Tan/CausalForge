Lemma~\texttt{lem:optimized-deficient-rate-bracket} gives the upper inequality with exponent \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\). Lemmas~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} and \texttt{lem:extreme-low-s-fuzzy-lower} give lower bounds with exponents \(2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\) and \(8/\{4+2/(\alpha\vee\beta)+d/s\}\), respectively. The larger of those two numerical lower bounds is, up to an \(n\)-free constant, \(n^{-r_{\mathrm{low}}}\), proving the lower inequality.

If \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}>r_{\mathrm{low}}\), then the displayed inequalities would imply \(C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-r_{\mathrm{low}}}\le C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}}\) for every large \(n\), which is impossible after division and passage to infinity. Hence \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le r_{\mathrm{low}}\). If \(\mathcal M_n\asymp n^{-\rho}\), comparison of powers in the two inequalities gives \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le\rho\le r_{\mathrm{low}}\), and equality of the endpoints forces the asserted exact rate.

For \(\beta\le\alpha\), \(\alpha\vee\beta=\alpha\), proving the first invariance assertion. In \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\), the only nontruncated occurrence of \(B_0\) is multiplied by \((1/2-B_0/A_0)_+\), which vanishes when \(B_0\ge A_0/2\). Finally, if \(\beta\ge1\), then \(B_0=1\), so \(E_\pi\), \(R_{\mathrm{sep}}\), and \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\) no longer change with the numerical value of \(\beta\). These are exactly the stated partial invariance regions.
