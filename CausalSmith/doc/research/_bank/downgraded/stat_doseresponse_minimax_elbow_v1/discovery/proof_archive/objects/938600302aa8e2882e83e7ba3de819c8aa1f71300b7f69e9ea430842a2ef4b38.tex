The population Gram has the representation
\[
D_h=\int_{-1}^1K(u)S(u)S(u)^\top f_A(t_0+hu)\,du
\succeq c\int_{-1}^1K(u)S(u)S(u)^\top\,du.
\]
Thus its least eigenvalue is at least the deterministic \(\lambda_0\) used in the estimator definition, and \(\lambda_*=\lambda_0/2\). Each summand \(V_i=K_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)^\top\) has operator norm at most \(C/h\), while \(\mathbb E\lVert V_i\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2\le C/h\). For completeness, the scalar Bernstein bound needed here follows by applying
\[
e^x\le1+x+\frac{x^2}{2(1-\lvert x\rvert/3)},\qquad \lvert x\rvert<3,
\]
to centered bounded summands and then optimizing the exponential Markov inequality. It gives, for each fixed unit vector \(v\),
\[
P\!\left(\left\lvert v^\top(\widehat D_h-D_h)v\right\rvert>\lambda_0/4\right)
\le2e^{-c_2N_{\mathrm{lp}}h}.
\]
A fixed \(1/4\)-net of the unit sphere in dimension \(p+1\) has at most \(9^{p+1}\) elements, and the elementary net inequality for a symmetric matrix converts the preceding quadratic-form bounds into
\[
P\{\lVert\widehat D_h-D_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}>\lambda_0/2\}
\le C e^{-c_1N_{\mathrm{lp}}h}.
\]
The fold size is proportional to \(n\). Weyl's inequality then gives \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0/2=\lambda_*\) off this event, proving the display.
