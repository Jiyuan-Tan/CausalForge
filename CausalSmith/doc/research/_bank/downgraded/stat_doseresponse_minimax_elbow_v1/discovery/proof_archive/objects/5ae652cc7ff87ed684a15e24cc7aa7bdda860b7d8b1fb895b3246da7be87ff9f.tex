Write \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), and \(S_0=s\wedge1\). The histogram bound optimizes explicitly to
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}=\left[1+\frac1{2A_0}+\frac{d}{4S_0}+\frac{(1/2-B_0/A_0)_+}{2\max\{B_0,S_0/d\}}\right]^{-1}.
\]
Indeed, for a desired exponent \(t\), the constraints are \(u\ge t/(2A_0)\), \(v\ge t/(4S_0)\), \(2B_0u+2S_0v\ge t\), and \(u+dv\le1-t\). At the two coordinatewise lower bounds the third constraint has deficit \(t(1/2-B_0/A_0)_+\); filling it through the cheaper of \(u\) and \(v\) costs the final displayed term. Hence the maximal feasible \(t\) is \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\).

The separate-pilot estimator has the computable exponent \(R_{\mathrm{sep}}\) given by the following finite linear program: maximize \(t\) over nonnegative \(k,r_\mu,q_\mu,r_\pi,q_\pi,e_\mu,e_\pi,t\) subject to
\[
t\le2\alpha k,
\quad t\le2(e_\mu+e_\pi),
\quad t\le1-k,
\]
\[
e_\mu\le S_0q_\mu,
\quad e_\mu\le A_0r_\mu,
\quad 2e_\mu+r_\mu+dq_\mu\le1,
\quad e_\mu\le A_0k,
\]
\[
e_\pi\le S_0q_\pi,
\quad e_\pi\le B_0r_\pi,
\quad 2e_\pi+r_\pi+dq_\pi\le1,
\quad e_\pi\le B_0k.
\]
Choosing \(h\asymp n^{-k}\), \(b_\mu\asymp n^{-r_\mu}\), \(\delta_\mu\asymp n^{-q_\mu}\), and analogously for the density pilot proves the upper rate at this LP value. Consequently, throughout both residual regions,
\[
C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
\le\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le Cn^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{ach}}=\max\{R_{\mathrm{hist}},R_{\mathrm{sep}}\}.
\]
This is an explicit, evaluable two-sided bracket. It is exact whenever \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}=2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\); otherwise tightness and residual \(\beta\)-invariance remain open.
