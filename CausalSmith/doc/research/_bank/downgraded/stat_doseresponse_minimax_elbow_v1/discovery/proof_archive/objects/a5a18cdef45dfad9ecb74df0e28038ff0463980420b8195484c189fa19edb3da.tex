Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} gives, for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\ge C_-n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=C_-r_n(\alpha),
\]
where \(C_->0\) does not depend on \(n\). Its two-point submodel has \(p_X(x)=1\) and \(\pi_P(a\mid x)=1\), so it belongs to the class at the declared \(\beta\) and \(s\).

For the upper bound, apply Lemma~\texttt{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk} with the tuning supplied by Lemma~\texttt{lem:oracle-region-tuning}. Membership of \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\) in \(\Omega\), together with Definitions~\texttt{def:oracle-threshold} and~\texttt{def:oracle-region}, makes those bandwidths and grid widths admissible for every sufficiently large \(n\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:oracle-region-tuning} bounds every term in the master-risk envelope by a constant multiple of \(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\). Consequently, for some \(n_0\) and \(C_+^0<\infty\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\le C_+^0n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=C_+^0r_n(\alpha)
\qquad(n\ge n_0).
\]
For \(1\le n<n_0\), the constant-zero estimator belongs to \(\mathfrak T_n\). Since \(\mu_P(t_0,x)\in[-M_Y,M_Y]\),
\[
\lvert\theta_P(t_0)\rvert
=\left\lvert\int\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx\right\rvert\le M_Y,
\]
so its worst-case squared error is at most \(M_Y^2\). Enlarging \(C_+^0\) to
\[
C_+=\max\left\{C_+^0,\ M_Y^2\max_{1\le n<n_0}r_n(\alpha)^{-1}\right\},
\]
with the finite maximum omitted when its index set is empty, proves the two-sided bound for every \(n\ge1\).

If \(\beta<\alpha\), then \(\alpha\wedge\beta=\beta\). The function \(x\mapsto2x/(2x+1)\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), because its derivative is \(2/(2x+1)^2>0\). Therefore
\[
\frac{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)}{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)+1}
=\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+1}
<\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
Lemma~\texttt{lem:bonvini-kennedy-treatment-smoothing-bound} records that the smaller exponent comes from the treatment-smoothing term in Bonvini and Kennedy's conditional-risk bound for their specified higher-order estimator. Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-mse}, by contrast, takes an infimum over all Borel measurable estimators. The strict improvement on \(\Omega\) is therefore a same-class minimax achievability result and does not contradict their estimator-specific upper bound.
