Put \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), \(S_0=s\wedge1\), \(E_\mu=(2+A_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1})^{-1}\), and \(E_\pi=(2+B_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1})^{-1}\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:optimized-deficient-rate-bracket} proves
\[
R_{\mathrm{sep}}=\max_{0\le\gamma\le1}\min\left\{2\alpha\gamma,1-\gamma,2\left[\min\{A_0\gamma,E_\mu\}+\min\{B_0\gamma,E_\pi\}\right]\right\},
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}=\left[1+\frac1{2A_0}+\frac d{4S_0}+\left(\frac12-\frac{B_0}{A_0}\right)_+\min\left\{\frac1{2B_0},\frac d{2S_0}\right\}\right]^{-1},
\qquad R_{\mathrm{ach}}=\max\{R_{\mathrm{sep}},R_{\mathrm{hist}}\},
\]
and, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\le\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)\le C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}}.
\]
Thus any exact residual exponent must satisfy \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le\rho(\alpha,\beta,s,d)\le2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\). The regressogram exponent is independent of \(\beta\) when \(B_0\ge A_0/2\), and all three achievable exponents are independent of the numerical value of \(\beta\) on \(\beta\ge1\); neither is a minimax-invariance theorem. The candidate population higher-order exponent \(R_{\mathrm{HOIF}}=8\alpha s/\{\alpha(4s+d)+4s\}\) reaches the oracle exponent at \(s=d/4\). At \((\alpha,\beta,d,s)=(1,1,1,1/5)\), \(R_{\mathrm{HOIF}}=8/13\), strictly above the proved \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}=1/2\), with \(k\asymp n^{10/13}=o(n)\). This is the strongest proved bracket and surviving-band disclosure; exactness remains open.
