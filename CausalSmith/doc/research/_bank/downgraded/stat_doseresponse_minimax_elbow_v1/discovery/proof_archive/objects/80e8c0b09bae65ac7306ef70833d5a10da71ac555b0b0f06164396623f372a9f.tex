Assume \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\in\Omega\). By Lemma~\texttt{lem:oracle-region-tuning}, there are deterministic choices of the bandwidths and grid widths in Definition~\texttt{def:beta-free-handle} for which Lemma~\texttt{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk} yields, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\{\widehat\theta-\theta_P(t_0)\}^2\right]
\le C_+^0 r_n(\alpha).
\]
The count conventions, clipping, eigenvalue test, inversion on the good-Gram event, and assigned value on its complement in Definition~\texttt{def:beta-free-handle} make \(\widehat\theta\) a Borel measurable function of the sample. Hence \(\widehat\theta\in\mathfrak T_n\). Taking the infimum over \(\mathfrak T_n\) in Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-mse} proves the upper inequality for all sufficiently large \(n\). For each of the finitely many remaining positive sample sizes, the zero estimator has risk at most \(M_Y^2\), since \(\lvert\theta_P(t_0)\rvert\le M_Y\). Because \(r_n(\alpha)>0\), increasing \(C_+^0\) to a finite constant \(C_+\), independent of \(n\), extends the upper inequality to every \(n\ge1\).

Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} supplies a constant \(C_->0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\ge C_- r_n(\alpha)
\]
for every \(n\ge1\). Enlarging \(C_+\), if necessary, ensures \(C_-\le C_+\), and proves the asserted exact order.

It remains to verify the numerical comparison. Suppose \(\beta<\alpha\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:bonvini-kennedy-treatment-smoothing-bound} states that the relevant treatment-smoothing term in Bonvini and Kennedy's conditional bias bound is \(h^{\alpha\wedge\beta}=h^\beta\), while the leading conditional variance order is \((nh)^{-1}\). Because \(h^\beta\) is an absolute bias term, its contribution to mean-squared error is \(h^{2\beta}\). Thus the bias--variance balance used for the stated MSE exponent is
\[
h^{2\beta}\asymp (nh)^{-1},
\qquad
h\asymp n^{-1/(2\beta+1)},
\]
and both terms then have order
\[
n^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
The map \(x\mapsto 2x/(2x+1)\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), because its derivative is \(2/(2x+1)^2>0\). Therefore
\[
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}>
\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+1}.
\]
The first exponent is the same-class minimax exponent established above, whereas the second comes from a componentwise balance in a conditional risk bound for Bonvini and Kennedy's specified estimator under their conditions. Accordingly, the inequality is only a numerical contrast between unlike guarantees; it implies neither a strict tightening of their theorem nor domination of their estimator.
