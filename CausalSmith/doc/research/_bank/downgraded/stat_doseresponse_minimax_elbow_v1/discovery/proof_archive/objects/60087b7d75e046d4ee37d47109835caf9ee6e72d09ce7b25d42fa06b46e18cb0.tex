For power choices \(h\asymp n^{-u}\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-v}\), Lemma~\texttt{lem:explicit-histogram-window-upper} gives
\[
R(u,v)=\min\{2a_0u,4q_0v,2q_0v+2b_0u,1-u-dv,1\}.
\]
Fix a candidate exponent \(r>0\). The inequalities \(R(u,v)\ge r\) are equivalent to
\[
u\ge\frac{r}{2a_0},\qquad
v\ge\frac{r}{4q_0},\qquad
b_0u+q_0v\ge\frac r2,
\qquad
u+dv\le1-r,
\]
together with \(r\le1\). Set \(x=u/r\) and \(y=v/r\). The minimum normalized bandwidth cost needed to satisfy the first three inequalities is the value of the finite linear program
\[
C_*=\min\left\{x+dy:
 x\ge\frac{1}{2a_0},\quad
 y\ge\frac{1}{4q_0},\quad
 b_0x+q_0y\ge\frac12
\right\}.
\]
At the lower corner \(x_0=(2a_0)^{-1}\), \(y_0=(4q_0)^{-1}\), the deficit in the last constraint is
\[
\left(\frac12-b_0x_0-q_0y_0\right)_+
=
\frac12\left(\frac12-\frac{b_0}{a_0}\right)_+.
\]
Increasing \(x\) supplies one unit of this deficit at objective cost \(1/b_0\), whereas increasing \(y\) supplies one unit at objective cost \(d/q_0\). Linearity implies that assigning all additional mass to a cheaper coordinate is optimal. Hence
\[
C_*=\frac{1}{2a_0}+\frac{d}{4q_0}
+\left(\frac12-\frac{b_0}{a_0}\right)_+
\min\left\{\frac{1}{2b_0},\frac{d}{2q_0}\right\}.
\]
The final rate constraint is feasible precisely when
\[
rC_*\le1-r.
\]
Since \(C_*>0\), this inequality also implies \(r<1\), so the separate constraint \(r\le1\) is redundant. It follows that the largest feasible exponent is
\[
r_*=(1+C_*)^{-1}=R_{\mathrm{hist}}.
\]
Let \((x_*,y_*)\) be an optimizer of the displayed linear program and set \(u=r_*x_*\), \(v=r_*y_*\). Then
\[
u+dv=r_*C_*=1-r_*<1,
\]
and all four nonconstant terms in \(R(u,v)\) are at least \(r_*\), while the variance exponent \(1-u-dv\) equals \(r_*\). Thus \(R(u,v)=r_*\). Also \(nh\delta^d\asymp n^{r_*}\to\infty\), so the count restriction in Lemma~\texttt{lem:explicit-histogram-window-upper} holds for all sufficiently large \(n\). That lemma therefore yields
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{hist}}}.
\]
The lower inequality in the statement is Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor}.

If \(b_0\ge a_0/2\), the positive-part term in \(C_*\) vanishes, proving the asserted \(\beta\)-free formula. Finally, if \(0<\alpha\le1\), then \(a_0=\alpha\), and the strictly positive term \(d/(4q_0)\) gives
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}
<
\left(1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)^{-1}
=
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
If \(\alpha>1\), then \(a_0=1\), so the same strict positivity gives \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}<2/3\), whereas
\[
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}>\frac23.
\]
This proves the strict gap for every admissible tuple.
