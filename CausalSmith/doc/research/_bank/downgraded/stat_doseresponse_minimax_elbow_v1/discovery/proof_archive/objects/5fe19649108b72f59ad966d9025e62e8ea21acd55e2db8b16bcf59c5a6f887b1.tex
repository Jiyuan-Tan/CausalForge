Let \(p_j=P_X(B_j)\) and \(Q_j=P\{X\in B_j,\lvert A-t_0\rvert\le b\}\). Positivity implies \(2cbp_j\le Q_j\le2bp_j/c\). Within a cell, the slice functions oscillate by at most \(C\delta^{\bar s}\). Uniform treatment-direction Hölder control implies that replacing the window average by its value at \(t_0\) costs at most \(Cb^{\bar\alpha}\) for the outcome regression and at most \(Cb^{\bar\beta}\) for the treatment density. Hence the squared deterministic cell biases are bounded by \(C(\delta^{2\bar s}+b^{2\bar\alpha})\) and \(C(\delta^{2\bar s}+b^{2\bar\beta})\), respectively.

For the regression pilot, the selected count in cell \(j\) is binomial with parameters \((N_\mu,Q_j)\). The identity
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N_j+1}\right]
=\frac{1-(1-Q_j)^{N_\mu+1}}{(N_\mu+1)Q_j}
\le\frac1{(N_\mu+1)Q_j}
\]
and \(1/k\le2/(k+1)\) for \(k\ge1\) show that the nonempty-cell conditional variance contributes at most
\[
C\sum_j\frac{p_j}{nQ_j}\le\frac{C J}{nb}\le\frac{C}{nb\delta^d}.
\]
The empty-cell contribution is bounded in the same order because
\[
\sum_jp_j(1-Q_j)^{N_\mu}
\le\sum_jp_j e^{-2cN_\mu bp_j}
\le\frac{CJ}{nb};
\]
here \(xe^{-rx}\le1/(er)\) is applied cellwise. This proves the bound for \(U\).

For the density pilot, condition on the number \(N_j\) of observations with \(X\in B_j\). The window count is binomial with success probability between \(2cb\) and \(2b/c\), so division by \(2b(N_j\vee1)\) gives conditional variance at most \(C/(b(N_j\vee1))\). The same reciprocal-binomial calculation, now integrated with weights \(p_j\), gives \(CJ/(nb)\). Empty cells have the same bound. Finally, clipping to \([c/2,2/c]\) cannot increase squared error because the true slice lies in \([c,1/c]\). Adding the deterministic approximation error proves the claim. No lower bound on \(p_X\) was used.
