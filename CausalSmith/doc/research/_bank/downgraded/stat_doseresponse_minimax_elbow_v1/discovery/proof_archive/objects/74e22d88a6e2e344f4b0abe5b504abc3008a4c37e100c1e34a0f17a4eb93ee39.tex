Put \(p=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\). For each derivative order \(j\le p\) required by the frozen Hölder convention, the uniform Hölder-ball bound gives \(\lvert\partial_a^j\mu_P(a,x)\rvert\le L\) for every \((a,x)\). Difference quotients are jointly measurable, so their pointwise limits are measurable. Dominated convergence, with dominating constant \(L\), permits differentiation under the probability integral and yields
\[
\theta_P^{(j)}(a)=\int\partial_a^j\mu_P(a,x)p_X(x)\,dx,
\qquad \lvert\theta_P^{(j)}(a)\rvert\le L.
\]
For noninteger \(\alpha\), integration of the uniform remainder inequality gives
\[
\lvert\theta_P^{(p)}(a)-\theta_P^{(p)}(a')\rvert
\le\int L\lvert a-a'\rvert^{\alpha-p}p_X(x)\,dx
=L\lvert a-a'\rvert^{\alpha-p}.
\]
For integer \(\alpha\), the bounded-derivative convention is preserved by the same integration. Absolute integrability of the zeroth-order curve follows also from \(\lvert\mu_P\rvert\le M_Y\). Thus the partial mean inherits the asserted Hölder order and radius.
