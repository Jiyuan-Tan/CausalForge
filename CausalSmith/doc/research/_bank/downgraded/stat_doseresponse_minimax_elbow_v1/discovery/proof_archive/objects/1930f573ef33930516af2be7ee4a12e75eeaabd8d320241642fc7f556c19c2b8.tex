Put \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), and \(S_0=s\wedge1\), and define
\[
E_\mu=\left(2+A_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1}\right)^{-1},\qquad E_\pi=\left(2+B_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1}\right)^{-1},
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{sep}}=\max_{0\le\gamma\le1}\min\left\{2\alpha\gamma,1-\gamma,2\left[\min\{A_0\gamma,E_\mu\}+\min\{B_0\gamma,E_\pi\}\right]\right\},
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}=\left[1+\frac1{2A_0}+\frac d{4S_0}+\left(\frac12-\frac{B_0}{A_0}\right)_+\min\left\{\frac1{2B_0},\frac d{2S_0}\right\}\right]^{-1},\qquad R_{\mathrm{ach}}=\max\{R_{\mathrm{sep}},R_{\mathrm{hist}}\},
\]
and
\[
r_{\mathrm{low}}=\min\left\{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1},\frac{8}{4+2/(\alpha\vee\beta)+d/s}\right\}.
\]
For every fixed residual tuple and \(0<c<1\), the established estimator and converse lemmas give constants \(0<C_{\mathrm{low}}\le C_{\mathrm{up}}<\infty\) and \(n_0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-r_{\mathrm{low}}}\le\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)\le C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}}\qquad(n\ge n_0).
\]
Consequently \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le r_{\mathrm{low}}\). If an exact polynomial exponent \(\rho(\alpha,\beta,s,d)\) exists, then necessarily
\[
R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le\rho(\alpha,\beta,s,d)\le r_{\mathrm{low}}.
\]
At every tuple satisfying the explicit equality \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}=r_{\mathrm{low}}\), the exponent exists and equals this common value; it is \(\beta\)-independent on any subregion where that common value and equality persist without \(\beta\). Separately, at the excluded boundary \(c=1\), the exact exponent is \(2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\) for all \(\beta,s,d\).
