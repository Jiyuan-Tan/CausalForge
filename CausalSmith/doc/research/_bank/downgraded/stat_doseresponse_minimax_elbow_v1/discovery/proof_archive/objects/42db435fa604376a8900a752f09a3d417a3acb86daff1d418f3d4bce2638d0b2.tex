Write \(\mu_0(x)=\mu_P(t_0,x)\) and \(\pi_0(x)=\pi_P(t_0\mid x)\). For a regular grid of side \(\delta\), condition on the cell counts. The conditional variance of a bounded cell average based on the dose slice \(\lvert A-t_0\rvert\le b\) is at most a constant divided by its count. Summing the cell risks with weights \(P_X(B_j)\), and using \(\pi_P\ge c\), gives
\[
\mathbb E\lVert\widehat\mu_0-\mu_0\rVert_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\le C\left\{b_\mu^{2A_0}+\delta_\mu^{2S_0}+\frac1{nb_\mu\delta_\mu^d}\right\}.
\]
The same binomial calculation for the slice-density pilot, followed by contraction of clipping onto \([c/2,2/c]\), gives
\[
\mathbb E\lVert\widehat\pi_0-\pi_0\rVert_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\le C\left\{b_\pi^{2B_0}+\delta_\pi^{2S_0}+\frac1{nb_\pi\delta_\pi^d}\right\}.
\]
These integrated bounds require no lower bound on individual cell probabilities: empty and rare cells contribute at most \(C P_X(B_j)e^{-cnbP_X(B_j)}\), whose sum is \(O((nb\delta^d)^{-1})\) because there are \(O(\delta^{-d})\) cells. Treatment-direction H\"older continuity adds \(Ch^{A_0}\) and \(Ch^{B_0}\) when the pilots, which are constant in dose, are compared with the true nuisances at \(a\in[t_0-h,t_0+h]\). Thus the corresponding root integrated errors are bounded by \(Ce_\mu\) and \(Ce_\pi\).

Condition on the two pilot folds and average over the independent \(I_X\) fold. Direct expansion of the pseudo-outcome in Definition~\texttt{def:beta-free-handle} shows that its conditional mean at dose \(a\) equals \(\theta_P(a)\) plus
\[
\int\left\{\frac{\pi_P(a\mid x)}{\widehat\pi(a\mid x)}-1\right\}\{\mu_P(a,x)-\widehat\mu(a,x)\}\,P_X(dx)
\]
times a factor bounded above and away from zero, plus centered empirical averages from \(I_X\). Positivity and clipping bound the displayed remainder, by Cauchy--Schwarz, by \(Ce_\mu e_\pi\); the empirical averages contribute \(O(n^{-1})\) to mean squared risk. This is the Neyman-orthogonal product identity, derived here by adding and subtracting \(\widehat\mu\) and using \(\int\pi_P(a\mid x)P_X(dx)\) as the marginal treatment density.

Lemma~\texttt{lem:partial-mean-holder-transfer} and polynomial reproduction give local-polynomial approximation bias \(Ch^\alpha\). The pseudo-outcome is uniformly bounded because \(Y,\widehat\mu\) are bounded and \(\widehat\pi\ge c/2\); hence the conditional variance of the localized score is \(C/(nh)\). Each entry of the final Gram matrix is an average of variables bounded by \(C/h\) with variance at most \(C/h\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:bounded-sum-concentration}, a finite union bound, and Weyl's inequality give probability at most \(C e^{-c_1nh}\) for the bad-Gram event. On that event clipping bounds squared loss by \(4M_Y^2\); on its complement inversion is uniformly stable. Squaring the sum of the approximation and orthogonal biases, adding the conditional variance and fold-average terms, and absorbing \(n^{-1}\le(nh)^{-1}\) proves the display.
