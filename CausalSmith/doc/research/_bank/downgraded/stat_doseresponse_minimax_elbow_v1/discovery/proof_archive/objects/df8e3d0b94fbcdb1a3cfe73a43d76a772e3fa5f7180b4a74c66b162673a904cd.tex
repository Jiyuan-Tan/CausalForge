Let \(\mathcal C=C([0,1],[-M_Y,M_Y])\), let \(W=(Y(a))_{a\in[0,1]}\), and let \(x\mapsto Q_x\) be the probability kernel supplied by the corrected path-space Assumption~\texttt{ass:exchangeability}. Define
\[
m_P(a,x)=\int_{\mathcal C}w(a)\,Q_x(dw).
\]
The evaluation map \((w,a)\mapsto w(a)\) is continuous on \(\mathcal C\times[0,1]\) when \(\mathcal C\) has the uniform norm. Kernel integration, first for bounded simple functions and then by bounded pointwise approximation, therefore shows that \((a,x)\mapsto m_P(a,x)\) is jointly Borel. Moreover, if \(a_k\to a\), then \(w(a_k)\to w(a)\) for every \(w\in\mathcal C\), while \(\lvert w(a_k)\rvert\le M_Y\). Dominated convergence gives
\[
m_P(a_k,x)\longrightarrow m_P(a,x)
\]
for every \(x\); hence every dose section \(a\mapsto m_P(a,x)\) is continuous.

We next derive, rather than assume, the observed-to-causal conditional-mean bridge. Let \(r:[0,1]\times[0,1]^d\to\mathbb R\) be bounded and Borel. Consistency and the factorization in Assumption~\texttt{ass:exchangeability}, applied to the bounded Borel function \(F(w,a,x)=r(a,x)w(a)\), yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[r(A,X)Y]
&=\mathbb E[r(A,X)W(A)]\\
&=\mathbb E\!\left[
\int_0^1\int_{\mathcal C}r(a,X)w(a)\,Q_X(dw)\pi_P(a\mid X)\,da
\right]\\
&=\mathbb E\!\left[
\int_0^1 r(a,X)m_P(a,X)\pi_P(a\mid X)\,da
\right]\\
&=\mathbb E[r(A,X)m_P(A,X)].
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality uses that \(\pi_P(\cdot\mid X)\) is a conditional Lebesgue density of \(A\) given \(X\). Since this identity holds for every bounded Borel \(r\), \(m_P(A,X)\) is a version of \(\mathbb E[Y\mid A,X]\). By \(\mathrm{ass:mu\text{-}version}\), \(\mu_P(A,X)\) is another such version, so
\[
\mu_P(A,X)=m_P(A,X)\qquad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
The joint law of \((A,X)\) has density \(\pi_P(a\mid x)\) with respect to \(da\,dP_X(x)\). Boundedness and the preceding equality imply
\[
0=
\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
\lvert\mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)\rvert\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x).
\]
Uniform positivity now gives
\[
0\ge
c\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
\lvert\mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)\rvert\,da\,dP_X(x),
\]
and the integral on the right is nonnegative; it is therefore zero. By Fubini's theorem, for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
\mu_P(a,x)=m_P(a,x)
\quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }a\in[0,1].
\]
For each such \(x\), the first function is continuous in \(a\) by the treatment-direction H\"older condition in \(\mathrm{def:holder\text{-}class}\), and the second is continuous by its construction above. Their continuous difference vanishes Lebesgue-almost everywhere and hence vanishes everywhere. Consequently,
\[
\mu_P(t_0,x)=m_P(t_0,x)
\qquad\text{for }P_X\text{-almost every }x.
\]
The factorization assumption also shows that \(Q_X\) is a conditional law of \(W\) given \(X\): take \(F\) independent of its treatment argument and use \(\int_0^1\pi_P(a\mid X)\,da=1\). Thus \(m_P(t_0,X)\) is a version of \(\mathbb E[Y(t_0)\mid X]\). Boundedness, the density identity \(dP_X(x)=p_X(x)\,dx\), and the tower property give
\[
\theta_P(t_0)
=\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx
=\int_{[0,1]^d}m_P(t_0,x)\,dP_X(x)
=\mathbb E[Y(t_0)].
\]
The observed-data expression is independent of its admissible designated versions by Proposition~\texttt{prop:estimand-version-invariant}.

It remains to prove the claimed singleton-dose obstruction when the path-space condition is replaced by pointwise exchangeability. Fix an admissible parameter tuple and take the observed law
\[
X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]^d),
\qquad A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]),
\qquad Y=0,
\]
with designated versions \(p_X(x)=1\), \(\pi_P(a\mid x)=1\), and \(\mu_P(a,x)=0\). These versions are Borel, obey the two positivity bounds and the density envelope because \(0<c\le1\le pbar\), and their H\"older seminorms vanish; hence the observed law lies in \(\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)\). Put \(\eta=M_Y/2>0\), and consider the two causal completions
\[
Y^{(0)}(a)=0\quad\text{for all }a,
\qquad
Y^{(1)}(a)=\eta\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}\quad\text{for all }a.
\]
For every fixed \(a\), each \(Y^{(j)}(a)\) is deterministic, so \(Y^{(j)}(a)\perp A\mid X\). Also \(P(A=t_0)=0\), and hence both completions obey consistency:
\[
Y^{(0)}(A)=Y^{(1)}(A)=0=Y
\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\]
They induce exactly the same observed-data law, but
\[
\mathbb E[Y^{(0)}(t_0)]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[Y^{(1)}(t_0)]=\eta.
\]
Thus consistency and the separate conditions \(Y(a)\perp A\mid X\) for every fixed \(a\) do not identify the singleton-dose causal mean. The second completion is excluded by the corrected path-space assumption because \(a\mapsto Y^{(1)}(a)\) is discontinuous at \(t_0\). This proves both the positive identification result and the precise necessity claim.
