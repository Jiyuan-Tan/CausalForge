Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} gives, for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\ge C_-n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=C_-r_n(\alpha).
\]
For the upper bound, apply Lemma~\texttt{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk} with the tuning in Lemma~\texttt{lem:oracle-region-tuning}. Membership of \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\) in \(\Omega\), together with Definitions~\texttt{def:oracle-threshold} and~\texttt{def:oracle-region}, makes these bandwidths and grid widths admissible for all sufficiently large \(n\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:oracle-region-tuning} bounds every term in the master-risk envelope by a constant multiple of \(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\). Hence there are \(n_0\) and \(C_+^0<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\le C_+^0r_n(\alpha)
\qquad(n\ge n_0).
\]
For \(1\le n<n_0\), the constant-zero estimator belongs to \(\mathfrak T_n\). Because \(\lvert\theta_P(t_0)\rvert\le M_Y\), its worst-case squared error is at most \(M_Y^2\). Enlarging the constant to
\[
C_+=\max\left\{C_+^0,\ M_Y^2\max_{1\le n<n_0}r_n(\alpha)^{-1}\right\},
\]
with the finite maximum omitted if its index set is empty, proves the upper bound for every \(n\ge1\).

If \(\beta<\alpha\), then \(\alpha\wedge\beta=\beta\). Since \(x\mapsto2x/(2x+1)\) has derivative \(2/(2x+1)^2>0\),
\[
\frac{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)}{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)+1}
=
\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+1}
<
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
Lemma~\texttt{lem:bonvini-kennedy-treatment-smoothing-bound} records that the smaller exponent comes from the treatment-smoothing term in Bonvini and Kennedy's conditional-risk bound for their specified estimator. Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-mse} instead infimizes over all Borel estimators. Thus the strict same-class minimax improvement on \(\Omega\) does not contradict their estimator-specific upper bound.
