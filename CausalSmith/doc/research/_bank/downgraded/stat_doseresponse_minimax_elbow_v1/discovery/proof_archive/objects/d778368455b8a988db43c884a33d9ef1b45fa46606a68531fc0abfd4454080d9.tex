Put \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), \(S_0=s\wedge1\), and \(k=(2\alpha+1)^{-1}\). Assume \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\in\Omega\). By \(\mathrm{def:oracle\text{-}region}\), this entails \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\) and \(S_0\ge g(\alpha,\beta,d)\). The separate-pilot optimization in \(\mathrm{lem:oracle\text{-}region\text{-}tuning}\) therefore supplies a point \(z_*\in[(\alpha-B_0)_+,A_0]\), with both pilot denominators positive, and tuning sequences
\[
h\asymp n^{-k},\qquad b_\mu\asymp n^{-kz_*/A_0},\qquad \delta_\mu\asymp n^{-kz_*/S_0},
\]
\[
b_\pi\asymp n^{-k(\alpha-z_*)/B_0},\qquad \delta_\pi\asymp n^{-k(\alpha-z_*)/S_0}.
\]
For the regression and treatment-density pilot envelopes
\[
u_\mu=\delta_\mu^{S_0}+b_\mu^{A_0}+(n b_\mu\delta_\mu^d)^{-1/2},
\qquad
v_\pi=\delta_\pi^{S_0}+b_\pi^{B_0}+(n b_\pi\delta_\pi^d)^{-1/2},
\]
that lemma gives
\[
u_\mu=O(n^{-kz_*})=O(h^{z_*}),
\qquad
v_\pi=O(n^{-k(\alpha-z_*)})=O(h^{\alpha-z_*}).
\]
Because \(z_*\le A_0\) and \(\alpha-z_*\le B_0\), and because \(0<h\le1\),
\[
u_\mu v_\pi=O(h^\alpha),\qquad
u_\mu h^{B_0}=O(h^{z_*+B_0})=O(h^\alpha),
\]
\[
v_\pi h^{A_0}=O(h^{\alpha-z_*+A_0})=O(h^\alpha),
\qquad
h^{A_0+B_0}=O(h^\alpha).
\]
Consequently
\[
(u_\mu+h^{A_0})(v_\pi+h^{B_0})=O(h^\alpha).
\]
The two pilots in \(\mathrm{def:beta\text{-}free\text{-}handle}\) are trained on the disjoint folds \(I_\mu\) and \(I_{\mathrm{aux}}\). The independent-pilot product step incorporated in \(\mathrm{lem:beta\text{-}free\text{-}master\text{-}risk}\) therefore uses
\[
\mathbb E[U^2V^2]=\mathbb E[U^2]\mathbb E[V^2]\le C u_\mu^2v_\pi^2,
\]
with no fourth-moment premise. Applying that master-risk lemma to the displayed tuning sequences yields, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le C\left\{h^{2\alpha}+\frac1{nh}+\frac1n+e^{-c_1nh}\right\}.
\]
Since \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\alpha+1)}\),
\[
h^{2\alpha}\asymp\frac1{nh}\asymp n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=r_n(\alpha).
\]
Furthermore \(n^{-1}\le r_n(\alpha)\), and \(e^{-c_1nh}=O(r_n(\alpha))\), because \(nh\asymp n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\). Thus
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)\le C_{\mathrm{up}}r_n(\alpha)
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). For the finitely many remaining sample sizes, the constant estimator zero belongs to \(\mathfrak T_n\), while \(\lvert\theta_P(t_0)\rvert\le M_Y\); enlarging \(C_{\mathrm{up}}\) therefore makes the same upper bound valid for every \(n\ge1\).

The converse is \(\mathrm{lem:all\text{-}beta\text{-}oracle\text{-}lower\text{-}floor}\): for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\ge C_{\mathrm{low}}r_n(\alpha).
\]
Taking \(C_-=C_{\mathrm{low}}\) and \(C_+=C_{\mathrm{up}}\) proves the two-sided assertion. The constants may depend on the fixed model tuple and the fixed construction constants, but not on \(n\).

Finally suppose \(\beta<\alpha\). Then \(\alpha\wedge\beta=\beta\). The map \(q\mapsto2q/(2q+1)\) has derivative \(2/(2q+1)^2>0\), and hence
\[
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}>\frac{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)}{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)+1}.
\]
This is only a comparison, on \(\Omega\), with the treatment-smoothing term of the stated estimator-specific conditional-risk upper bound; it does not contradict that bound.
