Let \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), \(S_0=s\wedge1\), \(E_\mu=(2+A_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1})^{-1}\), and \(E_\pi=(2+B_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1})^{-1}\). Define
\[
R_{\mathrm{sep}}=\max_{0\le\gamma\le1}\min\left\{2\alpha\gamma,\ 1-\gamma,\ 2\left[\min\{A_0\gamma,E_\mu\}+\min\{B_0\gamma,E_\pi\}\right]\right\},
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}=\left[1+\frac{1}{2A_0}+\frac{d}{4S_0}+\left(\frac12-\frac{B_0}{A_0}\right)_+\min\left\{\frac{1}{2B_0},\frac{d}{2S_0}\right\}\right]^{-1},
\qquad R_{\mathrm{ach}}=\max\{R_{\mathrm{sep}},R_{\mathrm{hist}}\}.
\]
Lemma~\texttt{lem:optimized-deficient-rate-bracket} proves that, for constants independent of \(n\),
\[
C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\le\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)\le C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). In either residual region, \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}<2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\). Thus, if an exact polynomial exponent exists, it must satisfy
\[
R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le\rho(\alpha,\beta,s,d)\le\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
Moreover, \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\) is independent of \(\beta\) whenever \(B_0\ge A_0/2\), and \(R_{\mathrm{sep}}\), \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\), and \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\) are independent of the numerical value of \(\beta\) on \(\beta\ge1\). These are estimator-side invariance results only and do not establish residual minimax \(\beta\)-invariance.
