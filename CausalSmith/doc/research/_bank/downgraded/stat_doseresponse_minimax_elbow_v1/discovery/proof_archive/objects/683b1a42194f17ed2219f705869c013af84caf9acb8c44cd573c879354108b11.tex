Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)\). Let \(\mu_1\) and \(\mu_2\) be two admissible designated regression versions, and put \(D(a,x)=\mu_1(a,x)-\mu_2(a,x)\). Equality of conditional-expectation versions gives \(D(A,X)=0\) almost surely. The law of \((A,X)\) has density \(\pi_P(a\mid x)\) with respect to \(da\,dP_X(x)\). Hence
\[
0=\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1\lvert D(a,x)\rvert\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x)
\ge c\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1\lvert D(a,x)\rvert\,da\,dP_X(x),
\]
where uniform positivity supplies the inequality. By Fubini's theorem, for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\), \(D(a,x)=0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\). For each such \(x\), both treatment sections are Hölder and therefore continuous. Their continuous difference vanishes Lebesgue-almost everywhere and consequently vanishes everywhere, in particular at \(t_0\). Thus \(\mu_1(t_0,x)=\mu_2(t_0,x)\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\).

If \(p_{X,1}\) and \(p_{X,2}\) are two designated Lebesgue densities of the same marginal law, they agree Lebesgue-almost everywhere. Each regression slice is bounded by \(M_Y\), so the relevant integrals exist absolutely. Therefore
\[
\int\mu_1(t_0,x)p_{X,1}(x)\,dx
=\int\mu_2(t_0,x)p_{X,2}(x)\,dx.
\]
This proves that \(\theta_P(t_0)\) is well defined and independent of every admissible choice of designated versions.
