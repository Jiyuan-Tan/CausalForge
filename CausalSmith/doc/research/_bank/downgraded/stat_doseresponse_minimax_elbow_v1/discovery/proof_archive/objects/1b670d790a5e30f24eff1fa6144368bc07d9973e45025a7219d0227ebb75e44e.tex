Throughout this proof, the H\"older condition defining \(\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)\) uses the same seminorm convention as Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class}; in particular, \(L\) controls only the required positive-order derivatives and terminal H\"older modulus or Taylor remainder. Function values are controlled separately by \(\lvert Y\rvert\le M_Y\).

Fix \(P\in\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)\), and define
\[
\vartheta_P(a)=\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(a,x)\,P_X(dx).
\]
This quantity is independent of the admissible jointly Borel regression version. Indeed, if \(\mu_P\) and \(\mu'_P\) are two such versions, then the joint law of \((A,X)\) is \(da\,P_X(dx)\), so the two versions agree for \(da\,P_X(dx)\)-almost every \((a,x)\). Fubini's theorem implies that their treatment sections agree Lebesgue-almost everywhere for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). Each section is continuous because \(\alpha>0\), and hence the sections agree everywhere in \(a\) for such \(x\), in particular at \(t_0\). Their integrals at \(t_0\) therefore coincide.

Integration against \(P_X\) preserves the H\"older seminorm. When \(0<\alpha<1\), the defining modulus gives directly
\[
\lvert\vartheta_P(a)-\vartheta_P(a')\rvert
\le \int\lvert\mu_P(a,x)-\mu_P(a',x)\rvert\,P_X(dx)
\le L\lvert a-a'\rvert^\alpha.
\]
When \(\alpha>1\), the uniform positive-order derivative bounds permit differentiation under the integral by dominated convergence. Thus, for every integer \(1\le j\le\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\) whose derivative is required by the convention,
\[
\partial_a^j\vartheta_P(a)=\int\partial_a^j\mu_P(a,x)\,P_X(dx),
\qquad
\sup_a\lvert\partial_a^j\vartheta_P(a)\rvert\le L.
\]
If \(j=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\) and \(\alpha\notin\mathbb N\), then
\[
\lvert\partial_a^j\vartheta_P(a)-\partial_a^j\vartheta_P(a')\rvert
\le L\lvert a-a'\rvert^{\alpha-j}.
\]
For integer \(\alpha\), the same integration argument transfers the stipulated bounded highest derivative and its associated Taylor-remainder bound. Consequently \(\vartheta_P\) has uniformly bounded H\"older seminorm of order \(\alpha\). Moreover, since a conditional mean of a variable in \([-M_Y,M_Y]\) lies in that interval and the continuous sections determine the fixed-dose version, \(\lvert\vartheta_P(a)\rvert\le M_Y\) for every \(a\).

Because \(A\) is uniform and independent of \(X\), the defining conditional-regression identity yields
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a]=\vartheta_P(a)
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\). The continuous H\"older function \(\vartheta_P\) is therefore the relevant version of this one-dimensional regression curve, and the target is \(\vartheta_P(t_0)\).

We now construct one estimator valid for the whole class. Put \(p=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\), \(S(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^p)^\top\), and choose a bounded nonnegative kernel \(K\), supported on \([-1,1]\) and bounded below on a neighbourhood of zero. For \(0<h\le\min\{t_0,1-t_0\}\), define
\[
K_h(a)=h^{-1}K\!\left(\frac{a-t_0}{h}\right),
\qquad
S_h(a)=S\!\left(\frac{a-t_0}{h}\right),
\]
\[
\widehat D_h=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nK_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)^\top,
\qquad
\widehat R_h=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nK_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)Y_i.
\]
The deterministic matrix
\[
D=\int_{-1}^1K(u)S(u)S(u)^\top\,du
\]
is positive definite: if \(v^\top Dv=0\), then the polynomial \(v^\top S(u)\) vanishes on an interval on which \(K\) is bounded below, and therefore vanishes identically. Let \(\lambda_0=\lambda_{\min}(D)>0\), and set
\[
\widehat\vartheta_h=
\operatorname{clip}_{[-M_Y,M_Y]}\!\left[
 e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h\,
 \mathbf 1\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0/2\}
\right],
\]
where the bracketed quantity is defined as zero on the complementary event. This is a Borel measurable element of \(\mathfrak T_n\) and depends only on \((Y_i,A_i)_{i=1}^n\); hence no regularity or nonsingularity of \(P_X\) is needed.

Uniformity of \(A\) and the bandwidth restriction give \(\mathbb E_P\widehat D_h=D\). Define
\[
b_h=D^{-1}\mathbb E_P[K_h(A)S_h(A)Y],
\qquad
\vartheta_h=e_0^\top b_h.
\]
The linear functional
\[
q\longmapsto e_0^\top D^{-1}
\int_{-1}^1K(u)S(u)q(t_0+hu)\,du
\]
reproduces at \(t_0\) every polynomial of degree at most \(p\). Applying this identity to the H\"older Taylor expansion of \(\vartheta_P\) gives
\[
\lvert\vartheta_h-\vartheta_P(t_0)\rvert\le C h^\alpha.
\]
For integer \(\alpha\), expand only through order \(\alpha-1\) and use the bounded \(\alpha\)-th derivative in the integral Taylor remainder; polynomial reproduction still applies because \(p=\alpha\). This proves the same bound. Also \(\lVert b_h\rVert\le C\), since \(D^{-1}\) is fixed and \(Y\), \(K\), and \(S\) are bounded.

Let
\[
W_i=K_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)\{Y_i-S_h(A_i)^\top b_h\}.
\]
The population normal equation gives \(\mathbb E_PW_i=0\), while boundedness yields, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)\),
\[
\lVert W_i\rVert\le C h^{-1},
\qquad
\mathbb E_P\lVert W_i\rVert^2\le C h^{-1}.
\]
On \(G_h=\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0/2\}\), exact algebra gives
\[
e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h-\vartheta_h
=e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nW_i.
\]
Since \(\lVert\widehat D_h^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/\lambda_0\) on \(G_h\), independence and centering imply
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\left(e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h-\vartheta_h\right)^2
\mathbf 1_{G_h}
\right]
\le C\mathbb E_P\left\lVert\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nW_i\right\rVert^2
\le\frac{C}{nh}.
\]

Each centered entry of
\[
K_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)^\top-D
\]
is bounded by \(C/h\), and the sum of its \(n\) variances is at most \(Cn/h\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:bounded-sum-concentration}, applied entrywise at a fixed threshold, followed by a finite-dimensional union bound and Weyl's inequality, therefore gives constants \(C_0,c_0>0\) such that
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)}P(G_h^c)
\le C_0e^{-c_0nh}.
\]
Clipping cannot increase loss because \(\vartheta_P(t_0)\in[-M_Y,M_Y]\), and on \(G_h^c\) the estimator equals zero, so its squared loss is at most \(M_Y^2\). Combining squared bias, good-event variance, and the bad-event probability yields
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\vartheta_h-\vartheta_P(t_0))^2]
\le C\left(h^{2\alpha}+\frac1{nh}+e^{-c_0nh}\right).
\]
Taking \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\alpha+1)}\) and then increasing \(n_0\) so that the bandwidth lies inside the two-sided neighbourhood of \(t_0\), we obtain
\[
\mathcal N_n(\alpha,d,M_Y,L,t_0)
\le C_+n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
=C_+r_n(\alpha)
\qquad(n\ge n_0).
\]

For the lower bound, fix arbitrary \(\beta_0,s_0>0\). If \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta_0,s_0,d,M_Y,L,1,t_0)\), the lower and upper positivity inequalities force \(\pi_P(a\mid x)=1\) for every \((a,x)\). Thus \(A\) is uniform and independent of \(X\). The remaining class conditions, interpreted under the seminorm convention of Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class}, imply \(P\in\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)\), and the two targets coincide because
\[
\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)\,P_X(dx)
=
\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx
=
\theta_P(t_0).
\]
Minimax monotonicity and Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} now give
\[
\mathcal N_n(\alpha,d,M_Y,L,t_0)
\ge
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta_0,s_0,d,M_Y,L,1,t_0)
\ge C_-r_n(\alpha).
\]
Together with the upper bound, this proves the asserted two-sided rate.

It remains to prove properness of the inclusion. Let \(X\) be uniform on \([0,1]^d\), let \(A\) be an independent uniform variable, choose \(0<\delta<M_Y\), and put
\[
q(x)=\delta\mathbf 1\{x_1\le1/2\}.
\]
Conditionally on \((A,X)\), let \(Y\in\{-M_Y,M_Y\}\) have mean \(q(X)\). Then \(\mu_P(a,x)=q(x)\) is jointly Borel and constant in \(a\), so every positive-order treatment-direction derivative and every treatment-direction H\"older remainder vanishes. Its treatment-direction H\"older seminorm is therefore zero, and this law belongs to \(\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)\) for every \(L>0\).

Suppose this law belonged to \(\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,1,t_0)\). Any admissible designated regression version agrees with \(q(x)\) for \(da\,dP_X(x)\)-almost every \((a,x)\). Fubini's theorem and continuity in \(a\) force its \(t_0\)-slice to equal \(q\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). But the frozen class requires that slice to be H\"older, hence continuous, in \(x\). A continuous function equal to \(\delta\) almost everywhere on \(\{x:x_1<1/2\}\) and to zero almost everywhere on \(\{x:x_1>1/2\}\) would equal those constants everywhere on the respective open half-cubes, which contradicts continuity at their common boundary. Thus this member of \(\mathcal Q_1\) is outside every such frozen class.

There is also a distinct failure of the frozen density requirement: take \(P_X\) to be a point mass, take \(A\) independent and uniform, and set \(Y=0\). This law lies in \(\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)\), because its regression has H\"older seminorm zero, but its covariate marginal has no Lebesgue density. Hence, for every \(\beta,s>0\), the displayed inclusion is proper.
