Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)\), and abbreviate \(\Pi=\Pi_{k,P_X}\). By Assumptions~\texttt{ass:mu-x-holder} and~\texttt{ass:pi-x-holder}, the functions
+\[
+f(x)=\mu_P(t_0,x),\qquad q(x)=\pi_P(t_0\mid x)
+\]
+belong to the coordinatewise \(d\)-variate H\"older ball of order \(s\) and radius \(L\). Partition \([0,1]^d\) into a regular grid of half-open cubes, with the usual closed convention at the upper boundary, having at most \(C_d k\) cells and cell side length at most \(C_d'k^{-1/d}\). On each cell, expand a H\"older function about a fixed point of that cell using the coordinatewise Taylor polynomial of degree \(\lfloor s\rfloor\) allowed by the H\"older convention in Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class}. The H\"older remainder bound, uniformly over the cells, gives elements \(f_k,q_k\in V_k\) such that
+\[
+\lVert f-f_k\rVert_\infty\le C L k^{-s/d},
+\qquad
+\lVert q-q_k\rVert_\infty\le C L k^{-s/d}.
+\]
+The same conclusion holds when \(s\) is an integer under the bounded-derivative convention stated in Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class}. Since \(P_X\) is a probability measure,
+\[
+\lVert f-f_k\rVert_{L_2(P_X)}+
+\lVert q-q_k\rVert_{L_2(P_X)}
+\le C k^{-s/d}.
+\]
+No property of a Lebesgue-density representative of \(P_X\) is used in this passage.
+
+The operator \(\Pi\) is the orthogonal projection onto the image of \(V_k\) in \(L_2(P_X)\). Hence \(I-\Pi\) is an orthogonal projection and therefore an \(L_2(P_X)\)-contraction. Moreover, for every \(v\in V_k\), its equivalence class lies in the range of \(\Pi\), so \((I-\Pi)v=0\). In particular,
+\[
+(I-\Pi)(f-\widetilde f)
+=(I-\Pi)(f-f_k)
+\]
+because both \(f_k\) and \(\widetilde f\) belong to \(V_k\). Consequently,
+\[
+\lVert(I-\Pi)(f-\widetilde f)\rVert_{L_2(P_X)}
+\le \lVert f-f_k\rVert_{L_2(P_X)}
+\le Ck^{-s/d}.
+\]
+The identical argument with \(q_k\) and \(\widetilde q\) gives
+\[
+\lVert(I-\Pi)(q-\widetilde q)\rVert_{L_2(P_X)}
+\le Ck^{-s/d}.
+\]
+Adding the last two inequalities proves the first assertion. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality then yields
+\[
+\begin{aligned}
+&\left\lvert
+\left\langle(I-\Pi)(f-\widetilde f),
+(I-\Pi)(q-\widetilde q)\right\rangle_{P_X}
+\right\rvert\\
+&\qquad\le
+\lVert(I-\Pi)(f-\widetilde f)\rVert_{L_2(P_X)}
+\lVert(I-\Pi)(q-\widetilde q)\rVert_{L_2(P_X)}
+\le Ck^{-2s/d}.
+\end{aligned}
+\]
+
+It remains to establish the kernel identity. Let \(r=\operatorname{rank}(\Pi)\), and choose an orthonormal basis \(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r\) for the image of \(V_k\) in \(L_2(P_X)\). An orthonormal-basis kernel of \(\Pi\) is
+\[
+\Pi_{k,P_X}(x,x')=\sum_{j=1}^r\phi_j(x)\phi_j(x').
+\]
+For independent \(X_1,X_2\sim P_X\), Tonelli's theorem applies to the finite sum, and independence followed by orthonormality gives
+\[
+\begin{aligned}
+\mathbb E\!\left[\Pi_{k,P_X}(X_1,X_2)^2\right]
+&=\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{\ell=1}^r
+\mathbb E[\phi_j(X_1)\phi_\ell(X_1)]
+\mathbb E[\phi_j(X_2)\phi_\ell(X_2)]\\
+&=\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{\ell=1}^r\delta_{j\ell}^2
+=r.
+\end{aligned}
+\]
+Finally,
+\[
+r\le\dim(V_k)\le C_dk.
+\]
+Every step used only approximation supplied by the two slice H\"older assumptions and Hilbert-space geometry under the probability law \(P_X\); no upper bound, lower bound, or smoothness property of \(p_X\) was invoked.
