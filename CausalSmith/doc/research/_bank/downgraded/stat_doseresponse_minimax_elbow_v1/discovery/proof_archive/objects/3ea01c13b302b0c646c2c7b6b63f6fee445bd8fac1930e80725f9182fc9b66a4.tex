Fix \(0<\beta<\beta^{\prime}<\infty\) and an admissible parameter tuple such that \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\in\Omega\). By \(\mathrm{thm:oracle\text{-}high\text{-}s}\), applied to the rough class, there are \(C_{\mathrm{up}}<\infty\) and \(n_0\in\mathbb N\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le C_{\mathrm{up}}r_n(\alpha)
=C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
\]
for every \(n\ge n_0\).

Because \(\beta<\beta^{\prime}\), \(\mathrm{lem:beta\text{-}class\text{-}one\text{-}sided\text{-}monotonicity}\) gives
\[
\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\subseteq
\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0).
\]
For every fixed estimator, its supremum risk over the subclass on the left is no larger than its supremum risk over the class on the right. Taking the infimum over the common estimator class therefore yields
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0).
\]

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:all\text{-}beta\text{-}oracle\text{-}lower\text{-}floor}\) to the smoother class indexed by \(\beta^{\prime}\). It supplies \(C_{\mathrm{low}}>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\ge C_{\mathrm{low}}r_n(\alpha)
=C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
\]
for every \(n\ge1\). Replacing \(C_{\mathrm{up}}\) by \(C_{\mathrm{up}}\vee C_{\mathrm{low}}\), if necessary, ensures \(0<C_{\mathrm{low}}\le C_{\mathrm{up}}<\infty\). Combining the preceding three displays proves, for every \(n\ge n_0\),
\[
C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
\le \mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le \mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}.
\]
The first inequality makes the denominator below strictly positive. Dividing gives
\[
1\le
\frac{\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)}
{\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)}
\le
\frac{C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}}
{C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}}
=\frac{C_{\mathrm{up}}}{C_{\mathrm{low}}}.
\]
The only oracle-region invocation was for the rough tuple \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\). The smoother-class conclusion used only class nesting and the all-\(\beta\) lower floor, so no oracle-region condition involving \(\beta^{\prime}\) is needed.
