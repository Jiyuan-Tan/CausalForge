Put \(z=z_*\) and \(w=\alpha-z\). Membership in \(\Omega\) gives \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\), \(S_0\ge g(\alpha,\beta,d)\), and hence \(z\in[(\alpha-B_0)_+,A_0]\). The regression-pilot variance requirement
\[
(nb_\mu\delta_\mu^d)^{-1/2}\lesssim n^{-kz}
\]
is equivalent to
\[
S_0\ge \frac{dz}{2\alpha+1-z/A_0-2z}=F_\mu(z).
\]
Likewise,
\[
(nb_\pi\delta_\pi^d)^{-1/2}\lesssim n^{-kw}
\]
is equivalent to \(S_0\ge F_\pi(z)\). By the threshold-optimization lemma,
\[
\max\{F_\mu(z),F_\pi(z)\}=g(\alpha,\beta,d)\le S_0.
\]
The remaining pilot terms satisfy
\[
\delta_\mu^{S_0}\asymp b_\mu^{A_0}\asymp n^{-kz},
\qquad
\delta_\pi^{S_0}\asymp b_\pi^{B_0}\asymp n^{-kw}.
\]
Therefore \(u=O(h^z)\) and \(v=O(h^w)\). The same inequalities show \(nb_\mu\delta_\mu^d\gtrsim n^{2kz}\) and \(nb_\pi\delta_\pi^d\gtrsim n^{2kw}\), so all pilot occupancy requirements hold for large \(n\).

Because \(z+w=\alpha\), \(z\ge\alpha-B_0\), and \(z\le A_0\),
\[
uv=O(h^\alpha),
\qquad
uh^{B_0}=O(h^\alpha),
\qquad
vh^{A_0}=O(h^\alpha).
\]
The structural condition gives \(h^{A_0+B_0}=O(h^\alpha)\). It follows that
\[
(u+h^{A_0})(v+h^{B_0})=O(h^\alpha).
\]
Finally,
\[
h^{2\alpha}\asymp (nh)^{-1}\asymp n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)},
\]
while \(n^{-1}\) and \(e^{-cnh}\) are smaller. Substitution into the master-risk inequality proves the assertion.
