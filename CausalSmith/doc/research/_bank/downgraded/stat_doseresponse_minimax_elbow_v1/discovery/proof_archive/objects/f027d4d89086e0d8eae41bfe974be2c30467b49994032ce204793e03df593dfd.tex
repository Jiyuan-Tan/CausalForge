Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)\). Let \(\mu_1\) and \(\mu_2\) be two admissible designated regression versions, and put \(D(a,x)=\mu_1(a,x)-\mu_2(a,x)\). Equality of conditional-expectation versions gives \(D(A,X)=0\) for \(P\)-almost every \((A,X)\). With respect to Lebesgue measure in \(a\) and \(P_X\) in \(x\), the law of \((A,X)\) has density \(\pi_P(a\mid x)\). Consequently, \[0=\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1 \lvert D(a,x)\rvert\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x)\ge c\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1\lvert D(a,x)\rvert\,da\,dP_X(x),\] where the inequality uses uniform positivity. Fubini's theorem implies that, for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\), one has \(D(a,x)=0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\). For each such \(x\), both \(a\mapsto\mu_1(a,x)\) and \(a\mapsto\mu_2(a,x)\) are Hölder and therefore continuous. A continuous function on \([0,1]\) that vanishes Lebesgue-almost everywhere vanishes everywhere, so \(D(t_0,x)=0\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). Thus the fixed-dose slices agree \(P_X\)-almost surely. If \(p_{X,1}\) and \(p_{X,2}\) are two admissible designated Lebesgue densities of the same marginal law, they agree Lebesgue-almost everywhere. Since each regression slice is bounded by \(M_Y\), both integrals exist absolutely, and the preceding two facts give \[\int\mu_1(t_0,x)p_{X,1}(x)\,dx=\int\mu_2(t_0,x)p_{X,2}(x)\,dx.\] Hence \(\theta_P(t_0)\) is well defined and independent of all admissible designated versions.
