Let \((\mathsf Y,\mathcal Y)\), \(e\), and \(\mathbf Y\) be the objects furnished by Assumption~\texttt{ass:potential-outcome-path-typing}. Because \(\mathsf Y\) is standard Borel, fix a regular conditional-law kernel \(K_x=\mathcal L(\mathbf Y\mid X=x)\), and define
\[
\overline m_P(a,x)=\int_{\mathsf Y}e(y,a)K_x(dy).
\]
This function is jointly Borel measurable. Indeed, the collection of bounded jointly Borel functions \(f(y,a)\) for which \((a,x)\mapsto\int f(y,a)K_x(dy)\) is Borel contains the indicators of measurable rectangles, because their integrals have the form \(\mathbf 1_C(a)K_x(B)\), and is closed under bounded monotone limits; the monotone-class argument therefore applies to \(e\). Moreover, for every fixed \(a\), the function \(\overline m_P(a,\cdot)\) is a version of \(\mathbb E[Y(a)\mid X=\cdot]\), since \(Y(a)=e(\mathbf Y,a)\) pointwise.

Process-level conditional exchangeability says that the conditional law of \(\mathbf Y\) given \((A,X)\) is \(K_X\). Applying this equality of conditional laws to the bounded jointly measurable evaluation map, first to measurable simple functions and then by bounded convergence, gives
\[
\mathbb E[e(\mathbf Y,A)\mid A,X]
=
\int_{\mathsf Y}e(y,A)K_X(dy)
=
\overline m_P(A,X)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
The path-typing identity gives \(Y(A)=e(\mathbf Y,A)\) pointwise, and consistency gives \(Y=Y(A)\) almost surely. Hence
\[
\mathbb E[Y\mid A,X]=\overline m_P(A,X)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Since \(\mu_P(A,X)\) is another version of the same conditional mean,
\[
0
=
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\left|\mu_P(A,X)-\overline m_P(A,X)\right|\right]
=
\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
\left|\mu_P(a,x)-\overline m_P(a,x)\right|\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x).
\]
Uniform positivity yields
\[
0
\ge
c\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
\left|\mu_P(a,x)-\overline m_P(a,x)\right|\,da\,dP_X(x),
\]
so
\[
\mu_P(a,x)=\overline m_P(a,x)
\quad\text{for }da\,dP_X(x)\text{-almost every }(a,x). \tag{1}
\]

For each fixed \(a\), both \(m_P(a,\cdot)\) and \(\overline m_P(a,\cdot)\) are versions of \(\mathbb E[Y(a)\mid X=\cdot]\). Thus
\[
\int\left|m_P(a,x)-\overline m_P(a,x)\right|\,dP_X(x)=0
\]
for every \(a\). Joint measurability and Tonelli's theorem then imply
\[
m_P(a,x)=\overline m_P(a,x)
\quad\text{for }da\,dP_X(x)\text{-almost every }(a,x). \tag{2}
\]
Combining \((1)\) and \((2)\) proves
\[
\mu_P(a,x)=m_P(a,x)
\quad\text{for }da\,dP_X(x)\text{-almost every }(a,x).
\]

By Fubini's theorem, there is a set \(E\subseteq[0,1]^d\) with \(P_X(E)=1\) such that, for every \(x\in E\), the last equality holds for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\), the section \(a\mapsto m_P(a,x)\) is continuous at \(t_0\), and the section \(a\mapsto\mu_P(a,x)\) is continuous at \(t_0\) by the treatment-direction H\"older condition. Fix \(x\in E\). Because \(t_0\in(0,1)\) and a full-Lebesgue-measure subset of \([0,1]\) meets every neighbourhood of \(t_0\), there is a sequence \(a_k\to t_0\) along which \(\mu_P(a_k,x)=m_P(a_k,x)\). Continuity of both sections at \(t_0\) gives
\[
\mu_P(t_0,x)=m_P(t_0,x).
\]
This equality holds for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). Since \(p_X\) is a density of \(P_X\), and since \(m_P(t_0,\cdot)\) is a version of \(\mathbb E[Y(t_0)\mid X=\cdot]\), boundedness and the tower property give
\[
\theta_P(t_0)
=
\int\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx
=
\int m_P(t_0,x)\,dP_X(x)
=
\mathbb E[Y(t_0)].
\]
Proposition~\texttt{prop:estimand-version-invariant} ensures that the observed-data expression is independent of the admissible designated versions.

It remains to prove the negative assertion. Take the observed law from Definition~\texttt{def:nonempty-witness}: \(X\) is uniform on \([0,1]^d\), \(A\mid X\) is uniform on \([0,1]\), and \(Y=0\), with \(p_X=1\), \(\pi_P=1\), and \(\mu_P=0\). Proposition~\texttt{prop:class-nonempty} places this observed law in every admissible class. Fix \(0<\eta\le M_Y\), let \(\mathsf Y=\{y_0,y_1\}\) with its discrete sigma-field, and define the jointly Borel evaluation map
\[
e(y_0,a)=0,
\qquad
e(y_1,a)=\eta\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}.
\]
In the first causal completion let \(\mathbf Y=y_0\) surely, and in the second let \(\mathbf Y=y_1\) surely. Both completions satisfy measurable path typing. In each completion \(\mathbf Y\) is deterministic and therefore conditionally independent of \(A\) given \(X\). In the second completion, \(P(A=t_0)=0\), so \(Y(A)=0=Y\) almost surely; consistency also holds in the first completion. Consequently the two completions induce exactly the same observed law and satisfy consistency, path typing, and process-level conditional exchangeability, but
\[
\mathbb E[Y^{(0)}(t_0)]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[Y^{(1)}(t_0)]=\eta.
\]
The conditional-mean version in the second completion is \(m_P(a,x)=\eta\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}\), which is discontinuous at \(t_0\). Thus, when continuity at the singleton dose is omitted, the remaining three causal conditions do not identify \(\mathbb E[Y(t_0)]\).
