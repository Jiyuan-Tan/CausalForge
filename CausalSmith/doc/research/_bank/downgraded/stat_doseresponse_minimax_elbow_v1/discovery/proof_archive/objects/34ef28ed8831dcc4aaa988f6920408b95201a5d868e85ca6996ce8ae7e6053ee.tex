Choose the explicit smooth bump \[\psi(u)=\begin{cases}\exp\!\left(1-\dfrac{1}{1-u^2}\right),&\lvert u\rvert<1,\\0,&\lvert u\rvert\ge1,\end{cases}\] so that \(\psi(0)=1\), and let \(C_{\psi,\alpha}<\infty\) bound its Hölder-\(\alpha\) norm. Put \(b=M_Y\). Choose constants \(\lambda>0\) and \(\kappa>0\), depending only on the displayed model parameters, such that \[\lambda C_{\psi,\alpha}\le L,\qquad \lambda\kappa^{\alpha}\le b/2,\qquad \kappa<\min(t_0,1-t_0),\] and impose one further smallness condition on \(\kappa\) below. For \(h_n=\kappa n^{-1/(2\alpha+1)}\) and \(\zeta\in\{-1,1\}\), define a law \(P_{\zeta}\) by taking \(X\) uniform on \([0,1]^d\), \(A\) uniform on \([0,1]\) independently of \(X\), and \(Y\in\{-b,b\}\) conditionally on \((A=a,X=x)\) with \[P_{\zeta}(Y=b\mid A=a,X=x)=\frac{1+\mu_{\zeta}(a,x)/b}{2},\qquad \mu_{\zeta}(a,x)=\zeta\lambda h_n^{\alpha}\psi\!\left(\frac{a-t_0}{h_n}\right).\] The choice of \(\lambda\) makes the scaled bump a Hölder-\(\alpha\) function of radius at most \(L\), uniformly in \(x\). At \(a=t_0\), it is constant in \(x\), so it satisfies every Hölder-\(s\) slice condition. The designated densities \(p_X=1\) and \(\pi_{P_{\zeta}}=1\) satisfy all density, positivity, and Hölder conditions, for every \(\beta>0\). The outcome is bounded by \(M_Y\), and its displayed conditional mean is \(\mu_{\zeta}\). Thus \(P_{+}\) and \(P_{-}\) belong to the frozen class. Their targets are \[\theta_{P_{\zeta}}(t_0)=\zeta\lambda h_n^{\alpha},\] so their separation is \(2\lambda h_n^{\alpha}\).

We next bound their testing distance directly. Conditional on \((A=a,X=x)\), the two probabilities of \(Y=b\) differ by \(\mu_{+}(a,x)/b\), and both outcome probabilities under \(P_-\) are at least \(1/4\). Hence the one-observation chi-squared divergence satisfies \[\chi^2(P_+,P_-)\le \frac{8}{b^2}\int_0^1\mu_+(a,x)^2\,da\le C_0\frac{\lambda^2}{b^2}h_n^{2\alpha+1},\] where \(C_0=8\int\psi(u)^2du\). Independence gives \[1+\chi^2(P_+^n,P_-^n)=\{1+\chi^2(P_+,P_-)\}^n\le\exp\!\left(C_0\lambda^2\kappa^{2\alpha+1}/b^2\right).\] Choose \(\kappa\) small enough that the right side is at most \(5/4\). Cauchy--Schwarz then yields \(\operatorname{TV}(P_+^n,P_-^n)\le\tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2(P_+^n,P_-^n)}\le1/4\).

For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), use the test \(\phi=\mathbf 1\{T\ge0\}\). On \(\{\phi=0\}\) under \(P_+^n\), the squared error is at least \(\lambda^2h_n^{2\alpha}\); the same is true on \(\{\phi=1\}\) under \(P_-^n\). Therefore \[\max_{\zeta\in\{-1,1\}}\mathbb E_{P_{\zeta}}[(T-\theta_{P_{\zeta}}(t_0))^2]\ge\frac{\lambda^2h_n^{2\alpha}}{2}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_+^n,P_-^n)\}\ge\frac{3\lambda^2\kappa^{2\alpha}}{8}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}.\] The two laws form a subclass of the frozen model, so taking the infimum over all Borel estimators proves the claim with \(C_{\mathrm{low}}=3\lambda^2\kappa^{2\alpha}/8\).
