Fix \(P\in\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)\) and define
\[
\vartheta_P(a)=\int\mu_P(a,x)p_X(x)\,dx.
\]
Integration against the probability law \(P_X\) transfers the uniform treatment-direction H\"older bounds from \(\mu_P(\cdot,x)\) to \(\vartheta_P\). More explicitly, every derivative required by the frozen H\"older convention may be passed under the integral by dominated convergence, because the corresponding derivatives are uniformly bounded by the common H\"older radius; the terminal H\"older remainder is preserved by integration. Thus \(\vartheta_P\) lies in a univariate H\"older ball of order \(\alpha\), with radius bounded uniformly over \(\mathcal Q_1\). This argument uses no regularity in \(x\).

Because \(A\) is uniform and independent of \(X\), a version of its marginal regression is
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a]=\vartheta_P(a).
\]
We now construct one estimator for the entire class. Put \(p=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\), \(S(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^p)^\top\), and choose a bounded nonnegative kernel \(K\), supported on \([-1,1]\) and bounded below on a neighbourhood of zero. For \(h\le\min(t_0,1-t_0)\), put
\[
K_h(a)=h^{-1}K((a-t_0)/h),
\qquad
S_h(a)=S((a-t_0)/h),
\]
\[
\widehat D_h=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nK_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)^\top,
\qquad
\widehat R_h=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nK_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)Y_i.
\]
The fixed matrix
\[
D=\int_{-1}^1K(u)S(u)S(u)^\top\,du
\]
is positive definite: a polynomial with zero squared integral against a kernel that is positive on an interval vanishes identically. Let \(\lambda_0=\lambda_{\min}(D)>0\), and define the Borel estimator
\[
\widehat\vartheta_h
=
\operatorname{clip}_{[-M_Y,M_Y]}
\left[
e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h
\mathbf 1\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0/2\}
\right],
\]
with the bracketed value set to zero on the complementary event.

Uniformity of \(A\) gives \(\mathbb E_P\widehat D_h=D\). Define
\[
b_h=D^{-1}\mathbb E_P[K_h(A)S_h(A)Y],
\qquad
\vartheta_h=e_0^\top b_h.
\]
The functional \(q\mapsto e_0^\top D^{-1}\int K(u)S(u)q(t_0+hu)\,du\) reproduces every polynomial of degree at most \(p\) at \(t_0\). Applying it to the H\"older Taylor expansion of \(\vartheta_P\) therefore gives
\[
\lvert\vartheta_h-\vartheta_P(t_0)\rvert\le Ch^\alpha.
\]
Boundedness of \(Y\), \(K\), and \(S\), together with invertibility of \(D\), also gives \(\lVert b_h\rVert\le C\).

Put
\[
W_i=K_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)\{Y_i-S_h(A_i)^\top b_h\}.
\]
The normal equation gives \(\mathbb E_PW_i=0\). Uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal Q_1\),
\[
\lVert W_i\rVert\le C/h,
\qquad
\mathbb E_P\lVert W_i\rVert^2\le C/h.
\]
On \(G_h=\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat D_h)\ge\lambda_0/2\}\), exact algebra yields
\[
e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h-\vartheta_h
=
e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nW_i.
\]
Since \(\lVert\widehat D_h^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/\lambda_0\) on \(G_h\), independence and centering imply
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[
\left(e_0^\top\widehat D_h^{-1}\widehat R_h-\vartheta_h\right)^2
\mathbf 1_{G_h}
\right]
\le
C\mathbb E_P\left\lVert\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nW_i\right\rVert^2
\le\frac{C}{nh}.
\]
For the Gram event, every centered entry of \(K_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)^\top-D\) is bounded by \(C/h\), and the sum of its \(n\) entrywise variances is at most \(Cn/h\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:bounded-sum-concentration}, applied at a fixed entrywise average threshold and followed by a finite-dimensional union bound and Weyl's inequality, gives constants \(C_0,c_0>0\) such that
\[
P(G_h^c)\le C_0e^{-c_0nh}.
\]
This generic exponential form is exactly what Bernstein supplies; no sharper numerical constant is used. Clipping cannot increase squared loss because \(\vartheta_P(t_0)\in[-M_Y,M_Y]\), while the bad-event loss is at most \(M_Y^2\). Consequently
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1}
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\vartheta_h-\vartheta_P(t_0))^2]
\le
C\left(h^{2\alpha}+\frac1{nh}+e^{-c_0nh}\right).
\]
Taking \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\alpha+1)}\) proves
\[
\mathcal N_n(\alpha,d,M_Y,L,t_0)
\le C_+r_n(\alpha)
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

For the lower bound, fix any positive \(\beta_0,s_0\). At \(c=1\), every member of \(\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta_0,s_0,d,M_Y,L,1,t_0)\) has \(\pi_P(a\mid x)=1\) pointwise, hence has uniform \(A\) independent of \(X\), and all its remaining defining conditions place it in \(\mathcal Q_1(\alpha,d,M_Y,L)\). Therefore minimax monotonicity and Lemma~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor} give
\[
\mathcal N_n(\alpha,d,M_Y,L,t_0)
\ge
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta_0,s_0,d,M_Y,L,1,t_0)
\ge C_-r_n(\alpha).
\]
This proves the two-sided rate.

Finally, the inclusion is strict. Let \(X\) be uniform on \([0,1]^d\), let \(A\) be an independent uniform variable, choose \(0<\delta<\min\{M_Y,L\}\), and set \(q(x)=\delta\mathbf 1\{x_1\le1/2\}\). Given \((A,X)\), let \(Y\in\{-M_Y,M_Y\}\) have mean \(q(X)\). Then \(\mu_P(a,x)=q(x)\) is jointly Borel and constant, hence H\"older of every positive order, in the treatment coordinate. The law belongs to \(\mathcal Q_1\). If it belonged to a frozen class, any admissible treatment-continuous regression version would equal \(q(x)\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\) and \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\); continuity in \(a\) would then force its \(t_0\)-slice to equal \(q\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). No continuous, hence no positive-order H\"older, function on \([0,1]^d\) can equal the two distinct constants defining \(q\) almost everywhere on the two adjacent open half-cubes. Thus no admissible designated version satisfies the frozen \(x\)-H\"older condition. The law is outside every frozen class with \(s>0\), proving strict containment and showing that the boundary theorem does not consume the frozen regression-slice H\"older restriction.
