Let \(m_P\) be the compatible conditional-mean version in the statement. Its asserted agreement with \(\mu_P\) implies
\[
\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
\lvert \mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)\rvert\,
\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x)=0.
\]
The integrand is measurable and nonnegative. By \(\mathrm{ass:positivity\text{-}lower}\), \(\pi_P(a\mid x)\ge c>0\) everywhere, and hence
\[
0\ge c\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
\lvert \mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)\rvert\,da\,dP_X(x)\ge0.
\]
Thus the unweighted integral is zero. Tonelli's theorem gives a set \(E\subseteq[0,1]^d\) with \(P_X(E)=1\) such that, for every \(x\in E\),
\[
\mu_P(a,x)=m_P(a,x)
\quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }a\in[0,1].
\]
Intersect \(E\) with the \(P_X\)-full set on which both treatment-direction functions in the statement are continuous. For every \(x\) in this intersection, the continuous function
\[
a\longmapsto \mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)
\]
vanishes Lebesgue-almost everywhere on \([0,1]\), so it vanishes everywhere. In particular,
\[
\mu_P(t_0,x)=m_P(t_0,x)
\quad\text{for }P_X\text{-almost every }x.
\]
By the defining property of \(m_P(t_0,\cdot)\), it is a version of \(\mathbb E[Y(t_0)\mid X=\cdot]\). All terms are integrable because their absolute values are bounded by \(M_Y\). The tower property, followed by the fact that \(p_X\) is a Lebesgue density of \(P_X\), therefore yields
\[
\mathbb E[Y(t_0)]
=\int_{[0,1]^d}m_P(t_0,x)\,dP_X(x)
=\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)\,dP_X(x)
=\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx
=\theta_P(t_0).
\]
The last functional is independent of the admissible designated versions by \(\mathrm{prop:estimand\text{-}version\text{-}invariant}\).

It remains to justify the final assertion concerning the singleton dose. Consider the observable law under which \(X\) is uniform on \([0,1]^d\), \(A\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and independent of \(X\), and \(Y=0\) almost surely. Its designated versions are \(p_X(x)=1\), \(\pi_P(a\mid x)=1\), and \(\mu_P(a,x)=0\). By \(\mathrm{def:holder\text{-}class}\), this law belongs to the stated class for every admissible parameter tuple: the constant functions satisfy every Hölder condition, \(p_X\le pbar\), and \(c\le1\le1/c\). On a probability space carrying this same observable law, define two causal extensions. In the first, let \(Y^{(0)}(a)=0\) for every \(a\). In the second, with \(q=M_Y/2>0\), let
\[
Y^{(1)}(a)=q\,\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}
\quad\text{for every }a\in[0,1].
\]
For each fixed \(a\), both potential outcomes are deterministic and therefore conditionally independent of \(A\) given \(X\), as required by \(\mathrm{ass:exchangeability}\). Moreover, because the uniform treatment satisfies \(\mathbb P(A=t_0)=0\),
\[
Y^{(1)}(A)=q\,\mathbf 1\{A=t_0\}=0=Y
\quad\text{almost surely},
\]
so \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) holds for both extensions. They induce exactly the same observable law, but
\[
\mathbb E[Y^{(0)}(t_0)]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[Y^{(1)}(t_0)]=q.
\]
The second extension has the conditional-mean version \(a\mapsto q\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}\), which cannot be made continuous while retaining its value at every fixed dose. Hence consistency and conditional exchangeability alone do not determine the counterfactual mean at the null event \(A=t_0\); the compatible continuous version bridge rules out precisely this ambiguity.
