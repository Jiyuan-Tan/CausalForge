Put \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), \(S_0=s\wedge1\), \(E_\mu=(2+A_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1})^{-1}\), and \(E_\pi=(2+B_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1})^{-1}\). Define
\[
R_{\mathrm{sep}}
=
\max_{0\le\gamma\le1}
\min\left\{
2\alpha\gamma,
1-\gamma,
2\left[\min\{A_0\gamma,E_\mu\}+\min\{B_0\gamma,E_\pi\}\right]
\right\},
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}
=
\left[
1+\frac1{2A_0}+\frac d{4S_0}
+\left(\frac12-\frac{B_0}{A_0}\right)_+
\min\left\{\frac1{2B_0},\frac d{2S_0}\right\}
\right]^{-1},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{ach}}=\max\{R_{\mathrm{sep}},R_{\mathrm{hist}}\}.
\]
Lemma~\texttt{lem:optimized-deficient-rate-bracket} proves that there are constants \(0<C_{\mathrm{low}}\le C_{\mathrm{up}}<\infty\) and \(n_0\), independent of \(n\), such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\),
\[
C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
\le
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\le
C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}}.
\]
Moreover, \(R_{\mathrm{sep}}=2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\) if and only if \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\in\Omega\), while \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}<2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\) for every admissible tuple. Hence \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}<2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\) throughout the two residual regions. If an exact polynomial exponent \(\rho(\alpha,\beta,s,d)\) exists there, the proved bracket forces
\[
R_{\mathrm{ach}}
\le
\rho(\alpha,\beta,s,d)
\le
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
The achievable histogram exponent is independent of \(\beta\) whenever \(B_0\ge A_0/2\), and \(R_{\mathrm{sep}}\), \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\), and \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\) are all independent of the numerical value of \(\beta\) on \(\beta\ge1\). These are estimator-side invariance statements only; they do not prove invariance of the residual minimax exponent.
