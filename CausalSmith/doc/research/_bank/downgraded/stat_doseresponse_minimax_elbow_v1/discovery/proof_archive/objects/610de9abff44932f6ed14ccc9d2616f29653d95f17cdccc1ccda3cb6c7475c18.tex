The feasible set of the displayed linear program is closed and bounded: the last inequality and \(r\ge0\) imply \(u+dv\le1\), hence \(0\le u\le1\) and \(0\le v\le1/d\). It is nonempty. Choosing sufficiently small \(u,v>0\) with \(u+dv<1\) makes all four right-hand sides strictly positive, so the optimum satisfies \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}>0\). If an optimizer had \(u=0\), \(v=0\), or \(u+dv=1\), respectively the first, second, or fourth constraint would force \(r=0\), contradicting positivity of the optimum. Thus every optimizer \((u_*,v_*,r_*)\) relevant to the maximum has
\[
u_*>0,
\qquad v_*>0,
\qquad u_*+dv_*<1,
\]
and \(r_*=R_{\mathrm{hist}}\).

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:explicit\text{-}histogram\text{-}window\text{-}upper}\) with \(h\asymp n^{-u_*}\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-v_*}\). Its occupancy condition holds for all sufficiently large \(n\), because \(1-u_*-dv_*>0\). The exponent in that lemma is
\[
\min\{2\bar\alpha u_*,4\bar s v_*,2\bar s v_*+2\bar\beta u_*,1-u_*-dv_*,1\}.
\]
The first four terms have minimum \(r_*=R_{\mathrm{hist}}\). The fourth is at most one, so the additional constant term \(1\) does not lower the minimum. Hence
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{hist}}(\alpha,\beta,s,d)}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). The all-\(\beta\) oracle lower-floor lemma supplies
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\ge C_{\mathrm{low}}r_n(\alpha)
=C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
\]
for every \(n\ge1\). Taking \(n_0\) large enough for the histogram bound and, if necessary, enlarging \(C_{\mathrm{up}}\) proves the bracket. Neither argument uses membership in \(\Omega\), so the result applies to both residual regions.
