Put \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), and \(S_0=s\wedge1\). We prove the proposed statement. The lower bound follows immediately from \(\mathrm{lem:all\text{-}beta\text{-}oracle\text{-}lower\text{-}floor\): for a constant \(C_{\mathrm{low}}>0\) independent of \(n\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)\ge C_{\mathrm{low}}r_n(\alpha)=C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}.
\]
It remains to prove the upper bound.

Let
\[
I=[(\alpha-B_0)_+,A_0],\qquad
m(z)=\max\{z,\alpha-z\},\qquad
q(z)=\max\left\{\frac z{A_0},\frac{\alpha-z}{B_0}\right\}.
\]
The interval \(I\) is nonempty exactly when \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\). For \(z\in I\), define
\[
G(z)=\frac{d\,m(z)}{2\alpha+1-q(z)-2m(z)}.
\]
The denominators occurring below are positive under \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\). Direct minimization gives
\[
\min_{z\in I}G(z)=g(\alpha,\beta,d),
\]
where a minimizer is
\[
z_*=\begin{cases}
\alpha/2,&B_0\ge A_0,\\
\max\{\alpha/2,\alpha-B_0\},&B_0<A_0\text{ and }B_0\ge\alpha/(2\alpha+1),\\
\alpha A_0/(A_0+B_0),&B_0<A_0\text{ and }B_0<\alpha/(2\alpha+1).
\end{cases}
\]
Indeed, on each side of \(\alpha/2\), both \(m(z)\) and the active affine branch of \(q(z)\) are affine, so differentiating the corresponding linear-fractional function shows that its minimum is at the balancing point of the active branches or at an endpoint of \(I\). Substitution yields, respectively,
\[
\frac{d\alpha}{2\alpha+2-\alpha/A_0},\quad
\frac{d\alpha}{2\alpha+2-\alpha/B_0},\quad
\frac{d(\alpha-B_0)}{2B_0},\quad
\frac{d\alpha A_0}{A_0-\alpha+(2\alpha+1)B_0},
\]
with exactly the four cases stated in the proposed theorem. This also verifies the displayed closed form for \(g\).

Set \(k=(2\alpha+1)^{-1}\), \(z=z_*\), \(m=m(z)\), and \(q=q(z)\). Choose, up to fixed sufficiently small multiplicative constants,
\[
h=n^{-k},\qquad b=n^{-kq},\qquad \delta=n^{-km/S_0}.
\]
The hypothesis \(S_0\ge G(z)\) is equivalent to
\[
\frac{d m}{S_0}\le 2\alpha+1-q-2m.
\]
Consequently
\[
nb\delta^d=n^{1-kq-kdm/S_0}\ge n^{2km}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
so the tuning requirements of \(\mathrm{lem:localized\text{-}polynomial\text{-}master\text{-}risk\) hold for all sufficiently large \(n\). With
\[
u=\delta^{S_0}+b^{A_0}+(nb\delta^d)^{-1/2},\qquad
v=\delta^{S_0}+b^{B_0}+(nb\delta^d)^{-1/2},
\]
the definitions of \(m\) and \(q\), together with the preceding inequality, imply
\[
u\le C h^z,\qquad v\le C h^{\alpha-z}.
\]
Since \(z\in[(\alpha-B_0)_+,A_0]\) and \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\), each term in the product satisfies
\[
uv\le Ch^\alpha,\qquad uh^{B_0}\le Ch^\alpha,\qquad vh^{A_0}\le Ch^\alpha,\qquad h^{A_0+B_0}\le h^\alpha.
\]
Therefore
\[
(u+h^{A_0})(v+h^{B_0})\le Ch^\alpha.
\]
The master risk bound now gives
\[
\mathcal M_n\le C\left(h^{2\alpha}+\frac1{nh}+\frac1n+e^{-c_1nh}\right).
\]
For \(h=n^{-1/(2\alpha+1)}\),
\[
h^{2\alpha}=\frac1{nh}=n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}.
\]
Moreover, \(n^{-1}\le n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\), and \(e^{-c_1nh}\le Cn^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Hence
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)\le C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}.
\]
Combining this inequality with the lower floor proves the proposed two-sided oracle rate. All constants may depend on the fixed parameter tuple and on the fixed kernel and splitting proportions, but not on \(n\).
