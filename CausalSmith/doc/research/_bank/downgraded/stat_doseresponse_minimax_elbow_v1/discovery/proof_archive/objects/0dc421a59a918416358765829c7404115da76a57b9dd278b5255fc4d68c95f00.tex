Let \(m_P\) be the compatible conditional-mean version in the statement. Its asserted agreement with \(\mu_P\) gives
+\[
+\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
+\lvert \mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)\rvert\,
+\pi_P(a\mid x)\,da\,dP_X(x)=0.
+\]
+The integrand is measurable and nonnegative. By uniform positivity, \(\pi_P(a\mid x)\ge c>0\) everywhere, and therefore
+\[
+0\ge c\int_{[0,1]^d}\int_0^1
+\lvert \mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)\rvert\,da\,dP_X(x)\ge0.
+\]
+Thus the unweighted integral is zero. Tonelli's theorem yields a Borel set \(E\subseteq[0,1]^d\) with \(P_X(E)=1\) such that, for every \(x\in E\),
+\[
+\mu_P(a,x)=m_P(a,x)
+\quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }a\in[0,1].
+\]
+Intersect \(E\) with the \(P_X\)-full set on which both treatment-direction functions in the statement are continuous. For every \(x\) in this intersection, the continuous function
+\[
+a\longmapsto \mu_P(a,x)-m_P(a,x)
+\]
+vanishes Lebesgue-almost everywhere on \([0,1]\), hence vanishes everywhere. In particular,
+\[
+\mu_P(t_0,x)=m_P(t_0,x)
+\quad\text{for }P_X\text{-almost every }x.
+\]
+By hypothesis, \(m_P(t_0,\cdot)\) is a version of \(\mathbb E[Y(t_0)\mid X=\cdot]\). All terms are integrable because their absolute values are bounded by \(M_Y\). The tower property and the fact that \(p_X\) is a Lebesgue density of \(P_X\) now imply
+\[
+\begin{aligned}
+\mathbb E[Y(t_0)]
+&=\int_{[0,1]^d}m_P(t_0,x)\,dP_X(x)\\
+&=\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)\,dP_X(x)\\
+&=\int_{[0,1]^d}\mu_P(t_0,x)p_X(x)\,dx
+=\theta_P(t_0).
+\end{aligned}
+\]
+The last functional is independent of the admissible designated versions by \(\mathrm{prop:estimand\text{-}version\text{-}invariant}\).
+
+It remains to verify the claimed necessity of a compatible version at a singleton dose. Consider the observable law under which \(X\) is uniform on \([0,1]^d\), \(A\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and independent of \(X\), and \(Y=0\) almost surely. Designate \(p_X(x)=1\), \(\pi_P(a\mid x)=1\), and \(\mu_P(a,x)=0\). This law belongs to \(\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)\): the designated functions are Borel, the two constant nuisance functions satisfy every stated Hölder condition, \(p_X\le pbar\), and \(c\le1\le1/c\).
+
+On a probability space carrying this same observable law, define two causal extensions. In the first, let \(Y^{(0)}(a)=0\) for every \(a\in[0,1]\). In the second, put \(q=M_Y/2>0\) and let
+\[
+Y^{(1)}(a)=q\,\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}
+\quad\text{for every }a\in[0,1].
+\]
+For each fixed \(a\), both potential outcomes are deterministic and therefore conditionally independent of \(A\) given \(X\), so conditional exchangeability holds. Because a uniform treatment satisfies \(\mathbb P(A=t_0)=0\),
+\[
+Y^{(1)}(A)=q\,\mathbf 1\{A=t_0\}=0=Y
+\quad\text{almost surely},
+\]
+and consistency holds for both extensions. Thus the two extensions induce exactly the same observable law and satisfy consistency and conditional exchangeability, but
+\[
+\mathbb E[Y^{(0)}(t_0)]=0,
+\qquad
+\mathbb E[Y^{(1)}(t_0)]=q.
+\]
+The second extension has fixed-dose conditional means \(\mathbb E[Y^{(1)}(a)\mid X]=q\mathbf 1\{a=t_0\}\), which cannot be represented by a function continuous in \(a\) while preserving the conditional mean at every fixed dose. Hence consistency and conditional exchangeability alone do not identify the counterfactual mean at the null event \(A=t_0\); the compatible continuous-version condition excludes exactly this ambiguity.
