Put \(A=A_0\), \(B=B_0\), \(S=S_0\), \(C=2\alpha+1\), \(k=C^{-1}\), \(z=z_*\), and \(q=\alpha-z\).  The interval restrictions give \(0<z\le A\) and \(0<q\le B\).  Multiplicative constants may be chosen small enough to meet the fixed upper tuning bounds and do not affect any exponent.  Under the displayed powers,
\[
\delta_\mu^S\asymp b_\mu^A\asymp n^{-kz},
\qquad
\delta_\pi^S\asymp b_\pi^B\asymp n^{-kq}.
\]
Because \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\in\Omega\), \(S\ge g\ge F_\mu(z)\), and hence
\[
\frac{dz}{S}\le C-\frac zA-2z.
\]
It follows that
\[
(nb_\mu\delta_\mu^d)^{-1/2}
\asymp n^{-\{1-kz/A-kdz/S\}/2}
\le n^{-kz}.
\]
The identical argument using \(S\ge F_\pi(z)\) yields \((nb_\pi\delta_\pi^d)^{-1/2}\le Cn^{-kq}\).  Thus \(u=O(n^{-kz})\) and \(v=O(n^{-kq})\).  Since \(z\le A\) and \(q\le B\),
\[
h^A=O(n^{-kz}),
\qquad
h^B=O(n^{-kq}),
\]
so
\[
\{(u+h^A)(v+h^B)\}^2=O(n^{-2k(z+q)})=O(n^{-2k\alpha}).
\]
Also,
\[
h^{2\alpha}\asymp n^{-2k\alpha},
\qquad
(nh)^{-1}\asymp n^{-(1-k)}=n^{-2k\alpha},
\]
while \(n^{-1}=O(n^{-2k\alpha})\) and \(e^{-c_1nh}=o(n^{-2k\alpha})\).  The two count exponents are at least \(2kz>0\) and \(2kq>0\), so all tuning restrictions hold eventually.  Substitution into \(\mathrm{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk}\) proves the claim.
