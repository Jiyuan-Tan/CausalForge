Bayes' formula and \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]=\mu_P(a,x)\) give
\[
\mathbb E_P[\widetilde\xi\mid A=a]
=-\frac{\widetilde f_a}{f_a}P_X\!\left[\frac{v_a\pi_{P,a}}{\widetilde\pi_a}\right]+P_X\mu_{P,a}+P_Xv_a.
\]
Since \(\widetilde f_a=f_a+\bar e_a\) and
\[
P_Xv_a-P_X\!\left[\frac{v_a\pi_{P,a}}{\widetilde\pi_a}\right]
=P_X\!\left[v_a\left(1-\frac{\pi_{P,a}}{\widetilde\pi_a}\right)\right]
=P_X\!\left[\frac{v_ae_a}{\widetilde\pi_a}\right],
\]
the displayed identity follows exactly. For the bound, \(\widetilde\pi_a\ge c/2\), \(f_a\ge c\), and \(\pi_{P,a}\le1/c\). Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields
\[
\left\lvert P_X\!\left[\frac{v_ae_a}{\widetilde\pi_a}\right]\right\rvert
\le\frac2c\lVert v_a\rVert_2\lVert e_a\rVert_2,
\]
and
\[
\left\lvert\frac{\bar e_a}{f_a}P_X\!\left[\frac{v_a\pi_{P,a}}{\widetilde\pi_a}\right]\right\rvert
\le\frac{2}{c^3}\lVert e_a\rVert_2\lVert v_a\rVert_2.
\]
Adding the two inequalities proves the claim. In particular, there is no term involving \(e_a\) alone.
