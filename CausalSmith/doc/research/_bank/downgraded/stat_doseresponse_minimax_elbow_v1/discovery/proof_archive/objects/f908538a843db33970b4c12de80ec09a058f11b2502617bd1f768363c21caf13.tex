For power choices \(h\asymp n^{-u}\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-v}\), \(\mathrm{lem:explicit\text{-}histogram\text{-}window\text{-}upper}\) gives
\[
R(u,v)=\min\{2a_0u,4q_0v,2q_0v+2b_0u,1-u-dv,1\}.
\]
Fix a candidate exponent \(r>0\). The inequalities \(R(u,v)\ge r\) are equivalent to
\[
u\ge\frac{r}{2a_0},\qquad v\ge\frac{r}{4q_0},\qquad b_0u+q_0v\ge\frac r2,
\qquad u+dv\le1-r,
\]
together with \(r\le1\), which will be automatic. Set \(x=u/r\) and \(y=v/r\). The minimum normalized cost needed to satisfy the first three inequalities is
\[
C_*=\min\left\{x+dy:x\ge\frac{1}{2a_0},\ y\ge\frac{1}{4q_0},\ b_0x+q_0y\ge\frac12\right\}.
\]
At the lower corner \((2a_0)^{-1},(4q_0)^{-1}\), the last constraint has deficit
\[
\left\{\frac12-\frac{b_0}{2a_0}-\frac14\right\}_+
=\frac12\left(\frac12-\frac{b_0}{a_0}\right)_+.
\]
One unit of that deficit can be supplied through \(x\) at objective cost \(1/b_0\), or through \(y\) at objective cost \(d/q_0\). Because this is a two-variable linear program, allocating all additional mass to the cheaper coordinate is optimal. Hence
\[
C_*=\frac{1}{2a_0}+\frac{d}{4q_0}
+\left(\frac12-\frac{b_0}{a_0}\right)_+
\min\left\{\frac{1}{2b_0},\frac{d}{2q_0}\right\}.
\]
The remaining inequality is \(rC_*\le1-r\). Therefore the largest feasible exponent is
\[
r_*=(1+C_*)^{-1}=R_{\mathrm{hist}}.
\]
If \((x_*,y_*)\) is an optimizer in the displayed finite linear program, then \(u=r_*x_*\) and \(v=r_*y_*\) satisfy \(u+dv=1-r_*<1\) and all four rate inequalities, so \(R(u,v)=r_*\). Thus the upper bound in the statement follows from \(\mathrm{lem:explicit\text{-}histogram\text{-}window\text{-}upper}\). The lower bound follows from \(\mathrm{lem:all\text{-}beta\text{-}oracle\text{-}lower\text{-}floor}\).

If \(b_0\ge a_0/2\), the positive-part term vanishes, proving the asserted \(\beta\)-free formula. Finally, if \(\alpha\le1\), then
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}<\left(1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)^{-1}=\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1},
\]
because \(d/(4q_0)>0\). If \(\alpha>1\), then \(a_0=1\), so \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}<2/3<2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)\). This proves strict looseness of the bracket.
