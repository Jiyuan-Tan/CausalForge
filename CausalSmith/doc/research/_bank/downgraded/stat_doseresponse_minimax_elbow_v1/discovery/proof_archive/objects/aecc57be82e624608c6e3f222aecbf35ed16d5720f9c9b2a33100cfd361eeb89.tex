The lower inequality follows from the all-order lower-floor lemma and \(r_n(\alpha)=n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\) in the oracle-rate definition. We optimize the available histogram upper bound.

That upper bound asks us to maximize \(r\) over \(u,v,r\ge0\) subject to
\[
r\le2a_0u,
\qquad r\le4q_0v,
\qquad r\le2q_0v+2b_0u,
\qquad r\le1-u-dv.
\]
For fixed \(r>0\), set \(U=u/r\) and \(V=v/r\). The first three constraints are
\[
U\ge\frac{1}{2a_0},
\qquad V\ge\frac{1}{4q_0},
\qquad 2b_0U+2q_0V\ge1.
\]
Define
\[
m_*=
\min\left\{U+dV:
U\ge\frac{1}{2a_0},
\ V\ge\frac{1}{4q_0},
\ 2b_0U+2q_0V\ge1
\right\}.
\]
At \(U_0=(2a_0)^{-1}\) and \(V_0=(4q_0)^{-1}\), the mixed-constraint left side is
\[
2b_0U_0+2q_0V_0=\frac{b_0}{a_0}+\frac12.
\]
Its remaining deficit is therefore
\[
\Delta=\left(\frac12-\frac{b_0}{a_0}\right)_+.
\]
Writing \(U=U_0+x\) and \(V=V_0+y\), it remains to minimize \(x+dy\) over \(x,y\ge0\) subject to \(2b_0x+2q_0y\ge\Delta\). Let
\[
\lambda=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2b_0},\frac{d}{2q_0}\right\}.
\]
Because \(2b_0\lambda\le1\) and \(2q_0\lambda\le d\), every feasible pair satisfies
\[
x+dy\ge\lambda(2b_0x+2q_0y)\ge\lambda\Delta.
\]
This bound is attained by putting the entire deficit into \(x\) when \((2b_0)^{-1}\le d(2q_0)^{-1}\), and into \(y\) otherwise. Hence
\[
m_*
=
\frac{1}{2a_0}+\frac{d}{4q_0}
+
\left(\frac12-\frac{b_0}{a_0}\right)_+
\min\left\{\frac{1}{2b_0},\frac{d}{2q_0}\right\}
=
m_{\mathrm{hist}}(\alpha,\beta,s,d).
\]
The final exponent constraint is equivalent to
\[
r+u+dv=r\{1+U+dV\}\le1.
\]
Thus every feasible \(r\) is at most \((1+m_*)^{-1}\). Conversely, if \((U_*,V_*)\) minimizes the preceding problem, set
\[
r_*=(1+m_*)^{-1},
\qquad u_*=r_*U_*,
\qquad v_*=r_*V_*.
\]
All four constraints then hold, and \(1-u_*-dv_*=r_*\). Therefore the optimized exponent is
\[
r_*=R_{\mathrm{hist}}(\alpha,\beta,s,d).
\]
Since \(r_*>0\), \(u_*+dv_*=1-r_*<1\). The choices \(h\asymp n^{-u_*}\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-v_*}\) consequently satisfy the histogram lemma's count condition for all sufficiently large \(n\), and that lemma gives
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le
C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{hist}}(\alpha,\beta,s,d)}.
\]
The constants may depend on the fixed admissible tuple but not on \(n\).

If \(\alpha\le1\), then \(a_0=\alpha\) and \(d/(4q_0)>0\), so
\[
m_{\mathrm{hist}}>\frac{1}{2\alpha},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{hist}}<\left(1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)^{-1}
=
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
If \(\alpha>1\), then \(a_0=1\), whence \(m_{\mathrm{hist}}>1/2\) and
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}<\frac23<\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}.
\]
The exponent is positive because \(m_{\mathrm{hist}}\) is finite and nonnegative. Finally, when \(b_0\ge a_0/2\), the deficit \(\Delta\) is zero, and substitution gives the asserted \(\beta\)-free formula.
