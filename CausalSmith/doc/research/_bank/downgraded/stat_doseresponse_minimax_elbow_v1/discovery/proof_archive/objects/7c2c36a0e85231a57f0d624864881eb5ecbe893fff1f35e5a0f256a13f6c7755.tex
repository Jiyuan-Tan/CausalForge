Fix \(0<\beta<\beta^{\prime}<\infty\) and an admissible tuple with \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\in\Omega\). By \(\mathrm{thm:oracle\text{-}high\text{-}s}\), there are \(C_{\mathrm{up}}<\infty\) and \(n_0\in\mathbb N\), independent of \(n\), such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le C_{\mathrm{up}}r_n(\alpha)
=C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}.
\]
Because \(\beta<\beta^{\prime}\), the one-sided class-monotonicity lemma gives
\[
\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\subseteq
\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0).
\]
For every fixed estimator, its supremum risk over the subclass on the left is no larger than its supremum risk over the class on the right. Taking the infimum over the common estimator class yields
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0).
\]
Apply the all-\(\beta\) lower-floor lemma to the smoother class indexed by \(\beta^{\prime}\). It supplies \(C_{\mathrm{low}}>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\ge C_{\mathrm{low}}r_n(\alpha)
=C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}
\]
for every \(n\ge1\). Enlarging \(C_{\mathrm{up}}\) if necessary ensures \(C_{\mathrm{low}}\le C_{\mathrm{up}}\). Combining the preceding displays proves the asserted chain for every \(n\ge n_0\).

The lower bound makes the denominator below positive. Division gives
\[
1\le
\frac{\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)}
{\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)}
\le
\frac{C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}}
{C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}}
=\frac{C_{\mathrm{up}}}{C_{\mathrm{low}}}.
\]
Only the rough tuple was used to obtain the upper bound. The lower bound for \(\beta^{\prime}\) is uniform over every treatment-density smoothness order, and the middle inequality uses only class nesting. Thus no oracle-region condition on \(\beta^{\prime}\) is needed.
