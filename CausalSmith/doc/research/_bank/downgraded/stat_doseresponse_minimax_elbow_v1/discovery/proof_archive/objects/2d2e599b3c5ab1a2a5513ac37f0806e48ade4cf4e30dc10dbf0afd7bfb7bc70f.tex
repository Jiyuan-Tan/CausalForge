Put \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), \(S_0=s\wedge1\), and \(k=(2\alpha+1)^{-1}\). Membership of \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\) in \(\Omega\) means
\[
A_0+B_0\ge\alpha,
\qquad
S_0\ge g(\alpha,\beta,d).
\]
Let \(z_*\in[(\alpha-B_0)_+,A_0]\) be the minimizer furnished by \(\mathrm{lem:separated\text{-}threshold\text{-}optimization}\), and put \(w_*=\alpha-z_*\). The oracle-region tuning lemma permits the choices
\[
h\asymp n^{-k},\quad b_\mu\asymp n^{-kz_*/A_0},\quad \delta_\mu\asymp n^{-kz_*/S_0},
\]
\[
b_\pi\asymp n^{-kw_*/B_0},\quad \delta_\pi\asymp n^{-kw_*/S_0}.
\]
For these choices, with
\[
u=\delta_\mu^{S_0}+b_\mu^{A_0}+(nb_\mu\delta_\mu^d)^{-1/2},
\qquad
v=\delta_\pi^{S_0}+b_\pi^{B_0}+(nb_\pi\delta_\pi^d)^{-1/2},
\]
one has \(u=O(h^{z_*})\) and \(v=O(h^{w_*})\). Since \(z_*\le A_0\), \(w_*\le B_0\), and \(z_*+w_*=\alpha\),
\[
uv=O(h^\alpha),\qquad uh^{B_0}=O(h^\alpha),\qquad vh^{A_0}=O(h^\alpha).
\]
The structural inequality \(A_0+B_0\ge\alpha\) also gives \(h^{A_0+B_0}=O(h^\alpha)\). Hence
\[
(u+h^{A_0})(v+h^{B_0})=O(h^\alpha).
\]
The two pilots are trained on disjoint folds, so \(\mathrm{lem:independent\text{-}pilot\text{-}product}\) supplies the required product second moment without any fourth-moment condition. The exact remainder, constant-extension, design-adaptive bias, empirical-Gram, localized-variance, and concentration bounds are assembled in \(\mathrm{lem:beta\text{-}free\text{-}master\text{-}risk}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:localized\text{-}polynomial\text{-}master\text{-}risk}\). Therefore the explicit estimator satisfies, uniformly over the frozen class,
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\{\widehat\theta-\theta_P(t_0)\}^2\right]
\le C\left\{h^{2\alpha}+\frac1{nh}+\frac1n+e^{-c_1nh}\right\}.
\]
It is a Borel measurable member of \(\mathfrak T_n\), so the same display bounds the minimax risk. Because \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\alpha+1)}\),
\[
h^{2\alpha}\asymp(nh)^{-1}\asymp n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=r_n(\alpha).
\]
Also \(n^{-1}\le r_n(\alpha)\), and \(e^{-c_1nh}=O(r_n(\alpha))\). Thus \(\mathcal M_n\le C_+r_n(\alpha)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). For the finitely many remaining \(n\), the constant-zero estimator has risk at most \(M_Y^2\); enlarging \(C_+\) makes the upper bound valid for every \(n\ge1\).

The all-\(\beta\) lower-floor lemma applies to this same class and yields \(C_->0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)\ge C_-r_n(\alpha)
\]
for every \(n\ge1\). This proves the exact order.

Finally, if \(\beta<\alpha\), then \(\alpha\wedge\beta=\beta\). Since \(q\mapsto2q/(2q+1)\) has derivative \(2/(2q+1)^2>0\),
\[
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}>
\frac{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)}{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)+1}.
\]
The proved same-class minimax exponent on \(\Omega\) is therefore strictly larger than the treatment-smoothing exponent generated by the \(h^{\alpha\wedge\beta}\) conditional-bias term in Bonvini and Kennedy's Theorem 3.1. Their statement is an estimator-specific conditional-risk upper bound, whereas the present conclusion is a minimax result on the frozen class, so there is no contradiction.
