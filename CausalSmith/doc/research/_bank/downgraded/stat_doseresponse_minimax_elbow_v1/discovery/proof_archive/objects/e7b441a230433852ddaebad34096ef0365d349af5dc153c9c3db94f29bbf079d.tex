From the displayed bandwidth,
\[
nh\asymp n^{1-4s/\{\alpha(4s+d)+4s\}}
=n^{\alpha(4s+d)/\{\alpha(4s+d)+4s\}}.
\]
Raising this expression to the power \(2d/(4s+d)\) gives
\[
k\asymp n^{2\alpha d/\{\alpha(4s+d)+4s\}}.
\]
The exponent of \(n\) is larger than one if and only if
\[
2\alpha d>\alpha(4s+d)+4s,
\]
which is equivalent to \(\alpha d>4s(\alpha+1)\), or \(s<\alpha d/\{4(\alpha+1)\}\). Substitution of \((\alpha,d,s)=(1,1,1/10)\) gives bandwidth exponent \(2/9\), effective-sample exponent \(7/9\), and projection exponent \(10/9\). Finally, a Gram matrix built from \(n\) feature vectors is a sum of \(n\) rank-one matrices and therefore has rank at most \(n\). If \(k>n\), it has at least \(k-n\) zero eigenvalues and cannot define an invertible rank-\(k\) empirical projection.
