Let \(0<\beta<\beta^{\prime}<\infty\). On the unit interval, the frozen componentwise H\"older convention gives the constant-one embedding of the order-\(\beta^{\prime}\) ball into the order-\(\beta\) ball. When the integer parts agree, \(\lvert a-a'\rvert^{\beta^{\prime}-\lfloor\beta\rfloor}\le \lvert a-a'\rvert^{\beta-\lfloor\beta\rfloor}\). When the integer parts differ, repeated mean-value inequalities and the bounded higher derivatives give the required lower-order modulus, again because \(\lvert a-a'\rvert\le1\). Thus every designated section \(a\mapsto\pi_P(a\mid x)\) satisfying Assumption~\texttt{ass:pi-a-holder} at order \(\beta^{\prime}\) also satisfies it at order \(\beta\), with the same radius. All other conditions in Definition~\texttt{def:holder-class} are identical. Therefore
\[
\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta^{\prime},s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\subseteq
\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0).
\]
For a fixed estimator, taking the supremum of its squared risk over the larger class cannot decrease the result. Taking the infimum over the common decision class \(\mathfrak T_n\), as in Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-mse}, preserves the inequality and proves the asserted minimax monotonicity.
