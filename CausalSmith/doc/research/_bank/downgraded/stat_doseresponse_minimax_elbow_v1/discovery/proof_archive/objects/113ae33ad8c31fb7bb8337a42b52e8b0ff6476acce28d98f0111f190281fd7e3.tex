Put
\[
r_0=\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1},
\qquad
r_{\mathrm f}=\frac{8}{4+2/(\alpha\vee\beta)+d/s},
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{low}}=\min\{r_0,r_{\mathrm f}\}.
\]
For every fixed \(0<c<1\), Lemmas~\texttt{lem:all-beta-oracle-lower-floor}, \texttt{lem:extreme-low-s-fuzzy-lower}, and \texttt{lem:optimized-deficient-rate-bracket} give constants independent of \(n\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
C_{\mathrm{low}}n^{-r_{\mathrm{low}}}
\le
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,c,t_0)
\le
C_{\mathrm{up}}n^{-R_{\mathrm{ach}}}.
\]
Consequently, any exact exponent must obey \(R_{\mathrm{ach}}\le\rho(\alpha,\beta,s,d)\le r_{\mathrm{low}}\). The fuzzy converse strictly improves the oracle floor exactly when
\[
\frac ds>4+\frac4\alpha-\frac{2}{\alpha\vee\beta}.
\]
Its exponent is independent of the numerical value of \(\beta\) when \(\beta\le\alpha\). On the upper side, \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}\) is independent of \(\beta\) when \(\beta\wedge1\ge(\alpha\wedge1)/2\), and \(R_{\mathrm{sep}},R_{\mathrm{hist}},R_{\mathrm{ach}}\) are independent of its numerical value on \(\beta\ge1\); these are estimator-side facts, not residual minimax-invariance theorems.
