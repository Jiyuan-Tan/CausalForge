The clipped pilots satisfy \(\lvert\widetilde\mu\rvert\le M_Y\) and \(c/2\le\widetilde\pi\le2/c\). Their population marginal density obeys \(c/2\le\widetilde f\le2/c\). Hence every ideal pilot pseudo-outcome is bounded by a constant depending only on \(M_Y,c\). Let \(r_h\) be its population score and \(\gamma_h=D_h^{-1}r_h\). The population Gram bounds imply \(\lVert\gamma_h\rVert\le C\). The exact normal-equation identity is
\[
\widehat\gamma_h-\gamma_h
=\widehat D_h^{-1}\frac1{N_{\mathrm{lp}}}\sum_{i\in I_{\mathrm{lp}}}K_h(A_i)S_h(A_i)\{\widetilde\xi_i-S_h(A_i)^\top\gamma_h\}.
\]
On the good-Gram event, \(\lVert\widehat D_h^{-1}\rVert\le1/\lambda_*\). The summands on the right are centered, bounded by \(C/h\), and have second moment at most \(C/h\). Independence on the local-polynomial fold therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good}}\lVert\widehat\gamma_h-\gamma_h\rVert^2\,\middle|\,I_\mu\right]\le\frac{C}{nh}.
\]

It remains to compare empirical and population marginalizations. Conditional on the pilot fold, \(\widehat m-P_X\widetilde\mu\) and \(\widehat f-P_X\widetilde\pi\) are independent empirical averages of bounded functions on \(I_X\), so each has conditional second moment at most \(C/n\). The induced change in each pseudo-outcome is
\[
(\widehat f-\widetilde f)\frac{Y-\widetilde\mu(A,X)}{\widetilde\pi(A\mid X)}+(\widehat m-\widetilde m).
\]
On the good-Gram event the local-polynomial map has bounded \(L_1\) operator norm: its absolute empirical kernel weight sum has bounded second moment because \(\mathbb E K_h(A)^2\le C/h\) and \(nh\) is bounded below. Independence of \(I_X\) and \(I_{\mathrm{lp}}\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, therefore bounds the squared coefficient change by \(C/n\). This proves both assertions.
