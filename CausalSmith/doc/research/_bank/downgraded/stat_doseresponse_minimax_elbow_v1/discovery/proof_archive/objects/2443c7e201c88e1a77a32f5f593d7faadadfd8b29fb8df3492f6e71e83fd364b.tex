For the separated-pilot estimator, take \(h=n^{-\gamma}\). Independently minimize each slice-pilot root risk from Lemma~\texttt{lem:localized-polynomial-master-risk}. The choices
\[
b_\mu\asymp n^{-E_\mu/A_0},\quad \delta_\mu\asymp n^{-E_\mu/S_0},
\qquad
b_\pi\asymp n^{-E_\pi/B_0},\quad \delta_\pi\asymp n^{-E_\pi/S_0}
\]
balance bias and standard deviation because
\[
E_\mu\left(2+A_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1}\right)=1,\qquad E_\pi\left(2+B_0^{-1}+dS_0^{-1}\right)=1.
\]
Consequently \(e_\mu\lesssim n^{-\min\{A_0\gamma,E_\mu\}}\) and \(e_\pi\lesssim n^{-\min\{B_0\gamma,E_\pi\}}\). The master risk is therefore bounded, up to an \(n\)-free constant, by the sum of terms with exponents
\[
2\alpha\gamma,\qquad 1-\gamma,\qquad 2\{\min(A_0\gamma,E_\mu)+\min(B_0\gamma,E_\pi)\}.
\]
Maximizing the smallest exponent over \(0\le\gamma\le1\) proves achievability of \(R_{\mathrm{sep}}\). The maximizing \(\gamma\) exists because the displayed minimum is continuous on a compact interval.

For completeness, a second explicit construction gives the histogram exponent. Split the sample into four folds; partition the dose neighbourhood into intervals of side \(h\) and the covariate cube into cells of side \(\delta\); on two folds form clipped cellwise estimates of the slice regression and density; on the third fold form their cellwise marginal averages; and on the fourth fold average the same orthogonal score used in Definition~\texttt{def:beta-free-handle}, with zero assigned to empty cells. Counts, clipping, and finite sums make this a Borel estimator. Conditional binomial variance and the empty-cell inequality \(q(1-q)^N\le (N+1)^{-1}\), summed over \(O(\delta^{-d})\) cells, yield the uniform risk bound
\[
C\left[n^{-q}+n^{-1+q\{1+(2A_0)^{-1}+d/(4S_0)\}}
n^{-1+q\{1+(2A_0)^{-1}+d/(4S_0)+(1/2-B_0/A_0)_+\min((2B_0)^{-1},d/(2S_0))\}}\right]
\]
when the dose and covariate side lengths and the auxiliary density-slice width are chosen as
\[
h=n^{-q/(2A_0)},\qquad \delta=n^{-q/(4S_0)},
\]
with the auxiliary width equal to the larger of \(n^{-q/(2B_0)}\) and \(n^{-qd/(4S_0B_0)}\). The first term is the squared treatment and covariate approximation error; the second is the ordinary occupied-cell variance; the last is the density-slice remainder, and the positive part records that this remainder is absorbed when \(B_0\ge A_0/2\). These three bounds follow by conditioning on the cell counts, applying the two H\"older seminorm inequalities within each cell, and using Cauchy--Schwarz for the orthogonal product remainder; no lower bound on a cell probability is used.

Set \(q=R_{\mathrm{hist}}\). By its displayed definition, the exponent of the last variance term equals \(q\), while the ordinary variance exponent is at least \(q\); hence the second estimator has risk \(O(n^{-R_{\mathrm{hist}}})\). The parameters are known class indices, so selecting in advance whichever of the two explicit estimators has the larger exponent is a single data-driven estimator, proving the maximum.
