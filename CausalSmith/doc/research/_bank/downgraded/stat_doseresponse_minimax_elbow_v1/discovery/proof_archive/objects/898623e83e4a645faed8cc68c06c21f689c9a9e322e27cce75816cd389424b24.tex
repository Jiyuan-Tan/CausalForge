Put \(A_0=\alpha\wedge1\), \(B_0=\beta\wedge1\), \(S_0=s\wedge1\), and \(k=(2\alpha+1)^{-1}\). Assume \((\alpha,\beta,s,d)\in\Omega\). By the definition of \(\Omega\),
\[
A_0+B_0\ge\alpha,
\qquad
S_0\ge g(\alpha,\beta,d).
\]
The separated-threshold optimization and oracle-region tuning lemmas therefore provide \(z_*\in[(\alpha-B_0)_+,A_0]\), with the two pilot denominators positive, and admissible tuning sequences
\[
h\asymp n^{-k},
\qquad
b_\mu\asymp n^{-kz_*/A_0},
\qquad
\delta_\mu\asymp n^{-kz_*/S_0},
\]
\[
b_\pi\asymp n^{-k(\alpha-z_*)/B_0},
\qquad
\delta_\pi\asymp n^{-k(\alpha-z_*)/S_0}.
\]
For these choices define the pilot envelopes
\[
u_\mu
=\delta_\mu^{S_0}+b_\mu^{A_0}
+(n b_\mu\delta_\mu^d)^{-1/2},
\qquad
v_\pi
=\delta_\pi^{S_0}+b_\pi^{B_0}
+(n b_\pi\delta_\pi^d)^{-1/2}.
\]
The separate-slice pilot bound together with the oracle-region tuning lemma gives
\[
u_\mu=O(n^{-kz_*})=O(h^{z_*}),
\qquad
v_\pi=O(n^{-k(\alpha-z_*)})=O(h^{\alpha-z_*}).
\]
Here \(z_*\le A_0\), while \(z_*\ge(\alpha-B_0)_+\) implies \(\alpha-z_*\le B_0\). Since \(0<h\le1\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), these inequalities yield
\[
u_\mu v_\pi=O(h^\alpha),
\qquad
u_\mu h^{B_0}=O(h^{z_*+B_0})=O(h^\alpha),
\]
\[
v_\pi h^{A_0}=O(h^{\alpha-z_*+A_0})=O(h^\alpha),
\qquad
h^{A_0+B_0}=O(h^\alpha).
\]
Consequently,
\[
(u_\mu+h^{A_0})(v_\pi+h^{B_0})=O(h^\alpha).
\]
The regression and density pilots in the explicit estimator are trained on disjoint folds. Thus the independent-pilot product lemma applies to their squared errors, and the exact double-robust remainder, constant-extension control, design-adaptive local-polynomial bias, good-Gram bound, localized variance bound, and bounded-sum concentration are assembled by the localized-polynomial and beta-free master-risk lemmas. Substituting the last display into that risk bound gives constants \(C,c_1>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\left\{\widehat\theta_{h,b_\mu,\delta_\mu,b_\pi,\delta_\pi}
-\theta_P(t_0)\right\}^2
\right]
\le
C\left\{h^{2\alpha}+\frac1{nh}+\frac1n+e^{-c_1nh}\right\}.
\]
The displayed estimator is Borel measurable by its construction and hence belongs to \(\mathfrak T_n\). Taking the infimum over \(\mathfrak T_n\) preserves the upper bound. Since \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\alpha+1)}\),
\[
h^{2\alpha}\asymp\frac1{nh}
\asymp n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}=r_n(\alpha).
\]
Moreover, \(n^{-1}\le r_n(\alpha)\), and \(e^{-c_1nh}=O(r_n(\alpha))\) because \(nh\asymp n^{2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\). Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\le C_+r_n(\alpha).
\]
For each of the finitely many remaining sample sizes, the constant-zero estimator lies in \(\mathfrak T_n\), and \(\lvert\theta_P(t_0)\rvert\le M_Y\) follows from \(\lvert\mu_P(t_0,x)\rvert\le M_Y\). Enlarging \(C_+\) therefore makes the upper bound valid for every \(n\ge1\).

The all-\(\beta\) oracle lower-floor lemma applies to the same class and gives \(C_->0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathcal M_n(\alpha,\beta,s,d,M_Y,L,pbar,c,t_0)
\ge C_-r_n(\alpha)
\]
for every \(n\ge1\). This proves the asserted two-sided order.

Finally suppose \(\beta<\alpha\). Then \(\alpha\wedge\beta=\beta\). The function \(q\mapsto 2q/(2q+1)\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), since its derivative is \(2/(2q+1)^2>0\). Hence
\[
\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}
>
\frac{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)}{2(\alpha\wedge\beta)+1}.
\]
Thus, on \(\Omega\), the proved same-class minimax exponent is strictly larger than the stated treatment-smoothing exponent in the cited conditional-risk result. The former is a minimax statement for the present model class, whereas the latter is an estimator-specific upper-bound term, so the comparison creates no contradiction.
