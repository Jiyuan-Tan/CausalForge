The joint law of \((A,X)\) has density \(\pi_P(a\mid x)p_X(x)\), so \(f_A\) is the marginal density of \(A\), and Bayes' formula gives, for almost every \(a\),
\[
dP_{X\mid A=a}(x)
=
\frac{\pi_P(a\mid x)}{f_A(a)}\,dP_X(x).
\]
The denominators are positive by Lemma~\texttt{lem:marginal-treatment-positivity}. Since \(\mu_P(A,X)\) is a version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A,X]\), the tower property gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\frac{f_A(A)}{\pi_P(A\mid X)}\{Y-\mu_P(A,X)\}
\,\middle|\,A=a\right]=0.
\]
The remaining term is the \(A\)-measurable variable \(\theta_P(A)\), proving the identity almost everywhere. Lemma~\texttt{lem:partial-mean-holder-transfer} makes \(\theta_P\) continuous; it is therefore the asserted continuous version.
