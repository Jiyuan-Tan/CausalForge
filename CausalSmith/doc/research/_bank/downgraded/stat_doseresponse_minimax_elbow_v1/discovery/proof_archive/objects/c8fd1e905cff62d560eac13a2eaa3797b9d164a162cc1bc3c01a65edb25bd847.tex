Partition \([0,1]^d\) into at most \(C_dk\) half-open cubes of side length at most \(C_d'k^{-1/d}\). On each cube, the multivariate Taylor polynomial allowed by the coordinatewise H\"older convention has degree at most \(\lfloor s\rfloor\), and its uniform remainder is bounded by \(CLk^{-s/d}\). Applying this construction separately to \(f\) and \(q\) produces \(f_k,q_k\in V_k\) with
\[
\lVert f-f_k\rVert_\infty+\lVert q-q_k\rVert_\infty\le Ck^{-s/d}.
\]
This deterministic approximation uses only the two frozen slice-smoothness assumptions. Since \(P_X\) is a probability measure, each sup-norm bound also bounds the corresponding \(L_2(P_X)\) norm. Orthogonal projection is the best \(L_2(P_X)\) approximation from its range. Moreover, \((I-\Pi_{k,P_X})\widetilde f=(I-\Pi_{k,P_X})\widetilde q=0\). Hence
\[
\lVert(I-\Pi_{k,P_X})(f-\widetilde f)\rVert_2
\le\lVert f-f_k\rVert_2\le Ck^{-s/d},
\]
and the same argument applies to \(q\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives the bilinear bound.

For the last assertion, choose an orthonormal basis \(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r\) of the image of \(V_k\) in \(L_2(P_X)\), where \(r\le\dim(V_k)\le C_dk\). Then \(\Pi_{k,P_X}(x,x')=\sum_{j=1}^r\phi_j(x)\phi_j(x')\). Independence and orthonormality yield
\[
\mathbb E[\Pi_{k,P_X}(X_1,X_2)^2]
=
\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{\ell=1}^r
\mathbb E[\phi_j(X_1)\phi_\ell(X_1)]
\mathbb E[\phi_j(X_2)\phi_\ell(X_2)]
=r.
\]
No property of a Lebesgue-density representative of \(P_X\) entered the proof.
