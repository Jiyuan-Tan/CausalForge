\textbf{Proof.}
We proceed from the primitive finite design.  Write a global assignment as \(z=(z_{[1]},\ldots,z_{[L]})\).  Fix \(\ell\) and a representative \(a_{\ell r}\).  Global blinding and the bijection \(z\mapsto-z\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_\pi\{Z_{[\ell]}=a_{\ell r}\}
&=\sum_{z:z_{[\ell]}=a_{\ell r}}\pi(z)
=\sum_{z:z_{[\ell]}=a_{\ell r}}\pi(-z)\\
&=\sum_{w:w_{[\ell]}=-a_{\ell r}}\pi(w)
=\Pr_\pi\{Z_{[\ell]}=-a_{\ell r}\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The two events in (1) are disjoint and their union has probability \(p_{\ell r}(\pi)\).  The sign pairs partition the nonempty finite support \(\mathcal A_\ell\), so \(p_{\ell r}\ge0\) and \(\sum_rp_{\ell r}=1\).  It follows that
\[
[Q_\pi]_{\ell\ell}
=\mathbb E_\pi[Z_{[\ell]}Z_{[\ell]}^\top]
=\sum_{r=1}^{m_\ell}p_{\ell r}a_{\ell r}a_{\ell r}^\top
=\Sigma_\ell(p_\ell).
\tag{2}
\]

Under \(\pi_p\), a pair configuration \((r_1,\ldots,r_L)\) and sign configuration \((\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_L)\) has mass
\[
2^{-L}\prod_{\ell=1}^Lp_{\ell r_\ell}.
\tag{3}
\]
This is unchanged by simultaneous sign reversal, so \(\pi_p\in\Pi_{\mathrm{het}}\).  Equation (2) holds for \(\pi_p\) as well.  Independent symmetric signs give \(\mathbb E_{\pi_p}Z_{[\ell]}=0\), and block independence therefore gives
\[
[Q_{\pi_p}]_{\ell s}
=\mathbb E_{\pi_p}[Z_{[\ell]}Z_{[s]}^\top]=0,
\qquad \ell\ne s.
\tag{4}
\]
This proves all covariance-preservation, support, and boundary-mass claims without a positive lower bound on any \(p_{\ell r}\).

We next prove the spectral assertion.  Since \(H_\ell H_\ell^\top\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\mathbf1_{b_\ell}^\perp\), centering and positive semidefiniteness imply
\[
G_{j\ell}=H_\ell(H_\ell^\top G_{j\ell}H_\ell)H_\ell^\top,
\qquad
G_{j\ell}^{1/2}=H_\ell S_{j\ell}H_\ell^\top.
\tag{5}
\]
Every \(a_{\ell r}\) is balanced, hence \(a_{\ell r}=H_\ell w_{\ell r}\).  Combining (2) and (5), the \(\ell\)-th diagonal principal block of
\[
A_j(\pi):=G_j^{1/2}Q_\pi G_j^{1/2}
\]
is
\[
[A_j(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=H_\ell L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)H_\ell^\top.
\tag{6}
\]
The nonzero eigenvalues in (6) are exactly those of \(L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\).  Since \(A_j(\pi)\succeq0\), maximizing its Rayleigh quotient over all unit vectors is at least as large as maximizing over unit vectors supported in one block.  Thus
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\pi)\}
\ge\max_\ell\lambda_{\max}\{L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
By (4), \(A_j(\pi_p)\) is the direct sum of the matrices in (6), and hence
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\pi_p)\}
=\max_\ell\lambda_{\max}\{L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}.
\tag{8}
\]

For completeness, the possibly singular ellipsoid has the exact representation
\[
\mathcal M_j
=\{G_j^{1/2}u:\|P_{\operatorname{range}(G_j)}u\|_2\le1\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, diagonalizing \(G_j\) shows in each positive-eigenvalue coordinate that \(x^\top G_j^\dagger x\) is the squared Euclidean norm of the corresponding coordinate of \(u\); null-space coordinates do not change \(x\).  Substitution of (9), followed by the elementary Rayleigh-quotient identity for a positive-semidefinite matrix, gives
\[
R_j(\pi)=N^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\pi)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (7)--(10) prove simultaneous Pareto productization for the full finite menu.  Notice that neither a rank condition nor commutativity of \(G_{j\ell}\) across \(j\) was used.  The direct-sum property was used in (5)--(6) and is therefore an explicit boundary of the conclusion.

We now verify the causal statement directly.  Within every block, every assignment in \(\mathcal A_\ell\) has zero coordinate sum; hence \(Z^\top\mathbf1_N=0\).  From the observed-outcome formula and \(Z_{\ell u}^2=1\),
\[
2Z_{\ell u}Y_{\ell u}^{\mathrm{obs}}
=Y_{\ell u}(1)-Y_{\ell u}(0)
+Z_{\ell u}\{Y_{\ell u}(1)+Y_{\ell u}(0)\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Summing (11), dividing by \(N\), and using balance gives
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{het}}-\tau_N^{\mathrm{het}}
=N^{-1}Z^\top\mu_{\mathrm{het}}.
\tag{12}
\]
The potential outcomes are fixed by the quantifier in the theorem, and \(Z\sim\pi\) is the only random object.  Global blinding gives \(\mathbb E_\pi Z=0\), so (12) proves unbiasedness and
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{het}})
=N^{-2}\mu_{\mathrm{het}}^\top Q_\pi\mu_{\mathrm{het}}.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(\mu_{\mathrm{het}}\in\mathcal M_j\), (13) is bounded by the supremum defining \(R_j(\pi)\).  This establishes the causal variance guarantee while keeping the fixed effect \(\tau_N^{\mathrm{het}}\) distinct from the design-selection value \(\lambda_{\mathrm{het}}^\star\).

For the uniform product baseline, (8)--(10) with \(p_{\ell r}=1/m_\ell\) give
\[
R_j(\pi_0^{\mathrm{het}})=\beta_j.
\tag{14}
\]
For any design, (7) and the definition of \(\omega_{j\ell}\) yield
\[
R_j(\pi)\ge N^{-2}\max_\ell\omega_{j\ell}.
\tag{15}
\]
For a fixed class \(j\), compactness of each simplex and continuity of \(\lambda_{\max}\) produce a minimizer \(p_{j\ell}^\star\) for each local problem.  The product law using these blockwise minimizers attains equality in (15) by (8)--(10).  Therefore
\[
\min_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{het}}}R_j(\pi)
=N^{-2}\max_\ell\min_{p_\ell\in\Delta_{m_\ell}}
\lambda_{\max}\{L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}
=\rho_j.
\tag{16}
\]
Because \(L_{j\ell}\) is affine in \(p_\ell\), the inequality \(\lambda_{\max}(L_{j\ell})\le u\) is equivalent to \(L_{j\ell}\preceq uI_{b_\ell-1}\), proving the local oracle programs.

Assume now \(\delta_j>0\) for every class included in the minimum.  Productization cannot increase any \(R_j\), so it cannot decrease any \(g_j\).  It is therefore sufficient and necessary to optimize over \(p\in\prod_\ell\Delta_{m_\ell}\).  For a common scalar \(t\), equations (8)--(10) give the chain of equivalences
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_j(\pi_p)\ge t
&\iff R_j(\pi_p)\le\beta_j-\delta_jt\\
&\iff \lambda_{\max}\{L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}
\le N^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)\quad\text{for every }\ell\\
&\iff L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)
\preceq N^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_{b_\ell-1}
\quad\text{for every }\ell.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Imposing (17) for every \(j\) and maximizing \(t\) is exactly the boxed semidefinite program.  The product of the local simplices is compact, and the minimum gain is continuous, so an optimizer exists; (3), (8), and (10) certify that its product law attains the same value.  The uniform baseline is feasible and gives all gains zero, while (16) implies every class gain is at most one, so \(0\le\lambda_{\mathrm{het}}^\star\le1\).  If some \(\delta_j=0\), division by zero makes that class's gain undefined; this is precisely why strict gain positivity is required.

There are \(\sum_\ell m_\ell\) mass variables and one epigraph variable, one simplex equality per block, and one LMI for every class--block pair.  Expanding
\[
L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)
=\sum_{r=1}^{m_\ell}p_{\ell r}
S_{j\ell}w_{\ell r}w_{\ell r}^\top S_{j\ell}
\]
shows that the coefficient array has the stated size.  Since a balanced sign vector in a block of size \(b_\ell\) is one of \(\binom{b_\ell}{b_\ell/2}\) vectors, central symmetry gives
\[
m_\ell\le\frac12\binom{b_\ell}{b_\ell/2}.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus bounded block size gives bounded \(m_\ell\) and bounded LMI order.  The formulation enumerates only the supplied local representatives, never the Cartesian product of global assignments.

Finally specialize to \(b_\ell=4\), representatives \(a_{\ell r}=v_r\), and \(H_\ell=H=[h_1\ h_2\ h_3]\).  Since \(H^\top v_r=2e_r\),
\[
\Sigma_\ell(p_\ell)=4H\operatorname{Diag}(p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})H^\top,
\qquad
L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)=4S_{j\ell}\operatorname{Diag}(p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})S_{j\ell}.
\tag{19}
\]
These are exactly the objects in the matched-quadruple order-three program, and the uniform baseline has masses \(1/3\).  If a local kernel has rank one, write \(S_{j\ell}=\sigma_{j\ell}q_{j\ell}q_{j\ell}^\top\) with \(\|q_{j\ell}\|_2=1\).  Then
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{L_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}
=4\sigma_{j\ell}^2\sum_{r=1}^3p_{\ell r}q_{j\ell,r}^2,
\]
which is linear in the local masses; hence every local spectral constraint becomes the corresponding scalar linear constraint.  For the homogeneous two-class loading pattern, these scalar coefficients are block-independent and the common-gain epigraph reduces to the same three-direction two-class linear program; eliminating its simplex variables by the two active affine constraints yields its piecewise closed form.  Thus (19) proves the stated corollary chain without adding a rank or commutativity condition to the general result.

As the smallest boundary check, when \(L=1\), \(b_1=2\), and \(\mathcal A_1=\{(1,-1),(-1,1)\}\), one has \(m_1=1\), and global blinding already forces the unique symmetric law; productization is equality and \(\delta_j=0\), so the theorem correctly withholds the gain normalization. \(\square\)
