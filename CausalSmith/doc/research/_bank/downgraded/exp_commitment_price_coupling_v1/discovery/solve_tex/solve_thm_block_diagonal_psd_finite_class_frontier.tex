%%% FIELD block-diagonal-proof
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(q\ge1\), put \(n=4q\), and fix the finite menu in \(\mathrm{def:block-diagonal-psd-menu}\).  All potential outcomes and kernels are fixed before assignment by \(\mathrm{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:assignment-only-randomness}\).  No exposure-probability inverse occurs below, so the boundary values \(p_{\ell k}=0\) are admissible.

First consider an arbitrary \(\pi\in\Pi_n\).  The involution \(z\mapsto-z\), together with \(\pi(z)=\pi(-z)\), maps the event \(Z_{[\ell]}=v_k\) bijectively to the event \(Z_{[\ell]}=-v_k\).  Hence
\[
\Pr_\pi\{Z_{[\ell]}=v_k\}
=\Pr_\pi\{Z_{[\ell]}=-v_k\}
=\frac12p_{\ell k}(\pi). \tag{1}
\]
Since \(v_k=2h_k\) and the columns of \(H=[h_1\ h_2\ h_3]\) are orthonormal,
\[
[Q_\pi]_{\ell\ell}
=\mathbb E_\pi[Z_{[\ell]}Z_{[\ell]}^\top]
=\sum_{k=1}^3p_{\ell k}v_kv_k^\top
=4H\operatorname{Diag}(p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})H^\top. \tag{2}
\]
Construct \(\pi_p\) as in \(\mathrm{def:block-product-design}\).  Its block signs are independent, symmetric, and independent of the directions.  Thus \(\mathbb E_{\pi_p}Z_{[\ell]}=0\), different blocks have zero cross moment, and (2) is preserved:
\[
[Q_{\pi_p}]_{\ell\ell}=[Q_\pi]_{\ell\ell},
\qquad [Q_{\pi_p}]_{\ell r}=0\quad(\ell\ne r). \tag{3}
\]
The construction is supported on \(\mathcal Z_n\) and is globally sign-symmetric, so \(\pi_p\in\Pi_n\).

We next compute the ellipsoidal risk without a rank or invertibility assumption.  If \(G\succeq0\), spectral decomposition shows
\[
\{x\in\operatorname{range}(G):x^\top G^\dagger x\le1\}
=\{G^{1/2}y:y\in\operatorname{range}(G),\ \|y\|_2\le1\}. \tag{4}
\]
Rayleigh--Ritz applied to (4) therefore gives, for every \(Q\succeq0\),
\[
\sup_{x\in\operatorname{range}(G),\ x^\top G^\dagger x\le1}x^\top Qx
=\lambda_{\max}(G^{1/2}QG^{1/2}). \tag{5}
\]
Apply (5) with \(G=G_j^{\mathrm{bd}}\) and \(Q=Q_\pi\).  Because \(G_j^{\mathrm{bd}}\) is a direct sum and every local kernel is centered,
\[
G_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}=H\widehat G_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}H^\top,
\qquad
(G_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}})^{1/2}=HS_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}H^\top. \tag{6}
\]
Equations (2) and (6) show that the \(\ell\)-th diagonal principal block of
\((G_j^{\mathrm{bd}})^{1/2}Q_\pi(G_j^{\mathrm{bd}})^{1/2}\), in \(H\)-coordinates, is
\[
4S_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}
\operatorname{Diag}(p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})
S_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}
=L_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}(p_\ell). \tag{7}
\]
The largest eigenvalue of a positive-semidefinite matrix dominates the largest eigenvalue of each principal block.  Combining this fact with (5) and (7) yields
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi)
\ge \frac1{n^2}\max_\ell
\lambda_{\max}\{L_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}(p_\ell)\}. \tag{8}
\]
For \(\pi_p\), (3) makes the global matrix a direct sum of the blocks in (7), whence
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi_p)
=\frac1{n^2}\max_\ell
\lambda_{\max}\{L_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}(p_\ell)\}
\le R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi). \tag{9}
\]
The same \(\pi_p\) works for every \(j\), proving simultaneous Pareto productization; no commutativity between kernels was used.

For completeness, the causal target is separate from this risk calculation.  By \(\mathrm{ass:no-interference}\) and \(\mathrm{def:estimator}\), the pointwise identity proved in \(\mathrm{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\) is
\[
\widehat\tau-\tau_N=n^{-1}Z^\top\mu. \tag{10}
\]
Blinding gives \(\mathbb E_\pi Z=0\).  Thus the fixed-array assumptions imply
\[
\mathbb E_\pi\widehat\tau=\tau_N,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)=n^{-2}\mu^\top Q_\pi\mu. \tag{11}
\]
If \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{bd}}\), (11) is bounded by the supremum defining \(R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi)\).

Under \(\pi_0\), every \(p_{\ell k}=1/3\), so
\[
L_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}(\bar p_\ell)
=\frac43(S_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}})^2
=\frac43\widehat G_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}. \tag{12}
\]
The isometry \(H\) preserves the nonzero eigenvalues, and (9) gives
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi_0)
=\frac1{n^2}\max_\ell
\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\frac43G_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}\right)
=\beta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}. \tag{13}
\]
For one fixed class, productization reduces the oracle problem to independent choices of \(p_\ell\in\Delta_3\).  Continuity on the compact simplices gives local minimizers, and the elementary identity
\[
\min_{(p_1,\ldots,p_q)\in\Delta_3^q}
\max_\ell f_\ell(p_\ell)
=\max_\ell\min_{p_\ell\in\Delta_3}f_\ell(p_\ell) \tag{14}
\]
follows by the lower bound in each block and by choosing all local minimizers simultaneously.  Taking
\(f_\ell(p_\ell)=\lambda_{\max}\{L_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}(p_\ell)\}\) in (14) proves
\[
\min_{\pi\in\Pi_n}R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi)
=\rho_j^{\mathrm{bd}}
=\frac1{n^2}\max_\ell\omega_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}. \tag{15}
\]
Since \(L_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}(p_\ell)\) is affine in \(p_\ell\), the inequality
\(\lambda_{\max}(L_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}(p_\ell))\le u\) is exactly the order-three LMI
\(L_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}(p_\ell)\preceq uI_3\), proving the displayed local programs.

Assume now \(\delta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}>0\) for every class; this is precisely the division condition for \(g_j^{\mathrm{bd}}\).  By (9), every blinded law is weakly dominated in every class by the product law with the same marginals.  Hence the maximin may be restricted without loss to product laws.  For such a law, (9) implies
\[
g_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi_p)\ge t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{bd}}(p_\ell)
\preceq n^2(\beta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}-\delta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}t)I_3
\quad\text{for every }\ell. \tag{16}
\]
Together with \(p_\ell\in\Delta_3\), (16) is exactly the boxed semidefinite program.  Its feasible marginal set is compact, the objective after eliminating \(t\) is continuous, and therefore an optimizer exists.  The associated \(\pi_p\) attains its value.  Counting the three masses per block and the scalar \(t\) gives \(3q+1\) variables, \(q\) simplex equalities, and \(J_{\mathrm{blk}}q\) LMIs of order three.  Equation (16) permits zero masses.  Finally, (6)--(9) use the direct-sum identity essentially; if a kernel couples blocks, the principal-block lower bound remains valid but deleting cross-block covariance need not preserve or lower its full spectral risk.  This proves exactly the stated scope boundary.  For the smallest check \(q=1\), there are no cross-block terms and (7)--(9) are equalities for all six assignments, so the program reduces directly to one three-mass simplex and the claimed boundary solutions remain valid. \(\square\)

%%% FIELD block-specific-proof
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix the positive loadings in \(\mathrm{def:block-specific-spectral-menu}\), and write
\[
g_{j\ell}=\sum_{k=1}^3\sqrt{d_{j\ell k}}h_k\in\mathbf1_4^\perp,
\qquad u_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}}=E_\ell g_{j\ell}. \tag{1}
\]
The vectors in (1) are nonzero because every \(d_{j\ell k}>0\), and distinct columns have disjoint block supports.  Hence \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) has full column rank \(q\).  Spectral decomposition, exactly as in (4)--(5) of \(\mathrm{thm:block-diagonal-psd-finite-class-frontier}\), gives
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)
=n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{(K_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^{1/2}Q_\pi(K_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^{1/2}\}
=n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\}, \tag{2}
\]
where the second equality follows because the two displayed matrices have the same nonzero eigenvalues.  The fixed-array unbiasedness and variance bound follow from \(\mathrm{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\), whose algebra applies verbatim to this ellipsoid, or directly from the pointwise identity \(\widehat\tau-\tau_N=n^{-1}Z^\top\mu\) and (2).

If block \(\ell\) uses \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon v_k\), orthonormality and \(v_k=2h_k\) give
\[
(u_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}})^\top Z
=2\varepsilon\sqrt{d_{j\ell k}}. \tag{3}
\]
Therefore, for an arbitrary blinded law,
\[
[C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}(\pi). \tag{4}
\]
Since a positive-semidefinite matrix's largest eigenvalue dominates every diagonal entry, (2)--(4) prove the stated lower certificate.

The global kernel is the direct sum of the local rank-one kernels \(g_{j\ell}g_{j\ell}^\top\).  Thus \(\mathrm{thm:block-diagonal-psd-finite-class-frontier}\) applies.  Its product law preserves (4) and removes all off-diagonal score covariances.  Equivalently, independence and symmetric signs in (3) give directly
\[
C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)
=4\operatorname{diag}_{\ell}
\left(\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}\right), \tag{5}
\]
and its principal-block certificate gives \(R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)\le R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\) simultaneously for all classes.

At the baseline \(p_{\ell k}=1/3\), (5) yields
\[
B_j^{\mathrm{blk}}
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\frac13\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}
=\frac{4b_j}{n^2}. \tag{6}
\]
For the class-specific oracle, every convex combination satisfies
\[
\sum_kd_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}\ge\min_kd_{j\ell k}. \tag{7}
\]
Choosing in each block a minimizing direction makes (7) an equality in every block.  Equations (2), (5), and (7) therefore give
\[
(r_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^\star
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\min_kd_{j\ell k}
=\frac{4\bar r_j}{n^2},
\qquad
\Delta_j^{\mathrm{blk}}=\frac{4(b_j-\bar r_j)}{n^2}. \tag{8}
\]

Assume \(b_j>\bar r_j\) for every \(j\), so every gain denominator in (8) is positive.  Productization shows that the maximin may be restricted to the marginals \(p_{\ell k}\).  By (5)--(8), for a product law
\[
g_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)\ge t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}
\le b_j-(b_j-\bar r_j)t
\quad\text{for every }\ell. \tag{9}
\]
Together with the block simplex constraints, (9) is the boxed LP.  Compactness of the product of simplices gives an optimizer, and \(\mathrm{def:block-product-design}\) implements it.  Conversely, if an arbitrary commitment optimizer had marginals that did not solve the LP, its productization would strictly increase the minimum gain beyond the optimal value, a contradiction; hence every optimizer's marginals solve the LP.  The variable and constraint counts follow immediately.  No positive lower bound on \(p_{\ell k}\) was used.  The final scope statements follow from the arbitrary-rank local theorem and from its explicit direct-sum boundary. \(\square\)

%%% FIELD quadruple-proof
\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(q\ge1\), put \(n=4q\), and fix \(a,b,c_j,s_j>0\).  These positivity conditions make all displayed gain denominators positive.  Zero local direction probabilities remain allowed.

For compactness define
\[
(d_{1,1},d_{1,2},d_{1,3})=(c_1,c_1+s_1a,c_1+s_1b),
\]
\[
(d_{2,1},d_{2,2},d_{2,3})=(c_2+s_2a,c_2,c_2+s_2b). \tag{1}
\]
If \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon v_k\), then \(v_k=2h_k\) and orthonormality give
\[
u_{j\ell}^\top Z=2\varepsilon\sqrt{d_{j,k}},
\qquad (u_{j\ell}^\top Z)^2=4d_{j,k}. \tag{2}
\]
This identity is pointwise and needs no cross-block independence.  Summing the diagonal entries of \(C_j(\pi)\) and using \(\mathrm{def:direction-frequencies}\) yields
\[
q^{-1}\operatorname{tr}C_1(\pi)
=4\{c_1+s_1(ap_2+bp_3)\}, \tag{3}
\]
\[
q^{-1}\operatorname{tr}C_2(\pi)
=4\{c_2+s_2(ap_1+bp_3)\}. \tag{4}
\]
Each score matrix is positive semidefinite of order \(q\), so its largest eigenvalue is at least the average of its eigenvalues.  This proves the two trace certificates.

The specialization of \(\mathrm{thm:block-specific-finite-class-frontier}\) to (1) gives the spectral risk identity.  Equations (3)--(4) imply \(R_j(\pi)\ge4c_j/n^2\).  The product law using only \(v_1\) for class \(1\), respectively only \(v_2\) for class \(2\), makes the corresponding score covariance \(4c_jI_q\).  Hence
\[
r_j^\star=\frac{4c_j}{n^2}. \tag{5}
\]
Under \(\pi_0\), every direction has probability \(1/3\), and independent symmetric block signs give
\[
C_j(\pi_0)=4\left\{c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3\right\}I_q. \tag{6}
\]
Thus
\[
B_j=\frac4{n^2}\left\{c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3\right\},
\qquad
\Delta_j=\frac{4s_j(a+b)}{3n^2}>0. \tag{7}
\]

Set \(A=a+b\),
\[
L_1=ap_2+bp_3,
\qquad L_2=ap_1+bp_3. \tag{8}
\]
The trace certificates and (7) imply
\[
g_1(\pi)\le1-\frac{3L_1}{A},
\qquad
g_2(\pi)\le1-\frac{3L_2}{A}. \tag{9}
\]
Since \(p_1+p_2+p_3=1\),
\[
\max(L_1,L_2)
\ge\frac{L_1+L_2}{2}
=\frac a2(1-p_3)+bp_3
\ge\min\left\{\frac a2,b\right\}. \tag{10}
\]
Consequently
\[
\min_jg_j(\pi)
\le1-\frac3{a+b}\min\left\{\frac a2,b\right\}
=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{a-2b}{a+b},&a\ge2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2b-a}{2(a+b)},&a\le2b.
\end{cases} \tag{11}
\]

For a product law with common block frequencies \((p_1,p_2,p_3)\) and independent symmetric signs, (2) gives
\[
C_1=4(c_1+s_1L_1)I_q,
\qquad C_2=4(c_2+s_2L_2)I_q. \tag{12}
\]
If \(a>2b\), choose \((0,0,1)\); if \(a<2b\), choose \((1/2,1/2,0)\); and if \(a=2b\), choose
\[
p_1=p_2=t,
\qquad p_3=1-2t,
\qquad 0\le t\le\frac12. \tag{13}
\]
In the three cases \(L_1=L_2\) equals respectively \(b\), \(a/2\), and \(b=a/2\).  Thus (9)--(12) are equalities, proving attainment and the piecewise value.  The first witness is exactly \(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}}\).

It remains to prove necessity of the stated frequencies.  Equality in (11) requires equality throughout (10).  When \(a>2b\),
\[
\frac a2(1-p_3)+bp_3
=b+\left(\frac a2-b\right)(1-p_3),
\]
so equality forces \(p_3=1\).  When \(a<2b\), it forces \(p_3=0\); equality between maximum and average then forces \(L_1=L_2\), hence \(p_1=p_2=1/2\).  At \(a=2b\), the average is always \(b\), and equality of maximum and average is equivalent to \(p_1=p_2=t\), giving (13).  Conversely, (12) proves that every listed vector is attained by its product law.

For the regime-specific witness, with \(m=\min\{a/2,b\}\),
\[
B_j-R_j
=\frac{4s_j}{n^2}\left\{\frac{a+b}{3}-m\right\}
=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{4s_j(a-2b)}{3n^2},&a>2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2s_j(2b-a)}{3n^2},&a<2b,\\[6pt]
0,&a=2b.
\end{cases} \tag{14}
\]
Thus both classes improve strictly exactly off the elbow.  At the elbow, \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) belongs to (13), so \(\pi_0\) is maximin and \(\lambda^\star=0\).  Equations (3)--(4) and (12) are the matching lower and upper optimality certificates.  Finally, when \(q=1\), direct enumeration of the six assignments makes each \(C_j\) scalar and reproduces (3)--(4); minimizing the larger increment gives exactly (10), checking all boundary cases at the smallest population size. \(\square\)

%%% FIELD ryan-proof
\textbf{Proof.}
The published comparator supplies only the normalization recorded in \(\mathrm{def:ryan-relative-efficiency}\); the following calculation is internal to the present finite menu.  By \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\), \(r_j^\star=4c_j/n^2\).  Its trace certificates imply, with \(p_k=p_k(\pi)\),
\[
\operatorname{RE}_1(\pi)\le\frac1{1+F_1},
\qquad
\operatorname{RE}_2(\pi)\le\frac1{1+F_2}, \tag{1}
\]
where
\[
F_1=\kappa_1(ap_2+bp_3),
\qquad F_2=\kappa_2(ap_1+bp_3). \tag{2}
\]
All denominators are positive.  It therefore suffices to minimize \(\max(F_1,F_2)\), provided the trace bounds can be attained simultaneously.

Assume \(a>2b>0\) and \(\kappa_2>\kappa_1>0\), and put
\[
D=\kappa_1(a-b)+\kappa_2b,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{\kappa_2b}{D},
\qquad
1-\alpha=\frac{\kappa_1(a-b)}D. \tag{3}
\]
Then \(D>0\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).  Substituting \(p_3=1-p_1-p_2\) gives the affine certificate
\[
\alpha F_1+(1-\alpha)F_2
=\frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}
+\frac{a(a-2b)\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}p_1
\ge m:=\frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}. \tag{4}
\]
Since a maximum dominates every convex combination, \(\max(F_1,F_2)\ge m\).

Set
\[
p_1^\circ=0,
\qquad
p_2^\circ=\frac{b(\kappa_2-\kappa_1)}D,
\qquad
p_3^\circ=1-p_2^\circ. \tag{5}
\]
The second mass is positive, while
\[
D-b(\kappa_2-\kappa_1)=a\kappa_1>0, \tag{6}
\]
so (5) is in the simplex.  Substitution gives \(F_1=F_2=m\).  The independent-block, independent-sign law with (5) lies in \(\Pi_n\) and has scalar score matrices
\[
C_j=4c_j(1+F_j)I_q,
\qquad
R_j=\frac{4c_j}{n^2}(1+F_j). \tag{7}
\]
Thus (1) is attained for both classes and
\[
\eta_{\mathrm{REL}}
=\frac1{1+m}
=\left[1+\frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}
{\kappa_1(a-b)+\kappa_2b}\right]^{-1}. \tag{8}
\]

If any design attains (8), then both inequalities
\[
\min_j\operatorname{RE}_j(\pi)
\le\{1+\max(F_1,F_2)\}^{-1}
\le(1+m)^{-1} \tag{9}
\]
are equalities.  The coefficient of \(p_1\) in (4) is strictly positive, so \(p_1=0\).  Since \(0<\alpha<1\), equality of the maximum and the convex combination forces \(F_1=F_2=m\).  Solving on the face \(p_1=0\) gives exactly (5).  By contrast, the \(a>2b\) branch of \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) forces every gain-normalized optimizer to have \(p_3=1\), whereas (5) has \(p_2^\circ>0\).  The optimizer sets are therefore disjoint.  Both calculations use the same oracle risks, committed risks, and feasible laws; only the criterion normalization differs, so no submodel claim about the comparator is needed. \(\square\)

\textbf{Proof.}
Choose one representative \(s\) from every pair \(\{z,-z\}\).  Since \(|\mathcal Z_n|=6^q\) and no sign vector equals its negative, \(|\mathscr S|=m=6^q/2\).  Every blinded law corresponds uniquely to a mass vector \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\) through
\[
\alpha_s=\pi(s)+\pi(-s)=2\pi(s),
\qquad
Q_\pi=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_sss^\top. \tag{1}
\]
Conversely, (1) defines a blinded law by splitting each \(\alpha_s\) equally between \(s\) and \(-s\).  Therefore
\[
G_j^{1/2}Q_\pi G_j^{1/2}
=\sum_s\alpha_sG_j^{1/2}ss^\top G_j^{1/2}
=A_j(\alpha). \tag{2}
\]

For any nonzero \(G\succeq0\), spectral decomposition gives
\[
\{x\in\operatorname{range}(G):x^\top G^\dagger x\le1\}
=\{G^{1/2}y:y\in\operatorname{range}(G),\ \|y\|_2\le1\}. \tag{3}
\]
Rayleigh--Ritz and (3) imply
\[
\sup_x x^\top Qx=\lambda_{\max}(G^{1/2}QG^{1/2}), \tag{4}
\]
where the supremum is over the ellipsoid in (3).  Thus the generalized class risk is \(n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}\).  In particular, \(\alpha^0\) gives \(\beta_j\), and minimization over the mass simplex gives \(\rho_j\).

The causal target remains \(\tau_N\).  Under the three declared design-based assumptions, \(\mathrm{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\) gives
\[
\widehat\tau-\tau_N=n^{-1}Z^\top\mu,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\pi\widehat\tau=\tau_N,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)=n^{-2}\mu^\top Q_\pi\mu. \tag{5}
\]
If \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}\), (4)--(5) give the displayed variance bound.  No superpopulation randomness or asymptotic approximation is used.

Assume henceforth \(\delta_j>0\) for every class.  For a mass vector \(\alpha\), the gain constraint \(g_j(\alpha)\ge t\) is equivalent, using (2)--(4), to
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}
\le n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A_j(\alpha)\preceq n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n. \tag{6}
\]
Taking all classes and maximizing \(t\) proves the exact primal program \((\mathrm P)\).  Compactness of \(\Delta_m\) and continuity of the eigenvalue functions imply attainment.

Let \(r_j=\operatorname{rank}(G_j)\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:finite-trace-isotropy}\),
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}
\ge\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_j}. \tag{7}
\]
Replacing the left side of (6) by the lower bound (7) enlarges the feasible set and yields \((\mathrm T)\); hence \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\le\overline\lambda\).

Suppose first that \((\mathrm E)\) holds for some \(\alpha\).  Then \((\alpha,\overline\lambda)\) is feasible for \((\mathrm P)\), so the preceding inequality is an equality.  Conversely, equality of the two optimum values and a primal optimizer at that value give \((\mathrm E)\).  Moreover, (7) and \((\mathrm E)\) imply that \((\alpha,\overline\lambda)\) is feasible, and therefore optimal, for \((\mathrm T)\).  If class \(j\) is trace-active there, its trace threshold equals the right side of \((\mathrm E)\).  Thus (7) is an equality, and \(\mathrm{lem:finite-trace-isotropy}\) gives
\[
A_j(\alpha)
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_j}
P_{\operatorname{range}(G_j)}. \tag{8}
\]
For trace-slack classes, only the spectral threshold \((\mathrm E)\) is required.  Conversely, an optimizer of \((\mathrm T)\) satisfying (8) for every active class and \((\mathrm E)\) for every slack class satisfies \((\mathrm E)\) for all classes.  This proves the claimed necessary-and-sufficient active-isotropy characterization without a commutativity assumption.

If equality fails, both finite programs have attained, computable values, so
\(\Gamma_n=\overline\lambda-\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star>0\) is the sharp additive relaxation gap by definition.  If \((\alpha^\star,t^\star)\) solves \((\mathrm P)\), splitting its masses as in (1) produces \((\mathrm D)\), a law in \(\Pi_n\) with second moment \(\sum_s\alpha_s^\star ss^\top\).  Equations (2) and (6) show that it attains \(t^\star\), and hence pays exactly \(\Gamma_n\).

It remains to establish the dual certificate.  Put
\[
F_j(\alpha,t)=A_j(\alpha)-n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n. \tag{9}
\]
For multipliers \(X_j\succeq0\), the Lagrangian upper bound for the maximization problem is
\[
\mathcal L(\alpha,t;X)
=t-\sum_j\operatorname{tr}\{X_jF_j(\alpha,t)\}. \tag{10}
\]
The supremum over \(t\in\mathbb R\) is finite exactly when
\[
n^2\sum_j\delta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)=1. \tag{11}
\]
Under (11), the supremum over \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\) equals
\[
n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)
-\min_{s\in\mathscr S}\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js}). \tag{12}
\]
Introducing \(\gamma\) with
\(\gamma\le\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})\) for every \(s\) converts (12) exactly into the displayed dual \((\mathrm C)\).

For clarity, dual equality is not being assumed as a headline condition.  It follows here from the primitive finite program.  Take the relative-interior mass vector \(\alpha_s=1/m\).  Because every \(\delta_j>0\), choosing \(t\) sufficiently negative makes every matrix \(-F_j(\alpha,t)\) positive definite.  Thus the affine equality \(\sum_s\alpha_s=1\), the nonnegative-mass cone, and all semidefinite inequalities have a strictly feasible point relative to the simplex.  The finite-dimensional separating-hyperplane proof of Lagrange duality now applies: for any number larger than the primal optimum, separate the convex set of tuples \((F_1(\alpha,t),\ldots,F_J(\alpha,t),t)\) enlarged by the negative-semidefinite cones and the downward objective ray from the point with that objective and zero residuals.  Strict feasibility forces the objective coefficient of the separator to be positive; normalize it to one.  The matrix coefficients are positive semidefinite, and minimizing the separator over the simplex gives exactly (11)--(12).  Letting the separated objective decrease to the primal optimum yields a dual feasible limit.  Such a limit exists because (11), \(\delta_j>0\), and \(X_j\succeq0\) uniformly bound all \(\operatorname{tr}(X_j)\), hence all \(X_j\), while \(\gamma\) may be chosen as the finite minimum in (12).  Weak duality follows directly from (10), so the limiting upper bound equals the primal value.  This proves (C), dual attainment, and the equal-objective optimality certificate.

Program \((\mathrm P)\) has \(m\) masses, one simplex equality, and \(J_{\mathrm{gen}}\) LMIs of order \(n\); (D) implements it without rounding.  Since the feasible class is all of \(\Pi_n\), the attainment constant is one.  The enumeration has \(m=6^q/2\) columns, so it is exponential in \(q\).  Nothing in (1)--(12) deletes cross-block terms, which is why arbitrary coupled kernels are covered here but not by the local productization theorem.  If some \(\delta_j=0\), (6) divides by zero in the gain normalization; the stated convention boundary is therefore necessary.  At \(q=1\), (1) enumerates exactly three sign pairs, providing the requested finite smallest-instance check of the mass-simplex representation and all primal constraints. \(\square\)

\textbf{Proof.}
Every \(s\in\mathcal Z_n\) is balanced within each quadruple, so
\[
s\in\mathcal H
=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^q\mathbf1_4^\perp,
\qquad \dim(\mathcal H)=3q=d. \tag{1}
\]
Also every coordinate of \(s\) is \(\pm1\), whence
\[
\operatorname{tr}(ss^\top)=\|s\|_2^2=n. \tag{2}
\]
Let \(\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal H)\) denote the symmetric operators on \(\mathcal H\).  It has dimension
\(D=d(d+1)/2\).  The matrices \(ss^\top\) all lie in the affine hyperplane of \(\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal H)\) having trace \(n\), whose dimension is at most \(D-1\).

Write an optimal second moment as
\[
Q^\star=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_s^star ss^\top,
\qquad \alpha^star\in\Delta_m, \tag{3}
\]
using the mass representation in \(\mathrm{thm:higher-rank-menu-exact-program}\).  We give the support reduction explicitly.  If more than \(D\) coefficients in (3) are positive, the corresponding points in an affine space of dimension at most \(D-1\) are affinely dependent.  Hence there are numbers \(c_s\), not all zero and supported on the positive coefficients, such that
\[
\sum_sc_s=0,
\qquad
\sum_sc_sss^\top=0. \tag{4}
\]
After changing the common sign if necessary, some \(c_s>0\).  Set
\[
\varepsilon=\min_{c_s>0}\frac{\alpha_s^star}{c_s}>0,
\qquad
\alpha_s'=\alpha_s^star-\varepsilon c_s. \tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) preserve total mass and the matrix in (3), keep every coefficient nonnegative, and make at least one previously positive coefficient zero.  Repeating finitely many times yields \(\widetilde\alpha\in\Delta_m\) with
\[
Q^\star=\sum_s\widetilde\alpha_sss^\top,
\qquad
|\operatorname{supp}(\widetilde\alpha)|\le\min\{m,D\}. \tag{6}
\]

Define the blinded law
\[
\widetilde\pi(s)=\widetilde\pi(-s)=\frac12\widetilde\alpha_s,
\qquad s\in\mathscr S. \tag{7}
\]
It belongs to \(\Pi_n\), and (6)--(7) give \(Q_{\widetilde\pi}=Q^\star\).  Substituting \(m=6^q/2\), \(d=3q\), and \(D=d(d+1)/2\) gives the claimed sign-pair bound
\[
\min\left\{\frac{6^q}{2},\frac{3q(3q+1)}2\right\}. \tag{8}
\]
Splitting each positive sign-pair mass uses at most twice as many assignments.  Every generalized risk and every constraint of \((\mathrm P)\) depends on the law only through \(Q_\pi\), so \(\widetilde\pi\) has exactly the same risks and commitment value as the original optimizer.  The reduction starts from \(Q^\star\); it does not furnish that matrix or locate its sparse decomposition without access to the exponentially enumerated columns.  Hence it is a support-existence result, not a polynomial-time algorithm. \(\square\)
