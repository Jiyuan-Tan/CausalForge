\textbf{Proof.}
Every blinded law \(\pi\in\Pi_n\) has the unique sign-pair mass vector
\[
\alpha_s=\pi(s)+\pi(-s)=2\pi(s),
\qquad s\in\mathscr S,
\tag{1}
\]
and \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\). Conversely, every \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\) defines a blinded law by assigning mass \(\alpha_s/2\) to each of \(s\) and \(-s\). Since \((-s)(-s)^\top=ss^\top\),
\[
Q_\pi=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_sss^\top,
\qquad
G_j^{1/2}Q_\pi G_j^{1/2}=A_j(\alpha).
\tag{2}
\]
This parametrizes all of \(\Pi_n\), including boundary laws.  Equation (2) uses no property of \(G_j\) beyond positive semidefiniteness.

For a possibly singular nonzero positive-semidefinite \(G_j\), spectral decomposition gives
\[
\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}
=\{G_j^{1/2}u:\|P_{\operatorname{range}(G_j)}u\|_2\le1\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Rayleigh--Ritz and (2) therefore yield
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}}
\frac{x^\top Q_\pi x}{n^2}
=\frac1{n^2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}.
\tag{4}
\]

We derive the causal variance statement from the primitive design conditions.  Every assignment in \(\mathcal Z_n\) is balanced, hence \(Z^\top\mathbf1_n=0\).  Fixed potential outcomes and consistency give, unit by unit,
\[
2Z_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}
=Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)+Z_i\{Y_i(1)+Y_i(0)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Summing (5), dividing by \(n\), and using balance gives
\[
\widehat\tau-\tau_N=n^{-1}Z^\top\mu.
\tag{6}
\]
Global blinding gives \(\mathbb E_\pi Z=0\).  Since assignment is the only randomness and the potential outcomes are fixed,
\[
\mathbb E_\pi\widehat\tau=\tau_N,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)
=n^{-2}\mu^\top Q_\pi\mu.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (3)--(4) and (7) prove the variance bound whenever \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}\).  No centering or block-support property of \(G_j\) appears: components orthogonal to the balanced assignment span simply vanish after multiplication by \(Q_\pi\).

By (1)--(4), \(\beta_j\) is the class risk under \(\pi_0\), \(\rho_j\) is its minimum over \(\Pi_n\), and \(\delta_j\) is the baseline-to-oracle gain.  Suppose \(\delta_j>0\) for every class.  A common guarantee \(t\) is feasible exactly when
\[
\frac{\beta_j-n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{\delta_j}\ge t
\quad\text{for every }j,
\tag{8}
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
A_j(\alpha)\preceq n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n
\quad\text{for every }j.
\tag{9}
\]
Maximizing \(t\) proves \((\mathrm P)\).  Compactness of \(\Delta_m\) and continuity of the maximum eigenvalue give primal attainment.

The matrix \(A_j(\alpha)\) is supported on \(\operatorname{range}(G_j)\), whose positive dimension is \(r_j\).  Hence \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}trace\text{-}isotropy}\) gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}
\ge\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_j}.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus every feasible point of \((\mathrm P)\) is feasible for \((\mathrm T)\), and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\le\overline\lambda\).  Equality implies that a primal optimizer at \(t=\overline\lambda\) satisfies \((\mathrm E)\).  Conversely, any \(\alpha\) satisfying \((\mathrm E)\) makes \((\alpha,\overline\lambda)\) feasible for \((\mathrm P)\), forcing equality.  Taking traces in \((\mathrm E)\) shows that such an \(\alpha\) lies on the optimal face of \((\mathrm T)\).  For every trace-active class, (10) and \((\mathrm E)\) force equality in (10), which is exactly the isotropy identity \((\mathrm I)\).  For a trace-slack class, the remaining necessary and sufficient condition is \((\mathrm E)\).  This proves the equivalence without assuming commutativity.

If equality fails, \(\Gamma_n\) is positive by definition and computable from the two finite programs.  Applying the inverse construction after (1) to a primal optimizer gives \((\mathrm D)\); equations (2), (4), and (8) show that this law attains \(t^\star=\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\).

For the dual, define
\[
F_j(\alpha,t)
:=n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n-A_j(\alpha)\succeq0.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(X_j\succeq0\) and an equality multiplier \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), retain \(\alpha_s\ge0\) as the domain.  The Lagrangian is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L(\alpha,t;X,\gamma)
&=t+\sum_j\operatorname{tr}\{X_jF_j(\alpha,t)\}
+\gamma\left(\sum_s\alpha_s-1\right)\\
&=n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)-\gamma
+t\left\{1-n^2\sum_j\delta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)\right\}\\
&\quad+\sum_s\alpha_s
\left\{\gamma-\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Its supremum over \(t\in\mathbb R\) and \(\alpha_s\ge0\) is finite exactly when
\[
n^2\sum_j\delta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)=1,
\qquad
\gamma\le\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})
\quad\text{for every }s,
\tag{13}
\]
and then equals \(n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)-\gamma\).  This is \((\mathrm C)\).

There is no duality gap.  Choose \(\alpha\) in the relative interior of \(\Delta_m\) and then take \(t\) sufficiently negative.  Because every \(\delta_j>0\), all matrices in (11) are positive definite.  Thus Slater's condition holds on the affine simplex slice.  Finite-dimensional conic separation then gives strong duality and dual attainment; equivalently, separating the feasible hypograph from objective values above the primal optimum yields nonnegative matrix multipliers, and finiteness in the free \(t\)-coordinate and every nonnegative mass coordinate forces exactly (13).  Weak duality follows directly from (12), so any primal--dual pair with equal objective values certifies optimality.

The primal uses \(m=6^q/2\) masses and \(J_{\mathrm{gen}}\) order-\(n\) matrix inequalities.  The law \((\mathrm D)\) attains its value without rounding.  The argument never used centering, block support, or commutativity, so it covers arbitrary cross-block coupled nonzero positive-semidefinite kernels.  Its column count remains exponential; the polynomial local-LMI productization theorem is a separate consequence of block-diagonal centered geometry.  \(\square\)
