\textbf{Proof.}
Every blinded law \(\pi\in\Pi_n\) has the unique sign-pair mass vector
\[
\alpha_s=\pi(s)+\pi(-s)=2\pi(s),
\qquad s\in\mathscr S,
\tag{1}
\]
and \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\). Conversely, every \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\) defines a blinded law by putting mass \(\alpha_s/2\) on each of \(s\) and \(-s\).  Since \((-s)(-s)^\top=ss^\top\),
\[
Q_\pi=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_sss^\top,
\qquad
G_j^{1/2}Q_\pi G_j^{1/2}=A_j(\alpha).
\tag{2}
\]
This parametrizes all of \(\Pi_n\), including boundary laws, and uses no property of \(G_j\) beyond positive semidefiniteness.

For a possibly singular nonzero positive-semidefinite \(G_j\), spectral decomposition gives
\[
\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}
=\{G_j^{1/2}u:\|P_{\operatorname{range}(G_j)}u\|_2\le1\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Rayleigh--Ritz and (2) yield
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}}\frac{x^\top Q_\pi x}{n^2}
=\frac1{n^2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}.
\tag{4}
\]
The causal estimator algebra in \(\mathrm{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\), under the three stated design-based assumptions, gives
\[
\mathbb E_\pi\widehat\tau=\tau_N,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)=n^{-2}\mu^\top Q_\pi\mu.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (3)--(5) prove the variance bound whenever \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}\).  Neither centering nor block support of \(G_j\) is used: components outside the block-balanced assignment subspace simply do not contribute to (2).

By (1)--(4), \(\beta_j\) is the class risk under \(\pi_0\), \(\rho_j\) is its minimum over \(\Pi_n\), and \(\delta_j\) is the baseline-to-oracle gain.  If every \(\delta_j>0\), a common guarantee \(t\) is feasible exactly when
\[
\frac{\beta_j-n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{\delta_j}\ge t
\quad\text{for every }j.
\tag{6}
\]
This is equivalent to
\[
A_j(\alpha)\preceq n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n
\quad\text{for every }j,
\tag{7}
\]
so maximizing \(t\) gives \((\mathrm P)\). Compactness of the simplex and continuity of the largest eigenvalue give primal attainment.

The matrix \(A_j(\alpha)\) is supported on \(\operatorname{range}(G_j)\), whose positive dimension is \(r_j\).  Hence \(\mathrm{lem:finite-trace-isotropy}\) gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}
\ge\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_j}.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus every feasible point of \((\mathrm P)\) is feasible for \((\mathrm T)\), and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\le\overline\lambda\).  Equality implies that a primal optimizer at \(t=\overline\lambda\) satisfies (E). Conversely, any \(\alpha\) satisfying (E) makes \((\alpha,\overline\lambda)\) feasible for \((\mathrm P)\), forcing equality. Taking traces in (E) shows that such \(\alpha\) is on the optimal face of \((\mathrm T)\). For every trace-active class, (8) and (E) force equality in (8), which by \(\mathrm{lem:finite-trace-isotropy}\) is exactly (I). For a trace-slack class, the remaining necessary and sufficient condition is (E). This proves the stated equivalence without commutativity.

If equality fails, \(\Gamma_n\) is positive by definition and is computable from the two finite programs.  Applying the inverse construction following (1) to a primal optimizer produces (D), and (2), (4), and (6) show that it attains \(t^\star=\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\).

For the dual, write the primal matrix slack as
\[
F_j(\alpha,t)
:=n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n-A_j(\alpha)\succeq0.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(X_j\succeq0\) and an equality multiplier \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), retaining \(\alpha_s\ge0\) as the domain, the Lagrangian is
\[
\begin{aligned}
L(\alpha,t;X,\gamma)
&=t+\sum_j\operatorname{tr}\{X_jF_j(\alpha,t)\}
+\gamma\left(\sum_s\alpha_s-1\right)\\
&=n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)-\gamma
+t\left\{1-n^2\sum_j\delta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)\right\}\\
&\quad+\sum_s\alpha_s
\left\{\gamma-\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Its supremum over \(t\in\mathbb R\) and \(\alpha_s\ge0\) is finite exactly when
\[
n^2\sum_j\delta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)=1,
\qquad
\gamma\le\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})
\quad\text{for every }s,
\tag{11}
\]
and then equals \(n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)-\gamma\). This is (C).

For completeness, there is no duality gap. Choose \(\alpha\) in the relative interior of \(\Delta_m\), so every mass is positive, and choose \(t\) sufficiently negative. Because every \(\delta_j>0\), all matrices in (9) are then positive definite. Thus the feasible affine slice meets the relative interior of the product of the nonnegative orthant and the positive-semidefinite cones. Separate this strictly feasible cone from the open set of objective values larger than the primal optimum. The strict interior point forces the separating coefficient on the objective coordinate to be positive; normalize it to one. The remaining separating coefficients are nonnegative matrix multipliers and one equality multiplier, and finiteness in the free \(t\)-coordinate and every nonnegative \(\alpha_s\)-coordinate forces exactly (11). Taking the supremum in (10) gives the displayed dual objective at the primal optimum. Weak duality follows directly from (10), so the two values and both attainments agree. Equal primal and dual objectives consequently certify optimality.

The primal has \(m=6^q/2\) mass columns and \(J_{\mathrm{gen}}\) order-\(n\) matrix inequalities. The law (D) attains its optimum without rounding. The separate sparse-support proposition concerns existence of an equivalent representation and does not turn this exponential-column computation into a polynomial-time method.  The proof nowhere used block support or centering of \(G_j\), completing the directed premise repair. \(\square\)
