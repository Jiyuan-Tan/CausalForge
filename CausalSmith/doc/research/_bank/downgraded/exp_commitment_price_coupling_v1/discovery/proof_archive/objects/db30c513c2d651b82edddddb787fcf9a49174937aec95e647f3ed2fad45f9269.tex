\textbf{Proof.}
We derive the result as the rank-one specialization of \(\mathrm{thm:block\text{-}diagonal\text{-}psd\text{-}finite\text{-}class\text{-}frontier}\).  For each class and block put
\[
a_{j\ell}=(\sqrt{d_{j\ell1}},\sqrt{d_{j\ell2}},\sqrt{d_{j\ell3}})^\top,
\qquad
g_{j\ell}=Ha_{j\ell},
\qquad
G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}=g_{j\ell}g_{j\ell}^\top.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
K_j^{\mathrm{blk}}
=U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^\top
=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^qG^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}.
\tag{2}
\]
All \(d_{j\ell k}\) are positive, so \(g_{j\ell}\ne0\).  The columns of \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) are nonzero and have disjoint supports; hence they are linearly independent and \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) has rank \(q\).  The ellipsoidal spectral identity in \(\mathrm{lem:variance\text{-}and\text{-}spectral\text{-}risk}\), applied to (2), gives
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)
=n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The same lemma derives design unbiasedness from fixed potential outcomes, consistency, assignment-only randomness, balance, and blinding, and gives the stated variance bound when \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\).

For a realized assignment, write \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon_\ell v_{k_\ell}\).  Since \(v_k=2h_k\) and the \(h_k\)'s are orthonormal,
\[
(u_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}})^\top Z
=2\varepsilon_\ell\sqrt{d_{j\ell k_\ell}}.
\tag{4}
\]
Squaring and averaging proves
\[
[C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}(\pi).
\tag{5}
\]
The coordinate Rayleigh quotient for the positive-semidefinite score matrix and (3) gives the displayed lower bound.  Under \(\pi_p\), independent symmetric signs make distinct scores in (4) uncorrelated, so
\[
C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)
=4\operatorname{diag}_{\ell}
\left(\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (3), (5), and (6) prove simultaneous Pareto domination.

To reduce the local matrix program, note that
\[
\widehat G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}=a_{j\ell}a_{j\ell}^\top,
\qquad
S^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}
=\frac{a_{j\ell}a_{j\ell}^\top}{\|a_{j\ell}\|_2}.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(D(p_\ell)=\operatorname{Diag}(p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})\), (7) gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{L^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}
=4a_{j\ell}^\top D(p_\ell)a_{j\ell}
=4\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}.
\tag{8}
\]
The baseline and class-oracle values in the block-diagonal theorem are therefore
\[
\beta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\frac13\sum_kd_{j\ell k}
=\frac{4b_j}{n^2},
\qquad
\rho_j^{\mathrm{bd}}
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\min_kd_{j\ell k}
=\frac{4\bar r_j}{n^2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Since (2) makes the response classes and risks identical to this specialization, (9) proves the formulas for \(B_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\), \((r_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^\star\), and \(\Delta_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\).

Assume \(b_j>\bar r_j\) for every \(1\le j\le J_{\mathrm{blk}}\).  These are precisely all classes entering the displayed minimum, and the assumption makes every gain denominator positive.  Substituting (8)--(9) into the local matrix inequality of the block-diagonal theorem gives
\[
4\sum_kd_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}
\le4\{b_j-(b_j-\bar r_j)t\},
\tag{10}
\]
which is exactly the displayed scalar constraint after division by four.  The block-diagonal theorem now gives equality with the LP and attainment by \(\pi_p\).  If an original law is optimal, productization with the same marginals cannot reduce any class gain and cannot exceed the maximin optimum; hence those marginals, together with the optimal value \(t\), solve the LP.  The variable and constraint counts follow from the \(q\) three-simplices and (10).  Zero direction masses are permitted because the design and estimator use no inverse exposure probabilities.

When \(q=1\), (6) is scalar and direct enumeration of the six assignments gives (8).  Thus the certificate is exact at \(n=4\).  Equation (8), rather than productization itself, is the rank-one step that converts order-three semidefinite inequalities into scalar inequalities.  Full-rank or noncommuting within-block kernels retain the local-LMI program; cross-block coupled kernels do not satisfy the direct-sum step (2) and require the global exponential-column program.  \(\square\)
