\textbf{Proof.}
The published comparator supplies only the variance-ratio normalization and maximin operation recorded in \(\mathrm{def:ryan-relative-efficiency}\); the calculation below concerns the present finite menu.  By \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\), \(r_j^\star=4c_j/n^2\).  Its trace certificates imply, with \(p_k=p_k(\pi)\),
\[
\operatorname{RE}_1(\pi)\le\frac1{1+F_1},
\qquad
\operatorname{RE}_2(\pi)\le\frac1{1+F_2},
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
F_1=\kappa_1(ap_2+bp_3),
\qquad
F_2=\kappa_2(ap_1+bp_3).
\tag{2}
\]
All denominators are positive because \(c_j,s_j,a,b>0\).

Assume \(a>2b>0\) and \(\kappa_2>\kappa_1>0\), and define
\[
D=\kappa_1(a-b)+\kappa_2b,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{\kappa_2b}{D},
\qquad
1-\alpha=\frac{\kappa_1(a-b)}D.
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(D>0\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).  Substituting \(p_3=1-p_1-p_2\) into (2) gives the affine lower certificate
\[
\alpha F_1+(1-\alpha)F_2
=\frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}
+\frac{a(a-2b)\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}p_1
\ge \frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}=:m.
\tag{4}
\]
Since a maximum dominates a convex combination, \(\max(F_1,F_2)\ge m\).

Equality is feasible.  Set
\[
p_1^\circ=0,
\qquad
p_2^\circ=\frac{b(\kappa_2-\kappa_1)}D,
\qquad
p_3^\circ=1-p_2^\circ.
\tag{5}
\]
The numerator of \(p_2^\circ\) is positive, and
\[
D-b(\kappa_2-\kappa_1)=a\kappa_1>0,
\tag{6}
\]
so (5) lies in the simplex.  Direct substitution yields \(F_1=F_2=m\).  Choose directions independently across blocks with probabilities (5), and attach independent symmetric signs.  This law belongs to \(\Pi_n\).  Its block scores are independent and centered, with the same second moment in every block, whence
\[
C_j=4c_j(1+F_j)I_q,
\qquad
R_j=\frac{4c_j}{n^2}(1+F_j).
\tag{7}
\]
Thus both bounds in (1) are equalities, and
\[
\eta_{\mathrm{REL}}
=\frac1{1+m}
=\left[1+\frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{\kappa_1(a-b)+\kappa_2b}\right]^{-1}.
\tag{8}
\]

The certificate also characterizes every optimizer.  At an optimizer, equality must hold in
\[
\min_j\operatorname{RE}_j(\pi)
\le\{1+\max(F_1,F_2)\}^{-1}
\le(1+m)^{-1}.
\tag{9}
\]
The coefficient of \(p_1\) in (4) is strictly positive, so equality forces \(p_1=0\).  Since \(0<\alpha<1\), equality between the maximum and the convex combination in (4) forces \(F_1=F_2=m\).  Solving this equality on the face \(p_1=0\) gives exactly (5).

Finally, the \(a>2b\) branch of \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) shows that every gain-normalized commitment optimizer has \(p_3=1\), whereas (5) has \(p_2^\circ>0\).  The optimizer sets are therefore disjoint.  Both calculations use the same oracle risks, committed risks, and feasible blinded laws; only the normalization differs. \(\square\)
