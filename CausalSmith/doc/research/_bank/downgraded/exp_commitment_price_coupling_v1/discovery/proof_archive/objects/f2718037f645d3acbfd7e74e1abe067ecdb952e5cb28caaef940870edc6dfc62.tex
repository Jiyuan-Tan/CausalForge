\textbf{Proof.}
In the homogeneous specialization, direction \(v_1\) minimizes the class-\(1\) squared loading in every block, while direction \(v_2\) minimizes the class-\(2\) squared loading.  Therefore \(\mathrm{prop:block\text{-}specific\text{-}single\text{-}class\text{-}reduction}\) gives, for the corresponding independent-sign product laws,
\[
R_1=\frac{4c_1}{n^2}=r_1^\star,
\qquad
R_2=\frac{4c_2}{n^2}=r_2^\star.
\]
For either one-class problem, substituting the oracle risk into the positive-gain fraction gives
\[
g_j=\frac{B_j-r_j^\star}{B_j-r_j^\star}=1,
\]
and no design can exceed one because \(R_j(\pi)\ge r_j^\star\) by the definition of the oracle minimum. Hence the one-class value is one.  By \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple\text{-}menu\text{-}frontier}\), the two-class value is either \((a-2b)/(a+b)\) when \(a\ge2b\), or \((2b-a)/\{2(a+b)\}\) when \(a\le2b\).  The first is strictly below one because \(b>0\), and the second is strictly below one because \(a>0\).  Thus simultaneous commitment, and not failure of either oracle construction, causes the loss. \(\square\)
