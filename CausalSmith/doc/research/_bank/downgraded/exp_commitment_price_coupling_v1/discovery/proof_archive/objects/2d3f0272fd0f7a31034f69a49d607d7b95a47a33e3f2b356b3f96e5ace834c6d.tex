\textbf{Proof.}
Write a global assignment as \(z=(z_{[1]},\ldots,z_{[q]})\). Fix \(\ell\) and \(k\). Global blinding in \(\mathrm{def:design\text{-}space}\) and the bijection \(z\mapsto-z\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_\pi\{Z_{[\ell]}=v_k\}
&=\sum_{z:z_{[\ell]}=v_k}\pi(z)
=\sum_{z:z_{[\ell]}=v_k}\pi(-z)\\
&=\sum_{w:w_{[\ell]}=-v_k}\pi(w)
=\Pr_\pi\{Z_{[\ell]}=-v_k\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Their sum is \(p_{\ell k}(\pi)\) by \(\mathrm{def:block\text{-}direction\text{-}marginals}\), proving the asserted sign symmetry. Since \(v_k=2h_k\), (1) implies
\[
[Q_\pi]_{\ell\ell}
=\mathbb E_\pi[Z_{[\ell]}Z_{[\ell]}^\top]
=\sum_{k=1}^3p_{\ell k}v_kv_k^\top
=4H\operatorname{Diag}(p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})H^\top.
\tag{2}
\]

Construct \(\pi_p\) as in \(\mathrm{def:block\text{-}product\text{-}design}\). Its mass at a direction configuration \((k_1,\ldots,k_q)\) and sign configuration \((\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_q)\) is
\[
2^{-q}\prod_{\ell=1}^qp_{\ell k_\ell},
\tag{3}
\]
which is unchanged by simultaneous sign reversal. Thus \(\pi_p\in\Pi_n\). Equation (2) also holds under \(\pi_p\). For \(\ell\ne r\), independence and the symmetric signs give
\[
[Q_{\pi_p}]_{\ell r}
=\mathbb E_{\pi_p}[Z_{[\ell]}Z_{[r]}^\top]
=\mathbb E_{\pi_p}Z_{[\ell]}
\,\mathbb E_{\pi_p}Z_{[r]}^\top=0.
\tag{4}
\]
This proves the covariance identities and all support and blinding claims. No lower overlap condition was used.

We next exploit the exact block-diagonal geometry. Because \(H^\top H=I_3\), \(HH^\top\) is the projector onto \(\mathbf1_4^\perp\). The condition \(G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}\mathbf1_4=0\), symmetry, and positive semidefiniteness imply
\[
G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}
=H\widehat G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}H^\top,
\qquad
(G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell})^{1/2}
=HS^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}H^\top.
\tag{5}
\]
The direct sum commutes with taking the positive-semidefinite square root, so
\[
(G_j^{\mathrm{bd}})^{1/2}
=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^q(G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell})^{1/2}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (2), (5), and (6), the \(\ell\)-th diagonal principal block of
\[
A_j(\pi):=(G_j^{\mathrm{bd}})^{1/2}Q_\pi(G_j^{\mathrm{bd}})^{1/2}
\]
is
\[
[A_j(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=H L^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}(p_\ell)H^\top.
\tag{7}
\]
The nonzero eigenvalues of the matrix in (7) are those of \(L^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\). Since \(A_j(\pi)\succeq0\), the Rayleigh quotient on vectors supported in block \(\ell\) yields
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\pi)\}
\ge\max_\ell\lambda_{\max}\{L^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Under \(\pi_p\), (4) makes \(A_j(\pi_p)\) the direct sum of the matrices in (7). Therefore
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\pi_p)\}
=\max_\ell\lambda_{\max}\{L^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}.
\tag{9}
\]

For completeness, diagonalize the possibly singular matrix \(G_j^{\mathrm{bd}}\). Its ellipsoid has the representation
\[
\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{bd}}
=\{(G_j^{\mathrm{bd}})^{1/2}u:
\|P_{\operatorname{range}(G_j^{\mathrm{bd}})}u\|_2\le1\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Rayleigh--Ritz applied to (10) gives
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi)
=n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\pi)\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (8)--(11) prove the simultaneous Pareto productization claim. The fixed-outcome, consistency, assignment-only-randomness, support, and blinding conditions are exactly those used in \(\mathrm{lem:variance\text{-}and\text{-}spectral\text{-}risk}\), whose estimator identity gives
\[
\mathbb E_\pi\widehat\tau=\tau_N,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)=n^{-2}\mu^\top Q_\pi\mu.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{bd}}\), (10)--(12) imply \(\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)\le R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi)\). Thus the causal target \(\tau_N\) remains distinct from the robust design criterion.

Under \(\pi_0\), the local directions have probabilities \(1/3\), and independent symmetric signs kill the off-diagonal blocks. Hence (9) gives
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi_0)
=\frac1{n^2}\max_\ell
\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\frac43\widehat G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}\right)
=\frac1{n^2}\max_\ell
\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\frac43G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}\right)
=\beta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}.
\tag{13}
\]
For any design and its marginals, (8)--(11) show
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi)
\ge\frac1{n^2}\max_\ell\omega^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}.
\tag{14}
\]
Conversely, compactness of \(\Delta_3\) and continuity of the largest eigenvalue give, for every block, a minimizer \(p_{j\ell}^\star\) of the local problem. The product law using these blockwise minimizers has, by (9)--(11), risk equal to the right side of (14). Equivalently,
\[
\min_{(p_\ell)\in\Delta_3^q}
\max_\ell\lambda_{\max}\{L^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}
=\max_\ell\min_{p_\ell\in\Delta_3}
\lambda_{\max}\{L^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\},
\tag{15}
\]
because the decision variables are block-separable. This proves the oracle formula. The epigraph representation of each minimum in (15) is the displayed constant-size local semidefinite program.

Assume now \(\delta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}>0\) for every class in the minimum. Productizing any law cannot increase any \(R_j^{\mathrm{bd}}\), so it cannot decrease any \(g_j^{\mathrm{bd}}\). We may therefore optimize over \(p\in\Delta_3^q\). For a common scalar \(t\), equations (9)--(11) give
\[
g_j^{\mathrm{bd}}(\pi_p)\ge t
\iff
\lambda_{\max}\{L^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}
\le n^2(\beta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}-\delta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}t)
\quad\text{for every }\ell.
\tag{16}
\]
Since each local score is symmetric, its eigenvalue inequality in (16) is equivalent to the displayed order-three linear matrix inequality. Maximizing \(t\) gives the asserted program. Compactness of \(\Delta_3^q\) and continuity of the minimum gain guarantee existence of an optimizer; (3), (9), and (11) prove that its product law attains the same value. There are \(3q\) direction masses and one scalar \(t\), with the stated equality and matrix-inequality counts.

As the smallest-instance check, when \(q=1\) the original score itself has the single principal block (7), so the lower certificate (8) is equality for every law. The local program therefore exactly enumerates the three-direction simplex already at \(n=4\). The use of (6) identifies the theorem's boundary: it requires a block-diagonal square root and does not apply to a kernel coupling distinct blocks. \(\square\)
