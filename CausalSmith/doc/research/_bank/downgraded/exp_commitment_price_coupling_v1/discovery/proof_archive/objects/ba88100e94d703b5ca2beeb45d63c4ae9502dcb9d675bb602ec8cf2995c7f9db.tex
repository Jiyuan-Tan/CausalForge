\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a class \(j\).  Distinct columns of \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) have disjoint block supports.  Orthonormality of \(h_1,h_2,h_3\) and positivity of the loadings give
\[
\|u_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}}\|_2^2
=\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Hence the \(q\) columns are nonzero and mutually orthogonal, so \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) has rank \(q\).  The same polar-decomposition calculation used in \(\mathrm{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\) gives
\[
\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{blk}}
=\{U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\beta:\|\beta\|_2\le1\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, if \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}=WH\), where \(W^\top W=I_q\) and \(H\succ0\), then \(K_j^{\mathrm{blk}}=WH^2W^\top\) and
\[
(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\beta)^\top(K_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^\dagger
(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\beta)=\|\beta\|_2^2.
\]
Rayleigh--Ritz and \(\mathrm{def:block-specific-commitment-value}\) now yield
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)
=\frac1{n^2}\sup_{\|\beta\|_2\le1}
\beta^\top C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\beta
=\frac1{n^2}\lambda_{\max}\{C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\}.
\tag{3}
\]

The potential-outcome schedule is fixed by \(\mathrm{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}\), assignment is the only randomness by \(\mathrm{ass:assignment-only-randomness}\), and consistency is imposed by \(\mathrm{ass:no-interference}\).  Therefore the estimator algebra in \(\mathrm{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_\pi\widehat\tau=\tau_N,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)=n^{-2}\mu^\top Q_\pi\mu.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\), equations (2)--(4) imply the asserted variance bound.  This separates the causal estimand \(\tau_N\) from the design criterion \(R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\).

For any realized assignment, there are unique local directions \(k_\ell\in\{1,2,3\}\) and signs \(\varepsilon_\ell\in\{-1,1\}\) such that \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon_\ell v_{k_\ell}\).  Since \(v_k=2h_k\), orthonormality gives the pointwise identity
\[
(u_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}})^\top Z
=2\varepsilon_\ell\sqrt{d_{j\ell k_\ell}}.
\tag{5}
\]
Squaring (5), taking expectation, and using \(\mathrm{def:block-direction-marginals}\) proves
\[
[C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}(\pi).
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\succeq0\), its coordinate Rayleigh quotients and (3) give
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)
\ge\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell
\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}(\pi).
\tag{7}
\]

Given the same array \(p_{\ell k}(\pi)\), construct \(\pi_p\) as in \(\mathrm{def:block-product-design}\).  It is supported on \(\mathcal Z_n\), and the probability of a block-direction and sign configuration is
\[
2^{-q}\prod_{\ell=1}^q p_{\ell k_\ell},
\tag{8}
\]
which is unchanged when every sign is reversed.  Hence \(\pi_p(z)=\pi_p(-z)\) and \(\pi_p\in\Pi_n\).  Under \(\pi_p\), distinct block scores in (5) are independent and have mean zero because their signs are symmetric.  Thus all off-diagonal score covariances vanish and
\[
C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)
=4\operatorname{diag}_{\ell}
\left(\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}\right).
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (3), (7), and (9) imply
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}
\le R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)
\tag{10}
\]
simultaneously for every class.  This proves Pareto productization without assuming independence under the original design.

Under \(\pi_0\), each block direction has probability \(1/3\), while block directions and signs are independent.  Equation (9) yields
\[
B_j^{\mathrm{blk}}
=R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_0)
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\frac13\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}
=\frac{4b_j}{n^2}.
\tag{11}
\]
For every probability vector,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}
\ge\min_k d_{j\ell k}.
\]
Taking the maximum over blocks in (7) gives \(R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\ge4\bar r_j/n^2\) for every design.  Conversely, in each block choose any \(k_{j\ell}^\star\in\arg\min_kd_{j\ell k}\) with probability one and use independent symmetric signs.  Equation (9) gives
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell d_{j\ell k_{j\ell}^\star}
=\frac{4\bar r_j}{n^2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Boundary direction masses are allowed by \(\mathrm{def:design-space}\), so this oracle design is feasible.  Equations (11)--(12) prove the three baseline, oracle, and gain formulas.  They also show that \(b_j>\bar r_j\) is exactly the positivity boundary required to divide by the gain.

Assume that boundary condition for every active class.  By (10), replacing any design by its productization cannot decrease any retained-gain fraction.  Consequently
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{blk}}^\star
=\max_{p\in\prod_{\ell=1}^q\Delta_3}
\min_{1\le j\le J_{\mathrm{blk}}}
\frac{b_j-max_\ell\sum_kd_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}}
{b_j-\bar r_j}.
\tag{13}
\]
For a scalar \(t\), the condition that the \(j\)-th fraction in (13) is at least \(t\) is equivalent, because \(b_j-\bar r_j>0\), to
\[
\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}
\le b_j-(b_j-\bar r_j)t
\quad\text{for every }\ell.
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (13)--(14) gives exactly the displayed linear program.  Its direction array lies in the compact product of \(q\) three-simplices, and the largest feasible \(t\) is a continuous finite function of that array, so an optimizer exists.  Equation (9) proves that the corresponding \(\pi_p\) attains its objective.  If an original law \(\pi\) is optimal, its productization has value at least that of \(\pi\), but no value can exceed \(\lambda_{\mathrm{blk}}^\star\); hence its marginal array also solves the program.  Counting the \(3q\) masses and \(t\) gives \(3q+1\) variables, and (14) gives \(J_{\mathrm{blk}}q\) risk inequalities in addition to the stated simplex constraints.  No enumeration of the \(6^q\) global assignments is required.

For the smallest-instance check, let \(q=1\).  Then every \(C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) is scalar, direct enumeration of the six assignments gives \(4\sum_kd_{j1k}p_{1k}\), and the linear program has four variables and \(J_{\mathrm{blk}}\) risk inequalities.  The lower certificate and product witness therefore coincide exactly at the smallest design size.  The rank-one-per-block restriction is used in (5) and (9); a full-rank or noncommuting within-block kernel does not reduce to these three scalar loadings. \(\square\)
