The role of the published comparator is only to supply the variance-ratio normalization and the maximin operation recorded in \(\mathrm{def:ryan-relative-efficiency}\). The remaining argument is an exact calculation for this paper's finite matched-quadruple menu. By \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\), \(r_j^\star=4c_j/n^2\). For any feasible design, its trace certificates imply, with \(p_k=p_k(\pi)\),
\[
\operatorname{RE}_1(\pi)\le \frac1{1+F_1},
\qquad
\operatorname{RE}_2(\pi)\le \frac1{1+F_2}, \tag{1}
\]
where
\[
F_1=\kappa_1(ap_2+bp_3),
\qquad
F_2=\kappa_2(ap_1+bp_3). \tag{2}
\]
All denominators are strictly positive because \(c_j,s_j,a,b>0\). Thus maximizing the smaller upper bound in (1) reduces to minimizing \(\max(F_1,F_2)\) over the frequency simplex, provided a law attaining both trace bounds is exhibited; such a law will be constructed below.

Assume the theorem's local regime \(a>2b>0\) and \(\kappa_2>\kappa_1>0\). Define
\[
D=\kappa_1(a-b)+\kappa_2b,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{\kappa_2b}{D},
\qquad
1-\alpha=\frac{\kappa_1(a-b)}D. \tag{3}
\]
Here \(a-b>b>0\), so \(D>0\) and \(0<\alpha<1\). Substituting \(p_3=1-p_1-p_2\) into (2) and collecting coefficients gives the global affine certificate
\[
\alpha F_1+(1-\alpha)F_2
=\frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}
+\frac{a(a-2b)\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}p_1
\ge \frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}=:m. \tag{4}
\]
Because a maximum dominates every convex combination, \(\max(F_1,F_2)\ge m\).

Equality is feasible. Set
\[
p_1^\circ=0,
\qquad
p_2^\circ=\frac{b(\kappa_2-\kappa_1)}D,
\qquad
p_3^\circ=1-p_2^\circ. \tag{5}
\]
The numerator of \(p_2^\circ\) is positive, and
\[
D-b(\kappa_2-\kappa_1)=a\kappa_1>0, \tag{6}
\]
so (5) is in the simplex. Direct substitution yields \(F_1=F_2=m\). Choose directions independently across blocks with probabilities (5), and attach independent symmetric signs. This law lies in \(\Pi_n\). Its block scores are centered and independent, with the same second moment in every block, and therefore
\[
C_j=4c_j(1+F_j)I_q,
\qquad
R_j=\frac{4c_j}{n^2}(1+F_j). \tag{7}
\]
Thus both bounds in (1) are equalities. Equations (4)--(7) prove
\[
\eta_{\mathrm{REL}}
=\frac1{1+m}
=\left[1+\frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{\kappa_1(a-b)+\kappa_2b}\right]^{-1}. \tag{8}
\]

The same certificate characterizes every optimizer's average frequencies. If a design attains (8), both inequalities
\[
\min_j\operatorname{RE}_j(\pi)
\le \{1+\max(F_1,F_2)\}^{-1}
\le (1+m)^{-1} \tag{9}
\]
must be equalities. In (4), the coefficient of \(p_1\) is strictly positive, so equality forces \(p_1=0\). Since \(0<\alpha<1\), equality between \(\max(F_1,F_2)\) and the convex combination in (4) forces \(F_1=F_2=m\). Solving this equality on the face \(p_1=0\) gives exactly (5).

Finally, the \(a>2b\) branch of \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) shows that every gain-normalized optimizer has \(p_3=1\). In contrast, (5) has \(p_2^\circ>0\) under \(\kappa_2>\kappa_1\). Hence the optimizer sets are disjoint. Both witnesses are blinded block-balanced laws on the same finite support and are evaluated with the same pairs \((r_j^\star,R_j(\pi))\); only their normalization rules differ. This establishes the stated criterion-level separation without making any submodel or substantive nonrepresentability claim about the comparator's cluster-randomized outcome model.
