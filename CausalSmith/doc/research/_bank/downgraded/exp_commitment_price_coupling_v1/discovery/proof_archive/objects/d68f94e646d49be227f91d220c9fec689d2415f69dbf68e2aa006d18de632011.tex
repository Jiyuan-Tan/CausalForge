\textbf{Proof.}
In the homogeneous specialization, direction \(v_1\) is the unique minimum-loading direction for class \(1\), and direction \(v_2\) is the unique minimum-loading direction for class \(2\).  Applying \(\mathrm{prop:block\text{-}specific\text{-}single\text{-}class\text{-}reduction}\) therefore shows that the corresponding independent-sign product laws attain
\[
R_1=\frac{4c_1}{n^2}=r_1^\star,
\qquad
R_2=\frac{4c_2}{n^2}=r_2^\star.
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(a,b,s_j>0\), the gains \(\Delta_j\) in \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple\text{-}menu\text{-}frontier}\) are positive.  At the oracle law the single-class gain fraction is
\[
\frac{B_j-r_j^\star}{B_j-r_j^\star}=1.
\tag{2}
\]
No design can exceed one because \(r_j^\star\) is the minimum class risk, so each one-class commitment value is exactly one.

For two classes, the same frontier gives either
\[
\lambda^\star=\frac{a-2b}{a+b}<1
\quad\text{when }a\ge2b,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\lambda^\star=\frac{2b-a}{2(a+b)}<1
\quad\text{when }a\le2b.
\tag{3}
\]
Both strict inequalities use \(a,b>0\), including the elbow.  Hence the loss below one is caused by simultaneous commitment across the two incompatible classes.  \(\square\)
