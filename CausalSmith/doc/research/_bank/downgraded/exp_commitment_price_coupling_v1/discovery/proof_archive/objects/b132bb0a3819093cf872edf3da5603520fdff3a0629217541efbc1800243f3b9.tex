Fix \(\pi\in\Pi_n\).  Every \(z\in\mathcal Z_n\) is balanced within every quadruple, hence \(z^\top\mathbf 1_n=0\).  By \(\texttt{def:estimator}\) and consistency in \(\texttt{ass:no-interference}\), for each unit
\[
2Z_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}
=Z_i\{(1+Z_i)Y_i(1)+(1-Z_i)Y_i(0)\}
=Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)+Z_i\{Y_i(1)+Y_i(0)\}.
\]
Summing, dividing by \(n\), and using \(Z^\top\mathbf 1_n=0\) gives
\[
\widehat\tau-\tau_N
=n^{-1}Z^\top\{Y(1)+Y(0)\}
=n^{-1}Z^\top P_n\{Y(1)+Y(0)\}
=n^{-1}Z^\top\mu. \tag{1}
\]
The potential-outcome schedule is fixed by \(\texttt{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}\), and assignment is the only randomness by \(\texttt{ass:assignment-only-randomness}\).  Moreover, the blinding identity \(\pi(z)=\pi(-z)\) from \(\texttt{def:design-space}\) implies
\[
\mathbb E_\pi Z=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_n}z\pi(z)=0. \tag{2}
\]
Thus (1)--(2) yield \(\mathbb E_\pi\widehat\tau=\tau_N\).  Since \(Q_\pi=\mathbb E_\pi[ZZ^\top]\), they also yield
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)
=n^{-2}\mathbb E_\pi[(Z^\top\mu)^2]
=n^{-2}\mu^\top Q_\pi\mu
=V_\pi(\mu). \tag{3}
\]
No assignment-probability inverse is used; the boundary designs in \(\Pi_n\) are therefore allowed.  Blinding nevertheless gives treatment probability \(1/2\) to every unit.

It remains to compute the ellipsoidal supremum.  Fix \(j\).  The declared rank condition \(\operatorname{rank}(K_j)=q\), together with \(K_j=U_jU_j^\top\) and the fact that \(U_j\) has \(q\) columns, implies that \(U_j\) has full column rank.  Its polar decomposition is
\[
U_j=W_jH_j,\qquad W_j^\top W_j=I_q,qquad H_j=(U_j^\top U_j)^{1/2}\succ0. \tag{4}
\]
Consequently,
\[
K_j=W_jH_j^2W_j^\top,qquad
K_j^\dagger=W_jH_j^{-2}W_j^\top. \tag{5}
\]
Every \(x\in\operatorname{range}(K_j)=\operatorname{range}(U_j)\) has a unique representation \(x=U_j\beta\).  Equations (4)--(5) show
\[
x^\top K_j^\dagger x
=\beta^\top H_jW_j^\top W_jH_j^{-2}W_j^\top W_jH_j\beta
=\|\beta\|_2^2. \tag{6}
\]
Thus \(\texttt{def:response-classes}\) gives
\[
\mathcal M_j=\{U_j\beta:\|\beta\|_2\le1\}. \tag{7}
\]
The Rayleigh--Ritz identity, applied to the positive-semidefinite matrix \(C_j(\pi)=U_j^\top Q_\pi U_j\), now gives
\[
R_j(\pi)
=n^{-2}\sup_{\|\beta\|_2\le1}\beta^\top C_j(\pi)\beta
=n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{C_j(\pi)\}. \tag{8}
\]
Also, (4) gives
\[
K_j^{1/2}Q_\pi K_j^{1/2}
=W_j\{H_jW_j^\top Q_\pi W_jH_j\}W_j^\top,
\qquad
C_j(\pi)=H_jW_j^\top Q_\pi W_jH_j. \tag{9}
\]
The matrix on the left of (9) is the orthogonal embedding by \(W_j\) of the matrix on the right, with zeros on the orthogonal complement.  Their largest eigenvalues therefore agree.  This proves both spectral identities.  Finally, if \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j\), then (3) and the definition of the supremum in \(R_j\) give \(\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)=V_\pi(\mu)\le R_j(\pi)\), as claimed.
