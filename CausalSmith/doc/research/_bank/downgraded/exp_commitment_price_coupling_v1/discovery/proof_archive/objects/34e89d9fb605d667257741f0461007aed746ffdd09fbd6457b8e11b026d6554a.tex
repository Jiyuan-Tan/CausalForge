Consider class \(1\).  Under the product law that assigns \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon_\ell v_1\) with independent Rademacher signs, orthonormality of the \(h_k\)'s gives
\[
u_{1\ell}^\top Z=2\varepsilon_\ell\sqrt{c_1}.
\]
The scores are independent and centered across blocks, so \(C_1=4c_1I_q\).  The spectral-risk identity then gives \(R_1=4c_1/n^2\), which equals \(r_1^\star\) by \(\texttt{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\).  Replacing \(v_1,c_1\) by \(v_2,c_2\) gives \(C_2=4c_2I_q\) and \(R_2=4c_2/n^2=r_2^\star\) for class \(2\).  Both laws are in \(\Pi_n\), including at every finite \(q\ge1\), because independent symmetric signs imply \(\pi(z)=\pi(-z)\).

For either single class, \(R_j(\pi)\ge r_j^\star\) for every feasible design by the definition of the oracle minimum.  Since \(\Delta_j>0\), this implies \(g_j(\pi)\le1\), while the displayed oracle law attains \(g_j=1\).  Thus each one-class commitment value is exactly \(1\).  For both classes together, \(\texttt{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) gives
\[
\lambda^\star=\frac{a-2b}{a+b}.
\]
The conditions \(a>2b>0\) imply \(0<\lambda^\star<1\), proving that the loss relative to the one-class boundary is caused by simultaneous response-structure uncertainty.
