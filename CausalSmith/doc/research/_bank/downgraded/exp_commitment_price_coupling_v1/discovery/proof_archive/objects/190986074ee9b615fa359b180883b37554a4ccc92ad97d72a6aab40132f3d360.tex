\textbf{Proof.}
Apply \(\mathrm{thm:block-specific-finite-class-frontier}\) with \(J_{\mathrm{blk}}=2\) and the homogeneous loadings from \(\mathrm{def:block-geometry}\).  The specialized objects \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}},K_j^{\mathrm{blk}},\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{blk}},C_j^{\mathrm{blk}},R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) coincide, respectively, with \(U_j,K_j,\mathcal M_j,C_j,R_j\).  Positivity of \(a,b,c_j,s_j\) gives
\[
\bar r_j=c_j,
\qquad
b_j=c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3.
\tag{1}
\]
The general baseline, oracle, and gain formulas therefore become
\[
r_j^\star=\frac{4c_j}{n^2},
\qquad
B_j=\frac4{n^2}\left(c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3\right),
\qquad
\Delta_j=\frac{4s_j(a+b)}{3n^2}>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every gain normalization below is well defined.  No lower overlap bound is needed: zero local direction probabilities are allowed by \(\mathrm{def:design-space}\).

For an arbitrary blinded law, the diagonal identity in \(\mathrm{thm:block-specific-finite-class-frontier}\) gives
\[
[C_1(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\{c_1+s_1(ap_{\ell2}+bp_{\ell3})\},
\qquad
[C_2(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\{c_2+s_2(ap_{\ell1}+bp_{\ell3})\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Taking traces, dividing by \(q\), and using \(p_k=q^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^q p_{\ell k}\) proves
\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}C_1(\pi)}q
=4\{c_1+s_1(ap_2+bp_3)\},
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}C_2(\pi)}q
=4\{c_2+s_2(ap_1+bp_3)\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Because \(C_j(\pi)\succeq0\), its largest eigenvalue is at least the average of its \(q\) eigenvalues:
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{C_j(\pi)\}\ge q^{-1}\operatorname{tr}C_j(\pi).
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) are the asserted spectral certificates.

It remains to solve the specialized linear program.  For each block put
\[
L_{1\ell}=ap_{\ell2}+bp_{\ell3},
\qquad
L_{2\ell}=ap_{\ell1}+bp_{\ell3}.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(p_{\ell1}+p_{\ell2}+p_{\ell3}=1\),
\[
\max(L_{1\ell},L_{2\ell})
\ge \frac{L_{1\ell}+L_{2\ell}}2
=\frac a2(1-p_{\ell3})+bp_{\ell3}
\ge m:=\min\left\{\frac a2,b\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
The constraints in the general finite-class program and (1) say that a common retained fraction \(t\) is feasible exactly when
\[
L_{1\ell}\le\frac{a+b}{3}(1-t),
\qquad
L_{2\ell}\le\frac{a+b}{3}(1-t)
\quad\text{for every }\ell.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (7)--(8) imply
\[
t\le1-\frac{3m}{a+b}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{a-2b}{a+b},&a\ge2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2b-a}{2(a+b)},&a\le2b.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]

This bound is attained block by block.  If \(a>2b\), take \((p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})=(0,0,1)\); then \(L_{1\ell}=L_{2\ell}=b=m\).  If \(a<2b\), take \((p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})=(1/2,1/2,0)\); then both loads equal \(a/2=m\).  If \(a=2b\), take
\[
p_{\ell1}=p_{\ell2}=t_\ell,
\qquad
p_{\ell3}=1-2t_\ell,
\qquad
0\le t_\ell\le\frac12;
\tag{10}
\]
again both loads equal \(b=a/2=m\).  The product construction in \(\mathrm{def:block-product-design}\) makes every score matrix diagonal; homogeneity across blocks makes it scalar.  Hence the lower certificates are met with equality, proving (9) as the exact value.  The first witness is \(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}}\).

We next characterize every optimal average-frequency vector.  The local marginals of any commitment optimizer solve the specialized program by \(\mathrm{thm:block-specific-finite-class-frontier}\), so equality must hold in (7) in every block.  When \(a>2b\),
\[
\frac a2(1-p_{\ell3})+bp_{\ell3}
=b+\left(\frac a2-b\right)(1-p_{\ell3}),
\]
and equality forces \(p_{\ell3}=1\).  When \(a<2b\), equality forces \(p_{\ell3}=0\), and equality of the maximum and average in (7) then forces \(p_{\ell1}=p_{\ell2}=1/2\).  At \(a=2b\), direct substitution gives
\[
\max(L_{1\ell},L_{2\ell})
=b\{1+|p_{\ell1}-p_{\ell2}|\},
\]
so equality is equivalent to (10).  Averaging over blocks yields precisely the three families in the statement, with \(t=q^{-1}\sum_\ell t_\ell\).  Conversely, constant block marginals having any displayed average yield an attaining product law.

For an attaining product law, (2), (3), and (6) give
\[
B_j-R_j
=\frac{4s_j}{n^2}\left\{\frac{a+b}{3}-m\right\}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{4s_j(a-2b)}{3n^2},&a>2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2s_j(2b-a)}{3n^2},&a<2b,\\[6pt]
0,&a=2b.
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
Thus both class risks improve strictly over \(\pi_0\) exactly when \(a\ne2b\).  At \(a=2b\), the baseline marginals \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) belong to the optimal family, so block complete randomization is maximin and \(\lambda^\star=0\).

For the smallest-instance stress test, take \(q=1\) and \(n=4\).  Each score matrix is scalar.  Enumerating the six assignments gives the two loads in (3), while (7) exhausts the three-point simplex.  Thus the frontier, boundary optimizers, and elbow are exact already in the smallest admissible design. \(\square\)
