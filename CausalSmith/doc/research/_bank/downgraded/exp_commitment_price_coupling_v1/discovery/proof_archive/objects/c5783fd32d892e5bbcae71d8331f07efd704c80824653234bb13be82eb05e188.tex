\textbf{Proof.}
Apply \(\mathrm{thm:block-specific-finite-class-frontier}\) with \(J_{\mathrm{blk}}=2\) and the homogeneous loadings in \(\mathrm{def:block-geometry}\). The specialized objects coincide with \(U_j,K_j,\mathcal M_j,C_j,R_j\). Positivity of \(a,b,c_j,s_j\) gives
\[
\bar r_j=c_j,
\qquad
b_j=c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3.
\tag{1}
\]
The rank-one corollary therefore yields
\[
r_j^\star=\frac{4c_j}{n^2},
\qquad
B_j=\frac4{n^2}\left(c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3\right),
\qquad
\Delta_j=\frac{4s_j(a+b)}{3n^2}>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus both gain fractions are well defined for all \(a,b>0\).

For an arbitrary blinded law, the diagonal identity from that corollary gives
\[
[C_1(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\{c_1+s_1(ap_{\ell2}+bp_{\ell3})\},
\qquad
[C_2(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\{c_2+s_2(ap_{\ell1}+bp_{\ell3})\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Taking traces, dividing by \(q\), and using \(p_k=q^{-1}\sum_\ell p_{\ell k}\) gives the two displayed trace identities.  Since each score matrix is positive semidefinite, its largest eigenvalue is at least its trace divided by \(q\), proving the spectral certificates.

For each block put
\[
L_{1\ell}=ap_{\ell2}+bp_{\ell3},
\qquad
L_{2\ell}=ap_{\ell1}+bp_{\ell3}.
\tag{4}
\]
Since \(p_{\ell1}+p_{\ell2}+p_{\ell3}=1\),
\[
\max(L_{1\ell},L_{2\ell})
\ge\frac{L_{1\ell}+L_{2\ell}}2
=\frac a2(1-p_{\ell3})+bp_{\ell3}
\ge m:=\min\left\{\frac a2,b\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The specialized LP says that a common gain \(t\) is feasible exactly when
\[
L_{1\ell}\le\frac{a+b}{3}(1-t),
\qquad
L_{2\ell}\le\frac{a+b}{3}(1-t)
\quad\text{for every }\ell.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) imply
\[
t\le1-\frac{3m}{a+b}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{a-2b}{a+b},&a\ge2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2b-a}{2(a+b)},&a\le2b.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]

The bound is attained blockwise. If \(a>2b\), take \((p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})=(0,0,1)\); if \(a<2b\), take \((1/2,1/2,0)\); and if \(a=2b\), take
\[
p_{\ell1}=p_{\ell2}=t_\ell,
\qquad
p_{\ell3}=1-2t_\ell,
\qquad
0\le t_\ell\le\frac12.
\tag{8}
\]
In every case \(L_{1\ell}=L_{2\ell}=m\). The product construction makes each score matrix diagonal, and the homogeneous block choice makes it scalar, so the lower certificate is met with equality. The first witness is \(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}}\).

To characterize all optimal average frequencies, equality must hold in (5) in every block. If \(a>2b\), strict positivity of \(a/2-b\) forces \(p_{\ell3}=1\). If \(a<2b\), equality forces \(p_{\ell3}=0\), and equality of the maximum with the average then forces \(p_{\ell1}=p_{\ell2}=1/2\). At \(a=2b\), direct substitution gives
\[
\max(L_{1\ell},L_{2\ell})
=b\{1+|p_{\ell1}-p_{\ell2}|\},
\]
so equality is equivalent to (8). Averaging yields exactly the frequency vectors stated in the theorem, and constant block marginals show conversely that every listed average is attainable.

For an attaining product law, (2)--(4) yield
\[
B_j-R_j
=\frac{4s_j}{n^2}\left\{\frac{a+b}{3}-m\right\}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{4s_j(a-2b)}{3n^2},&a>2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2s_j(2b-a)}{3n^2},&a<2b,\\[6pt]
0,&a=2b.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]
Thus both classes improve strictly over block complete randomization exactly away from the elbow. At \(a=2b\), the baseline marginals \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) lie in (8), so block complete randomization is maximin and \(\lambda^\star=0\).

When \(q=1\), direct enumeration of the six assignments produces (3), and (5) exhausts the three-direction simplex. This verifies the frontier and all boundary cases at the smallest admissible population. \(\square\)
