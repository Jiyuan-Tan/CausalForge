Fix \(a,b,c_j,s_j>0\) and \(q\ge 1\). For class \(1\), consider the blinded product law with \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon_\ell v_1\), where the \(\varepsilon_\ell\)'s are independent symmetric signs. Orthonormality of the local contrasts gives
\[
u_{1\ell}^\top Z=2\varepsilon_\ell\sqrt{c_1}.
\]
The scores are centered and independent across blocks, so \(C_1=4c_1I_q\). By \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) and \(\mathrm{def:design-risk}\),
\[
R_1=\frac{4c_1}{n^2}=r_1^\star.
\]
The corresponding law on \(v_2\) gives \(C_2=4c_2I_q\) and
\[
R_2=\frac{4c_2}{n^2}=r_2^\star.
\]
Both laws lie in \(\Pi_n\): their assignments are block balanced, and independent symmetric signs imply \(\pi(z)=\pi(-z)\). No local-direction positivity is required because the estimator and class risk use no inverse assignment probabilities.

For either single class, the definition of \(r_j^\star\) gives \(R_j(\pi)\ge r_j^\star\) for every feasible design. Since \(\Delta_j>0\) by \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\), one has \(g_j(\pi)\le 1\), while the displayed oracle law attains \(g_j=1\). Thus either one-class commitment problem has value exactly \(1\).

For the two-class menu, the same theorem gives
\[
\lambda^\star=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{a-2b}{a+b},&a\ge 2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2b-a}{2(a+b)},&a\le 2b.
\end{cases}
\]
If \(a\ge 2b\), then \(0\le a-2b<a+b\) because \(b>0\). If \(a\le 2b\), then \(0\le 2b-a<2(a+b)\) because \(a>0\). Hence \(0\le\lambda^\star<1\) in every admissible regime, including \(\lambda^\star=0\) at \(a=2b\). This proves that restricting the solved two-class menu to either single class recovers the oracle value \(1\), whereas simultaneous commitment has a strictly smaller value.
