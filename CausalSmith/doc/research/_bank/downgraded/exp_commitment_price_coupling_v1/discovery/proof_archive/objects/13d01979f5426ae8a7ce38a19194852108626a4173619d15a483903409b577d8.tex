\textit{Design representation.}
The support \(\mathcal Z_n\) is finite and invariant under global sign reversal. For any blinded \(\pi\in\Pi_n\), define
\[
\alpha_s=\pi(s)+\pi(-s),\qquad s\in\mathscr S.
\]
Then \(\alpha_s\ge0\), \(\sum_s\alpha_s=1\), and, since \((-s)(-s)^\top=ss^\top\),
\[
Q_\pi=\mathbb E_\pi[ZZ^\top]
=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_sss^\top.
\tag{1}
\]
Conversely, every \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\) defines the blinded law in \((\mathrm D)\), and that law has second moment \((1)\). This is a bijection at the level of assignment second moments relevant to all the risks. It allows arbitrary dependence across blocks, exactly as does \(\Pi_n\). No exposure-probability inverse is used, so boundary masses \(\alpha_s=0\) are admissible and no overlap condition is needed.

\textit{Risk formula and oracle programs.}
Fix a class \(j\). By the spectral decomposition of \(G_j\), every \(x\in\operatorname{range}(G_j)\) can be written as \(x=G_j^{1/2}u\) with \(u\in\operatorname{range}(G_j)\), and under this representation
\[
x^\top G_j^\dagger x=\|u\|_2^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Combining \((1)\), \((2)\), and the variational characterization of the largest eigenvalue gives
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}}
\frac1{n^2}x^\top Q_\pi x
=\frac1{n^2}
\sup_{\substack{u\in\operatorname{range}(G_j)\\\|u\|_2\le1}}
u^\top A_j(\alpha)u
=\frac1{n^2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
This proves the generalized spectral-risk formula directly; it does not assume commutativity. The simplex is compact and the largest eigenvalue is continuous, so every class oracle \(\rho_j\) is attained. Equivalently, it is computed by
\[
\rho_j
=\min_{\alpha\in\Delta_m,\ u\in\mathbb R}
\{u:A_j(\alpha)\preceq n^2uI_n\}.
\tag{4}
\]
The variance identity in \(\mathrm{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\) is algebraic and gives \(\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)=n^{-2}\mu^\top Q_\pi\mu\) for the fixed causal schedule. If \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}\), equation \((3)\) therefore gives the variance bound in the statement. This separates the causal target \(\tau_N\) from the matrix-valued design criterion.

Thus \(\beta_j\), \(\rho_j\), and \(\delta_j\) are all finite computable functions of the known matrices and the known baseline design. The assumed inequalities \(\delta_j>0\) make every gain fraction well defined. They are a necessary boundary condition rather than an overlap condition.

\textit{Exact primal program.}
For a real number \(t\), the class-\(j\) retained-gain constraint is, by \((3)\),
\[
\frac{\beta_j-n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{\delta_j}\ge t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A_j(\alpha)\preceq n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n.
\tag{5}
\]
Maximizing \(t\) over the common mass vector \(\alpha\) proves \((\mathrm P)\). The baseline mass vector \(\alpha^0\) is feasible at \(t=0\), so the value is nonnegative. Since each class risk is bounded below by its oracle risk, no class gain exceeds one, so the value is at most one. This also verifies the asserted range of \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\).

\textit{Trace relaxation and its exactness condition.}
By \(\mathrm{lem:finite-trace-isotropy}\), for every \(j\) and \(\alpha\),
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}
\ge\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_j}.
\tag{6}
\]
Replacing the left side of each constraint in \((5)\) by the lower bound in \((6)\) gives \((\mathrm T)\). Its constraints are affine in \((\alpha,t)\), so it is a finite linear program, and relaxation gives
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\le\overline\lambda.
\tag{7}
\]

If equality holds in \((7)\), compactness supplies a primal optimizer \(\alpha^\star\) at \(t=\overline\lambda\); its semidefinite constraints are exactly \((\mathrm E)\). Conversely, if an \(\alpha\) satisfies \((\mathrm E)\), it is feasible for \((\mathrm P)\) at \(t=\overline\lambda\), so \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\ge\overline\lambda\), and \((7)\) gives equality. This proves the first equivalence.

Condition \((\mathrm E)\) implies all the trace constraints at \(t=\overline\lambda\), so any such \(\alpha\) lies on the optimal face of \((\mathrm T)\). If class \(j\) is trace-active there, then
\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_j}
=n^2(\beta_j-\delta_j\overline\lambda).
\tag{8}
\]
Equations \((\mathrm E)\) and \((8)\) force equality in \((6)\), and \(\mathrm{lem:finite-trace-isotropy}\) gives \((\mathrm I)\). For a trace-slack class, exactness requires only the remaining threshold in \((\mathrm E)\). Conversely, isotropy for the active classes makes their instances of \((\mathrm E)\) equalities, and imposing \((\mathrm E)\) on the slack classes completes the full condition. This proves the necessary-and-sufficient active-isotropy characterization.

\textit{Sharp gap, construction, and dual certificate.}
Both feasible sets are compact after restricting \(t\) to any interval containing their finite optima, so the values in \((\mathrm G)\) are attained. If \((\mathrm E)\) fails, the proved equivalence makes the inequality in \((7)\) strict; hence \(\Gamma_n>0\). Formula \((\mathrm G)\) is not an unspecified infimum or supremum: it is the difference of the displayed LP and SDP, each with \(m=6^q/2\) probability variables and explicit constraints. The optimizer \(\alpha^\star\) is converted by \((\mathrm D)\) into a member of \(\Pi_n\), and \((1)\)--\((5)\) show that its criterion is exactly \(t^\star=\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\). Thus the loss relative to the trace relaxation is exactly \(\Gamma_n\), with no rounding remainder.

For completeness, define \(F_j(\alpha,t)=n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n-A_j(\alpha)\). If matrices \(X_j\succeq0\) satisfy
\[
n^2\sum_j\delta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)=1,
\]
then every primal-feasible \((\alpha,t)\) obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
t
&\le n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)
-\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_s
\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})\\
&\le n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)
-\min_{s\in\mathscr S}\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Introducing \(\gamma\le\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})\) for every \(s\) yields the dual in \((\mathrm C)\). Strong duality holds: choose all \(\alpha_s>0\) and then take \(t\) sufficiently negative, which makes every \(F_j(\alpha,t)\) positive definite because \(\delta_j>0\). This is Slater's condition for the finite-dimensional conic program. Hence the minimum in \((\mathrm C)\) equals the primal maximum. At an optimal pair, the complementary conditions
\[
\operatorname{tr}\{X_j^\star F_j(\alpha^\star,t^\star)\}=0,
\qquad
\alpha_s^\star>0\Longrightarrow
\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j^\star A_{js})=\gamma^\star
\tag{10}
\]
give a directly checkable optimality certificate.

The construction therefore implements the convex assignment law exactly. The discrepancy and noncommutative-concentration steps suggested by \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{mat}}\) would be rounding devices; no such rounding is necessary in the frozen feasible class, which already contains every blinded mixture. Their constants can become substantive only after adding a restriction such as independent block assignments, deterministic assignments, or prescribed support size. Under the current \(\Pi_n\), the exact implementation has constant one.

\textit{Boundary and smallest-instance checks.}
If \(\delta_j=0\), the gain denominator for class \(j\) is zero, so the generalized normalized criterion is not a real-valued map; excluding that boundary or supplying a separate convention is logically necessary. Nonzero rank makes \(r_j^{-1}\) legitimate. Moore--Penrose inverses remain well defined for singular \(G_j\), and neither full rank nor commutativity is required.

For \(q=1\), \(n=4\) and
\[
\mathscr S=\{v_1,v_2,v_3\},
\qquad
Q(\alpha)=\sum_{k=1}^3\alpha_kv_kv_k^\top.
\]
Direct enumeration of all six assignments \(\{\pm v_1,\pm v_2,\pm v_3\}\) gives exactly this triangle of second moments, so \((\mathrm P)\), \((\mathrm T)\), and \((\mathrm D)\) are exact even at the smallest population size. Finally, in the frozen two-class menu, the product witnesses of \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) make each active score matrix scalar. This is precisely condition \((\mathrm I)\), so the general criterion specializes to the previously proved trace-certificate equality rather than relying on an asymptotic approximation.
