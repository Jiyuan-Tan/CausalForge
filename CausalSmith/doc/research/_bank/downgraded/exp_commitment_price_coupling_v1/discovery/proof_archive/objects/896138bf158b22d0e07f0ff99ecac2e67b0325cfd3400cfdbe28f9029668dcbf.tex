\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(\varepsilon\) be a symmetric Rademacher sign and set
\[
Z_{[1]}=\varepsilon v_1,
\qquad
Z_{[2]}=\varepsilon v_1.
\tag{1}
\]
This law is supported on \(\mathcal Z_8\), and replacing \(\varepsilon\) by \(-\varepsilon\) proves global blinding, so \(\pi\in\Pi_8\).  Since \(h_1^\top v_1=2\),
\[
w^\top Z
=h_1^\top Z_{[1]}-h_1^\top Z_{[2]}
=2\varepsilon-2\varepsilon=0.
\tag{2}
\]
For the rank-one matrix \(G=ww^\top\), its only possibly nonzero score eigenvalue is \(w^\top Q_\pi w=\mathbb E_\pi[(w^\top Z)^2]\), which is zero by (2).

The two local direction marginals put unit mass on direction \(1\).  Their productization uses independent symmetric signs \(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\), so
\[
w^\top Z=2(\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2),
\qquad
\mathbb E[(w^\top Z)^2]
=4\mathbb E[(\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2)^2]=8.
\tag{3}
\]
Again this is the unique nonzero eigenvalue of \(G^{1/2}Q_{\pi_p}G^{1/2}\).  Division by \(n^2\) proves the claim.  The vector \(w\) is orthogonal to \(\mathbf 1_8\), so \(G\) is centered as well as positive semidefinite, but its off-diagonal block is nonzero. \(\square\)
