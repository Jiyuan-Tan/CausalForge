Fix \(q\ge1\), put \(n=4q\), and let \(\mathscr S\) contain one representative from every global sign-pair \(\{z,-z\}\) in \(\mathcal Z_n\). Thus \(m:=|\mathscr S|=6^q/2\). For \(1\le j\le J_{\mathrm{gen}}\), write \(G_j=K_j^{\mathrm{gen}}\), assume that \(G_j\in\mathbb S_+^n\) is nonzero, block-supported, and centered, and put \(r_j=\operatorname{rank}(G_j)\). The matrices may be noncommuting. Interpret replacement of the two frozen classes by this menu as the ellipsoids
\[
\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}
=\{x\in\operatorname{range}(G_j):x^\top G_j^\dagger x\le1\}.
\]
For \(s\in\mathscr S\), \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\), and the mass vector \(\alpha^0\) induced by \(\pi_0\), define
\[
A_{js}=G_j^{1/2}ss^\top G_j^{1/2},
\qquad
A_j(\alpha)=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_sA_{js},
\]
\[
\beta_j=\frac1{n^2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha^0)\},
\qquad
\rho_j=\min_{\alpha\in\Delta_m}\frac1{n^2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\},
\qquad
\delta_j=\beta_j-\rho_j.
\]
The causal estimand remains the fixed finite-population effect \(\tau_N\), whereas \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\) is a design-selection criterion and not a causal effect. Under the fixed-potential-outcome, no-interference, and assignment-only-randomness assumptions, if \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}\), then the balanced difference-in-means estimator satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)
\le \frac1{n^2}\lambda_{\max}
\{G_j^{1/2}Q_\pi G_j^{1/2}\}.
\]
Suppose \(\delta_j>0\) for every \(j\); this is precisely the positivity boundary required for the gain normalization. Then the generalized commitment value is the following finite semidefinite program:
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star
=\max_{\alpha\in\Delta_m,\ t\in\mathbb R}
\left\{t:
A_j(\alpha)\preceq n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n
\ \text{for all }1\le j\le J_{\mathrm{gen}}
\right\}.}
\tag{P}
\]
The normalized trace relaxation is the finite linear program
\[
\overline\lambda
=\max_{\alpha\in\Delta_m,\ t\in\mathbb R}
\left\{t:
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_jn^2}
\le\beta_j-\delta_jt
\ \text{for all }j
\right\}.
\tag{T}
\]
It always obeys \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\le\overline\lambda\). Equality holds if and only if there is an \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\) such that
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}
\le n^2(\beta_j-\delta_j\overline\lambda)
\quad\text{for every }j.
\tag{E}
\]
Equivalently, \(\alpha\) lies on the optimal face of \((\mathrm T)\), every trace-active class satisfies
\[
A_j(\alpha)
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_j}
P_{\operatorname{range}(G_j)},
\tag{I}
\]
and every trace-slack class satisfies the spectral threshold in \((\mathrm E)\). Thus simultaneous isotropy is necessary only for active classes, and these conditions are necessary and sufficient even when the \(G_j\)'s do not commute.

When equality fails, the sharp finite-\(n\) additive trace-relaxation gap is the computable quantity
\[
\Gamma_n=\overline\lambda-\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star>0,
\tag{G}
\]
where the two terms are the optima of \((\mathrm T)\) and \((\mathrm P)\). If \((\alpha^\star,t^\star)\) solves \((\mathrm P)\), then
\[
\pi_{\alpha^\star}(s)=\pi_{\alpha^\star}(-s)=\frac{\alpha_s^\star}{2},
\qquad s\in\mathscr S,
\tag{D}
\]
is a blinded block-balanced law attaining \(t^\star=\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\), and hence paying exactly the unavoidable gap \(\Gamma_n\).

Moreover, \((\mathrm P)\) has the matching dual certificate
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star
=\min_{\substack{X_j\succeq0,\ \gamma\in\mathbb R\\
n^2\sum_j\delta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)=1\\
\gamma\le\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})\ \forall s\in\mathscr S}}
\left\{
n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)-\gamma
\right\}.
\tag{C}
\]
Any primal--dual pair with equal objectives certifies optimality. Consequently the trace and exact programs, followed by \((\mathrm D)\), give an explicit \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{mat}}\) construction using \(m\) masses, \(J_{\mathrm{gen}}\) matrix inequalities of order \(n\), and no rounding. In the unrestricted randomized class \(\Pi_n\), its attainment constant is one; a noncommutative-discrepancy rounding constant becomes relevant only after imposing an additional product-law, deterministic-law, or support-size restriction not present in \(\Pi_n\). If some \(\delta_j=0\), the frozen gain fraction divides by zero, so \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\) is not defined without first specifying a convention for that degenerate class.
