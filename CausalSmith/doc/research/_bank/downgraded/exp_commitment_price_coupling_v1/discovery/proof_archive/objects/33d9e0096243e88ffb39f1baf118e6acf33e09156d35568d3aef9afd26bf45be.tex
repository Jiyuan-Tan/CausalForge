Kallus (2018, Theorem 1 and Sections 1 and 7) makes a response-function class the primitive of a minimax balancing problem. Kallus (2021, Sections 1 and 4--6) shows why the optimizer for one class is generally a mixed assignment law characterized through covariance. These papers inform the class risk and the one-class reduction; the new theorem instead computes the exact loss from one commitment across a specified two-class menu. The single-class proposition recovers the oracle boundary without claiming a sharper one-class result. Ryan, Esserman, and Li (2023, Section 3.2, equation (8), and Algorithm 1) supply the variance-ratio normalization and its maximin rule: equation (8) defines an oracle-to-candidate variance ratio, and Algorithm 1 maximizes its worst scenario value. This paper applies that criterion form to its own matched-quadruple risks \(r_j^\star\) and \(R_j(\pi)\) and proves optimizer separation from baseline-gain retention; it does not claim that the present menu is a literal submodel of their cluster-randomized treatment-effect-heterogeneity formulas. Tommasi, Rodr\'iguez-D\'iaz, and L\'opez-Fidalgo (2023, Theorem 3) already establish the generic maximin binding-weight equivalence, which is methodological background only. Harshaw, S\"avje, Spielman, and Zhang (2024, Sections 1, 8, and 9) provide covariance and discrepancy tools for one feature geometry, motivating the matrix-discrepancy handle for the higher-rank open question. Bai (2023, Theorem 2.1) supplies the permutation-invariant complete-randomization benchmark but does not address the structured two-geometry commitment price.
