By \(\texttt{def:block-assignments}\), each block of any \(s\in\mathscr S\) is one of \(\pm v_1,\pm v_2,\pm v_3\).  Every \(v_k\) has coordinate sum zero, so \(s\in\mathcal H\).  Each coordinate of \(s\) is \(1\) or \(-1\), whence
\[
\operatorname{tr}(ss^\top)=\lVert s\rVert_2^2=n.
\tag{1}
\]
The space of symmetric linear operators on the \(d\)-dimensional space \(\mathcal H\) has dimension
\[
D=\frac{d(d+1)}2=\frac{3q(3q+1)}2.
\tag{2}
\]
By \((1)\), all atoms \(ss^\top\) lie in the affine trace-\(n\) slice of that space, whose affine dimension is at most \(D-1\).

Let an optimizer of \((\mathrm P)\) have sign-pair masses \(\alpha_s^\star\), so
\[
Q^\star=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_s^\star ss^\top.
\tag{3}
\]
If more than \(D\) coefficients in \((3)\) are positive, the corresponding points in an affine space of dimension at most \(D-1\) are affinely dependent.  Hence there are real numbers \(\xi_s\), not all zero, supported on the positive coefficients, such that
\[
\sum_s\xi_s=0,
\qquad
\sum_s\xi_sss^\top=0.
\tag{4}
\]
Because the first equality in \((4)\) holds and \(\xi\ne0\), the vector \(\xi\) has both a positive and a negative entry.  Set
\[
\varepsilon=\min_{\{s:\xi_s>0\}}
\frac{\alpha_s^\star}{\xi_s}>0,
\qquad
\alpha_s'=\alpha_s^\star-\varepsilon\xi_s.
\tag{5}
\]
Then \(\alpha_s'\ge0\) for all \(s\), at least one formerly positive coefficient becomes zero, and \((4)\) gives
\[
\sum_s\alpha_s'=1,
\qquad
\sum_s\alpha_s'ss^\top=Q^\star.
\tag{6}
\]
Repeating this finite support-reduction step terminates with a mass vector \(\widetilde\alpha\) supported on at most \(D\) sign pairs.  The original representation already used at most \(m=6^q/2\) pairs, proving the bound \(\min\{m,D\}\).

Define the blinded law
\[
\widetilde\pi(s)=\widetilde\pi(-s)=\frac{\widetilde\alpha_s}{2}.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations \((6)\)--\((7)\) give \(Q_{\widetilde\pi}=Q^\star\), and each positive sign-pair mass produces at most two assignments.  Every generalized score matrix is
\[
G_j^{1/2}Q_{\widetilde\pi}G_j^{1/2}
=G_j^{1/2}Q^\star G_j^{1/2},
\]
so all generalized spectral risks, every gain fraction, and the optimum are unchanged.  The argument is existential conditional on an optimal second moment.  Because the original atom list has \(6^q/2\) columns, neither solving for \(Q^\star\) nor finding the affine dependence from the full list is thereby made polynomial in \(q\).  This proves the stated computational caveat.
