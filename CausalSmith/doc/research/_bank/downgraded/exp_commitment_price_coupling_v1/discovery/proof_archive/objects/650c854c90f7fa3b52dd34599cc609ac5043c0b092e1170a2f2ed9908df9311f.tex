By \(\texttt{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\), \(r_j^\star=4c_j/n^2\).  The exact component criterion in \(\texttt{def:ryan-relative-efficiency}\) is therefore the stated ratio \(\operatorname{RE}_j(\pi)=r_j^\star/R_j(\pi)\); no identification or asymptotic substitution is being made.  The spectral certificates in that theorem imply, with \(p_k=p_k(\pi)\),
\[
\operatorname{RE}_1(\pi)
\le\frac1{1+F_1},qquad
\operatorname{RE}_2(\pi)
\le\frac1{1+F_2}, \tag{1}
\]
where
\[
F_1=\kappa_1(ap_2+bp_3),qquad
F_2=\kappa_2(ap_1+bp_3). \tag{2}
\]
All denominators are positive because \(c_j,s_j,a,b>0\).  Hence maximizing the worst upper bound in (1) is equivalent to minimizing \(\max(F_1,F_2)\) on the frequency simplex.

Assume \(\kappa_2>\kappa_1>0\), and define
\[
D=\kappa_1(a-b)+\kappa_2b,qquad
\alpha=\frac{\kappa_2b}{D},qquad
1-\alpha=\frac{\kappa_1(a-b)}D. \tag{3}
\]
Since \(a>2b>0\), one has \(D>0\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).  Substituting \(p_3=1-p_1-p_2\) into (2) and simplifying gives the global affine certificate
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha F_1+(1-\alpha)F_2
&=\frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}
+\frac{a(a-2b)\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}p_1\\
&\ge \frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{D}=:m. 
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
Because a maximum dominates every convex combination, (4) proves \(\max(F_1,F_2)\ge m\).

Equality is feasible.  Set
\[
p_1^\circ=0,qquad
p_2^\circ=\frac{b(\kappa_2-\kappa_1)}D,qquad
p_3^\circ=1-p_2^\circ. \tag{5}
\]
The numerator in \(p_2^\circ\) is positive, while
\[
D-b(\kappa_2-\kappa_1)=a\kappa_1>0, \tag{6}
\]
so (5) lies in the simplex.  Direct substitution yields \(F_1=F_2=m\).  Construct a law that, independently in every block, chooses direction \(k\) with probability \(p_k^\circ\) and then multiplies it by an independent Rademacher sign.  This law belongs to \(\Pi_n\).  Its block scores are centered and independent, and their second moments are identical across blocks; consequently
\[
C_j=4c_j(1+F_j)I_q,qquad
R_j=\frac{4c_j}{n^2}(1+F_j). \tag{7}
\]
Thus (1) is attained for both classes, and (4)--(7) prove
\[
\eta_{\mathrm{REL}}
=\frac1{1+m}
=\left[1+\frac{ab\kappa_1\kappa_2}{\kappa_1(a-b)+\kappa_2b}\right]^{-1}. \tag{8}
\]

The certificate also proves the asserted uniqueness of average frequencies.  If any design attains (8), then both inequalities
\[
\min_j\operatorname{RE}_j(\pi)
\le\{1+\max(F_1,F_2)\}^{-1}
\le(1+m)^{-1} \tag{9}
\]
must be equalities.  Equality in (4) forces \(p_1=0\), since its coefficient is strictly positive.  Equality between a maximum and the strictly interior convex combination in (4) forces \(F_1=F_2=m\).  Solving this equality on the face \(p_1=0\) yields exactly (5).  Hence every relative-efficiency optimizer has the displayed average frequencies.

For the gain-normalized problem, the proof of \(\texttt{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) showed
\[
\max\{ap_2+bp_3,ap_1+bp_3\}
\ge b+\left(\frac a2-b\right)(1-p_3). \tag{10}
\]
Attaining its commitment value requires equality with \(b\), and \(a/2-b>0\) forces \(p_3=1\).  In contrast, (5) has \(p_2^\circ>0\) whenever \(\kappa_2>\kappa_1\).  The two optimizer sets are therefore disjoint throughout the stated open parameter region.  Both witnesses are blinded block-balanced laws on precisely the same finite support, so the separation is caused by the normalization criterion and not by a change of feasible designs.
