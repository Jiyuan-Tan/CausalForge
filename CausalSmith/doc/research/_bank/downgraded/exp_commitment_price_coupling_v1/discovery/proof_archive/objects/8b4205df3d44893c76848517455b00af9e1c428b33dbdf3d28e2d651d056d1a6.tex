\textbf{Proof.}
Fix the positive loadings in \(\mathrm{def:block-specific-spectral-menu}\), and write
\[
g_{j\ell}=\sum_{k=1}^3\sqrt{d_{j\ell k}}h_k\in\mathbf1_4^\perp,
\qquad u_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}}=E_\ell g_{j\ell}. \tag{1}
\]
The vectors in (1) are nonzero because every \(d_{j\ell k}>0\), and distinct columns have disjoint block supports.  Hence \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) has full column rank \(q\).  Spectral decomposition, exactly as in (4)--(5) of \(\mathrm{thm:block-diagonal-psd-finite-class-frontier}\), gives
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)
=n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{(K_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^{1/2}Q_\pi(K_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^{1/2}\}
=n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\}, \tag{2}
\]
where the second equality follows because the two displayed matrices have the same nonzero eigenvalues.  The fixed-array unbiasedness and variance bound follow from \(\mathrm{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\), whose algebra applies verbatim to this ellipsoid, or directly from the pointwise identity \(\widehat\tau-\tau_N=n^{-1}Z^\top\mu\) and (2).

If block \(\ell\) uses \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon v_k\), orthonormality and \(v_k=2h_k\) give
\[
(u_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}})^\top Z
=2\varepsilon\sqrt{d_{j\ell k}}. \tag{3}
\]
Therefore, for an arbitrary blinded law,
\[
[C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}(\pi). \tag{4}
\]
Since a positive-semidefinite matrix's largest eigenvalue dominates every diagonal entry, (2)--(4) prove the stated lower certificate.

The global kernel is the direct sum of the local rank-one kernels \(g_{j\ell}g_{j\ell}^\top\).  Thus \(\mathrm{thm:block-diagonal-psd-finite-class-frontier}\) applies.  Its product law preserves (4) and removes all off-diagonal score covariances.  Equivalently, independence and symmetric signs in (3) give directly
\[
C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)
=4\operatorname{diag}_{\ell}
\left(\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}\right), \tag{5}
\]
and its principal-block certificate gives \(R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)\le R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\) simultaneously for all classes.

At the baseline \(p_{\ell k}=1/3\), (5) yields
\[
B_j^{\mathrm{blk}}
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\frac13\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}
=\frac{4b_j}{n^2}. \tag{6}
\]
For the class-specific oracle, every convex combination satisfies
\[
\sum_kd_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}\ge\min_kd_{j\ell k}. \tag{7}
\]
Choosing in each block a minimizing direction makes (7) an equality in every block.  Equations (2), (5), and (7) therefore give
\[
(r_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^\star
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\min_kd_{j\ell k}
=\frac{4\bar r_j}{n^2},
\qquad
\Delta_j^{\mathrm{blk}}=\frac{4(b_j-\bar r_j)}{n^2}. \tag{8}
\]

Assume \(b_j>\bar r_j\) for every \(j\), so every gain denominator in (8) is positive.  Productization shows that the maximin may be restricted to the marginals \(p_{\ell k}\).  By (5)--(8), for a product law
\[
g_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)\ge t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}
\le b_j-(b_j-\bar r_j)t
\quad\text{for every }\ell. \tag{9}
\]
Together with the block simplex constraints, (9) is the boxed LP.  Compactness of the product of simplices gives an optimizer, and \(\mathrm{def:block-product-design}\) implements it.  Conversely, if an arbitrary commitment optimizer had marginals that did not solve the LP, its productization would strictly increase the minimum gain beyond the optimal value, a contradiction; hence every optimizer's marginals solve the LP.  The variable and constraint counts follow immediately.  No positive lower bound on \(p_{\ell k}\) was used.  The final scope statements follow from the arbitrary-rank local theorem and from its explicit direct-sum boundary. \(\square\)
