\textbf{Proof.}
We separate the causal variance statement, the exact primal program, the trace certificate, and the dual certificate.

First, every blinded law \(\pi\in\Pi_n\) has a unique sign-pair mass vector
\[
\alpha_s=\pi(s)+\pi(-s)=2\pi(s),
\qquad s\in\mathscr S,
\tag{1}
\]
and \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\).  Conversely, every \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\) defines a blinded law by assigning mass \(\alpha_s/2\) to each of \(s\) and \(-s\).  Because \((-s)(-s)^\top=ss^\top\), these mutually inverse constructions satisfy
\[
Q_\pi=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_s ss^\top,
\qquad
G_j^{1/2}Q_\pi G_j^{1/2}=A_j(\alpha).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the mass simplex parametrizes the entire feasible design space, including its boundary.

For any positive-semidefinite \(G_j\), its ellipsoid has the equivalent representation
\[
\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}
=\{G_j^{1/2}u:\|u\|_2\le1\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the spectral decomposition of \(G_j\) shows that the quadratic form with \(G_j^\dagger\) is the squared Euclidean norm of the coordinates of \(u\) on \(\operatorname{range}(G_j)\); components in the null space may be deleted without changing \(G_j^{1/2}u\).  Hence Rayleigh--Ritz and (2) give
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}}
\frac{x^\top Q_\pi x}{n^2}
=\frac1{n^2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}.
\tag{4}
\]
The fixed-outcome, no-interference, and assignment-only-randomness assumptions, together with the algebra in \(\mathrm{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\), give
\[
\mathbb E_\pi\widehat\tau=\tau_N,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\widehat\tau)
=n^{-2}\mu^\top Q_\pi\mu.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining (3)--(5) proves the asserted causal variance bound whenever \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{gen}}\).  In particular, \(\tau_N\) is the causal estimand, while the quantities optimized below are design criteria.

By (1)--(4), \(\beta_j\) is exactly the class risk under \(\pi_0\), \(\rho_j\) is exactly its minimum over \(\Pi_n\), and \(\delta_j\) is the baseline-to-oracle gain.  Assume \(\delta_j>0\) for every class.  For a fixed pair \((\alpha,t)\), the requirement that every retained-gain fraction be at least \(t\) is
\[
\frac{\beta_j-n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{\delta_j}
\ge t
\quad\text{for every }j.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(\delta_j>0\), (6) is equivalent to
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}
\le n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt),
\tag{7}
\]
and, because \(A_j(\alpha)\) is symmetric, (7) is equivalent to
\[
A_j(\alpha)\preceq n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n.
\tag{8}
\]
Maximizing \(t\) subject to (8) and \(\alpha\in\Delta_m\) is exactly program \((\mathrm P)\).  The simplex is compact and the objective obtained by eliminating \(t\) is continuous, so an optimizer exists.

Next, \(A_j(\alpha)\) is supported on \(\operatorname{range}(G_j)\), whose dimension is \(r_j\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{lem:finite-trace-isotropy}\) yields
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}
\ge\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_j}.
\tag{9}
\]
Every point feasible for \((\mathrm P)\) is consequently feasible for \((\mathrm T)\), proving \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\le\overline\lambda\).

If equality holds, a primal optimizer \(\alpha\) at \(t=\overline\lambda\) satisfies (E).  Conversely, any \(\alpha\) satisfying (E) makes \((\alpha,\overline\lambda)\) feasible for \((\mathrm P)\), forcing equality of the two optima.  To obtain the equivalent active-class description, first take traces in (E).  This shows that \(\alpha\) is feasible for \((\mathrm T)\) at its optimum and hence lies on its optimal face.  If class \(j\) is trace-active, then its trace threshold is an equality, and (9) together with (E) forces
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{A_j(\alpha)\}
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_j(\alpha)\}}{r_j}.
\]
The equality characterization in \(\mathrm{lem:finite-trace-isotropy}\) is precisely (I).  For a trace-slack class, the remaining necessary and sufficient condition is the spectral threshold (E).  Conversely, active-class isotropy and the slack-class thresholds imply (E) class by class.  This proves all equivalences without any commutativity assumption.

If equality fails, the difference between the two explicitly computed optima is positive by definition and is exactly \(\Gamma_n\).  If \((\alpha^\star,t^\star)\) solves \((\mathrm P)\), construction (1) gives the law in (D).  Equations (2), (4), and (6) show that it attains \(t^\star=\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\), so the trace relaxation exceeds its exact achievable value by exactly \(\Gamma_n\).

It remains to verify the dual certificate.  Write the matrix constraints in slack form as
\[
F_j(\alpha,t)
:=n^2(\beta_j-\delta_jt)I_n-A_j(\alpha)\succeq0.
\tag{10}
\]
For multipliers \(X_j\succeq0\) and \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), the Lagrangian for the maximization problem, retaining \(\alpha_s\ge0\) as its domain and dualizing \(\sum_s\alpha_s=1\), is
\[
\begin{aligned}
L(\alpha,t;X,\gamma)
&=t+\sum_j\operatorname{tr}\{X_jF_j(\alpha,t)\}
+\gamma\left(\sum_s\alpha_s-1\right)\\
&=n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)-\gamma
+t\left\{1-n^2\sum_j\delta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)\right\}\\
&\quad+\sum_s\alpha_s
\left\{\gamma-\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The supremum over \(t\in\mathbb R\) and \(\alpha_s\ge0\) is finite exactly when
\[
n^2\sum_j\delta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)=1,
\qquad
\gamma\le\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(X_jA_{js})
\quad\text{for every }s.
\tag{12}
\]
Under (12), the supremum equals
\[
n^2\sum_j\beta_j\operatorname{tr}(X_j)-\gamma,
\tag{13}
\]
which is the displayed dual objective.

For completeness, there is no duality gap here.  Choose every \(\alpha_s>0\), and then choose \(t\) sufficiently negative.  Since every \(\delta_j>0\), (10) is then positive definite for all \(j\).  Thus the feasible affine slice meets the relative interior of the product cone consisting of the nonnegative orthant and the positive-semidefinite cones.  Separating the strictly feasible cone from the open set of objective values larger than the primal optimum yields nonnegative matrix multipliers and an equality multiplier.  The strict interior point forces the separating coefficient on the objective coordinate to be positive; normalizing that coefficient to one gives exactly (12).  Taking the supremum in (11) then gives (13), so the separating hyperplane supplies a dual feasible point with objective equal to the primal optimum.  Weak duality follows directly from (11), and together these two inequalities prove the equality (C).  This argument also proves dual attainment.

Finally, the primal uses \(m\) masses and \(J_{\mathrm{gen}}\) matrix inequalities of order \(n\).  Equation (D) implements every optimizer without rounding, so the attainment constant in unrestricted \(\Pi_n\) is one.  If some \(\delta_j=0\), equation (6) divides by zero and no gain-normalized criterion for that class has been defined; this is exactly the stated boundary. \(\square\)
