\textbf{Proof.}
We derive the result as the rank-one specialization of \(\mathrm{thm:block\text{-}diagonal\text{-}psd\text{-}finite\text{-}class\text{-}frontier}\), while recording the scalar reduction explicitly. For each class and block put
\[
a_{j\ell}=(\sqrt{d_{j\ell1}},\sqrt{d_{j\ell2}},\sqrt{d_{j\ell3}})^\top,
\qquad
g_{j\ell}=Ha_{j\ell},
\qquad
G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}=g_{j\ell}g_{j\ell}^\top.
\tag{1}
\]
Because the columns \(u^{\mathrm{blk}}_{j\ell}=E_\ell g_{j\ell}\) have disjoint supports,
\[
K_j^{\mathrm{blk}}
=U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^\top
=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^qG^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}.
\tag{2}
\]
Positivity of the loadings gives \(\|g_{j\ell}\|_2^2=\sum_kd_{j\ell k}>0\), so the columns are nonzero and mutually orthogonal. Hence \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) has full column rank \(q\). The polar-decomposition calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:variance\text{-}and\text{-}spectral\text{-}risk}\), applied to (2), gives
\[
R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)
=n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\}
=n^{-2}\lambda_{\max}\{(K_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^{1/2}Q_\pi(K_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^{1/2}\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The causal estimator identity from the same lemma gives design unbiasedness and the stated variance bound whenever \(\mu\in\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\).

For any realized assignment, write \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon_\ell v_{k_\ell}\). Orthonormality and \(v_k=2h_k\) give
\[
(u_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}})^\top Z
=2\varepsilon_\ell\sqrt{d_{j\ell k_\ell}}.
\tag{4}
\]
Squaring and averaging proves
\[
[C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}(\pi).
\tag{5}
\]
The coordinate Rayleigh quotient applied to \(C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi)\succeq0\) and (3) gives the displayed lower bound. Under the product law, independent symmetric signs make distinct scores in (4) uncorrelated, so
\[
C_j^{\mathrm{blk}}(\pi_p)
=4\operatorname{diag}_\ell\left(\sum_kd_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (3), (5), and (6) give simultaneous Pareto domination and directly verify the productization specialization of the block-diagonal theorem.

To reduce its local matrix program, note that
\[
\widehat G^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}=a_{j\ell}a_{j\ell}^\top,
\qquad
S^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}
=\frac{a_{j\ell}a_{j\ell}^\top}{\|a_{j\ell}\|_2}.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(D(p_\ell)=\operatorname{Diag}(p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})\), then (7) yields
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{L^{\mathrm{bd}}_{j\ell}(p_\ell)\}
=4a_{j\ell}^\top D(p_\ell)a_{j\ell}
=4\sum_{k=1}^3d_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}.
\tag{8}
\]
The baseline and local oracle values in the block-diagonal theorem are therefore
\[
\beta_j^{\mathrm{bd}}
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\frac13\sum_kd_{j\ell k}
=\frac{4b_j}{n^2},
\qquad
\rho_j^{\mathrm{bd}}
=\frac4{n^2}\max_\ell\min_kd_{j\ell k}
=\frac{4\bar r_j}{n^2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Because (2) makes the classes and risks identical, (9) proves the formulas for \(B_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\), \((r_j^{\mathrm{blk}})^\star\), and \(\Delta_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\).

Assume \(b_j>\bar r_j\) for every \(1\le j\le J_{\mathrm{blk}}\); these are exactly all classes in the displayed minimum. Substitution of (8)--(9) into the local matrix inequality of \(\mathrm{thm:block\text{-}diagonal\text{-}psd\text{-}finite\text{-}class\text{-}frontier}\) gives
\[
4\sum_kd_{j\ell k}p_{\ell k}
\le4\{b_j-(b_j-\bar r_j)t\},
\tag{10}
\]
which is precisely the displayed scalar constraint after division by four. This proves the LP equality, optimizer existence, and attainment by \(\pi_p\). If an original law is optimal, its productization has at least the same gain for every class and hence is also optimal; therefore its local marginals solve the LP. The variable and constraint counts follow immediately from (10) and the \(q\) three-simplices. Boundary masses are feasible because neither \(\mathrm{def:design\text{-}space}\) nor the estimator uses inverse assignment probabilities.

For \(q=1\), equation (6) is scalar and direct enumeration of the six assignments gives (8), so the certificate is exact at \(n=4\). The rank-one formula (8), rather than productization itself, is what turns the local order-three semidefinite constraints into scalar inequalities. Full-rank and noncommuting within-block kernels remain governed by the local-LMI theorem, whereas a kernel coupling distinct blocks falls outside the productization argument and uses the global exponential-column program. \(\square\)
