\textbf{Proof.}
Apply \(\mathrm{thm:block\text{-}specific\text{-}finite\text{-}class\text{-}frontier}\) with \(J_{\mathrm{blk}}=2\) and the homogeneous loadings in \(\mathrm{def:block\text{-}geometry}\).  Positivity of \(a,b,c_j,s_j\) is the only parameter boundary condition used.  It makes every block loading nonzero, so the block columns have disjoint supports and \(U_j\) has rank \(q\).  Boundary designs with zero direction masses remain admissible because \(\Pi_n\) requires only support and global blinding, and no inverse assignment probability is used.

The specialized oracle and baseline loading levels are
\[
\bar r_j=c_j,
\qquad
b_j=c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3.
\tag{1}
\]
The rank-one frontier theorem therefore gives
\[
r_j^\star=\frac{4c_j}{n^2},
\qquad
B_j=\frac4{n^2}\left(c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3\right),
\qquad
\Delta_j=\frac{4s_j(a+b)}{3n^2}>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus both gain normalizations are well defined.

For an arbitrary blinded law, the diagonal score identity in that theorem gives
\[
[C_1(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\{c_1+s_1(ap_{\ell2}+bp_{\ell3})\},
\qquad
[C_2(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\{c_2+s_2(ap_{\ell1}+bp_{\ell3})\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Taking traces, dividing by \(q\), and using
\(p_k=q^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^q p_{\ell k}\) proves the two trace identities.  Since every \(C_j(\pi)\) is positive semidefinite of order \(q\), the maximum of its nonnegative eigenvalues is at least their average.  Hence
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{C_j(\pi)\}\ge q^{-1}\operatorname{tr}C_j(\pi),
\tag{4}
\]
which proves the spectral certificates.

For each block put
\[
L_{1\ell}=ap_{\ell2}+bp_{\ell3},
\qquad
L_{2\ell}=ap_{\ell1}+bp_{\ell3}.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(p_{\ell1}+p_{\ell2}+p_{\ell3}=1\),
\[
\max(L_{1\ell},L_{2\ell})
\ge \frac{L_{1\ell}+L_{2\ell}}2
=\frac a2(1-p_{\ell3})+bp_{\ell3}
\ge m:=\min\left\{\frac a2,b\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The specialized exact linear program says that a common retained fraction \(t\) is feasible precisely when
\[
L_{1\ell}\le\frac{a+b}{3}(1-t),
\qquad
L_{2\ell}\le\frac{a+b}{3}(1-t)
\quad\text{for every }\ell.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) imply
\[
t\le1-\frac{3m}{a+b}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{a-2b}{a+b},&a\ge2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2b-a}{2(a+b)},&a\le2b.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]

The bound is attained blockwise.  If \(a>2b\), take \((p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})=(0,0,1)\).  If \(a<2b\), take \((1/2,1/2,0)\).  If \(a=2b\), take
\[
p_{\ell1}=p_{\ell2}=t_\ell,
\qquad
p_{\ell3}=1-2t_\ell,
\qquad 0\le t_\ell\le\frac12.
\tag{9}
\]
In every case \(L_{1\ell}=L_{2\ell}=m\).  The product construction makes distinct block scores uncorrelated by independent symmetric signs, and homogeneous block marginals make both score matrices scalar.  Thus (4) and (8) are equalities.  The first construction is exactly \(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}}\).

It remains to characterize the optimal average frequencies.  Equality in the blockwise bound (6) is necessary at every block of an optimizer, because otherwise the maximum block risk in (7) is strictly larger than \(m\).  If \(a>2b\), strict positivity of \(a/2-b\) forces \(p_{\ell3}=1\).  If \(a<2b\), equality forces \(p_{\ell3}=0\), and equality of the maximum with the average forces \(p_{\ell1}=p_{\ell2}=1/2\).  At \(a=2b\), direct substitution gives
\[
\max(L_{1\ell},L_{2\ell})
=b\{1+|p_{\ell1}-p_{\ell2}|\},
\tag{10}
\]
so equality is equivalent to (9).  Averaging produces exactly the frequency vectors in the statement.  Conversely, constant block marginals with any listed average give an attaining product law.

For an attaining product law, (2)--(5) yield
\[
B_j-R_j
=\frac{4s_j}{n^2}\left\{\frac{a+b}{3}-m\right\}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{4s_j(a-2b)}{3n^2},&a>2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2s_j(2b-a)}{3n^2},&a<2b,\\[6pt]
0,&a=2b.
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the regime-specific product law improves both classes strictly exactly away from the elbow.  At \(a=2b\), the baseline marginals \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) belong to (9), so block complete randomization is maximin and \(\lambda^\star=0\).

For the smallest admissible population, \(q=1\) and \(n=4\), direct enumeration of the six assignments gives (3), while (6) exhausts the three-direction simplex.  Hence the finite-sample certificate, the elbow, and all boundary cases hold already at \(n=4\).  \(\square\)
