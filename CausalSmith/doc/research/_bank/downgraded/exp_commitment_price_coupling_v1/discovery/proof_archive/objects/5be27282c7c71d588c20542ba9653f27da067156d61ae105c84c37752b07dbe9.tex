Fix \(q\ge1\).  The conditions \(c_j>0\) and \(s_j>0\), and the disjoint block supports, ensure that every \(U_j\) has rank \(q\); all eigenvalues and divisions below are therefore well defined.  For a block \(\ell\), write
\[
d_{1,1}=c_1,\quad d_{1,2}=c_1+s_1a,\quad d_{1,3}=c_1+s_1b,
\]
\[
d_{2,1}=c_2+s_2a,\quad d_{2,2}=c_2,\quad d_{2,3}=c_2+s_2b. \tag{1}
\]
Because \(h_k=v_k/2\) and the \(h_k\)'s are orthonormal, whenever \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon v_k\), with \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
u_{j\ell}^\top Z=2\varepsilon\sqrt{d_{j,k}},
\qquad
(u_{j\ell}^\top Z)^2=4d_{j,k}. \tag{2}
\]
No independence across blocks is needed for (2).  Since \(C_j(\pi)=\mathbb E_\pi[(U_j^\top Z)(U_j^\top Z)^\top]\), summing its diagonal entries and using \(\texttt{def:direction-frequencies}\) gives
\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}C_1(\pi)}q
=4\{c_1+s_1(ap_2(\pi)+bp_3(\pi))\}, \tag{3}
\]
\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}C_2(\pi)}q
=4\{c_2+s_2(ap_1(\pi)+bp_3(\pi))\}. \tag{4}
\]
The matrix \(C_j(\pi)\) is positive semidefinite and has dimension \(q\).  If its eigenvalues are \(\rho_1\ge\cdots\ge\rho_q\ge0\), then \(\rho_1\ge q^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^q\rho_\ell\).  Equations (3)--(4) therefore prove the two displayed spectral lower certificates.

Because \(p_1+p_2+p_3=1\) and all frequencies are nonnegative, (3)--(4), together with \(\texttt{lem:variance-and-spectral-risk}\), imply \(R_j(\pi)\ge4c_j/n^2\).  For class \(1\), take independent Rademacher signs on direction \(v_1\) in every block.  Then the block scores in (2) are independent, centered, and have squared magnitude \(4c_1\), so \(C_1=4c_1I_q\).  The analogous design on \(v_2\) gives \(C_2=4c_2I_q\).  Both laws belong to \(\Pi_n\).  Hence the lower bounds are attained and
\[
r_j^\star=\frac{4c_j}{n^2}. \tag{5}
\]
Under \(\pi_0\), every local direction has probability \(1/3\), independent signs center the block scores, and different blocks are independent.  Thus
\[
C_j(\pi_0)=4\left\{c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3\right\}I_q. \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) and the spectral-risk lemma give
\[
B_j=\frac4{n^2}\left\{c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3\right\},
\qquad
\Delta_j=\frac{4s_j(a+b)}{3n^2}>0. \tag{7}
\]
The positivity in (7) uses \(s_j>0\) and \(a+b>0\), so gain normalization is legitimate.

We next certify the commitment optimum.  Suppress the argument \(\pi\) and put
\[
L_1=ap_2+bp_3,qquad L_2=ap_1+bp_3,qquad A=a+b. \tag{8}
\]
The trace certificates, (7), and the definition of \(g_j\) imply
\[
g_1(\pi)\le1-\frac{3L_1}{A},
\qquad
g_2(\pi)\le1-\frac{3L_2}{A}. \tag{9}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\max(L_1,L_2)
\ge\frac{L_1+L_2}{2}
=\frac a2(1-p_3)+bp_3
=b+\left(\frac a2-b\right)(1-p_3)
\ge b, \tag{10}
\]
where the last inequality uses the primitive boundary condition \(a>2b\) and \(0\le p_3\le1\).  Combining (9)--(10), every feasible law obeys
\[
\min_{j\in\{1,2\}}g_j(\pi)
\le1-\frac{3b}{a+b}
=\frac{a-2b}{a+b}. \tag{11}
\]
Under \(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}}\), (2) and independent signs give
\[
C_j(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}})=4(c_j+s_jb)I_q,qquad
R_j(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}})=\frac{4(c_j+s_jb)}{n^2}. \tag{12}
\]
Thus both inequalities in (9) are equalities with \(p_3=1\), and each gain fraction equals the right side of (11).  The matching upper and lower bounds prove
\[
\lambda^\star=\frac{a-2b}{a+b}, \tag{13}
\]
and (3)--(4), together with (12), are the asserted finite-sample optimality certificate.  In fact, equality in (10) requires \(p_3=1\), because \(a/2-b>0\); hence every gain-normalized optimizer has \(p_3=1\).  Finally,
\[
B_j-R_j(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}})
=\frac{4s_j}{n^2}\left(\frac{a+b}{3}-b\right)
=\frac{4s_j(a-2b)}{3n^2}>0, \tag{14}
\]
which proves strict improvement.

For the required smallest-instance check, take \(q=1\), so \(n=4\).  A blinded law assigns total masses \(p_1,p_2,p_3\) to the three pairs \(\{\pm v_k\}\).  The score matrix is then scalar, so (3)--(4) are equalities for every one of the six assignments and every mixture of them.  Direct enumeration gives \(p_k=1/3\) for \(\pi_0\), \(p_3=1\) for \(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}}\), and exactly (5)--(14).  At the excluded boundary \(a=2b\), the strict coefficient in (10) vanishes and the strict improvement in (14) disappears; thus the stated boundary condition is load bearing rather than cosmetic.
