\textbf{Proof.}
Apply \(\mathrm{thm:block-specific-finite-class-frontier}\) with \(J_{\mathrm{blk}}=2\) and the homogeneous loadings from \(\mathrm{def:block-geometry}\). The specialized objects \(U_j^{\mathrm{blk}},K_j^{\mathrm{blk}},\mathcal M_j^{\mathrm{blk}},C_j^{\mathrm{blk}},R_j^{\mathrm{blk}}\) coincide respectively with \(U_j,K_j,\mathcal M_j,C_j,R_j\). Positivity of \(a,b,c_j,s_j\) gives
\[
\bar r_j=c_j,
\qquad
b_j=c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3,
\tag{1}
\]
so the general baseline, oracle, and gain formulas become exactly
\[
r_j^\star=\frac{4c_j}{n^2},
\qquad
B_j=\frac4{n^2}\left(c_j+\frac{s_j(a+b)}3\right),
\qquad
\Delta_j=\frac{4s_j(a+b)}{3n^2}>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every normalization below is well defined.

For an arbitrary blinded law, the general diagonal identity gives
\[
[C_1(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\{c_1+s_1(ap_{\ell2}+bp_{\ell3})\},
\qquad
[C_2(\pi)]_{\ell\ell}
=4\{c_2+s_2(ap_{\ell1}+bp_{\ell3})\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Taking traces, dividing by \(q\), and using \(p_k=q^{-1}\sum_\ell p_{\ell k}\) proves the two displayed trace identities. Since \(C_j(\pi)\succeq0\), its largest eigenvalue is at least the average of its \(q\) eigenvalues:
\[
\lambda_{\max}\{C_j(\pi)\}\ge q^{-1}\operatorname{tr}C_j(\pi).
\tag{4}
\]
This proves the spectral certificates.

It remains to solve the specialized LP. For each block define
\[
L_{1\ell}=ap_{\ell2}+bp_{\ell3},
\qquad
L_{2\ell}=ap_{\ell1}+bp_{\ell3}.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(p_{\ell1}+p_{\ell2}+p_{\ell3}=1\),
\[
\max(L_{1\ell},L_{2\ell})
\ge\frac{L_{1\ell}+L_{2\ell}}2
=\frac a2(1-p_{\ell3})+bp_{\ell3}
\ge m:=\min\left\{\frac a2,b\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The LP inequalities in the general theorem and (1) say that a common retained fraction \(t\) is feasible exactly when
\[
L_{1\ell}\le\frac{a+b}{3}(1-t),
\qquad
L_{2\ell}\le\frac{a+b}{3}(1-t)
\quad\text{for every }\ell.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) imply
\[
t\le1-\frac{3m}{a+b}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{a-2b}{a+b},&a\ge2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2b-a}{2(a+b)},&a\le2b.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]
If \(a>2b\), choose \((p_{\ell1},p_{\ell2},p_{\ell3})=(0,0,1)\) in every block; then \(L_{1\ell}=L_{2\ell}=b=m\). If \(a<2b\), choose \((1/2,1/2,0)\); then both loads equal \(a/2=m\). If \(a=2b\), choose \(p_{\ell1}=p_{\ell2}=t_\ell\), \(p_{\ell3}=1-2t_\ell\), where \(0\le t_\ell\le1/2\); again both loads equal \(b=a/2=m\). The product construction makes \(C_j\) diagonal, and homogeneity makes each diagonal constant, so both matrices are scalar. Hence equality is attained in (8); the first witness is precisely \(\pi_{\mathrm{sh}}\).

We now characterize the average frequencies of all minimax laws. By the productization part of the general theorem, the local marginals of any minimax law solve the specialized LP. Thus equality must hold in (6) for every block. When \(a>2b\),
\[
\frac a2(1-p_{\ell3})+bp_{\ell3}
=b+\left(\frac a2-b\right)(1-p_{\ell3}),
\]
so equality forces \(p_{\ell3}=1\). When \(a<2b\), equality forces \(p_{\ell3}=0\), and equality between the maximum and average in (6) forces \(p_{\ell1}=p_{\ell2}=1/2\). At \(a=2b\), direct substitution gives
\[
\max(L_{1\ell},L_{2\ell})
=b\{1+|p_{\ell1}-p_{\ell2}|\},
\]
so equality is equivalent to \(p_{\ell1}=p_{\ell2}=t_\ell\) and \(p_{\ell3}=1-2t_\ell\). Averaging over \(\ell\) yields exactly the three frequency families in the statement, with \(t=q^{-1}\sum_\ell t_\ell\). Conversely, constant block marginals with any displayed average are among the attaining product laws already constructed.

For an attaining product law, (3) and (5) give
\[
B_j-R_j
=\frac{4s_j}{n^2}\left\{\frac{a+b}{3}-m\right\}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{4s_j(a-2b)}{3n^2},&a>2b,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2s_j(2b-a)}{3n^2},&a<2b,\\[6pt]
0,&a=2b.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]
Therefore both class risks improve strictly over \(\pi_0\) exactly when \(a\ne2b\). At \(a=2b\), the baseline marginals \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) belong to the optimal family, so block complete randomization is maximin and (8) gives \(\lambda^\star=0\).

For the smallest-instance stress test, set \(q=1\) and \(n=4\). Each score matrix is scalar. Enumerating the six assignments gives the two loads in (3), and (6) exhausts the three-point simplex, confirming all boundary and elbow cases exactly. \(\square\)
