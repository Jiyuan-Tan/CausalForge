Fix \(q\geq 1\), let \(n=4q\), and fix \(a,b,c_j,s_j>0\).  These positivity conditions ensure in particular that every column of \(U_j\) is nonzero.  Because different columns are supported on disjoint quadruples, \(U_j\) has full column rank \(q\).

For class \(1\), define a product design \(\pi^{(1)}\) by
\[
 Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon_\ell v_1,\qquad \ell=1,\ldots,q,
\]
where \(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_q\) are independent symmetric signs.  This law is supported on \(\mathcal Z_n\), and simultaneous sign reversal maps every sign vector to an equally probable sign vector.  Hence \(\pi^{(1)}(z)=\pi^{(1)}(-z)\), so \(\pi^{(1)}\in\Pi_n\) by \(\mathrm{def:design-space}\).  The identities \(v_k=2h_k\) and \(h_k^\top h_{k'}=\mathbf 1\{k=k'\}\), together with the disjoint block supports, give
\[
 u_{1\ell}^{\top}Z
 =\varepsilon_\ell
 \left\{\sqrt{c_1}h_1+\sqrt{c_1+s_1a}h_2+
 \sqrt{c_1+s_1b}h_3\right\}^{\!\top}v_1
 =2\varepsilon_\ell\sqrt{c_1}.
\]
The score coordinates are therefore independent and centered, and their second moments are \(4c_1\).  Thus
\[
 C_1(\pi^{(1)})
 =\mathbb E_{\pi^{(1)}}[(U_1^\top Z)(U_1^\top Z)^\top]
 =4c_1I_q. \tag{1}
\]

For completeness, the risk conclusion follows directly from the response-class and risk definitions.  Since \(K_j=U_jU_j^\top\) and \(U_j\) has full column rank, every \(x\in\operatorname{range}(K_j)=\operatorname{range}(U_j)\) has a unique representation \(x=U_jw\).  A thin singular-value decomposition of \(U_j\) gives the elementary Moore--Penrose identity
\[
 (U_jw)^\top(U_jU_j^\top)^\dagger(U_jw)=w^\top w.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \mathcal M_j=\{U_jw:w\in\mathbb R^q,\ \lVert w\rVert_2\leq1\}.
\]
Using \(\mathrm{def:design-risk}\) and the Rayleigh-quotient identity therefore yields, for every \(\pi\in\Pi_n\),
\[
 R_j(\pi)
 =\frac1{n^2}\sup_{\lVert w\rVert_2\leq1}
 w^\top C_j(\pi)w
 =\frac1{n^2}\lambda_{\max}\{C_j(\pi)\}. \tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2) gives
\[
 R_1(\pi^{(1)})=\frac{4c_1}{n^2}.
\]
The oracle formula in \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) states that \(r_1^\star=4c_1/n^2\), so \(\pi^{(1)}\) attains the class-\(1\) oracle risk.

For class \(2\), define \(\pi^{(2)}\) analogously by \(Z_{[\ell]}=\varepsilon_\ell v_2\).  It belongs to \(\Pi_n\) by the same support and blinding argument.  Orthogonality now gives
\[
 u_{2\ell}^{\top}Z=2\varepsilon_\ell\sqrt{c_2},
 \qquad C_2(\pi^{(2)})=4c_2I_q.
\]
Equation (2) and the oracle formula from \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) imply
\[
 R_2(\pi^{(2)})=\frac{4c_2}{n^2}=r_2^\star.
\]

It remains to compare the one-class and two-class commitment values.  The frontier theorem gives
\[
 \Delta_j=\frac{4s_j(a+b)}{3n^2}>0. \tag{3}
\]
For any feasible \(\pi\), the definition of \(r_j^\star\) gives \(R_j(\pi)\geq r_j^\star\).  Dividing by the positive quantity in (3),
\[
 g_j(\pi)=\frac{B_j-R_j(\pi)}{\Delta_j}
 \leq\frac{B_j-r_j^\star}{\Delta_j}=1.
\]
The design \(\pi^{(j)}\) attains equality, so the commitment problem restricted to either single class has value exactly \(1\).

By contrast, \(\mathrm{thm:quadruple-menu-frontier}\) gives
\[
 \lambda^\star=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{a-2b}{a+b},&a\geq2b,\\[6pt]
 \dfrac{2b-a}{2(a+b)},&a\leq2b.
 \end{cases}
\]
In the first regime the denominator exceeds the numerator by \(3b>0\); in the second it exceeds the numerator by \(3a>0\).  The relevant numerator is nonnegative in each regime.  Hence \(0\leq\lambda^\star<1\) for all \(a,b>0\), including \(\lambda^\star=0\) at the boundary \(a=2b\).  Thus simultaneous commitment across the two response classes has strictly smaller value than either one-class problem.

No exposure-probability lower bound is used: the two oracle witnesses lie on the boundary of \(\Pi_n\), and neither the estimator nor the class-risk criterion divides by a direction probability.  As a smallest-instance check, when \(q=1\) each score matrix above is the scalar \(4c_j\), so direct enumeration of the two assignments \(\{v_j,-v_j\}\) gives the same oracle risk exactly.
