\textbf{Proof.}
Every block of every \(s\in\mathscr S\subseteq\mathcal Z_n\) is one of the balanced vectors \(\pm v_1,\pm v_2,\pm v_3\).  Hence each block is orthogonal to \(\mathbf 1_4\), so \(s\in\mathcal H\).  Also every coordinate of \(s\) is \(\pm1\), and therefore
\[
\operatorname{tr}(ss^\top)=\|s\|_2^2=n.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(\alpha\) be the sign-pair mass vector of an optimizer with second moment \(Q^\star\).  By the sign-pair parametrization proved in \(\mathrm{thm:higher-rank-menu-exact-program}\),
\[
Q^\star=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S}\alpha_s ss^\top,
\qquad
\alpha_s\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_s\alpha_s=1.
\tag{2}
\]
Regard each \(ss^\top\) as a symmetric operator on the \(d\)-dimensional space \(\mathcal H\).  Such operators form a vector space of dimension \(D=d(d+1)/2\).  By (1), all of the matrices in (2) lie in the affine hyperplane of trace \(n\), whose dimension is at most \(D-1\).

Suppose a representation (2) has more than \(D\) positive coefficients.  The corresponding matrices are affinely dependent, so there are real numbers \(c_s\), not all zero and supported only where \(\alpha_s>0\), such that
\[
\sum_s c_s=0,
\qquad
\sum_s c_s ss^\top=0.
\tag{3}
\]
The first identity implies that the nonzero \(c_s\)'s include both signs.  Replacing \(c\) by \(-c\) if necessary and setting
\[
\varepsilon=\min_{c_s>0}\frac{\alpha_s}{c_s}>0,
\qquad
\alpha_s'=\alpha_s-\varepsilon c_s,
\tag{4}
\]
preserves nonnegativity, the total mass, and the matrix in (2), by (3), while making at least one previously positive coefficient zero.  Iterating this finite elimination produces a vector \(\widetilde\alpha\in\Delta_m\) with the same second moment and at most \(D\) positive entries.  If \(m<D\), the original representation already uses at most \(m\) entries.  Thus the support size is at most
\[
\min\left\{m,D\right\}
=\min\left\{\frac{6^q}{2},\frac{3q(3q+1)}2\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define \(\widetilde\pi(s)=\widetilde\pi(-s)=\widetilde\alpha_s/2\).  This is a blinded member of \(\Pi_n\), and (2)--(4) give \(Q_{\widetilde\pi}=Q^\star\).  Every generalized class risk and every constraint of program \((\mathrm P)\) depends on the design only through this second moment, so \(\widetilde\pi\) has the same risks and the same value \(\lambda_{\mathrm{gen}}^\star\).  Blinding uses at most two assignments per positive sign-pair mass.  The elimination proves existence after \(Q^\star\) is known; it neither avoids solving the exponential-column program nor supplies a polynomial-time search for the representation. \(\square\)
