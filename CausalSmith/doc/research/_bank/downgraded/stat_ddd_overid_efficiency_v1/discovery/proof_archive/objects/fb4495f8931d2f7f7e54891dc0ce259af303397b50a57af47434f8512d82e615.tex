Each block of \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) sums to \(\omega_k\), and hence
\[
 B^{\prime}b^{\mathrm{sep}}=\omega.
\]
The columns of \(N\) are linearly independent and satisfy \(B^{\prime}N=0\). There are \(M-|\mathcal K|\) such columns, whereas \(B\) has rank \(|\mathcal K|\) because its columns have nonempty, disjoint row supports. Thus the columns of \(N\) form a basis for \(\ker(B^{\prime})\), and every feasible loading has a unique representation
\[
 b=b^{\mathrm{sep}}+N\gamma.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
 b^{\prime}\Omega b
 =V_{\mathrm{sep}}+2\gamma^{\prime}N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
 +\gamma^{\prime}N^{\prime}\Omega N\gamma.
\]
The score-spectrum condition makes \(\Omega\) positive definite. If \(N\) has positive column dimension, \(N^{\prime}\Omega N\) is positive definite and completion of the square yields
\[
 \gamma_*=-\bigl(N^{\prime}\Omega N\bigr)^{-1}N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
If \(N\) has zero columns, the feasible set contains only \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}\), and all ensuing quadratic forms are understood as zero. In either case the resulting loading is the unique constrained minimizer, which Theorem \(\ref{thm:joint-system-limit}\) identifies as \(b^{\mathrm{joint}}\). Therefore
\[
 V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}
 (N^{\prime}\Omega N)^{-1}
 (N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}),
\]
with the stated zero-dimensional convention.

The first-order condition at the minimizer is \(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}=0\). Since \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}}\in\operatorname{col}(N)\), the cross term in the \(\Omega\)-norm expansion vanishes, giving
\[
 V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
   \Omega(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}}).
\]
Positive definiteness shows that the gap is zero if and only if \(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}=0\), equivalently if and only if \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}=b^{\mathrm{joint}}\).

For a vector \(z\) partitioned according to the comparison sets, \(N_k^{\prime}z_k=0\) exactly when all coordinates of \(z_k\) equal its final coordinate. Applying this to \(z=\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) gives the blockwise-constancy criterion. Moreover,
\[
 \Omega_{kk}b_k^{\mathrm{sep}}
 =\frac{\omega_k}{\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega_{kk}^{-1}\mathbf 1}\mathbf 1,
\]
so the within-block contribution is already constant. Any nonconstancy of
\[
 (\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}})_k
 =\Omega_{kk}b_k^{\mathrm{sep}}
  +\sum_{\ell\ne k}\Omega_{k\ell}b_\ell^{\mathrm{sep}}
\]
is exactly nonconstancy of the net cross-block covariance contribution. This proves the population frontier without learner conditions.

Now impose the estimator-level hypotheses. The learner sequences and rates are conditional hypotheses only; the score-expansion lemma derives the fitted-regression envelope from them and the law primitives. The score and covariance lemmas give
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega_\lambda\to_P\Omega,
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega\to_P\Omega.
\]
Each principal block \(\Omega_{kk}\) is positive definite. Continuity of block inversion and of the positive normalizers yields \(\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}\to_Pb^{\mathrm{sep}}\). Also \(B^{\prime}\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}=\omega\) exactly. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sep}}-\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\}
 &=(\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}
   \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}\\
 &=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
   (b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The i.i.d. central-limit argument from Theorem \(\ref{thm:joint-system-limit}\) applies because the score-expansion lemma derived the required fourth moments. Thus the separate and joint estimators have asymptotic variances \(V_{\mathrm{sep}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}\), respectively. The population quadratic is therefore their exact asymptotic variance difference within the fixed constant score span.

Theorem \(\ref{thm:joint-system-limit}\) gives \(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}\to_Pb^{\mathrm{joint}}\). Together with the preceding loading convergence and \(\widehat\Omega\to_P\Omega\), continuity yields
\[
 \widehat\Delta
 \to_P
 (b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
 \Omega(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})
 =V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Because \(\widehat\Omega\) is positive semidefinite,
\[
 \widehat\Delta
 =(\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}-\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
 \widehat\Omega
 (\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}-\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})\ge0
\]
for every sample. This proves the descriptive diagnostic claim and no stronger inference claim for \(\widehat\Delta\).

If \(\mathcal K=\{k\}\), then \(\omega_k=1\) and
\[
 \Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
 =\frac{\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1},
\]
which is constant in the only block. Thus the two loadings coincide and the gap is zero. If \(g=2\), every outcome change in the score and covariance formulas is \(Y_{it}-Y_{i1}\), as required.
