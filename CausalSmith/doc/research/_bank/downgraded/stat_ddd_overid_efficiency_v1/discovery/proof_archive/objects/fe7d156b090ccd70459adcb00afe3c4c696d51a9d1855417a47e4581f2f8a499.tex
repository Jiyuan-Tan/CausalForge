The definition of label-preserving equivalence requires exact agreement of \(\mathfrak C(P)\), \(N\), \(\omega\), the comparison labels, and all residual incidences.  Since \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}(P)\) is computed blockwise from \(\mathfrak C(P)=\Omega(P)\), the vector \(N'\mathfrak C(P)b^{\mathrm{sep}}(P)\) is constant on each equivalence class.  The separate--joint frontier theorem then says that a compatible class has zero gain exactly when this vector is zero, and positive gain otherwise.  This proves the classification into \(\mathscr E_0\) and \(\mathscr E_+\).

We construct a member of \(\mathscr E_+\).  Let \(X_i=x_0\) almost surely and let \((G_i,S_i)\) be uniform on
\[
 \{(2,0),(2,1),(3,0),(3,1),(\infty,0),(\infty,1)\}.
\]
Let \(U_i\) be Rademacher.  Conditional on \(G_i\in\{2,3\}\), let \(V_i\) be an independent Rademacher.  Conditional on \(G_i=\infty\), let \(H_i\), independent of \(U_i\), equal \(1\) with probability \(3/4\) and \(-1\) with probability \(1/4\), and put \(V_i=U_iH_i\).  Thus \(U_i\) and \(V_i\) are centered Rademacher variables in every cohort,
\[
 E[U_iV_i\mid G_i=2]=E[U_iV_i\mid G_i=3]=0,
 \qquad
 E[U_iV_i\mid G_i=\infty]=\frac12.
\]
Put \(r=1/8\), set \(Z_{i1}=0\), \(Z_{i2}=rU_i\), and \(Z_{i3}=rV_i\), and define
\[
 Y_{it}(a,s)=Z_{it}quad\text{for every }a\in\{2,3,\infty\},
 \ s\in\{0,1\},\ t\in\{1,2,3\},
\]
with \(Y_{it}=Y_{it}(G_i,S_i)\).

All potential outcomes are bounded by \(1/8\).  Consistency holds by construction; all treatment effects are zero, so no anticipation and no ineligible spillover hold.  Every conditional outcome change has mean zero, so every pairwise conditional DDD restriction holds and every retained outcome regression and DDD contrast is zero.  Each retained cell probability is \(1/6>1/8\), and the fourth-moment bounds hold with \(C_4=1\).

Because \(p_{2,1}=\pi_{2,1}=1/6\), every nonzero residual coefficient in a score is \(6\) times its DDD sign.  Let the three score coordinates be \(j_1=((2,2),3)\), \(j_2=((2,2),\infty)\), and \(j_3=((2,3),\infty)\).  Coordinate \(j_1\) uses \(rU_i\) in the four cells with cohort \(2\) or \(3\); coordinate \(j_2\) uses \(rU_i\) in the four cells with cohort \(2\) or \(\infty\); and coordinate \(j_3\) uses \(rV_i\) in those same latter four cells.  Mutual exclusivity and the conditional covariances above therefore give
\[
 \Omega=r^2
 \begin{pmatrix}
 24&12&0\\
 12&24&6\\
 0&6&24
 \end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3/8&3/16&0\\
 3/16&3/8&3/32\\
 0&3/32&3/8
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The eigenvalues of the integer matrix are \(24\) and \(24\pm6\sqrt5\).  Hence \(\Omega\) is positive definite, its smallest eigenvalue exceeds \(1/8\), and its largest eigenvalue is less than \(8\).  The score-spectrum restriction holds with \(\kappa=1/8\), so the constructed law belongs to \(\mathcal M\).

The first block of \(\Omega\) has equal diagonal entries, so its componentwise optimal loading is \((\omega_1/2,\omega_1/2)'\); the singleton second block has loading \(\omega_2\).  With \(N=(1,-1,0)'\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 N'\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}=-\frac{3\omega_2}{32},
 \qquad
 N'\Omega N=\frac38.
\]
Applying the exact frontier identity yields
\[
 V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\frac{(3\omega_2/32)^2}{3/8}
 =\frac{3\omega_2^2}{128},
\]
which is strictly positive when \(\omega_2\ne0\).  This supplies the required bounded compatible strict-gap class.
