Write \(D_{i,j}=\widehat\Psi_{i,j}-\Psi_j(O_i)\).  Lemma \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) gives
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}P_nD_{i,j}^2=o_P(1).
\]
The fourth-moment assumptions, overlap, and the explicit bounded inverse-propensity loadings imply \(E_P|\Psi_j(O_i)|^4<\infty\) for every fixed coordinate.  Therefore \(\Psi_j\Psi_q\) has finite variance.  Independence and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
 P_n\{\Psi_j(O_i)\Psi_q(O_i)\}-\Omega_{jq}=o_P(1).
\]

Using \(\widehat\Psi_j=\Psi_j+D_j\), the following identity is exact:
\[
 \widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{raw}}_{jq}-\Omega_{jq}
 =\{P_n(\Psi_j\Psi_q)-\Omega_{jq}\}
  +P_n(D_j\widehat\Psi_q)+P_n(\Psi_jD_q).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{raw}}_{jq}-\Omega_{jq}|
 &\le |P_n(\Psi_j\Psi_q)-\Omega_{jq}|\\
 &\quad +(P_nD_j^2)^{1/2}(P_n\widehat\Psi_q^2)^{1/2}
 +(P_n\Psi_j^2)^{1/2}(P_nD_q^2)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Now \(P_n\Psi_j^2=O_P(1)\), while
\[
 P_n\widehat\Psi_q^2
 \le2P_n\Psi_q^2+2P_nD_q^2=O_P(1).
\]
Every term on the right is therefore \(o_P(1)\).  Since \(M\) is fixed,
\[
 \|\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{raw}}-\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le M\max_{j,q}|\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{raw}}_{jq}-\Omega_{jq}|
 =o_P(1).
\]

For completeness, Frobenius projection onto a closed convex set is nonexpansive.  Indeed, the two variational inequalities for projections of matrices \(A\) and \(C\) imply
\[
 \|\Pi(A)-\Pi(C)\|_F^2
 \le \langle \Pi(A)-\Pi(C),A-C\rangle_F
 \le \|\Pi(A)-\Pi(C)\|_F\|A-C\|_F.
\]
Take \(C=\Omega\), noting that \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_+^M\) and hence \(\Pi(C)=C\).  Fixed dimensionality then gives
\[
 \|\widehat\Omega-\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \|\widehat\Omega-\Omega\|_F
 \le \|\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{raw}}-\Omega\|_F=o_P(1).
\]
Finally, \(\lambda_n=n^{-1/4}\to0\), so
\[
 \|\widehat\Omega_\lambda-\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \|\widehat\Omega-\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\lambda_n=o_P(1).
\]
