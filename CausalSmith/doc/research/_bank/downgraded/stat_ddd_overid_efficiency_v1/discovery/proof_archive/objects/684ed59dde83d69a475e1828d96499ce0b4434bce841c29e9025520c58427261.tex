Each block of \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) sums to \(\omega_k\), and hence
\[
B^{\prime}b^{\mathrm{sep}}=\omega.
\]
The columns of \(N\) are linearly independent, satisfy \(B^{\prime}N=0\), and number \(M-|\mathcal K|\). Since \(B\) has rank \(|\mathcal K|\), these columns form a basis of \(\ker(B^{\prime})\). Consequently every loading satisfying \(B^{\prime}b=\omega\) is uniquely expressible as
\[
b=b^{\mathrm{sep}}+N\gamma.
\]
Substitution into the population variance criterion gives the exact identity
\[
b^{\prime}\Omega b
=(b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
+2\gamma^{\prime}N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
+\gamma^{\prime}N^{\prime}\Omega N\gamma.
\]
The score-spectrum assumption makes \(\Omega\) positive definite. Full column rank of \(N\) therefore makes \(N^{\prime}\Omega N\) positive definite, so the unique minimizing coefficient is
\[
\gamma_*=-(N^{\prime}\Omega N)^{-1}N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}},
\qquad
b^{\mathrm{joint}}=b^{\mathrm{sep}}+N\gamma_*.
\]
Completing the square yields
\[
V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
=(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}
(N^{\prime}\Omega N)^{-1}
(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}).
\]
The first-order condition is \(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}=0\). Therefore the cross term in the \(\Omega\)-norm expansion vanishes, and
\[
V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
=(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
\Omega(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}}).
\]
Both expressions vanish exactly when \(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}=0\). By the canonical construction of each block \(N_k\), one has \(N_k^{\prime}z_k=0\) if and only if all coordinates of \(z_k\) equal the final coordinate in that block. Taking \(z=\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) proves the blockwise-constancy characterization. This argument uses only \(Q\in\mathcal M\) and thus establishes the asserted population identity without any learner condition.

Now impose all estimator-level conditions stated in the theorem. Theorem \(\ref{thm:joint-system-limit}\) and Lemmas \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) and \(\ref{lem:fitted-covariance-consistency}\) give
\[
\sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}
=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
\qquad
\widehat\Omega_{\lambda}\to_P\Omega.
\]
Every principal block \(\Omega_{kk}\) is positive definite. Continuity of inversion and of the normalizing denominator consequently gives
\[
\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}
\to_P b^{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The definition of the blockwise loading gives \(B^{\prime}\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}=\omega\) exactly. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sep}}-\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\}
&=(\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}
\sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}\\
&=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n(b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The i.i.d. central limit argument used in Theorem \(\ref{thm:joint-system-limit}\) therefore gives asymptotic variance \(V_{\mathrm{sep}}\). That theorem gives the corresponding expansion and variance \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}\) for the joint estimator. The positive quadratic form proved above is thus exactly the asymptotic variance reduction under these estimator-level hypotheses, and it is strict precisely under the stated nonconstancy condition.

When \(\mathcal K=\{k\}\), the definition of \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) gives
\[
\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
=\omega_k\frac{\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1},
\]
which is constant in the only block. Thus \(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}=0\), the two loadings agree, and the variance gap is zero. Finally, for \(g=2\), the definition of the outcome change gives \(\Delta Y_{i,k}=Y_{it}-Y_{i1}\), so every score and covariance entry uses baseline period \(1\).
