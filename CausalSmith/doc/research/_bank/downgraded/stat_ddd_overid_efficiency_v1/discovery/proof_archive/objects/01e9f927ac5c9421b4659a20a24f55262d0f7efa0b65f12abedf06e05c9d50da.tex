By Lemmas \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) and \(\ref{lem:fitted-covariance-consistency}\), in which the regression envelopes are derived from the remaining primitives,
\[
 Z_n:=\sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega_\lambda\to_P\Omega.
\]
The learner rates in this display are exactly the theorem-level conditional hypotheses; the argument neither constructs a learner nor asserts that these rates hold uniformly over \(\mathcal M\).

The columns of \(B\) have nonempty, pairwise disjoint row supports, so \(B\) has full column rank. Since \(\Omega\) is positive definite, \(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B\) is positive definite. Continuity of matrix inversion on the positive-definite cone gives
\[
 \widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B
 (B^{\prime}\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 \to_P
 \Omega^{-1}B(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 =b^{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Also \(B^{\prime}\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}=\omega\) exactly. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\}
 &=(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
   \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}\\
 &=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\frac1{\sqrt n}
   \sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(Z_n=O_P(1)\) and \(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}}=o_P(1)\).

Let \(Z_i=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Psi(O_i)\). The variables \(Z_i\) are independent and identically distributed, centered, and have variance \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}\). Their fourth moments are finite by the primitive outcome fourth moment, overlap, and the regression-moment derivation in the score-expansion lemma. For every \(r>0\),
\[
 E_P\!\left[Z_i^2\mathbf 1\{|Z_i|>r\sqrt n\}\right]
 \le\frac{E_PZ_i^4}{r^2n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Apply Lemma \(\ref{lem:lindeberg-feller-central-limit}\) to \(Z_{n,i}=Z_i/\sqrt n\). Its variance sum is \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}\), and the last display verifies its Lindeberg condition. Hence
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_i\rightsquigarrow N(0,V_{\mathrm{joint}}).
\]
Slutsky's lemma gives the asserted limit law.

For the finite-span optimization, consider
\[
 \mathcal L(b,\lambda)=b^{\prime}\Omega b
 -2\lambda^{\prime}(B^{\prime}b-\omega).
\]
The first-order equations are \(b=\Omega^{-1}B\lambda\) and \(B^{\prime}b=\omega\), whence
\[
 \lambda=(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega,
 \qquad
 b=\Omega^{-1}B(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 =b^{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Strict convexity of \(b\mapsto b^{\prime}\Omega b\) makes this feasible solution unique. Since \(\mathbf 1^{\prime}\omega=1\), the constraint forces \(b^{\mathrm{joint}}\ne0\). The spectrum bound gives
\[
 V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \ge\kappa\|b^{\mathrm{joint}}\|_2^2>0.
\]

Covariance and loading consistency imply
\[
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
 \widehat\Omega\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \to_PV_{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
The causal-identification lemma gives \(\tau_\omega(Q)=\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\). Studentization and Slutsky's lemma therefore yield
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-\tau_\omega(Q)\}}
      {\sqrt{\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}}}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1).
\]
Continuity of the standard normal distribution at \(\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\) proves
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}P\{\tau_\omega(Q)\in\mathcal C_{n,1-\alpha}\}=1-\alpha.
\]
Because \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}>0\), the event \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}=0\) has probability tending to zero, so the finite-sample zero-variance convention is asymptotically inactive.

If \(g=2\), Definition \(\ref{def:joint-estimator}\) uses \(\Delta Y_{i,k}=Y_{it}-Y_{i1}\) in both oracle and fitted scores. If \(\mathcal K=\{k\}\), then \(B=\mathbf 1\), \(\omega_k=1\), and
\[
 b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\frac{\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}
 =b^{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The feasible loading, estimator, and variance formulas then coincide with the published within-\(k\) formulas. Every optimality statement proved here is confined to the finite constant retained-score span.
