Let two labelled invariants be equivalent. By Definition \(\ref{def:labelled-incidence-handle}\), a label-preserving equivalence identifies their comparison labels and agrees exactly in \(\mathfrak C(P)\), \(N\), and \(\omega\), as well as in the retained primitive-residual labels and signed incidences. For a compatible law, \(\mathfrak C(P)=\Omega(P)\); moreover, the score-spectrum condition in the definition of \(\mathcal M\) makes \(\Omega(P)\) positive definite. Every principal block \(\mathfrak C_{kk}(P)\) is therefore invertible. Hence the blockwise formula
\[
 b_k^{\mathrm{sep}}(P)
 =\omega_k\frac{\mathfrak C_{kk}(P)^{-1}\mathbf 1}
 {\mathbf 1^{\prime}\mathfrak C_{kk}(P)^{-1}\mathbf 1}
\]
is computable from the retained invariant: its denominator is strictly positive because \(\mathfrak C_{kk}(P)^{-1}\) is positive definite. It follows that
\[
 D([\mathfrak H(P)])
 :=N^{\prime}\mathfrak C(P)b^{\mathrm{sep}}(P)
\]
is evaluable and takes the same value on equivalent labelled invariants. Its zero and nonzero inverse images are disjoint, exhaustive, and equal to the displayed sets \(\mathscr E_0\) and \(\mathscr E_+\), respectively.

Now restrict attention to an invariant induced by a compatible law \(Q\in\mathcal M\), and write \(P=Q_O\). Theorem \(\ref{thm:separate-joint-frontier}\) gives
\[
 V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =D([\mathfrak H(P)])^{\prime}
 \{N^{\prime}\Omega(P)N\}^{-1}
 D([\mathfrak H(P)]).
\]
When \(N\) has at least one column, it has full column rank by construction, so positive definiteness of \(\Omega(P)\) implies that \(N^{\prime}\Omega(P)N\) is positive definite. Consequently the quadratic form is zero exactly when \(D([\mathfrak H(P)])=0\), and it is strictly positive otherwise. When \(N\) has no columns, the contrast and the quadratic are both zero, so only \(\mathscr E_0\) can occur. This proves the claimed zero-versus-positive frontier on the two bookkeeping classes.

It remains to prove that the strict class is nonempty in the stated architecture. Lemma \(\ref{lem:retained-cell-count-bounds}\) gives \(L_{\mathrm{ret}}=6\) for this architecture and therefore verifies the feasibility of the specified overlap constant:
\[
 0<\frac18<\frac16=L_{\mathrm{ret}}^{-1}.
\]
Choose
\[
 \rho=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \delta=\frac1{16}.
\]
These choices satisfy \(0<|\rho|<\sqrt7/3\) and \(0<|\delta|<1/8\). Theorem \(\ref{thm:open-strict-gap-family}\) therefore supplies a bounded compatible full-data law \(Q_{1/2,1/16}\in\mathcal M\), with nonzero constant post-adoption effect \(1/16\), for which
\[
 \Omega=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3/8&3/16&0\\
 3/16&3/8&3/32\\
 0&3/32&3/8
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 b^{\mathrm{sep}}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \omega_1/2\\
 \omega_1/2\\
 \omega_2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The same theorem evaluates the exact variance gain as
\[
 V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\frac{3(1/2)^2\omega_2^2}{32}
 =\frac{3\omega_2^2}{128}>0,
\]
where strict positivity follows from \(\omega_2\ne0\). By the frontier just proved, this law has \(D([\mathfrak H(P)])\ne0\), so its invariant lies in \(\mathscr E_+\). Because it is obtained by specializing the open two-parameter family of Theorem \(\ref{thm:open-strict-gap-family}\), it is one point of the claimed open nonzero-effect family. This completes the proof.
