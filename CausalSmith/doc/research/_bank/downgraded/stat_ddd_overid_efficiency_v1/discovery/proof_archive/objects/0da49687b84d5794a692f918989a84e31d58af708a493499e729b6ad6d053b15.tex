Fix a score coordinate \(j=(k,c)\), write \(k=(g,t)\), and abbreviate \(I_{a,s}=\mathbf 1\{G_i=a,S_i=s\}\).  Four mutually exclusive cells have conditional probabilities at least \(\varepsilon\), so each retained \(p_{a,s}\) lies in
\[
 [\varepsilon,1-3\varepsilon]\subset[\varepsilon/2,1-\varepsilon/2]
\]
almost surely.  Therefore \(C_\varepsilon(p_{a,s})=p_{a,s}\).  Projection onto an interval is one-Lipschitz, and hence, fold by fold,
\[
 \|\widetilde p_{a,s,-f}-p_{a,s}\|_{P_X,4}
 \le \|\widehat p_{a,s,-f}-p_{a,s}\|_{P_X,4}=O_P(a_n).
\]
On the clipping interval the map \((u,v)\mapsto u/v\) is Lipschitz with a constant depending only on \(\varepsilon\).  The fourth-moment assumptions, Jensen's inequality, and overlap imply uniformly over the finitely many retained cells that
\[
 \|\Delta Y_{i,k}-m_{a,s,k}(X_i)\|_{P(\cdot\mid G_i=a,S_i=s),4}=O(1).
\]
H\"older's inequality consequently gives
\[
 \left\|I_{a,s}\left(
 \frac{\widetilde p_{g,1}}{\widetilde p_{a,s}}-
 \frac{p_{g,1}}{p_{a,s}}\right)
 \{\Delta Y_k-m_{a,s,k}\}\right\|_{P,2}=O_P(a_n)
\]
and
\[
 \left\|I_{a,s}\frac{\widetilde p_{g,1}}{\widetilde p_{a,s}}
 (\widehat m_{a,s,k}-m_{a,s,k})\right\|_{P,2}=O_P(b_n).
\]
The analogous bound for the fitted target contrast is at most the sum of its four regression-error norms.  Thus the fitted numerator summand differs in \(L_2(P)\) from its oracle counterpart by \(O_P(a_n+b_n)=o_P(1)\).

For completeness, the orthogonal bias cancellation can be seen directly.  Let \(e_{a,s}=\widehat m_{a,s,k}-m_{a,s,k}\), and condition on a training sample and on \(X=x\).  The fitted numerator's conditional mean minus the oracle numerator's conditional mean equals
\[
 \sum_{a,s}\sigma_{a,s}^{g,c}
 \left\{p_{g,1}-\widetilde p_{g,1}\frac{p_{a,s}}{\widetilde p_{a,s}}\right\}e_{a,s}
 =\sum_{a,s}\sigma_{a,s}^{g,c}
 \frac{p_{g,1}(\widetilde p_{a,s}-p_{a,s})
       -p_{a,s}(\widetilde p_{g,1}-p_{g,1})}
      {\widetilde p_{a,s}}e_{a,s}.
\]
By H\"older's inequality, clipping, and the two learner rates, its unconditional absolute value is \(O_P(a_nb_n)=o_P(n^{-1/2})\).

Let
\[
 A^0_{i,j}=I_{g,1}d_{k,c}(X_i)
 +p_{g,1}(X_i)\sum_{a,s}\sigma_{a,s}^{g,c}
 \frac{I_{a,s}}{p_{a,s}(X_i)}
 \{\Delta Y_{i,k}-m_{a,s,k}(X_i)\}.
\]
The identification lemma gives \(E_P[A^0_{i,j}]=\pi_{g,1}\Theta_k(P)\), and direct comparison with the definition of \(\Psi_j\) gives the exact identity
\[
 \frac{A^0_{i,j}-\Theta_k(P)I_{g,1}}{\pi_{g,1}}=\Psi_j(O_i).
\]
Conditional on each training sample, validation observations are independent.  Conditional Chebyshev bounds and the preceding \(L_2(P)\) estimate show, for fixed \(F\),
\[
 \sqrt n(P_n-P)\{\widehat A_j-A^0_j\}=O_P(a_n+b_n)=o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \sqrt nP\{\widehat A_j-A^0_j\}=o_P(1).
\]
Also \(P_nI_{g,1}-\pi_{g,1}=O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Since \(\pi_{g,1}\) lies a fixed positive distance inside the clipping interval, target-share clipping is inactive with probability tending to one.  Expanding the resulting ratio therefore yields
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu_j-\Theta_k(P)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi_j(O_i)+o_P(1).
\]
There are only \(M\) fixed coordinates, so the conclusion holds jointly and is the displayed vector expansion.

Finally, compare the displayed fitted influence coordinate term by term with \(\Psi_j\).  The denominator converges to \(\pi_{g,1}>0\), \(\widehat\mu_j-\Theta_k(P)=o_P(1)\), the fitted contrast has \(L_2(P)\) error \(O_P(b_n)\), and the two residual bounds above are \(o_P(1)\).  Conditional Markov bounds on each validation fold pass these population \(L_2(P)\) bounds to empirical squared norms.  Hence
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{\widehat\Psi_{i,j}-\Psi_j(O_i)\}^2=o_P(1).
\]
Taking the maximum over fixed \(M\) completes the proof.
