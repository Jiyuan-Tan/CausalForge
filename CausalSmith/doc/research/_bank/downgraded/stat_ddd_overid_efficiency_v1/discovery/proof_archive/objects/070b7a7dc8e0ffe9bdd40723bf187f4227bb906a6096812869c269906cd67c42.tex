Fix the pair consisting of \(Q\) and the supplied learner sequences. Because a retained score contains the four distinct cells \((g,0),(g,1),(c,0),(c,1)\), the retained-cell definition gives \(L_{\mathrm{ret}}\ge4\). The overlap domain therefore implies \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\), and overlap with the other three cells gives \(p_{a,s}\le1-3\varepsilon\). Lemmas \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) and \(\ref{lem:fitted-covariance-consistency}\) consequently give
\[
 Z_n:=\sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega\to_P\Omega,
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega_\lambda\to_P\Omega.
\]
These statements derive the fitted-score and covariance behavior from the displayed learner-rate hypotheses; they do not construct the learners.

Each column of \(B\) has a nonempty row support, and supports of distinct columns are disjoint. Thus \(B\) has full column rank. The score-spectrum assumption makes \(\Omega\) positive definite, so \(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B\) is positive definite. Continuity of matrix inversion yields
\[
 \widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B
 (B^{\prime}\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 \to_P
 \Omega^{-1}B(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 =b^{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
For every sample, \(\widehat\Omega_\lambda\) is positive definite because \(\widehat\Omega\) is positive semidefinite and \(\lambda_n>0\). The same rank argument makes \(B^{\prime}\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B\) positive definite. Together with propensity and target-share clipping, this proves that every denominator and inverse in \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}\) is defined for every realized sample. Direct multiplication gives \(B^{\prime}\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}=\omega\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-
 \Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\}
 &=(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}\\
 &=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\frac1{\sqrt n}
 \sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(Z_n=O_P(1)\) and \(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}}=o_P(1)\).

Put \(Z_i=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Psi(O_i)\). Independent sampling and Lemma \(\ref{lem:causal-identification}\) show that the \(Z_i\) are independent and identically distributed with mean zero and variance
\[
 E_P[Z_i^2]=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Omega
 b^{\mathrm{joint}}=V_{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
The fourth-moment and overlap derivations in Lemma \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) imply \(E_P|\Psi_j(O_i)|^4<\infty\) for every fixed coordinate. Fixed \(M\) therefore gives \(E_P|Z_i|^4<\infty\). For \(Z_{n,i}=Z_i/\sqrt n\),
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}(Z_{n,i})=V_{\mathrm{joint}},
\]
and, for every \(r>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i=1}^nE_P[Z_{n,i}^2\mathbf 1\{|Z_{n,i}|>r\}]
 &=E_P[Z_i^2\mathbf 1\{|Z_i|>r\sqrt n\}]\\
 &\le \frac{E_P[Z_i^4]}{r^2n}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Lindeberg--Feller theorem in Lemma \(\ref{lem:lindeberg-feller-central-limit}\) gives
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_i
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,V_{\mathrm{joint}}).
\]
If \(r_n=o_P(1)\), then for every \(h>0\) and continuity point \(x\) of the limiting normal distribution,
\[
 P(X_n\le x-h)-P(|r_n|>h)
 \le P(X_n+r_n\le x)
 \le P(X_n\le x+h)+P(|r_n|>h),
\]
where \(X_n=n^{-1/2}\sum_iZ_i\). Taking limits and then \(h\downarrow0\) absorbs the remainder and proves the asserted limit law.

For optimality, consider
\[
 \mathcal L(b,\lambda)=b^{\prime}\Omega b
 -2\lambda^{\prime}(B^{\prime}b-\omega).
\]
Its first-order conditions are \(b=\Omega^{-1}B\lambda\) and \(B^{\prime}b=\omega\), so
\[
 \lambda=(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega,
 \qquad
 b=\Omega^{-1}B(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 =b^{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Strict convexity of \(b\mapsto b^{\prime}\Omega b\) makes this feasible solution the unique minimizer. Because \(\mathbf 1^{\prime}\omega=1\), the constraint \(B^{\prime}b^{\mathrm{joint}}=\omega\) implies \(b^{\mathrm{joint}}\ne0\). The spectrum bound gives
\[
 V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \ge\kappa\|b^{\mathrm{joint}}\|_2^2>0.
\]
The loading and covariance convergences imply
\[
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
 \widehat\Omega\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \to_PV_{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Lemma \(\ref{lem:causal-identification}\) gives \(\tau_\omega(Q)=\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\). Therefore
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-\tau_\omega(Q)\}}
 {\sqrt{\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}}}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1).
\]
Indeed, \(\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{joint}}/\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}}-1=o_P(1)\); its product with the tight unstudentized standardized statistic is \(o_P(1)\), and the same distribution-function sandwich transfers the limit. Continuity of the standard normal law at \(\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\) proves
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}P\{\tau_\omega(Q)\in\mathcal C_{n,1-\alpha}\}
 =1-\alpha.
\]
Since \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}>0\) and \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}\to_PV_{\mathrm{joint}}\), the event \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}=0\) has probability tending to zero, so the finite-sample zero-variance convention is asymptotically inactive.

When \(g=2\), the outcome-change definition gives \(\Delta Y_{i,k}=Y_{it}-Y_{i1}\) in both oracle and fitted scores. If \(\mathcal K=\{k\}\), then \(B=\mathbf 1\) and \(\omega_k=1\), so
\[
 b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\frac{\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}
 {\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}
 =b^{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The feasible loading, estimator, and variance formulas reduce accordingly. Every conclusion is pointwise for the fixed law--learner pair and confined to the finite constant retained-score span; no tangent-space or full semiparametric-efficiency assertion has been used.
