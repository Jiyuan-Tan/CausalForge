Let \(X_i=x_0\) almost surely and let \((G_i,S_i)\) be uniform on
\[
 \{(2,0),(2,1),(3,0),(3,1),(\infty,0),(\infty,1)\}.
\]
On a common probability space, take \(U_i\) and \(E_i\) to be independent Rademacher variables.  Take \(H_i\), independent of \((U_i,E_i,G_i,S_i)\), with
\[
 P(H_i=1)=\frac{1+\rho}{2},
 \qquad
 P(H_i=-1)=\frac{1-\rho}{2},
\]
and take \((U_i,E_i,H_i)\) independent of \((G_i,S_i)\).  Define
\[
 V_i=
 \begin{cases}
 E_i,&G_i\in\{2,3\},\\
 U_iH_i,&G_i=\infty.
 \end{cases}
\]
For every cohort-eligibility cell,
\[
 E[U_i\mid G_i=a,S_i=s]=E[V_i\mid G_i=a,S_i=s]=0,
 \qquad
 E[U_i^2\mid G_i=a,S_i=s]=E[V_i^2\mid G_i=a,S_i=s]=1,
\]
and
\[
 E[U_iV_i\mid G_i=a,S_i=s]
 =\begin{cases}
 0,&a\in\{2,3\},\\
 \rho,&a=\infty.
 \end{cases}
\]

Put \(r=1/8\), \(Z_{i1}=0\), \(Z_{i2}=rU_i\), and \(Z_{i3}=rV_i\).  For \(s\in\{0,1\}\) and \(t\in\{1,2,3\}\), define
\[
 Y_{it}(\infty,s)=Z_{it},
\]
and, for \(a\in\{2,3\}\), define
\[
 Y_{it}(a,s)=Z_{it}+s\delta\mathbf 1\{t\ge a\}.
\]
Finally set \(Y_{it}=Y_{it}(G_i,S_i)\).  Consistency holds by construction.  The added term is zero before adoption, proving no anticipation, and is identically zero for ineligible units, proving no ineligible spillover.

Under never enabling, the two eligibility states have the same history \((Z_{i1},Z_{i2},Z_{i3})\).  The conditional centering above makes both sides of every retained pairwise conditional DDD restriction equal to zero.  The target effects and retained contrasts are
\[
 d_{(2,2),3}=d_{(2,2),\infty}
 =d_{(2,3),\infty}=\delta,
 \qquad
 \theta_{(2,2)}=\theta_{(2,3)}=\delta.
\]
Thus every target-cell contrast term \(d_{k,c}(X_i)-\Theta_k(P)\) is zero.  The effect is nonzero because \(\delta\ne0\), but it disappears after residual centering.

All six cohort-eligibility probabilities equal \(1/6>1/8\), so overlap holds.  Moreover,
\[
 \max_t|Y_{it}|\le r+|\delta|<\frac14.
\]
This proves the outcome fourth-moment condition with \(C_4=1\).  Every retained true outcome-change regression has absolute value at most \(|\delta|<1/8\), so the true-regression fourth-moment envelope also holds.

Because \(p_{2,1}=\pi_{2,1}=1/6\), every nonzero residual loading is six times its DDD sign.  Order the coordinates as
\[
 j_1=((2,2),3),\qquad
 j_2=((2,2),\infty),\qquad
 j_3=((2,3),\infty).
\]
The residual for \(j_1\) is \(rU_i\) in the four cohort-\(2\) or cohort-\(3\) cells; the residual for \(j_2\) is \(rU_i\) in the four cohort-\(2\) or never-enabled cells; and the residual for \(j_3\) is \(rV_i\) in the four cohort-\(2\) or never-enabled cells.  Mutually exclusive cells have zero covariance.  A shared cell with matching signs contributes
\[
 \frac16\,6^2r^2\operatorname{Cov}(U_i,V_i\mid G_i=a,S_i=s)
 =6r^2\operatorname{Cov}(U_i,V_i\mid G_i=a,S_i=s).
\]
There are four cells on each diagonal, two cohort-\(2\) cells shared by \(j_1,j_2\), no cell with nonzero \(U_i,V_i\) covariance shared by \(j_1,j_3\), and two never-enabled cells contributing covariance \(\rho\) to \(j_2,j_3\).  Since \(r^2=1/64\), Lemma \(\ref{lem:shared-covariance-expansion}\) gives
\[
 \Omega(\rho)=r^2
 \begin{pmatrix}
 24&12&0\\
 12&24&12\rho\\
 0&12\rho&24
 \end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3/8&3/16&0\\
 3/16&3/8&3\rho/16\\
 0&3\rho/16&3/8
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are
\[
 \frac3{16}\left(2,\ 2-\sqrt{1+\rho^2},\ 2+\sqrt{1+\rho^2}\right).
\]
The restriction \(|\rho|<\sqrt7/3\) implies \(\sqrt{1+\rho^2}<4/3\).  Hence the smallest eigenvalue is greater than \(1/8\), while the largest is less than \(5/8<8\).  Thus
\[
 \frac18I_3\preceq\Omega(\rho)\preceq8I_3.
\]
All defining conditions of \(\mathcal M\) have now been verified, so \(Q_{\rho,\delta}\in\mathcal M\).

The first covariance block has equal diagonal entries and symmetric off-diagonal entries.  Its componentwise optimum therefore assigns equal weights, while the second block is a singleton:
\[
 b^{\mathrm{sep}}=
 \begin{pmatrix}\omega_1/2\\ \omega_1/2\\ \omega_2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(N=(1,-1,0)^{\prime}\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 N^{\prime}\Omega N=\frac38,
 \qquad
 N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}=-\frac{3\rho\omega_2}{16}.
\]
The contrast correction from Theorem \(\ref{thm:separate-joint-frontier}\) is therefore
\[
 -(N^{\prime}\Omega N)^{-1}N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
 =\frac{\rho\omega_2}{2}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =b^{\mathrm{sep}}+\frac{\rho\omega_2}{2}N
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 \omega_1/2+\rho\omega_2/2\\
 \omega_1/2-\rho\omega_2/2\\
 \omega_2
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
and
\[
 V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\frac{(3\rho\omega_2/16)^2}{3/8}
 =\frac{3\rho^2\omega_2^2}{32}>0.
\]
Both \(\rho\) and \(\delta\) range over nonempty open sets, and the class conditions and strict inequality persist throughout the displayed ranges.  This proves the asserted nondegenerate open family.
