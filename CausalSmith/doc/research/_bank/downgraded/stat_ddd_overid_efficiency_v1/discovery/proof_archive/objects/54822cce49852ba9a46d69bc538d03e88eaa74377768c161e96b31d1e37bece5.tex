Lemmas \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) and \(\ref{lem:fitted-covariance-consistency}\) give
\[
 Z_n:=\sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega_\lambda\to_P\Omega.
\]
The learner rates in this display are precisely the theorem-level hypotheses; the argument neither constructs such learners nor asserts uniform achievability over \(\mathcal M\).  The regression envelopes used in these lemmas were derived from the law primitives.

The columns of \(B\) have nonempty, pairwise disjoint row supports, so \(B\) has full column rank.  The spectrum assumption makes \(\Omega\) positive definite, and hence \(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B\) is positive definite.  Continuity of inversion on the positive-definite cone gives
\[
 \widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B
 (B^{\prime}\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 \to_P
 \Omega^{-1}B(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 =b^{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Furthermore \(B^{\prime}\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}=\omega\) exactly.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\}
 &=(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
   \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}\\
 &=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\frac1{\sqrt n}
   \sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(Z_n=O_P(1)\) and \(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}}=o_P(1)\).

Let \(Z_i=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Psi(O_i)\).  These variables are i.i.d., centered by Lemma \(\ref{lem:causal-identification}\), and have variance \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}\).  Their fourth moments are finite by the moment derivation in Lemma \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\).  For \(Z_{n,i}=Z_i/\sqrt n\),
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}(Z_{n,i})=V_{\mathrm{joint}},
\]
and, for every \(r>0\),
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^nE_P[Z_{n,i}^2\mathbf 1\{|Z_{n,i}|>r\}]
 =E_P[Z_i^2\mathbf 1\{|Z_i|>r\sqrt n\}]
 \le\frac{E_P[Z_i^4]}{r^2n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Lemma \(\ref{lem:lindeberg-feller-central-limit}\) therefore gives
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_i\rightsquigarrow N(0,V_{\mathrm{joint}}).
\]
To pass through the \(o_P(1)\) remainder without another external result, let \(X_n=n^{-1/2}\sum_iZ_i\), let \(r_n=o_P(1)\), and fix a continuity point \(x\) of the \(N(0,V_{\mathrm{joint}})\) distribution.  For every \(h>0\),
\[
 P(X_n\le x-h)-P(|r_n|>h)
 \le P(X_n+r_n\le x)
 \le P(X_n\le x+h)+P(|r_n|>h).
\]
Taking limits and then letting \(h\downarrow0\) proves that \(X_n+r_n\) has the same limit.  This establishes the displayed limit law.

For the finite-span optimization, consider
\[
 \mathcal L(b,\lambda)=b^{\prime}\Omega b
 -2\lambda^{\prime}(B^{\prime}b-\omega).
\]
The first-order equations are \(b=\Omega^{-1}B\lambda\) and \(B^{\prime}b=\omega\).  Thus
\[
 \lambda=(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega,
 \qquad
 b=\Omega^{-1}B(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega=b^{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Strict convexity of \(b\mapsto b^{\prime}\Omega b\) makes this feasible solution the unique minimizer.  Since \(\mathbf 1^{\prime}\omega=1\), feasibility implies \(b^{\mathrm{joint}}\ne0\).  The spectrum bound therefore gives
\[
 V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \ge\kappa\|b^{\mathrm{joint}}\|_2^2>0.
\]

Covariance and loading consistency imply
\[
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
 \widehat\Omega\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \to_PV_{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Lemma \(\ref{lem:causal-identification}\) gives \(\tau_\omega(Q)=\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\).  The resulting studentized statistic satisfies
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-\tau_\omega(Q)\}}
      {\sqrt{\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}}}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1)
\]
on an event whose probability tends to one.  More explicitly, the unstudentized normal convergence just proved implies tightness of its standardized version, while \(\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{joint}}/\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}}-1=o_P(1)\).  Their product is \(o_P(1)\), and the preceding distribution-function sandwich transfers the standard-normal limit to the studentized statistic.  Continuity of the standard normal distribution at \(\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\) proves
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}P\{\tau_\omega(Q)\in\mathcal C_{n,1-\alpha}\}=1-\alpha.
\]
Because \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}>0\) and \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}\to_PV_{\mathrm{joint}}\), the event \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}=0\) has probability tending to zero.  Thus the zero-variance convention is asymptotically inactive.

If \(g=2\), Definition \(\ref{def:joint-estimator}\) uses \(\Delta Y_{i,k}=Y_{it}-Y_{i1}\) in the oracle and fitted scores.  If \(\mathcal K=\{k\}\), then \(B=\mathbf 1\), \(\omega_k=1\), and
\[
 b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\frac{\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}
 =b^{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The feasible loading, estimator, and variance formulas consequently reduce to the published within-\(k\) objects.  Every optimality statement above is confined to the finite constant retained-score span.
