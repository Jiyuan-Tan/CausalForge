Each block of \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) sums to \(\omega_k\), so \(B'b^{\mathrm{sep}}=\omega\).  The columns of \(N\) are linearly independent, satisfy \(B'N=0\), and number \(M-|\mathcal K|\); hence they form a basis of \(\ker(B')\).  Every feasible loading is therefore uniquely of the form
\[
 b=b^{\mathrm{sep}}+N\gamma.
\]
Substitution into the variance criterion gives
\[
 b'\Omega b
 =b^{\mathrm{sep}\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
 +2\gamma'N'\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
 +\gamma'N'\Omega N\gamma.
\]
Positive definiteness of \(\Omega\) and full column rank of \(N\) make \(N'\Omega N\) positive definite.  Thus the unique minimizing coefficient is
\[
 \gamma_* =-(N'\Omega N)^{-1}N'\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}},
 \qquad
 b^{\mathrm{joint}}=b^{\mathrm{sep}}+N\gamma_*.
\]
Completing the square gives
\[
 V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(N'\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}})'(N'\Omega N)^{-1}
   (N'\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}).
\]
The first-order condition also says \(N'\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}=0\).  Hence the cross term in the \(\Omega\)-norm decomposition vanishes and
\[
 V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})'
   \Omega(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}}).
\]
Both expressions vanish exactly when \(N'\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}=0\).  By the canonical definition of \(N_k\), \(N_k'z_k=0\) if and only if every coordinate of the block vector \(z_k\) equals its last coordinate.  Taking \(z=\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) proves the stated blockwise-constancy characterization.

The score and covariance lemmas also apply to the componentwise loading.  Covariance consistency and continuous blockwise inversion give \(\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}\to_Pb^{\mathrm{sep}}\); the score expansion then yields an asymptotic linear representation with influence \(b^{\mathrm{sep}\prime}\Psi\) and variance \(V_{\mathrm{sep}}\).  Therefore the positive quadratic form above is exactly the asymptotic variance reduction of the joint estimator, and it is strict precisely under the stated nonconstancy condition.

When \(\mathcal K=\{k\}\), the definition of \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) gives
\[
 \Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
 =\omega_k\frac{\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1'\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1},
\]
which is constant within the sole block.  Thus \(N'\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}=0\), the two loadings and variances coincide, and the within-cell result follows.  The baseline assertion for \(g=2\) is immediate from \(\Delta Y_{i,k}=Y_{it}-Y_{i1}\).
