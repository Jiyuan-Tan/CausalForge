By Lemmas \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) and \(\ref{lem:fitted-covariance-consistency}\),
\[
 Z_n:=\sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega_\lambda\to_P\Omega.
\]
The incidence matrix \(B\) has full column rank because its columns have nonempty, pairwise disjoint row supports.  Since \(\Omega\) is positive definite, \(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B\) is positive definite.  Matrix inversion is continuous on the positive-definite cone, and hence
\[
 \widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B
 (B^{\prime}\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 \to_P
 \Omega^{-1}B(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 =b^{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Moreover, \(B^{\prime}\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}=\omega\) exactly for every sample.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\}
 &=(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
   \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}\\
 &=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
   \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The replacement of \(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}\) by its limit is valid because \(Z_n=O_P(1)\).

Let \(Z_i=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Psi(O_i)\).  These variables are i.i.d., centered, and have variance \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}\).  Their fourth moment is finite by overlap and the primitive fourth-moment envelopes.  In particular, for every \(r>0\),
\[
 E_P\left[Z_i^2\mathbf 1\{|Z_i|>r\sqrt n\}\right]
 \le \frac{E_P[Z_i^4]}{r^2n}\longrightarrow0,
\]
which verifies the Lindeberg condition for the normalized i.i.d. sum.  The Lindeberg--Feller central limit theorem and Slutsky's lemma consequently give
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,V_{\mathrm{joint}}).
\]

We next prove the optimization claim directly.  The strictly convex Lagrangian for minimizing \(b^{\prime}\Omega b\) subject to \(B^{\prime}b=\omega\) is
\[
 \mathcal L(b,\lambda)=b^{\prime}\Omega b-2\lambda^{\prime}(B^{\prime}b-\omega).
\]
Its first-order equations give \(b=\Omega^{-1}B\lambda\) and
\(\lambda=(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega\).  Thus its unique solution is \(b^{\mathrm{joint}}\).  Because the entries of \(\omega\) sum to one, \(\omega\ne0\); the constraint implies \(b^{\mathrm{joint}}\ne0\).  The spectrum bound then yields
\[
 V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \ge\kappa\|b^{\mathrm{joint}}\|_2^2>0.
\]

Covariance consistency and loading consistency imply
\[
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 = (\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
   \widehat\Omega\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \to_PV_{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
The causal-identification lemma gives
\(\tau_\omega(Q)=\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\).  Studentization and Slutsky's lemma therefore yield
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-\tau_\omega(Q)\}}
      {\sqrt{\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}}}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1).
\]
Continuity of the standard normal distribution at \(\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\) proves
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}P\{\tau_\omega(Q)\in\mathcal C_{n,1-\alpha}\}=1-\alpha.
\]
Since the variance limit is strictly positive, the event \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}=0\) has probability tending to zero, so the finite-sample zero-variance convention is asymptotically inactive.

If \(g=2\), the definition of the outcome change gives \(\Delta Y_{i,k}=Y_{it}-Y_{i1}\), and the same change appears in the fitted score.  Finally, if \(\mathcal K=\{k\}\), then \(B=\mathbf 1\), the singleton aggregate weight equals one, and
\[
 b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\frac{\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}
 =b^{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The feasible formulas and their variance formulas then coincide as well, proving the stated reduction.
