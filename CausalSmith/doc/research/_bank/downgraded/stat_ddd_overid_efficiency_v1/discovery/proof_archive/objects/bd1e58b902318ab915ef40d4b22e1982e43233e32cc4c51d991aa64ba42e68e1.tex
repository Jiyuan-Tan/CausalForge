Fix a score coordinate \(j=(k,c)\), write \(k=(g,t)\), and abbreviate \(I_{a,s}=\mathbf 1\{G_i=a,S_i=s\}\), \(p_a^s=p_{a,s}\), \(m_a^s=m_{a,s,k}\), and \(\pi=\pi_{g,1}\).  All constants below may depend on the fixed objects \(F,T,\mathcal K,M,\varepsilon\), but not on \(n\).

First consider clipping.  The four events \((G_i,S_i)=(g,0),(g,1),(c,0),(c,1)\) are disjoint.  Conditional overlap therefore implies, for every one of these cells and almost every \(x\),
\[
 \varepsilon\le p_a^s(x)\le 1-3\varepsilon
 <1-\varepsilon/2.
\]
Thus \(C_\varepsilon(p_a^s)=p_a^s\).  Since projection onto an interval is one-Lipschitz,
\[
 \|\widetilde p_a^s-p_a^s\|_{P_X,4}
 \le \|\widehat p_a^s-p_a^s\|_{P_X,4}=O_P(a_n)
\]
fold by fold.  On \([\varepsilon/2,1-\varepsilon/2]^2\), the map \((u,v)\mapsto u/v\) is Lipschitz with a constant depending only on \(\varepsilon\).  Consequently,
\[
 \left\|\frac{\widetilde p_g^1}{\widetilde p_a^s}
       -\frac{p_g^1}{p_a^s}\right\|_{P_X,4}=O_P(a_n).
\]

The outcome fourth-moment condition gives \(E_P|\Delta Y_{i,k}|^4<\infty\).  Because every retained cell has unconditional probability at least \(\varepsilon\), conditioning on such a cell changes this bound by at most the factor \(\varepsilon^{-1}\).  The true-regression envelope therefore yields
\[
 \|\Delta Y_{i,k}-m_a^s(X_i)\|_{P(\cdot\mid a,s),4}=O(1).
\]
Hölder's inequality and the preceding ratio bound now imply
\[
 \left\|I_{a,s}\left(
 \frac{\widetilde p_g^1}{\widetilde p_a^s}
 -\frac{p_g^1}{p_a^s}\right)
 \{\Delta Y_{i,k}-m_a^s(X_i)\}\right\|_{P,2}=O_P(a_n),
\]
and bounded clipping gives
\[
 \left\|I_{a,s}\frac{\widetilde p_g^1}{\widetilde p_a^s}
 \{\widehat m_a^s(X_i)-m_a^s(X_i)\}\right\|_{P,2}=O_P(b_n).
\]
The fitted contrast error \(\widehat d_{k,c}-d_{k,c}\) is the signed sum of the four corresponding regression errors and hence is \(O_P(b_n)\) in \(L_4(P_X)\).

Define the oracle numerator summand
\[
 A_j(O_i)=I_{g,1}d_{k,c}(X_i)
 +p_{g,1}(X_i)
 \sum_{a,s}\sigma_{a,s}^{g,c}
 \frac{I_{a,s}}{p_{a,s}(X_i)}
 \{\Delta Y_{i,k}-m_{a,s,k}(X_i)\},
\]
where the sum is over the four retained cells, and let \(\widehat A_{i,j}\) be its fitted version in \(\mathrm{Def.}\ \ref{def:joint-estimator}\).  The bounds just proved give, conditionally on each training sample,
\[
 \|\widehat A_{i,j}-A_j(O_i)\|_{P,2}=O_P(a_n+b_n)=o_P(1).
\]
There is also an exact orthogonality calculation.  Put \(e_{a,s}=\widehat m_{a,s,k}-m_{a,s,k}\).  Conditional on \(X_i=x\), residual centering gives
\[
 E_P[\widehat A_{i,j}-A_j(O_i)\mid X_i=x]
 =\sum_{a,s}\sigma_{a,s}^{g,c}
 \left\{p_{g,1}(x)-\widetilde p_{g,1}(x)
              \frac{p_{a,s}(x)}{\widetilde p_{a,s}(x)}\right\}e_{a,s}(x).
\]
The bracket is bounded in \(L_4(P_X)\) by a constant times the sum of the two relevant propensity errors.  Hölder's inequality therefore gives
\[
 \left|E_P[\widehat A_{i,j}-A_j(O_i)\mid\text{training fold}]\right|
 =O_P(a_nb_n)=o_P(n^{-1/2}).
\]

For a validation fold, condition on its training sample.  The observations in that fold are independent of the fitted functions.  Conditional Chebyshev, the preceding \(L_2\) bound, and the fact that \(F\) is fixed show that the sum over folds of
\[
 \sqrt n\,(P_{n,f}-P)(\widehat A_{j,f}-A_j)
\]
is \(o_P(1)\).  The conditional-bias display shows that its noncentered counterpart is also \(o_P(1)\).  Hence
\[
 \sqrt n,P_n(\widehat A_j-A_j)=o_P(1).
\]

Let \(\bar I_g=P_nI_{g,1}\).  Overlap gives \(\pi\in[\varepsilon,1-3\varepsilon]\).  Since \(I_{g,1}\) is Bernoulli, Chebyshev gives \(\bar I_g-\pi=O_P(n^{-1/2})\), and therefore \(\widetilde\pi_{g,1}=\bar I_g\) with probability tending to one.  By causal identification and residual centering,
\[
 E_P A_j=\pi\Theta_k(P),
 \qquad
 A_j(O_i)-\Theta_k(P)I_{g,1}=\pi\Psi_j(O_i).
\]
On the event where clipping is inactive at \(\bar I_g\), it follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu_j-\Theta_k(P)\}
 &=\frac{\sqrt n,P_n\{A_j-\Theta_k(P)I_{g,1}\}}
         {\bar I_g}
   +\frac{\sqrt n,P_n(\widehat A_j-A_j)}{\bar I_g}\\
 &=\frac{\pi}{\bar I_g}\frac1{\sqrt n}
   \sum_{i=1}^n\Psi_j(O_i)+o_P(1)\\
 &=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi_j(O_i)+o_P(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The final equality uses \(\bar I_g/\pi\to_P1\) and the fact that the mean-zero sum is \(O_P(1)\), which follows from its finite variance.  Since \(M\) is fixed, stacking gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1).
\]

It remains to compare fitted and oracle influence coordinates.  On the same probability-tending-to-one event, subtract the two displayed influence formulas.  The difference is a finite sum of: the denominator error \(\bar I_g^{-1}-\pi^{-1}\); the fitted-contrast error; the centering error \(\widehat\mu_j-\Theta_k(P)\); the propensity-ratio error multiplied by a true residual; and a bounded fitted ratio multiplied by a regression error.  Conditional Markov and the foldwise \(L_4\) bounds used above yield
\[
 P_n\{\widehat\Psi_{i,j}-\Psi_j(O_i)\}^2
 \le O_P(1)\left\{(\bar I_g-\pi)^2
 +|\widehat\mu_j-\Theta_k(P)|^2+a_n^2+b_n^2\right\}=o_P(1).
\]
Here the empirical second moments of the oracle residual terms are \(O_P(1)\) by the fourth-moment conditions, and the expansion already proved gives \(\widehat\mu_j-\Theta_k(P)=O_P(n^{-1/2})+o_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Taking the maximum over the fixed \(M\) coordinates proves
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \{\widehat\Psi_{i,j}-\Psi_j(O_i)\}^2=o_P(1),
\]
as required.
