The full-data law \(Q\) defines the potential outcomes and causal target, while all score, nuisance, covariance, estimator, diagnostic, and limit calculations use the observed-data pushforward \(P=Q_O\); the causal identification lemma is the bridge. The horizon, target support, comparison sets, score dimension, folds, and event-study weights are fixed. The estimator result requires the explicit i.i.d., foldwise-independence, fourth-moment-envelope, \(L_4(P_X)\) nuisance-rate, product-rate, overlap, spectrum, and ridge conditions stated in the joint-system theorem; the universal population frontier itself does not require learner conditions. The strict-gap family uses \(T=3\), feasible overlap \(\varepsilon=1/8\), \(0<|\rho|<\sqrt7/3\), and \(0<|\delta|<1/8\), and it satisfies the spectrum bound with \(\kappa=1/8\). The results exclude growing score systems, singular population covariance, data-adaptive target weights, unbalanced panels, and repeated cross-sections. The classification and strict-gap construction are closed; the genuinely broader full conditional-moment semiparametric efficiency problem remains outside the paper's claim.
