Each block of \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) sums to \(\omega_k\). Therefore
\[
B^{\prime}b^{\mathrm{sep}}=\omega.
\]
By construction, the columns of \(N\) are linearly independent and satisfy \(B^{\prime}N=0\). There are \(M-|\mathcal K|\) such columns, while \(B\) has rank \(|\mathcal K|\) because its columns have nonempty, pairwise disjoint row supports. Hence the columns of \(N\) form a basis of \(\ker(B^{\prime})\). Every loading satisfying \(B^{\prime}b=\omega\) consequently has a unique representation
\[
b=b^{\mathrm{sep}}+N\gamma.
\]

Substitution into the population variance criterion gives the exact equality
\[
b^{\prime}\Omega b
=(b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
+2\gamma^{\prime}N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
+\gamma^{\prime}N^{\prime}\Omega N\gamma.
\]
Membership of \(Q\) in \(\mathcal M\) and the score-spectrum condition make \(\Omega\) positive definite. Since \(N\) has full column rank, \(N^{\prime}\Omega N\) is positive definite. Completing the square therefore shows that the unique minimizing coefficient is
\[
\gamma_*=-\bigl(N^{\prime}\Omega N\bigr)^{-1}N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The resulting loading is the unique minimizer of \(b^{\prime}\Omega b\) subject to \(B^{\prime}b=\omega\). By the Lagrange-multiplier calculation in Theorem \(\ref{thm:joint-system-limit}\), that unique minimizer is \(b^{\mathrm{joint}}\). Thus
\[
b^{\mathrm{joint}}=b^{\mathrm{sep}}+N\gamma_*.
\]
Evaluating the completed square at \(\gamma_*\) yields
\[
V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}
=(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}
 (N^{\prime}\Omega N)^{-1}
 (N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}).
\]
The first-order condition at the minimizer is \(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}=0\). Hence the cross term in the following \(\Omega\)-norm expansion is zero:
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathrm{sep}}
&=(b^{\mathrm{joint}}+b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
  \Omega
  (b^{\mathrm{joint}}+b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})\\
&=V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 +(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
   \Omega(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}}).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves both population identities. Positive definiteness shows that either displayed gap is zero if and only if
\[
N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}=0,
\]
which is in turn equivalent to \(b^{\mathrm{sep}}=b^{\mathrm{joint}}\), and hence to joint optimality of the componentwise loading.

For a vector \(z\) partitioned conformably with the comparison sets, the canonical block \(N_k\) satisfies \(N_k^{\prime}z_k=0\) if and only if all coordinates of \(z_k\) equal its final coordinate. Applying this observation to \(z=\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}\) proves the blockwise-constancy characterization. More specifically, the definition of the componentwise loading gives
\[
\Omega_{kk}b_k^{\mathrm{sep}}
=\frac{\omega_k}{\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega_{kk}^{-1}\mathbf 1}\,\mathbf 1.
\]
Thus the within-block contribution is already constant across the comparison cohorts in block \(k\), and any nonconstancy of
\[
(\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}})_k
=\Omega_{kk}b_k^{\mathrm{sep}}
 +\sum_{\ell\ne k}\Omega_{k\ell}b_\ell^{\mathrm{sep}}
\]
is exactly nonconstancy of the net cross-block covariance contribution. This proves the asserted strict-gap criterion without learner conditions.

Now impose the estimator-level conditions in the theorem. Lemmas \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) and \(\ref{lem:fitted-covariance-consistency}\) give
\[
\sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
\qquad
\widehat\Omega_\lambda\to_P\Omega,
\qquad
\widehat\Omega\to_P\Omega.
\]
Every principal block \(\Omega_{kk}\) is positive definite. Continuity of matrix inversion and of the positive normalizing denominators therefore gives
\[
\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}\to_Pb^{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The componentwise formula also gives \(B^{\prime}\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}=\omega\) for every sample. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sep}}-\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\}
&=(\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}
  \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
  (b^{\mathrm{sep}})^{\prime}\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The i.i.d. central-limit argument in Theorem \(\ref{thm:joint-system-limit}\) applies to this fixed linear combination because the primitive fourth-moment and overlap conditions give it finite variance. It follows that \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sep}}\) has asymptotic variance \(V_{\mathrm{sep}}\). Theorem \(\ref{thm:joint-system-limit}\) gives the corresponding expansion with asymptotic variance \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}\) for \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}\). The positive quadratic form established above is therefore exactly the asymptotic variance reduction, and it is strictly positive precisely under the stated within-block nonconstancy condition.

The same convergence statements prove the diagnostic claim. Theorem \(\ref{thm:joint-system-limit}\) gives \(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}\to_Pb^{\mathrm{joint}}\), while the preceding block-inversion argument gives \(\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}\to_Pb^{\mathrm{sep}}\), and Lemma \(\ref{lem:fitted-covariance-consistency}\) gives \(\widehat\Omega\to_P\Omega\). Since \(\widehat\Omega\) is the projection of \(\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{raw}}\) onto the positive-semidefinite cone,
\[
\widehat\Delta
=(\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}-\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
  \widehat\Omega
  (\widehat b^{\mathrm{sep}}-\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})
\ge0
\]
for every realized sample. Continuity of the finite-dimensional quadratic-form map yields
\[
\widehat\Delta
\to_P
(b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
 \Omega
 (b^{\mathrm{sep}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})
=V_{\mathrm{sep}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}},
\]
where the last equality is the population identity proved above. Thus \(\widehat\Delta\) is a nonnegative, consistent descriptive diagnostic of the efficiency loss within the constant retained-score span. This conclusion supplies neither a convergence rate nor a test or confidence interval, and it makes no claim of full semiparametric efficiency.

If \(\mathcal K=\{k\}\), the weights sum to one, so \(\omega_k=1\), and
\[
\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}
=\frac{\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}
\]
is constant in the only block. Hence \(N^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{sep}}=0\), the two loadings agree, and the variance gap is zero. Finally, when \(g=2\), the definition of the outcome change gives \(\Delta Y_{i,k}=Y_{it}-Y_{i1}\), so every score and covariance entry uses baseline period \(1\).
