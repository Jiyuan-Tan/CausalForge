Fix the pair consisting of \(Q\) and the supplied learner sequences. Because a retained score contains the four distinct cells \((g,0),(g,1),(c,0),(c,1)\), the retained-cell definition gives \(L_{\mathrm{ret}}\ge4\). The overlap domain therefore implies \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\), and overlap for the other three cells gives the per-score upper bound \(p_{a,s}(x)\le1-3\varepsilon\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). Hence the clipping and moment arguments in Lemmas \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) and \(\ref{lem:fitted-covariance-consistency}\) apply pointwise to the fixed law and learner sequences and give
\[
 Z_n:=\sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega\to_P\Omega,
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega_\lambda\to_P\Omega.
\]
These conclusions derive the needed fitted-score and covariance behavior from the learner-rate hypotheses; they do not construct the learners.

Each column of \(B\) has nonempty row support because every \(\mathcal J_k\) is nonempty, and the supports of distinct columns are disjoint. Thus \(B\) has full column rank. The score-spectrum assumption makes \(\Omega\) positive definite, so, for every nonzero \(u\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal K|}\),
\[
 u^{\prime}B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}Bu
 =(Bu)^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}(Bu)>0.
\]
Consequently \(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B\) is positive definite. Continuity of matrix inversion on the positive-definite cone yields
\[
\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
=\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B
 (B^{\prime}\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
\to_P
\Omega^{-1}B(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
=b^{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
For every sample, \(\widehat\Omega_\lambda\) is positive definite because \(\widehat\Omega\) is positive semidefinite and \(\lambda_n>0\). The same full-rank argument makes \(B^{\prime}\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B\) positive definite. Together with propensity and target-share clipping, this proves that every denominator and inverse in \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}\) is defined for every realized sample. Direct multiplication gives
\[
 B^{\prime}\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =B^{\prime}\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B
 (B^{\prime}\widehat\Omega_\lambda^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 =\omega.
\]
Because \(\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)=\omega^{\prime}\Theta(P)\), it follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\}
&=(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
  \sqrt n\{\widehat\mu-B\Theta(P)\}\\
&=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\frac1{\sqrt n}
  \sum_{i=1}^n\Psi(O_i)+o_P(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the score expansion and finite second moments imply \(Z_n=O_P(1)\), so \((\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}-b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}Z_n=o_P(1)\).

Put \(Z_i=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Psi(O_i)\). Independent sampling and Lemma \(\ref{lem:causal-identification}\) imply that the \(Z_i\) are independent and identically distributed with mean zero and variance
\[
 E_P[Z_i^2]=(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =V_{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
The fourth-moment and overlap derivations in Lemma \(\ref{lem:cross-fitted-score-expansion}\) imply \(E_P|\Psi_j(O_i)|^4<\infty\) for every fixed coordinate. Fixed \(M\) therefore gives \(E_P|Z_i|^4<\infty\). For the triangular array \(Z_{n,i}=Z_i/\sqrt n\),
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}(Z_{n,i})=V_{\mathrm{joint}},
\]
and, for every \(r>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i=1}^nE_P[Z_{n,i}^2\mathbf 1\{|Z_{n,i}|>r\}]
 &=E_P[Z_i^2\mathbf 1\{|Z_i|>r\sqrt n\}]\\
 &\le\frac{E_P[Z_i^4]}{r^2n}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Lindeberg--Feller central limit theorem in Lemma \(\ref{lem:lindeberg-feller-central-limit}\) therefore gives
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_i\rightsquigarrow N(0,V_{\mathrm{joint}}).
\]
To absorb the \(o_P(1)\) remainder without an additional theorem, let \(X_n=n^{-1/2}\sum_iZ_i\) and let \(r_n=o_P(1)\). For every \(h>0\) and every continuity point \(x\) of the limiting normal distribution,
\[
 P(X_n\le x-h)-P(|r_n|>h)
 \le P(X_n+r_n\le x)
 \le P(X_n\le x+h)+P(|r_n|>h).
\]
Taking lower and upper limits and then sending \(h\downarrow0\) proves the asserted limit law.

It remains to establish the finite-span optimum. The feasible set is nonempty because \(B\) has full column rank. Consider the Lagrangian
\[
 \mathcal L(b,\lambda)=b^{\prime}\Omega b
 -2\lambda^{\prime}(B^{\prime}b-\omega).
\]
Its first-order conditions are \(b=\Omega^{-1}B\lambda\) and \(B^{\prime}b=\omega\), whence
\[
 \lambda=(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega,
 \qquad
 b=\Omega^{-1}B(B^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}B)^{-1}\omega
 =b^{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Strict convexity of \(b\mapsto b^{\prime}\Omega b\) makes this feasible solution the unique minimizer. Because \(\mathbf 1^{\prime}\omega=1\), the constraint \(B^{\prime}b^{\mathrm{joint}}=\omega\) implies \(b^{\mathrm{joint}}\ne0\). The spectrum bound then gives
\[
 V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}\Omega b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \ge\kappa\|b^{\mathrm{joint}}\|_2^2>0.
\]

The convergences of the loading and covariance imply
\[
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}
 =(\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}})^{\prime}
  \widehat\Omega\widehat b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 \to_PV_{\mathrm{joint}}.
\]
Lemma \(\ref{lem:causal-identification}\) gives \(\tau_\omega(Q)=\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{agg}}(P)\). Hence
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{joint}}-\tau_\omega(Q)\}}
      {\sqrt{\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}}}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1).
\]
Indeed, \(\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{joint}}/\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}}-1=o_P(1)\), and multiplying this error by the tight unstudentized standardized statistic is \(o_P(1)\); the same distribution-function sandwich used above transfers the limit. Since the standard normal distribution is continuous at \(\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\),
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}P\{\tau_\omega(Q)\in\mathcal C_{n,1-\alpha}\}
 =P\{|N(0,1)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}=1-\alpha.
\]
Because \(V_{\mathrm{joint}}>0\) and \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}\to_PV_{\mathrm{joint}}\),
\[
 P\{\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}=0\}
 \le P\{|\widehat V_{\mathrm{joint}}-V_{\mathrm{joint}}|\ge V_{\mathrm{joint}}/2\}
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
so the finite-sample zero-variance convention is asymptotically inactive.

When \(g=2\), the outcome-change definition gives \(\Delta Y_{i,k}=Y_{it}-Y_{i1}\) in both oracle and fitted scores. If \(\mathcal K=\{k\}\), then \(B=\mathbf 1\) and \(\omega_k=1\), so
\[
 b^{\mathrm{joint}}
 =\frac{\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}\mathbf 1}
 =b^{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The feasible loading, estimator, and variance formulas reduce accordingly. Every conclusion is pointwise for the fixed law and learner sequences and is confined to the finite constant retained-score span; no tangent-space or full semiparametric-efficiency assertion has been used.
