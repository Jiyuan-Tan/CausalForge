\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}selection\_\allowbreak{}intervention\_\allowbreak{}complete\_\allowbreak{}pag}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Pre-treatment selection couples the indicator-free observational MAG on \(V\) and all indicator-bearing nonobservational MAGs on \(V\cup\{\zeta_k\}\) through one basal DAG.  The note defines the canonical SI-PAG by endpoint invariance over Dai et al.'s full causally sufficient compatibility class and computes it exactly by target-residual certificate enumeration: with \(m_0(\mathbf M)=|E(\mathcal M^{(0)})|\), only observed-skeleton pairs receive an absent/either-orientation state, giving at most \(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D\) candidates and actual opposing common-basal witnesses for every circle.  The dense envelope is \(3^{\binom D2}2^D\), while \(m_0(\mathbf M)=O(D)\) yields \(\exp(O(D))\); both are non-tight, nonpolynomial bounds with polynomial dependence on \(K\).  Construction of a complete fixed finite local SI orientation calculus remains open, and no polynomial claim is made for discovering the supplied oracle MAGs by conditional-independence or invariance queries.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Dai et al. characterize pre-treatment-selection interventional equivalence and prove oracle CDIS soundness, but do not give the canonical endpoint-invariance representative, a complete population procedure, or a complete fixed local orientation calculus.

\paragraph{Niche.} Ordinary FCI completeness treats one MAG class, and the unknown-target soft-intervention theory of Jaber et al. excludes selection; neither supplies a common-basal certificate spanning Dai's indicator-free observational branch and all indicator-bearing regimes.  Luo et al.'s F-PAG instead concerns post-treatment selection and different endpoint semantics.

\paragraph{Fill.} The note gives an exact finite population CDIS map on Dai et al.'s full class by enumerating only basal DAG states on the supplied observational skeleton and ancestor-closed selection summaries, deriving rather than enumerating co-selection and target residuals, and taking endpointwise feasible-mark invariance.  It proves the candidate bound \(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D\), the runtime \(O(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D[D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}])\), and paired common-basal witnesses, while leaving fixed-local-calculus completeness as a separate open question.

\section{Related work}

DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention supplies the published pre-treatment-selection twin-graph semantics, regime-MAG formulas, adjacency-and-unshielded-collider equivalence theorem, two-variable selection-versus-causation witness, and a sound but not complete CDIS procedure.  The present result uses that substrate to prove an exact global certificate enumerator and the sparse input-conditioned factor \(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D\); it does not claim that Dai's open local-rule problem is solved.  Zhang2008CompleteFCI supplies completeness architecture for one observational MAG class.  JaberKocaogluShanmugamBareinboim2020 handles unknown soft targets without selection and therefore does not provide common-basal selected witnesses.  LuoEtAl2026PostTreatmentSelection gives a complete F-PAG under post-treatment selection, whose square marks and inducing-node semantics differ from Dai's basal-selection SI-PAG.  AliRichardsonSpirtes2009 supplies ordinary MAG equivalence substrate.  VenkateswaranPerkovic2026 and WangDuZhou2025 concern background-knowledge or listing rules rather than the full selected interventional-twin compatibility class.  ColomboMaathuisKalischRichardson2012 is relevant only to a later finite-sample rung.  SrivatsanEtAl2020SciPlex motivates the sciPlex2 A549 oracle workflow, but no transcriptomic sampling guarantee is claimed here.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-dag]\label{ass:finite-dag}
For candidate \(\eta\), \(\mathcal G_\eta\) is a finite directed acyclic graph on \(V\cup S_\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite DAG model, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:causal-sufficiency]\label{ass:causal-sufficiency}
Candidate \(\eta\) contains no latent common cause of two vertices in \(V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Dai et al. causal sufficiency, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selection-binary]\label{ass:selection-binary}
Every vertex in \(S_\eta\) is binary.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Dai et al. binary selection-node convention, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selection-childless]\label{ass:selection-childless}
Every vertex in \(S_\eta\) has no child in \(\mathcal G_\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Dai et al. childless selection nodes, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selection-parents]\label{ass:selection-parents}
Every parent in \(\mathcal G_\eta\) of a vertex in \(S_\eta\) belongs to \(V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Dai et al. observed-parent selection nodes, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selected-stratum]\label{ass:selected-stratum}
The observed stratum for candidate \(\eta\) conditions on \(S_\eta=\mathbf 1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Dai et al. selected-stratum convention, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pretreatment-selection]\label{ass:pretreatment-selection}
The conditioned selection-node set in \(\operatorname{Tw}(\eta,0)\) and in \(\operatorname{Tw}(\eta,k)\) for every \(k\in\mathcal K_+\) equals the basal copy \(S_\eta^*\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Dai et al. pre-treatment-selection timing, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observational-target]\label{ass:observational-target}
The observational regime has empty intervention target \(I_\eta^{(0)}=\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Observational baseline regime, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:soft-intervention-modularity]\label{ass:soft-intervention-modularity}
For each \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), every structural mechanism outside \(I_\eta^{(k)}\) and its exogenous input are shared with the basal world.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Unknown-target soft-intervention modularity, \cite{JaberKocaogluShanmugamBareinboim2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observational-equivalence-match]\label{ass:observational-equivalence-match}
The indicator-free observational projection \(\mathcal M_\eta^{(0)}\) on \(V\) is Markov equivalent to the supplied observational representative \(\mathcal M^{(0)}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Dai et al. observational compatibility, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:interventional-equivalence-match]\label{ass:interventional-equivalence-match}
For every \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), the projected twin MAG \(\mathcal M_\eta^{(k)}\) on \(V\cup\{\zeta_k\}\) is Markov equivalent to the supplied nonobservational representative \(\mathcal M^{(k)}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Dai et al. interventional-twin-graph compatibility, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:compatible-input]\label{ass:compatible-input}
The supplied family has at least one compatible model: \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\ne\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonempty graphical compatibility class, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:twin-graph]\label{def:twin-graph}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Separated observational and interventional twin graphs}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Define \(\operatorname{Tw}(\eta,0)\) as the indicator-free selected observational DAG on reality vertices \(X_V\) and basal selection copies \(S_\eta^*\): copy \(\mathcal G_\eta[V]\) onto \(X_V\), copy each selection-parent edge into \(S_\eta^*\), and introduce no basal system copy, shared-noise vertex, or intervention-indicator vertex. Separately, for each \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), form \(\operatorname{Tw}(\eta,k)\) on reality vertices \(X_V\), basal copies \(X_i^*\) and shared noises \(\varepsilon_i\) for \(i\in\operatorname{de}_{\mathcal G_\eta}(I_\eta^{(k)})\), basal selection copies \(S_\eta^*\), and \(\zeta_k\). Copy each edge of \(\mathcal G_\eta[V]\) into each available world, route unaffected parents into affected basal children, copy selection-parent edges only into \(S_\eta^*\), add \(\varepsilon_i\to X_i\) and \(\varepsilon_i\to X_i^*\), and add \(\zeta_k\to X_i\) exactly for \(i\in I_\eta^{(k)}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{eta}; \texttt{k}.
\end{definition}

In either branch, join two retained vertices exactly when an inducing path exists after the stated marginalization and conditioning, then place a tail at an endpoint exactly when that endpoint is an ancestor of the other endpoint or of \(S_\eta^*\), and an arrowhead otherwise.

\begin{definition}[def:oracle-input-encoding]\label{def:oracle-input-encoding}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite oracle-\allowbreak{}MAG encoding}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Encode \(\mathcal M^{(0)}\) by one \(D\times D\) adjacency-mark matrix on \(V\), and encode each \(\mathcal M^{(k)}\), \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), by one \((D+1)\times(D+1)\) adjacency-mark matrix on \(V\cup\{\zeta_k\}\). The representation length is \(L_{\mathrm{in}}=D^2+K(D+1)^2\), and regime zero has no row or column for an indicator.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Mfam}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:finite-rule-encoding]\label{def:finite-rule-encoding}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite SI-\allowbreak{}rule encoding and matcher}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Encode each \(r\in\mathsf R_{\mathrm{SI}}\) by a finite tuple of \(a_r\) typed regime placeholders, \(b_r\) typed vertex placeholders, adjacency and endpoint-mark premises, finite-state uncovered-path or discriminating-path predicates, and a nonempty list of circle-to-tail or circle-to-arrow updates. Regime placeholders are typed as either \(\{0\}\) or \(\mathcal K_+\), and an indicator term may occur only with the latter type. Match \(r\) by enumerating its regime and vertex assignments and deciding every path predicate by breadth-first search on its finite product-state graph. One exhaustive scan has cost \(T_r(D,K)=O((K+1)^{a_r}(D+1)^{b_r+3})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{RSI}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:monotone-closure]\label{def:monotone-closure}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Monotone rule closure}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathsf R}(\mathcal P)=\bigvee_{t\ge0}\mathsf R^t(\mathcal P)\), where the join is taken in the endpoint information order \(\circ\prec\mathrm{tail}\) and \(\circ\prec\mathrm{arrow}\), and every strict step fixes at least one previously circular endpoint.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{mathsf R}; \texttt{mathcal P}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:closure-runtime-certificate]\label{def:closure-runtime-certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Oracle-\allowbreak{}input closure schedule}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Materialize \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{init}}(\mathbf M)\) from the encoded oracle family by enumerating triples, then scan the finitely encoded rules in a fixed order and apply every consistent match found in a scan. Repeat after each scan that makes at least one strict endpoint update and stop after the first no-update scan. Record the certificate consisting of the ordered strict updates and the terminal no-match result. Its runtime envelope is \(T_{\mathrm{cl}}(D,K)=O((K+1)(D+1)^3+(N_{\mathrm{end}}+1)\sum_{r\in\mathsf R_{\mathrm{SI}}}T_r(D,K))\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Mfam}; \texttt{RSI}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:normalized-dai-certificate]\label{def:normalized-dai-certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Input-\allowbreak{}conditioned normalized target-\allowbreak{}residual Dai certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Given the encoded oracle family \(\mathbf M\), write \(S_0(\mathbf M)=E(\mathcal M^{(0)})\).  For every \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), define the supplied indicator-neighborhood and its decomposition at \(A\) by
\[
Z_k(\mathbf M)=\{j\in V:\zeta_k\text{ is adjacent to }j\text{ in }\mathcal M^{(k)}\},
\qquad
R_k(\mathbf M,A)=Z_k(\mathbf M)\cap A,
\qquad
O_k(\mathbf M,A)=Z_k(\mathbf M)\setminus A.
\]
For a directed acyclic graph \(E\) on \(V\) and \(A\subseteq V\), define
\[
B(E,A)=\left\{\{i,j\}\in\binom V2:
i\to j\in E\ \text{or}\ j\to i\in E\ \text{or}\
\exists c\in A:\ i\to c\leftarrow j\ \text{in }E
\right\},
\qquad
Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)=S_0(\mathbf M)\setminus B(E,A).
\]
Call \((E,A)\) residual-admissible when
\[
\operatorname{an}_E(A)=A,
\qquad
B(E,A)\subseteq S_0(\mathbf M),
\qquad
Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\subseteq\binom A2,
\]
and, for every \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), \(R_k(\mathbf M,A)\) is descendant-closed in the induced DAG \(E[A]\), equivalently
\[
r\in R_k(\mathbf M,A)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{de}_E(r)\cap A\subseteq R_k(\mathbf M,A).
\]
For a residual-admissible pair, set
\[
\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)=
\left\{r\in R_k:
\bigl(\operatorname{an}_E(r)\setminus\{r\}\bigr)\cap R_k=\varnothing
\right\},
\]
\[
T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)=O_k(\mathbf M,A)\cup\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k(\mathbf M,A)),
\qquad
F^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)=\operatorname{de}_E\!\left(T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\right).
\]
The input-conditioned normalized target-residual Dai certificate is the uniquely derived tuple
\[
\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A)=
\left(E,A,Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A),
\bigl(T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\bigr)_{k\in\mathcal K_+}\right).
\]
Neither \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\) nor any target set is independently enumerated.  When \(K=0\), the tuple has no nonobservational coordinate and no objects \(Z_k,R_k,O_k,T^*_{k,\mathbf M},F^*_{k,\mathbf M}\) are introduced; set only the observational target to \(T_0^*=\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Mfam}; \texttt{V}; \texttt{Kplus}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:complete-cdis]\label{def:complete-cdis}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Complete population target-\allowbreak{}residual certificate CDIS}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} From the encoded oracle family \(\mathbf M\), compute the observational skeleton \(S_0(\mathbf M)=E(\mathcal M^{(0)})\) and its size \(m_0(\mathbf M)=|S_0(\mathbf M)|\).  Enumerate in a fixed lexicographic order only pairs \((E,A)\) as follows: for each unordered pair in \(S_0(\mathbf M)\), assign one of the three basal states absent, oriented one way, or oriented the other way; force every unordered pair outside \(S_0(\mathbf M)\) to be absent in \(E\); and enumerate \(A\subseteq V\).  For each pair derive \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\) and, for every \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), derive \(Z_k(\mathbf M)\), \(R_k(\mathbf M,A)\), \(O_k(\mathbf M,A)\), \(T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\), and \(F^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\) as in \(\texttt{def:normalized-dai-certificate}\).  Reject the pair unless \(E\) is acyclic, \(A\) is ancestor-closed, the residual-skeleton conditions hold, and every \(R_k(\mathbf M,A)\) is descendant-closed in \(E[A]\).  For each survivor form the unique target-residual certificate
\[
\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A)=
\left(E,A,Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A),
\bigl(T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\bigr)_{k\in\mathcal K_+}\right)
\]
and retain it precisely when \(\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A),\mathbf M)=1\).  Neither the co-selection residual nor any target set is independently enumerated.  If no certificate passes, return \(\mathsf{incompatible}\).  Otherwise, for every \(e=(k,u;uv)\in\mathcal E(\mathbf M)\), form
\[
B_e(\mathbf M)=\left\{\operatorname{mark}_e\bigl(\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A))\bigr):
\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A),\mathbf M)=1\right\}
\subseteq\{\mathrm{tail},\mathrm{arrow}\}.
\]
Set the output mark at \(e\) to the unique element when \(B_e(\mathbf M)\) is a singleton and to a circle when it contains both marks.  For every circle, retain the first accepted tail certificate and the first accepted arrowhead certificate.  Define \(\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)\) to be this partial-graph family together with those coupled certificates.  Its observational component is on \(V\), and only a component indexed by \(k\in\mathcal K_+\) is on \(V\cup\{\zeta_k\}\).  When \(K=0\), enumerate only \((E,A)\), construct only the observational component, and introduce no nonobservational target-residual objects.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Mfam}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:dai-certificate-checker]\label{def:dai-certificate-checker}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite input-\allowbreak{}conditioned target-\allowbreak{}residual Dai checker}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Given \(\mathbf M\), enumerate a pair \((E,A)\) and reject it unless it is residual-admissible under \(\texttt{def:normalized-dai-certificate}\).  For a survivor derive, rather than enumerate,
\[
Q=Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A),
\qquad
T_k^*=T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A),
\qquad
F_k^*=F^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)=\operatorname{de}_E(T_k^*)
\quad(k\in\mathcal K_+),
\]
and set
\[
\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A)=
\left(E,A,Q,(T_k^*)_{k\in\mathcal K_+}\right).
\]
Construct \(\mathbf N(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M})=(\mathcal N^{(0)},\ldots,\mathcal N^{(K)})\) as follows.  The observational graph has vertex set \(V\); distinct \(i,j\in V\) are adjacent exactly when
\[
i\to j\in E\text{ or }j\to i\in E,
\quad\text{or}\quad
\exists c\in A:\ i\to c\leftarrow j\text{ in }E,
\quad\text{or}\quad
\{i,j\}\in Q.
\]
Its adjacency set is \(B(E,A)\cup Q=S_0(\mathbf M)\).  At an observational adjacency, the endpoint at \(i\) is a tail exactly when \(i\in A\) or \(i\in\operatorname{an}_E(j)\), and is an arrowhead otherwise.

For \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), the graph \(\mathcal N^{(k)}\) has vertex set \(V\cup\{\zeta_k\}\).  Vertices \(i,j\in V\) are adjacent exactly when they are adjacent in \(\mathcal N^{(0)}\), or there is a path \((i=v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_m=j)\) in \(\mathcal N^{(0)}\) such that every \(v_\ell\) belongs to \(A\) and every internal vertex belongs to \(F_k^*\).  The indicator \(\zeta_k\) is adjacent to \(j\) exactly when
\[
j\in T_k^*\cup(F_k^*\cap A)=Z_k(\mathbf M),
\]
and every such edge is \(\zeta_k\to j\).  For an adjacency between \(i,j\in V\), preserve an observational directed orientation when one exists.  Otherwise orient it by the exhaustive cases
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{condition}&\text{orientation}\\ \hline
i,j\notin F_k^*&i\mathbin{-}j\\
j\in F_k^*,\ i\notin F_k^*\text{ or }i\in\operatorname{an}_E(j)&i\to j\\
i\in F_k^*,\ j\notin F_k^*\text{ or }j\in\operatorname{an}_E(i)&j\to i\\
i,j\in F_k^*,\ i\notin\operatorname{an}_E(j),\ j\notin\operatorname{an}_E(i)&i\leftrightarrow j.
\end{array}
\]
Define \(\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A),\mathbf M)=1\) exactly when, for every \(k\in\mathcal K\), \(\mathcal N^{(k)}\) and \(\mathcal M^{(k)}\) have the same adjacency set and the same unshielded collider triples; otherwise set it to zero.  All tests and all derived sets are finite matrix computations.  If \(K=0\), construct and compare only the indicator-free observational graph and introduce no \(Z_k,R_k,O_k,T_k^*\), or \(F_k^*\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Rcert}; \texttt{Mfam}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:oracle-closure-runtime]\label{lem:oracle-closure-runtime}
For any finite \(\mathsf R_{\mathrm{SI}}\) represented by \(\texttt{def:finite-rule-encoding}\) and monotone in the endpoint information order, the schedule in \(\texttt{def:closure-runtime-certificate}\) terminates after at most \(N_{\mathrm{end}}=D(D-1)+KD(D+1)\) strict endpoint updates and one terminal no-update scan. Rule \(r\) is matched in \(T_r(D,K)=O((K+1)^{a_r}(D+1)^{b_r+3})\) time, and the total oracle-input orientation runtime is \(T_{\mathrm{cl}}(D,K)=O((K+1)(D+1)^3+(N_{\mathrm{end}}+1)\sum_{r\in\mathsf R_{\mathrm{SI}}}T_r(D,K))\), which is polynomial in the finite input dimensions because the rule set and every \(a_r,b_r\) are fixed independently of \(D,K\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
The observational matrix has at most \(D(D-1)\) ordered endpoint positions, and the \(K\) nonobservational matrices have at most \(KD(D+1)\) such positions.  Hence the total number of positions at which a circle can be replaced is at most
\[
N_{\mathrm{end}}=D(D-1)+KD(D+1).
\]
By the monotonicity hypothesis and \(\texttt{def:monotone-closure}\), a strict update replaces at least one circle by a tail or an arrowhead, and no later update can erase or reverse that mark.  Thus the endpoint potential, defined as the number of circular positions, decreases by at least one at every strict update and is initially at most \(N_{\mathrm{end}}\).  There are consequently at most \(N_{\mathrm{end}}\) strict endpoint updates, at most \(N_{\mathrm{end}}\) scans that make an update, and exactly one final scan that certifies that no rule matches consistently.

For a fixed rule \(r\), \(\texttt{def:finite-rule-encoding}\) enumerates at most \((K+1)^{a_r}\) assignments of its typed regime placeholders and at most \((D+1)^{b_r}\) assignments of its vertex placeholders.  Its finite-state path predicates are decided on product-state graphs of size polynomial in \(D+1\); the stipulated cubic envelope gives
\[
T_r(D,K)=O\!\left((K+1)^{a_r}(D+1)^{b_r+3}\right).
\]
Materializing the initial family by enumerating triples costs \(O((K+1)(D+1)^3)\).  Summing one exhaustive match scan for every rule over at most \(N_{\mathrm{end}}\) successful scans and the terminal unsuccessful scan yields
\[
T_{\mathrm{cl}}(D,K)
=O\!\left((K+1)(D+1)^3
+(N_{\mathrm{end}}+1)\sum_{r\in\mathsf R_{\mathrm{SI}}}T_r(D,K)\right).
\]
The rule list and the integers \(a_r,b_r\) are fixed independently of \(D,K\), so the displayed expression is polynomial in \(D,K\), hence also in the finite matrix-input dimensions.  This argument is conditional on the separately supplied finite monotone rule list; it asserts no semantic completeness property and uses no conditional-independence discovery bound. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The endpoint potential gives an auditable runtime certificate for any separately supplied fixed finite monotone rule list. \textit{Gap.} This lemma is conditional only and does not establish semantic completeness of Dai's local rules. \textit{Consumer.} Conditional on any fixed finitely encoded sound SI rule system, a population implementation can audit every scan and certify polynomial mark propagation; this does not establish that a complete such rule system exists.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:si-extension-calculus]\label{oeq:si-extension-calculus}
Using \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{ext}}\), can one construct a finite within- and cross-regime rule system \(\mathsf R_{\mathrm{SI}}\), independent of \(D,K\), such that every rule has the typed finite representation in \(\texttt{def:finite-rule-encoding}\), has per-scan cost \(T_r(D,K)=O((K+1)^{a_r}(D+1)^{b_r+3})\), and is sound over \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\); and such that for every endpoint \(e\) left circular by \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathsf R_{\mathrm{SI}}}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{init}}(\mathbf M))\) there exist \(\eta_e^{\mathrm t},\eta_e^{\mathrm a}\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\) whose indicator-free observational projection and indicator-bearing nonobservational projections agree with every fixed closure mark while realizing respectively a tail and an arrowhead at \(e\)?
\end{openendedquestion}
\textbf{Established partial result.}

Assume \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\ne\varnothing\).  The finite target-residual certificate results already establish an exact global answer, but not the requested fixed local calculus.  Let
\[
S_0(\mathbf M)=E(\mathcal M^{(0)}),
\qquad
m_0(\mathbf M)=|S_0(\mathbf M)|.
\]
Residual admissibility implies
\[
\operatorname{skel}(E)\subseteq B(E,A)\subseteq S_0(\mathbf M).
\]
Hence it suffices to assign the three states absent, one direction, and the other direction only to pairs in \(S_0(\mathbf M)\), force all remaining pairs absent, and enumerate \(A\subseteq V\).  This visits at most
\[
3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D
\]
pairs \((E,A)\) and loses no residual-admissible certificate.

For each \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), the supplied indicator neighborhood determines
\[
Z_k=\{j\in V:\zeta_k\text{ is adjacent to }j\text{ in }\mathcal M^{(k)}\},
\qquad
R_k=Z_k\cap A,
\qquad
O_k=Z_k\setminus A.
\]
When \(R_k\) is descendant-closed in \(E[A]\), the normalized target is
\[
T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)=O_k\cup\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k),
\qquad
F^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)=\operatorname{de}_E\!\left(T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\right).
\]
The target-residual characterization proves that this replacement preserves both the affected set and the indicator neighborhood:
\[
F^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)=F_k,
\qquad
T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\cup(F^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\cap A)=Z_k.
\]
By \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), every accepted certificate therefore reconstructs one member of \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\) with a common basal DAG, a common ancestor-closed selected set, and one ordered target family, and every compatible member has an accepted normalized certificate.

It follows from \(\texttt{thm:enumerative-si-pag-completeness}\) that
\[
\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M).
\]
Every circle in this output has two accepted common-basal certificates that agree with every fixed output mark and realize respectively a tail and an arrowhead at the circular endpoint.  The global population runtime is bounded by
\[
O\!\left(
3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D
\left[D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]
\right).
\]
This bound is finite, non-tight, and nonpolynomial in general; since \(m_0(\mathbf M)\le\binom D2\), it implies the dense envelope \(3^{\binom D2}2^D\), while \(m_0(\mathbf M)=O(D)\) gives a singly exponential candidate factor.

Independently, \(\texttt{lem:oracle-closure-runtime}\) proves that any supplied finite monotone rule list satisfying \(\texttt{def:finite-rule-encoding}\) makes at most
\[
N_{\mathrm{end}}=D(D-1)+KD(D+1)
\]
strict endpoint updates and one terminal scan, with mark-propagation time
\[
O\!\left(
(K+1)(D+1)^3+
(N_{\mathrm{end}}+1)
\sum_{r\in\mathsf R_{\mathrm{SI}}}T_r(D,K)
\right).
\]
Thus exact global recovery, exact target residualization, opposing common-basal witnesses for every canonical circle, and polynomial termination conditional on a supplied fixed finite rule list are established.  The unresolved step is to construct such a \(D,K\)-independent list and prove that its local closure has exactly the same fixed marks as the global certificate functional.  No support, positivity, overlap, rank, inverse, division, boundary, or limiting assumption is used.  The input consists of oracle regime MAGs, so none of these conclusions asserts polynomial conditional-independence or invariance discovery.
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Exact global enumeration settles semantic SI-PAG completeness but does not construct the finite sound within- and cross-regime calculus requested by this separate question. \textit{Gap.} Zhang's one-MAG completions and Jaber et al.'s no-selection augmented I-MAG realizability do not preserve one basal selected DAG and all unknown-target regimes simultaneously; the paired local extension remains unproved. \textit{Consumer.} Resolving the question would replace semantic global enumeration by a complete polynomial mark-closure stage; until then, the enumerative certificates already make residual circles genuine nonidentifiabilities.

\begin{theorem}[thm:complete-population-cdis]\label{thm:complete-population-cdis}
For every compatible encoded oracle family \(\mathbf M\), the target-residual certificate algorithm satisfies \(\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M)\), is population sound and complete, and returns actual paired compatible certificates for every residual circle.  Writing \(m_0(\mathbf M)=|S_0(\mathbf M)|\), it checks at most \(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D\) pairs \((E,A)\), deriving both \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\) and every \(T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\) from the supplied oracle MAGs, with crude runtime
\[
O\!\left(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D
\left[D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]\right).
\]
Since \(m_0(\mathbf M)\le\binom D2\), this recovers the dense worst-case envelope \(3^{\binom D2}2^D\), while \(m_0(\mathbf M)=O(D)\) gives candidate factor \(\exp(O(D))\).  The bounds are non-tight and nonpolynomial, with \(K\) appearing only polynomially.  The observational output is on \(V\), each nonobservational certificate component is on \(V\cup\{\zeta_k\}\) for \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), and the case \(K=0\) contains no indicator or target-residual objects.  No polynomial claim is made for the conditional-independence and invariance queries needed to obtain \(\mathbf M\), and no completeness assertion is made for a fixed polynomial local CDIS calculus.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
The matrix family in \(\texttt{def:oracle-input-encoding}\) is exactly the input consumed by \(\texttt{def:complete-cdis}\).  Applying the proposed sparse form of \(\texttt{thm:enumerative-si-pag-completeness}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M),
\]
the candidate bound
\[
3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D,
\]
and the runtime bound
\[
O\!\left(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D
\left[D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]\right).
\]
The endpointwise feasible-mark identity proved there establishes population soundness because every fixed output mark occurs in every compatible member.  It establishes population completeness because every invariant mark is fixed, while every residual circle is accompanied by accepted tail and arrowhead certificates.  By \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), those certificates reconstruct actual members of the full Dai compatibility class and are globally coherent across all regimes.

The domain statement follows from the two branches of \(\texttt{def:dai-certificate-checker}\): \(\mathcal N^{(0)}\) is on \(V\) and contains no intervention indicator, while \(\mathcal N^{(k)}\) is on \(V\cup\{\zeta_k\}\) only for \(k\in\mathcal K_+\).  When \(K=0\), the nonobservational branch is absent and no indicator-neighborhood or target-residual object is formed.

Finally,
\[
m_0(\mathbf M)=|S_0(\mathbf M)|\le\binom D2.
\]
Thus the sparse input-conditioned bound recovers the dense worst-case envelope \(3^{\binom D2}2^D\).  When \(m_0(\mathbf M)=O(D)\), the candidate factor is \(\exp(O(D))\).  These bounds are non-tight and nonpolynomial, and \(K\) appears only in the polynomial per-candidate factor.  The procedure begins with supplied oracle MAG matrices, so its correctness and runtime exclude their discovery by conditional-independence or invariance queries.  No step invokes a complete fixed local calculus; that remains the separate open-ended question.  No support, positivity, overlap, rank, inverse, division, boundary, or limiting condition is used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The headline combines exact target-residual common realizability with the sparse candidate factor \(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D\), its dense envelope, and polynomial regime dependence. \textit{Gap.} Dai et al. prove local-rule soundness but not complete recovery of the endpoint-invariance SI-PAG; this theorem supplies exact global population recovery while preserving their full class. \textit{Consumer.} Population benchmarking on supplied sciPlex2 A549 regime MAGs obtains the exact target graph, globally compatible opposing certificates, and an input-conditioned search certificate.

\begin{proposition}[prop:dai-two-variable-boundary]\label{prop:dai-two-variable-boundary}
For Dai et al.'s two-variable regime family with \(K=1\) and intervention on the first variable, the selected collider model \(1\to S\leftarrow2\) and the unselected directed model \(2\to1\) belong to the same \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\). Consequently, the canonical observational edge has a circle at the endpoint of vertex \(1\) and a tail at the endpoint of vertex \(2\); no sound SI-PAG calculus may orient the first endpoint.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\paragraph{Proof.}
\emph{Construction of the boundary instance.}
Set \(D=2\), \(V=\{1,2\}\), and \(K=1\).  Consider
\[
 \eta_{\mathrm{sel}}
 =\bigl(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{sel}},\{S\},(\varnothing,\{1\})\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathcal G_{\mathrm{sel}}:\ 1\longrightarrow S\longleftarrow2,
\]
where \(S\) is binary and the observed stratum is \(S=1\), and
\[
 \eta_{\mathrm{dir}}
 =\bigl(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{dir}},\varnothing,(\varnothing,\{1\})\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathcal G_{\mathrm{dir}}:\ 2\longrightarrow1.
\]
These are the two candidates in the clinical two-variable witness of
\cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}.  Define the supplied oracle representatives by
\[
 \mathcal M^{(0)}:\ X_1\mathbin{---}X_2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal M^{(1)}:\
 \zeta_1\longrightarrow X_1\longleftarrow X_2,
 \qquad
 \mathbf M=(\mathcal M^{(0)},\mathcal M^{(1)}).
 \tag{1}
\]
In accordance with the boundary convention in \(\texttt{def:twin-graph}\), the
observational representative has no intervention-indicator vertex.

Both basal graphs are finite DAGs and neither has a latent common cause of the two
system vertices.  In \(\eta_{\mathrm{sel}}\), the unique selection node is binary,
childless, and has only observed parents; for \(\eta_{\mathrm{dir}}\), the corresponding
selection conditions are vacuous.  The observational target is empty and the sole
nonobservational target is \(\{1\}\) in both candidates.  Selection is imposed on the
basal copy, and the intervention changes only the mechanism of \(X_1\), while all
other mechanisms and exogenous inputs are shared.  Thus the structural member
conditions in \(\texttt{def:dai-compatible-class}\) hold.  It remains to calculate the
two regime projections.

For \(\eta_{\mathrm{sel}}\), the indicator-free observational graph is
\[
 X_1\longrightarrow S^*\longleftarrow X_2.
\]
After conditioning on \(S^*\), the displayed path induces an adjacency between the
two retained vertices.  Each endpoint is an ancestor of \(S^*\); hence the endpoint
rule in \(\texttt{def:mag-projection}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal M_{\eta_{\mathrm{sel}}}^{(0)}:
 X_1\mathbin{---}X_2.
 \tag{2}
\]
For target \(\{1\}\), the nonobservational twin graph has the relevant edges
\[
 \zeta_1\to X_1,

\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A),\mathbf M)=1.
\]

Conversely, suppose the checker accepts.  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:compatible-input}\) supplies an anchor \(\eta_0\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).  Therefore \(\mathcal M_{\eta_0}^{(k)}\) and \(\mathcal M^{(k)}\) have the same adjacency and unshielded-collider patterns.  Acceptance supplies those same two patterns for \(\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A))\) and \(\mathcal M^{(k)}\).  Both the reconstructed family and the anchor arise from one basal DAG, childless pre-treatment selection, and one ordered target family.  The cross-regime equivalence criterion in \(\texttt{lem:dai-two-section-checker-characterization}\) thus yields
\[
\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A))
\equiv_m\mathcal M_{\eta_0}^{(k)}
\equiv_m\mathcal M^{(k)}
\qquad(k\in\mathcal K).
\]
The remaining class clauses hold by construction, so the reconstruction belongs to \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).

For exhaustiveness, take any \(\eta\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).  Lemma \(\texttt{lem:selection-normalization}\) constructs from it a residual-admissible pair \((E,A)\) whose canonical selection-and-target reconstruction has exactly the same fully oriented projected MAG in every regime.  The forward implication just proved gives
\[
\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A),\mathbf M)=1.
\]
Conversely, every passing pair reconstructs a compatible member by the reverse implication.  Passing target-residual certificates and compatible models therefore realize exactly the same regime-MAG families.  In particular,
\[
\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists E,A\;
\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A),\mathbf M)=1.
\]
When \(K=0\), the nonobservational index set is empty, and the same proof uses only the observational component.  No overlap, rank, inverse, division, or limiting condition is used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Passing pairs are exactly the derived certificates of actual common-basal models in the full class. \textit{Gap.} The published equivalence result does not itself give the finite residual checker. \textit{Consumer.} Every feasible mark and every circle witness receives an actual compatible-model certificate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:enumerative-si-pag-completeness]\label{thm:enumerative-si-pag-completeness}
For every compatible oracle family \(\mathbf M\), target-residual certificate enumeration in \(\texttt{def:complete-cdis}\) terminates and satisfies
\[
\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M).
\]
For every endpoint left circular, the retained tail and arrowhead certificates reconstruct two members of \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\) that agree with every fixed output mark and realize the two opposite marks at that endpoint.  Writing \(m_0(\mathbf M)=|S_0(\mathbf M)|\), both \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\) and every \(T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\) are uniquely computed from \((\mathbf M,E,A)\), and the algorithm visits at most
\[
3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D
\]
pairs \((E,A)\) and admits the crude finite runtime bound
\[
O\!\left(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D
\left[D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]\right).
\]
Since \(m_0(\mathbf M)\le\binom D2\), the dense worst-case envelope is \(3^{\binom D2}2^D\); when \(m_0(\mathbf M)=O(D)\), the candidate factor is \(\exp(O(D))\).  These bounds are non-tight and nonpolynomial, with only polynomial dependence on \(K\) outside the search factor.  This is an exact population algorithm, not a polynomial global search or a polynomial local-rule completeness result.  For \(K=0\), no indicator-neighborhood or target-residual object is formed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof.}
Write
\[
S_0(\mathbf M)=E(\mathcal M^{(0)}),
\qquad
m_0(\mathbf M)=|S_0(\mathbf M)|.
\]
The corrected search in \(\texttt{def:complete-cdis}\) gives each unordered pair in \(S_0(\mathbf M)\) one of three basal states---absent, oriented in one direction, or oriented in the other direction---and forces every pair outside \(S_0(\mathbf M)\) to be absent.  It therefore visits at most \(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}\) basal directed graphs before rejecting cyclic choices, and it visits \(2^D\) subsets \(A\) before checking ancestor closure.

The forced absences remove no residual-admissible pair.  Indeed, residual admissibility in \(\texttt{def:normalized-dai-certificate}\) requires
\[
B(E,A)\subseteq S_0(\mathbf M).
\]
Every adjacency of the basal DAG \(E\) belongs to \(B(E,A)\) by the first two clauses in the definition of \(B\).  Consequently
\[
\operatorname{skel}(E)\subseteq B(E,A)\subseteq S_0(\mathbf M).
\]
Thus any pair carrying a basal edge outside \(S_0(\mathbf M)\) would fail residual admissibility, whereas every residual-admissible pair is still enumerated.  In particular, no passing certificate is lost.

Once \((E,A)\) is fixed, \(\texttt{def:normalized-dai-certificate}\) uniquely computes
\[
Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)=S_0(\mathbf M)\setminus B(E,A)
\]
and, for every \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), uniquely computes
\[
Z_k(\mathbf M),\quad R_k(\mathbf M,A),\quad O_k(\mathbf M,A),
\quad T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A),\quad F^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A).
\]
The residual-skeleton and descendant-closure tests either reject the pair or admit this single derived certificate; neither \(Q_{\mathbf M}\) nor a target set is independently enumerated.  The total number of candidate pairs is therefore at most
\[
3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D,
\]
and the enumeration terminates.  When \(K=0\), all nonobservational objects in the preceding display are absent.

Let
\[
\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)=
\left\{(E,A):
\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A),\mathbf M)=1
\right\}.
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:compatible-input}\) and \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\) imply \(\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)\ne\varnothing\).  Fix an endpoint \(e=(k,u;uv)\in\mathcal E(\mathbf M)\).  The two directions and the exhaustiveness clause of \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), together with the no-loss argument above, give
\[
\left\{
\operatorname{mark}_e\bigl(\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A))\bigr):
(E,A)\in\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)
\right\}
=
\left\{
\operatorname{mark}_e\bigl(\mathcal M_\eta^{(k)}\bigr):
\eta\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)
\right\}.
\]
For the left-to-right inclusion, every passing pair reconstructs a compatible member.  For the reverse inclusion, target residualization of every compatible member supplies a passing pair with the same entire fully oriented regime-MAG family, and that pair is enumerated because its basal skeleton is contained in \(S_0(\mathbf M)\).

Every fully oriented MAG endpoint is either a tail or an arrowhead.  Hence the common feasible-mark set is one of
\[
\{\mathrm{tail}\},
\qquad
\{\mathrm{arrow}\},
\qquad
\{\mathrm{tail},\mathrm{arrow}\}.
\]
The output rule in \(\texttt{def:complete-cdis}\) returns respectively a tail, an arrowhead, or a circle, exactly as \(\texttt{def:canonical-si-pag}\) does.  Accepted certificates also have the supplied skeleton in each regime.  Therefore, endpoint by endpoint,
\[
\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M).
\]

If \(e\) is circular, its feasible-mark set contains both marks.  The finite lexicographic enumeration retains one accepted tail certificate and one accepted arrowhead certificate.  By \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), their canonical reconstructions are actual members of the common-basal class \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).  At every fixed output endpoint the feasible-mark set is a singleton, so both reconstructed witnesses agree with every fixed mark; at \(e\) they realize opposite marks.  These are whole-family common-basal witnesses, not unrelated regime-wise MAG completions.

For the runtime, materializing \(S_0(\mathbf M)\) and the indicator-neighborhoods costs \(O(D^2+KD)\).  For a fixed candidate \((E,A)\), acyclicity, transitive closure, ancestor closure, all descendant closures, and all minimal-generator sets are computable in
\[
O((K+1)(D+1)^3)
\]
time.  Constructing \(B(E,A)\) checks at most \(D\) possible common children for each of \(O(D^2)\) pairs and is absorbed by the same cubic envelope.  Forming the derived residuals, constructing all regime matrices, deciding the finite path conditions, and comparing all unshielded triples are also covered by that envelope.  Updating endpointwise feasible-mark sets and retaining the first opposing certificates costs \(O(N_{\mathrm{end}})\).  Hence a conservative per-candidate envelope is
\[
O\!\left(D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right).
\]
Multiplying by the candidate count gives
\[
O\!\left(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D
\left[D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]\right).
\]
Because \(S_0(\mathbf M)\subseteq\binom V2\),
\[
m_0(\mathbf M)\le\binom D2,
\]
so the dense worst-case envelope is \(3^{\binom D2}2^D\), whose logarithm is \(O(D^2)\).  If \(m_0(\mathbf M)=O(D)\), then
\[
3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D=\exp(O(D)).
\]
Both are non-tight, nonpolynomial upper bounds; \(K\) occurs only in the displayed polynomial per-candidate factor.  The result proves neither completeness of a fixed local orientation calculus nor a polynomial bound for the conditional-independence and invariance queries needed to obtain the oracle MAGs.  No support, positivity, overlap, rank, inverse, division, boundary, or limiting condition is used. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Sparse endpointwise enumeration over the exact target-residual quotient is a computable semantic representative with actual opposing witnesses. \textit{Gap.} Dai et al. leave local CDIS completeness open; this theorem instead gives exact global completeness without enumerating unknown targets and sharpens the candidate envelope to \(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D\). \textit{Consumer.} The sciPlex2 A549 oracle workflow can distinguish invariant marks from certified ambiguity using paired full-class certificates and can audit the search cost from observational skeleton sparsity.

\section{Interpretation}

A fixed output mark is a feature shared by every common-basal pre-treatment-selection model compatible with the supplied regime MAGs.  A circle is stronger than failure of a local rule: the algorithm returns one accepted tail certificate and one accepted arrowhead certificate, each reconstructing a complete model in the same Dai class and agreeing on every invariant mark.  In a sciPlex2 A549 population-oracle workflow, these certificates distinguish genuine ambiguity from incomplete search, subject to the exponential global enumeration bound.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exact result is a global certificate search, not a complete polynomial local orientation calculus.  Even when \(m_0(\mathbf M)=O(D)\), its stated candidate factor is \(\exp(O(D))\), and in the dense case the envelope is singly exponential in \(D^2\).  Independent within-regime completions are never valid witnesses: each retained completion must reconstruct one basal DAG, one selection summary, and one ordered unknown-target family.  The finite twin-compatible local extension calculus and its paired-completion proof remain open, and the oracle-MAG discovery queries are outside the runtime bound.

\section{Honest scope}

The proved contribution is population mark completeness on Dai et al.'s published causally sufficient pre-treatment-selection class, using supplied oracle regime MAGs.  It gives an exact target-residual enumerator with at most \(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D\) candidates, dense envelope \(3^{\binom D2}2^D\), sparse \(m_0(\mathbf M)=O(D)\) consequence \(\exp(O(D))\), polynomial dependence on \(K\) outside the search factor, exact \(Q\)- and target-residualization, and paired common-basal witnesses.  Regime zero remains indicator-free, the \(K=0\) case introduces no target-residual objects, and Dai et al.'s two-variable witness remains an unavoidable circle boundary.  A complete fixed finite local calculus, polynomial conditional-independence or invariance discovery, finite-sample consistency, latent common causes, post-treatment filtering, and causal-effect identification are outside the theorem.
