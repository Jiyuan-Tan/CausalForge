\textbf{Proof.}
The encoded input in \(\texttt{def:oracle-input-encoding}\) is exactly the family consumed by \(\texttt{def:complete-cdis}\).  Applying \(\texttt{thm:enumerative-si-pag-completeness}\) therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M),
\]
the certificate-count bound, and the corrected runtime
\[
O\!\left(
3^{\binom D2}2^{2^D+DK}
\left[D^2 2^D+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]
\right).
\]
The endpointwise feasible-mark identity in that theorem proves population soundness: every fixed output mark occurs in every compatible member.  It also proves population completeness: every invariant mark is fixed by the output, while every residual circle is accompanied by accepted tail and arrowhead certificates.  By \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), those certificates reconstruct actual members of the full common compatibility class.

The domain assertion follows directly from the two branches of \(\texttt{def:dai-certificate-checker}\): \(\mathcal N^{(0)}(\mathcal R)\) is constructed on \(V\) and has no intervention indicator, whereas \(\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R)\) is constructed on \(V\cup\{\zeta_k\}\) only for \(k\in\mathcal K_+\).  The procedure begins with supplied oracle matrices, so neither its correctness proof nor its runtime includes the conditional-independence and invariance queries used to discover those matrices.  Finally, the proof uses a generally doubly exponential global certificate search and never invokes a complete fixed local calculus.  Thus it entails neither a polynomial global-search claim nor completeness of a polynomial local CDIS calculus. \(\square\)
