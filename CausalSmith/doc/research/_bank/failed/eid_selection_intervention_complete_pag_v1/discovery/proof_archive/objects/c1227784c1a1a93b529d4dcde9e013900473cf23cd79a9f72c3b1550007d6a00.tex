\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(C=\binom D2\).  For every unordered pair in \(V\), the candidate directed graph \(E\) has at most three states: absent, oriented one way, or oriented the other way.  Hence there are at most \(3^C\) choices before filtering for acyclicity.  There are at most \(2^D\) choices of \(A\), after which the condition \(\operatorname{an}_E(A)=A\) is checked.  There are at most \(2^C\) simple graphs \(Q\), after which \(Q\subseteq\binom A2\) is checked.  Finally, the ordered targets have \(2^{DK}\) possibilities.  Thus the lexicographic enumeration is finite and visits no more than
\[
3^C2^D2^C2^{DK}
=3^{\binom D2}2^{\binom D2+D+DK}
=6^{\binom D2}2^{D+DK}
\]
normalized certificates.  Filtering invalid tuples can only decrease this number.

Let
\[
\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)
=\{\mathcal R:\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R,\mathbf M)=1\}.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:compatible-input}\) and \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), \(\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)\ne\varnothing\).  Fix \(e=(k,u;uv)\in\mathcal E(\mathbf M)\).  The two directions of that theorem give the exact feasible-mark identity
\[
\left\{
\operatorname{mark}_e\bigl(\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R)\bigr):
\mathcal R\in\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)
\right\}
=
\left\{
\operatorname{mark}_e\bigl(\mathcal M_\eta^{(k)}\bigr):
\eta\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)
\right\}.
\]
Indeed, a passing certificate reconstructs a compatible member, proving left-to-right inclusion, while every compatible member has a passing two-section certificate with the same entire regime-MAG family, proving right-to-left inclusion.

Every endpoint of a fully oriented MAG is either a tail or an arrowhead.  The common feasible set is therefore exactly one of
\[
\{\mathrm{tail}\},\qquad
\{\mathrm{arrow}\},\qquad
\{\mathrm{tail},\mathrm{arrow}\}.
\]
The rule in \(\texttt{def:complete-cdis}\) assigns respectively a tail, an arrowhead, or a circle, precisely as \(\texttt{def:canonical-si-pag}\) does.  The accepted certificates also match the supplied skeleton in every regime.  Hence, endpoint by endpoint,
\[
\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M).
\]

If \(e\) is circular, its feasible set contains both marks.  The finite enumeration therefore retains an accepted tail certificate and an accepted arrowhead certificate.  By \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), their canonical reconstructions are actual members of the single common-basal compatibility class \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).  At every fixed output endpoint the feasible set is a singleton, so both witnesses agree with every fixed output mark; at \(e\) they realize opposite marks.  These are globally coupled regime-family witnesses, not independent regime-wise MAG completions.

For the runtime, a certificate stores \(E\), \(A\), and \(Q\) in \(O(D^2)\) bits beyond its \(DK\) target bits.  Checking acyclicity and ancestor closure, computing the transitive closure of \(E\), forming all \(F_k=\operatorname{de}_E(T_k)\), constructing the regime matrices, deciding the path conditions, and comparing all unshielded triples costs
\[
O\bigl(D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3\bigr).
\]
Updating the endpointwise feasible sets and retaining the first opposing certificates costs \(O(N_{\mathrm{end}})\).  Multiplication by the certificate count gives
\[
O\!\left(
6^{\binom D2}2^{D+DK}
\left[D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]
\right).
\]
Because the logarithm of the multiplicative count is \(O(D^2+DK)\), the search is singly exponential in \(D^2+DK\).  It is not polynomial, does not establish completeness of any fixed local-rule calculus, and does not count the conditional-independence or invariance queries required to obtain the oracle MAG family. \(\square\)
