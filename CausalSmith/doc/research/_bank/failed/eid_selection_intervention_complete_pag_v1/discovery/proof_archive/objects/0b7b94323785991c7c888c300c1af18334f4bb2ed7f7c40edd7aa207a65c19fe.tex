\paragraph{Proof.}
\emph{Construction of the boundary instance.}
Set \(D=2\), \(V=\{1,2\}\), and \(K=1\).  Consider
\[
 \eta_{\mathrm{sel}}
 =\bigl(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{sel}},\{S\},(\varnothing,\{1\})\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathcal G_{\mathrm{sel}}:\ 1\longrightarrow S\longleftarrow2,
\]
where \(S\) is binary and the observed stratum is \(S=1\), and
\[
 \eta_{\mathrm{dir}}
 =\bigl(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{dir}},\varnothing,(\varnothing,\{1\})\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathcal G_{\mathrm{dir}}:\ 2\longrightarrow1.
\]
These are the two candidates in the clinical two-variable witness of
\cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}.  Define the supplied oracle representatives by
\[
 \mathcal M^{(0)}:\ X_1\mathbin{---}X_2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal M^{(1)}:\
 \zeta_1\longrightarrow X_1\longleftarrow X_2,
 \qquad
 \mathbf M=(\mathcal M^{(0)},\mathcal M^{(1)}).
 \tag{1}
\]
In accordance with the boundary convention in \(\texttt{def:twin-graph}\), the
observational representative has no intervention-indicator vertex.

Both basal graphs are finite DAGs and neither has a latent common cause of the two
system vertices.  In \(\eta_{\mathrm{sel}}\), the unique selection node is binary,
childless, and has only observed parents; for \(\eta_{\mathrm{dir}}\), the corresponding
selection conditions are vacuous.  The observational target is empty and the sole
nonobservational target is \(\{1\}\) in both candidates.  Selection is imposed on the
basal copy, and the intervention changes only the mechanism of \(X_1\), while all
other mechanisms and exogenous inputs are shared.  Thus the structural member
conditions in \(\texttt{def:dai-compatible-class}\) hold.  It remains to calculate the
two regime projections.

For \(\eta_{\mathrm{sel}}\), the indicator-free observational graph is
\[
 X_1\longrightarrow S^*\longleftarrow X_2.
\]
After conditioning on \(S^*\), the displayed path induces an adjacency between the
two retained vertices.  Each endpoint is an ancestor of \(S^*\); hence the endpoint
rule in \(\texttt{def:mag-projection}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal M_{\eta_{\mathrm{sel}}}^{(0)}:
 X_1\mathbin{---}X_2.
 \tag{2}
\]
For target \(\{1\}\), the nonobservational twin graph has the relevant edges
\[
 \zeta_1\to X_1,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_1\to X_1,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_1\to X_1^*,
 \qquad
 X_1^*\to S^*,
 \qquad
 X_2\to S^*.
 \tag{3}
\]
Marginalizing \(X_1^*,\varepsilon_1\) and conditioning on \(S^*\), the path
\[
 X_1\leftarrow\varepsilon_1\to X_1^*\to S^*\leftarrow X_2
\]
induces \(X_2\to X_1\): its endpoint at \(X_2\) is a tail because \(X_2\) is an
ancestor of \(S^*\), and its endpoint at \(X_1\) is an arrowhead because \(X_1\) is
an ancestor of neither \(X_2\) nor \(S^*\).  The direct edge
\(\zeta_1\to X_1\) is retained.  There is no inducing path between \(\zeta_1\) and
\(X_2\): the only graph path between them contains the retained nonendpoint
\(X_1\) as the collider \(\zeta_1\to X_1\leftarrow\varepsilon_1\), and \(X_1\)
has no descendant in \(\{X_2\}\cup\{S^*\}\).  Consequently,
\[
 \mathcal M_{\eta_{\mathrm{sel}}}^{(1)}:
 \zeta_1\longrightarrow X_1\longleftarrow X_2.
 \tag{4}
\]

For \(\eta_{\mathrm{dir}}\), there is neither marginalization nor conditioning in the
observational branch, so
\[
 \mathcal M_{\eta_{\mathrm{dir}}}^{(0)}:
 X_2\longrightarrow X_1.
 \tag{5}
\]
In the target-\(1\) twin graph, the reality-world edges
\(\zeta_1\to X_1\) and \(X_2\to X_1\) are retained.  Every graph path from
\(\zeta_1\) to \(X_2\) contains the retained collider \(X_1\) as a nonendpoint,
and there is no selected vertex to activate that collider.  Hence
\[
 \mathcal M_{\eta_{\mathrm{dir}}}^{(1)}:
 \zeta_1\longrightarrow X_1\longleftarrow X_2.
 \tag{6}
\]
The two observational MAGs in (2) and (5) are Markov equivalent: each has the
single adjacency \(X_1-X_2\), and a two-vertex graph has no unshielded triple or
discriminating path.  The two nonobservational MAGs in (4) and (6) are identical.
Thus both equivalence-match conditions hold, and therefore
\[
 \eta_{\mathrm{sel}},\eta_{\mathrm{dir}}
 \in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M).
 \tag{7}
\]

\emph{Invariance of the endpoint at vertex \(2\).}
The pair in (7) alone shows noninvariance at vertex \(1\); to establish the claimed
tail at vertex \(2\), fix an arbitrary
\(\eta\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).  Observational equivalence to
\(\mathcal M^{(0)}\) gives the adjacency \(X_1-X_2\).  Interventional equivalence to
\(\mathcal M^{(1)}\) gives exactly the skeleton
\[
 \zeta_1-X_1-X_2,
 \qquad
 \zeta_1\not\sim X_2,
 \tag{8}
\]
and makes the unshielded triple in (8) a collider at \(X_1\).

Suppose for contradiction that the observational mark at the endpoint of \(X_2\)
were an arrowhead.  The observational projection retains both system vertices,
marginalizes no system or exogenous vertex, and conditions only on childless selection
sinks.  Since \(D=2\), a nondirect path between \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) can have only
selection vertices as nonendpoints.  Any such adjacency-producing path passes through
a selected common child, making both endpoints ancestors of the conditioned set and
therefore giving tails at both endpoints.  A bidirected edge cannot arise because causal
sufficiency excludes a latent common cause of \(X_1\) and \(X_2\).  The assumed
arrowhead at \(X_2\) must therefore come from the basal causal edge
\[
 1\longrightarrow2.
 \tag{9}
\]

Next, (8) determines the intervention target.  If
\(2\in I_\eta^{(1)}\), then \(\texttt{def:twin-graph}\) inserts the direct retained
edge \(\zeta_1\to X_2\), contradicting \(\zeta_1\not\sim X_2\).  If
\(I_\eta^{(1)}=\varnothing\), the exogenous indicator \(\zeta_1\) has no incident
edge at all, contradicting \(\zeta_1\sim X_1\).  As \(V=\{1,2\}\), these two
facts imply
\[
 I_\eta^{(1)}=\{1\}.
 \tag{10}
\]
By (9)--(10), the nonobservational twin graph contains the retained reality-world
edge \(X_1\to X_2\).  Thus \(X_1\) is an ancestor of \(X_2\), and the endpoint
rule of \(\texttt{def:mag-projection}\) puts a tail at \(X_1\) on their
interventional MAG edge.  This contradicts the arrowhead at \(X_1\) required by the
unshielded collider in (8).  Therefore the supposition was false, and
\[
 \operatorname{mark}_{(0,2;12)}(\mathcal M_\eta^{(0)})
 =\mathrm{tail}
 \quad\text{for every }\eta\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M).
 \tag{11}
\]

\emph{Canonical marks and the soundness boundary.}
Equation (2) supplies a tail at the observational endpoint of \(X_1\), whereas
(5) supplies an arrowhead there.  By (7), both are compatible witnesses, so the
definition of the canonical SI-PAG gives a circle at that endpoint.  Equation (11)
and the same definition give a tail at the endpoint of \(X_2\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{mark}_{(0,1;12)}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}})=\circ,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{mark}_{(0,2;12)}(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}})=\mathrm{tail}.
 \tag{12}
\]
A sound SI-PAG calculus may fix an endpoint only to a mark shared by every member
of \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).  Fixing the first endpoint to a tail is refuted by
\(\eta_{\mathrm{dir}}\), while fixing it to an arrowhead is refuted by
\(\eta_{\mathrm{sel}}\).  Thus no sound calculus may orient that endpoint.

This is a finite graphical boundary result.  No distributional support, positivity,
overlap, rank, division, inverse, or limiting condition is used.  The operative boundary
conditions are \(D=2\), \(K=1\), an indicator-free observational branch, and one
indicator-bearing intervention on vertex \(1\); nonemptiness of the compatibility class
is witnessed explicitly by (7).  \(\square\)
