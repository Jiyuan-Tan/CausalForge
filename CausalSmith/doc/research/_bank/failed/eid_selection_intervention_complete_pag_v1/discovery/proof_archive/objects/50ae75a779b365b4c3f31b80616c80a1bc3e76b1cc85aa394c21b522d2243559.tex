\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(C=\binom D2\).  Each unordered pair of vertices has at most three basal states in \(E\): absent, oriented in one direction, or oriented in the other direction.  Thus there are at most \(3^C\) candidate directed graphs before rejecting cyclic choices.  There are \(2^D\) choices of \(A\), before checking \(\operatorname{an}_E(A)=A\), and \(2^{DK}\) ordered target families.  Once \((E,A)\) is fixed, \(\texttt{def:normalized-dai-certificate}\) computes the only allowable selection two-section,
\[
Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)=S_0(\mathbf M)\setminus B(E,A).
\]
The tests \(B(E,A)\subseteq S_0(\mathbf M)\) and \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\subseteq\binom A2\) either reject the candidate or admit that unique residual; they introduce no additional choices.  Therefore the enumeration visits at most
\[
3^C2^D2^{DK}=3^{\binom D2}2^{D+DK}
\]
candidates and terminates.

Let
\[
\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)
=\left\{\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}:
\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M},\mathbf M)=1\right\}.
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:compatible-input}\) and \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\) imply \(\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)\ne\varnothing\).  Fix \(e=(k,u;uv)\in\mathcal E(\mathbf M)\).  The two directions and the exhaustiveness clause of that theorem yield the exact feasible-mark identity
\[
\left\{
\operatorname{mark}_e\bigl(\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M})\bigr):
\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}\in\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)
\right\}
=
\left\{
\operatorname{mark}_e\bigl(\mathcal M_\eta^{(k)}\bigr):
\eta\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)
\right\}.
\]
Indeed, a passing residual certificate reconstructs a compatible member, which gives the left-to-right inclusion.  Conversely, normalization of each compatible member supplies a passing residual certificate with the same entire fully oriented regime-MAG family, which gives the right-to-left inclusion.

Every fully oriented MAG endpoint is either a tail or an arrowhead.  Hence the common feasible set is exactly one of
\[
\{\mathrm{tail}\},
\qquad
\{\mathrm{arrow}\},
\qquad
\{\mathrm{tail},\mathrm{arrow}\}.
\]
The rule in \(\texttt{def:complete-cdis}\) returns respectively a tail, an arrowhead, or a circle, exactly as \(\texttt{def:canonical-si-pag}\) does.  The accepted certificates also have the supplied skeleton in every regime.  Thus, endpoint by endpoint,
\[
\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M).
\]

If \(e\) is circular, its feasible set contains both marks.  The finite lexicographic enumeration therefore retains one accepted tail certificate and one accepted arrowhead certificate.  By \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), their canonical reconstructions are actual members of the same common-basal class \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).  At every fixed output endpoint the feasible set is a singleton, so both witnesses agree with every fixed mark; at \(e\) they realize opposite marks.  These are coupled whole-family witnesses, not independent regime-wise MAG completions.

For the runtime, materializing \(S_0(\mathbf M)\) costs \(O(D^2)\).  Under the declared matrix model, constructing \(B(E,A)\) checks, for each of \(O(D^2)\) unordered pairs, whether some one of at most \(D\) vertices \(c\in A\) is a common child; this costs \(O(D^3)\).  Forming \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\) and checking both residual inclusions costs another \(O(D^2)\).  Checking acyclicity and ancestor closure, computing the transitive closure of \(E\), forming all \(F_k=\operatorname{de}_E(T_k)\), constructing the regime matrices, deciding the path conditions, and comparing all unshielded triples costs
\[
O\bigl((K+1)(D+1)^3\bigr)
\]
per candidate.  The \(O(D^3)\) construction of \(B(E,A)\) is absorbed by this term, including when \(K=0\).  Updating all endpointwise feasible sets and retaining first opposing certificates costs \(O(N_{\mathrm{end}})\).  Therefore the conservative per-candidate envelope is
\[
O\bigl(D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\bigr),
\]
and multiplication by the candidate count gives
\[
O\!\left(
3^{\binom D2}2^{D+DK}
\left[D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]
\right).
\]
Its logarithmic search factor is \(O(D^2+DK)\), so this is a singly exponential upper bound in \(D^2+DK\).  It is neither polynomial nor asserted tight.  It proves no completeness result for a fixed local orientation calculus and excludes the conditional-independence and invariance queries used to obtain the supplied oracle MAGs.  The boundary \(K=0\) has an empty target-family enumeration and is covered by the same argument. \(\square\)
