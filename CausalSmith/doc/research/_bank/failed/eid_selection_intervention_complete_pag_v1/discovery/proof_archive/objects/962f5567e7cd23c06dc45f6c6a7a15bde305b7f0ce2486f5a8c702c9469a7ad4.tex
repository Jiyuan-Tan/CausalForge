\textbf{Proof.}
There are at most \(3^{\binom D2}\) labeled directed graphs with at most one directed edge for each unordered pair; filtering by acyclicity enumerates every DAG \(E\).  There are \(2^{2^D}\) choices of \(H\subseteq 2^V\), and \(2^{DK}\) ordered choices of \((T_k)_{k\in\mathcal K_+}\).  Thus the certificate list is finite and has the displayed cardinality bound.

Let
\[
\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)=\{\mathcal R:\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R,\mathbf M)=1\}.
\]
By compatibility and \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), this set is nonempty.  That theorem and selection normalization also show, for each endpoint \(e=(k,u;uv)\), that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\{\operatorname{mark}_e(\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R)):\mathcal R\in\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)\}
=\{\operatorname{mark}_e(\mathcal M_\eta^{(k)}):\eta\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, every accepted certificate reconstructs a compatible candidate, giving the left-to-right inclusion, and every compatible candidate normalizes without changing its projections to an accepted certificate, giving the reverse inclusion.

Each fully oriented MAG endpoint is either a tail or an arrowhead.  Hence the common set in the last display is either \(\{\mathrm{tail}\}\), \(\{\mathrm{arrow}\}\), or \(\{\mathrm{tail},\mathrm{arrow}\}\).  The endpoint rule in \(\texttt{def:complete-cdis}\) returns respectively the common tail, the common arrowhead, or a circle.  By \(\texttt{def:canonical-si-pag}\), these are exactly the three defining cases of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M)\).  Equality follows endpoint by endpoint, and all accepted graphs have the supplied skeletons by the checker.

In the circle case, finiteness lets the algorithm retain one certificate attaining each of the two marks.  Their reconstructions belong to \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\) by finite common realizability.  Every fixed output mark corresponds to a singleton feasible-mark set, so both reconstructions agree with every such mark; at the chosen circle they realize opposite marks.  These are global common-basal-DAG witnesses, not independent regime-wise MAG completions.

For the runtime, reading and unioning \(H\) costs at most \(O(D2^D)\).  Transitive closure of \(E\), all descendant sets, the observational adjacency matrix, and the connected-component tests for intervention-induced paths are computable in \(O((K+1)(D+1)^3)\) time after this scan.  Enumerating all unshielded triples and comparing their collider status has the same cubic envelope.  Accumulating feasible marks and first witnesses costs \(O(N_{\mathrm{end}})\) per accepted certificate.  Multiplication by the certificate-count bound proves the displayed runtime.  It is finite but not polynomial; it makes no complexity assertion about obtaining the oracle MAGs. \(\square\)
