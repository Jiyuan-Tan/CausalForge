\textbf{Proof.}
Fix an input-conditioned normalized certificate
\[
\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}
=\bigl(E,A,Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A),(T_k)_{k\in\mathcal K_+}\bigr),
\qquad
Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)=S_0(\mathbf M)\setminus B(E,A).
\]
By \(\texttt{def:normalized-dai-certificate}\), \(E\) is acyclic, \(\operatorname{an}_E(A)=A\),
\[
B(E,A)\subseteq S_0(\mathbf M),
\qquad
Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\subseteq\binom A2,
\]
and each \(T_k\subseteq V\).  These are precisely the residual admissibility conditions asserted in the statement.

Construct the canonical selection structure with one binary childless node having parent set \(\{i,j\}\) for every \(\{i,j\}\in Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\), and one binary childless node having singleton parent set \(\{i\}\) for every vertex \(i\in A\) isolated in \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\).  Because \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\subseteq\binom A2\), every reconstructed parent belongs to \(A\).  Conversely, every nonisolated vertex of \(A\) occurs in a pair parent set and every isolated vertex occurs in its singleton parent set.  Hence the union of all reconstructed parent sets is exactly \(A\), and therefore its ancestor closure is
\[
\operatorname{an}_E(A)=A.
\]
Two distinct observed vertices share a reconstructed selection child exactly when their unordered pair belongs to \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\).  Thus the reconstruction has two-section exactly \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\), including the boundary cases \(A=\varnothing\) and \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)=\varnothing\).

We now match the checker to \(\texttt{lem:dai-two-section-substrate}\) term by term.  The substrate's observational adjacency clauses are a basal edge of \(E\), a common observed collider child \(c\in A\), or a common selection child.  The first two clauses form \(B(E,A)\), and the last clause is membership in \(Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)\).  Consequently the checker uses exactly the substrate adjacency set, and residual admissibility gives
\[
B(E,A)\cup Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A)
=B(E,A)\cup\bigl(S_0(\mathbf M)\setminus B(E,A)\bigr)
=S_0(\mathbf M).
\]
The observational endpoint criterion in the substrate is exactly that the endpoint at \(i\) is a tail when \(i\in A\) or \(i\in\operatorname{an}_E(j)\), and an arrowhead otherwise, as in the checker.

For \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), set \(F_k=\operatorname{de}_E(T_k)\).  The substrate's intervention-induced adjacency is exactly the checker path in the observational graph whose vertices lie in \(A\) and whose internal vertices lie in \(F_k\).  Its indicator adjacency set is exactly
\[
T_k\cup(F_k\cap A),
\]
with every such edge oriented \(\zeta_k\to j\).  Finally, after preserving an observational directed orientation, the substrate's four exhaustive interventional endpoint cases are respectively: both endpoints outside \(F_k\); only the second endpoint in \(F_k\), or ancestry from the first to the second; the symmetric case; and both endpoints in \(F_k\) with neither an ancestor of the other.  These are exactly the four rows of \(\texttt{def:dai-certificate-checker}\).  Hence every graph \(\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M})\), including the indicator-free branch \(k=0\), is the regime MAG specified by Dai et al.'s formulas for the canonical reconstruction.

The final clause of \(\texttt{lem:dai-two-section-substrate}\) states that two families generated by this common-basal, ordered-target construction are Markov equivalent across all regimes if and only if every corresponding pair of regime MAGs has the same adjacencies and unshielded collider triples.  That is exactly the acceptance comparison in \(\operatorname{Check}_{D}\).  No support, overlap, rank, inverse, division, or limiting condition is used. \(\square\)
