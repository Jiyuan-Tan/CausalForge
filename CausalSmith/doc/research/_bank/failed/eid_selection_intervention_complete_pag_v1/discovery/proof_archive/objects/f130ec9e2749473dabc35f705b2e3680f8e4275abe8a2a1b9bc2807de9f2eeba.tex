\textbf{Proof.}
Fix a residual-admissible pair \((E,A)\), abbreviate
\[
Q=Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A),
\quad R_k=R_k(\mathbf M,A),
\quad O_k=O_k(\mathbf M,A),
\quad T_k^*=T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A),
\quad F_k^*=F^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A).
\]
The residual conditions give \(\operatorname{an}_E(A)=A\), \(B(E,A)\subseteq S_0(\mathbf M)\), and \(Q\subseteq\binom A2\).

Construct one binary childless selection node with parent set \(\{i,j\}\) for every \(\{i,j\}\in Q\), and one with singleton parent set \(\{i\}\) for every \(i\in A\) isolated in \(Q\).  Since \(Q\subseteq\binom A2\), all reconstructed parents lie in \(A\).  Every nonisolated vertex of \(A\) occurs in a pair parent set and every isolated vertex occurs in its singleton parent set.  Hence the union of selection-parent sets is \(A\), its ancestor closure is \(\operatorname{an}_E(A)=A\), and the two-section is exactly \(Q\), including \(A=\varnothing\) and \(Q=\varnothing\).

Because \(R_k\) is descendant-closed in the finite DAG \(E[A]\), every \(r\in R_k\) descends from some \(m\in\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)\): repeatedly choose a strict ancestor in \(R_k\), which must terminate by finiteness and acyclicity.  Conversely, descendant closure keeps every descendant in \(A\) of such an \(m\) inside \(R_k\).  Thus
\[
\operatorname{de}_E(\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k))\cap A=R_k.
\]
Since \(O_k\subseteq V\setminus A\) and \(\operatorname{an}_E(A)=A\), no vertex of \(O_k\) has a descendant in \(A\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{de}_E(O_k)\cap A=\varnothing,
\qquad
F_k^*\cap A
=\operatorname{de}_E(O_k\cup\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k))\cap A
=R_k.
\]
Since \(\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)\subseteq R_k\),
\[
T_k^*\cup(F_k^*\cap A)
=O_k\cup\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)\cup R_k
=O_k\cup R_k
=Z_k(\mathbf M).
\]

We now match \(\texttt{def:dai-certificate-checker}\) to \(\texttt{lem:dai-two-section-substrate}\).  An observational adjacency is generated by a basal edge, by a common observed collider child in \(A\), or by a common selection child.  The first two clauses form \(B(E,A)\), and the third is membership in \(Q\).  Hence
\[
B(E,A)\cup Q
=B(E,A)\cup(S_0(\mathbf M)\setminus B(E,A))
=S_0(\mathbf M).
\]
The substrate's observational endpoint criterion is exactly that the endpoint at \(i\) is a tail when \(i\in A\) or \(i\in\operatorname{an}_E(j)\), and an arrowhead otherwise.

For \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), the substrate's intervention-induced adjacency is exactly a path in the observational graph whose vertices lie in \(A\) and whose internal vertices lie in \(F_k^*\).  Its indicator adjacency set is \(T_k^*\cup(F_k^*\cap A)=Z_k(\mathbf M)\), with every such edge \(\zeta_k\to j\).  After preserving an observational directed orientation, its four exhaustive endpoint cases depend only on membership in \(F_k^*\) and ancestry in \(E\), exactly as in the checker.  Thus \(\mathbf N(\mathcal R_{\mathbf M}(E,A))\) is the regime-MAG family given by Dai et al.'s formulas for the reconstruction.

Finally, the cross-regime clause of \(\texttt{lem:dai-two-section-substrate}\) says that two common-basal, ordered-target families are Markov equivalent in all regimes exactly when their regime MAGs have equal adjacency and unshielded-collider patterns.  This is precisely the terminal checker comparison.  If \(K=0\), \(\mathcal K_+\) is empty, so only the observational argument remains.  No support, overlap, rank, inverse, division, or limiting condition is used. \(\square\)
