\textbf{Established partial result.}

Assume \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\ne\varnothing\), as required by \(\texttt{ass:compatible-input}\).  The established target-residual certificate theorems give a finite global construction, although they do not give the requested fixed local rule calculus.

Let \(S_0(\mathbf M)\) be the edge set of the supplied observational skeleton and set
\[
m_0(\mathbf M)=|S_0(\mathbf M)|.
\]
For a candidate acyclic basal directed edge set \(E\) and an ancestor-closed set \(A\subseteq V\), define
\[
B(E,A)=\left\{\{i,j\}\in\binom V2:
i\to j\in E\ \text{or}\ j\to i\in E\ \text{or}\
\exists c\in A:\ i\to c\leftarrow j\ \text{in }E
\right\}.
\]
Residual admissibility requires
\[
\operatorname{skel}(E)\subseteq B(E,A)\subseteq S_0(\mathbf M),
\qquad
Q_{\mathbf M}(E,A):=S_0(\mathbf M)\setminus B(E,A)
\subseteq\binom A2.
\]
Consequently, if \(\{i,j\}\notin S_0(\mathbf M)\), then \(\{i,j\}\notin\operatorname{skel}(E)\).  It is therefore sufficient to enumerate the three states ``absent'', \(i\to j\), and \(j\to i\) only for pairs in \(S_0(\mathbf M)\), forcing every other pair absent.  This removes no admissible, and hence no passing, certificate.  There are at most
\[
3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D
\]
pairs \((E,A)\).  Since \(m_0(\mathbf M)\le\binom D2\), the earlier dense bound \(3^{\binom D2}2^D\) remains a valid worst-case envelope.

For each \(k\in\mathcal K_+\), define from the supplied oracle MAG
\[
Z_k=\{j\in V:\zeta_k\text{ is adjacent to }j\text{ in }\mathcal M^{(k)}\},
\qquad
R_k=Z_k\cap A,
\qquad
O_k=Z_k\setminus A.
\]
Reject the candidate unless \(R_k\) is descendant-closed in the induced DAG \(E[A]\).  Put
\[
\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)
=\{r\in R_k:\text{ no strict }E\text{-ancestor of }r\text{ belongs to }R_k\},
\]
\[
T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)=O_k\cup\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k),
\qquad
F_k^*=\operatorname{de}_E\!\left(T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\right).
\]

To verify exact target residualization, take any passing certificate with target \(T_k\) and affected set \(F_k=\operatorname{de}_E(T_k)\).  By \(\texttt{lem:dai-two-section-checker-characterization}\),
\[
Z_k=T_k\cup(F_k\cap A).
\]
Since \(T_k\subseteq F_k\), intersecting with \(A\) and subtracting \(A\) give
\[
R_k=F_k\cap A,
\qquad
O_k=T_k\setminus A.
\]
Ancestor closure of \(A\) implies that no vertex outside \(A\) has an \(E\)-directed path to a vertex of \(A\).  Thus outside-\(A\) targets cannot reach \(A\), and \(R_k\) is descendant-closed in \(E[A]\).  Because \(E[A]\) is finite and acyclic, backward traversal from each vertex of \(R_k\) terminates at a vertex of \(\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)\); hence
\[
\operatorname{de}_{E[A]}\!\left(\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)\right)=R_k.
\]
Every minimal vertex of \(R_k\) lies in \(T_k\cap A\): it descends from a target in \(T_k\cap A\), and a strict target ancestor would be a strict ancestor in \(R_k\), contradicting minimality.  Conversely, every vertex of \(T_k\cap A\) belongs to \(R_k\) and therefore descends from a minimal vertex.  It follows that
\[
\operatorname{de}_E(T_k\cap A)
=\operatorname{de}_E\!\left(\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)\right).
\]
Together with \(O_k=T_k\setminus A\), this yields
\[
F_k^*
=\operatorname{de}_E\!\left(O_k\cup\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)\right)
=\operatorname{de}_E(T_k)
=F_k,
\]
and
\[
T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\cup(F_k^*\cap A)
=O_k\cup\operatorname{Min}_E(R_k)\cup R_k
=O_k\cup R_k
=Z_k.
\]
Thus the normalized replacement preserves the affected set and all indicator adjacencies.  The reconstruction direction of \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\) supplies a common-basal compatible model with the canonical targets \(T^*_{k,\mathbf M}(E,A)\).

By \(\texttt{thm:enumerative-si-pag-completeness}\), the accepted canonical reconstructions realize exactly the endpointwise feasible marks of \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M),
\]
and every output circle has two accepted common-basal certificates agreeing with every fixed output mark and realizing respectively a tail and an arrowhead at that endpoint.  By \(\texttt{thm:complete-population-cdis}\), the resulting global population runtime is
\[
O\!\left(
3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D
\left[D^2+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]
\right).
\]
The bound is non-tight and nonpolynomial in general, while its dependence on \(K\) is polynomial.  If \(m_0(\mathbf M)=O(D)\), its search factor is singly exponential in \(D\), rather than in \(D^2\).  When \(K=0\), there are no sets \(Z_k,R_k,O_k\), no target reconstruction, and only the indicator-free observational branch is checked.

Separately, \(\texttt{lem:oracle-closure-runtime}\) proves that any supplied finite monotone rule list satisfying \(\texttt{def:finite-rule-encoding}\) makes at most
\[
N_{\mathrm{end}}=D(D-1)+KD(D+1)
\]
strict endpoint updates and one terminal scan, with total mark-propagation time
\[
O\!\left(
(K+1)(D+1)^3+
(N_{\mathrm{end}}+1)
\sum_{r\in\mathsf R_{\mathrm{SI}}}T_r(D,K)
\right).
\]
No support, positivity, overlap, rank, inverse, division, or boundary assumption beyond finiteness and compatibility is used.  The input is the supplied oracle regime-MAG family, so neither result makes a polynomial claim for conditional-independence or invariance discovery.

These conclusions establish exact global population SI-PAG computation, exact co-selection and target residualization, opposing common-basal witnesses for every canonical circle, the sparse candidate bound \(3^{m_0(\mathbf M)}2^D\), and polynomial termination conditional on a supplied finite rule list.  They do not construct a fixed \(D,K\)-independent local calculus or prove that the closure of such a calculus equals the canonical SI-PAG.
