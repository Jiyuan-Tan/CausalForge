\textbf{Proof.}
For every unordered pair of vertices in \(V\), a directed graph \(E\) has one of three states: no edge, the first direction, or the second direction.  Thus there are at most
\[
3^{\binom D2}
\]
candidate directed graphs, and filtering them for acyclicity retains every possible DAG.  There are \(2^{2^D}\) choices of \(H\subseteq 2^V\), and there are \(2^{DK}\) ordered choices of \((T_k)_{k\in\mathcal K_+}\).  Therefore the lexicographic enumeration is finite and visits at most
\[
3^{\binom D2}2^{2^D+DK}
\]
normalized certificates.

Define the finite accepted set
\[
\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)
=\{\mathcal R:\operatorname{Check}_{D}(\mathcal R,\mathbf M)=1\}.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:compatible-input}\) and \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), this set is nonempty.  Fix an endpoint \(e=(k,u;uv)\in\mathcal E(\mathbf M)\).  The same theorem, together with \(\texttt{lem:selection-normalization}\) as used in its proof, gives the exact feasible-mark identity
\[
\left\{
\operatorname{mark}_e\bigl(\mathcal N^{(k)}(\mathcal R)\bigr):
\mathcal R\in\mathfrak A(\mathbf M)
\right\}
=
\left\{
\operatorname{mark}_e\bigl(\mathcal M_\eta^{(k)}\bigr):
\eta\in\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)
\right\}.
\]
Indeed, an accepted certificate reconstructs a compatible candidate, which proves inclusion from left to right.  Conversely, every compatible candidate has a passing normalized certificate with exactly the same projected MAGs, which proves inclusion from right to left.

Every fully oriented MAG endpoint is a tail or an arrowhead.  The common set in the last display is therefore exactly one of
\[
\{\mathrm{tail}\},
\qquad
\{\mathrm{arrow}\},
\qquad
\{\mathrm{tail},\mathrm{arrow}\}.
\]
The output rule in \(\texttt{def:complete-cdis}\) assigns, respectively, a tail, an arrowhead, or a circle.  These are exactly the endpointwise cases in \(\texttt{def:canonical-si-pag}\).  The checker also enforces the supplied skeleton in every regime.  Hence, endpoint by endpoint,
\[
\operatorname{CDIS}^{\mathrm{cmp}}(\mathbf M)
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathbf M).
\]

If the output endpoint \(e\) is circular, its feasible-mark set contains both marks.  Finiteness of the enumeration therefore supplies a first accepted tail certificate and a first accepted arrowhead certificate.  By \(\texttt{thm:finite-common-realizability}\), their canonical reconstructions are members of \(\mathfrak C_D(\mathbf M)\).  At every fixed output endpoint, the feasible-mark set is a singleton, so both reconstructions agree with that fixed mark.  At \(e\), the two reconstructions realize opposite marks.  They are thus the required paired common-basal-DAG witnesses, not unrelated regime-wise MAG completions.

We finally account for runtime.  For one certificate, materializing every selected parent set and all unordered co-parent pairs costs
\[
O\!\left(\sum_{h\in H}|h|^2\right)
\le O(D^2 2^D).
\]
Computing the transitive closure of \(E\), the sets \(A(\mathcal R)\) and \(F_k(\mathcal R)\), the observational adjacency and mark matrices, and the regime-specific connectivity conditions in \(\texttt{def:dai-certificate-checker}\) costs
\[
O\bigl((K+1)(D+1)^3\bigr).
\]
The same cubic envelope covers enumeration and comparison of unshielded triples.  Updating all feasible-mark sets and retaining the first opposing witnesses costs \(O(N_{\mathrm{end}})\) per certificate.  Multiplying the sum of these per-certificate bounds by the certificate-count bound gives
\[
O\!\left(
3^{\binom D2}2^{2^D+DK}
\left[D^2 2^D+(K+1)(D+1)^3+N_{\mathrm{end}}\right]
\right).
\]
This is a finite, generally doubly exponential population computation.  It is not a polynomial local-rule algorithm and says nothing about the cost of obtaining the oracle regime MAGs by conditional-independence or invariance queries. \(\square\)
