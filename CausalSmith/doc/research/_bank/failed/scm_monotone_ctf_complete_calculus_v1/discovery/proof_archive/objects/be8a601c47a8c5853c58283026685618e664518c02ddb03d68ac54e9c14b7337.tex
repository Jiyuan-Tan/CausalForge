\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(d=2^{|V|}\), fix an enumeration \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) of \(\{0,1\}^{V}\), and fix an arbitrary generating model
\[
 \mathfrak m\in\mathcal F_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(p).
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:menu\text{-}compatibility}\) ensures that this fiber is nonempty.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:menu\text{-}fiber}\), membership of \(\mathfrak m\) gives
\[
 P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m)=p. \tag{1}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:positive\text{-}query\text{-}denominator}\) gives
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}(B_Q)\geq\kappa>0. \tag{2}
\]
The successful-output soundness clause of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}oracle\text{-}complete\text{-}mctfid}\), applied to
\[
 \mathsf{MCTFID}(G,M,\mathcal Z,Q)=F\in\mathcal R(p),
\]
therefore yields
\[
 F\bigl(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m)\bigr)=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m). \tag{3}
\]
Combining (1) and (3) proves the causal centering identity
\[
 F(p)=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m). \tag{4}
\]
Thus the causal target is not identified with the observed-law functional by definition; equality (4) follows from successful-output soundness and the positive-denominator condition.

We next establish local regularity.  The expression tree of \(F\) is finite, and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:analytic\text{-}branch\text{-}margin}\) states that
\[
 \lambda_F(p)\geq\kappa>0. \tag{5}
\]
We claim that there are an ambient open neighborhood \(U\subset\mathbb R^{Kd}\) of \(p\) and a continuously differentiable map \(F_p^{\circ}:U\to\mathbb R\) such that \(F(q)=F_p^{\circ}(q)\) for every \(q\in U\) in the domain of the expression grammar.  On \(U\), the finite branch selected at \(p\) is unchanged, every active quotient denominator remains nonzero, and every active root selector returns the continuation of the root selected at \(p\).

We prove the claim by induction through the finite active expression tree.  Cell coordinates and rational constants are analytic, and finite sums and products preserve continuous differentiability.  If \(a(q)/b(q)\) is an active quotient, Definition \(\mathrm{def:analytic\text{-}branch\text{-}margin}\) and (5) give \(|b(p)|\geq\kappa\).  Continuity gives a neighborhood on which \(|b(q)|\geq\kappa/2\).  If a total finite branch is selected by polynomial sign guards \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\), then (5) gives \(|g_j(p)|\geq\kappa\) for every active guard.  After shrinking the neighborhood, every \(g_j(q)\) has the same sign as \(g_j(p)\), so the branch is fixed.

Consider an active rational-interval root node.  Write
\[
 t_0=\operatorname{Root}_j(h;I)(p),
 \qquad h(t_0,p)=0,
 \qquad I=(a,b).
\]
The margin in (5) implies
\[
 |\partial_t h(t_0,p)|\geq\kappa,
 \qquad
 \min\{t_0-a,b-t_0\}\geq\kappa. \tag{6}
\]
Choose \(0<\varepsilon<\kappa/2\).  Because \(h\) is a polynomial and \(\partial_t h(t_0,p)\neq0\), there is a neighborhood \(U_0\) of \(p\) on which \(\partial_t h(t,q)\) has constant sign and absolute value at least \(\kappa/2\) whenever \(|t-t_0|\leq\varepsilon\).  At \(q=p\), the mean-value theorem makes \(h(t_0-\varepsilon,p)\) and \(h(t_0+\varepsilon,p)\) have opposite signs.  After shrinking \(U_0\), the same holds for every \(q\in U_0\).  The intermediate-value theorem and strict monotonicity in \(t\) consequently give a unique root
\[
 t(q)\in(t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon). \tag{7}
\]
Because \(I\) isolates \(t_0\) at \(p\), the continuous function \(|h(\,\cdot\,,p)|\) has a positive minimum on the compact set
\[
 [a,b]\setminus(t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon).
\]
Continuity of the polynomial coefficients permits a further shrinking of \(U_0\) on which no root enters that compact set or crosses an endpoint of \(I\).  The active existence, uniqueness, and index guards retain their signs.  Hence the exact \(\operatorname{Root}_j(h;I)\) selector equals \(t(q)\) throughout this smaller neighborhood.

For an active Thom selector
\[
 t_0=\operatorname{ThomRoot}_{\sigma}(h)(p),
\]
the active guard certifies that \(t_0\) is the unique real root with the displayed Thom encoding.  The isolated-root derivative is part of the margin, so
\[
 h(t_0,p)=0,
 \qquad |\partial_t h(t_0,p)|\geq\kappa. \tag{8}
\]
The same local monotonicity argument gives a unique continuous root continuation \(t(q)\) near \(t_0\).  The finitely many polynomial sign predicates in the selected Thom branch are active guards; by (5) their defining nonzero values retain their signs after another shrinking.  The active uniqueness guard therefore shows that \(\operatorname{ThomRoot}_{\sigma}(h)(q)=t(q)\).

For either root syntax, take \(q,q'\) in a sufficiently small common neighborhood.  The mean-value theorem in \(t\), the lower bound on \(|\partial_t h|\), and local Lipschitz continuity of the polynomial in its parameter coordinates give a finite constant \(C\) such that
\[
 |t(q)-t(q')|
 \leq \frac{2}{\kappa}
       |h(t(q'),q)-h(t(q'),q')|
 \leq C\|q-q'\|. \tag{9}
\]
Apply a coordinatewise difference quotient to \(h(t(q),q)=0\), use (9), and pass to the limit.  This gives
\[
 \nabla t(q)
 =-\frac{\nabla_q h(t(q),q)}{\partial_t h(t(q),q)}. \tag{10}
\]
The right-hand side is continuous after one more shrinking.  Formula (10) is exactly the recursive differentiation rule for either root syntax.  Intersecting the finitely many neighborhoods for all active guards, quotients, and roots proves the claim.  In particular,
\[
 q\longmapsto\nabla F_p^{\circ}(q)
 \quad\text{is continuous on }U. \tag{11}
\]
This is an ambient extension, so (11) remains valid even when some cells of \(p\) lie on the boundary of their probability simplices.  No positivity or rank condition on individual multinomial cells is needed.

We now derive the joint sampling limit.  For observation \(i\) in regime \(k\), define the one-hot vector
\[
 Y_{ki}=\bigl(\mathbf 1\{X_{ki}=x_1\},\ldots,
                    \mathbf 1\{X_{ki}=x_d\}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:independent\text{-}regime\text{-}sampling}\), these vectors are independent and identically distributed within each regime, samples from distinct regimes are independent, and
\[
 E(Y_{ki})=p_k,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y_{ki})
 =\operatorname{diag}(p_k)-p_kp_k^{\mathsf T}
 =\Sigma_k. \tag{12}
\]
Their coordinates are bounded, so all second moments exist.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:stable\text{-}regime\text{-}shares}\) implies \(n_k\to\infty\) and
\[
 \frac{n}{n_k}\longrightarrow\rho_k^{-1}. \tag{13}
\]
Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:multivariate\text{-}iid\text{-}clt}\) in each regime.  Independence across the fixed finite number \(K\) of regimes makes the joint characteristic function the product of the regime characteristic functions.  Together with (13), this yields
\[
 \sqrt n\,(\widehat p-p)
 \rightsquigarrow
 N_{Kd}(0,\Omega),
 \qquad
 \Omega=\operatorname{blockdiag}
 \bigl(\rho_1^{-1}\Sigma_1,\ldots,
       \rho_K^{-1}\Sigma_K\bigr). \tag{14}
\]
The covariance in (14) may be singular because every multinomial cell vector sums to one; the multivariate central limit theorem permits a singular covariance, so no rank assumption is required.  Directly from (12),
\[
 E_{\mathfrak m}\|\widehat p-p\|^2
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_k)}{n_k}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{15}
\]
Markov's inequality gives \(\widehat p\to p\) in probability.  Since \(U\) is a neighborhood of \(p\),
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}(\widehat p\in U)\longrightarrow1. \tag{16}
\]
This is the asserted branch-stability conclusion.

On the event in (16), \(F(\widehat p)=F_p^{\circ}(\widehat p)\).  Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}dimensional\text{-}delta\text{-}method}\) to (14) and the ambient derivative in (11).  Equality with probability tending to one transfers its conclusion from \(F_p^{\circ}\) to \(F\).  The scalar variance is
\[
 \nabla F(p)^{\mathsf T}\Omega\nabla F(p)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho_k^{-1}
   \nabla_kF(p)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_k\nabla_kF(p)
 =\sigma_F^2(p). \tag{17}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}
 \rightsquigarrow N\bigl(0,\sigma_F^2(p)\bigr). \tag{18}
\]

For variance consistency, put
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_k
 =\operatorname{diag}(\widehat p_k)
  -\widehat p_k\widehat p_k^{\mathsf T}.
\]
On the event in (16), equations (11), (13), and (15) imply, for every \(k\),
\[
 \nabla_kF(\widehat p)\to\nabla_kF(p),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Sigma_k\to\Sigma_k,
 \qquad
 (n_k/n)^{-1}\to\rho_k^{-1} \tag{19}
\]
in probability.  The branch-local standard-error rule may be assigned any fixed finite completion when its local recursive evaluation is unavailable; this choice is immaterial because the complement of (16) has probability tending to zero.  Substitution into the finite sum therefore gives
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F^2
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}(n_k/n)^{-1}
   \nabla_kF(\widehat p)^{\mathsf T}
   \widehat\Sigma_k
   \nabla_kF(\widehat p)
 \longrightarrow\sigma_F^2(p) \tag{20}
\]
in probability.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\text{-}variance}\) states that \(\sigma_F^2(p)>0\).  Hence
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F\longrightarrow\sigma_F(p)>0 \tag{21}
\]
in probability.

Combining (18), (21), and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:slutsky\text{-}studentization}\) gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_F}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1), \tag{22}
\]
where the ratio may be assigned any fixed value on \(\{\widehat\sigma_F=0\}\), an event whose probability tends to zero by (21).  On the event \(\{\widehat p\in U,\widehat\sigma_F>0\}\), whose probability tends to one, (4) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:wald\text{-}interval}\) give
\[
 \left\{
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\in\mathsf{CI}_{1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 =
 \left\{
 \left|
 \frac{\sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_F}
 \right|
 \leq z_{1-\alpha/2}
 \right\}. \tag{23}
\]
The standard normal distribution has no mass at either boundary point in (23).  Weak convergence in (22), together with the vanishing probability of the complementary event, therefore implies
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}\!\left\{
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\in\mathsf{CI}_{1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 \longrightarrow
 P\{|Z|\leq z_{1-\alpha/2}\}
 =1-\alpha,
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,1). \tag{24}
\]
The model \(\mathfrak m\) was arbitrary in the fiber, and every such model induces the same regime-law vector \(p\).  Hence the argument applies to every generating \(\mathfrak m\in\mathcal F_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(p)\).  This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
