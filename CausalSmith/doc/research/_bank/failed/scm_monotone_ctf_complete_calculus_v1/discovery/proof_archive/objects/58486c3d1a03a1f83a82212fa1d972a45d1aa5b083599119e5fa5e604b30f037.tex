\paragraph{Finite parameterization and enumeration.}
For \(v\in V\), let \(U(v)=\{u\in U_G:v\in\operatorname{ch}(u)\}\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\) permits every \(u\) to be padded to exactly \(b_u\) states.  A canonical mechanism at \(v\) assigns one of at most \(T_v\) signed-isotone response tables to each of the \(\prod_{u\in U(v)}b_u\) configurations of its exogenous parents.  Hence the number of joint structural profiles is at most
\[
 \prod_{v\in V}T_v^{\prod_{u\in U(v)}b_u}=A_{G,M}.
\]
After one mass in each simplex is eliminated, a model has at most \(N_{G,M}=\sum_u(b_u-1)\) free mass coordinates.  A pair has at most \(2N_{G,M}\), and each model has at most \(R_{G,M}\) joint latent configurations.  These are the asserted enumeration and size bounds.

For a fixed profile \(a\), use the rational polynomials \(\phi_{a,k,x}\), \(h_a\), and \(d_a\) constructed in the proof of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\).  Each is a sum of at most \(R_{G,M}\) product-law monomials.  The pair sentence in Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\) is therefore a rational-coefficient semialgebraic system.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) proves that its finite disjunction is satisfiable if and only if \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=0\), and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}real\text{-}closed\text{-}field\text{-}decision}\) makes the decision and sample extraction effective.

\paragraph{The empty-sign branch.}
Suppose \(M=\varnothing\).  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\), the first operation is published conditional \(\mathsf{CTFID}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}ctfid\text{-}completeness}\) states that this procedure returns an expression exactly for the queries identified in the unrestricted recursive semi-Markovian class.  With \(M=\varnothing\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) imposes no response-function restriction beyond the finite binary recursive semi-Markovian assumptions, so the published criterion is precisely Definition \(\mathrm{def:identification\text{-}criterion}\).  On success, \(\mathsf{MCTFID}\) returns the \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) expression unchanged and Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\) lifts it into \(\mathcal R(p)\).  On failure, conditional completeness gives unrestricted nonidentification, which is \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,\varnothing,\mathcal Z}(Q)=0\); Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) then makes the canonical pair sentence satisfiable, so the continuation returns \(\mathsf{FAIL}\).  Thus this branch has exactly the \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) success set.

\paragraph{The \(\mathsf{MID}\) comparison.}
First suppose \(M\ne\varnothing\).  If \(\mathsf{MID}\) succeeds with expression \(H\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:mid\text{-}soundness}\) gives
\[
 H(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m))=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m)
\]
for every legal model with positive query denominator.  Thus any two such models with the same menu law have the same query value, so \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\) returns \(H\) unchanged before invoking the algebraic oracle.

If instead \(M=\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{MID}\) succeeds, the same soundness implication gives global identification in the now unrestricted class.  Conditional \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) completeness then makes the preceding empty-sign branch succeed.  This proves success-set containment at \(M=\varnothing\), but the definition runs \(\mathsf{CTFID}\), not \(\mathsf{MID}\), so no equality of the two literal expressions is claimed.  Combining the two cases proves
\[
 \operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MID})\subseteq\operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MCTFID}).
\]

\paragraph{Failure certificate.}
If the two-model sentence is satisfiable, exact sample-point extraction provides two enumerated profiles and exact algebraic product-simplex mass vectors.  They define recursive semi-Markovian models because their masses factor over \(U_G\); their tables belong to the enumerated signed-isotone sets, so Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:signed\text{-}local\text{-}monotonicity}\) holds.  Exogenous indices belonging to distinct districts are disjoint, which gives district factorization.  The sampled constraints give positive denominators, equality of every menu cell, and
\[
 h_{a_0}(\lambda^0)d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)
 \ne h_{a_1}(\lambda^1)d_{a_0}(\lambda^0).
\]
Division by the two positive denominators yields unequal query values.  Definition \(\mathrm{def:fail\text{-}compiler\text{-}handle}\) decodes and verifies these recorded data, so it returns the claimed \((\mathfrak m_0,\mathfrak m_1,p^\star)\) with \(|\Omega_u|\le b_u\).  Conversely, Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) shows that failure of global identification makes some pair sentence satisfiable.  Therefore the procedure returns \(\mathsf{FAIL}\) exactly when the criterion is zero.

\paragraph{Identifying expression after an unsatisfiable pair sentence.}
Suppose the pair sentence is unsatisfiable.  Consider
\[
 \Gamma_Q
 =\bigcup_a\left\{(p,t):
 \exists\lambda\;\bigl[
 \Lambda_a(\lambda)\wedge p=\phi_a(\lambda)
 \wedge d_a(\lambda)>0\wedge t d_a(\lambda)=h_a(\lambda)
 \bigr]\right\}.
\]
This is a finite union of projections of rational semialgebraic sets and is effectively semialgebraic by quantifier elimination.  Every nonempty fiber of \(\Gamma_Q\) is a singleton: two distinct ordinates over the same \(p\) would supply two feasible canonical models with a common menu law, positive denominators, and unequal cross-products, contradicting unsatisfiability.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}cad\text{-}thom\text{-}sections}\) produces a finite sign-invariant cylindrical decomposition of the \(p\)-space.  On each cell in the projection of \(\Gamma_Q\), the singleton ordinate is an algebraic section selected by a rational polynomial and a Thom derivative-sign encoding.  Use the cell's polynomial sign description as a finite branch guard and its section as \(\operatorname{ThomRoot}_{\sigma}(h)\); Definition \(\mathrm{def:expression\text{-}grammar}\) admits these terms.  Give every cell outside the projection the rational value zero.  This constructs a globally totalized \(F\in\mathcal R(p)\).

The exact-input semantics are as follows.  At an arbitrary population vector \(p\), an exact ordered-field sign oracle selects the unique CAD guard, and the Thom encoding denotes the uniquely certified real section; this is an exact symbolic functional.  If \(p\) is represented by rational or real-algebraic coordinates, all specialized polynomial coefficients are algebraic and standard sign determination, Thom encoding, and real-root isolation evaluate the tree in finitely many exact operations.  In particular, empirical cell vectors are rational.  Nothing in the construction asserts finite bit computation for an arbitrarily encoded transcendental \(p\) without a sign oracle.

If \(\mathfrak m\in\mathfrak M(G,M)\) has \(P_{\mathfrak m}(B_Q)>0\), Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\) replaces it by a canonical model preserving its menu law, numerator, and denominator.  Therefore
\[
 \left(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m),\theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\right)\in\Gamma_Q,
\]
and the singleton-section construction gives
\[
 F(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m))=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m).
\]
Thus unsatisfiability yields a successful identifying expression.  Together with the failure argument and the two front ends, this proves
\[
 \operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MCTFID})
 =\{(G,M,\mathcal Z,Q):\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1\}.
\]

All response-table sets, structural-profile sets, polynomial lists, and CAD cells are finite.  The procedure makes at most \(A_{G,M}^2\) profile-pair tests and one finite CAD compilation after those tests, and every invoked exact real-algebraic algorithm terminates by the two declared real-algebraic lemmas.  Strictness of the \(\mathsf{MID}\) inclusion and district-compositional graphical compression remain separate open questions and are not used in this proof.
