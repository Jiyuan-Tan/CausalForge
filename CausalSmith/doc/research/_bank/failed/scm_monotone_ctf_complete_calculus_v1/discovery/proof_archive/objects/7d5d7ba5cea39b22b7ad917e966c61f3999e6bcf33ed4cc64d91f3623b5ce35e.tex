\paragraph{Finite canonical parameterization.}
For \(v\in V\), write
\[
 U(v)=\{u\in U_G:v\in\operatorname{ch}(u)\}.
\]
By Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\), every model relevant to the menu laws and to the two counterfactual probabilities in \(Q\) may be replaced by a counterfactually equivalent model for which \(|\Omega_u|\le b_u\).  Pad each smaller support by zero-mass states.  For any fixed assignment of the exogenous parents of \(v\), the local mechanism is one of at most \(T_v\) complete signed-isotone response tables.  There are \(\prod_{u\in U(v)}b_u\) such exogenous-parent assignments.  Consequently, the number of canonical joint structural profiles is at most
\[
 \prod_{v\in V}T_v^{\prod_{u\in U(v)}b_u}
 =A_{G,M}.
\]
For one such profile, the exogenous law is the product of the simplices
\[
 \lambda_{u,j}\ge 0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^{b_u}\lambda_{u,j}=1
 \quad (u\in U_G).
\]
Eliminating one coordinate from each simplex leaves at most
\[
 N_{G,M}=\sum_{u\in U_G}(b_u-1)
\]
free mass coordinates for one model and at most \(2N_{G,M}\) for an ordered pair.  A single model has at most
\[
 \prod_{u\in U_G}b_u=R_{G,M}
\]
joint latent configurations, so an ordered pair has at most \(2R_{G,M}\).  This proves all asserted enumeration and size bounds.

\paragraph{The exact nonidentification test.}
Fix a canonical structural profile \(a\).  The proof of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) constructs rational-coefficient polynomials \(\phi_{a,k,x}(\lambda)\), \(h_a(\lambda)\), and \(d_a(\lambda)\) for, respectively, every menu cell, the probability of \(A_Q\cap B_Q\), and the probability of \(B_Q\).  Each polynomial is a sum of at most \(R_{G,M}\) product-mass monomials.  Let \(\Lambda_a(\lambda)\) denote the displayed simplex constraints.  For an ordered profile pair \((a_0,a_1)\), consider
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \exists\lambda^0,\lambda^1\;[\;&
 \Lambda_{a_0}(\lambda^0)\wedge\Lambda_{a_1}(\lambda^1)
 \wedge d_{a_0}(\lambda^0)>0
 \wedge d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)>0\\
 &\wedge\ \phi_{a_0,k,x}(\lambda^0)
       =\phi_{a_1,k,x}(\lambda^1)
       \quad\text{for every \(k,x\)}\\
 &\wedge\ h_{a_0}(\lambda^0)d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)
       \ne h_{a_1}(\lambda^1)d_{a_0}(\lambda^0)\;].
 \end{aligned}
\]
The two strict inequalities are precisely the support conditions needed for the conditional query.  Because both denominators are positive, the final inequality is equivalent to inequality of the two conditional query values.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) proves that the finite disjunction of these systems is satisfiable if and only if
\[
 \operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=0.
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}real\text{-}closed\text{-}field\text{-}decision}\) makes every such test effective and returns an exact algebraic sample point when a system is satisfiable.

 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m_0)\ne\theta_Q(\mathfrak m_1).

\paragraph{Compilation on the identified branch.}
Suppose instead that the pair disjunction is unsatisfiable.  Form the finite semialgebraic relation
\[
 \Gamma_Q
 =\bigcup_a\left\{(p,t):
 \exists\lambda\;\left[
 \Lambda_a(\lambda)
 \wedge p=\phi_a(\lambda)
 \wedge d_a(\lambda)>0
 \wedge t d_a(\lambda)=h_a(\lambda)
 \right]\right\}.
\]
It is effectively semialgebraic by Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}real\text{-}closed\text{-}field\text{-}decision}\).  Every nonempty fiber over \(p\) is a singleton.  Indeed, if \((p,t_0)\) and \((p,t_1)\) belonged to \(\Gamma_Q\) with \(t_0\ne t_1\), their two witnessing profiles and masses would have positive denominators and equal menu cells, while
\[
 \frac{h_{a_0}(\lambda^0)}{d_{a_0}(\lambda^0)}=t_0
 \ne t_1=
 \frac{h_{a_1}(\lambda^1)}{d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)}.
\]
Cross-multiplication by the positive denominators would satisfy one of the forbidden pair systems, a contradiction.

Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}cad\text{-}thom\text{-}sections}\) to a cylindrical algebraic decomposition adapted to \(\Gamma_Q\).  Over every base cell in its projection, the unique ordinate is an algebraic section selected by a rational polynomial together with a finite Thom derivative-sign encoding.  The cell's rational polynomial sign description supplies a branch guard, and the section supplies a term \(\operatorname{ThomRoot}_{\sigma}(h)\).  There are finitely many cells.  Assigning the rational value zero to every cell outside the projection gives a globally total expression
\[
 F\in\mathcal R(p)
\]
under Definition \(\mathrm{def:expression\text{-}grammar}\).  At an arbitrary real population vector, its exact meaning uses the stipulated ordered-real-field polynomial-sign oracle.  For rational or real-algebraic coordinates, sign determination, Thom encodings, algebraic arithmetic, and real-root isolation evaluate it by a finite exact algorithm.  In particular, this applies to empirical cell vectors.  No division is performed without the guard \(d_a>0\), and the value off the positive-denominator projection is merely the declared totalization, not a causal identification claim.

Let \(\mathfrak m\in\mathfrak M(G,M)\) satisfy \(P_{\mathfrak m}(B_Q)>0\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\) supplies a canonical counterfactually equivalent model preserving every menu cell, \(P(A_Q\cap B_Q)\), and \(P(B_Q)\).  Hence
\[
 \bigl(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m),\theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\bigr)
 \in\Gamma_Q.
\]
The singleton-fiber construction therefore gives
\[
 F(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m))=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m).
\]
Thus an unsatisfiable pair disjunction produces the required identifying functional.

\paragraph{Front ends and success sets.}
First let \(M=\varnothing\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\) runs conditional \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) first and lifts every successful expression unchanged.  In this case Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) is the unrestricted finite binary recursive semi-Markovian class.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}ctfid\text{-}completeness}\) therefore shows that this branch succeeds exactly when \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,\varnothing,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1\), and that every returned expression has the asserted causal value.  If \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) fails, the same completeness result gives \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,\varnothing,\mathcal Z}(Q)=0\), so the exact pair system is satisfiable and the continuation returns a failure certificate.  Hence the \(M=\varnothing\) branch has exactly the \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) success set.

Now let \(M\ne\varnothing\).  If \(\mathsf{MID}\) returns an expression \(H\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:mid\text{-}soundness}\) gives
\[
 H(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m))=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m)
\]
for every legal model with positive query denominator.  Thus two such models with the same menu law must have the same query value, and \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1\).  The procedure returns \(H\) unchanged.  If \(\mathsf{MID}\) fails, the exact pair test and the preceding two paragraphs return a countermodel certificate when the criterion is zero and compile \(F\) when it is one.  Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MCTFID})
 =\{(G,M,\mathcal Z,Q):
   \operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1\}.
\]

It remains to compare success sets with \(\mathsf{MID}\) when \(M=\varnothing\).  If \(\mathsf{MID}\) succeeds, its soundness implies global identification in the unrestricted class.  Conditional \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) completeness then makes the empty-sign front end succeed.  This proves containment, but that front end returns the \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) expression, so it need not return the literal \(\mathsf{MID}\) expression.  Together with the nonempty-sign case,
\[
 \operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MID})
