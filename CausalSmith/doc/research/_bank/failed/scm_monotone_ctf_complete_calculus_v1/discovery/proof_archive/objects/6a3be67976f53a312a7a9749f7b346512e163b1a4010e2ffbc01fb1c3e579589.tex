\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(d=2^{|V|}\), fix an enumeration \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) of \(\{0,1\}^{V}\), and fix a generating model
\[
 \mathfrak m\in\mathcal F_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(p).
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:menu\text{-}compatibility}\) ensures that this fiber is nonempty. Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:positive\text{-}query\text{-}denominator}\) gives
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}(B_Q)\geq\kappa>0. \tag{1}
\]
By the successful-output soundness clause of the signed \(\mathsf{MCTFID}\) completeness-and-soundness theorem resolving \(\mathrm{oeq:complete\text{-}mctfid}\), the premise
\[
 \mathsf{MCTFID}(G,M,\mathcal Z,Q)=F\in\mathcal R(p)
\]
implies, for every signed-monotone model with positive query denominator,
\[
 F\bigl(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m)\bigr)=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m). \tag{2}
\]
The definition of the fiber gives \(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m)=p\). Combining this identity, (1), and (2) yields the causal centering identity
\[
 F(p)=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m). \tag{3}
\]
Thus the causal target has not been identified by definition: equation (3) is the consequence of the declared successful-output soundness result and positivity.

We next establish local regularity of the returned expression. The expression tree of \(F\) is finite. By \(\mathrm{ass:analytic\text{-}branch\text{-}margin}\),
\[
 \lambda_F(p)\geq\kappa>0. \tag{4}
\]
We claim that there is an ambient open neighborhood \(U\subset\mathbb R^{Kd}\) of \(p\) on which the branch selected at \(p\) is unchanged, every active quotient denominator is nonzero, every active root remains the same isolated simple root, and the selected expression is continuously differentiable.

This claim follows by induction through the finite expression tree. Cell coordinates and rational constants are analytic; finite sums and products preserve continuous differentiability. If \(a(q)/b(q)\) is an active quotient, then (4) and the definition of \(\lambda_F\) give \(|b(p)|\geq\kappa\). Continuity therefore gives a neighborhood on which \(|b(q)|\geq\kappa/2\). If a branch is selected by a guard \(g\), then \(|g(p)|\geq\kappa\), so continuity gives a neighborhood on which \(g(q)\) has the same sign as \(g(p)\).

It remains to justify a root node. Write its selected value as
\[
 t_0=\operatorname{Root}_j(h;I)(p),\qquad h(t_0,p)=0,
\]
where \(I=(a,b)\) is its rational isolating interval. From (4),
\[
 |\partial_t h(t_0,p)|\geq\kappa,
 \qquad
 \min\{t_0-a,b-t_0\}\geq\kappa. \tag{5}
\]
Choose \(0<\varepsilon<\kappa/2\). Since \(h\) is polynomial and \(\partial_t h(t_0,p)\ne0\), after shrinking a neighborhood \(U_0\) of \(p\), the derivative \(\partial_t h(t,q)\) has a constant sign and absolute value at least \(\kappa/2\) whenever \(|t-t_0|\leq\varepsilon\) and \(q\in U_0\). At \(q=p\), the mean-value identity makes \(h(t_0-\varepsilon,p)\) and \(h(t_0+\varepsilon,p)\) have opposite signs. Continuity preserves those signs after another shrinking of \(U_0\). Hence, for every \(q\in U_0\), the intermediate-value identity and strict monotonicity in \(t\) give a unique root \(t(q)\in(t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon)\subset I\).

For \(q,q'\in U_0\), the mean-value identity in \(t\) and the lower derivative bound give, locally for a finite constant \(C\),
\[
 |t(q)-t(q')|
 \leq \frac{2}{\kappa}
       |h(t(q'),q)-h(t(q'),q')|
 \leq C\|q-q'\|. \tag{6}
\]
Thus \(t\) is continuous. Applying the ordinary difference quotient to \(h(t(q),q)=0\), using (6), and then taking the limit gives
\[
 \nabla t(q)
 =-\frac{\nabla_q h(t(q),q)}{\partial_t h(t(q),q)}. \tag{7}
\]
The right-hand side is continuous on a smaller neighborhood, so the root node is continuously differentiable and (7) is exactly the recursive root-differentiation rule. Because the tree has finitely many guards, quotients, and roots, intersecting their neighborhoods proves the claim. In particular, the selected branch has an ambient \(C^1\) extension even if some coordinates of \(p\) lie on the boundary of a probability simplex, and
\[
 q\longmapsto \nabla F(q)
 \quad\text{is continuous on }U. \tag{8}
\]

We now derive the joint sampling limit. For observation \(i\) in regime \(k\), define the one-hot vector
\[
 Y_{ki}=\bigl(\mathbf 1\{X_{ki}=x_1\},\ldots,
                    \mathbf 1\{X_{ki}=x_d\}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:independent\text{-}regime\text{-}sampling}\), the vectors are independent and identically distributed within each regime, distinct regimes are independent, and
\[
 E(Y_{ki})=p_k,qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y_{ki})
 =\operatorname{diag}(p_k)-p_kp_k^{\mathsf T}
 =\Sigma_k. \tag{9}
\]
The coordinates of \(Y_{ki}\) are bounded, so all second moments exist. Moreover, \(\mathrm{ass:stable\text{-}regime\text{-}shares}\) implies \(n_k\to\infty\) and
\[
 \frac{n}{n_k}\longrightarrow\rho_k^{-1}. \tag{10}
\]
Applying \(\mathrm{lem:multivariate\text{-}iid\text{-}clt}\) in each regime, using independence across the fixed finite number \(K\) of regimes, and then using (10), we obtain
\[
 \sqrt n\,(\widehat p-p)
 \rightsquigarrow
 N_{Kd}(0,\Omega),
 \qquad
 \Omega=\operatorname{blockdiag}
 \bigl(\rho_1^{-1}\Sigma_1,\ldots,
       \rho_K^{-1}\Sigma_K\bigr). \tag{11}
\]
The covariance in (11) may be singular, as it must be because each multinomial vector sums to one; no full-rank condition is required. Equation (11) also implies \(\widehat p\to p\) in probability. Since \(U\) is a neighborhood of \(p\),
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}(\widehat p\in U)\longrightarrow1. \tag{12}
\]
This proves the asserted branch-stability probability.

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}dimensional\text{-}delta\text{-}method}\) to (11) and the ambient derivative established in (8). The resulting scalar variance is
\[
 \nabla F(p)^{\mathsf T}\Omega\nabla F(p)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho_k^{-1}
   \nabla_kF(p)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_k\nabla_kF(p)
 =\sigma_F^2(p). \tag{13}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}
 \rightsquigarrow N\bigl(0,\sigma_F^2(p)\bigr). \tag{14}
\]

For variance consistency, set
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_k
 =\operatorname{diag}(\widehat p_k)
  -\widehat p_k\widehat p_k^{\mathsf T}.
\]
On the event in (12), (8), (10), and \(\widehat p\to p\) give, for every \(k\),
\[
 \nabla_kF(\widehat p)\to\nabla_kF(p),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Sigma_k\to\Sigma_k,
 \qquad
 (n_k/n)^{-1}\to\rho_k^{-1} \tag{15}
\]
in probability. The complement of that event has probability tending to zero, so any fixed completion of the branch-local recursive derivative away from its regular branch is asymptotically immaterial. Substituting (15) into the finite sum defining the variance estimator yields
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F^2
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}(n_k/n)^{-1}
   \nabla_kF(\widehat p)^{\mathsf T}
   \widehat\Sigma_k
   \nabla_kF(\widehat p)
 \longrightarrow\sigma_F^2(p) \tag{16}
\]
in probability. Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\text{-}variance}\) states that \(\sigma_F^2(p)>0\). Hence
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F\longrightarrow\sigma_F(p)>0 \tag{17}
\]
in probability.

Combining (14), (17), and \(\mathrm{lem:slutsky\text{-}studentization}\) gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_F}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1). \tag{18}
\]
By (3), the event that the Wald interval covers the causal target is exactly
\[
 \left\{
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\in\mathsf{CI}_{1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 =
 \left\{
 \left|
 \frac{\sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_F}
 \right|
 \leq z_{1-\alpha/2}
 \right\}. \tag{19}
\]
The standard normal law has no mass at the two boundary points in (19), so (18) implies
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}\!left\{
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\in\mathsf{CI}_{1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 \longrightarrow
 P\{|Z|\leq z_{1-\alpha/2}\}
 =1-\alpha,
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,1). \tag{20}
\]
Every \(\mathfrak m\) in the fiber induces the same regime-law vector \(p\), so the sampling argument applies to each such generating model. This proves all assertions. \(\square\)
