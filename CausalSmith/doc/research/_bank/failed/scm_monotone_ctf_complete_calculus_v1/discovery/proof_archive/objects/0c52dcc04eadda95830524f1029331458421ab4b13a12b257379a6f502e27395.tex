By Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\), it suffices to quantify over canonical models with the support bounds in Definition \(\mathrm{def:canonical\text{-}bound}\).  Pad a support of size less than \(b_u\) by zero-mass states.  For a fixed enumeration \(a\) of all signed-isotone structural tables, write
\[
 \lambda_{u,j}\ge0,\qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^{b_u}\lambda_{u,j}=1.
\]
Eliminating \(\lambda_{u,b_u}\) leaves at most \(\sum_{u\in U_G}(b_u-1)\) free real mass coordinates for one model.

For \(\omega\in\prod_u\{1,\ldots,b_u\}\), its product-law probability is the monomial
\[
 w_\lambda(\omega)=\prod_{u\in U_G}\lambda_{u,\omega_u}.
\]
The table profile \(a\) deterministically produces an outcome \(X^{a,z_k}(\omega)\) under regime \(z_k\).  Thus every menu cell is the integer-coefficient polynomial
\[
 \phi_{a,k,x}(\lambda)
 =\sum_{\omega}
 \mathbf 1\{X^{a,z_k}(\omega)=x\}w_\lambda(\omega).
\]
Recursive evaluation of the counterfactual literals similarly gives the denominator and numerator polynomials
\[
 d_a(\lambda)=\sum_{\omega}\mathbf 1\{B_Q(\omega)\}w_\lambda(\omega),
 \qquad
 h_a(\lambda)=\sum_{\omega}\mathbf 1\{A_Q(\omega)\cap B_Q(\omega)\}w_\lambda(\omega).
\]
Every displayed sum has at most \(R_{G,M}\) monomials.

Let \(\Lambda_a(\lambda)\) denote the simplex equalities and inequalities for profile \(a\).  Nonidentification is exactly the finite disjunction, over ordered profile pairs \((a_0,a_1)\), of
\[
 \exists\lambda^0,\lambda^1\;\left[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\Lambda_{a_0}(\lambda^0)\wedge\Lambda_{a_1}(\lambda^1),\\
 &d_{a_0}(\lambda^0)>0\wedge d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)>0,\\
 &\phi_{a_0,k,x}(\lambda^0)=\phi_{a_1,k,x}(\lambda^1)
       &&\text{for every \(k,x\)},\\
 &h_{a_0}(\lambda^0)d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)
   \ne h_{a_1}(\lambda^1)d_{a_0}(\lambda^0).
 \end{aligned}\right]
\]
The last line is equivalent to inequality of the conditional query values because both denominators are positive.  Each disjunct has at most \(2\sum_{u\in U_G}(b_u-1)\) free mass coordinates.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}real\text{-}closed\text{-}field\text{-}decision}\) decides this rational semialgebraic sentence and extracts an exact algebraic sample point whenever it is satisfiable.

If a disjunct is satisfiable, its two profiles and sampled mass vectors define \(\mathfrak m_0,\mathfrak m_1\in\mathfrak M(G,M)\).  They have positive query denominators and
\[
 p^\star_{k,x}
 =\phi_{a_0,k,x}(\lambda^0)
 =\phi_{a_1,k,x}(\lambda^1)
\]
for every menu cell, while the cross-product inequality gives \(\theta_Q(\mathfrak m_0)\ne\theta_Q(\mathfrak m_1)\).  Each model uses at most \(R_{G,M}\) joint latent configurations, so the pair uses at most \(2R_{G,M}\).

Conversely, if \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=0\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:identification\text{-}criterion}\) gives two models with a common menu law, positive denominators, and unequal query values.  Apply Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\) to each.  Counterfactual equivalence preserves its menu law, numerator, and denominator, so the resulting canonical pair satisfies one displayed disjunct.  The disjunction is therefore satisfiable exactly when the global identification criterion is false.  Exact decision and sample-point extraction prove all claims.
