\textbf{Proof.}
Assume \(M=\varnothing\), and fix \(v\in V\). By the definition of the signed-parent set,
\[
 S_v=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 m_v=|S_v|=0,
 \qquad
 r_v=|\operatorname{pa}_G(v)\setminus S_v|
     =|\operatorname{pa}_G(v)|.
\]
The Boolean lattice \(\{0,1\}^{0}\) has one element. Its only up-sets are the empty set and the whole singleton, so \(D_0=2\). More explicitly, for each of the \(2^{r_v}\) unrestricted-parent configurations, Definition \(\mathrm{def:signed\text{-}response\text{-}types}\) chooses one of those two up-sets. Membership of the unique lattice element in the chosen up-set is an arbitrary output bit. Consequently this choice is in bijection with an arbitrary Boolean map
\[
 \{0,1\}^{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}.
\]
Thus the local atoms are exactly the unrestricted Boolean response functions, and not merely an equinumerous collection. Their number is
\[
 T_v=D_{m_v}^{2^{r_v}}
     =D_0^{2^{r_v}}
     =2^{2^{|\operatorname{pa}_G(v)|}}.
\]

There is no signed coordinate to normalize or compare. Therefore the extra order labels in Definition \(\mathrm{def:order\text{-}amwn}\) impose no restriction beyond the ordinary ancestral multi-world network. Since the local response-table atoms just exhibited are precisely the unrestricted atoms, the fixed exact-atom expansion of Definition \(\mathrm{def:order\text{-}normal\text{-}form}\) is precisely the ordinary AMWN event normalization. This proves the asserted collapse of \(\mathsf N_{G,\varnothing}(Q)\).

It remains to prove equality of the two success sets, including the failure branch. First suppose that \(\mathsf{CTFID}(G,\mathcal Z,Q)\) succeeds with expression \(H\). The first branch of Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\) runs that procedure when \(M=\varnothing\) and returns \(H\) immediately, merely viewing it as an element of \(\mathcal R(p)\). Hence the two returned functionals are algebraically equivalent; indeed, the expression is unchanged.

Conversely, suppose that \(\mathsf{CTFID}(G,\mathcal Z,Q)\) fails. When \(M=\varnothing\), signed local monotonicity is vacuous, so Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) gives exactly the finite-binary unrestricted recursive semi-Markovian class. By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}ctfid\text{-}completeness}\), failure of \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) means that the positive-denominator conditional query is not identifiable on this class. Definition \(\mathrm{def:identification\text{-}criterion}\) therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{GID}_{G,\varnothing,\mathcal Z}(Q)=0.
\]
Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) now says that the finite pair system examined by the continuation is satisfiable: it has two legal models \(\mathfrak m_0,\mathfrak m_1\in\mathfrak M(G,\varnothing)\) with positive query denominators and
\[
 P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m_0)=P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m_1),
 \qquad
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m_0)\ne\theta_Q(\mathfrak m_1).
\]
By the terminal branches of Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\), exact quantifier elimination detects this satisfiable system, extracts its sample certificate, and returns \((\mathsf{FAIL},\tau_{\mathsf F})\). Thus \(\mathsf{MCTFID}\) cannot succeed after an empty-sign \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) failure. Combining the two cases proves equality of success sets and algebraic equivalence of the successful outputs. Notice that the \(M\ne\varnothing\) \(\mathsf{MID}\) branch is never invoked in this argument. \(\square\)
