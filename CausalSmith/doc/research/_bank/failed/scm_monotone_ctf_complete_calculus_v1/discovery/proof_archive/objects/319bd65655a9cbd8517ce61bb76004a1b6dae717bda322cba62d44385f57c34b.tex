By Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\), it is enough to quantify over canonical models with the displayed support bounds.  Pad a support of size smaller than \(b_u\) by zero-mass states.  For each fixed enumeration \(a\) of all signed-isotone structural tables, write
\[
 \lambda_{u,j}\geq0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^{b_u}\lambda_{u,j}=1
 \]
for its exogenous masses.  Eliminating \(\lambda_{u,b_u}\) leaves at most \(\sum_{u\in U_G}(b_u-1)\) free real variables for one model.

For a joint latent state \(\omega\in\prod_u\{1,\ldots,b_u\}\), its probability is the monomial
\[
 w_\lambda(\omega)=\prod_{u\in U_G}\lambda_{u,\omega_u}.
\]
The fixed structural-table profile \(a\) deterministically produces an outcome \(X^{a,z_k}(\omega)\) in regime \(z_k\).  Therefore every menu cell is the polynomial
\[
 \phi_{a,k,x}(\lambda)
 =\sum_{\omega}
 \mathbf 1\{X^{a,z_k}(\omega)=x\}
 w_\lambda(\omega).
\]
Likewise, with the counterfactual literals evaluated recursively from \(a\), define the polynomial denominator and numerator
\[
 d_a(\lambda)
 =\sum_{\omega}\mathbf 1\{B_Q(\omega)\}w_\lambda(\omega),
 \qquad
 h_a(\lambda)
 =\sum_{\omega}\mathbf 1\{A_Q(\omega)\cap B_Q(\omega)\}w_\lambda(\omega).
\]
All coefficients in these polynomials are integers, and every sum has at most \(R_{G,M}\) terms.

For two enumerated table profiles \(a_0,a_1\), let \(\Lambda(\lambda^i)\) denote their finitely many simplex inequalities and equalities.  Nonidentification is exactly the following finite disjunction of existential sentences:
\[
 \bigvee_{a_0,a_1}
 \exists\lambda^0,\lambda^1\;\left[
 \begin{array}{l}
 \Lambda(\lambda^0)\wedge\Lambda(\lambda^1),\\
 d_{a_0}(\lambda^0)>0\wedge d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)>0,\\
 \phi_{a_0,k,x}(\lambda^0)=\phi_{a_1,k,x}(\lambda^1)
 \quad\text{for every }k,x,\\
 h_{a_0}(\lambda^0)d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)
 \neq h_{a_1}(\lambda^1)d_{a_0}(\lambda^0)
 \end{array}
 \right].
\]
The final inequality is equivalent to inequality of the two conditional query values because both displayed denominators are positive.  The formula has at most
\[
 2\sum_{u\in U_G}(b_u-1)
\]
free mass coordinates for each enumerated pair.  By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}real\text{-}closed\text{-}field\text{-}decision}\), exact real quantifier elimination decides every disjunct and produces an exact algebraic sample point whenever a disjunct is satisfiable.

If a disjunct is satisfiable, the sampled masses and its two enumerated table profiles define canonical models \(\mathfrak m_0,\mathfrak m_1\in\mathfrak M(G,M)\).  They use at most \(R_{G,M}\) joint latent configurations each, their denominators are positive, and
\[
 p^\star_{k,x}
 =\phi_{a_0,k,x}(\lambda^0)
 =\phi_{a_1,k,x}(\lambda^1)
\]
is their common menu law, while the cross-product inequality gives \(\theta_Q(\mathfrak m_0)\neq\theta_Q(\mathfrak m_1)\).

Conversely, if \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=0\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:identification\text{-}criterion}\) supplies a pair of models with the same menu law, positive denominators, and unequal query values.  Apply Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\) separately to the two models.  It preserves their menu laws and counterfactual numerator and denominator probabilities, so the resulting canonical pair satisfies one of the displayed disjuncts.  Thus the disjunction is satisfiable if and only if the global identification criterion is false.  Its exact decision proves the first assertion, and exact sample-point extraction proves the stated witness assertion and both size bounds.
