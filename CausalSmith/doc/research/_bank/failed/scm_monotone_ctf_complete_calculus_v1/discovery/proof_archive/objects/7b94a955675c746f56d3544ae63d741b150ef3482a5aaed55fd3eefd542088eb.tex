\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(d=2^{|V|}\), fix an enumeration \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) of \(\{0,1\}^{V}\), and fix an arbitrary generating model
\[
 \mathfrak m\in\mathcal F_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(p).
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:menu\text{-}compatibility}\) ensures that the displayed fiber is nonempty.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:menu\text{-}fiber}\), membership of \(\mathfrak m\) gives
\[
 P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m)=p. \tag{1}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:positive\text{-}query\text{-}denominator}\) gives
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}(B_Q)\geq\kappa>0. \tag{2}
\]
The successful-output soundness clause of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}oracle\text{-}complete\text{-}mctfid}\), applied to the premise
\[
 \mathsf{MCTFID}(G,M,\mathcal Z,Q)=F\in\mathcal R(p),
\]
therefore yields
\[
 F\bigl(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m)\bigr)=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m). \tag{3}
\]
Combining (1) and (3) proves the causal centering identity
\[
 F(p)=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m). \tag{4}
\]
Thus the causal target and the observed-law functional are not identified with one another by definition: their equality in (4) follows from successful-output soundness and the positive-denominator condition.

We next establish the local regularity needed for inference.  The expression tree of \(F\) is finite, and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:analytic\text{-}branch\text{-}margin}\) states that
\[
 \lambda_F(p)\geq\kappa>0. \tag{5}
\]
We claim that there are an ambient open neighborhood \(U\subset\mathbb R^{Kd}\) of \(p\) and a continuously differentiable map \(F_p^{\circ}:U\to\mathbb R\) such that \(F(q)=F_p^{\circ}(q)\) for every \(q\in U\) in the domain of the expression grammar.  On \(U\), the finite branch selected at \(p\) is unchanged, every active quotient denominator remains nonzero, and every active root selector returns the continuation of the root selected at \(p\).

We prove the claim by induction through the finite active expression tree.  Cell coordinates and rational constants are analytic, and finite sums and products preserve continuous differentiability.  If \(a(q)/b(q)\) is an active quotient, Definition \(\mathrm{def:analytic\text{-}branch\text{-}margin}\) and (5) give \(|b(p)|\geq\kappa\).  Continuity gives a neighborhood on which \(|b(q)|\geq\kappa/2\).  If a total finite branch is selected by polynomial sign guards \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\), then (5) gives \(|g_j(p)|\geq\kappa\) for every active guard.  After shrinking the neighborhood, every \(g_j(q)\) has the same sign as \(g_j(p)\), so the branch is fixed.

Consider first an active rational-interval root node.  Write
\[
 t_0=\operatorname{Root}_j(h;I)(p),
 \qquad h(t_0,p)=0,
 \qquad I=(a,b).
\]
The margin in (5) implies
\[
 |\partial_t h(t_0,p)|\geq\kappa,
 \qquad
 \min\{t_0-a,b-t_0\}\geq\kappa. \tag{6}
\]
Choose \(0<\varepsilon<\kappa/2\).  Since \(h\) is a polynomial and \(\partial_t h(t_0,p)\neq0\), there is a neighborhood \(U_0\) of \(p\) on which \(\partial_t h(t,q)\) has constant sign and absolute value at least \(\kappa/2\) whenever \(|t-t_0|\leq\varepsilon\).  At \(q=p\), the mean-value theorem makes \(h(t_0-\varepsilon,p)\) and \(h(t_0+\varepsilon,p)\) have opposite signs.  After shrinking \(U_0\), the same is true for every \(q\in U_0\).  The intermediate-value theorem and strict monotonicity in \(t\) consequently give a unique root
\[
 t(q)\in(t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon). \tag{7}
\]
Because \(I\) isolates \(t_0\) at \(p\), the continuous function \(|h(\,·\,,p)|\) has a positive minimum on the compact set
\[
 [a,b]\setminus(t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon).
\]
Uniform continuity of the polynomial coefficients permits a further shrinking of \(U_0\) on which no root enters that compact set or crosses either endpoint of \(I\).  The active existence, uniqueness, and index guards remain true because their signs were already fixed.  Hence the exact \(\operatorname{Root}_j(h;I)\) selector equals \(t(q)\) throughout this smaller neighborhood.

Now consider an active Thom selector
\[
 t_0=\operatorname{ThomRoot}_{\sigma}(h)(p).
\]
Its active guard certifies that \(t_0\) is the unique real root of \(h(\,·\,,p)\) having the derivative-sign vector \(\sigma\).  The isolated-root derivative is one of the quantities in Definition \(\mathrm{def:analytic\text{-}branch\text{-}margin}\), so (5) again gives
\[
 h(t_0,p)=0,
 \qquad |\partial_t h(t_0,p)|\geq\kappa. \tag{8}
\]
The preceding local monotonicity argument, without an interval endpoint, gives a unique continuous root continuation \(t(q)\) near \(t_0\).  Each entry
\[
 q\longmapsto \partial_t^{\ell}h(t(q),q)
\]
appearing in the finite Thom encoding is continuous.  Every nonzero derivative-sign test used by the active Thom guard is separated from zero by (5), and hence retains its sign.  A zero derivative-sign equality that were not identically preserved on the selected section would itself be an active zero-valued guard and would force \(\lambda_F(p)=0\), contrary to (5); identities that hold on the section remain identities.  Thus \(t(q)\) retains the encoding \(\sigma\).  The fixed active uniqueness guard then shows that \(\operatorname{ThomRoot}_{\sigma}(h)(q)=t(q)\).  This proves local stability for guarded Thom-root nodes as well as for rational-interval root nodes.

For either kind of active root, take \(q,q'\) in a sufficiently small common neighborhood.  The mean-value theorem in \(t\), the lower bound on \(|\partial_t h|\), and local Lipschitz continuity of the polynomial in its parameter coordinates give a finite constant \(C\) such that
\[
 |t(q)-t(q')|
 \leq \frac{2}{\kappa}
       |h(t(q'),q)-h(t(q'),q')|
 \leq C\|q-q'\|. \tag{9}
\]
Apply a coordinatewise difference quotient to \(h(t(q),q)=0\), use (9), and pass to the limit.  This gives
\[
 \nabla t(q)
 =-\frac{\nabla_q h(t(q),q)}{\partial_t h(t(q),q)}. \tag{10}
\]
The right-hand side is continuous after one more shrinking of the neighborhood.  Formula (10) is exactly the recursive differentiation rule for either root syntax.  Intersecting the finitely many neighborhoods for all active guards, quotients, and roots proves the claim.  In particular,
\[
 q\longmapsto\nabla F_p^{\circ}(q)
 \quad\text{is continuous on }U. \tag{11}
\]
The construction is an ambient extension, so (11) remains valid even when some cells of \(p\) lie on the boundary of their probability simplices.  No positivity or rank condition on individual multinomial cells is needed.

We now derive the joint sampling limit.  For observation \(i\) in regime \(k\), define the one-hot vector
\[
 Y_{ki}=\bigl(\mathbf 1\{X_{ki}=x_1\},\ldots,
                    \mathbf 1\{X_{ki}=x_d\}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:independent\text{-}regime\text{-}sampling}\), these vectors are independent and identically distributed within each regime, samples from distinct regimes are independent, and
\[
 E(Y_{ki})=p_k,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y_{ki})
 =\operatorname{diag}(p_k)-p_kp_k^{\mathsf T}
 =\Sigma_k. \tag{12}
\]
Their coordinates are bounded, so all second moments exist.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:stable\text{-}regime\text{-}shares}\) implies \(n_k\to\infty\) and
\[
 \frac{n}{n_k}\longrightarrow\rho_k^{-1}. \tag{13}
\]
Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:multivariate\text{-}iid\text{-}clt}\) in each regime.  Independence across the fixed finite number \(K\) of regimes makes the joint characteristic function the product of the regime characteristic functions.  Together with (13), this yields
\[
 \sqrt n\,(\widehat p-p)
 \rightsquigarrow
 N_{Kd}(0,\Omega),
 \qquad
 \Omega=\operatorname{blockdiag}
 \bigl(\rho_1^{-1}\Sigma_1,\ldots,
       \rho_K^{-1}\Sigma_K\bigr). \tag{14}
\]
The covariance in (14) may be singular because every multinomial cell vector sums to one; the multivariate central limit theorem permits such a covariance, so no rank assumption is required.  Directly from (12),
\[
 E_{\mathfrak m}\|\widehat p-p\|^2
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_k)}{n_k}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{15}
\]
Markov's inequality gives \(\widehat p\to p\) in probability.  Since \(U\) is a neighborhood of \(p\),
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}(\widehat p\in U)\longrightarrow1. \tag{16}
\]
This is the asserted branch-stability conclusion.

On the event in (16), \(F(\widehat p)=F_p^{\circ}(\widehat p)\).  Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}dimensional\text{-}delta\text{-}method}\) to (14) and the ambient derivative in (11).  Equality with probability tending to one transfers its conclusion from \(F_p^{\circ}\) to \(F\).  The scalar variance is
\[
 \nabla F(p)^{\mathsf T}\Omega\nabla F(p)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho_k^{-1}
   \nabla_kF(p)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_k\nabla_kF(p)
 =\sigma_F^2(p). \tag{17}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}
 \rightsquigarrow N\bigl(0,\sigma_F^2(p)\bigr). \tag{18}
\]

For variance consistency, put
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_k
 =\operatorname{diag}(\widehat p_k)
  -\widehat p_k\widehat p_k^{\mathsf T}.
\]
On the event in (16), equations (11), (13), and (15) imply, for every \(k\),
\[
 \nabla_kF(\widehat p)\to\nabla_kF(p),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Sigma_k\to\Sigma_k,
 \qquad
 (n_k/n)^{-1}\to\rho_k^{-1} \tag{19}
\]
in probability.  The branch-local standard-error rule may be assigned any fixed finite completion when its local recursive evaluation is unavailable; this choice is immaterial because the complement of (16) has probability tending to zero.  Substitution into the finite sum therefore gives
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F^2
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}(n_k/n)^{-1}
   \nabla_kF(\widehat p)^{\mathsf T}
   \widehat\Sigma_k
   \nabla_kF(\widehat p)
 \longrightarrow\sigma_F^2(p) \tag{20}
\]
in probability.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\text{-}variance}\) states that \(\sigma_F^2(p)>0\).  Hence
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F\longrightarrow\sigma_F(p)>0 \tag{21}
\]
in probability.

Combining (18), (21), and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:slutsky\text{-}studentization}\) gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_F}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1), \tag{22}
\]
where the ratio may be assigned any fixed value on \(\{\widehat\sigma_F=0\}\), an event whose probability tends to zero by (21).  On the event \(\{\widehat p\in U,\widehat\sigma_F>0\}\), whose probability tends to one, (4) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:wald\text{-}interval}\) give
\[
 \left\{
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\in\mathsf{CI}_{1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 =
 \left\{
 \left|
 \frac{\sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_F}
 \right|
 \leq z_{1-\alpha/2}
 \right\}. \tag{23}
\]
The standard normal distribution has no mass at either boundary point in (23).  The scalar characterization of weak convergence by distribution functions at continuity points, applied to (22), and the vanishing probability of the complementary event therefore imply
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}\!\left\{
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\in\mathsf{CI}_{1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 \longrightarrow
 P\{|Z|\leq z_{1-\alpha/2}\}
 =1-\alpha,
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,1). \tag{24}
\]
The model \(\mathfrak m\) was arbitrary in the fiber, and every such model induces the same regime-law vector \(p\).  Hence the argument applies to every generating \(\mathfrak m\in\mathcal F_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(p)\).

Finally, the exact-real input convention is as follows.  At an arbitrary population vector \(p\), the guards and root selectors in \(F(p)\) have exact symbolic real semantics, equivalently semantics relative to an exact sign oracle; the argument does not claim finite rational arithmetic for an arbitrarily encoded transcendental input.  In the estimator, every coordinate of \(\widehat p\) is a ratio of integers.  Therefore the rational polynomials at \(\widehat p\) are exactly represented, and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}cad\text{-}thom\text{-}sections}\) supplies finite standard algebraic sign determination, root isolation, and Thom-root selection.  Thus \(F(\widehat p)\), its active branch, and the recursively differentiated gradient are finite exact maps of the empirical cell counts whenever the selected branch is regular; (16) shows that this is the relevant branch with probability tending to one.  This proves every assertion. \(\square\)
