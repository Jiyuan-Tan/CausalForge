\paragraph{Response tables.}
Fix \(\mathfrak m\in\mathfrak M(G,M)\).  For a complete assignment \(u\) of the exogenous variables, define the local response table
\[
 R_v(u)(x_{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)})
 =f_v(x_{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)},u_{\operatorname{pa}_U(v)}).
\]
For \(w\in S_v\), put \(z_w=x_w\) when the sign of \(w\to v\) is \(+1\), and put \(z_w=1-x_w\) when it is \(-1\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:signed\text{-}local\text{-}monotonicity}\) implies, coordinate by coordinate, that the normalized table is nondecreasing in every \(z_w\).  Thus, for each fixed assignment of the \(r_v\) unrestricted parents, the set of normalized signed-parent assignments on which the output is one is an up-set of \(\{0,1\}^{m_v}\).  There are \(D_{m_v}\) possible up-sets and \(2^{r_v}\) unrestricted-parent assignments.  Hence the set \(\mathcal T_v\) of possible complete local tables satisfies
\[
 |\mathcal T_v|\le D_{m_v}^{2^{r_v}}=T_v.
\]

\paragraph{Finite support.}
For a district \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\), let
\[
 U_D=\{u\in U_G:C(u)=D\},\qquad
 \mathcal T_D=\prod_{v\in D}\mathcal T_v,\qquad
 q_D=|\mathcal T_D|\le\prod_{v\in D}T_v.
\]
Every exogenous parent of a vertex in \(D\) belongs to \(U_D\), so the district response-table vector is a measurable map
\[
 R_D=(R_v:v\in D)=H_D(U_D)\in\mathcal T_D.
\]
The sets \(U_D\) are disjoint across districts, and their coordinates are independent by \(\mathrm{ass:semi\text{-}markovian}\).

Fix \(u_0\in U_D\), temporarily retaining the laws of the other coordinates.  For \(a\in\Omega_{u_0}\), let
\[
 q_a(t)=P\{H_D(a,U_D\setminus\{u_0\})=t\},
 \qquad t\in\mathcal T_D.
\]
Each \(q_a\) is in the simplex \(\Delta_{q_D-1}\), and the present law of \(R_D\) is the barycenter \(\bar q=\int q_a\,d\mu_{u_0}(a)\).  A barycenter of points in \(\mathbb R^{q_D}\) belongs to their convex hull: approximation by simple functions places it in the closed convex hull, and a separating affine functional would otherwise strictly separate the barycenter from every approximant.  Carath\'eodory reduction can be proved directly here.  If a convex representation has more than \(q_D\) points in the affine hyperplane \(\sum_t q(t)=1\), affine dependence supplies coefficients \(c_j\), not all zero, with \(\sum_jc_j=0\) and \(\sum_jc_jq_{a_j}=0\).  Moving the weights by \(-\varepsilon c_j\), with \(\varepsilon\) maximal subject to nonnegativity, deletes one point without changing the barycenter.  Repetition leaves at most \(q_D\) points.  Consequently, there are \(a_1,\ldots,a_\ell\in\Omega_{u_0}\) and \(\lambda_j\ge0\) such that
\[
 \ell\le q_D,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\lambda_j=1,
 \qquad \bar q=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\lambda_jq_{a_j}.
\]
Replacing \(\mu_{u_0}\) by \(\sum_j\lambda_j\delta_{a_j}\) leaves the complete law of \(R_D\) unchanged and preserves the product form of the exogenous law.  Perform this replacement successively for every \(u\in U_D\) and then for every district.  Each step preserves the current law of the relevant district response vector, so after finitely many steps
\[
 |\Omega_u|\le q_{C(u)}\le\prod_{v\in C(u)}T_v=b_u
 \qquad (u\in U_G).
\]

The selected structural tables are unchanged, hence remain signed-isotone.  The construction preserves the law of the response-table vector in each district, and independence of the disjoint district exogenous blocks preserves their joint law.  By acyclicity, every finite vector of potential responses is a deterministic recursive function of the complete response-table vector.  Its law is therefore unchanged.  The resulting \(\widetilde{\mathfrak m}\) is counterfactually equivalent to \(\mathfrak m\), and
\[
 \left|\prod_{u\in U_G}\Omega_u\right|
 \le\prod_{u\in U_G}b_u=R_{G,M}.
\]
This proves preservation of every finite menu law and every finite counterfactual law.

Finally, \(S_v\) and \(\operatorname{pa}_G(v)\setminus S_v\) partition the parents, so \(|\operatorname{pa}_G(v)|=m_v+r_v\).  If \(m_v>0\), the Boolean function \(z\mapsto1-z_1\) is not isotone, whereas all \(2^{2^{m_v}}\) Boolean functions are counted without the restriction.  Hence
\[
 D_{m_v}<2^{2^{m_v}},
 \qquad
 T_v=D_{m_v}^{2^{r_v}}
 <\left(2^{2^{m_v}}\right)^{2^{r_v}}
 =2^{2^{m_v+r_v}}
 =2^{2^{|\operatorname{pa}_G(v)|}}.
\]
This proves the strict response-type reduction.
