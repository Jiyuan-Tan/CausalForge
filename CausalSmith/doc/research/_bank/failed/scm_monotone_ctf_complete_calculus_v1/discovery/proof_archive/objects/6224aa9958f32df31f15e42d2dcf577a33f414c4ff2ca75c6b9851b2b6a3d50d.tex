\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(d=2^{|V|}\), fix an enumeration \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) of \(\{0,1\}^{V}\), and fix an arbitrary generating model
\[
 \mathfrak m\in\mathcal F_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(p).
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:menu\text{-}compatibility}\) ensures that this fiber is nonempty. Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:positive\text{-}query\text{-}denominator}\) gives
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}(B_Q)\geq\kappa>0. \tag{1}
\]
By the successful-output soundness clause of the theorem resolving \(\mathrm{oeq:complete\text{-}mctfid}\), the premise
\[
 \mathsf{MCTFID}(G,M,\mathcal Z,Q)=F\in\mathcal R(p)
\]
implies, for every signed-monotone model with positive query denominator,
\[
 F\bigl(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m)\bigr)=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m). \tag{2}
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def:menu\text{-}fiber}\) gives \(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m)=p\). Combining this identity with (1) and (2) yields the causal centering identity
\[
 F(p)=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m). \tag{3}
\]
Thus (3) is a consequence of successful-output soundness and the positive-denominator condition; it does not identify the causal target by definition.

We next prove local regularity of the returned expression. The expression tree of \(F\) is finite. By \(\mathrm{ass:analytic\text{-}branch\text{-}margin}\),
\[
 \lambda_F(p)\geq\kappa>0. \tag{4}
\]
We claim that there are an ambient open neighborhood \(U\subset\mathbb R^{Kd}\) of \(p\) and a continuously differentiable function \(F_p^{\circ}:U\to\mathbb R\) such that \(F(q)=F_p^{\circ}(q)\) for every \(q\in U\) in the domain of the expression grammar. Moreover, throughout \(U\), the branch selected at \(p\) is unchanged, every active quotient denominator is nonzero, and every active indexed root is the continuation of the root selected at \(p\).

The claim follows by induction through the finite active expression tree. Cell coordinates and rational constants are analytic, and finite sums and products preserve continuous differentiability. If \(a(q)/b(q)\) is an active quotient, then (4) and the definition of \(\lambda_F\) give \(|b(p)|\geq\kappa\). Continuity therefore gives a neighborhood on which \(|b(q)|\geq\kappa/2\). If a finite branch is selected by the sign of a guard \(g\), the induction hypothesis makes \(g\) continuous locally, while (4) gives \(|g(p)|\geq\kappa\). Hence \(g(q)\) has the same sign as \(g(p)\) on a smaller neighborhood.

It remains to treat an active root node. Write
\[
 t_0=\operatorname{Root}_j(h;I)(p),
 \qquad h(t_0,p)=0,
 \qquad I=(a,b),
\]
where \(I\) is the rational isolating interval attached to this branch. From (4),
\[
 |\partial_t h(t_0,p)|\geqkappa,
 \qquad
 min\{t_0-a,b-t_0\}\geqkappa. \tag{5}
\]
Choose \(0<\varepsilon<\kappa/2\). Since \(h\) is polynomial and \(\partial_t h(t_0,p)\neq0\), continuity gives a neighborhood \(U_0\) of \(p\) on which \(\partial_t h(t,q)\) has constant sign and absolute value at least \(\kappa/2\) whenever \(|t-t_0|\leq\varepsilon\). At \(q=p\), the mean-value identity makes \(h(t_0-\varepsilon,p)\) and \(h(t_0+\varepsilon,p)\) have opposite signs. After shrinking \(U_0\), the same is true for every \(q\in U_0\). Thus the intermediate value theorem and strict monotonicity in \(t\) give a unique root
\[
 t(q)\in(t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon). \tag{6}
\]
Because \(I\) isolates \(t_0\) at \(p\), the polynomial \(h(\,cdot\,,p)\) is nonzero on the compact set
\[
 [a,b]\setminus(t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon).
\]
Its absolute value therefore has a positive minimum there. Uniform continuity of the polynomial coefficients permits a further shrinking of \(U_0\) on which no root enters that compact set or crosses either endpoint of \(I\). Consequently, (6) is the unique root in \(I\), and the index \(j\) is unchanged.

For \(q,q'\in U_0\), the mean-value identity in \(t\), the lower derivative bound, and local Lipschitz continuity of the polynomial in its parameter coordinates give a finite constant \(C\) such that
\[
 |t(q)-t(q')|
 \leq \frac{2}{\kappa}
       |h(t(q'),q)-h(t(q'),q')|
 \leq C\|q-q'\|. \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(t\) is continuous. Apply a coordinatewise difference quotient to \(h(t(q),q)=0\), use (7), and take the limit. This gives
\[
 \nabla t(q)
 =-\frac{\nabla_q h(t(q),q)}{\partial_t h(t(q),q)}. \tag{8}
\]
The right-hand side is continuous on a smaller neighborhood, so the root node is continuously differentiable, and (8) is exactly the recursive root-differentiation rule. Intersecting the finitely many neighborhoods for the active guards, quotients, and roots proves the claim. In particular,
\[
 q\longmapsto\nabla F_p^{\circ}(q)
 \quad\text{is continuous on }U. \tag{9}
\]
This is an ambient extension, so it remains valid when some coordinates of \(p\) are on the boundary of their probability simplices.

We now derive the joint sampling limit. For observation \(i\) in regime \(k\), define the one-hot vector
\[
 Y_{ki}=\bigl(\mathbf 1\{X_{ki}=x_1\},\ldots,
                    \mathbf 1\{X_{ki}=x_d\}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:independent\text{-}regime\text{-}sampling}\), these vectors are independent and identically distributed within each regime, samples from distinct regimes are independent, and
\[
 E(Y_{ki})=p_k,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y_{ki})
 =\operatorname{diag}(p_k)-p_kp_k^{\mathsf T}
 =\Sigma_k. \tag{10}
\]
The coordinates of \(Y_{ki}\) are bounded, so all second moments exist. Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:stable\text{-}regime\text{-}shares}\) implies \(n_k\to\infty\) and
\[
 \frac{n}{n_k}\longrightarrow\rho_k^{-1}. \tag{11}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:multivariate\text{-}iid\text{-}clt}\) in each regime. Independence across the fixed finite number \(K\) of regimes makes the joint characteristic function the product of the regime characteristic functions. Together with (11), this yields
\[
 \sqrt n\,(\widehat p-p)
 \rightsquigarrow
 N_{Kd}(0,\Omega),
 \qquad
 \Omega=\operatorname{blockdiag}
 \bigl(\rho_1^{-1}\Sigma_1,\ldots,
       \rho_K^{-1}\Sigma_K\bigr). \tag{12}
\]
The covariance in (12) may be singular because every multinomial cell vector sums to one; no rank condition is required. Directly, using (10),
\[
 E_{\mathfrak m}\|\widehat p-p\|^2
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_k)}{n_k}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{13}
\]
Markov's inequality applied to \(\|\widehat p-p\|^2\) gives \(\widehat p\to p\) in probability. Since \(U\) is a neighborhood of \(p\),
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}(\widehat p\in U)\longrightarrow1. \tag{14}
\]
This proves the asserted branch stability.

On the event in (14), \(F(\widehat p)=F_p^{\circ}(\widehat p)\). Apply \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}dimensional\text{-}delta\text{-}method}\) to (12) and the ambient derivative in (9). Equality with probability tending to one transfers its conclusion from \(F_p^{\circ}\) to \(F\). The scalar variance is
\[
 \nabla F(p)^{\mathsf T}\Omega\nabla F(p)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho_k^{-1}
   \nabla_kF(p)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_k\nabla_kF(p)
 =\sigma_F^2(p), \tag{15}
\]
and hence
\[
 \sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}
 \rightsquigarrow N\bigl(0,\sigma_F^2(p)\bigr). \tag{16}
\]

For variance consistency, put
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_k
 =\operatorname{diag}(\widehat p_k)
  -\widehat p_k\widehat p_k^{\mathsf T}.
\]
On the event in (14), equations (9), (11), and (13) imply, for every \(k\),
\[
 \nabla_kF(\widehat p)\to\nabla_kF(p),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Sigma_k\to\Sigma_k,
 \qquad
 (n_k/n)^{-1}\to\rho_k^{-1} \tag{17}
\]
in probability. The branch-local standard-error rule may be completed by any fixed finite value when its local recursive evaluation is unavailable; this choice is immaterial because the complement of (14) has probability tending to zero. Substitution into the finite sum therefore gives
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F^2
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K}(n_k/n)^{-1}
   \nabla_kF(\widehat p)^{\mathsf T}
   \widehat\Sigma_k
   \nabla_kF(\widehat p)
 \longrightarrow\sigma_F^2(p) \tag{18}
\]
in probability. Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\text{-}variance}\) states that \(\sigma_F^2(p)>0\). Hence
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F\longrightarrow\sigma_F(p)>0 \tag{19}
\]
in probability.

Combining (16), (19), and \(\mathrm{lem:slutsky\text{-}studentization}\) gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_F}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1), \tag{20}
\]
where the ratio may be assigned any fixed value on \(\{\widehat\sigma_F=0\}\), an event whose probability tends to zero by (19). On the event \(\{\widehat p\in U,\widehat\sigma_F>0\}\), whose probability tends to one, (3) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:wald\text{-}interval}\) give
\[
 \left\{
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\in\mathsf{CI}_{1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 =
 \left\{
 \left|
 \frac{\sqrt n\{F(\widehat p)-F(p)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_F}
 \right|
 \leq z_{1-\alpha/2}
 \right\}. \tag{21}
\]
The standard normal law has no mass at the two boundary points in (21). Therefore (20), (21), and the vanishing probability of the complementary event imply
\[
 P_{\mathfrak m}\!\left\{
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\in\mathsf{CI}_{1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 \longrightarrow
 P\{|Z|\leq z_{1-\alpha/2}\}
 =1-\alpha,
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,1). \tag{22}
\]
The model \(\mathfrak m\) was arbitrary in the fiber, and every such model induces the same regime-law vector \(p\). Hence the argument applies to every generating \(\mathfrak m\in\mathcal F_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(p)\), proving all assertions. \(\square\)
