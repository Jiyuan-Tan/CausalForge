\paragraph{Finite parameterization and enumeration.}
For \(v\in V\), let \(U(v)=\{u\in U_G:v\in\operatorname{ch}(u)\}\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\) permits every \(u\) to be padded to exactly \(b_u\) states.  A canonical mechanism at \(v\) assigns one of at most \(T_v\) signed-isotone response tables to each of the \(\prod_{u\in U(v)}b_u\) configurations of its exogenous parents.  Hence the number of joint structural profiles is at most
\[
 \prod_{v\in V}T_v^{\prod_{u\in U(v)}b_u}=A_{G,M}.
\]
After one mass in each simplex is eliminated, a model has at most \(N_{G,M}=\sum_u(b_u-1)\) free mass coordinates.  A pair has at most \(2N_{G,M}\), and each model has at most \(R_{G,M}\) joint latent configurations.  These are the stated enumeration and size bounds.

For a fixed profile \(a\), use the polynomials \(\phi_{a,k,x}\), \(h_a\), and \(d_a\) constructed in the proof of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\).  Each is a sum of at most \(R_{G,M}\) product-law monomials.  The pair sentence in Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\) is therefore a rational-coefficient semialgebraic system.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) proves that its finite disjunction is satisfiable if and only if \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=0\), and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}real\text{-}closed\text{-}field\text{-}decision}\) makes the decision and sample extraction effective.

\paragraph{The empty-sign branch.}
Suppose \(M=\varnothing\).  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\), the first operation is the published conditional \(\mathsf{CTFID}\) procedure.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}ctfid\text{-}completeness}\) says that it returns an expression exactly for the counterfactual queries identified in the unrestricted model class.  When \(M=\varnothing\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) imposes no response-function restriction beyond the finite binary recursive semi-Markovian assumptions, so this is exactly the criterion in Definition \(\mathrm{def:identification\text{-}criterion}\).  On success, the algorithm returns the published expression unchanged; its rational operations are contained in \(\mathcal R(p)\).  On failure, completeness implies that the finite two-model sentence is satisfiable, and the certificate step extracts the promised pair.

\paragraph{The \(\mathsf{MID}\) front end.}
Suppose \(M\ne\varnothing\).  If \(\mathsf{MID}\) succeeds with expression \(H\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:mid\text{-}soundness}\) gives
\[
 H(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m))=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m)
\]
for every legal model with positive query denominator.  Thus any two such models with the same menu law have the same query value, so \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1\).  The definition returns \(H\) unchanged before invoking the oracle.  This proves
\(\operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MID})\subseteq\operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MCTFID})\).

\paragraph{Failure certificate.}
If the two-model sentence is satisfiable, exact sample-point extraction provides two enumerated profiles and two exact algebraic product-simplex mass vectors.  They define recursive semi-Markovian models because the masses factor over \(U_G\); their tables belong to the enumerated signed-isotone sets, so Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:signed\text{-}local\text{-}monotonicity}\) holds.  Exogenous indices belonging to distinct districts are disjoint, which gives district factorization.  The sampled constraints give positive denominators, equality of every menu cell, and
\[
 h_{a_0}(\lambda^0)d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)
 \ne h_{a_1}(\lambda^1)d_{a_0}(\lambda^0).
\]
Division by the two positive denominators yields unequal query values.  Definition \(\mathrm{def:fail\text{-}compiler\text{-}handle}\) merely decodes and verifies these recorded data, so it returns the claimed \((\mathfrak m_0,\mathfrak m_1,p^\star)\) with \(|\Omega_u|\le b_u\).  Conversely, Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) shows that failure of global identification makes some pair sentence satisfiable.  Therefore the procedure returns \(\mathsf{FAIL}\) exactly when the criterion is zero.

\paragraph{Identifying expression after an unsatisfiable pair sentence.}
Suppose the pair sentence is unsatisfiable.  Consider
\[
 \Gamma_Q
 =\bigcup_a\left\{(p,t):
 \exists\lambda\;\bigl[
 \Lambda_a(\lambda)\wedge p=\phi_a(\lambda)
 \wedge d_a(\lambda)>0\wedge t d_a(\lambda)=h_a(\lambda)
 \bigr]\right\}.
\]
This is a finite union of projections of rational semialgebraic sets and is therefore effectively semialgebraic by quantifier elimination.  Every nonempty fiber of \(\Gamma_Q\) is a singleton: two ordinates in the same fiber would supply two feasible canonical models with a common menu law, positive denominators, and unequal cross-products, contradicting unsatisfiability.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}cad\text{-}thom\text{-}sections}\) produces a finite sign-invariant cylindrical decomposition of the \(p\)-space.  On each cell in the projection of \(\Gamma_Q\), the singleton ordinate is an algebraic section selected by a polynomial and a Thom encoding.  The glue from that cited result to the present grammar is direct: use the cell's polynomial sign description as a finite branch guard and its section as \(\operatorname{ThomRoot}_{\sigma}(h)\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:expression\text{-}grammar}\) admits exactly these guarded terms.  Give every remaining cell the rational value zero.  The result is a globally defined, exactly evaluable \(F\in\mathcal R(p)\).

If \(\mathfrak m\in\mathfrak M(G,M)\) has \(P_{\mathfrak m}(B_Q)>0\), Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\) replaces it by a canonical model preserving its menu law, numerator, and denominator.  Its point
\[
 \left(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m),\theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\right)
\]
belongs to \(\Gamma_Q\).  The singleton-section construction therefore gives
\[
 F(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m))=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m).
\]
Thus unsatisfiability yields a successful identifying expression.  Together with the failure argument and the two front ends, this proves
\[
 \operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MCTFID})
 =\{(G,M,\mathcal Z,Q):\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1\}.
\]

Finally, all table sets, profile sets, polynomial lists, and CAD cells are finite.  The procedure makes at most \(A_{G,M}^2\) profile-pair tests and one finite CAD compilation after those tests, and every invoked exact real-algebraic algorithm terminates by the two cited real-algebraic lemmas.  This proves the asserted effective termination.  Strictness of the \(\mathsf{MID}\) inclusion and district-compositional graphical compression are logically separate questions and are not used in this proof.
