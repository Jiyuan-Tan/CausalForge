\paragraph{Scope and finite parameterization.}
Throughout the proof, the input belongs to the class stated in the theorem: \(V\) is finite, all endogenous variables are binary, \(G\) is acyclic, the exogenous law has the recursive semi-Markovian product expansion indexed by \(U_G\), and the local tables obey the signs in \(M\).  These are exactly Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:finite\text{-}binary}\), \(\mathrm{ass:semi\text{-}markovian}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:signed\text{-}local\text{-}monotonicity}\), hence exactly the models in Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).  No model outside this domain is quantified over below.

For \(v\in V\), write
\[
 U(v)=\{u\in U_G:v\in\operatorname{ch}(u)\}.
\]
By Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\), every model relevant to menu equivalence may be replaced, without changing any finite menu or counterfactual law, by one satisfying \(|\Omega_u|\le b_u\) for every \(u\).  Pad a smaller support with zero-mass states so that its displayed support has exactly \(b_u\) states.  For a fixed configuration of the exogenous parents indexed by \(U(v)\), the mechanism at \(v\) selects one of at most \(T_v\) signed-isotone complete response tables.  There are \(\prod_{u\in U(v)}b_u\) such exogenous configurations.  Consequently, the number of joint structural-table profiles is at most
\[
 \prod_{v\in V}T_v^{\prod_{u\in U(v)}b_u}
 =\prod_{v\in V}T_v^{\prod_{u\in U_G:\,v\in\operatorname{ch}(u)}b_u}
 =A_{G,M}.
\]
Here and below an empty product equals one.  All support bounds are positive integers because every \(T_v\) counts a nonempty finite response-table set.

Fix one enumerated profile \(a\).  Its remaining parameters are the product-simplex masses
\[
 \lambda_{u,j}\ge 0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^{b_u}\lambda_{u,j}=1.
\]
Eliminating one mass from each simplex leaves at most
\[
 N_{G,M}=\sum_{u\in U_G}(b_u-1)
\]
free coordinates for one model and at most \(2N_{G,M}\) for an ordered pair.  A model sum ranges over at most
\[
 \prod_{u\in U_G}b_u=R_{G,M}
\]
joint latent configurations, and hence a pair uses at most \(2R_{G,M}\) such configurations.

For this profile, let \(\phi_{a,k,x}(\lambda)\), \(h_a(\lambda)\), and \(d_a(\lambda)\) be the menu-cell, query-numerator, and query-denominator polynomials constructed in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\).  Each is a rational polynomial obtained by summing at most \(R_{G,M}\) product-law monomials.  The ordered-profile-pair system in Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\) is therefore a finite rational semialgebraic system.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) proves that the disjunction of these systems is satisfiable if and only if
\[
 \operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=0.
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}real\text{-}closed\text{-}field\text{-}decision}\) makes both the decision and, in the satisfiable case, exact algebraic sample-point extraction effective.

\paragraph{The empty-sign front end.}
Suppose \(M=\varnothing\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\) first runs conditional \(\mathsf{CTFID}\).  In this case Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) imposes no response-function restriction beyond the finite-binary recursive semi-Markovian conditions, so the model class in Definition \(\mathrm{def:identification\text{-}criterion}\) is the restriction to this theorem's domain of the unrestricted class appearing in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}ctfid\text{-}completeness}\).  That lemma therefore gives
\[
 \mathsf{CTFID}(G,\mathcal Z,Q)\text{ succeeds}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{GID}_{G,\varnothing,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1
\]
on the stated finite-binary recursive semi-Markovian domain.  On success, the definition returns the CTFID expression unchanged, merely viewing it as an element of \(\mathcal R(p)\).  On CTFID failure, the displayed equivalence gives global nonidentification on this domain; Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) then makes a canonical pair system satisfiable, so the continuation returns \((\mathsf{FAIL},\tau_{\mathsf F})\).  Thus, when \(M=\varnothing\), the MCTFID success set is exactly the CTFID success set on the common domain and the successful expression is its unchanged lift.

\paragraph{The M-ID front end and scoped containment.}
Suppose first that \(M\ne\varnothing\) and that \(\mathsf{MID}\) returns \(H\) on an input in the common domain.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:mid\text{-}soundness}\) gives
\[
 H(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m))=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m)
\]
for every \(\mathfrak m\in\mathfrak M(G,M)\) having \(P_{\mathfrak m}(B_Q)>0\).  Hence any two such models with the same menu law have equal query values, and Definition \(\mathrm{def:identification\text{-}criterion}\) gives \(\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:mctfid\text{-}handle}\) returns this same \(H\) before invoking the algebraic back end.

If instead \(M=\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{MID}\) succeeds on an input in the common domain, the same soundness lemma implies identification in the finite-binary recursive semi-Markovian class.  Conditional CTFID completeness then makes the empty-sign front end succeed.  This proves, with both procedures restricted to the common domain,
\[
 \operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MID})
 \subseteq \operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MCTFID}).
\]
In the empty-sign case it does not identify the two returned expression trees, because the procedure actually runs CTFID there.

\paragraph{The failure branch.}
Suppose the finite pair disjunction is satisfiable.  Exact sample-point extraction supplies two enumerated signed-isotone profiles and two exact algebraic product-simplex mass vectors.  The profile constraints make every local table obey Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:signed\text{-}local\text{-}monotonicity}\), and the product-simplex constraints give mutually independent exogenous coordinates as required by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:semi\text{-}markovian}\).  Thus the sampled objects are models \(\mathfrak m_0,\mathfrak m_1\in\mathfrak M(G,M)\), with \(|\Omega_u|\le b_u\).

For a district \(D\), all exogenous indices affecting its vertices lie in the block \(\{u:C(u)=D\}\).  These blocks are disjoint across districts, and their coordinates are independent.  The sampled models are therefore district-factorized.  The sampled equalities give, for every \(k\) and \(x\),
\[
 \phi_{a_0,k,x}(\lambda^0)
 =\phi_{a_1,k,x}(\lambda^1)
 =p^{\star}_{k,x},
\]
whereas the strict constraints give \(d_{a_0}(\lambda^0)>0\), \(d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)>0\), and
\[
 h_{a_0}(\lambda^0)d_{a_1}(\lambda^1)
 \ne
 h_{a_1}(\lambda^1)d_{a_0}(\lambda^0).
\]
Division is legitimate precisely because both displayed denominators are positive.  It follows that
\[
 P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m_0)=p^{\star}
 =P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m_1),
 \qquad
 \theta_Q(\mathfrak m_0)\ne\theta_Q(\mathfrak m_1).
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def:fail\text{-}compiler\text{-}handle}\) deletes only zero-mass padding states and verifies exactly these constraints, so its decoded models retain all asserted properties.  Conversely, Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) shows that failure of global identification on the stated class makes some enumerated pair system satisfiable.  Hence the algebraic back end returns a failure certificate exactly in the nonidentified case on that class.

\paragraph{Compilation of the identifying functional.}
It remains to treat an invocation of the algebraic back end for which the pair disjunction is unsatisfiable.  Consider the finite semialgebraic relation
\[
 \Gamma_Q
 =\bigcup_a\left\{(p,t):
 \exists\lambda\;\left[
 \Lambda_a(\lambda)\wedge p=\phi_a(\lambda)
 \wedge d_a(\lambda)>0
 \wedge t d_a(\lambda)=h_a(\lambda)
 \right]\right\}.
\]
It is effectively semialgebraic by Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}real\text{-}closed\text{-}field\text{-}decision}\).  Every nonempty fiber over \(p\) is a singleton.  Indeed, two values \(t_0\ne t_1\) in one fiber would provide two feasible canonical models with the same menu cells and positive denominators.  Since \(t_i=h_{a_i}(\lambda^i)/d_{a_i}(\lambda^i)\), their inequality would be equivalent, after multiplication by the positive denominators, to the unequal cross-product in the pair system, contradicting its unsatisfiability.

Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}cad\text{-}thom\text{-}sections}\) to rational polynomials defining \(\Gamma_Q\).  A finite sign-invariant cylindrical algebraic decomposition partitions the projected \(p\)-space into semialgebraic cells.  On every cell contained in the projection of \(\Gamma_Q\), its unique ordinate is a real-root section distinguished either by a rational isolating interval or by a finite Thom derivative-sign encoding.  Use the cell's rational polynomial sign description as a branch guard and the corresponding \(\operatorname{Root}_j\) or \(\operatorname{ThomRoot}_{\sigma}\) term as its value.  Assign the rational value zero to every cell outside the projection.  Definition \(\mathrm{def:expression\text{-}grammar}\) then gives a globally totalized finite expression \(F\in\mathcal R(p)\).

This construction is effective in the precise input model of that definition.  At an arbitrary real \(p\), exact polynomial-sign queries select the active branch and the certified root section supplies its exact value.  If \(p\) has rational or real-algebraic coordinates, sign determination, algebraic arithmetic, Thom encodings, and real-root isolation give a finite exact evaluation algorithm.  No step requires a finite bit representation of an arbitrary transcendental input.

Now let \(\mathfrak m\in\mathfrak M(G,M)\) satisfy \(P_{\mathfrak m}(B_Q)>0\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:signed\text{-}canonical\text{-}reduction}\) supplies a canonical model preserving its menu law, query numerator, and query denominator.  Thus
\[
 \bigl(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m),\theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\bigr)
 \in\Gamma_Q.
\]
The singleton-fiber construction consequently gives
\[
 F(P_{\mathcal Z}(\mathfrak m))=\theta_Q(\mathfrak m).
\]
This also separates the causal target \(\theta_Q(\mathfrak m)\) from the observed-law functional \(F\): their equality is a theorem on the positive-denominator model class, not a definition.

If either front end returns an expression, its declared soundness gives global identification on the common domain.  If the algebraic back end returns an expression, unsatisfiability of the pair system and Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}decision}\) give global identification there.  Conversely, if the criterion equals one, the pair system is unsatisfiable; unless a sound front end has already returned, the preceding CAD construction returns \(F\).  Therefore, with the domain restriction understood on both sides,
\[
 \operatorname{Succ}(\mathsf{MCTFID})
 =\{(G,M,\mathcal Z,Q):\operatorname{GID}_{G,M,\mathcal Z}(Q)=1\}.
\]
Together with the scoped containment already proved, this is the claimed success-set identity.

Finally, all response-table sets, profile sets, polynomial systems, and CAD cell decompositions are finite.  At most \(A_{G,M}\) profiles and \(A_{G,M}^2\) ordered pairs are examined, with the coordinate and latent-configuration bounds proved above.  Termination of every exact decision, sample-point, and root-section compilation call follows from the two declared effective real-algebraic lemmas.  The argument establishes completeness only for the class fixed in the first paragraph; it neither extends that completeness to the broader ordered-variable or general-SCM domain of the published M-ID work nor supplies a district-local graphical failure compiler.  This proves the theorem exactly at its stated scope.
