Can one establish a positive centered converse over the unrestricted estimator class by a first-principles Bayes-risk calculation?  Concretely, for every fixed law-independent \(D\in\mathfrak D_{n,\varepsilon}\), can one construct a prior \(\Pi_n\) supported on an active-weight submodel of \(\mathcal P(s,r)\) and a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
\inf_{T\in\mathfrak T_n}
E_{P\sim\Pi_n}E_{\mathbb P_n^{D,P}}[(T-\theta(P))^2]
\geq E_{P\sim\Pi_n}\frac{V^*(P)}n
+c n^{-1-2\alpha_d}?
\]
Every vertex-dependent contribution to \(V^*(P)\) must be calculated inside the Bayes decomposition.  Total-risk testing bounds and target separation alone are insufficient because their leading \(n^{-1}\) term can be absorbed by the law-dependent benchmark subtraction.  A positive answer would give the missing unrestricted regression-driven lower bound; a negative answer must exhibit an explicit design--estimator obstruction.  This question is separate from the variance-driven allocation-sensitivity converse when \(r<s\); smoothness adaptation of the upper construction is settled by \(\mathrm{thm{:}smoothness\text{-}adaptive\text{-}log\text{-}free\text{-}upper}\).
