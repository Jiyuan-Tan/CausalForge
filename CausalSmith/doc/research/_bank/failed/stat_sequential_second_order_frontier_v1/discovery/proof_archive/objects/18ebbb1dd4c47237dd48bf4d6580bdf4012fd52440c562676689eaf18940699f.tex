\textbf{Support and boundary conditions.}
The strict variance-range condition is \(v_{\min}<v_{\max}\), the overlap condition gives \(0<\varepsilon<1/K\), and the proposed weight choice gives
\[
w_{\min}\leq |w|\leq W.
\]
Thus the constructed weights satisfy both boundedness and the active-weight-region condition, because \(|\omega_{a_0}(x)|=|w|\geq w_{\min}\) for every \(x\in B_0\).  All constants below may depend on the fixed class constants, including \(w\), but not on \(n\).

\textbf{A law-independent oracle design.}
Put
\[
p:=1-(K-1)\varepsilon.
\]
Since \(\varepsilon<1/K\), one has \(p>\varepsilon\).  Consider the static kernels
\[
q_t^D(a_0\mid x,h)=p,
\qquad
q_t^D(a\mid x,h)=\varepsilon\quad(a\neq a_0).
\]
They are measurable, normalized, non-anticipating, law-independent, and obey the positivity floor, so \(D\in\mathfrak D_{n,\varepsilon}\).  For the chosen weights,
\[
g(x):=w\mu_{a_0}(x),
\qquad
m_{a_0}(x)=w^2\sigma_{a_0}^2(x),
\qquad
m_a(x)=0\quad(a\neq a_0),
\]
and \(\theta(P)=E_P[g(X_t)]\).  Every feasible static allocation \(e\) satisfies
\[
e_{a_0}(x)\leq 1-(K-1)\varepsilon=p.
\]
Because \(w^2\sigma_{a_0}^2(x)>0\), the map \(u\mapsto w^2\sigma_{a_0}^2(x)/u\) is strictly decreasing.  Hence the displayed static design minimizes the oracle objective pointwise for every \(P\in\mathcal P(s,r)\), and the benchmark definition gives
\[
V^*(P)=\operatorname{Var}_P\{g(X_t)\}
+\frac{w^2}{p}E_P\{\sigma_{a_0}^2(X_t)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, its oracle allocation is independent of the unknown variance functions.

\textbf{A log-free regression bound.}
We record the elementary calculation for the learner used below.  On a training sample of size \(N\), partition \([0,1]^d\) into boundary-corrected cubes of side \(h\), and on each cube fit, using the observations with \(A_i=a_0\), a polynomial of degree \(\lfloor s\rfloor\) in rescaled coordinates.  If the empirical polynomial Gram matrix is singular, use zero on that cube, and finally clip the fit to \([-M,M]\).  This is a Borel function of the training records.

The Hölder Taylor remainder on each cube is at most \(C h^s\).  The density bounds and the fixed assignment probability \(p\) bound the eigenvalues of each population rescaled Gram matrix above and away from zero.  Applying exponential Markov's inequality entrywise to the bounded empirical Gram summands, and taking a union over the fixed number of polynomial coordinates, shows that the bad-Gram probability on a cube is at most \(C\exp(-cNh^d)\).  Conditional on a good Gram matrix, the variance range gives integrated conditional variance at most \(C/(Nh^d)\).  Clipping cannot increase squared error because \(|\mu_{a_0}|\leq M\), and on the bad event it bounds squared error by \(4M^2\).  Summing the integrated cell bounds therefore yields
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(s,r)}
E_P\|\widehat\mu-\mu_{a_0}\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C\left\{h^{2s}+\frac{1}{Nh^d}+e^{-cNh^d}\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Choosing \(h\asymp N^{-1/(2s+d)}\) in (2) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(s,r)}
E_P\|w\widehat\mu-w\mu_{a_0}\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C N^{-2s/(2s+d)}.
\tag{3}
\]
There is no supremum-norm concentration or bandwidth search in this calculation, so (3) has no logarithmic factor.

\textbf{The unrestricted-estimator upper bound.}
Split the indices deterministically into two folds \(I_1,I_2\) whose sizes differ by at most one.  Let \(\widehat g_j=w\widehat\mu_j\) be the learner (3) trained on fold \(I_j\), and define
\[
T:=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^2\sum_{i\in I_j}
\left[
\widehat g_{3-j}(X_i)
+\frac{\mathbf 1\{A_i=a_0\}}{p}
\{wY_i-\widehat g_{3-j}(X_i)\}
\right].
\tag{4}
\]
Formula (4) is one law-independent Borel map of the observed path, so \(T\in\mathfrak T_n\); it is not an oracle family indexed by \(P\).

Write \(I_i:=\mathbf 1\{A_i=a_0\}\), \(\epsilon_i:=Y_i-\mu_{a_0}(X_i)\) on \(\{I_i=1\}\), and
\[
\phi_i:=g(X_i)-\theta(P)+\frac{I_i}{p}w\epsilon_i,
\qquad
\delta_j:=\widehat g_j-g.
\]
Then direct algebra in (4) gives
\[
T-\theta(P)=S+R,
\qquad
S:=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_i,
\qquad
R:=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^2\sum_{i\in I_j}
\delta_{3-j}(X_i)\left(1-\frac{I_i}{p}\right).
\tag{5}
\]
Under the static design the \(\phi_i\) are independent and centered.  Conditional randomization, consistency, and \(E_P[\epsilon_i\mid X_i,A_i=a_0]=0\) imply
\[
E_P[\phi_i^2]
=\operatorname{Var}_P\{g(X_i)\}
+E_P\left[\frac{I_i}{p^2}w^2\epsilon_i^2\right]
=\operatorname{Var}_P\{g(X_i)\}
+\frac{w^2}{p}E_P\{\sigma_{a_0}^2(X_i)\}
=V^*(P),
\]
where the last equality is (1).  Consequently
\[
E_P[S^2]=\frac{V^*(P)}{n}.
\tag{6}
\]

The cross term in (5) vanishes exactly.  Indeed, conditional on the opposite training fold, each evaluation-fold summand in \(R\) is centered because \(E_P[1-I_i/p\mid X_i]=0\).  For a same-record product, conditional on \(X_i\) and the opposite fold,
\[
E_P\left[
\phi_i\delta_{3-j}(X_i)\left(1-\frac{I_i}{p}\right)
\ \middle|\ X_i,\widehat g_{3-j}
\right]=0:
\]
the \(g(X_i)-\theta(P)\) term is killed by assignment centering, and the residual term is killed by \(E_P[\epsilon_i\mid X_i,A_i=a_0]=0\).  Products with oracle scores in the training fold vanish after conditioning on that entire fold and averaging the evaluation fold.  Independence handles distinct evaluation records.  Thus
\[
E_P[SR]=0.
\tag{7}
\]
Also, conditional on the opposite fold,
\[
E_P\left[
\delta_{3-j}(X_i)^2\left(1-\frac{I_i}{p}\right)^2
\ \middle|\ \widehat g_{3-j}
\right]
=\frac{1-p}{p}\|\delta_{3-j}\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2.
\]
Using \((u+v)^2\leq2u^2+2v^2\), conditional independence within each evaluation fold, and (3), we obtain
\[
E_P[R^2]
\leq \frac{C}{n}
\sum_{j=1}^2E_P\|\delta_j\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq Cn^{-1-2s/(2s+d)}
=Cn^{-1-2\alpha_d}.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (5)--(8) imply, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P(s,r)\),
\[
E_P[(T-\theta(P))^2]-\frac{V^*(P)}n
=E_P[R^2]
\leq Cn^{-1-2\alpha_d}.
\]
Taking the two infima in the excess-risk definition proves
\[
\mathsf R_n(s,r,d;\omega)\leq Cn^{-1-2\alpha_d}.
\tag{9}
\]

\textbf{The standalone variance-risk lower bound.}
The cube \([0,1]^d\) has unit volume.  In the nonvacuous bounded-density model, integration of \(c_X\leq p_X\leq C_X\) implies \(c_X\leq1\leq C_X\); hence the uniform covariate density is admissible.  Fix a nonzero \(C^\infty\) function \(\psi\), supported in the interior of the unit cube, and normalized so that \(\|\psi\|_{L_2}>0\).  For all sufficiently small \(h\), one can pack
\[
N_h\geq c_Bh^{-d}
\]
disjoint translates of its \(h\)-rescaled support inside \(B_0\), because \(\operatorname{Leb}(B_0)\geq b_0>0\).  Let their anchor points be \(x_1,\ldots,x_{N_h}\), put \(v_0=(v_{\min}+v_{\max})/2\), and, for \(\xi\in\{-1,1\}^{N_h}\), define
\[
v_\xi(x):=v_0+\rho h^r\sum_{j=1}^{N_h}\xi_j
\psi\left(\frac{x-x_j}{h}\right).
\tag{10}
\]
Because the supports are disjoint, scaling of the Hölder seminorm shows that a fixed sufficiently small \(\rho>0\) makes every \(v_\xi\) belong to \(\mathcal H^r([0,1]^d,L_\sigma)\).  The strict inequality \(v_{\min}<v_{\max}\) lets the same fixed \(\rho\) ensure
\[
v_{\min}\leq v_\xi(x)\leq v_{\max}
\]
for every \(\xi\), every sufficiently small \(h\), and every \(x\).  Set all outcome means to zero, take \(\sigma_{a_0}^2=v_\xi\), keep the other arm variances equal to \(v_0\), and take the potential outcomes conditionally independent given \(X\).  These choices satisfy the support, Gaussian, Hölder, boundedness, and variance-range conditions, and therefore define a submodel of \(\mathcal P(s,r)\).

Let \(P_\xi^{(n)}\) denote the law of the \(n\) standalone observations \(Z_{1,a_0},\ldots,Z_{n,a_0}\).  If \(\xi^{(j)}\) differs from \(\xi\) only in bit \(j\), then, for \(v,v'\in[v_{\min},v_{\max}]\),
\[
\operatorname{KL}\{\mathcal N(0,v),\mathcal N(0,v')\}
=\frac12\left\{\frac{v}{v'}-1-\log\frac{v}{v'}\right\}
\leq C(v-v')^2.
\]
Integrating over the uniform covariate law and using (10) gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}\bigl(P_\xi^{(n)},P_{\xi^{(j)}}^{(n)}\bigr)
\leq Cn\rho^2h^{2r+d}.
\tag{11}
\]
Choose \(h=c_0n^{-1/(2r+d)}\), with fixed \(c_0>0\) sufficiently small.  Then the right side of (11) is at most a constant \(\kappa<1/2\).  For completeness, the testing bound used next follows directly from densities: the sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\), while the log-sum inequality applied to the set \(\{dP\geq dQ\}\), followed by the elementary binary relative-entropy bound, gives
\[
\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)^2\leq\frac12\operatorname{KL}(P,Q).
\tag{12}
\]
Thus every adjacent pair in (11) has testing affinity bounded below by \(1-\sqrt{\kappa/2}>0\).

For the target variance functions \(m_\xi=w^2v_\xi\), adjacent vertices satisfy
\[
\int\{m_\xi(x)-m_{\xi^{(j)}}(x)\}^2\,dP_X(x)
=4w^4\rho^2h^{2r+d}\|\psi\|_{L_2}^2
=:\Delta_h^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Given any estimator \(\widehat h\), decide bit \(j\) by which of the two adjacent restrictions in (13) is closer to \(\widehat h\) on the \(j\)-th support.  If this decision is wrong, the triangle inequality forces squared loss on that support to be at least \(\Delta_h^2/4\).  Pairing and averaging the \(2^{N_h}\) vertices, then using (11)--(13), yields the elementary hypercube bound
\[
2^{-N_h}\sum_\xi
E_{P_\xi^{(n)}}\|\widehat h-m_\xi\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\geq \frac{\Delta_h^2}{8}
\sum_{j=1}^{N_h}\left(1-\sqrt{\kappa/2}\right)
\geq cN_hh^{2r+d}
\geq ch^{2r}.
\tag{14}
\]
The supremum risk is at least its hypercube average.  Taking the infimum over \(\widehat h\), and then using that the maximum over arms in the standalone-risk definition includes \(a_0\), (14) proves
\[
\mathsf Q_n^\sigma(s,r,d;\omega)
\geq c h^{2r}
=c n^{-2r/(2r+d)}
=c n^{-2\beta_d}.
\tag{15}
\]

\textbf{Contradiction to a universal variance converse.}
The derivative of \(x\mapsto x/(2x+d)\) is \(d/(2x+d)^2>0\).  Hence \(r<s\) implies \(\beta_d<\alpha_d\).  If a fixed positive constant \(c_0\) satisfied \(\mathsf R_n\geq c_0\mathsf Q_n^\sigma/n\) uniformly over the frozen weight class, then (9) and (15) would give
\[
c_0c n^{-1-2\beta_d}
\leq \mathsf R_n(s,r,d;\omega)
\leq Cn^{-1-2\alpha_d},
\]
which is impossible for large \(n\), since the ratio of the right rate to the left rate is \(n^{-2(\alpha_d-\beta_d)}\to0\).  The same comparison directly rules out a universal lower bound of order \(n^{-1-2\beta_d}\).  This proves the proposed statement.
