We give the algorithm and its expected-risk analysis.  The construction knows \(d,K,\varepsilon,M,v_{\min},v_{\max}\) and the fixed weights, but never uses \(s\) or \(r\).

Partition time into geometric epochs \(I_j\) of length comparable to \(N_j=2^j\).  Permanently divide each completed epoch, in chronological order, into four deterministic subfolds of comparable sizes: mean training, mean validation, variance training, and variance validation.  Fits selected during epoch \(I_j\) are frozen and used for assignment and scoring only in \(I_{j+1}\).  The first finitely many epochs use fixed bounded fits and any fixed allocation in the positivity simplex.

For a training fold of size \(m\), define the smoothness-independent library
\[
\mathcal L_m:=
\left\{(p,k):0\leq p\leq\lceil\log m\rceil,
\ b_k=2^{-k},\ mb_k^d\geq c\log m\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
There are at most \(C(\log m)^2\) pairs.  For every arm and every pair in (22), form the recorded-inverse-propensity local-polynomial fit of degree \(p\) and bandwidth \(b_k\), using a fixed bounded fallback when its empirical Gram matrix is ill-conditioned.  Mean candidates are clipped to \([-M,M]\); variance candidates are clipped to \([v_{\min},v_{\max}]\).  All ties below are broken lexicographically, so the algorithm is a Borel-measurable map of the observed path.

We first prove the finite-library selection inequality used for both nuisances.  Conditional on a training fold, let \(g_1,\ldots,g_N\) be the resulting bounded candidate functions, frozen before a later validation fold \(J\) of size \(m\).  Let \(Z_i(a)\) be either \(Y_i(a)\), for mean selection, or
\(\{Y_i(a)-\widehat\mu_a(X_i)\}^2\), where the mean fit was selected and frozen before the variance folds.  Define
\[
\widehat L_\ell:=\frac1m\sum_{i\in J}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{e_{i,a}}
\{Z_i-g_\ell(X_i)\}^2,
\qquad
\widehat\ell\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{1\leq\ell\leq N}\widehat L_\ell.
\tag{23}
\]
Recorded propensities and non-anticipation imply, for every integrable \(q\),
\[
E\!\left[
\frac{\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{e_{i,a}}q(X_i,Z_i(a))
\,\middle|\,\mathcal G_{i-1}
\right]
=E_Pq(X,Z(a)).
\tag{24}
\]
Conditional on the frozen training objects, put \(f(x)=E[Z(a)\mid X=x]\) and
\(R_\ell=\|g_\ell-f\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2\).  The difference of two squared losses factorizes as
\[
\{Z-g_\ell(X)\}^2-\{Z-g_k(X)\}^2
=\{g_k(X)-g_\ell(X)\}\{2Z-g_\ell(X)-g_k(X)\}.
\tag{25}
\]
The candidate differences are uniformly bounded.  In the mean case, the remaining random factor is a bounded shift of a Gaussian with variance at most \(v_{\max}\).  In the variance case, it is a bounded affine function of the square of such a Gaussian, because both the true and fitted means are in \([-M,M]\).  Direct Gaussian integration therefore gives constants \(B,C<\infty\), independent of the candidates, such that the centered martingale increment \(\zeta_i^{\ell k}\) associated with (25) satisfies, for every integer \(q\geq2\),
\[
E[|\zeta_i^{\ell k}|^q\mid\mathcal G_{i-1}]
\leq\frac{q!}{2}B^{q-2}C(R_\ell+R_k).
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed, the Gaussian moment-generating function is finite for every linear argument, while the moment-generating function of a noncentral Gaussian square is uniformly finite on a neighborhood of zero under the mean and variance bounds; multiplication by the bounded candidate difference and by the inverse propensity costs only fixed powers of the positivity constant.  Its second moment is bounded by a constant times
\(E_P(g_\ell-g_k)^2\leq2(R_\ell+R_k)\), and the higher moments give (26).

Expanding the exponential in (26) shows, for \(|\lambda|B<1\),
\[
E[e^{\lambda\zeta_i^{\ell k}}\mid\mathcal G_{i-1}]
\leq
\exp\!\left\{
\frac{C\lambda^2(R_\ell+R_k)}{2(1-|\lambda|B)}
\right\}.
\]
Conditional iteration and exponential Markov yield the Bernstein bound for the validation average.  A union bound over at most \(N^2\) pairs, followed by
\(2\sqrt{xy}\leq x/4+4y\), implies that, with probability at least \(1-\eta\), simultaneously for all \(\ell,k\),
\[
\left|\{\widehat L_\ell-\widehat L_k\}-(R_\ell-R_k)\right|
\leq\frac14(R_\ell+R_k)
+C\frac{\log(N/\eta)}m.
\tag{27}
\]
Use (27) with \(\ell=\widehat\ell\), the empirical minimizing inequality
\(\widehat L_{\widehat\ell}\leq\widehat L_k\), and absorb \(R_{\widehat\ell}/4\).  Integrating the tail in \(\eta\) gives the expected oracle inequality
\[
E R_{\widehat\ell}
\leq C\min_{1\leq k\leq N}ER_k
+C\frac{\log N}{m}.
\tag{28}
\]
This derivation remains valid under history-dependent assignment because (24) supplies the martingale centering and positivity supplies the uniform moment constants.

For any fixed Hölder order \(u>0\), once \(m\) is sufficiently large the library (22) contains a degree \(p\geq\lfloor u\rfloor\) and a dyadic bandwidth within a factor two of \(m^{-1/(2u+d)}\).  The Taylor, good-Gram, and conditional-variance calculation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}predictable\text{-}aipw\text{-}holder\text{-}upper}\), applied to this one fixed-degree candidate, gives
\[
E\|\widehat f_{p,k}-f\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C\{b_k^{2u}+(mb_k^d)^{-1}\}
\leq C m^{-2u/(2u+d)}.
\tag{29}
\]
The optimal bandwidth satisfies \(mb_k^d\asymp m^{2u/(2u+d)}\), so it obeys the occupancy restriction in (22) for all large \(m\).  Since \(N\leq C(\log m)^2\) and \(2u/(2u+d)<1\),
\[
\frac{\log N}{m}
\leq C_u m^{-2u/(2u+d)}
\tag{30}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\).  Equations (28)--(30), with \(u=s\), prove for the selected mean
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(s,r)}
E_P\|\widehat\mu_{j,a}-\mu_a\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C_sN_j^{-2s/(2s+d)}.
\tag{31}
\]

On the later variance-training fold use the raw residual-square response
\(R=\{Y(a)-\widehat\mu_{j,a}(X)\}^2\).  Conditional on the earlier mean folds,
\[
E[R\mid X=x]
=\sigma_a^2(x)+\{\widehat\mu_{j,a}(x)-\mu_a(x)\}^2.
\tag{32}
\]
For each variance candidate, the local-polynomial stability calculation separates smoothing of the \(r\)-Hölder function \(\sigma_a^2\) from smoothing of the second term in (32).  Because both means are bounded,
\[
E\|\{\widehat\mu_{j,a}-\mu_a\}^2\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq4M^2E\|\widehat\mu_{j,a}-\mu_a\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2.
\tag{33}
\]
Apply (28)--(30) on the variance-validation fold with \(u=r\), and use (31)--(33).  Projection onto \([v_{\min},v_{\max}]\) cannot increase error relative to \(\sigma_a^2\), so
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(s,r)}
E_P\|\widehat v_{j,a}-\sigma_a^2\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C_{s,r}\left\{
N_j^{-2r/(2r+d)}\vee N_j^{-2s/(2s+d)}
\right\}.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus both selectors are independent of smoothness, and their library-selection remainders have been absorbed rather than carried as logarithmic multipliers.

It remains to transfer (31)--(34) to the causal excess risk.  For
\[
F_z(u)=\sum_a z_a^2/u_a,
\qquad z_a=|\omega_a|\sigma_a,
\]
let \(u^*(z)\) minimize \(F_z\) over \(u_a\geq\varepsilon\), \(\sum_a u_a=1\).  Expansion at \(u^*(\widehat z)\) and convexity of the minimized value give the deterministic inequality
\[
0\leq F_z\{u^*(\widehat z)\}-F_z\{u^*(z)\}
\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\|z-\widehat z\|_2^2.
\tag{35}
\]
With \(\widehat z_a=|\omega_a|\sqrt{\widehat v_a}\), the variance lower bound implies
\[
|\widehat z_a-z_a|^2
\leq\frac{W^2}{4v_{\min}}|\widehat v_a-\sigma_a^2|^2.
\tag{36}
\]
Use \(e_{t,a}(x)=u_a^*\{\widehat z_j(x)\}\) in the next epoch.  Measurability, normalization, and positivity follow from the explicit water-filling rule, so this defines a law-independent \(D\in\mathfrak D_{n,\varepsilon}\).  Equations (34)--(36) give expected allocation regret of order
\[
N_j^{-2s/(2s+d)}\vee N_j^{-2r/(2r+d)}.
\tag{37}
\]

Finally, use the selected mean and the recorded propensities in the augmented inverse-probability score
\[
\Psi_t=
\sum_a\omega_a(X_t)\widehat\mu_{j,a}(X_t)
+\sum_a\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}}{e_{t,a}(X_t)}
\omega_a(X_t)\{Y_t-\widehat\mu_{j,a}(X_t)\},
\qquad
T=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n\Psi_t.
\tag{38}
\]
All fits in (38) were frozen in the preceding epoch.  Hence conditional randomization and latent i.i.d. sampling make \(\Psi_t-\theta(P)\) a martingale difference.  If
\(\Delta_a=\omega_a(\widehat\mu_{j,a}-\mu_a)\), direct square expansion gives
\[
E[(\Psi_t-\theta(P))^2\mid\mathcal G_{t-1}]
=V(e_t;P)+E\!\left[
\sum_a\frac{\Delta_a(X)^2}{e_{t,a}(X)}
-\left\{\sum_a\Delta_a(X)\right\}^2
\,\middle|\,\mathcal G_{t-1}\right].
\tag{39}
\]
Positivity and (31) bound the second term from above at the mean rate.  Martingale orthogonality, (37), and (39) now give
\[
E[(T-\theta(P))^2]-\frac{V^*(P)}n
\leq\frac{C}{n^2}\sum_j|I_j|
\left{N_j^{-2s/(2s+d)}\vee N_j^{-2r/(2r+d)}\right}
+\frac{C_{s,r}}{n^2}.
\]
Both exponents lie strictly between zero and one.  Geometric summation therefore proves
\[
E[(T-\theta(P))^2]-\frac{V^*(P)}n
\leq C_{s,r}\left\{
n^{-1-2s/(2s+d)}\vee n^{-1-2r/(2r+d)}
\right\}
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P(s,r)\).  The finitely many epochs preceding availability of the required fixed degrees contribute only \(C_{s,r}n^{-2}\), which is smaller.  This proves the theorem in both smoothness orderings.  Nothing in the construction or proof lower-bounds the centered unrestricted risk, so no converse follows.
