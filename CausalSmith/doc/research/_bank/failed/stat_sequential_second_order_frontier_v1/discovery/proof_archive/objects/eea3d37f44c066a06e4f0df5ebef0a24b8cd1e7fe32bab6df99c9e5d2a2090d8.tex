The natural routes are sample splitting with dyadic Lepski selection and online aggregation of foldwise local-polynomial and pairwise-difference learners.  Sample splitting loses the second-order scale through discarded observations unless cross-fitting is used, and the standard high-probability Lepski or union-over-updates analysis pays a logarithm.  Global \(L_2\)-risk adaptation may avoid that penalty in an i.i.d. regression problem, but transporting it through predictable, arm-dependent sampling and a plug-in allocation optimizer remains the unresolved step.
