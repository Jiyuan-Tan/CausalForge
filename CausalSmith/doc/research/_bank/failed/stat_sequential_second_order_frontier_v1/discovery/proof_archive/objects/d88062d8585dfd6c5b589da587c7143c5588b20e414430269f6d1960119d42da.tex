Put
\[
h_P(x):=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\omega_a(x)\mu_a(x),
\qquad
z_a(x):=|\omega_a(x)|\sigma_a(x),
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal E_{\varepsilon}:=
\left\{u\in[\varepsilon,1]^K:\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}u_a=1\right\},
\qquad
F_z(u):=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{z_a^2}{u_a}.
\tag{64}
\]
The boundary condition \(0<\varepsilon<1/K\) makes \(\mathcal E_{\varepsilon}\) nonempty and compact. The variance range and bounded weights give \(0\leq z_a(x)\leq W\sqrt{v_{\max}}\), and every division below is valid because \(u_a\geq\varepsilon\).

Let \(u^*(z)\) be the lexicographically selected minimizer of \(F_z\) on \(\mathcal E_{\varepsilon}\), and write \(\Phi(z):=F_z\{u^*(z)\}\). If \(z\neq0\), the multiplier equations for this finite-dimensional constrained problem give
\[
u_a^*(z)=\max\{\varepsilon,z_a/\lambda\},
\qquad
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}u_a^*(z)=1,
\tag{65}
\]
for a unique normalizing \(\lambda>0\). Coordinates with \(z_a=0\) equal \(\varepsilon\) whenever another coordinate is positive; if \(z=0\), use the stated deterministic tie-break. Formula (65), together with that tie-break, shows directly that \(u^*\) is Borel measurable. Consequently \(x\mapsto u^*\{z(x)\}\) is an admissible allocation. Since every admissible allocation has pointwise objective at least \(\Phi\{z(x)\}\), the oracle-benchmark definition yields
\[
V^*(P)=\operatorname{Var}_P\{h_P(X)\}+E_P\Phi\{z(X)\}.
\tag{66}
\]

We next record the deterministic allocation inequality. The map \((z,u)\mapsto\sum_a z_a^2/u_a\) is jointly convex, because each summand is the perspective of the square function. Partial minimization over the convex set \(\mathcal E_{\varepsilon}\) makes \(\Phi\) convex. On each active-set piece, direct differentiation of (65) gives
\[
\nabla_a\Phi(z)=\frac{2z_a}{u_a^*(z)}.
\]
The derivatives agree when an active set changes; at a zero coordinate both one-sided derivatives are zero. Thus this is also a subgradient at every boundary point. For \(z,\widetilde z\in\mathbb R_+^K\), put \(\delta=z-\widetilde z\) and \(\widetilde u=u^*(\widetilde z)\). Expanding the square at the fixed allocation \(\widetilde u\), and then applying the subgradient inequality for \(\Phi\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_z(\widetilde u)
&=\Phi(\widetilde z)+\nabla\Phi(\widetilde z)^{\mathsf T}\delta
+\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{\delta_a^2}{\widetilde u_a},\\
\Phi(z)&\geq
\Phi(\widetilde z)+\nabla\Phi(\widetilde z)^{\mathsf T}\delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
It follows that
\[
0\leq F_z\{u^*(\widetilde z)\}-F_z\{u^*(z)\}
\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\|z-\widetilde z\|_2^2.
\tag{67}
\]
This controls changes of the positivity-constrained active set without requiring the optimizer itself to be Lipschitz.

We now construct the predictable learners. Let
\[
\mathcal G_{t-1}:=\sigma(O_1,\ldots,O_{t-1})
\]
augmented only by randomization already used before time \(t\), and put \(\mathcal G_{t-1}^{+}:=\sigma(\mathcal G_{t-1},X_t)\). The constructed kernels ignore any additional future baseline covariates that the ambient history convention may place in \(H_0\). By latent i.i.d. sampling, \(X_t\) is independent of \(\mathcal G_{t-1}\), and conditional on \(\mathcal G_{t-1}^{+}\) the current potential outcomes have their superpopulation conditional law given \(X_t\).

Partition time into geometric blocks
\[
I_j:=\{2^j,\ldots,(2^{j+1}-1)\wedge n\},
\qquad N_j:=2^j.
\tag{68}
\]
Use \(I_0\cup I_1\) as a fixed initialization prefix. For an evaluation block \(I_j\), \(j\geq2\), use \(I_{j-2}\) to fit the means and the later disjoint block \(I_{j-1}\) to fit the variances. The completed training-block sizes are within fixed factors of \(N_j\); a truncated final evaluation block causes no change.

For clarity, we give the expected local-polynomial calculation used twice below. On each boundary-corrected cube of side \(b\), take the rescaled tensor-polynomial basis of degree at least \(\lfloor u\rfloor\). For arm \(a\), weight its empirical Gram and response contributions by \(\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}/e_{i,a}\), using the recorded predictable propensity. Conditional randomization makes the conditional expectation of each Gram contribution equal to its full-data counterpart. Its centered entries are bounded by a constant depending on \(\varepsilon\), while the density lower bound makes every population Gram matrix uniformly nonsingular.

Here is a self-contained concentration step. If \((\xi_i)\) is a bounded martingale-difference sequence with \(|\xi_i|\leq B\), then expansion of the exponential and the elementary inequality
\[
E[e^{\lambda\xi_i}\mid\mathcal G_{i-1}]
\leq
\exp\!\left\{
\frac{\lambda^2E[\xi_i^2\mid\mathcal G_{i-1}]}
{2(1-|\lambda|B/3)}
\right\},
\qquad |\lambda|B<3,
\]
follow by using \(q!\geq2\,3^{q-2}\) for \(q\geq2\). Iterating conditional expectations, applying exponential Markov, and optimizing \(\lambda\) gives
\[
\Pr\!\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^m\xi_i\right|\geq x\right)
\leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{x^2}{2(v+Bx/3)}\right\}
\tag{69}
\]
when the sum of conditional variances is at most \(v\). Applying (69) to a fixed net of the fixed-dimensional polynomial basis shows that the empirical Gram matrix is singular or has eigenvalue below half its population bound with probability at most \(C\exp(-cmb^d)\). This concentration is applied only to bounded Gram increments, not to the unbounded Gaussian responses. On the exceptional event use a fixed bounded fit. On its complement, Taylor expansion gives integrated squared bias \(Cb^{2u}\), while martingale orthogonality and the conditional second-moment bound of the response give variance \(C/(mb^d)\). Hence
\[
E\|\widehat f-f\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C\{b^{2u}+(mb^d)^{-1}\}.
\tag{70}
\]

Apply (70) on \(I_{j-2}\) with \(u=s\) and \(b_{\mu,j}\asymp N_j^{-1/(2s+d)}\), and project the fitted regression onto \([-M,M]\). Projection cannot increase pointwise error because \(|\mu_a|\leq M\). Thus
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(s,r)}
E_P\|\widehat\mu_{j,a}-\mu_a\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C N_j^{-2\alpha_d}.
\tag{71}
\]
The fit is frozen before \(I_{j-1}\).

For \(i\in I_{j-1}\) assigned to arm \(a\), form the raw response
\[
R_i:=\{Y_i-\widehat\mu_{j,a}(X_i)\}^2.
\tag{72}
\]
Conditional on the mean-training block and on \(X_i=x,A_i=a\),
\[
E_P[R_i\mid X_i=x,A_i=a]
=\sigma_a^2(x)+\{\widehat\mu_{j,a}(x)-\mu_a(x)\}^2.
\tag{73}
\]
No response clipping is used, so (73) is exact. Gaussianity, the variance range, and clipping of \(\widehat\mu_{j,a}\) give a uniform conditional second moment for \(R_i\). Apply the degree-\(\lfloor r\rfloor\) local-polynomial smoother with \(b_{\sigma,j}\asymp N_j^{-1/(2r+d)}\), and then project the fitted function onto \([v_{\min},v_{\max}]\); call the result \(\widehat v_{j,a}\). On the good-Gram event, weighted least squares and Cauchy--Schwarz give the expected conditional stability bound
\[
E\|S_{j,a}g\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C\|g\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\]
for the frozen function used here, with an exponentially small clipped contribution on the bad event. With \(g=(\widehat\mu_{j,a}-\mu_a)^2\), boundedness gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P\|g\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
&=E_P|\widehat\mu_{j,a}(X)-\mu_a(X)|^4\\
&\leq4M^2E_P|\widehat\mu_{j,a}(X)-\mu_a(X)|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{74}
\]
Equations (70)--(74), with projection at the end, imply
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(s,r)}
E_P\|\widehat v_{j,a}-\sigma_a^2\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C\left(N_j^{-2\beta_d}\vee N_j^{-2\alpha_d}\right).
\tag{75}
\]

At the start of \(I_j\), set
\[
\widehat z_{j,a}(x):=|\omega_a(x)|\sqrt{\widehat v_{j,a}(x)},
\qquad
e_{t,a}(x):=u_a^*(\widehat z_j(x)),
\quad t\in I_j.
\tag{76}
\]
This is a law-independent measurable function of the current covariate and revealed past observations. Equations (64)--(65) give normalization and \(e_{t,a}\geq\varepsilon\), so the resulting design \(D_n^{\circ}\) belongs to \(\mathfrak D_{n,\varepsilon}\). Since fitted and true variances are at least \(v_{\min}>0\),
\[
|\widehat z_{j,a}(x)-z_a(x)|^2
\leq\frac{W^2}{4v_{\min}}
|\widehat v_{j,a}(x)-\sigma_a^2(x)|^2.
\tag{77}
\]
Using (66)--(67) and then (75)--(77) gives, uniformly for \(t\in I_j\),
\[
E_P\{V(e_t;P)-V^*(P)\}
\leq C\left(N_j^{-2\beta_d}\vee N_j^{-2\alpha_d}\right).
\tag{78}
\]

For \(t\in I_j\), define the recorded-propensity score
\[
\Psi_t:=
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\omega_a(X_t)\widehat\mu_{j,a}(X_t)
+\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}}{e_{t,a}(X_t)}
\omega_a(X_t)\{Y_t-\widehat\mu_{j,a}(X_t)\},
\qquad
T_n^{\circ}:=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n\Psi_t.
\tag{79}
\]
Use fixed bounded predictable fits and a fixed positive allocation on the initialization prefix. Every learned quantity in (79) is \(\mathcal G_{t-1}\)-measurable. Conditional randomization, consistency, and positivity give
\[
E[\Psi_t\mid\mathcal G_{t-1}^{+}]=h_P(X_t).
\]
Latent i.i.d. sampling and the deliberate exclusion of future covariates from the constructed kernel then imply
\[
E[\Psi_t\mid\mathcal G_{t-1}]=E_Ph_P(X)=\theta(P).
\tag{80}
\]
Thus \(\Psi_t-\theta(P)\) is a square-integrable martingale difference.

Put \(\delta_{j,a}:=\omega_a(\widehat\mu_{j,a}-\mu_a)\). Conditional on \(\mathcal G_{t-1}^{+}\), the outcome residual in arm \(a\) has mean zero and variance \(\sigma_a^2(X_t)\). Direct expansion of (79), followed by integration over the fresh \(X_t\), gives the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
E[(\Psi_t-\theta(P))^2\mid\mathcal G_{t-1}]
&=V(e_t;P)\\
&\quad+E_P\!\left[
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{\delta_{j,a}(X)^2}{e_{t,a}(X)}
-\left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\delta_{j,a}(X)\right\}^2
\,\middle|\,\mathcal G_{t-1}\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{81}
\]
The final square is nonnegative. Positivity, bounded weights, and (71) bound the remaining term above by \(CN_j^{-2\alpha_d}\). Martingale orthogonality, (78), and (81) therefore yield
\[
E[(T_n^{\circ}-\theta(P))^2]-\frac{V^*(P)}n
\leq\frac{C}{n^2}\sum_{j\geq2}|I_j|
\left(N_j^{-2\alpha_d}\vee N_j^{-2\beta_d}\right)
+\frac{C}{n^2}.
\]
Because \(|I_j|\leq N_j\), \(N_j=2^j\), and \(0<\alpha_d,\beta_d<1/2\), the geometric sum is at most
\[
C\left(n^{1-2\alpha_d}\vee n^{1-2\beta_d}\right).
\]
This proves the asserted uniform bound after multiplication by \(n^{-2}\). Equations (76) and (79) define one common law-independent, data-driven pair, and the argument used expected epoch risks rather than a maximum over epochs, so no logarithmic factor occurs.
