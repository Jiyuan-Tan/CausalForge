The constant nondegenerate weights imply the active-weight-region condition on the fixed cube \(B_0\).  The proposed variance-risk absorption lemma with \(d=1\) gives
\[
\mathsf Q_n^{\mu}(s,1;\omega)\asymp n^{-2s/(2s+1)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathsf Q_n^{\sigma}(s,r,1;\omega)\geq c n^{-2r/(2r+1)}.
\]
The transferred random-design variance upper theorem gives
\[
\mathsf Q_n^{\sigma}(s,r,1;\omega)\leq Cn^{-2\gamma_1}.
\]
It remains only to verify the exact maximum.  Put
\[
\alpha_1=\frac{s}{2s+1},\qquad
\beta_1=\frac{r}{2r+1},\qquad
g_1=\frac{4sr}{4sr+2s+r}.
\]
If \(s\geq r/(4r+2)\), direct cross multiplication gives \(g_1\geq\beta_1\), so \(\gamma_1=\beta_1\).  If \(s<r/(4r+2)\), the same calculation gives \(g_1<\beta_1\), while
\[
g_1-\alpha_1
=\frac{s\{s(4r-2)+3r\}}{(4sr+2s+r)(2s+1)}\geq0.
\]
For \(r\geq1/2\) the numerator is immediate.  For \(0<r<1/2\), the regime condition implies
\(s(2-4r)<r(2-4r)/(4r+2)<r<3r\), which proves the same sign.  Hence in all cases
\[
\gamma_1\geq\alpha_1\wedge\beta_1.
\]
The variance upper bound is therefore at most a constant times
\(n^{-2\alpha_1}\vee n^{-2\beta_1}\).  The mean lower bound supplies the first term and the known-mean variance lower bound supplies the second.  Taking their maximum proves the last display.
