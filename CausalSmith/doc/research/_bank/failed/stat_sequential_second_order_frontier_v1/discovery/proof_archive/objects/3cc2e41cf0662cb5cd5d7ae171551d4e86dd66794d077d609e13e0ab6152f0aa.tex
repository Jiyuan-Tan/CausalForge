Write \(q_{\mu,n}=n^{-2s/(2s+d)}\) and \(q_{\sigma,n}=n^{-2r/(2r+d)}\).  We first record the elementary random-design regression bound used twice below.  If \(f\in\mathcal H^u([0,1]^d,L)\), the design density lies between fixed positive constants, and the regression noise has uniformly bounded second moment, a boundary-corrected tensor local-polynomial estimator of degree \(\lfloor u\rfloor\), clipped to a fixed envelope, satisfies
\[
\sup_f E\|\widehat f-f\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq C\{h^{2u}+(nh^d)^{-1}\}.
\]
To see this without invoking an undeclared theorem, on each cube of side \(h\) expand \(f\) to degree \(\lfloor u\rfloor\).  The Hölder remainder is bounded by \(Lh^u\), hence its integrated squared bias is at most \(C h^{2u}\).  The density lower bound makes every rescaled polynomial Gram matrix have its smallest eigenvalue bounded away from zero, while the density upper bound and the noise second-moment bound give integrated conditional variance at most \(C/(nh^d)\).  On the event that an empirical Gram matrix is singular use zero and clip; a matrix Bernstein bound on the fixed-dimensional polynomial basis makes that event contribute at most \(Ce^{-cnh^d}\).  Taking \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2u+d)}\) proves the upper rate \(n^{-2u/(2u+d)}\).  Conversely, place disjoint smooth bumps of width \(h\) and height \(h^u\) on a regular grid inside any fixed cube of positive volume.  Adjacent Gaussian-location experiments have Kullback--Leibler divergence at most \(Cn h^{2u+d}\).  With \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2u+d)}\), Assouad's bit-testing inequality gives integrated squared risk at least
\[
c h^{-d}\,h^{2u+d}=c h^{2u}=c n^{-2u/(2u+d)}.
\]
All bump amplitudes can be multiplied by one fixed sufficiently small constant, so the Hölder radius and sup-norm constraints are respected.

For the mean risk, estimate each \(\mu_a\) by the preceding local polynomial rule and put \(\widehat g_a=\omega_a\widehat\mu_a\).  Since \(|\omega_a|\leq W\),
\[
E\|\widehat g_a-\omega_a\mu_a\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq W^2E\|\widehat\mu_a-\mu_a\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\leq Cq_{\mu,n}.
\]
For the reverse inequality choose the active arm \(a_0\), take the uniform covariate density, keep all other nuisances fixed in the interiors of their allowed ranges, and place the preceding mean bumps inside \(B_0\).  On \(B_0\), \(|\omega_{a_0}|\geq w_{\min}\), so the weighted loss is at least \(w_{\min}^2\) times the unweighted bump loss.  The Gaussian-location Kullback--Leibler calculation above therefore gives \(\mathsf Q_n^{\mu}\geq c q_{\mu,n}\).

For the variance upper bound, split the arm-specific sample into two halves.  On the first half construct the clipped mean estimator \(\widehat\mu_a\).  On the second half regress
\[
R_i=\{Y_i(a)-\widehat\mu_a(X_i)\}^2
\]
on \(X_i\) with the degree-\(\lfloor r\rfloor\) local-polynomial operator \(S_h\), and set \(\widehat h_a=\omega_a^2S_hR\).  Conditional on the first half,
\[
E[R_i\mid X_i=x,\widehat\mu_a]
=\sigma_a^2(x)+\{\widehat\mu_a(x)-\mu_a(x)\}^2.
\]
Gaussianity and the variance range bound the conditional second moment of \(R_i\) after clipping.  The same bias--variance calculation gives risk \(Cq_{\sigma,n}\) for smoothing the \(r\)-Hölder function \(\sigma_a^2\).  The local-polynomial operator is uniformly bounded on \(L_2(P_X)\) by the same Gram-matrix argument.  Since clipping makes \(|\widehat\mu_a-mu_a|\leq2M\),
\[
\|\{\widehat\mu_a-\mu_a\}^2\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
=E_P|\widehat\mu_a-mu_a|^4
\leq4M^2E_P|\widehat\mu_a-mu_a|^2
\leq Cq_{\mu,n}.
\]
Multiplication by \(\omega_a^2\) costs at most \(W^4\).  Thus
\[
\mathsf Q_n^{\sigma}(s,r,d;\omega)
\leq C(q_{\sigma,n}\vee q_{\mu,n}).
\]

For the variance lower bound, the nonempty variance interval permits a baseline \(v_0\in(v_{\min},v_{\max})\).  Set every mean to zero and, for the active arm, use the scale family
\[
\sigma_{a_0,\xi}^2(x)=v_0+\rho h^r\sum_{j=1}^{N_h}\xi_j
\psi\!\left(\frac{x-x_j}{h}\right),\qquad \xi_j\in\{-1,1\},
\]
where the bump supports are disjoint and contained in \(B_0\), \(N_h\asymp h^{-d}\), and \(\rho>0\) is fixed sufficiently small.  These functions lie in the variance Hölder ball and remain strictly inside the variance range.  For adjacent vertices \(\xi,\xi'\), the Gaussian-scale identity
\[
\operatorname{KL}\{\mathcal N(0,v),\mathcal N(0,v')\}
=\frac12\left\{\frac{v}{v'}-1-\log\frac{v}{v'}\right\}
\leq C(v-v')^2
\]
holds uniformly on the variance interval.  Hence the \(n\)-sample divergence is at most \(Cn h^{2r+d}\).  Set \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2r+d)}\) and reduce \(\rho\), if necessary, so adjacent testing affinity is bounded below.  Because \(|\omega_{a_0}|\geq w_{\min}\) on every bump,
\[
\|m_{a_0,\xi}-m_{a_0,\xi'}\|_{L_2(P_X)}^2
\geq c w_{\min}^4 h^{2r+d}
\]
for adjacent vertices.  Assouad's inequality then yields
\[
\mathsf Q_n^{\sigma}(s,r,d;\omega)
\geq cN_hh^{2r+d}=c h^{2r}=c q_{\sigma,n}.
\]
Combining the four bounds and substituting \(q_{\mu,n}=n^{-2\alpha_d}\) and \(q_{\sigma,n}=n^{-2\beta_d}\) proves every display in the proposed statement.
