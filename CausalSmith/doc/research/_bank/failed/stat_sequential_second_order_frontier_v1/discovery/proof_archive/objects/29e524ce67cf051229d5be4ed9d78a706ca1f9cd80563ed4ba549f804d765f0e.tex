Fix geometric epochs and permanent folds.  Can the candidate handle \(\mathsf H_n\) use, from each fold's prior epochs, an \(L_2(P_X)\)-adaptive mean learner and a squared-pairwise-difference Goldenshluger--Lepski or wavelet variance learner; assign the next epoch by the positivity-clipped generalized Neyman rule; and evaluate a fold-complement augmented inverse-probability estimator with recorded propensities?  The first target is the polylogarithmic upper bound
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(s,r)}
\left\{E_P[(T_n-\theta(P))^2]-\frac{V^*(P)}n\right\}
\leq \operatorname{polylog}(n)
\left(n^{-1-2s/(2s+d)}\vee n^{-1-2r/(2r+d)}\right).
\]
Can the selection penalties then be removed or absorbed to recover the constant-factor upper side of the fixed-smoothness bracket?  A positive answer must derive: \(\mathrm{(i)}\) expected adaptive-risk oracle inequalities under predictable inverse-propensity sampling; \(\mathrm{(ii)}\) quadratic allocation sensitivity using \(v_{\min}>0\); and \(\mathrm{(iii)}\) a same-scale expected cross-fit remainder.  The analysis must separate \(s\leq r\), where the fixed-smoothness bracket would match if its unrestricted lower transport is established, from \(r<s\), where an allocation-sensitive converse remains a distinct open problem.  Fixed-smoothness risk bounds alone do not establish log-free adaptation.
