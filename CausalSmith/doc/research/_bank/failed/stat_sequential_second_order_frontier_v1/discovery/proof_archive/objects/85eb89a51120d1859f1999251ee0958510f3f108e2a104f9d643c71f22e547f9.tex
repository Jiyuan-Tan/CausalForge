Put \(g=4sr/(4sr+2s+r)\).  Because every denominator is positive,
\[
g<\beta_1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
4s(2r+1)<4sr+2s+r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
s<\frac{r}{4r+2}.
\]
Thus below the threshold \(\gamma_1=g<\beta_1\), while at or above it \(\gamma_1=\beta_1\).  Also
\[
g-\alpha_1
=\frac{s\{s(4r-2)+3r\}}
{(4sr+2s+r)(2s+1)}.
\]
For \(r\geq1/2\) its numerator is positive.  For \(0<r<1/2\) in the below-threshold regime,
\[
s(2-4r)<\frac{r(2-4r)}{4r+2}<r<3r,
\]
and hence \(s(4r-2)+3r>0\).  Therefore \(\alpha_1\leq g=\gamma_1\) below the threshold.  In that regime both \(\alpha_1\wedge\gamma_1\) and \(\alpha_1\wedge\beta_1\) equal \(\alpha_1\); at or above the threshold \(\gamma_1=\beta_1\).  This proves the identity.
