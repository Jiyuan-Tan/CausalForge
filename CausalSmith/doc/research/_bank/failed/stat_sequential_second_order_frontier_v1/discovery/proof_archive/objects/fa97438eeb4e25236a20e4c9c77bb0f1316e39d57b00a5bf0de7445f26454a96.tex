Under the one-dimensional constant-weight conditions of Theorem 3, if \(s<r/(4r+2)\), then \(\gamma_1=4sr/(4sr+2s+r)<\beta_1=r/(2r+1)\), but \(\alpha_1=s/(2s+1)\le\gamma_1\). If \(s\ge r/(4r+2)\), then \(\gamma_1=\beta_1\). Hence, in every \((s,r)\)-regime, \(\alpha_1\wedge\gamma_1=\alpha_1\wedge\beta_1\), and Theorem 3 simplifies to \[\mathsf R_n(s,r,1;\omega)\asymp n^{-1-2s/(2s+1)}\vee n^{-1-2r/(2r+1)}.\]
